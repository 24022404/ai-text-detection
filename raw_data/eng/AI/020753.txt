This paper addresses the challenge of action recognition and domain adaptation in 3D body skeleton data using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). The objective is to improve the accuracy and generalizability of action recognition models across diverse datasets with varying data distributions. We propose a novel approach that leverages kernel feature maps to transform 3D skeleton data into a structured format suitable for CNN-based processing. This transformation enables the effective extraction of spatiotemporal features, enhancing the model's ability to recognize complex human actions. For domain adaptation, we introduce a supervised framework that aligns feature distributions between source and target domains, ensuring robust performance even when training and testing data originate from different environments. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in action recognition accuracy compared to traditional methods, with our approach achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. The proposed method also shows superior adaptability to new domains, reducing the need for extensive retraining. This research contributes to advancing action recognition systems in real-world applications, such as surveillance, healthcare, and human-computer interaction, by providing a scalable and domain-agnostic solution.  

**Keywords**: Action recognition, 3D body skeletons, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), domain adaptation, kernel feature maps, spatiotemporal features.
