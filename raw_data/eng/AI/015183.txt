Human pose estimation is a critical task in computer vision with applications ranging from activity recognition to human-computer interaction. This paper presents a novel approach that jointly trains a convolutional neural network (CNN) and a graphical model to improve the accuracy and robustness of pose estimation. The proposed method leverages the strengths of CNNs for feature extraction and graphical models for capturing spatial dependencies between body parts. The CNN is designed to predict initial pose keypoints, while the graphical model refines these predictions by modeling the relationships between joints. A unified training framework is introduced to optimize both components simultaneously, ensuring seamless integration and improved performance. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves state-of-the-art results, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The joint training strategy not only enhances pose estimation but also provides a scalable solution for real-world applications. This work contributes to advancing human pose estimation by combining deep learning and probabilistic graphical models, offering insights into the synergy between these techniques for complex vision tasks.  

**Keywords**: Human pose estimation, convolutional neural networks, graphical models, joint training, computer vision, deep learning.
