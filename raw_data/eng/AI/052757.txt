This paper addresses the challenge of improving kernel learning in machine learning models, particularly in the context of high-dimensional data and complex patterns. Traditional kernel methods often struggle with scalability and adaptability, limiting their effectiveness in modern applications. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel approach called Convolutional Spectral Kernel Learning (CSKL), which integrates convolutional operations with spectral techniques to enhance kernel flexibility and computational efficiency. The CSKL framework leverages spectral decomposition to approximate kernels in a low-dimensional space, while convolutional layers capture local dependencies and hierarchical features in the data. We evaluate the proposed method on benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in both accuracy and computational performance compared to conventional kernel methods. Experimental results show that CSKL achieves superior generalization capabilities, particularly in tasks involving image classification and signal processing. The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to combine the strengths of convolutional architectures with spectral kernel learning, offering a scalable and adaptable solution for complex data analysis. This research opens new avenues for applying advanced kernel methods in domains such as computer vision, natural language processing, and bioinformatics.  

**Keywords**: Kernel learning, convolutional methods, spectral decomposition, machine learning, high-dimensional data, scalability.
