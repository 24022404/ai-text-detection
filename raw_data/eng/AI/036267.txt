Active contour models have been widely used in image segmentation and object boundary detection, but they often face challenges such as sensitivity to initialization and computational inefficiency. This paper introduces a novel approach to address these limitations by reformulating active contour problems using minimal geodesic paths derived from Eikonal equations. The proposed method leverages fast marching algorithms to compute geodesic distances, enabling robust and efficient segmentation of complex structures in images. By integrating Eikonal-based geodesic paths with active contour frameworks, our approach significantly reduces sensitivity to initial conditions and improves computational performance. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate superior accuracy and speed compared to traditional active contour methods. The proposed solution not only advances the state-of-the-art in image segmentation but also opens new avenues for applications in medical imaging, computer vision, and beyond. Key contributions include a mathematically grounded framework, enhanced robustness, and practical scalability, making it a valuable tool for researchers and practitioners in the field.  

**Keywords**: Active contours, minimal geodesic paths, Eikonal equations, image segmentation, fast marching algorithms, computer vision.
