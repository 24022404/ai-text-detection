Abstract:

Object detection systems are increasingly deployed in critical applications where reliable uncertainty estimation is essential for decision-making. However, traditional object detection methods often produce deterministic outputs, lacking mechanisms to quantify predictive uncertainty. This paper introduces the concept of probabilistic object detection, formally defining it as the task of not only localizing and classifying objects within images but also providing calibrated probability estimates for both spatial localization and semantic recognition. We propose a comprehensive evaluation framework tailored to probabilistic object detection, incorporating metrics that assess both detection accuracy and the quality of uncertainty estimates. To demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, we analyze state-of-the-art object detectors augmented with probabilistic modeling techniques, including variational inference and Monte Carlo dropout. Experimental results on benchmark datasets reveal that probabilistic detectors offer improved reliability, enabling more robust downstream processing in risk-sensitive environments. Our contributions establish a clearer standard for future developments in object detection, promoting the integration of uncertainty quantification as a core aspect. This work has practical implications for safety-critical applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and medical imaging. Key keywords: probabilistic object detection, uncertainty estimation, object detection evaluation, uncertainty calibration, probabilistic modeling.
