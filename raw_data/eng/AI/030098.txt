This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing video super-resolution (VSR) for action recognition tasks by proposing a novel two-stream framework. The objective is to improve the quality of low-resolution videos while preserving critical motion and spatial details essential for accurate action recognition. Traditional VSR methods often focus solely on spatial reconstruction, neglecting the temporal dynamics crucial for understanding actions. To overcome this limitation, we introduce a two-stream architecture that simultaneously processes spatial and temporal information. The spatial stream employs a deep convolutional neural network (CNN) to reconstruct high-resolution frames, while the temporal stream leverages optical flow estimation to capture motion patterns. The two streams are integrated through a fusion mechanism, enabling the model to generate high-quality videos that are both visually sharp and temporally coherent. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing VSR techniques in terms of both reconstruction quality and action recognition accuracy. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance, with significant improvements in peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM). Furthermore, the enhanced videos lead to a notable increase in action recognition precision, validating the importance of joint spatial-temporal modeling. This research contributes to the advancement of video analysis by providing a robust solution for applications requiring high-resolution video inputs, such as surveillance, sports analytics, and autonomous systems.  

**Keywords**: Video super-resolution, action recognition, two-stream architecture, spatial-temporal modeling, optical flow, deep learning.
