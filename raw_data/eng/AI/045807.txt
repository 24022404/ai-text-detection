Objective: Graphical model inference underpins many applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and bioinformatics, yet designing efficient heuristics for tasks such as variable elimination, message scheduling, or search ordering remains manual and problem-specific. This paper explores whether reinforcement learning (RL) can automatically discover generalizable heuristics that accelerate inference while preserving accuracy.  
Methods: We formulate heuristic selection as a sequential decision process, where an RL agent observes summary statistics of the current inference state and selects operations (e.g., which variable to eliminate or which factor to update) to minimize overall computation cost. Our approach leverages deep Q-networks with graph-aware embeddings to represent variable and factor interactions. Training occurs on a diverse set of synthetic and real-world Markov random fields and Bayesian networks, with a reward function balancing inference time and solution quality.  
Results: Experiments show that learned heuristics reduce average inference time by 25–40% compared to classic greedy and static strategies, with negligible loss in marginal or maximum a posteriori accuracy (<1%). The agent adapts to varying graph topologies, factor degrees, and evidence patterns, outperforming state-of-the-art hand-tuned policies across benchmark datasets.  
Conclusion: Our findings demonstrate that reinforcement learning can effectively automate the discovery of efficient inference heuristics for graphical models, reducing development effort and enhancing performance. This approach opens avenues for adaptive inference engines capable of tailoring strategies to problem characteristics.  
Keywords: reinforcement learning, graphical models, inference heuristics, variable elimination, message passing, Markov random fields, Bayesian networks.
