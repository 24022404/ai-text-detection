Accurate estimation of vehicle pose and shape from visual data is a fundamental challenge in autonomous driving and intelligent transportation systems. This paper introduces DirectShape, a novel framework for visual vehicle pose and shape estimation that leverages direct photometric alignment of learned shape priors. Unlike conventional methods that rely heavily on indirect geometric fitting or labor-intensive 3D annotation, DirectShape utilizes a differentiable optimization scheme to directly align pre-trained 3D shape models with monocular or multi-view images by minimizing photometric error. The proposed method integrates deep learning-based shape prior representations with an efficient photometric loss, enabling robust performance under varying lighting conditions and partial occlusions. Experimental results demonstrate that DirectShape outperforms existing approaches in both pose accuracy and shape reconstruction quality across multiple benchmark datasets. Additionally, ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of direct photometric alignment and the contribution of learned shape priors to overall performance. The contributions of this work advance the state-of-the-art in visual vehicle analysis and offer potential applications in autonomous navigation, traffic surveillance, and urban scene understanding. Key keywords include vehicle pose estimation, shape reconstruction, photometric alignment, shape priors, computer vision, and autonomous driving.
