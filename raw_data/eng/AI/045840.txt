Knowledge graph reasoning, particularly answering complex queries involving logical operators, remains a challenge. This paper introduces Query2box, a novel approach that represents entities and queries in a vector space using box embeddings. Entities are embedded as points, while queries, formulated as conjunctive and disjunctive logical expressions, are represented as hyper-rectangles (boxes). Complex query answering is then translated into geometric operations within the embedding space; specifically, intersection corresponds to conjunction, union to disjunction, and projection to existential quantification. This framework allows for efficient computation of query results by assessing the containment relationship between entity points and query boxes. Experiments on benchmark knowledge graph datasets demonstrate that Query2box significantly outperforms existing embedding-based methods and rule-based systems in terms of accuracy and scalability, particularly for complex queries with multiple logical operations. The results highlight the effectiveness of box embeddings for capturing the compositional semantics inherent in knowledge graph queries, offering a promising direction for enhancing knowledge graph reasoning capabilities.
