This paper presents a comprehensive empirical study on the effectiveness of graph-based approaches for semi-supervised time series classification. Recent advances have demonstrated that utilizing graph structures can enhance learning by capturing relationships between labeled and unlabeled time series data. In this study, we systematically evaluate several state-of-the-art graph-based algorithms, including graph convolutional networks (GCN), label propagation, and graph attention networks (GAT), across multiple benchmark time series datasets. Our methodology examines different strategies for graph construction, feature representation, and the integration of limited labeled samples within a semi-supervised learning framework. Experiments measure classification accuracy, robustness to label scarcity, and computational efficiency, alongside comparisons with conventional non-graph semi-supervised methods. The results indicate that graph-based methods consistently outperform baseline approaches, particularly in scenarios with very limited labeled data, and highlight the importance of appropriate graph design for maximizing classification performance. The study also identifies key challenges, such as scalability and sensitivity to hyperparameters, providing insights into practical deployment. Our findings contribute to the understanding of graph-based learning in time series contexts and offer guidelines for selecting suitable models based on application-specific requirements. Keywords: semi-supervised learning, time series classification, graph-based methods, graph convolutional networks, label propagation, graph neural networks.
