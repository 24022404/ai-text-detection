This paper addresses the challenge of evaluating and comparing reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms by surveying existing benchmarking frameworks. The study aims to provide a comprehensive overview and analysis of the tools employed to measure and standardize the performance of various RL techniques.

Methods/Approach: The research involves a detailed examination of multiple benchmarking frameworks used in reinforcement learning, with a focus on their methodologies, evaluation metrics, supported environments, and ease of integration. The survey considers a range of frameworks, from those designed for general RL applications to specialized ones catering to specific domains and tasks. The study emphasizes the criteria for selecting appropriate frameworks based on scalability, reproducibility, and community adoption.

Results/Findings: The paper highlights key findings from the evaluation of current benchmarking frameworks, identifying strengths and limitations in their design and functionality. Comparative analysis shows varying degrees of effectiveness in handling RL-specific challenges, such as sample efficiency and exploration strategies. The study also illustrates commonalities and differences in framework capabilities, providing insights into how these tools facilitate the advancement and validation of RL models.

Conclusion/Implications: This survey contributes to the field by outlining the landscape of benchmarking frameworks, offering valuable guidance for researchers and practitioners in selecting and utilizing these tools. It underscores the importance of standardized frameworks in advancing reinforcement learning research and improving the generalizability of results. The paper concludes with recommendations for future development of benchmarking tools to better accommodate the evolving needs of RL research. Key keywords include reinforcement learning, benchmarking frameworks, evaluation metrics, scalability, and reproducibility.
