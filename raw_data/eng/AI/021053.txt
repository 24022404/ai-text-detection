This paper addresses the challenge of image segmentation in cross-country scenes captured in the infrared (IR) spectrum. The objective is to develop an effective method for accurately segmenting IR images of diverse outdoor environments, facilitating applications such as autonomous navigation and surveillance. Our approach employs a deep learning-based technique, leveraging a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture to learn features from IR images. The methodology involves training the CNN model on a dataset of annotated IR images, followed by evaluation on a separate test set. The results demonstrate significant improvement in segmentation accuracy compared to traditional methods, with an average precision of 92% and recall of 90%. The key contribution of this research lies in its ability to adapt to varying IR spectrum conditions and scene complexities, making it a valuable tool for real-world applications. The proposed technique has potential implications for enhancing the performance of IR-based systems in fields such as robotics, security, and environmental monitoring. Key keywords: image segmentation, infrared spectrum, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, autonomous navigation, surveillance.
