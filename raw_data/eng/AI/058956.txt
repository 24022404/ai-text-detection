Image segmentation in the presence of intensity inhomogeneity remains a significant challenge for many conventional techniques, often resulting in inaccurate region boundaries. This paper presents a novel local active contour model specifically designed to address the segmentation of images affected by intensity inhomogeneity. The proposed approach integrates a localized region-based energy term, allowing the contour evolution to adaptively respond to spatial variations in image intensity. Unlike global models, which are sensitive to non-uniform illumination, our method leverages local statistical information to drive the contour, achieving more precise delineation of object boundaries. Experimental evaluations on both synthetic and real-world medical and natural images demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the model, outperforming several state-of-the-art segmentation algorithms in scenarios with strong intensity variations. The results indicate superior segmentation accuracy and stability, particularly in medical imaging tasks such as tumor and organ delineation. This work contributes a significant advancement in the field of image segmentation by overcoming critical limitations of existing active contour models and enabling more reliable analysis in applications where intensity inhomogeneity is prevalent. Keywords: image segmentation, active contour, intensity inhomogeneity, local region-based energy, medical imaging.
