Stereo depth estimation is a critical task in computer vision, with applications ranging from autonomous driving to 3D reconstruction. This paper presents MTStereo 2.0, an enhanced version of the Max-tree-based stereo matching algorithm, designed to improve the accuracy and robustness of depth estimation. The proposed method leverages the hierarchical structure of Max-trees to efficiently process image pairs, enabling precise disparity map generation even in challenging scenarios such as low-texture regions and occlusions. Key improvements include a refined cost aggregation strategy and an adaptive weighting mechanism that enhances the algorithm's ability to handle complex scenes. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that MTStereo 2.0 outperforms its predecessor and several state-of-the-art methods, achieving higher accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency. The findings highlight the potential of Max-tree-based approaches for advancing stereo vision systems, with implications for real-time applications in robotics, augmented reality, and beyond.  

**Keywords**: stereo depth estimation, Max-trees, disparity maps, cost aggregation, computer vision, 3D reconstruction.
