This paper addresses the challenge of integrating spectral and spatial information in Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). Our objective is to develop a novel framework that seamlessly bridges the gap between these two domains, enabling more accurate and robust graph representation learning. We propose a hybrid approach that combines the strengths of spectral graph theory with the spatial awareness of convolutional neural networks. Our method, dubbed Spectro-Spatial Graph Convolution (SSGC), leverages a learnable spectral-spatial transformation to adaptively fuse node features and graph topology. Experimental results demonstrate that SSGC outperforms state-of-the-art GNN models on various graph classification and node classification tasks, showcasing improved performance and generalizability. The key contributions of this research lie in its ability to reconcile the spectral and spatial domains, providing a more comprehensive understanding of graph-structured data. Our work has significant implications for applications in computer vision, network analysis, and recommender systems, and paves the way for future research in graph neural networks, spectral graph theory, and geometric deep learning. Key keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Spectral Graph Theory, Spatial Domain, Convolutional Neural Networks, Graph Representation Learning.
