This paper explores the application of transfer learning with deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for gender recognition and age estimation, two critical tasks in computer vision. The objective is to leverage pre-trained CNN models to improve accuracy and efficiency in these tasks, addressing the challenges of limited labeled datasets and computational complexity. We employ transfer learning techniques using state-of-the-art architectures such as ResNet and EfficientNet, fine-tuning them on publicly available datasets for gender and age classification. Our approach involves preprocessing input images, optimizing hyperparameters, and evaluating performance metrics such as accuracy, precision, and recall. Experimental results demonstrate that transfer learning significantly enhances model performance, achieving competitive accuracy rates compared to traditional methods. Specifically, our fine-tuned models outperform baseline approaches in both gender recognition and age estimation tasks. The findings highlight the effectiveness of transfer learning in reducing training time and improving generalization, even with limited data. This research contributes to advancing automated demographic analysis, with potential applications in security, marketing, and healthcare. Key innovations include the adaptation of pre-trained CNNs for dual-task learning and the demonstration of their robustness across diverse datasets. Keywords: transfer learning, deep CNNs, gender recognition, age estimation, computer vision, ResNet, EfficientNet.
