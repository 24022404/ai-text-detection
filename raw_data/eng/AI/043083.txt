Hierarchical agglomerative clustering (HAC) is a widely used technique for grouping data into nested structures based on similarity. However, traditional HAC methods often fail to preserve the order of data points, which can be critical in applications such as time-series analysis, genomic sequencing, and document organization. This paper introduces a novel approach to order-preserving hierarchical agglomerative clustering (OP-HAC), which ensures that the inherent sequence of data points is maintained throughout the clustering process. Our method leverages a modified distance metric and a constrained merging strategy to achieve this goal. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that OP-HAC outperforms conventional HAC in preserving order while maintaining competitive clustering accuracy. The proposed approach is particularly effective in scenarios where temporal or spatial relationships are essential. This work contributes to the field by providing a robust framework for order-aware clustering, with potential applications in bioinformatics, natural language processing, and network analysis.  

**Keywords**: hierarchical clustering, order preservation, agglomerative clustering, data sequencing, constrained clustering.
