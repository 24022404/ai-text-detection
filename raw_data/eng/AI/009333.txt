Imitation learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training agents to perform complex tasks by mimicking expert demonstrations. However, most existing approaches rely on first-person demonstrations, which are often costly or impractical to obtain. This paper introduces a novel framework for *third-person imitation learning*, where an agent learns to perform tasks by observing expert demonstrations from a third-person perspective. Our approach leverages a combination of deep reinforcement learning and domain adaptation techniques to bridge the gap between third-person observations and first-person actions. We propose a two-stage training process: first, a feature extraction module aligns third-person observations with the agent's first-person state space, and second, a policy network learns to map these aligned states to actions. Experiments conducted in simulated and real-world environments demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance compared to traditional first-person imitation learning approaches, while significantly reducing the need for costly first-person data collection. The results highlight the potential of third-person imitation learning as a scalable and practical alternative for training intelligent agents in diverse applications, including robotics and autonomous systems.  

**Keywords**: imitation learning, third-person perspective, reinforcement learning, domain adaptation, robotics, autonomous systems.
