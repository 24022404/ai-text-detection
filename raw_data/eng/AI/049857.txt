**Abstract:**

Handwritten digit recognition represents a fundamental challenge in pattern recognition and has been extensively studied as a benchmark for evaluating machine learning models. This paper investigates the application of massively deep artificial neural networks (ANNs) to the task of handwritten digit classification, aiming to assess the potential performance improvements offered by increased network depth. Using a range of deep neural architectures with varying numbers of layers and neurons, we employ supervised learning techniques to train on large-scale datasets, notably the MNIST benchmark. Advanced optimization strategies, such as stochastic gradient descent with momentum and dropout regularization, are integrated to address overfitting and improve generalization. Experimental results demonstrate that massively deep ANNs achieve superior recognition accuracy, outperforming conventional shallow models and several recent deep learning approaches. Furthermore, our analysis explores the trade-offs between network depth, computational efficiency, and inference speed. The findings highlight the feasibility and benefits of leveraging deep architectures for complex pattern recognition tasks. This work contributes to the ongoing advancement of artificial intelligence in optical character recognition, offering insights for practical deployment in document processing and automated data entry systems. Keywords: deep learning, artificial neural networks, handwritten digit recognition, MNIST, pattern recognition, deep architectures.
