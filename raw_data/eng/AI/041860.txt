This paper reexamines the approach to monocular depth estimation by introducing an adversarial training methodology. The objective is to improve the accuracy and robustness of depth prediction models by leveraging the strengths of both generative and discriminative models. Our approach employs a novel architecture that combines a depth estimation network with an adversarial module, which competes with the estimator to produce more realistic depth maps. The methodology utilizes a large-scale dataset of monocular images with corresponding depth information, enabling the model to learn complex scene geometries and textures. Experimental results demonstrate that our adversarial training framework outperforms state-of-the-art monocular depth estimation methods, achieving significant improvements in depth accuracy and visual coherence. The proposed approach has important implications for various computer vision applications, including 3D reconstruction, scene understanding, and autonomous systems. Key contributions of this research include the development of a robust and accurate monocular depth estimation model, as well as insights into the effectiveness of adversarial training for improving model performance. Relevant keywords: monocular depth estimation, adversarial training, generative models, computer vision, 3D reconstruction, deep learning.
