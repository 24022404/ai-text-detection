This paper presents a novel approach to spatial mapping by combining Gaussian processes with nonstationary Fourier features. The objective is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of spatial modeling for complex, large-scale environments. Our method leverages the strengths of Gaussian processes in capturing spatial correlations and nonstationary Fourier features in representing complex patterns. We employ a flexible and expressive model that can adapt to varying spatial structures and scales. The results demonstrate significant improvements in predictive performance and computational efficiency compared to existing spatial mapping techniques. Key findings include the ability to capture nonstationary patterns and spatial anisotropy, leading to more accurate predictions and better uncertainty quantification. The proposed approach has important implications for various applications, including geospatial analysis, environmental monitoring, and robotic mapping. By integrating Gaussian processes and nonstationary Fourier features, our research contributes to the development of more accurate and efficient spatial mapping methods, with potential applications in fields such as spatial statistics, machine learning, and computer vision. Keywords: Gaussian processes, nonstationary Fourier features, spatial mapping, geospatial analysis, uncertainty quantification.
