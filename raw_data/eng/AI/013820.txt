This study aims to investigate the performance of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in generating synthetic data, with a focus on comparing different architectures and training methods. We employ a range of GAN variants, including Deep Convolutional GANs (DCGANs) and Conditional GANs (CGANs), and evaluate their ability to produce realistic and diverse samples. Our approach involves training these models on various datasets, including images and videos, and assessing their performance using metrics such as inception score and Fréchet inception distance. The results show that CGANs outperform DCGANs in terms of sample quality and diversity, and that the use of techniques such as batch normalization and dropout can significantly improve training stability. Our findings have important implications for the use of GANs in applications such as data augmentation, image-to-image translation, and generative modeling. The study contributes to the understanding of GANs and their potential applications, highlighting the importance of careful architecture design and training methodology. Key keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, Synthetic Data Generation.
