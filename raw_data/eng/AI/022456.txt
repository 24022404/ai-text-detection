This paper addresses the challenge of achieving fine-grained control over image caption generation, a critical task in computer vision and natural language processing. Traditional methods often produce generic or contextually limited captions, lacking the ability to incorporate specific user-defined constraints or preferences. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel framework leveraging *Abstract Scene Graphs* (ASGs), which enable precise manipulation of visual and semantic elements in image descriptions. Our approach integrates ASGs with a transformer-based captioning model, allowing users to specify desired attributes, relationships, and objects in the generated captions. We evaluate our method on benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in caption relevance, diversity, and adherence to user constraints compared to state-of-the-art models. Experimental results show that our framework achieves a 15% increase in BLEU-4 score and a 20% improvement in user satisfaction metrics. The proposed system not only enhances the flexibility of image captioning but also opens new avenues for applications in personalized content generation, assistive technologies, and human-computer interaction. Keywords: image captioning, abstract scene graphs, fine-grained control, transformer models, personalized content generation.
