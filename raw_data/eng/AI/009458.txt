Corporate credit rating prediction plays a crucial role in financial risk management, but its performance is often hampered by the imbalanced nature of real-world rating distributions. This paper addresses this challenge by proposing a contrastive pre-training framework designed to learn robust representations from imbalanced data. Our method leverages contrastive learning to pull together representations of companies with similar creditworthiness while pushing apart those with dissimilar ratings, particularly focusing on enhancing the representation of minority classes. We pre-train a transformer-based model on a large corpus of financial text and numerical features, subsequently fine-tuning it on a smaller, imbalanced dataset of corporate credit ratings. Experiments on real-world datasets demonstrate that our contrastive pre-training approach significantly improves the prediction accuracy, especially for under-represented rating categories, compared to traditional classification methods and standard pre-training techniques. This leads to more reliable credit risk assessments. Our findings highlight the effectiveness of contrastive learning in addressing data imbalance in financial applications, offering a valuable tool for improved credit risk management and investment strategies.
Keywords: Contrastive Learning, Imbalanced Data, Credit Rating Prediction, Financial Risk, Pre-training, Transformer.
