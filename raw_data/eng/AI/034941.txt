This paper introduces a novel approach to constructing tight Bayesian ambiguity sets for robust Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). Robust MDPs address decision-making under uncertainty in the transition probabilities. We propose a method for defining ambiguity sets based on Bayesian principles, leveraging posterior distributions over transition dynamics. Unlike existing approaches that often lead to conservative policies, our method constructs ambiguity sets that are provably tighter, resulting in less conservative and potentially higher-performing robust policies. We achieve this by carefully considering the concentration properties of the posterior distribution. Empirically, we demonstrate on benchmark MDPs that our approach yields robust policies that outperform those derived from alternative ambiguity set constructions, offering a better trade-off between robustness and performance. This improved performance is particularly notable in scenarios with limited data. Keywords: Robust MDPs, Bayesian Methods, Ambiguity Sets, Markov Decision Processes, Reinforcement Learning, Uncertainty.
