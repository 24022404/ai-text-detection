Dynamic textures, characterized by their temporal and spatial variability, play a critical role in applications such as computer vision, video synthesis, and virtual reality. However, synthesizing high-quality dynamic textures remains a challenging task due to the complexity of capturing their intricate patterns and motions. This paper addresses the problem of predicting the synthesizability of dynamic texture samples, aiming to identify which textures can be effectively synthesized using existing methods. We propose a novel machine learning-based framework that evaluates the synthesizability of dynamic textures by analyzing their spatiotemporal features. Our approach leverages a combination of deep learning models and feature extraction techniques to quantify the complexity and variability of texture samples. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves high accuracy in predicting synthesizability, outperforming traditional heuristic-based approaches. Furthermore, we provide insights into the factors that influence synthesizability, such as motion consistency and pattern regularity. The findings of this research contribute to advancing the field of texture synthesis by enabling more efficient and targeted synthesis processes, with potential applications in video editing, animation, and immersive environments.  

**Keywords**: dynamic textures, texture synthesis, machine learning, spatiotemporal analysis, video synthesis, deep learning.
