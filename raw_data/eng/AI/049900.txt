Zero-shot learning (ZSL) on 3D point clouds presents a significant challenge due to the inherent sparsity and irregularity of point cloud data, coupled with the domain gap between visual and geometric features. This paper introduces a novel ZSL framework designed to effectively recognize unseen 3D object categories by leveraging semantic relationships and cross-modal knowledge transfer. Our approach combines a graph-based representation learning module to capture structural information within point clouds with a semantic embedding space aligned with object attributes. We propose a cross-modal projection technique that maps visual features extracted from synthetic 3D models to the semantic space, enabling recognition of real-world point cloud objects without any prior training examples. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing ZSL techniques for 3D object recognition, achieving state-of-the-art performance in identifying unseen categories. Furthermore, we extend our framework to incorporate contextual information, showing improved robustness and generalization capabilities in complex scenes. This research provides a new direction for ZSL in 3D vision and opens up opportunities for applying 3D object recognition in scenarios with limited labeled data.

**Keywords:** Zero-Shot Learning, 3D Point Clouds, Object Recognition, Cross-Modal Learning, Semantic Embedding, Graph Neural Networks.
