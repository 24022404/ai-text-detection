Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have revolutionized the field of artificial intelligence by enabling the creation of highly realistic synthetic data, including images, videos, and audio. However, the increasing use of GAN-generated content raises concerns about authenticity and the potential for misuse. This study investigates whether GANs leave detectable artificial fingerprints in their outputs, which could serve as a means to distinguish synthetic data from real data. We propose a novel forensic analysis framework that leverages deep learning techniques to identify and analyze subtle artifacts introduced by GAN architectures. Our approach involves training a convolutional neural network (CNN) to classify images as either GAN-generated or authentic, focusing on pixel-level inconsistencies and spectral anomalies. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves high accuracy in detecting GAN-generated content across multiple datasets and GAN variants. Furthermore, we identify specific patterns and features that are consistently present in GAN outputs, providing insights into the inherent limitations of current GAN models. These findings have significant implications for digital forensics, content verification, and the development of more robust GAN architectures. By uncovering the artificial fingerprints left by GANs, this research contributes to the ongoing efforts to ensure the ethical and secure use of generative AI technologies.  

**Keywords**: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), artificial fingerprints, digital forensics, deep learning, synthetic data detection, convolutional neural networks (CNNs).
