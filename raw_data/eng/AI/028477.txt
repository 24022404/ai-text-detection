Airline crew pairing optimization is a complex combinatorial problem with significant impact on operational costs. This paper investigates the application of machine learning to identify and learn combinatorial patterns within optimal and near-optimal solutions to guide large-scale pairing generation. We propose a novel approach that utilizes a graph neural network (GNN) to represent and learn the structural characteristics of promising pairings. The GNN is trained on a dataset of high-quality pairings generated by a state-of-the-art optimization solver. The learned patterns are then used to bias the search process, focusing on areas of the solution space likely to contain improved pairings. Experimental results on real-world airline datasets demonstrate that our learning-assisted approach significantly reduces the computational time required to achieve high-quality solutions compared to traditional optimization methods. Furthermore, the learned patterns provide valuable insights into the underlying structure of optimal crew pairings. This work contributes a new methodology for integrating machine learning with combinatorial optimization, offering a promising direction for tackling large-scale, real-world problems. Keywords: crew pairing optimization, combinatorial optimization, machine learning, graph neural networks, airline operations.
