Deep learning approaches have achieved state-of-the-art results in action recognition, but their performance heavily relies on the availability of large, labeled datasets. Acquiring and annotating such datasets is a time-consuming and expensive process. This paper explores the use of procedural content generation (PCG) to automatically create synthetic video datasets for training deep action recognition networks. Our approach leverages a 3D animation environment to generate diverse video sequences of human-like characters performing various actions. We introduce a novel PCG pipeline that allows for precise control over scene parameters, camera angles, and actor movements, enabling the creation of targeted training data. We evaluate the effectiveness of our approach by training a convolutional recurrent neural network (CRNN) on synthetically generated videos and testing its performance on real-world action recognition benchmarks. Results demonstrate that networks trained on synthetic data generated by our PCG pipeline achieve competitive accuracy compared to those trained on traditional datasets, while significantly reducing data acquisition costs. This work contributes a practical and scalable method for generating training data, opening new avenues for action recognition research. Keywords: Procedural Content Generation, Action Recognition, Deep Learning, Synthetic Data, Computer Vision, CRNN.
