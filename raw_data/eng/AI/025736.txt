**Abstract:**

Self-supervised learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning useful data representations without relying on explicit human annotations. However, existing self-supervised methods often depend heavily on the specific choice of pretext tasks, leading to representations that may not generalize well across diverse downstream applications. This paper introduces a novel framework for Self-Supervised Learning of Pretext-Invariant Representations (PIR), designed to encourage the model to capture intrinsic data features that are robust and agnostic to pretext task variations. The proposed method integrates multiple pretext tasks during training and incorporates a consistency objective to align the representations across these tasks. Extensive experiments on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach yields representations with superior transferability and robustness compared to single-task self-supervised and supervised baselines. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations on image classification, clustering, and retrieval tasks further validate the effectiveness of our method. The results show significant improvements in accuracy and generalization, highlighting the potential of pretext-invariant representations for a wide range of computer vision and machine learning applications. Keywords: self-supervised learning, representation learning, pretext tasks, invariance, transfer learning, deep learning.
