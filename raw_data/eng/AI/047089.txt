Accurate segmentation of complex structures in images is crucial for various applications in computer vision and medical imaging. However, most existing loss functions for segmentation models fail to sufficiently emphasize boundary accuracy, leading to imprecise delineation of intricate object structures. This paper introduces InverseForm, a novel loss function designed specifically for structured boundary-aware segmentation tasks. InverseForm explicitly incorporates boundary information into the loss calculation by leveraging both global shape features and local edge cues, enabling models to more effectively learn and preserve structural details. We evaluate InverseForm on multiple benchmark segmentation datasets in both natural and biomedical image domains, comparing its performance to widely-used loss functions such as Dice and Cross-Entropy. Our experiments demonstrate that integrating InverseForm yields substantial improvements in segmentation accuracy, especially around complex boundaries and fine structures, without introducing significant computational overhead. The proposed method enhances both pixel-level and structural consistency, as evidenced by higher boundary and region-based metrics. In summary, InverseForm provides an effective and easily-adoptable solution for improving boundary fidelity in structured segmentation tasks, offering potential benefits for a range of real-world applications where precise object delineation is essential. Keywords: segmentation, loss function, boundary-aware, structured prediction, image analysis, computer vision.
