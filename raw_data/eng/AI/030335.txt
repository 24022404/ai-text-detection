This paper presents an innovative approach to automatic model-based dataset generation for efficient and accurate crop and weeds detection. The primary objective is to address the scarcity of annotated datasets, which hinders the development of robust machine learning models for precision agriculture. Our method leverages a combination of computer vision and machine learning techniques to generate synthetic datasets that mimic real-world scenarios. The approach utilizes a 3D reconstruction algorithm to create realistic models of crops and weeds, which are then used to generate a diverse range of images under various environmental conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that models trained on our generated dataset achieve significant improvements in detection accuracy and speed compared to traditional methods. The key contributions of this research include the development of a novel dataset generation framework, the creation of a large-scale synthetic dataset for crop and weeds detection, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in improving model performance. This work has significant implications for precision agriculture, enabling fast and accurate detection of crops and weeds, which can lead to improved crop yields, reduced herbicide usage, and enhanced environmental sustainability. Key keywords: crop and weeds detection, synthetic dataset generation, precision agriculture, computer vision, machine learning, 3D reconstruction.
