This paper introduces a novel approach to assistive navigation systems by developing personalized dynamics models that adapt to individual user movement patterns and preferences. Traditional navigation aids often rely on generic models, leading to suboptimal performance and user discomfort. To address this, we propose a framework that leverages wearable sensor data and machine learning techniques to create individualized models capturing unique aspects of user locomotion. Our method employs a combination of Kalman filtering and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to predict user trajectories and adapt navigation guidance in real-time. The system was evaluated through experiments with a diverse group of participants, demonstrating a significant reduction in navigation errors and improved user satisfaction compared to non-adaptive systems. Key findings highlight the importance of personalized models in enhancing the effectiveness and usability of assistive navigation technologies. The proposed system holds promise for improving mobility and independence for individuals with visual impairments or other mobility challenges. Keywords: Assistive Navigation, Personalized Models, Dynamics Modeling, Kalman Filtering, Recurrent Neural Networks, Human-Robot Interaction, Wearable Sensors.
