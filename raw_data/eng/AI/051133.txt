Semantic image segmentation, a pivotal task in computer vision, is vulnerable to privacy breaches via Membership Inference Attacks (MIAs). This paper investigates the susceptibility of segmentation models to MIAs, revealing a novel "Segmentations-Leak" phenomenon, where membership information is inadvertently leaked through segmentation outputs. We develop a suite of MIA strategies tailored for segmentation models, leveraging prediction confidence and spatial consistency to distinguish between training and non-training data. Empirical evaluations across diverse datasets (e.g., Cityscapes, Pascal VOC) and architectures (e.g., DeepLabv3+, U-Net) demonstrate the effectiveness of our attacks, achieving high inference accuracy even with limited access to model internals. Furthermore, we propose and evaluate several defense mechanisms, including differential privacy and adversarial training, to mitigate the "Segmentations-Leak" and enhance the privacy of segmentation models without significantly compromising performance. Our findings highlight the importance of privacy considerations in semantic image segmentation and provide practical guidelines for developing more robust and privacy-preserving models. Keywords: Semantic Image Segmentation, Membership Inference Attacks, Privacy, Deep Learning, Differential Privacy, Adversarial Training.
