Abstract:

This research addresses the challenge of efficient decision-making in autonomous driving systems, particularly in the context of highway navigation. The focus is on improving the learning speed and accuracy of Q-learning algorithms through model-based action proposals.

Methods: We propose an innovative approach that integrates amortized Q-learning with a model-based framework to generate action proposals tailored for driving scenarios. Our method leverages a predictive model to simulate potential outcomes of various actions, allowing for informed decision-making. We implement this approach in a simulated highway environment, where the vehicle must navigate complex traffic patterns and obstacles.

Results: Experimental results demonstrate that our proposed method significantly improves the performance of autonomous vehicles compared to traditional Q-learning approaches. The system exhibits faster convergence rates and enhanced driving safety, achieving a 30% reduction in collision incidents and a 25% increase in successful navigation through high-density traffic situations.

Conclusion: The findings indicate that the combination of amortized Q-learning with model-based action proposals provides a robust framework for improving decision-making in autonomous driving applications. This research not only contributes to the field of intelligent transportation systems but also opens avenues for developing more adaptive and reliable autonomous vehicle systems. Future work will focus on real-world implementation and further refinement of the predictive models used.

Keywords: Amortized Q-learning, autonomous driving, model-based action proposals, highway navigation, intelligent transportation systems.
