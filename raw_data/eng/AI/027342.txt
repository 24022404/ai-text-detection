This paper introduces a novel meta-learning framework to accelerate sequential decision-making. The objective is to develop surrogate models that can efficiently predict the performance of different decision-making policies across various environments. Our approach leverages meta-learning to train a surrogate model that generalizes to new environments with limited data. We propose a model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML) strategy combined with a neural network architecture tailored for capturing the dynamics of sequential decision processes. The meta-learned surrogate model is then used to rapidly evaluate and optimize policies in unseen environments. Experimental results on benchmark reinforcement learning tasks demonstrate that our method significantly reduces the sample complexity required to find effective policies compared to traditional reinforcement learning algorithms and non-meta-learned surrogate models. Specifically, our approach achieves comparable performance with significantly fewer environment interactions. This work offers a promising avenue for deploying reinforcement learning in real-world scenarios where data acquisition is expensive and time-consuming. Keywords: Meta-learning, Surrogate Models, Sequential Decision Making, Reinforcement Learning, Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning.
