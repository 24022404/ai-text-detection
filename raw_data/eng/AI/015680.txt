This paper addresses the challenge of semi-supervised classification by introducing a novel graph convolution approach that enhances linear separability and out-of-distribution generalization. Our objective is to improve the performance of graph neural networks in scenarios where labeled data is scarce. We propose a method that leverages graph convolution to learn node representations that are more linearly separable, allowing for better classification accuracy. Our approach utilizes a graph convolutional layer to aggregate node features and capture complex relationships between nodes. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing graph neural network architectures in semi-supervised classification tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets. Notably, our approach also exhibits improved generalization to out-of-distribution data, making it more robust to real-world applications. The key contributions of this research include the development of a novel graph convolution technique, improved linear separability, and enhanced out-of-distribution generalization, with potential applications in graph-based machine learning, node classification, and graph neural networks. Relevant keywords: graph convolution, semi-supervised classification, linear separability, out-of-distribution generalization, graph neural networks.
