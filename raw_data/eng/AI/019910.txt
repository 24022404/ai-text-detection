Conditional Generative Adversarial Networks (cGANs) have shown promise in generating diverse and controllable outputs, but their training remains susceptible to instability and mode collapse, especially with complex conditional inputs. This paper introduces a robust training strategy for cGANs based on a novel discriminator architecture and a spectral-normalized generator. The proposed discriminator incorporates a self-attention mechanism, enabling it to capture long-range dependencies within the generated and real data distributions, leading to improved discrimination accuracy. Simultaneously, the generator employs spectral normalization to constrain its Lipschitz constant, preventing mode collapse and promoting stable training dynamics. Experimental results on several benchmark datasets, including image synthesis and data augmentation tasks, demonstrate that our robust cGAN (RCGAN) significantly outperforms existing cGAN variants in terms of both generation quality (FID score) and conditional control accuracy. Furthermore, RCGAN exhibits greater resilience to variations in training data and hyperparameter settings. Our findings highlight the importance of robust training techniques for realizing the full potential of conditional generative models. Keywords: cGAN, generative adversarial network, robust training, spectral normalization, self-attention, image synthesis.
