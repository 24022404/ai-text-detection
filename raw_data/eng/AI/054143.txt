This paper addresses the challenge of training robust image classification models in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or unavailable. Our objective is to develop an approach that enables on-the-fly learning of image labels, facilitating the training of accurate and reliable classification models. We propose a novel framework that leverages active learning and weak supervision to learn image labels dynamically during the training process. Our method utilizes a combination of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and graph-based algorithms to identify the most informative samples and assign labels accordingly. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms traditional supervised learning methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets. The key findings of our research include improved model robustness, increased labeling efficiency, and enhanced adaptability to new datasets. Our contributions have significant implications for real-world applications, such as image recognition, object detection, and autonomous systems, where labeled data is often limited. Key keywords: on-the-fly learning, image classification, active learning, weak supervision, CNNs, graph-based algorithms, robust models.
