This paper addresses the problem of estimating prediction uncertainty in deep neural networks. With the growing deployment of these models in critical applications, ensuring reliable predictions is essential. The focus is on constructing Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) confidence sets that provide users with calibrated uncertainty measures, thus improving trust and decision-making in AI systems.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel method for creating PAC confidence sets by leveraging the concept of calibrated prediction. The developed approach integrates statistical calibration techniques with modern deep learning architectures. By systematically balancing prediction accuracy and uncertainty estimation, our method dynamically adjusts confidence intervals to align with a specified error tolerance, providing robust interpretations of model predictions.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations on diverse benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms existing uncertainty quantification techniques in terms of coverage probability and set efficiency. Our PAC confidence sets offer tighter bounds while maintaining high coverage rates compared to traditional approaches, thereby enhancing the reliability of deep neural network predictions.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed approach enables practitioners to quantify prediction uncertainty more effectively, potentially transforming applications in domains such as autonomous driving, medical diagnosis, and financial forecasting. This research contributes to the advancement of deep learning techniques by bridging the gap between theoretical guarantees and practical deployment, paving the way for more trustworthy and interpretable AI systems.

Keywords: PAC confidence sets, deep neural networks, calibrated prediction, uncertainty quantification, AI trustworthiness.
