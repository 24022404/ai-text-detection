This research investigates the instance-based interpretability of Variational Auto-Encoders (VAEs), which are widely used in generating complex data representations. The study aims to address the challenge of understanding how VAEs make decisions at the instance level, which remains a crucial barrier in deploying these models in sensitive applications requiring transparency.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel interpretability approach that combines feature attribution techniques with instance-based analysis. By introducing a framework that leverages perturbation-based methods and latent space manipulation, we facilitate the examination of input-output relationships on a per-instance basis. This method enables the identification of pivotal features that influence the reconstruction and latent variable formation in VAEs.

Results/Findings: Our findings reveal significant insights into the internal workings of VAEs, demonstrating that specific modifications in input data can lead to predictable changes in latent representations. Experimental evaluations show that our approach effectively highlights critical input features across various datasets, offering substantial improvements in understanding model behavior compared to traditional methods.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed instance-based interpretability framework offers a powerful tool for dissecting VAE decision-making processes, enhancing trust and transparency in AI models. These insights pave the way for deploying VAEs in areas such as healthcare and finance, where understanding model decisions is paramount. Our contributions include the development of methodologies that enhance the robustness and applicability of VAEs, providing a foundation for future advancements in neural network interpretability.

Keywords: Variational Auto-Encoder, interpretability, instance-based analysis, latent space, feature attribution, neural networks, transparency.
