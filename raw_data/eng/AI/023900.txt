Phase retrieval, the problem of recovering a signal from the magnitude of its Fourier transform, arises in various scientific domains. This paper introduces a novel stochastic alternating minimizing (SAM) method for solving the sparse phase retrieval problem, where the underlying signal is assumed to be sparse. The proposed algorithm iteratively refines signal estimates by alternating between data fitting and sparsity promoting steps, incorporating stochastic sampling to enhance computational efficiency and escape local minima. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that the SAM method achieves superior recovery performance compared to state-of-the-art algorithms, particularly in high-dimensional settings with limited measurements. Furthermore, the method exhibits robustness to noise and outperforms existing approaches in terms of both recovery accuracy and computational time. This research contributes a practical and efficient solution for sparse phase retrieval, offering significant implications for applications such as X-ray crystallography, diffraction imaging, and astronomical imaging.

**Keywords:** Phase Retrieval, Sparse Recovery, Stochastic Optimization, Alternating Minimization, Signal Processing.
