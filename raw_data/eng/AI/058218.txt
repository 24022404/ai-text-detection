Distributed learning offers significant advantages in training machine learning models across multiple devices, but it also introduces vulnerabilities to malicious attacks such as data poisoning and model manipulation. This paper introduces Aspis, a novel robust detection system designed to identify and mitigate malicious contributions in distributed learning environments. Aspis employs a multi-faceted approach, combining statistical analysis of model updates, anomaly detection techniques, and a reputation-based scoring system to assess the trustworthiness of participating devices. We evaluate Aspis on several benchmark datasets and demonstrate its effectiveness in detecting various attack strategies, including Byzantine attacks and targeted poisoning attacks. The results show that Aspis achieves a high detection rate with minimal impact on model accuracy and convergence speed compared to existing defense mechanisms. Aspis provides a practical and efficient solution for securing distributed learning systems, enabling reliable model training in adversarial settings. Keywords: Distributed Learning, Security, Anomaly Detection, Data Poisoning, Robustness, Byzantine Fault Tolerance.
