Linear stochastic approximation (LSA) algorithms are fundamental tools in optimization, signal processing, and reinforcement learning for estimating solutions to systems influenced by noise. This paper investigates the behavior and performance of LSA algorithms under constant step-size regimes, addressing challenges related to stability and convergence. We introduce an analytical framework that leverages iterate averaging—a technique where algorithm iterates are averaged over time—to enhance the statistical efficiency and stability of LSA updates. Through theoretical analysis and empirical studies, we establish that combining constant step-size with iterate averaging yields improved convergence rates and reduces variance, even in the presence of stochastic disturbances. Our results demonstrate that this approach outperforms traditional decreasing step-size schemes in terms of both accuracy and robustness. The findings have significant implications for large-scale machine learning and adaptive signal processing applications, where rapid convergence and reliable performance are critical. Key contributions include new error bounds, insights into parameter selection, and guidelines for practical implementation. The proposed methodology facilitates more efficient deployment of LSA algorithms in environments with persistent noise, paving the way for advancements in online learning, adaptive control, and dynamic system identification.

Keywords: linear stochastic approximation, constant step-size, iterate averaging, convergence analysis, machine learning, adaptive algorithms, variance reduction.
