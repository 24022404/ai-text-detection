In this paper, we address the challenge of outlier detection within the framework of multi-armed bandit (MAB) problems, a critical issue impacting the decision-making efficacy and overall performance of agents that rely on these models. The presence of outliers can significantly skew results and affect the learning process, necessitating robust detection methodologies.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel generic outlier detection technique tailored specifically for multi-armed bandit setups. By leveraging statistical analysis and adaptive algorithms, our approach identifies anomalies that deviate from typical behavioral patterns of bandits. The methodology incorporates a dynamic threshold mechanism to adjust to varying levels of bandit activity and environmental unpredictability, ensuring high sensitivity and accuracy.

Results/Findings: Our experimental evaluation demonstrates that the proposed outlier detection method significantly enhances the robustness of MAB solutions in both synthetic and real-world scenarios. The approach consistently identifies outliers with high precision, reducing false positives compared to traditional detection strategies. Additionally, the integration of our method into MAB models results in a marked improvement in cumulative rewards and reduces exploration-exploitation trade-offs.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of this generic outlier detection method for multi-armed bandits represents a substantial advancement in improving the reliability and efficiency of these models in dynamic environments. The research offers significant implications for applications across various domains, including adaptive learning systems, recommendation engines, and automated decision-making processes, where the presence of outliers can critically impact performance. By enhancing outlier robustness, our work contributes to the development of more resilient and effective MAB-based applications.

Keywords: outlier detection, multi-armed bandit, anomaly detection, adaptive algorithms, decision-making, model robustness, dynamic environments.
