This paper explores the computational efficiency of training neural networks using Neural Tangent Kernels (NTKs) in scenarios where input data exhibits sparsity. The objective is to address the challenge of scaling kernel-based methods to large-scale datasets while maintaining near-linear time complexity with respect to input sparsity. We propose a novel algorithmic framework that leverages the inherent sparsity of input data to accelerate NTK computations, reducing the time complexity from quadratic to near-linear in the number of non-zero features. Our approach combines sparse matrix operations with kernel approximation techniques, enabling efficient training without sacrificing model accuracy. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance with state-of-the-art models while significantly reducing computational overhead. The findings highlight the potential of sparse NTK-based methods for scalable machine learning applications, particularly in domains with high-dimensional, sparse data such as natural language processing and recommendation systems. This work contributes to the growing body of research on efficient kernel methods and provides a practical solution for deploying NTK-based models in resource-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: Neural Tangent Kernels, input sparsity, kernel methods, scalable machine learning, sparse matrix operations.
