This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing weights and activation functions in neural networks for time series prediction, a critical task in various domains such as finance, weather forecasting, and energy management. The objective is to enhance prediction accuracy by systematically exploring the impact of weight initialization strategies and activation function selection on model performance. We propose a hybrid approach combining gradient-based optimization techniques with heuristic methods to fine-tune network parameters. Our methodology leverages adaptive learning rates and a novel activation function selection framework to improve convergence and generalization. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in prediction accuracy compared to traditional methods, with reductions in mean squared error (MSE) of up to 15%. The findings highlight the importance of tailored weight initialization and activation functions in achieving robust time series forecasting. This research contributes to the field by providing a systematic framework for optimizing neural network architectures, offering practical insights for applications requiring high-precision predictions. Keywords: neural networks, time series prediction, weight optimization, activation functions, machine learning, forecasting.
