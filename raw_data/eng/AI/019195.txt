This paper presents a novel approach to 3D reconstruction by introducing Deep Implicit Moving Least-Squares (DIMLS) functions. The research aims to address the limitations of existing 3D reconstruction methods which often struggle with accurately capturing complex geometries and ensuring smoothness across reconstructed surfaces.

Methods/Approach: The proposed DIMLS method integrates deep learning techniques with moving least-squares (MLS) principles to construct implicit surfaces from unorganized point cloud data. Utilizing a neural network architecture, the DIMLS framework models the geometric surface by predicting local surface patches which are then aggregated to form a continuous implicit representation.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that DIMLS significantly outperforms traditional MLS-based methods in terms of accuracy and resilience. The results show marked improvements in preserving intricate surface details while maintaining global smoothness. Comparative analyses indicate that the proposed method reduces reconstruction errors and artifacts when benchmarked against state-of-the-art techniques.

Conclusion/Implications: This research advances the field of 3D reconstruction by offering a robust and versatile solution capable of reconstructing high-fidelity surfaces from arbitrary datasets. The introduction of deep implicit functions is a crucial innovation that promises enhanced performance in applications such as computer-aided design, virtual reality environments, and autonomous navigation systems. DIMLS opens new avenues for future research focused on improving computational efficiency and scalability.

Keywords: 3D reconstruction, deep learning, implicit functions, moving least-squares, neural networks, point cloud, surface modeling.
