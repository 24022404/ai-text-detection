This paper investigates the design and implementation of autonomous agents with distinct behavioral traits—prosocial and selfish—for contract negotiation scenarios using reinforcement learning (RL). The objective is to explore how varying agent behaviors influence negotiation outcomes, fairness, and efficiency in multi-agent systems. We propose a novel RL-based framework where agents are trained to exhibit either prosocial (cooperative) or selfish (competitive) tendencies, enabling them to adapt their strategies during negotiations. The approach leverages deep Q-learning with reward structures tailored to each behavior type, ensuring agents learn optimal policies aligned with their designated traits. Experimental results demonstrate that prosocial agents achieve higher overall system utility and fairness, while selfish agents maximize individual gains at the expense of collective outcomes. Comparisons with baseline negotiation strategies highlight the effectiveness of the proposed framework in modeling diverse agent behaviors. The findings provide valuable insights into the design of multi-agent systems for real-world applications, such as supply chain management and automated trading, where balancing individual and collective interests is critical. This research contributes to the growing field of AI-driven negotiation by introducing a behavior-aware RL approach that enhances adaptability and realism in agent interactions.  

**Keywords**: reinforcement learning, contract negotiation, prosocial behavior, selfish behavior, multi-agent systems, deep Q-learning, fairness, utility optimization.
