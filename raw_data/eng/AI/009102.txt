Few-shot learning aims to recognize new categories given only a few labeled examples. Current meta-learning approaches often rely on feature embeddings and metric learning. However, these methods can struggle with significant intra-class variation and inter-class similarity in complex datasets. This paper introduces a novel few-shot learning framework that integrates spatial attention mechanisms to improve feature discrimination. Our approach, Few-Shot Few-Shot Learning with Spatial Attention (FS-FSL-SA), learns to focus on the most relevant spatial regions within an image, enhancing the representation of crucial features while suppressing irrelevant background information. We employ a two-stage few-shot learning strategy where the first stage leverages global features for initial classification, and the second stage refines the classification based on spatially attentive features. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including miniImageNet and tieredImageNet, demonstrate that FS-FSL-SA achieves significant performance gains compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly in challenging scenarios with extremely limited labeled data. The results highlight the importance of spatial attention in improving feature representation and generalization in few-shot learning, offering a promising direction for developing more robust and accurate few-shot classifiers. Keywords: Few-shot learning, meta-learning, spatial attention, image classification, machine learning.
