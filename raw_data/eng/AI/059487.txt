This paper addresses the challenge of cross-domain adaptation, where a model trained on one dataset is applied to a different but related dataset, often resulting in decreased performance. The objective of this research is to develop an importance filtered cross-domain adaptation approach that selectively transfers knowledge from the source domain to the target domain. Our method utilizes a novel filtering mechanism to identify and weigh the importance of features from the source domain, allowing for more effective adaptation to the target domain. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art cross-domain adaptation methods, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and robustness. The key findings of this study highlight the importance of feature selection and weighting in cross-domain adaptation, and our approach has important implications for real-world applications where data distributions may vary. This research contributes to the field of domain adaptation, transfer learning, and machine learning, with potential applications in areas such as computer vision, natural language processing, and AI models. Key keywords: cross-domain adaptation, transfer learning, importance filtering, feature selection, domain adaptation, machine learning.
