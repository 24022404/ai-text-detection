This paper explores the application of neural message passing to quantum chemistry, aiming to improve the accuracy and efficiency of molecular property predictions. Our approach utilizes a graph neural network framework to learn effective representations of molecular structures, enabling the prediction of quantum chemical properties such as energies and forces. By leveraging the strengths of both machine learning and quantum mechanics, our method achieves state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets, outperforming traditional quantum chemistry methods in terms of accuracy and computational cost. The key innovation of our work lies in the design of a novel message passing scheme, which allows for the efficient exchange of information between atoms in a molecule. Our research contributes to the development of more accurate and efficient quantum chemistry models, with potential applications in fields such as drug discovery and materials science. Key keywords: neural message passing, quantum chemistry, graph neural networks, molecular property prediction, machine learning.
