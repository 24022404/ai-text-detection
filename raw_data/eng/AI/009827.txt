The paper addresses the challenge of fast and accurate object detection, particularly focusing on the segmentation of small objects, which are often overlooked in existing methods. This research seeks to enhance object detection by integrating multiple receptive fields and a novel weakly-supervised segmentation approach.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a segmentation network that utilizes multiple receptive fields to capture diverse context information effectively. The network is designed with a specific emphasis on small-object targeting, using a weakly-supervised learning framework that reduces the need for extensive labeled data. Our approach leverages a combination of feature pyramid networks and attention mechanisms to prioritize small object regions, ensuring they are accurately detected alongside larger objects.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our proposed method significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques in terms of speed and accuracy. The integration of multiple receptive fields allows the model to adapt to various object scales, while the small-object-focusing mechanism ensures enhanced precision for smaller targets. Comparisons across standard datasets reveal a marked improvement in detection rates and computational efficiency.

Conclusion/Implications: This research presents a novel contribution to object detection methodologies by combining multiple receptive fields with a weakly-supervised framework, particularly optimizing the detection of small objects. The proposed approach offers substantial implications for real-time applications in areas such as surveillance, autonomous vehicles, and robotics where fast and reliable object detection is crucial. This study paves the way for further advancements in segmentation networks, with potential extensions towards more complex detection tasks and broader applications.

Keywords: object detection, multiple receptive fields, weakly-supervised learning, segmentation network, small-object detection, feature pyramid networks, attention mechanisms.
