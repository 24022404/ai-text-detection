Meta-transfer learning has emerged as a powerful approach for enabling models to adapt efficiently to new tasks with limited data. This paper investigates a novel strategy for meta-transfer learning, focusing specifically on the impact of hard tasks—those with high intra-class similarity or challenging decision boundaries—on the transferability and generalization capabilities of deep neural networks. We propose a meta-transfer learning framework that dynamically identifies and leverages hard tasks during the meta-training phase, thereby enhancing the model’s capacity to extract robust and transferable features. Experimental evaluations are conducted on benchmark few-shot learning datasets, where our approach is compared against conventional meta-learning and meta-transfer learning methods. Results indicate that integrating hard tasks into meta-training leads to significant improvements in adaptation speed and overall task accuracy, particularly in scenarios with limited support samples. The findings suggest that prioritizing hard tasks during the training process enables more effective knowledge transfer and model generalization. This research provides valuable insights into meta-transfer learning processes, and paves the way for future development of adaptive learning algorithms in scenarios with complex or ambiguous tasks. Key keywords: meta-learning, transfer learning, hard tasks, deep neural networks, few-shot learning, model generalization.
