This paper introduces Compressive Transformers, a novel model architecture designed to address the challenges associated with long-range sequence modeling in natural language processing and related fields. Traditional transformer models face limitations in efficiently modeling long sequences due to high computational and memory demands. The objective is to enhance sequence processing capabilities while maintaining performance efficiency.

Methods/Approach: The proposed Compressive Transformers leverage a compression mechanism that reduces the effective sequence length by summarizing past information into compact representations. This is achieved by adding a compression layer that intermittently compresses parts of the sequence, allowing the model to focus on essential patterns and dependencies over extended contexts. The approach maintains the transformer's architecture but enhances scalability for longer sequences.

Results/Findings: The Compressive Transformer demonstrates superior performance on multiple long-range sequence benchmarks compared to standard transformer-based architectures. Experimental results indicate a significant reduction in memory usage and processing time, with comparable or improved predictive accuracy. Key findings reveal that the model excels in tasks involving extensive context, such as document summarization, long-form dialogue understanding, and other sequence-heavy applications.

Conclusion/Implications: Compressive Transformers provide a promising direction for efficient long-range sequence modeling, opening up possibilities for advanced applications in areas requiring the processing of voluminous sequential data. The model's ability to handle extended context without sacrificing efficiency offers substantial implications for the deployment of transformer models in resource-constrained environments. This contribution advances the capability to effectively address AI challenges across various domains requiring intricate sequence analysis.

Keywords: Compressive Transformers, long-range sequence modeling, natural language processing, memory efficiency, sequence compression, AI models, transformer architecture.
