This paper presents a novel approach, Label-PEnet, designed to address the challenging problem of weakly supervised instance segmentation. The main goal is to improve segmentation accuracy by effectively utilizing limited annotated data through a sequential label propagation and enhancement strategy.

Methods/Approach: Label-PEnet incorporates a unique dual-stage framework. The first stage uses a label propagation network to leverage partially labeled data, efficiently spreading the available information across unlabeled instances. In the second stage, an enhancement network refines the propagated labels, ensuring greater precision and segmentation quality. The integration of these two stages enables robust performance with minimal supervision while minimizing computational complexity.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that Label-PEnet significantly outperforms existing weakly supervised segmentation methods across multiple benchmark datasets. The approach achieves higher accuracy in instance segmentation tasks, showing improvements in key metrics such as Intersection over Union (IoU) and precision-recall curves. Notably, the network is able to generate finer segmentation boundaries and reduce false positives.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of Label-PEnet marks a substantial advancement in the field of weakly supervised learning for instance segmentation. By combining sequential label propagation with enhancement phases, this research offers a promising direction for future developments in computer vision, particularly in scenarios with limited annotated data. Potential applications span various domains, including medical imaging, autonomous driving, and remote sensing, where high-quality segmentation is crucial. 

Keywords: Weakly Supervised Learning, Instance Segmentation, Label Propagation, Enhancement Networks, Computer Vision, Label-PEnet.
