This paper investigates the theoretical and practical advantages of model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) over model-free approaches in contextual decision processes. While model-free methods have dominated recent advancements in reinforcement learning (RL), their sample inefficiency and lack of interpretability remain significant challenges. We address these limitations by proposing a novel MBRL framework that leverages structured environment dynamics to achieve exponential improvements in sample complexity. Our approach integrates probabilistic modeling with efficient exploration strategies, enabling the algorithm to generalize across diverse contexts with minimal data. Theoretical analysis provides Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) bounds, demonstrating that our method achieves significantly faster convergence rates compared to state-of-the-art model-free techniques. Empirical evaluations on benchmark environments confirm these findings, showcasing superior performance in terms of both learning speed and final policy quality. The results highlight the potential of MBRL to bridge the gap between theoretical guarantees and practical applicability in complex decision-making tasks. This work contributes to the growing body of research advocating for the integration of model-based reasoning in RL, offering a scalable and interpretable alternative for real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Model-based reinforcement learning, contextual decision processes, PAC bounds, sample efficiency, exploration strategies, interpretability.
