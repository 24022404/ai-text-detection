This paper addresses the challenge of learning Linear Non-Gaussian Acyclic Models (LiNGAM) from high-dimensional data where the number of variables exceeds the number of observations. The objective is to develop an efficient method for estimating the causal relationships between variables in such scenarios. Our approach utilizes a novel combination of dimensionality reduction techniques and sparse regression to identify the underlying causal structure. The results show that our method outperforms existing approaches in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, especially when dealing with large numbers of variables. The key findings highlight the importance of incorporating non-Gaussianity and sparsity priors to improve the robustness of LiNGAM estimation. This research contributes to the field of causal discovery and has significant implications for applications in genetics, finance, and social sciences, where high-dimensional data are common. The proposed method enables researchers to uncover causal relationships in complex systems, even when the number of observations is limited. Key keywords: LiNGAM, causal discovery, high-dimensional data, dimensionality reduction, sparse regression, non-Gaussianity.
