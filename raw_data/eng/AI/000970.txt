Few-shot learning aims to generalize to new tasks with limited labeled data. While transformer-based models have shown promise in various domains, their spatial reasoning capabilities are often limited, hindering effective transfer learning in spatially-aware tasks. This paper introduces CrossTransformers, a novel architecture designed to enhance spatial awareness in few-shot transfer learning. CrossTransformers incorporate cross-attention mechanisms that explicitly model spatial relationships between features, enabling the model to better understand and leverage spatial context. We evaluate CrossTransformers on several spatially-dependent few-shot classification benchmarks, including remote sensing scene classification and medical image diagnosis. Our results demonstrate that CrossTransformers outperform existing transformer-based methods and other state-of-the-art few-shot learning algorithms, achieving significant improvements in classification accuracy, especially in scenarios with extremely limited data. The enhanced spatial reasoning capabilities of CrossTransformers facilitate more effective knowledge transfer, leading to robust performance across diverse tasks. The proposed model offers a promising direction for improving few-shot learning in applications where spatial information plays a crucial role.
