This paper introduces PISE, a novel approach to person image synthesis and editing utilizing a decoupled Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) architecture. The objective is to generate high-quality, photorealistic person images while allowing for flexible editing capabilities. Our method employs a two-stage framework, where the first stage focuses on generating a person's appearance, and the second stage concentrates on pose and shape adjustment. This decoupling enables more precise control over the synthesis process. We evaluate PISE on various benchmarks, demonstrating its superiority over existing methods in terms of image quality, diversity, and editing flexibility. The results show that PISE achieves state-of-the-art performance in person image synthesis, with significant improvements in preserving identity and pose consistency. Our approach has potential applications in fields such as computer vision, robotics, and virtual reality, particularly in areas like character generation, video game development, and social media content creation. Key contributions of this work include the introduction of a decoupled GAN architecture for person image synthesis and editing, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in generating high-quality, editable person images. Relevant keywords: person image synthesis, generative adversarial networks, image editing, computer vision, deep learning.
