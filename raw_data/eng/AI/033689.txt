**Abstract:**  
Mutual information (MI) is a fundamental metric for quantifying dependencies between random variables in numerous applications spanning machine learning, data mining, and information theory. However, accurate and scalable estimation of MI, particularly from high-dimensional or limited data, remains a significant challenge. In this paper, we propose a novel inductive approach for mutual information estimation by leveraging a convex maximum-entropy copula framework. Our method employs copula theory to decouple marginal distributions from dependency structures, enabling flexible modeling of complex, non-linear relationships. By formulating the MI estimation as a convex optimization problem under maximum-entropy constraints, we ensure global optimality and improved robustness compared to traditional non-convex or heuristic methods. Experimental evaluations on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves superior accuracy, reduced bias, and enhanced scalability relative to existing estimators, especially in high-dimensional contexts. Theoretical analysis establishes consistency and convergence guarantees for our estimator. The proposed inductive mutual information estimation framework has broad applicability in domains such as feature selection, deep learning, and causal inference, paving the way for more reliable dependency discovery in complex data environments.  
**Keywords:** mutual information, copula, maximum entropy, convex optimization, dependency estimation, high-dimensional data, information theory.
