This paper introduces AAG, a novel self-supervised representation learning framework that leverages Auxiliary Augmentation in conjunction with the GNT-Xent loss function to enhance feature extraction from unlabeled data. Our objective is to address the limitations of existing contrastive learning methods, which often rely on limited augmentation strategies and conventional loss functions, potentially restricting representation diversity and robustness. To this end, we propose an Auxiliary Augmentation Generator that produces additional, informative data transformations beyond standard augmentation pipelines. These diverse views are integrated within a Graph-based Normalized Temperature Cross-Entropy (GNT-Xent) loss, designed to strengthen the model's ability to distinguish subtle differences and similarities between samples. We evaluate AAG on several benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, demonstrating significant improvements in downstream classification and clustering tasks compared to baseline self-supervised models. Results indicate that our approach yields richer and more transferable representations, leading to enhanced performance and generalization. The proposed framework is model-agnostic and fully unsupervised, offering broad applicability across various data domains. Our findings highlight the potential of combining auxiliary augmentation strategies with advanced contrastive objectives for advancing self-supervised learning. Keywords: self-supervised learning, auxiliary augmentation, contrastive learning, representation learning, GNT-Xent loss, deep learning.
