Text generation models are increasingly prevalent, yet evaluating their performance remains challenging, particularly concerning the balance between diversity and quality. This paper introduces a novel joint metric for assessing both aspects simultaneously. Our approach leverages contextual embeddings and variational inference to quantify the semantic distinctiveness of generated texts (diversity) while assessing their coherence and relevance to the input prompt (quality). We propose a new variational autoencoder-based framework that learns a joint representation of text quality and diversity, enabling a more nuanced evaluation than traditional metrics. Experiments on several benchmark datasets demonstrate that our metric correlates strongly with human judgments of both diversity and quality, outperforming existing metrics in capturing the trade-off between these two critical factors. This work provides a practical and interpretable tool for guiding the development and refinement of text generation models, facilitating the generation of high-quality and diverse textual outputs. Keywords: Text Generation, Diversity, Quality, Evaluation Metric, Variational Autoencoder, Natural Language Processing.
