This paper addresses the challenge of evaluating machine learning models for graph topology inference, a crucial task in various domains such as network analysis and computational biology. Our objective is to establish a comprehensive benchmarking framework to assess the performance of different machine learning approaches in inferring graph topologies from node and edge attributes. We propose a novel methodology that combines graph generation algorithms with machine learning models to create a diverse set of benchmark graphs. Our approach utilizes techniques such as graph neural networks and node embedding to learn representations of graph structures. The results show that our benchmark framework can effectively evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of various machine learning models, including graph convolutional networks and graph attention networks. Our findings have significant implications for the development of more accurate and efficient graph topology inference methods, with potential applications in fields like social network analysis and traffic forecasting. Key contributions of this research include the introduction of a scalable benchmarking framework, the evaluation of state-of-the-art machine learning models for graph topology inference, and the identification of promising directions for future research in this area. Relevant keywords: graph topology inference, machine learning, graph neural networks, node embedding, benchmarking framework.
