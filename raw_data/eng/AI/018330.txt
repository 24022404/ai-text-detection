Metric learning plays a pivotal role in enhancing the performance of various machine learning tasks, such as image retrieval, clustering, and classification, by learning effective distance metrics. However, existing approaches often struggle with scalability and generalization across diverse datasets. This paper introduces a novel framework, *Divide and Conquer the Embedding Space* (DACES), which partitions the embedding space into smaller, manageable subspaces to optimize metric learning. The proposed method employs a hierarchical clustering mechanism to divide the data into semantically coherent groups, followed by subspace-specific metric learning to capture fine-grained relationships within each partition. By leveraging this divide-and-conquer strategy, DACES significantly reduces computational complexity while improving the discriminative power of learned embeddings. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that DACES outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of retrieval accuracy and clustering performance. Furthermore, the framework exhibits superior scalability, making it suitable for large-scale applications. The key contributions of this work include a scalable and efficient approach to metric learning, enhanced generalization across datasets, and a robust framework adaptable to various downstream tasks. This research opens new avenues for improving metric learning in high-dimensional spaces, with potential applications in computer vision, recommendation systems, and beyond.  

**Keywords**: metric learning, embedding space, divide-and-conquer, hierarchical clustering, scalability, machine learning.
