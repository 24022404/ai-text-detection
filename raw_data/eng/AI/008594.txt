Spectral methods have long been fundamental in machine learning, providing powerful tools for dimensionality reduction, clustering, and manifold learning. However, they often struggle with scalability and adaptability to complex data distributions. In contrast, deep learning excels at feature extraction but lacks interpretability and theoretical guarantees. To bridge this gap, we propose Spectral Inference Networks (SIN), a novel framework that unifies spectral learning and deep neural networks to leverage the strengths of both approaches.

Our method integrates spectral decomposition techniques within a deep learning architecture, enabling efficient inference of eigenfunctions and low-dimensional representations in an end-to-end manner. By parameterizing spectral components with neural networks, SIN can adaptively learn task-specific embeddings while preserving the mathematical foundations of spectral learning. Additionally, we introduce a regularization mechanism that maintains stability in the learned spectral representations, improving robustness to noisy and high-dimensional data.

Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that SIN achieves state-of-the-art performance in spectral clustering, graph-based learning, and representation learning tasks, surpassing conventional spectral methods and deep learning baselines. These results highlight the effectiveness of combining spectral insights with deep learning for improved generalization and interpretability.

Keywords: Spectral Learning, Deep Learning, Eigenfunction Inference, Representation Learning, Spectral Decomposition, Graph-Based Learning.
