This paper presents a novel approach to imitation learning, leveraging the Wasserstein distance metric to improve the robustness and efficiency of adversarial imitation learning algorithms. The objective is to develop a framework that enables an agent to learn from demonstrations by minimizing the Wasserstein distance between the agent's policy and the expert's policy. Our method employs a critic network to estimate the Wasserstein distance, which is then used to update the agent's policy via reinforcement learning. The results show that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of sample efficiency and robustness to demonstration noise. Key findings include improved performance on complex tasks and reduced sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning. The proposed framework has significant implications for real-world applications, such as robotics and autonomous driving, where imitation learning can be used to learn from human demonstrations. Our contributions include the introduction of the Wasserstein distance metric to adversarial imitation learning, which provides a more robust and efficient alternative to existing methods. Keywords: Imitation Learning, Adversarial Learning, Wasserstein Distance, Reinforcement Learning, Robotics.
