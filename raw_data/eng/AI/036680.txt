Deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) achieve high accuracy but are computationally expensive, motivating network pruning to reduce redundancy. This paper investigates efficient pruning methods, addressing the question of whether retraining pruned networks is always necessary. We propose a novel pruning strategy based on layer-wise sensitivity analysis that identifies less important filters for removal. We compare our method against traditional magnitude-based pruning, with and without retraining, across various CNN architectures and datasets. Our results demonstrate that selectively pruning layers based on sensitivity analysis, combined with a novel weight recovery strategy, can achieve comparable accuracy to retrained networks, significantly reducing the computational cost. Specifically, on the ImageNet dataset, our method achieves a 4x reduction in FLOPs with minimal accuracy loss compared to the baseline, without requiring retraining. This work provides insights into the trade-offs between pruning intensity, retraining effort, and model accuracy, contributing to the development of more efficient deep learning deployment strategies. Keywords: CNN pruning, model compression, deep learning, network efficiency, sensitivity analysis, retraining.
