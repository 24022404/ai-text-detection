Stereo depth estimation is a critical problem in computer vision, with applications ranging from autonomous vehicles to augmented reality. This paper presents MTStereo 2.0, an enhanced framework designed to improve the accuracy of stereo depth estimation leveraging the novel integration of Max-trees. The objective of this research is to address the limitations of previous stereo estimation methods, which often struggle with textureless regions and complex surfaces. The proposed approach utilizes Max-trees to effectively model image intensities and structural patterns, enabling more precise depth calculations.

Through comprehensive testing, MTStereo 2.0 demonstrates a significant improvement in accuracy over traditional methods, as well as the original version of MTStereo. Our findings show improved performance particularly in challenging scenarios such as variable lighting conditions and cluttered environments. Additionally, the system maintains competitive computational efficiency, making it suitable for real-time applications. The implications of these advancements extend to enhanced performance in applications like robotics navigation and 3D modeling.

MTStereo 2.0's contribution lies in its innovative use of Max-trees for depth estimation, setting a new benchmark for accuracy without compromising speed. This work paves the way for future research in further refining stereo vision techniques and integrating these methodologies into more complex visual processing systems. Key achievements and technical approaches are discussed in detail, ensuring the system's applicability across a wide array of scenarios. Keywords: stereo depth estimation, MTStereo 2.0, Max-trees, computer vision, image processing, real-time applications.
