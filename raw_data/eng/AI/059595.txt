This paper explores the potential of multitask and transfer learning in autotuning exascale applications, aiming to optimize performance and efficiency in high-performance computing. Our approach leverages machine learning techniques to learn from multiple related tasks and adapt to new, unseen scenarios, reducing the need for extensive manual tuning. We employ a novel framework that integrates multitask learning with transfer learning, enabling the reuse of knowledge across different applications and hardware architectures. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in autotuning efficiency and application performance, outperforming traditional methods. Our research contributes to the development of scalable, adaptive, and efficient exascale computing systems, with potential applications in fields such as climate modeling, materials science, and genomics. Key findings highlight the benefits of multitask and transfer learning in autotuning, including reduced tuning time, improved performance portability, and enhanced scalability. This work advances the state-of-the-art in exascale computing, enabling faster and more efficient execution of complex applications on next-generation hardware architectures, and paving the way for widespread adoption of AI-driven autotuning techniques in high-performance computing.
