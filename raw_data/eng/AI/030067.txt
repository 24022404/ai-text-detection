Single image dehazing is a critical challenge in computer vision, particularly for applications in autonomous driving, surveillance, and remote sensing, where haze significantly degrades image quality. This paper presents a novel classification-driven approach to single image dehazing, aiming to improve visibility and restore details in hazy images. Unlike traditional methods that rely on physical models or handcrafted priors, our approach leverages a deep learning framework to classify haze density levels and adaptively apply dehazing strategies. We propose a dual-branch network architecture that combines a classification module for haze level estimation with a dehazing module for image restoration. The classification module employs a lightweight convolutional neural network (CNN) to categorize haze intensity, while the dehazing module utilizes a transformer-based model to enhance texture and structural details. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art techniques in terms of both quantitative metrics (e.g., PSNR and SSIM) and visual quality. The proposed approach is computationally efficient and robust across diverse haze conditions, making it suitable for real-time applications. This work contributes to the field by introducing a classification-driven paradigm that bridges the gap between haze density estimation and image restoration, offering a scalable solution for practical deployment.  

**Keywords**: Single image dehazing, deep learning, classification-driven approach, transformer-based model, haze density estimation, image restoration.
