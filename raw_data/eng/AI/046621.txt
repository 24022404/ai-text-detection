This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised video decomposition, aiming to separate complex video sequences into interpretable spatio-temporal components without relying on labeled data. We propose a novel framework based on spatio-temporal iterative inference, which leverages iterative refinement to disentangle motion, appearance, and background dynamics in videos. Our approach combines deep generative models with a recurrent architecture to capture temporal dependencies and spatial correlations, enabling the model to learn meaningful representations from raw video data. The proposed method is evaluated on benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance in decomposing videos into coherent components compared to existing unsupervised techniques. Results show significant improvements in reconstruction accuracy and interpretability, with applications in video understanding, motion analysis, and scene segmentation. This work contributes a scalable and efficient solution for unsupervised video decomposition, offering insights into the potential of iterative inference for spatio-temporal data analysis.  

**Keywords**: unsupervised learning, video decomposition, spatio-temporal modeling, iterative inference, deep generative models, motion analysis.
