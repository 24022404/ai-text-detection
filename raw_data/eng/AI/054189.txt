The self-attention mechanism in transformer models has revolutionized natural language processing, but its quadratic computational complexity remains a significant bottleneck. This paper introduces a novel approach to approximate self-attention by leveraging spectral shifting techniques, building upon the foundation of Nyströmformer. Our method, Spectral Shifted Attention (SSA), reduces the computational overhead while maintaining the expressive power of traditional self-attention. By strategically shifting the eigenvalues of the attention matrix, we achieve a more efficient low-rank approximation without sacrificing performance. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that SSA outperforms existing approximation methods, including Nyströmformer, in both computational efficiency and accuracy. The proposed approach achieves up to 30% faster inference times while maintaining competitive results on tasks such as text classification and machine translation. This work contributes a scalable and effective alternative to self-attention, enabling the deployment of transformer models in resource-constrained environments. Key innovations include the integration of spectral analysis into attention mechanisms and a robust theoretical framework for eigenvalue manipulation. The implications of this research extend to real-time applications, edge computing, and large-scale language models, where computational efficiency is critical.  

**Keywords**: self-attention, transformer models, spectral shifting, Nyströmformer, low-rank approximation, computational efficiency, natural language processing.
