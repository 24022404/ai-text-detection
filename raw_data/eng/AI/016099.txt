Deepfakes, AI-synthesized media convincingly depicting individuals performing actions they never did, pose a significant threat to information integrity. This paper addresses the challenging problem of deepfake attribution: identifying the generative model or method used to create a given deepfake. We propose a novel forensic technique leveraging inherent fingerprints left by different deepfake generation pipelines. Our method analyzes subtle, high-frequency artifacts within the generated media, employing a convolutional neural network trained to classify deepfakes based on their generator. Experiments on a diverse dataset of deepfakes generated by various state-of-the-art techniques demonstrate high accuracy in attributing deepfakes to their respective generation methods, outperforming existing forensic approaches. This work contributes a valuable tool for deepfake detection and source identification, aiding in combating disinformation campaigns and protecting against malicious use of AI-generated media. Keywords: Deepfake, Attribution, Forensics, Generative Models, Convolutional Neural Networks, Disinformation.
