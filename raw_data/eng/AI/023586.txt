This paper addresses the challenge of zero-shot learning, where a model is required to recognize objects from unseen classes without prior training examples. Our objective is to develop a novel approach that leverages class descriptions as a regularization technique to improve the model's ability to generalize to new classes. We propose a method that incorporates class descriptions into the learning process, enabling the model to learn a more robust and transferable representation. Our approach utilizes a combination of semantic embeddings and visual features to regularize the model, promoting better alignment between the class descriptions and the visual data. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing zero-shot learning approaches, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of class description regularization in zero-shot learning, leading to improved model generalizability and adaptability. This work contributes to the advancement of zero-shot learning and has potential applications in areas such as image recognition, natural language processing, and multimodal learning, with relevant keywords including zero-shot learning, class description regularization, semantic embeddings, and transfer learning.
