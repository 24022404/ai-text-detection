Meta-transfer learning aims to quickly adapt models to new tasks with limited data by leveraging knowledge from previously learned tasks. This paper investigates the challenge of optimizing meta-learning models for efficient transfer, particularly when encountering hard or dissimilar tasks during the meta-training phase. We propose a novel meta-training strategy that explicitly focuses on hard tasks, adaptively sampling them with higher probability to improve the model's robustness and generalization ability. Our approach incorporates a difficulty-aware sampling mechanism that estimates task difficulty based on the loss gradient magnitude during meta-training. By prioritizing hard tasks, the meta-learner is forced to learn more generalizable representations and develop better adaptation strategies. Experiments on benchmark meta-learning datasets, including image classification and few-shot regression tasks, demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing meta-transfer learning algorithms, especially when the target task distribution differs substantially from the source task distribution. The results highlight the importance of hard task mining in meta-learning and its potential to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of transfer learning in real-world applications. Keywords: Meta-learning, Transfer learning, Hard task mining, Few-shot learning, Generalization.
