This paper presents Embedded-State Latent Conditional Random Fields (ES-LCRF), a novel framework for sequence labeling tasks in natural language processing and related domains. The primary objective is to enhance the modeling of complex, contextual dependencies in sequential data while capturing rich latent structure within label sequences. Our approach extends traditional Conditional Random Fields (CRFs) by introducing an embedded latent state space, allowing the model to learn nuanced sub-label representations and capture higher-level abstractions without significantly increasing computational complexity. We propose an efficient training algorithm leveraging stochastic gradient methods and scalable inference techniques to handle large datasets.

Experimental results demonstrate that ES-LCRF outperforms standard CRFs and existing latent CRF variants across benchmark sequence labeling tasks, including part-of-speech tagging and named entity recognition, achieving substantial improvements in accuracy and generalization. Analysis shows that the embedded latent states enable the model to disambiguate challenging label transitions and adapt to diverse input structures. Our findings highlight the flexibility and robustness of ES-LCRF in learning from varied and noisy data sources.

In conclusion, the Embedded-State Latent Conditional Random Fields model advances sequence labeling by integrating latent state representations and efficient inference, setting a new state-of-the-art on multiple benchmarks. Potential applications extend to information extraction, speech recognition, and bioinformatics, where capturing latent sequence regularities is crucial. Keywords: sequence labeling, Conditional Random Fields, latent states, natural language processing, structured prediction, embedded representations.
