This paper revisits the concept of parameter sharing in multi-agent deep reinforcement learning, aiming to improve the efficiency and scalability of cooperative learning in complex environments. We propose a novel approach that adaptively shares parameters among agents based on their behavioral similarities, leveraging the strengths of both shared and independent learning. Our method utilizes a hierarchical clustering algorithm to group agents with similar policies, enabling the sharing of parameters and reducing the dimensionality of the learning space. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in several multi-agent environments, achieving improved convergence rates and enhanced cooperation among agents. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of adaptive parameter sharing in multi-agent deep reinforcement learning, contributing to the development of more efficient and scalable cooperative learning algorithms. This work has significant implications for applications in robotics, game playing, and autonomous systems, where multi-agent cooperation is crucial. Key keywords: multi-agent deep reinforcement learning, parameter sharing, cooperative learning, adaptive sharing, hierarchical clustering.
