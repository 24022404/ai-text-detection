This paper proposes a novel Multi-Modal Graph Neural Network (MMGNN) designed to facilitate joint reasoning on vision and scene text, enabling more accurate and comprehensive scene understanding. The objective is to develop an approach that effectively integrates visual features from images with textual information extracted from scene text, enhancing the ability to reason about complex scenes. Our method employs a graph-based architecture that models the relationships between visual objects and text, leveraging attention mechanisms to weigh the importance of different modalities. Experimental results demonstrate the superiority of MMGNN over existing state-of-the-art models in several benchmarks, showcasing improved performance in tasks such as visual question answering and scene text-based image retrieval. The key contributions of this research lie in its ability to seamlessly fuse multi-modal information and its potential applications in areas like autonomous driving, smart surveillance, and human-computer interaction. By advancing the field of multi-modal learning, this work paves the way for more sophisticated AI systems capable of interpreting and understanding real-world scenes. Key keywords: Multi-Modal Graph Neural Network, Vision and Scene Text, Joint Reasoning, Graph Neural Networks, Attention Mechanisms, Visual Question Answering.
