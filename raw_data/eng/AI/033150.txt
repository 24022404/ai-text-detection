This paper explores the integration of multiple data fusion techniques, including laser scanning, structured light, and photogrammetry, for the accurate and detailed 3D reconstruction of cultural heritage objects. The objective is to address the challenges of preserving and digitizing fragile artifacts by combining the strengths of these technologies to achieve high-resolution, geometrically precise, and texturally rich digital models. A hybrid approach is proposed, leveraging laser scanning for geometric accuracy, structured light for fine surface details, and photogrammetry for high-quality texture mapping. The methods are evaluated through a series of experiments on real-world cultural heritage objects, comparing the fused results with individual techniques. The findings demonstrate that the proposed fusion approach significantly enhances the quality and completeness of 3D reconstructions, outperforming single-method solutions in terms of detail preservation and computational efficiency. The study concludes that this integrated framework offers a robust solution for cultural heritage digitization, with potential applications in restoration, virtual museums, and educational platforms. The novelty lies in the optimized combination of these techniques, providing a scalable and adaptable workflow for diverse heritage preservation projects. Keywords: data fusion, laser scanning, structured light, photogrammetry, 3D reconstruction, cultural heritage, digitization.
