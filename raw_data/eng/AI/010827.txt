This research addresses the challenge of estimating light direction and color from a single image, a critical task for realistic rendering and computer vision applications. Current methods often require multiple images or extensive pre-calibration, limiting their practicality.

Methods: We propose a novel deep regression framework that leverages convolutional neural networks (CNN) to predict light direction and color directly from a single input image. Our approach employs a carefully designed architecture optimized for feature extraction and regression tasks, with a focus on training efficiency and accuracy. The model is trained on a diverse dataset, ensuring robustness across various lighting conditions and scenes.

Results: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our model outperforms existing approaches in both light direction and color estimation accuracy. The system achieves significant improvements in performance metrics when benchmarked against traditional multi-image methods and recent single-image algorithms. The framework also exhibits impressive generalization to new, unseen environments, emphasizing its applicability to real-world scenarios.

Conclusion: This research contributes a unique method for light estimation that combines the precision of deep learning with the convenience of single-image inputs. The proposed model can be applied to fields such as augmented reality, digital content creation, and advanced robotics, where accurate light estimation enhances environmental interaction and scene realism. Our findings pave the way for further exploration into single-image based lighting analysis, ultimately advancing the capabilities of computer vision technologies.

Keywords: light estimation, single image, deep regression, CNN, computer vision, augmented reality, realism.
