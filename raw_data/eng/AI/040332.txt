Quantization of neural networks into low-bit representations is a critical technique for reducing model size and computational costs, facilitating deployment on resource-constrained devices. However, extreme quantization often leads to substantial accuracy degradation due to information loss and the discrete nature of low-bit weights. This paper introduces a novel training framework, Semi-Relaxed Quantization with DropBits, designed to address these challenges. Our approach integrates a semi-relaxed quantization scheme that allows gradient flow through relaxed weight representations during training, combined with a unique Bit-wise Regularization strategy, termed DropBits. DropBits randomly deactivates select quantization bits during each training iteration, promoting robustness and feature diversity across quantized channels. Extensive experiments on benchmark image classification datasets and various neural network architectures demonstrate that our method achieves superior accuracy compared to existing low-bit quantization techniques, especially at ultra-low (2- and 3-bit) settings. Notably, our framework yields models with improved generalization and stability, with minimal overhead added to the training process. The proposed method bridges the gap between model efficiency and performance, offering significant implications for AI deployment on edge devices and real-time applications. Keywords: neural network quantization, low-bit training, bit-wise regularization, DropBits, edge AI, model compression.
