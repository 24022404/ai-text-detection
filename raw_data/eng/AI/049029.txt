This paper presents DCNAS, a novel approach to neural architecture search (NAS) for semantic image segmentation. The objective is to automate the design of densely connected neural networks that can efficiently segment images into semantically meaningful regions. Our method employs a NAS algorithm to search for optimal architectures, leveraging densely connected blocks to facilitate feature reuse and reduce parameter redundancy. The results show that DCNAS achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets, outperforming manually designed architectures. Key findings include improved segmentation accuracy, reduced computational cost, and enhanced robustness to variations in image quality. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to efficiently search for high-performance architectures, making it a valuable tool for applications such as autonomous driving, medical image analysis, and robotics. By highlighting the benefits of densely connected NAS for semantic image segmentation, this work demonstrates the potential for automated architecture design to drive advancements in computer vision, with relevant keywords including neural architecture search, semantic image segmentation, densely connected networks, and deep learning.
