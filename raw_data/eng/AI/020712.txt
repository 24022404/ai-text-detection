Neural Jump Stochastic Differential Equations (NJSDEs) represent a novel framework that combines the expressive power of neural networks with the stochastic modeling capabilities of jump stochastic differential equations. This paper addresses the challenge of accurately modeling complex dynamical systems that exhibit both continuous evolution and abrupt, discrete transitions, such as those found in finance, biology, and engineering. We propose a new computational architecture that leverages neural networks to parameterize both the drift, diffusion, and jump components of SDEs, enabling data-driven learning of intricate temporal behaviors directly from observed sequences. Our approach utilizes efficient training algorithms based on the adjoint sensitivity method and Monte Carlo sampling to optimize system parameters under real-world noise and jump conditions. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that NJSDEs outperform traditional SDE-based models and state-of-the-art neural ODEs in capturing systems with jump discontinuities, providing significant improvements in predictive accuracy and sample efficiency. The proposed method opens new avenues for robust time series forecasting, anomaly detection, and generative modeling in environments characterized by sudden events. Keywords: Neural SDE, jump processes, deep learning, time series modeling, stochastic dynamics, adjoint method, generative models.
