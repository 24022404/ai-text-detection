This paper addresses the challenge of classifying neural signals with an emphasis on resource efficiency, introducing ResOT, an innovative oblique decision tree algorithm designed to reduce computational overhead while maintaining high classification performance.

Methods/Approach: ResOT leverages a novel integration of oblique decision trees with optimized resource management strategies to enhance the classification of neural signals. The approach utilizes a hybrid combination of geometric margin maximization and data dimension reduction to develop a less resource-intensive model. Additionally, the algorithm incorporates efficient pruning techniques to minimize unnecessary computations, ensuring a scalable and adaptable framework suitable for diverse neural datasets.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that ResOT consistently outperforms traditional decision trees in both accuracy and efficiency. When applied to benchmark neural signal datasets, ResOT shows a marked improvement in classification speed and resource utilization, achieving comparable or superior accuracy. The model's ability to maintain performance across varying signal qualities highlights its robustness and versatility.

Conclusion/Implications: ResOT introduces a significant advancement in the classification of neural signals, offering a resource-efficient solution that is capable of being deployed in real-time applications without sacrificing accuracy. The contributions of this research provide valuable insights into the design of lightweight yet powerful classification systems, with potential applications in brain-computer interfaces, biomedical signal processing, and other fields requiring efficient neural data analysis. By improving the computational efficiency of signal classification, ResOT paves the way for broader accessibility and integration of advanced neural analysis techniques in resource-constrained environments.

Keywords: ResOT, resource-efficient, oblique decision trees, neural signal classification, computational efficiency, brain-computer interfaces.
