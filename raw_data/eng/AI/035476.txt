This paper addresses the challenge of accurately predicting network-wide traffic speed, an essential component for effective traffic management and planning in intelligent transportation systems. Traditional methods often struggle with capturing the complex temporal dependencies and nonlinear patterns inherent in traffic data.

Methods/Approach: To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel approach utilizing both deep bidirectional and unidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Recurrent Neural Networks. The bidirectional LSTM model is designed to capture contextual information by processing data in both forward and backward directions, while the unidirectional LSTM focuses on sequential dependencies in a single temporal direction. This hybrid framework combines the strengths of both strategies to enhance predictive accuracy and adaptability.

Results/Findings: Our experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model significantly outperforms conventional traffic speed prediction methods and previous LSTM-based models in terms of prediction accuracy and robustness. The evaluation metrics indicate substantial improvements across various benchmarks, affirming the model's efficacy in handling complex traffic patterns and achieving real-time prediction capabilities.

Conclusion/Implications: The study introduces a robust machine learning framework for traffic speed prediction that can be applied to diverse urban traffic networks. By leveraging the complementary advantages of bidirectional and unidirectional LSTMs, this approach not only enhances prediction performance but also offers potential applications in optimizing traffic flow, reducing congestion, and augmenting smart city initiatives. Our contribution highlights the importance of advanced deep learning models in transforming traffic management and laying the groundwork for future research in dynamic traffic prediction systems.

Keywords: Traffic speed prediction, LSTM, bidirectional LSTM, recurrent neural network, intelligent transportation systems, deep learning.
