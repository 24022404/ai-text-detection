This paper addresses the challenge of colorizing images with limited data, a problem that has garnered significant attention in the field of computer vision. Our objective is to develop a novel approach that enables effective colorization using few-shot learning, where only a small number of labeled examples are available. To achieve this, we propose a memory-augmented network that leverages external memory modules to store and retrieve relevant color information from a limited set of reference images. Our approach combines the strengths of few-shot learning and memory-augmented neural networks to produce high-quality colorizations. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing state-of-the-art colorization techniques, particularly in scenarios where data is scarce. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of leveraging external memory and few-shot learning in colorization tasks, enabling potential applications in image and video editing, as well as computer vision. Our contributions include the development of a novel memory-augmented network architecture and a new few-shot colorization framework, which can be applied to various image and video processing tasks, including image editing, restoration, and enhancement, with relevant keywords including few-shot learning, colorization, memory-augmented networks, and computer vision.
