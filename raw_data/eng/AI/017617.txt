Video summarization is a critical task in multimedia analysis, aiming to condense lengthy videos into concise, informative summaries. This paper addresses the challenge of effectively capturing and prioritizing multi-concept attention within videos to enhance summarization quality. We propose a novel framework that leverages advanced attention mechanisms to identify and weigh key concepts, such as objects, actions, and scenes, dynamically across video frames. Our approach integrates a multi-concept attention model with a deep learning-based summarization pipeline, enabling the system to focus on the most salient elements while maintaining temporal coherence. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques in terms of summary accuracy, relevance, and user satisfaction. The results highlight the effectiveness of multi-concept attention in improving video summarization, offering a robust solution for applications in video indexing, surveillance, and content recommendation. This research contributes to the field by introducing a scalable and adaptable framework that bridges the gap between attention modeling and summarization tasks, paving the way for future advancements in multimedia analysis.  

**Keywords**: video summarization, multi-concept attention, deep learning, multimedia analysis, attention mechanisms.
