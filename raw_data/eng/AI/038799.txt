Fine-grained classification, which distinguishes among visually similar categories, poses significant challenges due to the scarcity of labeled data and the high cost of annotation. Semi-supervised learning (SSL) approaches have emerged as promising solutions by leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data to enhance classification performance. This paper presents a realistic evaluation of state-of-the-art semi-supervised learning methods for fine-grained classification tasks. We systematically benchmark a range of SSL algorithms, including consistency regularization and pseudo-labeling techniques, across multiple fine-grained datasets. Our evaluation framework considers practical aspects such as limited labeled samples, class imbalance, and real-world noise in unlabeled data. Experimental results reveal that while SSL methods substantially improve accuracy compared to fully supervised baselines, their performance is sensitive to dataset characteristics and annotation quality. Furthermore, we identify critical failure cases and analyze the impact of data augmentation and model selection on SSL effectiveness. The study provides actionable insights into the strengths and limitations of current SSL techniques for fine-grained problems and offers guidelines for practitioners in selecting and deploying these methods. Our findings highlight avenues for future research aimed at developing more robust and adaptive SSL approaches for fine-grained classification.

Keywords: fine-grained classification, semi-supervised learning, consistency regularization, pseudo-labeling, dataset evaluation, limited annotations, machine learning.
