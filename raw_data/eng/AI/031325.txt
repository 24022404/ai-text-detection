Coral reef ecosystems are under increasing threat from climate change and anthropogenic activities. Accurate and efficient coral classification is crucial for monitoring reef health and guiding conservation efforts. This paper introduces a novel approach for sparse coral classification using deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs). We address the challenge of limited labeled coral imagery by employing a combination of transfer learning and data augmentation techniques. Specifically, we fine-tune a pre-trained ResNet architecture on a sparsely labeled dataset of coral images, incorporating techniques such as random rotations, flips, and color jittering to enhance model generalization. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves competitive classification accuracy compared to models trained on denser datasets, while significantly reducing the annotation effort. Furthermore, we investigate the impact of different sparsity levels on model performance, providing insights into the trade-off between annotation cost and classification accuracy. Our findings suggest that effective sparse coral classification is feasible with deep CNNs, offering a promising tool for large-scale reef monitoring and conservation. Keywords: Coral Classification, Deep Learning, Convolutional Neural Networks, Sparse Data, Transfer Learning, Reef Monitoring.
