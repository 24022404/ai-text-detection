Dynamic graphs, which evolve over time through changes in both attributes and structure, are fundamental to modeling real-world systems such as social networks, recommendation systems, and biological networks. This paper addresses the challenge of learning attribute-structure co-evolutions in dynamic graphs, where the interplay between node attributes and graph topology must be captured effectively. We propose a novel framework that integrates temporal graph neural networks (TGNNs) with a co-evolution mechanism to simultaneously model attribute updates and structural changes. Our approach leverages attention mechanisms to prioritize influential interactions and employs a dual-stream architecture to separately process attribute and structural dynamics while ensuring their mutual influence is preserved. Experiments on real-world datasets demonstrate that our model outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in tasks such as link prediction, node classification, and graph reconstruction. The results highlight the importance of jointly modeling attribute and structural dynamics for accurate predictions in dynamic graph settings. This work contributes a scalable and interpretable solution for dynamic graph analysis, with potential applications in recommendation systems, anomaly detection, and network evolution studies.  

**Keywords**: dynamic graphs, graph neural networks, co-evolution, temporal modeling, attention mechanisms, link prediction.
