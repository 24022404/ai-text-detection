This paper presents a novel approach to tracking road users using constraint programming, addressing the complex problem of accurately identifying and predicting the movements of pedestrians, vehicles, and cyclists in dynamic traffic environments. Our method leverages constraint programming techniques to model the interactions between road users and the physical environment, incorporating constraints such as traffic rules, road geometry, and user behavior. The approach is evaluated using real-world data, demonstrating improved tracking accuracy and efficiency compared to existing methods. Key findings include the ability to handle occlusions, reduce false positives, and provide real-time predictions of road user trajectories. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to integrate constraint programming with computer vision and machine learning techniques, enabling more accurate and robust tracking of road users. This work has significant implications for intelligent transportation systems, autonomous vehicles, and smart city infrastructure, with potential applications in traffic management, safety analysis, and urban planning. Keywords: constraint programming, road user tracking, computer vision, machine learning, intelligent transportation systems.
