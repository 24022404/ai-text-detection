This paper introduces a novel unsupervised approach for decomposing video sequences into meaningful layers representing distinct foreground and background elements. Our method leverages a spatio-temporal iterative inference framework to jointly estimate layer segmentation, motion, and appearance without requiring any prior training data or manual annotation. Specifically, we formulate a probabilistic model that encourages temporal consistency and spatial smoothness in layer assignments. An iterative expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm is then employed to infer the parameters of the model, effectively separating the video into coherent, moving objects and a static background. Experiments on various video datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive performance compared to existing unsupervised video decomposition techniques, while requiring significantly less computational resources. The resulting decomposition enables applications such as video editing, object tracking, and anomaly detection. Keywords: Video Decomposition, Unsupervised Learning, Spatio-Temporal Inference, Layer Segmentation, EM Algorithm.
