This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing multi-agent systems using game-theoretic approaches, focusing on the development of a gradient-based method leveraging the Nikaido-Isoda function. The objective is to provide an efficient and scalable solution for computing Nash equilibria in non-cooperative games, which are critical in applications such as resource allocation, autonomous systems, and adversarial learning. We propose a novel algorithm that integrates gradient descent techniques with the Nikaido-Isoda function to iteratively approximate equilibrium points, ensuring convergence under mild assumptions. Our approach is computationally efficient, avoiding the need for exhaustive search or complex heuristics. Experimental results demonstrate the algorithm's superior performance in terms of convergence speed and accuracy compared to existing methods, particularly in high-dimensional and large-scale scenarios. The proposed method also exhibits robustness to varying game structures and initial conditions. This work contributes to the field of game theory by providing a practical and theoretically grounded tool for equilibrium computation, with potential applications in AI, economics, and multi-agent systems. Key innovations include the integration of gradient-based optimization with the Nikaido-Isoda framework and the demonstration of its scalability. Keywords: game theory, Nash equilibrium, Nikaido-Isoda function, gradient-based optimization, multi-agent systems.
