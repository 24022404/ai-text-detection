This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the performance of k-means clustering, a widely used algorithm in data analysis and machine learning, by integrating disentangled internal representations. The goal is to improve clustering accuracy and efficiency, particularly in complex, high-dimensional data sets where traditional methods often struggle.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel approach that incorporates disentangled features obtained through an advanced machine learning model. This model learns to extract and represent data features in a more structured form, allowing k-means to operate on a set of simplified and highly informative variables. The method is designed to decouple correlated features, yielding a more interpretable and robust clustering.

Results/Findings: Empirical evaluations conducted on several benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in clustering performance, evidenced by higher accuracy and reduced computation time compared to standard k-means. Additionally, our approach showcases superior capability in managing high-dimensional data, revealing clearer cluster formations and higher silhouette scores.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of disentangled internal representations significantly boosts the efficacy of k-means clustering, contributing to both the fields of clustering technology and unsupervised learning. This advancement not only facilitates better analysis of complex datasets but also opens avenues for improved performance in various applications, from image segmentation to customer segmentation in business. Our findings suggest the potential for further exploration into combining classic algorithms with modern representational learning techniques.

Keywords: k-means clustering, disentangled representations, performance improvement, high-dimensional data, unsupervised learning, machine learning models.
