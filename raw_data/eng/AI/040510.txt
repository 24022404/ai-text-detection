This paper addresses the challenge of detecting fake images by introducing imperceptible adversarial examples as a novel approach to improve the robustness of fake image detection models. Our objective is to develop a method that can effectively identify manipulated images while minimizing the impact on legitimate images. We propose a new algorithm that utilizes generative adversarial networks to create imperceptible perturbations in images, making them indistinguishable from authentic ones. Our approach involves training a deep neural network to detect these subtle perturbations, thereby enhancing the model's ability to distinguish between real and fake images. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing state-of-the-art fake image detection models, achieving a significant reduction in false positives and false negatives. The key contributions of this research include the development of a robust fake image detection model, the introduction of imperceptible adversarial examples, and the potential applications in digital forensics and image authentication. Our study highlights the importance of considering adversarial attacks in the development of fake image detection systems, and our approach has significant implications for improving the security and reliability of image-based applications, including those using computer vision, machine learning, and artificial intelligence. Key keywords: fake image detection, adversarial examples, generative adversarial networks, deep learning, computer vision, digital forensics.
