Approximate Bayesian Computation (ABC) is an essential framework for performing Bayesian inference in scenarios where likelihood functions are intractable or computationally prohibitive. Traditional ABC methods often rely on handcrafted summary statistics and manual tuning, which can limit efficiency and accuracy. This paper introduces a novel approach, termed Multi-Statistic Approximate Bayesian Computation with Multi-Armed Bandits (MS-ABC-MAB), which leverages the adaptive exploration capabilities of multi-armed bandit algorithms to dynamically select and weight multiple candidate summary statistics during inference. By casting the selection of informative statistics as a sequential decision-making problem, the proposed method balances exploration and exploitation, optimizing the choice of statistics that best approximate the underlying posterior distribution. Extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that MS-ABC-MAB significantly improves posterior estimation accuracy and computational efficiency compared to standard ABC techniques and static statistic selection strategies. The findings reveal that the multi-armed bandit integration not only accelerates convergence but also enhances robustness to irrelevant or noisy summary statistics. The proposed framework offers a scalable and data-driven solution for challenging likelihood-free inference tasks, with potential applications in computational biology, epidemiology, and complex systems modeling. Key contributions include the formulation of a bandit-based statistic selection mechanism within ABC, empirical validation across diverse benchmarks, and analysis of method robustness. Keywords: Approximate Bayesian Computation, Multi-Armed Bandits, Bayesian Inference, Summary Statistics, Likelihood-Free Inference.
