This paper presents a novel non-alternating graph hashing algorithm specifically designed to enhance large scale image search. The primary objective is to address the limitations of conventional hashing techniques, which often rely on alternating optimization strategies that can hinder scalability and affect retrieval accuracy when dealing with vast image datasets. Our proposed approach utilizes a unified, non-alternating optimization framework that directly integrates graph structure preservation and hash code learning. By leveraging intrinsic relationships between image features through an affinity graph, the algorithm efficiently generates compact and discriminative binary codes without the iterative back-and-forth updates characteristic of traditional methods. Experimental evaluations on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed algorithm not only accelerates the training process but also achieves superior retrieval performance in terms of precision and recall compared to state-of-the-art hashing techniques. Moreover, the method exhibits robust scalability, maintaining high efficiency and accuracy as the database size increases. The results highlight the potential of non-alternating optimization in large scale visual information retrieval and offer new insights for future research in efficient graph-based hashing. Keywords: image search, graph hashing, large scale retrieval, non-alternating optimization, image retrieval, scalability.
