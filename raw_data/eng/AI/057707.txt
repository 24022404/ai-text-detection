The objective of this research is to address the issue of conservativeness in offline reinforcement learning (RL), which often results from overly cautious policy estimations that limit policy improvement. To tackle this problem, we propose a novel framework that effectively balances the trade-off between exploration and exploitation without compromising reliability. Our approach incorporates a dynamic boundary adjustment mechanism that adapts exploration based on the context of the learned policy, thereby reducing unnecessary conservativeness. We implement this approach using a scalable algorithm integrated with a reward-shaping technique, which encourages more efficient use of the existing dataset. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvement over traditional offline RL methods, showcasing enhanced policy performance and greater adaptability across various benchmark environments. Key findings indicate that our method substantially decreases policy restriction while ensuring robust performance, as evidenced by superior reward acquisition in comparative evaluations. The study offers valuable insights into optimizing offline RL processes and suggests potential applications in autonomous systems and real-world decision-making scenarios where conservative estimates can hinder progress. This work contributes to the field by introducing a methodology that not only mitigates excessive conservativeness but also enhances the effectiveness of offline RL frameworks. Keywords: Offline Reinforcement Learning, Policy Conservativeness, Reward Shaping, Dynamic Exploration, Autonomous Systems.
