This paper explores the challenges of applying deep reinforcement learning (DRL) in dynamic dispatching systems. The objective is to investigate the limitations and difficulties of utilizing DRL algorithms in real-time dispatching environments, where rapid decision-making and adaptability are crucial. We employ a model-free DRL approach, leveraging techniques such as deep Q-networks and policy gradients, to optimize dispatching policies in dynamic scenarios. Our results show that while DRL can learn effective dispatching strategies, it struggles with issues like partial observability, high-dimensional state spaces, and changing environmental conditions. Key findings include the importance of careful state representation, reward function design, and exploration-exploitation trade-offs in DRL-based dispatching systems. The conclusion highlights the need for further research into developing more robust and adaptable DRL methods for dynamic dispatching, with potential applications in fields like logistics, transportation, and manufacturing. Keywords: deep reinforcement learning, dynamic dispatching, model-free learning, real-time decision-making, adaptive control.
