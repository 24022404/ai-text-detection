The study of scaling laws in machine learning has provided critical insights into the relationship between model size, dataset size, and performance. However, the behavior of these laws in the context of transfer learning remains underexplored. This paper investigates the scaling laws for transfer learning, focusing on how pre-trained models adapt to downstream tasks with varying dataset sizes and task complexities. We propose a systematic framework to analyze the transferability of pre-trained models across diverse domains, leveraging large-scale experiments with varying architectures, dataset scales, and fine-tuning strategies. Our findings reveal that the performance of transferred models follows predictable scaling patterns, which are influenced by the similarity between the source and target tasks, as well as the size of the downstream dataset. We identify key thresholds where transfer learning outperforms training from scratch and provide guidelines for optimizing model selection and fine-tuning strategies. The results demonstrate that transfer learning can significantly reduce computational costs while maintaining competitive performance, particularly in low-data regimes. This work contributes to a deeper understanding of transfer learning dynamics and offers practical insights for deploying pre-trained models in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: transfer learning, scaling laws, pre-trained models, fine-tuning, machine learning, computational efficiency.
