Nighttime satellite imagery is critical for applications such as urban planning, disaster response, and environmental monitoring. However, generating high-quality nighttime imagery from traditional satellite observations remains challenging due to limited visible light data. This paper introduces *NightVision*, a novel framework for generating realistic nighttime satellite imagery using infra-red (IR) observations. The proposed approach leverages advanced deep learning techniques, specifically a conditional generative adversarial network (cGAN), to transform IR data into visually interpretable nighttime images. The model is trained on a curated dataset of paired IR and visible-light nighttime images, enabling it to learn the complex relationships between thermal signatures and visual features. Experimental results demonstrate that *NightVision* outperforms existing methods in terms of image quality, spatial resolution, and perceptual realism. The generated imagery achieves a structural similarity index (SSIM) of 0.92 and a peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) of 28.5 dB, significantly improving upon baseline approaches. Furthermore, the framework is computationally efficient, making it suitable for real-time applications. This research contributes to the field of remote sensing by providing a robust tool for nighttime image generation, with potential applications in urban development, disaster management, and climate studies.  

**Keywords**: nighttime satellite imagery, infra-red observations, deep learning, generative adversarial networks, remote sensing, image synthesis.
