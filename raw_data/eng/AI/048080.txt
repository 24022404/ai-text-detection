This paper presents Adversarial Domain Randomization, a novel approach to improve the generalizability and robustness of deep learning models across diverse environments. The objective is to address the challenge of domain shift in real-world applications, where models often struggle to perform well on unseen data distributions. Our method leverages adversarial training to randomize the input data, effectively creating a more robust and domain-agnostic representation. We employ a generative model to produce randomized samples, which are then used to train a discriminator to distinguish between real and randomized data. The results show significant improvements in model performance on unseen domains, outperforming state-of-the-art domain adaptation methods. Our approach has important implications for real-world applications, such as robotics, autonomous driving, and medical imaging, where domain shift is a major concern. Key contributions include the introduction of adversarial domain randomization, which enables more effective domain adaptation and improved model generalizability. Relevant keywords: domain adaptation, adversarial training, deep learning, robustness, generalizability.
