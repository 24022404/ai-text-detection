This paper presents a novel approach to enhancing neural predictors by introducing Homogeneous Architecture Augmentation (HAA), a methodology aimed at improving prediction accuracy and efficiency in neural network-based systems. The primary objective of this research is to address the limitations of heterogeneous architectural designs, which often lead to suboptimal generalization and increased complexity in neural predictors. Our approach systematically augments standard neural network architectures with homogeneous structural elements, facilitating better feature representation and more consistent learning dynamics across tasks. The methodology involves integrating uniform layer configurations and standardized activation functions within existing neural prediction frameworks, thereby reducing irregularity in network behavior. Experimental evaluation was conducted on benchmark datasets in time series forecasting and classification, revealing that architectures enhanced with HAA consistently outperform traditional heterogeneous designs in terms of accuracy, convergence speed, and model robustness. Notably, our results demonstrate up to a 12% improvement in prediction accuracy and a reduction in training time without increasing model size. The findings suggest that homogeneous augmentation offers a scalable and easily implementable strategy for optimizing neural predictors. This work contributes a generalizable framework that can be applied across various machine learning tasks, with potential implications for fields such as deep learning, neural architecture search, and automated model optimization. Keywords: neural predictor, homogeneous architecture, deep learning, architecture augmentation, model optimization, machine learning.
