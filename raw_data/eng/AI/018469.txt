Predicting future events based on observed sequences is a critical task in many domains, from healthcare to finance. This paper introduces a novel approach to learn latent event-predictive encodings by incorporating counterfactual regularization. Our method leverages a recurrent neural network (RNN) architecture to encode event sequences into a latent space, subsequently utilizing these encodings for event prediction. To enhance the robustness and causal validity of these encodings, we introduce counterfactual regularization. This involves generating counterfactual examples by intervening on past events and training the model to predict consistent outcomes under these interventions. This encourages the model to learn representations that are invariant to irrelevant variations in the input. We evaluated our method on several real-world datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in prediction accuracy compared to state-of-the-art baselines. Specifically, our approach exhibits enhanced performance in scenarios involving noisy or incomplete event sequences. The findings suggest that counterfactual regularization can effectively improve the quality and reliability of latent event encodings, leading to more accurate and robust event prediction models. This has significant implications for decision-making in dynamic and uncertain environments.

**Keywords:** Event prediction, latent encoding, counterfactual regularization, recurrent neural networks, causal inference, machine learning.
