Airline crew pairing optimization is a critical and highly complex task in airline operations, involving the selection of feasible and cost-effective crew schedules that comply with regulatory, operational, and contractual constraints. Traditional optimization methods often struggle with the scale and combinatorial nature of real-world instances, leading to suboptimal solutions or prohibitive computation times. This paper presents a novel machine learning-based approach to augment large-scale airline crew pairing optimization by learning combinatorial patterns from historical data. Our method leverages graph neural networks (GNNs) to capture intricate relationships among flight legs and crew assignments, enabling the identification of promising substructures and variable reductions prior to optimization. We integrate the learned patterns into a branch-and-price framework, significantly reducing the solution space and improving computational efficiency. Experimental evaluations on industry-scale datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves up to 35% faster convergence and produces pairings with lower operational costs compared to state-of-the-art optimization techniques. The proposed framework showcases significant potential in real-world airline scheduling, offering robust scalability and adaptability to evolving operational requirements. Keywords: airline crew pairing, combinatorial optimization, machine learning, graph neural networks, branch-and-price, operational research.
