This paper re-examines the relationship between the softmax function and cross-entropy loss in neural network classifiers, proposing a novel perspective that frames the classifier as a mutual information estimator. The objective is to explore how the softmax-cross-entropy combination inherently captures the mutual information between input data and class labels, offering a deeper understanding of its effectiveness in classification tasks. We present a theoretical framework that connects the optimization of cross-entropy loss to the maximization of mutual information, providing insights into the underlying principles of neural network training. To validate this perspective, we conduct experiments on benchmark datasets, comparing the performance of our mutual information-based interpretation with traditional approaches. Results demonstrate that this reinterpretation not only aligns with empirical observations but also offers a principled foundation for improving classifier design. The findings suggest that mutual information maximization can serve as a guiding principle for developing more robust and interpretable neural network models. This work contributes to the broader understanding of deep learning by bridging information theory and neural network optimization, with potential applications in enhancing model generalization and interpretability.  

**Keywords**: Softmax, Cross-Entropy, Mutual Information, Neural Networks, Classification, Information Theory, Deep Learning.
