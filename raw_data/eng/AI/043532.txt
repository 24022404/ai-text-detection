**Abstract:**

Continual semantic segmentation aims to enable models to incrementally learn new visual classes without forgetting previously acquired knowledge, a challenge critical for real-world deployments such as autonomous driving and medical imaging. However, most existing methods rely on fully supervised adaptation with labeled data from new domains or tasks, limiting scalability and practical applicability. This paper introduces an unsupervised model adaptation framework for continual semantic segmentation, which effectively adapts segmentation models to evolving data distributions without requiring annotations for new tasks. Our approach employs self-supervised learning objectives and dynamic memory mechanisms to preserve representations of previously learned classes while integrating new visual concepts from unlabeled data. To further enhance adaptation, we introduce a domain-agnostic regularization technique that reduces catastrophic forgetting and facilitates knowledge transfer across domains. Experimental evaluation on multiple continual segmentation benchmarks demonstrates that our method outperforms existing approaches in terms of segmentation accuracy, adaptability, and retention of prior knowledge, without access to target domain labels. The proposed framework offers a scalable and robust solution for real-world applications where continuous learning and unsupervised adaptation are essential. Keywords: continual learning, semantic segmentation, unsupervised adaptation, catastrophic forgetting, self-supervised learning.
