Abstract:

Understanding the decision-making processes of deep learning models is crucial when applying them to multiscale systems, where intricate dependencies exist across various spatial or temporal scales. This paper investigates the application of mutual information as a quantitative tool for enhancing explainability in deep neural networks operating on multiscale data. We propose a novel framework that leverages mutual information to identify and interpret salient features and interactions at different scales within complex systems. Our method integrates mutual information estimation with feature attribution techniques to uncover how deep learning models aggregate and transmit information across layers and scales. Through extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world multiscale datasets, including climate modeling and complex network analysis, our approach reveals interpretable patterns and influential variables that drive model predictions. Comparative analysis demonstrates superior interpretability and competitive predictive accuracy compared to existing explainable AI methods. The results highlight the potential of mutual information-based explanations to foster transparency, trust, and actionable insight in the deployment of deep learning for multiscale system analysis. This research paves the way for more informed model diagnostics and improved stewardship of AI in scientific and engineering domains. Key keywords include mutual information, explainable AI (XAI), deep learning, multiscale systems, feature attribution, and model interpretability.
