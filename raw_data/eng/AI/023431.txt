Meta-learning aims to develop models capable of rapidly adapting to new tasks with limited data. However, current meta-learning algorithms often suffer from performance degradation when applied to target domains significantly different from the training environment. This paper addresses the challenge of learning transferable representations in meta-learning, specifically focusing on scenarios where only a single target domain is available for adaptation. We propose a novel meta-learning framework that leverages domain-invariant feature extraction and domain-specific fine-tuning to achieve robust transferability. Our approach employs a multi-task learning objective during meta-training, encouraging the model to learn representations that are both generalizable and adaptable. Experiments on several benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing meta-learning algorithms in few-shot learning scenarios with a single target domain. The results highlight the effectiveness of our approach in learning transferable representations, enabling rapid adaptation and improved performance in new environments with limited target-specific data. Keywords: Meta-learning, Transfer Learning, Domain Adaptation, Few-shot Learning, Representation Learning.
