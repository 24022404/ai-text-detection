This paper addresses the intricate relationship between explicit and implicit feature maps within graph kernels, a crucial tool in the analysis and comparison of graph-structured data. The research aims to provide a unified view that clarifies how these maps contribute to the effectiveness of graph kernels in machine learning applications.

Methods/Approach: We propose a comprehensive framework that integrates the explicit feature maps, typically represented by kernel matrices, and the implicit feature maps, leveraged through indirect computations in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS). By examining various graph kernels, such as the Weisfeiler-Lehman and shortest-path kernels, we analyze their feature map structures using both algebraic and statistical perspectives.

Results/Findings: Our findings reveal that explicit and implicit feature maps, despite their different computational approaches, can be represented under a common theoretical framework. We demonstrate that this unified view enhances the interpretability of graph kernel methods, allows for improved kernel selection processes, and facilitates the development of new kernels by blending implicit and explicit features. In empirical evaluations across multiple datasets, our framework shows significant improvements in classification accuracy and computational efficiency compared to traditional methods.

Conclusion/Implications: The research provides important insights into the design and application of graph kernels by bridging the gap between explicit and implicit feature maps. This unified approach not only advances the theoretical understanding of graph kernels but also opens new avenues for their application in areas such as bioinformatics, social network analysis, and natural language processing. The enhanced interpretability and performance promise substantial contributions to the future development of graph-based machine learning models.

Keywords: graph kernels, explicit feature maps, implicit feature maps, machine learning, graph-structured data, unified framework, RKHS.
