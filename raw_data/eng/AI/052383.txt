This paper presents a novel hybrid modeling approach for simulating the human cardiovascular system by integrating Neural Networks (NNs) with Functional Mock-up Units (FMUs), termed NeuralFMUs. The objective of this research is to address the limitations of traditional cardiovascular models, which often struggle to balance computational efficiency with physiological accuracy. By combining the interpretability of physics-based FMUs with the flexibility of data-driven NNs, we propose a framework that leverages the strengths of both paradigms. The methodology involves training NNs to approximate complex, non-linear relationships within the cardiovascular system while maintaining compatibility with FMU-based simulations. Experimental results demonstrate that the NeuralFMU approach achieves superior accuracy in predicting key hemodynamic parameters, such as blood pressure and flow rates, compared to standalone FMU or NN models. Additionally, the hybrid model exhibits improved computational performance, making it suitable for real-time applications such as medical diagnostics and personalized treatment planning. The findings highlight the potential of NeuralFMUs to advance cardiovascular modeling by enabling more accurate and efficient simulations. This research contributes to the growing field of hybrid modeling in biomedical engineering, offering a scalable and adaptable solution for complex physiological systems.  

**Keywords**: Hybrid modeling, NeuralFMU, cardiovascular system, Functional Mock-up Units, neural networks, hemodynamics, biomedical simulation.
