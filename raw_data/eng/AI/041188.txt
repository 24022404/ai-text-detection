This paper reexamines the application of kernel methods for node representation learning on graphs, aiming to improve the accuracy and efficiency of existing graph neural networks. Our approach introduces a novel kernel-based framework that leverages the structural information of graphs to learn informative node representations. By combining traditional kernel methods with modern graph learning techniques, we develop a new model that outperforms state-of-the-art methods in various graph-based tasks. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, showing significant improvements in node classification, graph clustering, and link prediction. The key contributions of this research include the proposal of a kernel-based graph learning framework, the development of a scalable and efficient algorithm for large-scale graphs, and the demonstration of the superiority of our method over existing graph neural networks. Our work has important implications for graph-based applications, such as social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics, and highlights the potential of kernel methods in node representation learning on graphs. Key keywords: graph neural networks, kernel methods, node representation learning, graph learning, node classification, graph clustering, link prediction.
