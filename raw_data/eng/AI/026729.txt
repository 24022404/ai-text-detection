Data augmentation is a critical technique in improving the generalization and robustness of machine learning models, particularly in computer vision tasks. This paper introduces **GridMask**, a novel and effective data augmentation method designed to enhance model performance by strategically masking regions of input images during training. Unlike traditional augmentation techniques such as random cropping or flipping, GridMask systematically removes rectangular grid-like regions from images, forcing the model to focus on diverse and less prominent features. This approach addresses the limitations of existing methods, which often either under-augment or excessively distort the data.  

The proposed method is evaluated on several benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10, ImageNet, and COCO, across various tasks such as image classification and object detection. Experimental results demonstrate that GridMask consistently outperforms baseline augmentation techniques, achieving higher accuracy and improved generalization. For instance, on ImageNet, GridMask yields a 1.2% improvement in top-1 accuracy compared to standard augmentation methods. Additionally, the method is computationally efficient and easy to integrate into existing training pipelines.  

The key contribution of this work lies in its simplicity and effectiveness, offering a new perspective on how structured masking can enhance data diversity without compromising the integrity of the original data. GridMask has broad applicability in domains requiring robust visual recognition systems, such as autonomous driving, medical imaging, and surveillance. This research underscores the importance of innovative augmentation strategies in advancing the state-of-the-art in computer vision.  

**Keywords**: Data augmentation, GridMask, computer vision, image classification, object detection, machine learning.
