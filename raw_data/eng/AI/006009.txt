This paper explores the composition properties of inferential privacy for time-series data, addressing the critical challenge of protecting sensitive information in sequential data releases. Our objective is to develop a framework that ensures the privacy of time-series data while maintaining its utility for analysis and modeling. We propose a novel approach based on differential privacy, incorporating a combination of data perturbation and aggregation techniques to prevent inference attacks. Our method utilizes a hierarchical clustering algorithm to group similar data points, reducing the risk of privacy breaches. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in preserving privacy while maintaining acceptable levels of data accuracy. Key findings include significant reductions in inference attack success rates and minimal impact on data utility for common time-series analysis tasks. Our research contributes to the development of robust privacy-preserving methods for time-series data, with potential applications in fields such as finance, healthcare, and smart cities. Relevant keywords: inferential privacy, time-series data, differential privacy, data perturbation, hierarchical clustering, privacy-preserving methods.
