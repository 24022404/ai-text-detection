This research addresses the challenge of improving convergence speed and learning efficiency in Temporal Difference (TD) learning, a fundamental method in reinforcement learning. Traditional TD methods often suffer from slow convergence and high computational costs in complex environments.

Methods/Approach: We introduce "Source Traces," an innovative mechanism designed to augment the standard TD learning process. Source Traces leverage past learning experiences to enhance current learning updates by incorporating additional historical context, allowing the algorithm to dynamically adjust the significance of past rewards and transitions. The approach is validated through integration with existing TD learning algorithms and tested in diverse simulation environments to evaluate its impact on performance.

Results/Findings: The integration of Source Traces into TD learning resulted in significant improvements in convergence speed and overall learning efficiency across various test environments. Comparative analysis demonstrated that Source Traces-enhanced algorithms consistently outperformed traditional TD methods, achieving faster learning rates and higher accuracy in policy determination. Additionally, the method maintained robustness under different parameter settings, highlighting its adaptability and wide applicability.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed Source Traces mechanism provides a novel enhancement to Temporal Difference learning, offering a viable solution for reducing learning time and increasing efficiency in reinforcement learning tasks. This advancement holds considerable potential for applications in fields requiring adaptive control systems, such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and real-time decision-making processes. By outperforming traditional approaches, Source Traces contribute substantially to the body of knowledge in reinforcement learning, paving the way for more efficient and intelligent learning systems.

Keywords: Temporal Difference Learning, Reinforcement Learning, Convergence Speed, Source Traces, Learning Efficiency, Adaptive Systems.
