Unsupervised skeleton-based action recognition remains a challenging problem due to the lack of labeled data and inherent complexities in human motion. This paper introduces a novel approach, Prototypical Contrast and Reverse Prediction (PCRP), to address this challenge. PCRP leverages a contrastive learning framework to learn discriminative action prototypes from unlabeled skeleton sequences. Specifically, we minimize the distance between each skeleton sequence and its corresponding prototype while maximizing the distance between different prototypes. Furthermore, we introduce a reverse prediction module that learns to predict previous skeleton frames from future frames, encouraging the model to capture temporal dependencies and motion dynamics. Our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets for unsupervised skeleton-based action recognition. The results demonstrate that PCRP effectively learns robust action representations without requiring any manual annotations. The learned prototypes provide meaningful insights into action characteristics and enable effective action classification. Keywords: Action Recognition, Unsupervised Learning, Skeleton Data, Contrastive Learning, Temporal Modeling.
