Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) is a critical task in robotics and artificial intelligence, requiring agents to interpret natural language instructions and navigate through complex environments. However, existing methods often struggle with generalization due to limited environmental diversity in training datasets. This paper introduces a novel approach, *Random Environmental Mixup* (REM), to enhance the robustness and adaptability of VLN agents. REM dynamically generates diverse training scenarios by combining elements from multiple environments, thereby simulating a broader range of real-world conditions. We evaluate our approach on benchmark datasets, including R2R and REVERIE, using state-of-the-art transformer-based models. Experimental results demonstrate that REM significantly improves navigation success rates and reduces errors, particularly in unseen environments. Our findings highlight the importance of environmental diversity in training and provide a scalable solution for improving VLN systems. This work contributes to advancing the field of embodied AI by offering a practical method to enhance generalization and performance in vision-language navigation tasks.  

**Keywords**: Vision-Language Navigation, Environmental Diversity, Random Environmental Mixup, Transformer Models, Embodied AI, Generalization.
