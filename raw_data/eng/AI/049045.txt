In multi-agent systems and game theory, achieving no-regret learning is a critical challenge. This paper addresses this problem by introducing R2-B2, a novel Recursive Reasoning-Based Bayesian Optimization method, designed to enhance decision-making in strategic game scenarios. 

Methods/Approach: R2-B2 integrates recursive reasoning techniques with Bayesian optimization to enable agents to predict and adapt to the behaviors of other players dynamically. By leveraging recursive reasoning, the approach models the potential strategies of opponents at varying levels of complexity, providing a robust framework for optimizing decision-making processes in games. The use of Bayesian optimization facilitates efficient exploration and exploitation, ensuring adaptive learning without extensive computational overhead.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that R2-B2 consistently achieves superior performance in terms of cumulative regret compared to existing learning algorithms in strategic games. The method is tested across a variety of game settings, showing significant improvements in achieving near-optimal strategies with minimal learning iterations. The recursive component of the algorithm effectively anticipates opponents' strategies, leading to more informed and successful decision-making outcomes.

Conclusion/Implications: The R2-B2 method represents a significant advancement in the field of game theory and multi-agent systems by addressing the no-regret learning challenge with an innovative approach. The ability to recursively reason about opponents' behaviors opens new pathways for applications in automated negotiations, multi-agent collaborations, and real-time gaming environments. The findings suggest that R2-B2 can be scaled and applied to complex strategic situations, enhancing the decision-making capabilities of autonomous agents in competitive and cooperative settings.

Keywords: recursive reasoning, Bayesian optimization, no-regret learning, game theory, multi-agent systems, strategic games, decision-making.
