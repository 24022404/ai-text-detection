The rapid advancement of neural networks has opened new possibilities for automating complex tasks, including the generation of structured markup from visual inputs. This paper addresses the challenge of teaching machines to generate accurate and semantically meaningful code or markup by leveraging visual attention mechanisms. We propose a novel neural architecture that integrates convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with attention-based sequence models to interpret visual inputs, such as graphical user interfaces (GUIs), and translate them into corresponding markup languages (e.g., HTML, XML). Our approach focuses on improving the model's ability to capture spatial relationships and hierarchical structures within visual data, which are critical for accurate markup generation.  

To evaluate the proposed method, we conduct experiments on a large-scale dataset of GUI images paired with their corresponding markup code. The results demonstrate that our model outperforms existing baselines in terms of both accuracy and efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art performance in markup generation tasks. Additionally, we analyze the role of visual attention in enhancing the model's interpretability and its ability to handle complex layouts.  

The implications of this research extend beyond markup generation, offering potential applications in automated web development, UI prototyping, and assistive tools for designers. By bridging the gap between visual perception and structured code generation, this work contributes to the broader goal of enabling machines to understand and manipulate visual information in a human-like manner.  

**Keywords**: neural networks, visual attention, markup generation, convolutional neural networks, sequence models, GUI interpretation, automated coding.
