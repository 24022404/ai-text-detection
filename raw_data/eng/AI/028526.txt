This paper explores the concept of instance-based generalization in reinforcement learning, aiming to improve the ability of agents to generalize across diverse environments and tasks. Our approach utilizes a novel instance-based learning framework, which enables agents to learn from specific experiences and generalize to new situations. We employ a combination of model-free and model-based reinforcement learning methods, incorporating techniques such as meta-learning and episodic memory to enhance generalization capabilities. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms traditional reinforcement learning methods in terms of generalization performance, achieving significant improvements in transfer learning and few-shot learning scenarios. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of instance-based generalization in reinforcement learning, with potential applications in areas such as robotics, game playing, and autonomous systems. Our work contributes to the development of more robust and adaptable reinforcement learning algorithms, with implications for improving the efficiency and effectiveness of learning in complex environments. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, instance-based generalization, meta-learning, episodic memory, transfer learning, few-shot learning.
