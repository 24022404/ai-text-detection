This paper addresses the challenge of policy learning in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with continuous state and action spaces, a fundamental problem in reinforcement learning and control. Traditional methods for these settings often struggle with computational complexity and poor scalability due to the infinite dimensionality of the search space. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel randomized policy learning framework that leverages stochastic optimization techniques to efficiently explore the policy space. Our approach introduces a randomized policy parameterization that enables flexible function approximation, along with variance reduction strategies to enhance learning stability and convergence speed. Experiments conducted on benchmark continuous control tasks demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art algorithms in terms of sample efficiency and achieved reward, particularly in high-dimensional environments. Theoretical analysis further establishes the convergence guarantees of our approach under standard MDP assumptions. These results highlight the potential of randomized methods for robust and scalable policy optimization in complex, real-world scenarios. Key contributions include the development of a scalable framework for continuous MDPs, improved learning stability, and demonstrated performance gains on challenging tasks. Keywords: Markov Decision Process, reinforcement learning, continuous control, randomized algorithms, policy optimization, stochastic methods.
