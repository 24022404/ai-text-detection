This study addresses the critical challenge of creating adversarial attacks that can transfer across different image and video object detection frameworks. The aim is to test the resilience and robustness of widely used detection models against adversarial perturbations which hold the potential to generalize beyond specific model architectures.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel methodology for generating transferable adversarial attacks, leveraging perturbation techniques that exploit inherent vulnerabilities within object detection algorithms. Our approach synthesizes adversarial examples by optimizing perturbations that maintain their effectiveness across multiple detection frameworks, including both deep learning-based and non-deep learning-based models. Techniques from both gradient-based attack strategies and sophisticated video frame manipulation algorithms are integrated to ensure robustness and effectiveness.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed adversarial attacks successfully degrade the object detection capabilities of diverse models, achieving a significant reduction in detection accuracy. Tests conducted on popular models, such as Faster R-CNN and YOLO, reveal that our attacks consistently maintain high transferability rates. Additionally, our approach requires minimal computational overhead, proving its efficiency and practical applicability.

Conclusion/Implications: The research highlights the vulnerabilities present in current image and video object detection systems and underscores the need for the development of more robust models. The transferable adversarial attacks we introduce serve both as a tool for enhancing adversarial training techniques and as a benchmark for evaluating the security of object detection frameworks. By elucidating these weaknesses, our work contributes to advancing the field toward more resilient AI systems capable of withstanding adversarial threats in real-world scenarios.

Keywords: adversarial attacks, transferable perturbations, object detection, deep learning, image security, video analysis, model robustness.
