**Abstract:**

Scene flow estimation is fundamental for understanding 3D motion in dynamic environments, with critical applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality. Traditional approaches to self-supervised scene flow often rely on local neighborhood consistency, making them susceptible to errors introduced by ambiguous or adversarial points. In this paper, we propose a novel adversarial metric learning framework designed to robustly estimate scene flow in a self-supervised manner without trusting potentially misleading neighbor relationships. Our approach integrates an adversarial loss component that explicitly identifies and downweights unreliable neighbors during the learning process, thereby enhancing the robustness and accuracy of metric learning. Leveraging deep neural architectures, our method dynamically adjusts feature space distances to discourage the propagation of erroneous associations. Experimental results on standard benchmarks demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art self-supervised methods, particularly in challenging scenarios with occlusions or significant scene changes. The proposed framework establishes new baselines for adversarial self-supervised scene flow estimation, offering improved generalization and reliability. This work advances self-supervised learning by highlighting and mitigating the risks of naive neighborhood trust and suggests broader implications for adversarial robustness in other computer vision tasks. Key keywords: scene flow, self-supervised learning, adversarial metric learning, 3D motion estimation, deep learning.
