This paper addresses the challenge of learning robust policies in Block Markov Decision Processes (Block MDPs) by leveraging invariant causal prediction. Block MDPs are characterized by rich observations and latent states, making it difficult to generalize across environments with varying dynamics. The objective of this work is to identify and utilize causal relationships that remain invariant across different environments, enabling the development of policies that are robust to distributional shifts. We propose a novel approach that integrates causal inference with reinforcement learning, employing invariant causal prediction to identify state features that are causally linked to rewards and transitions. Our method involves learning a representation of the latent state space that captures invariant causal structures, followed by policy optimization using these representations. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of generalization and robustness, particularly in environments with significant distributional changes. The findings highlight the importance of causal reasoning in reinforcement learning and provide a framework for developing policies that are resilient to environmental variations. This research contributes to advancing the understanding of causal invariance in Block MDPs and offers practical implications for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and other domains where robustness to changing environments is critical.  

**Keywords**: Block MDPs, invariant causal prediction, reinforcement learning, causal inference, robust policies, distributional shifts.
