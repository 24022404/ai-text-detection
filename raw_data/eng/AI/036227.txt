The increasing prevalence of naturalistic driving studies necessitates advanced methodologies to analyze complex driving behaviors, such as lane changes during on-ramp merges. This paper introduces an innovative approach for unsupervised lane-change identification aimed at enhancing the understanding of driver behavior in real-world driving situations.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel unsupervised machine learning framework that leverages clustering algorithms and trajectory analysis to identify lane-change maneuvers in on-ramp merging scenarios. The methodology utilizes naturalistic driving data collected from vehicular sensors, focusing on parameters such as vehicle speed, acceleration, and lateral position, to detect and analyze lane-change events without reliance on labeled data.

Results/Findings: The developed framework demonstrated high accuracy in detecting lane-change maneuvers, achieving a significant improvement over existing supervised techniques. Our results reveal distinct patterns in lane-change behavior, highlighting key factors influencing decision-making during on-ramp merges. Comparative analysis shows the robustness of our approach across diverse driving conditions and vehicle types.

Conclusion/Implications: This study contributes to the field of traffic safety and intelligent transportation systems by providing a scalable and efficient method for uncovering subtle driving behaviors in unstructured environments. The unsupervised lane-change identification model not only aids in traffic flow analysis but also lays the groundwork for future research in autonomous vehicle systems and driver assistance technologies. Keywords include unsupervised learning, lane-change identification, naturalistic driving data, on-ramp merge, trajectory analysis.
