This paper introduces a novel explanation-based weakly-supervised learning (EWSL) framework for visual relation detection, leveraging graph networks to reason about object interactions. Our method addresses the challenge of limited labeled data by utilizing readily available scene graph annotations as weak supervision. EWSL incorporates an explanation module that generates textual explanations for predicted relations, which are then used to refine the model's understanding of visual relationships. We employ graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to model the relationships between objects, enabling effective information propagation and reasoning. The explanation module provides crucial feedback, guiding the model to focus on relevant visual features and improving the accuracy of relation prediction. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our EWSL approach significantly outperforms existing weakly-supervised methods and achieves comparable performance to some fully-supervised techniques. The proposed framework offers a promising direction for learning visual relations with minimal annotation effort, facilitating the development of more robust and scalable vision systems. Keywords: visual relation detection, weakly-supervised learning, graph networks, explanation-based learning, scene graphs.
