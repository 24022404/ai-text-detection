This paper addresses the challenge of 3D object localization in computer vision, a critical task for applications such as autonomous navigation, robotics, and augmented reality. While traditional methods rely heavily on 3D sensors or complex depth estimation techniques, we propose a novel approach that leverages 2D estimates to infer accurate 3D object positions. Our method utilizes a combination of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and geometric optimization to map 2D image features to 3D spatial coordinates. By integrating pose estimation and depth prediction into a unified framework, we reduce computational overhead while maintaining high localization accuracy. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art 3D localization methods, with a significant reduction in resource requirements. The proposed method is particularly advantageous for real-time applications, offering a balance between accuracy and efficiency. This work contributes to advancing 3D vision systems by providing a lightweight yet effective solution for 3D object localization, with potential applications in robotics, surveillance, and interactive systems.  

**Keywords**: 3D object localization, 2D estimates, computer vision, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), pose estimation, depth prediction, real-time applications.
