The increasing reliance on deep learning models for image and video object detection has raised significant concerns regarding their vulnerability to adversarial attacks. This paper investigates the transferability of adversarial attacks across different object detection models, aiming to identify universal vulnerabilities in widely used architectures. We propose a novel adversarial attack framework that leverages gradient-based optimization techniques to generate transferable perturbations capable of deceiving multiple state-of-the-art object detection systems. Our approach incorporates a multi-model ensemble strategy to enhance the generalization of adversarial examples across diverse architectures, including Faster R-CNN, YOLO, and SSD. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves a high success rate in transferring adversarial examples between models, with an average attack success rate of 85% across benchmark datasets such as COCO and ImageNet. Furthermore, we extend our framework to video object detection, showcasing its effectiveness in temporal contexts. The findings highlight critical security gaps in object detection systems and emphasize the need for robust defense mechanisms. This research contributes to the field by providing a deeper understanding of adversarial transferability and offering insights into improving the resilience of object detection models against such attacks.  

**Keywords**: adversarial attacks, object detection, transferability, deep learning, image processing, video analysis, model robustness.
