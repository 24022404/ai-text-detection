This paper investigates the vulnerability of semantic image segmentation models to membership inference attacks, where an attacker aims to determine whether a given data point was used in the model's training dataset. Our objective is to develop effective attacks and defenses for such models, which are crucial in various applications, including healthcare and autonomous driving. We propose a novel membership inference attack, termed Segmentations-Leak, which exploits the unique characteristics of semantic image segmentation models. Our approach utilizes the segmentation masks produced by the model to infer membership information, achieving high attack success rates. To counter such attacks, we also develop defense mechanisms, including data augmentation and regularization techniques, which significantly reduce the attack's effectiveness. Our results demonstrate the feasibility and potential risks of membership inference attacks in semantic image segmentation, highlighting the need for robust defenses. The contributions of this research include the introduction of a new attack vector, the development of effective defense strategies, and a comprehensive evaluation of the trade-offs between model accuracy and privacy. Key keywords: membership inference attacks, semantic image segmentation, model privacy, defense mechanisms, data augmentation, regularization techniques.
