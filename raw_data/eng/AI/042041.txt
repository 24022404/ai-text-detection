Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) is a popular framework in various machine learning applications where labels are assigned to sets of instances, or "bags," rather than individual elements. This paper investigates the effect of different MIL pooling filters—mechanisms used to aggregate instance-level information into bag-level representations—on the performance and reliability of MIL tasks. Employing commonly used datasets across domains such as image classification and bioinformatics, we systematically evaluate several pooling techniques, including max-pooling, mean-pooling, attention-based pooling, and softmax pooling. Our experiments involve both traditional and deep learning MIL models, allowing us to analyze how the choice of pooling function impacts model accuracy, robustness to noise, and ability to capture relevant instance features. The results reveal distinct performance trade-offs: while max-pooling demonstrates strong performance in scenarios with highly discriminative instances, attention-based pooling offers superior interpretability and stability in more complex or ambiguous settings. Our findings provide practical guidelines for selecting appropriate MIL pooling filters based on task requirements and data characteristics. This work advances the understanding of pooling strategies in MIL and paves the way for more effective deployment of MIL systems in real-world applications. Key keywords: Multiple Instance Learning, pooling filters, attention pooling, deep learning, aggregation methods, classification tasks.
