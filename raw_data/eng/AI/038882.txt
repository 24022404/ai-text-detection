This paper introduces a novel approach to training Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) by integrating self-labeling and self-attention mechanisms to address common challenges such as mode collapse and instability. The proposed method leverages self-labeling to generate pseudo-labels for unlabeled data, enabling the discriminator to better distinguish between real and synthetic samples. Simultaneously, a self-attention mechanism is incorporated into both the generator and discriminator to enhance feature representation and improve the model's ability to capture complex data distributions. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves superior performance in terms of image quality and diversity compared to existing GAN variants. Quantitative evaluations, including Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception Score (IS), highlight significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods. The results suggest that the unified approach not only stabilizes training but also enhances the generative capabilities of GANs, making it a promising solution for applications in image synthesis, data augmentation, and beyond. This work contributes to advancing GAN research by providing a robust and efficient training framework that combines self-supervised learning and attention-based modeling.  

**Keywords**: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), self-labeling, self-attention, image synthesis, mode collapse, pseudo-labels, feature representation.
