The objective of this research is to enhance video summarization by introducing a novel approach termed Dual Mixture Attention Meta Learning. Video summarization involves the extraction of keyframes or segments from lengthy videos to generate a concise yet informative summary, which is essential for efficient content consumption and management. Our approach leverages a dual query mechanism within a mixture attention framework that significantly improves the adaptability and accuracy of video summarization models. Methodologically, the model employs a meta-learning strategy that allows for rapid generalization across a diverse set of video contents by fine-tuning the mixture of attentions based on specific querying twice per video context. This dual querying system facilitates the dynamic capture of temporal and spatial dependencies within the video sequences, thus offering a comprehensive summary. 

In our findings, the proposed model outperforms existing methods in both the quality and efficiency of video summaries, as demonstrated through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets. The mixture attention mechanism reduces redundancy and enhances the semantic representation of video segments, leading to summaries that are closer to human-level summarization benchmarks. Implications of this study suggest that the incorporation of dual mixture attention and meta-learning can substantially advance the field of automated video summarization, providing substantial improvements in areas such as video archiving, video search, and content recommendation systems. This innovative framework opens avenues for further research into adaptive summarization techniques and their application in real-world scenarios. Key keywords include video summarization, meta learning, mixture attention, and automated content analysis.
