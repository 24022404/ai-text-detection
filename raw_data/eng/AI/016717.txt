This paper presents a novel approach to learning navigation costs from demonstration using semantic observations. The objective is to enable autonomous agents to learn effective navigation strategies by observing human demonstrations and understanding the semantic meaning of their environment. Our method utilizes a combination of machine learning and computer vision techniques to extract relevant information from demonstrations and semantic observations, such as object detection and scene understanding. The approach is based on a deep reinforcement learning framework that incorporates semantic features to learn a cost function that guides the agent's navigation. Experimental results show that our method outperforms existing approaches in terms of navigation efficiency and accuracy, demonstrating the effectiveness of incorporating semantic observations into the learning process. The key contributions of this research include the development of a semantic-aware cost learning framework and the demonstration of its potential to improve navigation performance in complex environments. This work has significant implications for applications such as autonomous robotics, self-driving cars, and intelligent assistants, where efficient navigation is crucial. Key keywords: navigation costs, semantic observations, deep reinforcement learning, autonomous agents, computer vision.
