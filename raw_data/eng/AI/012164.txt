This paper addresses the challenge of skeleton-based action recognition by proposing a novel approach that fuses higher-order features in Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). The objective is to improve the accuracy of action recognition models by effectively capturing complex spatial and temporal relationships between human joints. Our method employs a multi-scale feature fusion strategy, combining node, edge, and graph-level features to enhance the representation power of GNNs. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods on benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in recognition accuracy. The key findings highlight the importance of incorporating higher-order features in GNNs for skeleton-based action recognition. This research contributes to the development of more accurate and robust action recognition systems, with potential applications in human-computer interaction, surveillance, and healthcare. The proposed approach leverages graph convolutional networks, attention mechanisms, and feature fusion techniques, making it a valuable addition to the field of computer vision and machine learning, particularly in the areas of action recognition, graph neural networks, and human behavior analysis.
