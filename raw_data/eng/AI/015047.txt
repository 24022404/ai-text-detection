In the realm of sequential decision-making, developing efficient models to handle diverse tasks with limited data remains a critical challenge. This study explores the application of meta-learning approaches to create surrogate models that can effectively generalize across various sequential decision-making scenarios.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel meta-learning framework designed to learn surrogate models that inherently possess the ability to adapt rapidly to new decision-making tasks. Our approach leverages state-of-the-art techniques in meta-learning to train these surrogate models by simulating diverse environments, ensuring robustness and adaptability. The framework combines both model-based and model-free techniques to optimize performance and accelerate the learning process.

Results/Findings: Through extensive experimentation, our surrogate models demonstrate significantly improved adaptability and performance compared to traditional models in diverse sequential decision-making settings. Key results indicate faster convergence rates and enhanced flexibility when encountering previously unseen tasks. The proposed framework outperforms baseline models by a substantial margin in terms of efficiency and accuracy in different case studies, highlighting its capability to generalize effectively.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes significantly to the field by presenting a meta-learning approach that equips surrogate models with heightened generalization abilities across sequential decision-making tasks. The implications of this study extend to various domains, including robotics, autonomous systems, and financial modeling, where quick adaptation to dynamic environments is crucial. Our findings encourage further exploration into meta-learning strategies to enhance the performance of models deployed in ever-changing operational contexts.

Key Keywords: meta-learning, surrogate models, sequential decision making, adaptability, generalization, model-based, model-free.
