This paper proposes a novel deep neural network architecture designed to capture the sequential behaviors and long-short term preferences of online users, transitioning from implicit to explicit feedback. The objective is to develop a model that accurately predicts user preferences and interests based on their historical interactions. Our approach utilizes a combination of recurrent neural networks and attention mechanisms to learn complex patterns in user behavior. The model is trained on a large dataset of user interactions, incorporating both implicit feedback (e.g., clickstream data) and explicit feedback (e.g., ratings). Results show that our model outperforms existing methods in predicting user preferences, demonstrating improved accuracy and robustness. The key contributions of this research include the development of a scalable and flexible architecture for modeling sequential user behavior, and the ability to effectively integrate implicit and explicit feedback. This work has significant implications for personalized recommendation systems, user modeling, and online behavior analysis, with potential applications in e-commerce, social media, and online advertising. Key keywords: deep neural networks, sequential behavior modeling, implicit and explicit feedback, user preferences, personalized recommendation systems.
