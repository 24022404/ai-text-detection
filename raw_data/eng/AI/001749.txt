Abstract:

Distribution matching is a fundamental challenge in generative modeling, where the goal is to produce samples that accurately reflect the underlying data distribution. This paper presents a novel "Best-of-Many-Samples" (BMS) distribution matching technique, designed to improve sample quality and diversity in probabilistic generative models. The proposed method involves generating multiple candidate samples for each input and selecting the one that best aligns with the target distribution, according to a predefined similarity metric. Our approach is evaluated using both synthetic and real-world datasets, encompassing image generation and sequence modeling tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that the BMS strategy consistently outperforms conventional single-sample generation methods in terms of fidelity and mode coverage, as measured by standard metrics such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and precision-recall scores. Furthermore, the BMS framework integrates seamlessly with existing generative architectures, including variational autoencoders (VAEs) and normalizing flows, without imposing significant computational overhead. The findings illustrate that selectively choosing the best sample from multiple candidates leads to a more effective approximation of complex target distributions. This work advances the field of distribution matching by offering a simple yet powerful enhancement to generative sampling procedures, with broad applicability in tasks such as data augmentation, synthetic data generation, and simulation. Keywords: generative modeling, distribution matching, best-of-many-samples, sample quality, data generation, VAEs, normalizing flows.
