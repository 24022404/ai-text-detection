This study addresses the problem of distinguishing between crops and weeds, a critical task for precision agriculture aimed at optimizing crop yield and reducing herbicide usage. The research focuses on developing a supervised learning model that utilizes color and texture features for accurate classification of crop and weed imagery.

Methods/Approach: We propose a supervised learning approach leveraging a machine learning classifier trained on a dataset containing images of crops and weeds. The model extracts salient color and texture features, such as hue, saturation, brightness, and texture descriptors, to optimize classification performance. Various preprocessing techniques were applied to enhance image quality and ensure robust feature extraction. The chosen algorithm was tuned for optimal performance through cross-validation.

Results/Findings: The developed model demonstrated high accuracy and robust classification capabilities, achieving superior results compared to traditional methods. The inclusion of color and texture features as primary inputs significantly improved the discrimination between crops and weeds. The model's performance was evaluated against a benchmark dataset, showing substantial improvements in precision and recall metrics.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field of precision agriculture by providing a reliable methodology for crop-weed classification. The model's efficacy in utilizing color and texture features enables efficient weed management strategies, potentially reducing reliance on broad-spectrum herbicides and promoting sustainable farming practices. Future work may expand the model's applicability to diverse agricultural environments and integrate real-time decision-making capabilities.

Keywords: supervised learning, crop-weed classification, precision agriculture, color features, texture features, machine learning, image processing.
