Reinforcement learning (RL) in environments with high-dimensional or rich observations presents significant challenges in terms of sample efficiency and policy optimization. This paper addresses these challenges by introducing a provably efficient RL framework that leverages latent state decoding. The proposed approach utilizes representation learning to infer a compact latent state space from complex observational data, enabling the agent to operate in a lower-dimensional space while preserving essential environment dynamics. We develop a novel algorithm that integrates latent state inference with RL policy updates, ensuring accurate state estimation and efficient exploration. Our theoretical analysis establishes sample complexity bounds, demonstrating that the method achieves near-optimal learning performance under reasonable assumptions. Extensive experiments on benchmark environments with rich observations showcase superior performance compared to existing baselines, with notable improvements in convergence speed and policy effectiveness. This work contributes a principled methodology for scaling RL to more realistic settings with high-dimensional inputs and highlights the benefits of combining representation learning with sample-efficient policy optimization. Keywords: reinforcement learning, latent state decoding, rich observations, sample efficiency, representation learning, provable guarantees.
