Action recognition in video data remains a challenging task due to the complexity of capturing temporal dynamics and distinguishing subtle motion patterns. This paper introduces a novel framework, Temporal Distinct Representation Learning (TDRL), designed to enhance the discriminative power of temporal features for action recognition. The proposed approach leverages a dual-stream architecture that separately processes spatial and temporal information, ensuring robust feature extraction. A key innovation is the introduction of a temporal distinctness module, which emphasizes unique motion patterns by minimizing redundancy across time steps and maximizing inter-class separability. The model is trained using a combination of contrastive learning and cross-entropy loss to optimize both feature distinctiveness and classification accuracy. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including UCF101 and HMDB51, demonstrate that TDRL achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods by significant margins. The results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed temporal distinctness mechanism in improving action recognition accuracy. This work contributes to the field by providing a scalable and efficient solution for temporal feature learning, with potential applications in video surveillance, human-computer interaction, and sports analytics. Keywords: action recognition, temporal representation, contrastive learning, video analysis, deep learning.
