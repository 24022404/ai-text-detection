This paper addresses the challenge of generalized zero-shot learning, where the goal is to recognize unseen classes without prior training data. Our objective is to develop a novel approach that effectively couples visual and semantic information to enhance model generalizability. We propose a two-level adversarial visual-semantic coupling framework, which leverages adversarial training to align visual features with semantic embeddings. At the first level, our model learns to generate synthetic visual features for unseen classes, while at the second level, it adapts the semantic embeddings to better match the visual features. Our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods on benchmark datasets, demonstrating improved recognition accuracy and robustness to unseen classes. The key findings of our research highlight the importance of adversarial training and visual-semantic coupling in generalized zero-shot learning. Our contributions have significant implications for applications such as image recognition, object detection, and robotic vision, where recognizing unseen classes is crucial. Key keywords: generalized zero-shot learning, adversarial training, visual-semantic coupling, deep learning, computer vision.
