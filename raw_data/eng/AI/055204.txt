This paper addresses the challenge of efficient feature extraction and moment reconstruction in dynamical systems through an innovative information-geometric approach. The objective of this research is to leverage the principles of information geometry to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of extracting relevant features from complex dynamical data. Our methodology employs Riemannian geometry to define a novel framework for feature representation and moment reconstruction, allowing for a seamless integration with existing dynamical models. 

The results demonstrate significant improvements in feature extraction accuracy compared to traditional methods, as evidenced by extensive experiments across benchmark dynamical systems. Specifically, we achieve a reduction in computational complexity while maintaining high fidelity in the reconstructed moments. These findings suggest that our proposed approach not only optimizes performance but also offers valuable insights into the underlying structures of dynamical systems.

In conclusion, our research contributes to the field by providing a new perspective on feature extraction and moment reconstruction that is rooted in information geometry. The implications of these findings extend to various applications, including robotics, control systems, and predictive modeling, where accurate representation of dynamics is crucial. Keywords: information geometry, feature extraction, moment reconstruction, dynamical systems, Riemannian geometry.
