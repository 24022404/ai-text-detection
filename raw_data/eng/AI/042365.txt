This paper presents a novel approach to high-quality image inpainting through the development of a Pyramid-Context Encoder Network (PCEN). The objective of our research is to address the challenging problem of restoring visually plausible and coherent images with missing or corrupted regions, especially in complex and high-resolution scenarios. Our method leverages a multi-scale pyramid architecture integrated with a context-aware encoder that captures global and local structural information at various resolutions. By progressively refining features at each pyramid level, the network effectively fuses contextual cues and reconstructs realistic textures and object boundaries. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed PCEN model outperforms existing state-of-the-art inpainting techniques in terms of perceptual quality and quantitative metrics, including PSNR and SSIM. Notably, our framework exhibits strong generalization across diverse image domains and occlusion patterns. The research highlights the significance of hierarchical feature extraction and contextual reasoning in advancing image completion tasks. Future applications of this approach may include photo restoration, object removal, and digital image editing. Keywords: image inpainting, pyramid network, context encoder, deep learning, image restoration, computer vision.
