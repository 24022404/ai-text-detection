This paper presents a comprehensive overview of the NIPS 2016 Workshop on Representation Learning in Artificial and Biological Neural Networks (MLINI 2016), focusing on the intersection of artificial intelligence and neuroscience. The objective of this workshop was to explore the latest advances in representation learning, a crucial aspect of both artificial and biological neural networks. 

The approach involved bringing together experts from various fields, including computer science, neuroscience, and engineering, to share their insights and research on representation learning. The workshop featured a range of topics, from the theoretical foundations of deep learning to the application of representation learning in biological neural networks.

The key findings of the workshop highlighted the importance of understanding the representations learned by artificial neural networks and their potential applications in neuroscience. The workshop also showcased novel methods for analyzing and interpreting the representations learned by deep neural networks, as well as new techniques for applying representation learning to real-world problems.

The conclusion of the workshop emphasized the potential for representation learning to drive progress in both artificial intelligence and neuroscience. The research presented at the workshop contributed to the development of more sophisticated artificial neural networks and shed light on the neural mechanisms underlying biological intelligence. Key keywords: representation learning, artificial neural networks, biological neural networks, deep learning, neuroscience.
