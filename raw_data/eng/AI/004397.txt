This paper investigates the vulnerability of skeleton-based human action recognition systems to adversarial attacks. With the increasing use of these systems across various applications such as security surveillance and human-computer interaction, understanding their robustness against potential adversarial threats is crucial.

Methods/Approach: We design a novel adversarial attack framework specifically targeting skeletal representation data used in action recognition algorithms. The proposed framework generates adversarial perturbations to manipulate the skeletal data, thereby deceiving the recognition model into making incorrect classifications. Our method leverages a gradient-based approach to optimize the perturbations while maintaining the natural appearance of the human movement.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed adversarial attack significantly degrades the performance of several state-of-the-art skeleton-based action recognition models. Our attacks achieve high success rates with minimal perturbations, revealing a substantial vulnerability in current recognition systems. Comparisons with existing attack methods show our approach to be more effective in both attack success rate and robustness.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings highlight a critical security concern within the domain of human action recognition, emphasizing the need for more resilient recognition models. This research contributes to the field by identifying weaknesses and prompting the development of more robust defenses against adversarial attacks. Future work should focus on designing countermeasures and enhancing the inherent robustness of skeleton-based recognition systems. Key keywords include adversarial attack, skeleton-based recognition, human action, vulnerability, and security.
