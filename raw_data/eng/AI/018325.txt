Corporate credit rating prediction is a critical task in financial risk management, yet it is often challenged by imbalanced datasets where certain rating classes are underrepresented. This paper introduces a novel contrastive pre-training framework designed to address the imbalance issue in corporate credit rating prediction. The proposed approach leverages contrastive learning to enhance the representation of minority classes by maximizing the similarity between positive pairs (e.g., companies with similar financial profiles) while minimizing the similarity between negative pairs. We pre-train the model on a large-scale financial dataset using self-supervised learning, followed by fine-tuning on the imbalanced credit rating dataset. Experimental results demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms traditional supervised learning approaches and state-of-the-art imbalanced learning techniques, achieving higher accuracy and F1 scores across all rating classes. The framework also exhibits robustness to varying levels of data imbalance, making it a practical solution for real-world financial applications. This research contributes to the field by providing a scalable and effective pre-training strategy for imbalanced datasets, with potential applications in credit risk assessment, fraud detection, and other financial analytics tasks.  

**Keywords**: contrastive learning, pre-training, imbalanced datasets, corporate credit ratings, financial risk management, self-supervised learning.
