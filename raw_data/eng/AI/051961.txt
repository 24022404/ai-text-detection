This paper addresses the challenge of learning latent manifold representations within high-dimensional data spaces, which is crucial for robust data generation and efficient dimensionality reduction. Traditional autoencoder frameworks often struggle with capturing the true underlying structure of datasets due to their reliance on fixed priors.

Methods/Approach: We introduce the Encoded Prior Sliced Wasserstein AutoEncoder (EPSWAE), an innovative model that integrates the flexibility of learning suitable latent distributions with the stability of Sliced Wasserstein metrics. Our approach extends conventional autoencoders by adopting an encoded prior mechanism that aligns the latent space more effectively with the data manifold. The model leverages the computational efficiency of the Sliced Wasserstein distance to facilitate scalable and accurate approximation of distributional similarities in the latent space.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that EPSWAE significantly outperforms existing models in terms of latent space representation quality and reconstruction accuracy. Evaluations conducted on commonly used benchmark datasets reveal enhanced performance in generating realistic samples and preserving data variability. The integration of the Wasserstein metric has proven beneficial for reducing mode collapse, a common issue in generative models, thereby ensuring a more comprehensive capture of the data distribution.

Conclusion/Implications: Our findings illustrate the potential of EPSWAE as a powerful tool for manifold learning and generative data analysis. By reshaping the conventional paradigm of fixed priors in autoencoders, the model opens new pathways for applications in fields where accurate data representation and generation are pivotal, such as image synthesis, anomaly detection, and data compression. This work lays a foundation for future explorations into more adaptable and efficient generative frameworks.

Keywords: AutoEncoder, Sliced Wasserstein, Latent Manifold, Encoded Prior, Generative Models, Data Representation, High-Dimensional Spaces.
