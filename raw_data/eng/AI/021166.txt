This paper addresses the challenge of improving learning efficiency and convergence speed in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) systems, a domain where training times and computational costs often hinder practical deployment. We propose a suite of accelerated methods that integrate advanced optimization algorithms, adaptive exploration strategies, and efficient experience replay techniques to enhance the performance of DRL agents. Specifically, our approach incorporates momentum-based gradient updates, prioritized sampling, and dynamic adjustment of exploration rates to target slow-converging regimes in complex environments. Experimental evaluations were conducted across several standard benchmarks, including Atari and robotic control tasks, where our accelerated methods consistently achieved faster convergence and higher cumulative rewards compared to baseline DRL algorithms such as Deep Q-Networks (DQN) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The results demonstrate that the proposed techniques significantly reduce training epochs without compromising policy quality. This work contributes a novel framework for accelerating deep reinforcement learning, with practical implications for real-time decision-making, robotics, and large-scale autonomous systems. Key keywords include deep reinforcement learning, acceleration, optimization, experience replay, convergence speed, adaptive exploration, and policy improvement.
