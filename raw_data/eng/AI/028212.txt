Autonomous driving systems often struggle with the computational demands of processing high-resolution visual data. This paper introduces a novel periphery-fovea multi-resolution driving model inspired by human visual attention. Our approach leverages a high-resolution "fovea" region focused on attended areas, while processing the remaining "periphery" at lower resolutions, significantly reducing computational cost. We employ an attention mechanism trained to predict human gaze patterns to dynamically select the foveal region. The driving model is trained using a deep convolutional neural network that integrates multi-resolution input to predict driving actions. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our model achieves comparable or superior driving performance to full high-resolution models, with a substantial reduction in computational complexity. This bio-inspired approach offers a promising direction for efficient and robust autonomous driving systems. Keywords: autonomous driving, attention mechanism, multi-resolution, deep learning, human gaze, computational efficiency.
