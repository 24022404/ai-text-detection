Deep neural networks (DNNs) often suffer from performance degradation due to ill-conditioned weight matrices, leading to exploding or vanishing gradients. This paper introduces a novel approach to improve DNN training by enforcing controllable orthogonalization of weight matrices. We propose a regularization technique that encourages orthogonality while allowing for controlled deviations, enabling a flexible trade-off between orthogonality and model expressiveness. The method involves adding a penalty term to the loss function that explicitly measures the deviation from orthogonality. The strength of this penalty is controlled by a hyperparameter, offering fine-grained control over the degree of orthogonalization. Experiments on various benchmark datasets demonstrate that our controllable orthogonalization technique consistently improves the generalization performance of DNNs compared to standard training and existing orthogonalization methods. We also show that our method allows for faster convergence and more stable training, particularly in deep architectures. The results suggest that controlled orthogonalization provides a valuable tool for training more robust and efficient DNNs. Keywords: Deep Neural Networks, Orthogonalization, Regularization, Training Stability, Generalization Performance.
