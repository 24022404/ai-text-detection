Unsupervised many-to-many image translation aims to learn mappings between different image domains without paired training data, enabling diverse and realistic image generation. However, evaluating the correctness of these translations remains a significant challenge due to the lack of ground truth. This paper introduces a novel evaluation framework leveraging cycle consistency and semantic consistency to assess the accuracy of translated images. Our approach employs a combination of image reconstruction error, semantic segmentation analysis, and feature-based comparisons to quantify the preservation of essential image characteristics during translation. We evaluate our framework on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating its ability to effectively differentiate between translation methods with varying levels of accuracy and fidelity. Results indicate that our proposed metrics correlate strongly with human perception of correctness and offer a more comprehensive assessment compared to existing evaluation techniques. This work provides valuable tools for researchers to objectively measure the performance of unsupervised image translation models and facilitate further advancements in the field. Keywords: Image Translation, Unsupervised Learning, Evaluation Metrics, Cycle Consistency, Semantic Consistency, Image Quality.
