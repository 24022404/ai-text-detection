This paper investigates discrete-time mean field control systems incorporating explicit environment states, aiming to enhance the modeling and coordination of large-scale, multi-agent systems. The primary objective is to address the complex interplay between agents’ collective behavior and dynamically evolving environments, which is critical in applications such as smart grids, swarm robotics, and societal systems. We propose a novel control framework where both agent states and environment states are jointly considered within the mean field control paradigm. Our approach extends the traditional mean field theory by introducing environment dynamics, leading to a coupled system of discrete-time equations governing the evolution of both agents and their external context. We develop efficient algorithms for deriving optimal control policies under this extended model and analyze their convergence properties. Numerical experiments demonstrate that our method achieves significant improvements in system performance and adaptability compared to conventional mean field control approaches that neglect environmental feedback. The findings underscore the value of integrating environment state dynamics into mean field control, offering improved scalability and flexibility for real-world multi-agent systems. Potential applications and future extensions are discussed, illustrating the broader impact of this research on distributed control and networked system optimization. 

Keywords: mean field control, discrete-time systems, environment states, multi-agent systems, distributed optimization, dynamic systems
