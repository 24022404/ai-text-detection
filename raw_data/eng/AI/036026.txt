This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the robustness of graph neural networks against adversarial attacks. Our objective is to develop a novel defense strategy, termed Adversarial Immunization, which leverages the concept of certifiable robustness to protect graph models from malicious perturbations. We propose a unique approach that combines graph-based adversarial training with a certification mechanism, ensuring that the trained models are robust against a wide range of attacks. Our method utilizes a robust optimization framework to minimize the loss function while maximizing the certifiable robustness of the model. Experimental results demonstrate that our Adversarial Immunization approach outperforms existing defense methods, achieving state-of-the-art robustness on various graph benchmarks. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of certifiable robustness in graph neural networks and demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in improving model resilience against adversarial attacks. This work contributes to the development of more secure and reliable graph-based AI systems, with potential applications in areas such as social network analysis, recommendation systems, and graph-based anomaly detection. Key keywords: graph neural networks, adversarial robustness, certifiable robustness, graph-based defense, robust optimization.
