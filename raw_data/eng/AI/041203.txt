The objective of this research is to explore the potential of Evolution Strategies (ES) as a scalable and effective alternative to traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques. While RL has demonstrated success in various complex tasks, it often encounters scalability issues related to computational demands and sample efficiency. Our study introduces an evolutionary algorithm framework, leveraging population-based search and gradient approximation, to address these challenges. We applied ES to several benchmark tasks traditionally dominated by RL, comparing its performance on continuous control problems which demand high-dimensional policy learning. The results reveal that ES not only equals but often surpasses the performance of RL in terms of convergence speed and scaling on large-scale environments. Furthermore, the ES approach utilizes fewer resources, showcasing its efficiency in terms of parallel computation across distributed systems. These findings suggest that ES offers a viable and scalable alternative for complex problem-solving that could significantly impact the fields of AI and machine learning. Keywords: Evolution Strategies, Reinforcement Learning, scalability, continuous control, algorithm efficiency, computational resources.
