Object detection remains a critical challenge in computer vision, requiring robust and efficient methods to accurately identify and localize objects in complex scenes. This paper introduces WW-Nets, a novel dual neural network architecture designed to enhance object detection performance by leveraging complementary feature extraction and fusion mechanisms. The proposed approach consists of two parallel neural networks: one focused on spatial feature extraction and the other on contextual understanding, which are seamlessly integrated to improve detection accuracy. We employ a multi-scale feature fusion strategy to combine outputs from both networks, enabling the model to capture fine-grained details and global context simultaneously. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including COCO and Pascal VOC, demonstrate that WW-Nets achieve state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of precision and recall. The results highlight the effectiveness of the dual-network architecture in handling occlusions, scale variations, and complex backgrounds. This research contributes a scalable and efficient framework for object detection, with potential applications in autonomous driving, surveillance, and robotics. Keywords: object detection, dual neural networks, feature fusion, computer vision, WW-Nets.
