This paper addresses the challenge of relational generalized few-shot learning, where the goal is to enable machines to learn from limited data and generalize across diverse relational structures. Our objective is to develop a framework that can effectively learn to recognize and adapt to new relationships from few examples. We propose a novel approach that combines graph neural networks with meta-learning, allowing the model to learn relational patterns and generalize to unseen data. Our method, termed Relational Generalized Few-Shot Learning (RGFSL), leverages the strengths of both graph representation learning and few-shot learning to achieve state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. The results demonstrate significant improvements in accuracy and efficiency compared to existing methods, particularly in scenarios with limited training data. The implications of this research are substantial, enabling applications in areas such as knowledge graph completion, question answering, and recommender systems. Key contributions include the introduction of a relational meta-learning framework, the development of a graph-based few-shot learning algorithm, and the demonstration of improved performance on real-world datasets. Relevant keywords: few-shot learning, graph neural networks, meta-learning, relational learning, knowledge graph completion.
