Object detection models traditionally require large quantities of accurately annotated data, making their deployment challenging in scenarios with limited labeling resources. This paper addresses the problem of training effective object detectors when only a small set of weakly-labeled images and a large collection of unlabeled images are available. We propose a novel semi-supervised learning framework that leverages weak labels—such as image-level tags or imprecise bounding boxes—combined with abundant unlabeled data. The method integrates a multi-stage training process: initial detector training with weak labels, pseudo-label generation on unlabeled images using the preliminary model, and iterative refinement through self-training. To mitigate the noise inherent to weak supervision, we introduce an adaptive pseudo-label filtering mechanism and confidence-based sample selection. Extensive experiments conducted on benchmark object detection datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing weakly-supervised and semi-supervised methods in detecting challenging object categories, while requiring significantly less annotation effort. The results highlight the effectiveness of exploiting weak and unlabeled data, paving the way for scalable object detection in low-resource and real-world settings. Keywords: object detection, weakly-labeled data, semi-supervised learning, pseudo-labeling, deep learning.
