**Abstract**

Light field imaging captures rich spatial and angular information of scenes, enabling advanced applications such as 3D reconstruction and computational photography. However, practical light field systems often trade spatial resolution for angular resolution, resulting in limited image quality. This paper presents a novel framework for light field super-resolution utilizing an attention-guided fusion of hybrid lens data. The proposed method integrates images from both lens array-based and coded-aperture light field cameras, leveraging their complementary characteristics. An attention mechanism is employed to adaptively fuse multi-source observations, selectively emphasizing informative features while suppressing noise and redundancy. Experiments conducted on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly enhances spatial details and preserves angular consistency compared to traditional super-resolution techniques. The results indicate notable improvements in peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity (SSIM), confirming the effectiveness of attention-guided fusion. This work contributes a practical and scalable solution to overcoming inherent resolution limitations in light field cameras, opening new opportunities for high-fidelity imaging in computational photography and immersive media applications. Key terms: light field, super-resolution, attention mechanism, hybrid lens, image fusion, computational photography.
