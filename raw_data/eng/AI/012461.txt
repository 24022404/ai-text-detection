Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a promising approach for automating the pruning of neural networks, aiming to optimize model efficiency without compromising accuracy. However, RL-based auto-pruning often suffers from slow convergence due to the high-dimensional search space and sparse reward signals. This paper addresses this challenge by proposing a novel framework that leverages historical pruning data to accelerate the convergence of RL-based auto-pruning algorithms. Our approach integrates a data-driven initialization strategy and a reward-shaping mechanism, utilizing past pruning experiences to guide the RL agent more effectively. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method significantly reduces training time while achieving competitive pruning performance compared to state-of-the-art techniques. The results highlight the potential of incorporating historical data to enhance the efficiency of RL-driven optimization tasks in neural network compression. This work contributes to the growing field of automated model optimization by introducing a practical and scalable solution for improving RL-based pruning systems.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Auto-pruning, Neural Network Compression, Historical Data, Reward Shaping, Model Optimization.
