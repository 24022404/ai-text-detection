In this paper, we address the challenge of image restoration through the innovative approach of Deeply Learned Spectral Total Variation Decomposition (DLSTVD). The objective is to effectively separate complex images into their constituent parts—reflecting structures and textures—while maintaining fidelity and minimizing artifacts. Our proposed method leverages deep learning techniques combined with spectral analysis to enhance the total variation regularization framework, resulting in superior performance compared to traditional methods.

We introduce a novel deep neural network architecture that incorporates spectral decomposition and total variation principles to facilitate the modeling of spatial and spectral information. Our approach is evaluated on a diverse dataset, benchmarked against existing state-of-the-art image restoration algorithms. The results demonstrate significant improvements in terms of visual quality and quantitative metrics, such as Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM).

The findings suggest that DLSTVD not only outperforms conventional techniques but also exhibits scalability and adaptability across various image types and degradation scenarios. In conclusion, this research contributes valuable insights into the intersection of deep learning and image processing, showcasing the potential applications of the DLSTVD method in fields such as medical imaging, remote sensing, and digital photography. 

Keywords: Deep Learning, Total Variation, Image Restoration, Spectral Decomposition, Neural Networks.
