Graphical models play a fundamental role in representing complex dependencies among variables in information technology, enabling efficient inference and learning across various data-driven applications. This paper addresses the challenging problem of structure estimation for discrete graphical models, where determining the presence or absence of edges is crucial for accurate model interpretation and decision-making. We introduce a novel framework based on generalized covariance matrices and their inverses to infer the underlying structure of discrete graphical models, including Markov random fields and Ising models. Our approach extends traditional covariance-based techniques, which are primarily designed for Gaussian models, to accommodate the unique properties of discrete data.

We develop an efficient algorithm that computes generalized covariance matrices, integrating higher-order moments that capture intricate dependencies in categorical data. Utilizing these matrices, we demonstrate that the zeros of their inverses correspond to conditional independencies among variables, providing a principled method for structure estimation. Extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets validate the proposed method’s accuracy and computational efficiency, outperforming existing state-of-the-art approaches in both edge recovery and scalability.

The findings highlight the broader applicability of covariance-based principles beyond Gaussian settings and offer practical implications for discrete data analysis in fields such as machine learning, computational biology, and social network analysis. By bridging the gap between generalized covariance analysis and discrete graphical modeling, this work provides a robust, interpretable, and scalable tool for learning the structure of complex discrete systems.

Keywords: graphical models, structure estimation, discrete data, generalized covariance, Markov random fields, conditional independence, machine learning.
