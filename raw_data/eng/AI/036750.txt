Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) is a fundamental statistical technique for identifying and quantifying linear relationships between two sets of multivariate variables. This paper investigates novel formulations and applications of CCA, focusing on improving its computational efficiency and extending its applicability to high-dimensional datasets. We propose a new iterative algorithm for solving the CCA optimization problem that demonstrates significant speed improvements compared to traditional eigenvalue decomposition methods, particularly for large-scale data. The algorithm leverages stochastic gradient descent and mini-batch processing to reduce memory requirements and computational load. Furthermore, we introduce a regularized CCA variant incorporating sparsity constraints to enhance interpretability and prevent overfitting in scenarios with a high number of features. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets, including gene expression analysis and image recognition tasks, demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed methods. Specifically, our algorithm achieves a 2x-5x speedup in runtime compared to standard CCA implementations while maintaining comparable accuracy. The regularized CCA variant also exhibits improved performance in feature selection and prediction accuracy. This research contributes efficient and robust CCA techniques suitable for analyzing complex, high-dimensional data, opening new possibilities for data exploration and knowledge discovery in various domains.
Keywords: Canonical Correlation Analysis, High-Dimensional Data, Machine Learning, Feature Selection, Regularization, Optimization, Statistical Analysis.
