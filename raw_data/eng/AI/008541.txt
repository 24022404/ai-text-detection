Abstract:

The challenge of hierarchical disentanglement—decomposing complex data into structured, multi-level representations—remains central in machine learning and representation learning. This paper addresses the critical need for standardized evaluation by introducing a comprehensive suite of benchmarks, algorithms, and metrics specifically designed for hierarchical disentanglement tasks. We propose a set of novel synthetic and real-world benchmark datasets that capture varying degrees of hierarchical structure, serving as a robust testbed for algorithmic assessment. Furthermore, we develop and analyze new algorithms that exploit both supervised and unsupervised frameworks to extract hierarchical factors from high-dimensional data. To enable objective comparison and progress tracking, we define a family of quantitative metrics that measure the fidelity, independence, and interpretability of disentangled representations at multiple hierarchy levels. Experimental results demonstrate that our benchmarks and metrics can reliably distinguish between the capabilities of existing and newly proposed algorithms, highlighting both their strengths and limitations. Our framework provides a foundation for further research and development in hierarchical disentanglement, offering clear guidelines for evaluation and facilitating the advancement of transparent, interpretable machine learning systems. Keywords: hierarchical disentanglement, benchmarks, representation learning, interpretability, machine learning metrics, unsupervised learning, algorithm evaluation.
