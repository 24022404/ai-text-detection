This paper addresses the challenge of accurately demultiplexing color image sensor measurements, a critical step in reconstructing high-quality color images from raw sensor data. Traditional linear demultiplexing methods often struggle with noise and crosstalk, leading to suboptimal image quality. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel non-linear approach based on Random Forest modeling. Our method leverages the inherent non-linear relationships in sensor data to improve demultiplexing accuracy. We evaluate the proposed approach using a comprehensive dataset of color images, comparing its performance against conventional linear techniques. Results demonstrate significant improvements in color fidelity, noise reduction, and overall image quality, with our method achieving up to a 20% reduction in reconstruction error. The findings highlight the potential of machine learning-based non-linear modeling for advancing color image processing pipelines. This research contributes to the field by introducing a robust, data-driven solution that can be integrated into modern imaging systems, offering enhanced performance for applications in digital photography, medical imaging, and computer vision.  

**Keywords**: color image sensors, demultiplexing, Random Forest, non-linear modeling, image reconstruction, machine learning.
