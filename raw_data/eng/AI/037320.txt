This paper investigates the phenomenon of "blind spots" in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), a prevalent architectural choice in deep learning for image processing tasks. The research focuses on understanding how padding, a common technique used during the convolutional operations, may inadvertently lead to omissions or inaccuracies in the processing of visual information, subsequently affecting model performance.

Methods/Approach: We conduct a comprehensive analysis of CNN architectures, examining the role of padding and its influence on the receptive field of the networks. By systematically varying padding strategies and employing a series of controlled experiments, our study evaluates the impact on CNN performance across multiple benchmark image classification datasets. Advanced visualization techniques are utilized to identify and articulate regions of blind spots in trained models.

Results/Findings: Our findings reveal that padding, while essential for preserving input dimensions, can cause significant degradations in feature representation, leading to blind spots in output predictions. The experiments demonstrate that certain padding configurations lead to reduced accuracy in identifying specific visual patterns, which can be mitigated through alternative techniques such as dilated convolutions or carefully designed network architectures.

Conclusion/Implications: This research highlights a critical and often overlooked limitation in CNN design—blind spots induced by conventional padding methods. Our work underscores the importance of considering these effects when developing CNN-based solutions, particularly for applications requiring high precision in imagery analysis, such as autonomous driving and medical imaging. We propose guidelines for avoiding blind spots through optimized architectural design and improved padding strategies, contributing to more robust and reliable performance in CNN applications. Key Keywords: CNNs, padding, blind spots, image processing, deep learning.
