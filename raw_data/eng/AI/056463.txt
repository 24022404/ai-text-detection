This paper introduces SSD-GAN, a novel approach to measuring the realness of generated images in both spatial and spectral domains. The objective is to develop a more comprehensive evaluation metric for Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) that goes beyond traditional methods. Our approach combines spatial and spectral analysis to assess the realism of generated images, leveraging the strengths of both domains. The SSD-GAN framework utilizes a two-branch architecture, incorporating a spatial branch to evaluate texture and structure, and a spectral branch to examine frequency characteristics. Experimental results demonstrate that SSD-GAN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in distinguishing real from fake images, with improved accuracy and robustness. The proposed method has significant implications for applications such as image synthesis, data augmentation, and fake image detection, highlighting the importance of considering both spatial and spectral aspects in evaluating image realness. Key contributions include the development of a multi-domain evaluation metric and the demonstration of its effectiveness in improving GAN performance. Relevant keywords: GANs, image realness, spatial and spectral analysis, deep learning, image synthesis.
