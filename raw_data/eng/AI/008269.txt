This paper addresses the challenge of enabling autonomous agents to learn and execute complex soccer skills through reinforcement learning, with a focus on effective sim-to-real transfer for real-world competition. The objective is to bridge the gap between simulation environments and physical robot soccer, ensuring that strategies and controls learned in simulation can robustly adapt to the unpredictability of real-world conditions. To achieve this, we employ advanced deep reinforcement learning algorithms to train agents in high-fidelity simulated soccer environments. Key techniques, such as domain randomization and curriculum learning, are introduced to enhance the robustness and generalization of learned policies. The trained models are then transferred to physical robotic platforms, and their performance is evaluated against both simulated and human-operated teams in a series of real-world soccer matches. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly improves the adaptability and strategic behavior of soccer robots, enabling competitive performance under dynamic and partially observable conditions. The research contributes novel methodologies for sim-to-real transfer, minimizing the performance drop often seen when transitioning from simulation to reality. Our findings highlight the potential of reinforcement learning and transfer techniques in autonomous robotics and competitive sports settings. Keywords: reinforcement learning, sim-to-real transfer, robot soccer, deep learning, domain randomization, curriculum learning, autonomous agents.
