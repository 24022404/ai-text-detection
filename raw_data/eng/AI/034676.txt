This paper introduces the Deep Differentiable Simplex Layer (DDSL), a novel neural network layer designed for learning and processing geometric signals residing on simplicial complexes. The objective is to create a learnable layer that respects the inherent structure of simplicial complexes, enabling effective feature extraction and analysis on irregular geometric domains. The DDSL leverages differentiable operators to propagate signals across simplices of varying dimensions, allowing for the learning of complex relationships between vertices, edges, and higher-order faces. We demonstrate the effectiveness of the DDSL in several geometric learning tasks, including mesh denoising, shape classification, and signal reconstruction. Experimental results show that networks incorporating the DDSL achieve superior performance compared to traditional convolutional neural networks and other graph-based methods on simplicial complexes. The DDSL provides a powerful tool for geometric deep learning, offering improved accuracy and efficiency in processing geometric data. Our key contributions include the formulation of differentiable simplex operators and the demonstration of their utility in various applications. Keywords: Geometric Deep Learning, Simplicial Complexes, Differentiable Operators, Mesh Processing, Signal Processing.
