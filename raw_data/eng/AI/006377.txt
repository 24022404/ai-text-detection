This paper addresses the challenge of predicting crowd flow by modeling heterogeneous relations across multiple modes of transportation. The objective is to develop a predictive model that captures the complex interactions between different modes, such as pedestrian, vehicle, and public transit flows, to forecast potential crowd congestion. Our approach utilizes a graph-based framework, incorporating multiple layers to represent various modes and their interactions. We employ a novel attention mechanism to weigh the importance of different modes and relations, enabling the model to adapt to dynamic crowd flow patterns. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our model in predicting crowd flow, outperforming existing methods by capturing nuanced relationships between modes. The key findings highlight the importance of considering heterogeneous relations and mode interactions in crowd flow prediction. This research contributes to the development of intelligent transportation systems, providing insights for optimizing crowd management and urban planning. Our model has potential applications in smart cities, event management, and public safety, with keywords including crowd flow prediction, multimodal transportation, graph-based modeling, and attention mechanisms.
