This paper presents EgoNet, a novel deep learning framework designed for first person action-object detection. The objective is to accurately identify and classify objects being manipulated by the user in egocentric videos, enabling a wide range of applications in human-computer interaction, robotics, and augmented reality. Our approach leverages a multi-stream architecture that combines spatial and temporal features to model the complex relationships between the user's hands, objects, and actions. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of EgoNet, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets with significant improvements in accuracy and efficiency. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a new attention mechanism that focuses on the user's hands and objects, and a large-scale dataset of annotated egocentric videos. Our work has important implications for the development of intelligent systems that can understand and respond to human behavior, with potential applications in areas such as virtual reality, smart homes, and assistive technologies. Key keywords: first person vision, action-object detection, egocentric videos, deep learning, human-computer interaction.
