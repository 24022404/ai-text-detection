Neural networks play a crucial role in modern machine learning applications, yet their optimization processes are often plagued with challenges stemming from nondeterminism and instability. This paper aims to investigate the factors contributing to these issues and to propose methods to mitigate them, ensuring more reliable and consistent training outcomes.

Methods/Approach: We explore the optimization dynamics of neural networks by examining factors such as initial weight distributions, learning rate schedules, and random seed dependencies. Utilizing a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical experiments, we scrutinize the variabilities introduced during the training process. Our approach also involves comparing various optimization algorithms, including stochastic gradient descent (SGD) and its variants, with a focus on mitigating instabilities.

Results/Findings: Our study reveals significant insights into how minor changes in initial conditions can drastically affect model performance. Through extensive experimentation, we demonstrate that certain adjustments to the weight initialization and learning rate strategies can substantially reduce optimization variability. Our findings are validated across multiple neural network architectures and datasets, showing improved performance stability and reduced convergence times.

Conclusion/Implications: The research advances our understanding of the inherent challenges in neural network optimization related to nondeterminism and instability. By identifying key factors and proposing practical mitigation strategies, this study offers crucial contributions that can enhance the reliability of neural network applications. These insights are applicable across a diverse range of machine learning domains, from computer vision to natural language processing, paving the way for more predictable and robust model training processes.

Keywords: Neural networks, optimization, nondeterminism, instability, stochastic gradient descent, machine learning, model training.
