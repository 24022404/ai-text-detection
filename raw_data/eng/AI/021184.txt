**Abstract:**

Graph-structured data are increasingly prevalent across numerous domains in Information Technology, prompting the need for robust methods to analyze complex graphs. This paper introduces Graph Filtration Learning, a novel framework that integrates graph theory concepts with hierarchical feature extraction to improve the understanding and representation of graph data. The proposed approach utilizes filtration techniques, enabling the progressive simplification of complex graphs while preserving structural and topological information. We implement a graph neural network (GNN)-based model that adapts dynamically to filtration stages, effectively capturing multi-scale invariants and relationships within the data. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including social, biological, and citation networks, demonstrate that Graph Filtration Learning achieves significant improvements in classification accuracy and graph similarity tasks compared to existing methods. Our findings highlight enhanced robustness to noise and improved interpretability of the learned representations. This work contributes a scalable and generalizable methodology for extracting meaningful information from large-scale graph datasets, with potential applications in social network analysis, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems. **Keywords:** graph learning, filtration, graph neural networks, topological data analysis, representation learning.
