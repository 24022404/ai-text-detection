This paper presents a semi-supervised natural language approach for fine-grained classification of medical reports, addressing the challenge of accurate diagnosis and treatment planning. Our objective is to develop a model that can effectively classify medical reports into specific categories with limited labeled data. We propose a novel approach that combines the strengths of pre-trained language models and semi-supervised learning techniques, leveraging a large corpus of unlabeled medical reports to improve classification performance. Our method utilizes a transformer-based architecture and fine-tuning strategy to adapt to the medical domain. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in classification accuracy and F1-score compared to supervised baselines, particularly in cases with limited labeled data. The proposed approach has important implications for clinical decision support systems and medical informatics, enabling more accurate and efficient diagnosis and treatment planning. Key contributions include the development of a semi-supervised framework for fine-grained classification of medical reports, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in real-world clinical scenarios, with relevant keywords including natural language processing, semi-supervised learning, medical informatics, and clinical decision support systems.
