This paper investigates the concept of learnability within the framework of the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle, a powerful method for extracting relevant information from data while discarding noise. The objective of our research is to address fundamental questions about what makes a data representation learnable under the constraints imposed by the information bottleneck, and to determine which classes of functions or models can efficiently approximate optimal IB solutions. Our approach combines theoretical analysis with empirical validation, examining the behavior of machine learning models trained under the IB objective across various real-world datasets and synthetic scenarios. We introduce novel metrics for quantifying learnability with respect to compression and relevance, and propose algorithmic strategies to enhance the learnability of representations produced by IB methods. Experimental results demonstrate that our learnability criteria enable more stable and interpretable representations, improving both generalization and robustness compared to traditional models. The findings provide key insights into the interaction between information theory and practical learning systems, offering guidance for future architectures and algorithms that aim to balance information preservation with compression. Our contributions deepen the theoretical understanding of learnability in the context of the information bottleneck and provide practical tools for designing more effective and efficient data representations. Keywords: Information Bottleneck, Learnability, Representation Learning, Machine Learning, Generalization, Data Compression, Information Theory.
