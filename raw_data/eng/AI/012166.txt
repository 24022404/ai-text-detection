This paper introduces Task-Agnostic Morphology Evolution, a novel approach to evolving morphological structures that can adapt to various tasks without requiring task-specific training. The objective is to develop a methodology that enables the creation of versatile and efficient morphologies that can be applied across a range of applications, from robotics to computer-aided design. Our approach utilizes a combination of evolutionary algorithms and machine learning techniques to generate morphologies that can be easily fine-tuned for specific tasks. The results show that our method can produce morphologies that outperform task-specific designs in terms of adaptability and efficiency. Key findings include the ability to evolve morphologies that can be applied to multiple tasks, such as locomotion, manipulation, and sensing, with minimal modification. The implications of this research are significant, as it enables the creation of more versatile and efficient systems that can be applied to a wide range of applications, including robotics, prosthetics, and assistive technologies. The contributions of this work include the development of a task-agnostic morphology evolution framework, which can be used to generate innovative solutions for complex problems. Relevant keywords: morphology evolution, task-agnostic design, evolutionary algorithms, machine learning, adaptability, efficiency.
