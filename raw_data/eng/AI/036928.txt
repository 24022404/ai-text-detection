Pedestrian trajectory prediction is crucial for various applications, including autonomous driving and social robotics. This paper introduces a novel Context-Augmented Transformer Network (CATNet) to improve the accuracy and robustness of pedestrian trajectory prediction. CATNet integrates both local and global contextual information by employing a multi-head attention mechanism to capture complex interactions between pedestrians and their surrounding environment. Specifically, we incorporate scene context encoded through convolutional neural networks and social context derived from relative pedestrian positions into the transformer architecture. Experimental results on benchmark datasets, including ETH-UCY and Stanford Drone Dataset, demonstrate that CATNet outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of prediction accuracy and error rate. The proposed model exhibits superior performance in crowded scenes and complex interaction scenarios, suggesting its effectiveness in capturing the intricacies of pedestrian movement. Our findings highlight the importance of context awareness in trajectory prediction and demonstrate the potential of transformer networks for modeling pedestrian behavior.
Keywords: trajectory prediction, transformer networks, context awareness, pedestrian behavior, deep learning.
