Continual semantic segmentation aims to incrementally learn new classes without forgetting previously learned ones. However, catastrophic forgetting and adaptation to new environments remain significant challenges. This paper introduces a novel unsupervised model adaptation framework designed to mitigate these issues in continual semantic segmentation scenarios. Our approach leverages self-training with pseudo-labels generated from a teacher-student network to adapt the model to new, unlabeled data distributions while preserving previously learned knowledge. Specifically, we employ a confidence-aware pseudo-labeling strategy and a feature alignment technique to reduce the domain gap between the source and target data. We evaluate our framework on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing continual learning methods. The results show significant improvements in segmentation accuracy and a reduction in catastrophic forgetting, highlighting the effectiveness of our unsupervised adaptation approach. This work contributes to the advancement of robust and adaptable semantic segmentation models for real-world applications.

**Keywords:** Continual Learning, Semantic Segmentation, Unsupervised Domain Adaptation, Self-Training, Pseudo-Labeling, Deep Learning.
