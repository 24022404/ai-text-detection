Accurate monocular depth estimation is a fundamental problem in computer vision with numerous applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented reality. Traditional approaches often struggle with handling large variations in depth, resulting in suboptimal performance in complex scenes. In this paper, we propose AdaBins, a novel depth estimation framework that leverages adaptive binning to dynamically partition the depth range into variable-width intervals based on scene content. Our approach utilizes a deep neural network to predict both the width and the position of depth bins, enabling finer resolution where necessary and coarser segmentation where appropriate. We evaluate AdaBins on benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in depth estimation accuracy compared to state-of-the-art fixed-bin methods. The adaptive bin allocation leads to more precise boundary delineation and better handling of object discontinuities. In addition, our method maintains computational efficiency and is compatible with real-time processing constraints. Our findings establish AdaBins as an effective and scalable solution for monocular depth estimation, offering enhanced flexibility and generalization for diverse scene geometries. Keywords: depth estimation, adaptive bins, monocular vision, computer vision, deep learning.
