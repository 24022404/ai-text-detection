The rapid advancement of deep neural networks (DNNs) has highlighted the need for efficient and automated methods to optimize their architectures. This paper addresses the challenge of structure learning in DNNs by proposing a novel approach that leverages Q-learning, a reinforcement learning technique, to dynamically determine optimal network configurations. The objective is to reduce the manual effort and computational cost associated with traditional architecture design while improving model performance. Our method employs a Q-learning agent to iteratively explore and evaluate potential network structures, guided by a reward function based on validation accuracy and computational efficiency. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art manually designed and automated architectures. Key findings reveal that the Q-learning-based method not only identifies efficient network structures but also adapts to varying dataset complexities. The implications of this research extend to applications in resource-constrained environments, where automated and lightweight DNN architectures are critical. This work contributes to the growing field of neural architecture search by introducing a reinforcement learning-driven solution that balances performance and computational feasibility.  

**Keywords**: Deep Neural Networks, Structure Learning, Q-Learning, Reinforcement Learning, Neural Architecture Search, Automated Machine Learning.
