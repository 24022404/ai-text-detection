Self-supervised learning has emerged as a powerful approach in visual feature extraction, enabling deep neural networks to learn meaningful representations from unlabeled data. This survey paper systematically reviews advancements in self-supervised visual feature learning using deep neural networks, addressing the growing demand for scalable and efficient learning paradigms in the field of computer vision. We begin by outlining the limitations of supervised learning, such as reliance on large labeled datasets, and present the motivation for self-supervised techniques. The survey categorizes recent methods based on their core strategies, including pretext tasks, contrastive learning, and generative approaches. We analyze the architectural choices, loss functions, and evaluation protocols employed within these frameworks. A comparative analysis of self-supervised methods demonstrates significant progress in tasks like image classification, object detection, and segmentation, achieving performance increasingly competitive with fully supervised counterparts. The paper also discusses the strengths and challenges of current self-supervised algorithms, including generalization, transfer learning, and scalability. Finally, we highlight open research directions and real-world applications, underscoring the potential of self-supervised learning to drive future breakthroughs in computer vision. Key keywords: self-supervised learning, visual features, deep neural networks, contrastive learning, computer vision, representation learning, pretext tasks.
