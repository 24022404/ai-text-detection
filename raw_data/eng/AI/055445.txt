The accurate classification of crops and weeds is critical for precision agriculture, enabling targeted weed control and reducing herbicide usage. This study addresses the challenge of crop-weed classification by leveraging supervised learning techniques combined with color and texture features extracted from agricultural imagery. A dataset of high-resolution images was collected, capturing diverse crop and weed species under varying environmental conditions. Color features were derived using RGB and HSV color spaces, while texture features were extracted using Gray-Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM) and Local Binary Patterns (LBP). These features were then used to train and evaluate several supervised learning models, including Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest, and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN). Experimental results demonstrate that the combination of color and texture features significantly improves classification accuracy compared to using either feature type alone. The Random Forest model achieved the highest performance, with an accuracy of 94.7%, outperforming traditional methods. The proposed approach offers a robust and scalable solution for real-time crop-weed discrimination, with potential applications in autonomous weeding systems and precision farming. This research contributes to the advancement of agricultural technology by providing a cost-effective and environmentally friendly alternative to conventional weed management practices.  

**Keywords**: crop-weed classification, supervised learning, color features, texture features, precision agriculture, Random Forest, image processing.
