Semantic segmentation of urban scenes is crucial for applications such as autonomous driving and urban planning. However, the performance of deep learning models trained on synthetic datasets often degrades when applied to real-world images due to domain shift. This paper introduces a novel curriculum domain adaptation approach to bridge this gap. Our method incrementally adapts a model trained on synthetic data to real-world data by employing a curriculum learning strategy. We leverage a self-training framework where pseudo-labels are generated for the target domain and progressively refined based on a difficulty measure. Specifically, we prioritize adapting to easily segmentable regions before tackling more complex areas, thereby mitigating the impact of noisy pseudo-labels during early stages of adaptation. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing domain adaptation techniques in terms of segmentation accuracy. Furthermore, ablation studies validate the effectiveness of the proposed curriculum learning strategy and the impact of different difficulty measures. This work provides a practical and effective solution for semantic segmentation in real-world urban environments, enhancing the applicability of deep learning models trained on synthetic data.

**Keywords:** Semantic Segmentation, Domain Adaptation, Curriculum Learning, Urban Scenes, Self-Training, Deep Learning.
