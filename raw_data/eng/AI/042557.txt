This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing sequential decision-making processes under uncertainty, leveraging the principles of free energy minimization. The objective is to derive a generalized framework for decision-making that integrates probabilistic reasoning and optimization to enhance computational efficiency and decision accuracy. We propose a novel approach based on the free energy principle, which extends traditional optimality equations to account for complex, dynamic environments. Our method employs variational inference techniques to approximate posterior distributions, enabling scalable and adaptive decision-making strategies. Through extensive simulations, we demonstrate that the proposed framework outperforms existing methods in terms of both computational efficiency and decision quality, particularly in high-dimensional and uncertain scenarios. The results highlight the potential of free energy-based approaches to bridge the gap between theoretical optimality and practical applicability in sequential decision-making tasks. This work contributes to the advancement of decision-making algorithms in artificial intelligence, robotics, and autonomous systems, offering a robust and generalizable solution for real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: free energy, sequential decision-making, optimality equations, variational inference, probabilistic reasoning, adaptive systems.
