This paper addresses the challenge of session-based recommendation in dynamic environments, where user preferences and item popularity evolve rapidly. Our objective is to develop a novel reinforcement learning approach that captures the distributional nature of user behavior and incorporates batch constraints to improve recommendation accuracy. We propose a batch-constrained distributional reinforcement learning framework, which leverages a distributional critic to estimate the expected return of each action and a batch-constrained actor to generate recommendations that balance exploration and exploitation. Our approach utilizes a transformer-based architecture to model complex user-item interactions and incorporates techniques such as fine-tuning and regularization to enhance performance. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in terms of precision, recall, and F1-score, particularly in scenarios with high user churn and item cold-start. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of batch constraints to distributional reinforcement learning and the application of transformer-based architectures to session-based recommendation. Our work has significant implications for the development of more effective and adaptive recommendation systems, with potential applications in e-commerce, content streaming, and social media platforms. Key keywords: distributional reinforcement learning, session-based recommendation, transformer, batch constraints, fine-tuning.
