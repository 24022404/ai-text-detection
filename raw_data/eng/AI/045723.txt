This study aims to investigate the efficacy of various statistical skin detection algorithms on sub-continental human images, addressing the challenge of accurate skin tone classification in diverse populations. A comparative analysis is conducted using multiple algorithms, including Gaussian Mixture Models, Bayesian Classification, and K-Means Clustering, to evaluate their performance on a dataset of images from different sub-continental regions. The results show that the Bayesian Classification approach outperforms other methods, achieving an accuracy of 92.5% in detecting skin tones. The study highlights the importance of considering sub-continental variations in skin tone distribution and demonstrates the potential of statistical skin detection algorithms in applications such as face recognition, image segmentation, and human-computer interaction. The findings contribute to the development of more robust and inclusive computer vision systems, with implications for fields like artificial intelligence, machine learning, and image processing. Key keywords: skin detection, statistical algorithms, sub-continental human images, computer vision, image processing, Bayesian classification.
