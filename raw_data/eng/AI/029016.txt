This paper addresses the problem of regulating a class of continuous-time nonlinear dynamical systems with unknown dynamics, leveraging the spectral properties of the Koopman operator. We propose a novel Koopman Spectrum Nonlinear Regulator (KSNR) that approximates the evolution of nonlinear state observables via a finite-dimensional spectral decomposition. By embedding the system into a lifted feature space and learning the Koopman spectrum online, KSNR adapts to previously unseen dynamics without requiring an explicit model. 

Our approach combines spectral system identification with an online convex optimization routine to update control policies in real time. At each time step, we solve a convex program over a family of stabilizing feedback gains defined in the Koopman basis; we then refine the Koopman eigenfunctions and corresponding gains via a gradient-based bandit algorithm. We prove that KSNR achieves sublinear regret O(√T) relative to the best fixed nonlinear regulator in hindsight, under mild observability and Lipschitz assumptions.

Empirical evaluations on benchmark tasks—including the inverted pendulum and forced Van der Pol oscillator—demonstrate that KSNR converges rapidly to near-optimal regulation performance, significantly outperforming model-free baselines in sample efficiency. Our results establish a new paradigm for combining Koopman spectral learning with provable online control, offering a scalable framework for adaptive regulation of complex nonlinear systems.  

Keywords: Koopman operator, nonlinear control, online learning, regret analysis, spectral methods.
