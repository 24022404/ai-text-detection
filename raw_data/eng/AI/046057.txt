This study aims to enhance the accuracy of predicting county-level corn yields, an essential task for optimizing agricultural productivity and resource management. Methods: We employed deep learning techniques, specifically Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) models, to analyze temporal dependencies and patterns in large-scale agricultural datasets. The LSTM models were trained using historical weather data, soil characteristics, and corn yield records from multiple counties across different growing seasons. Results: The proposed LSTM model demonstrated superior predictive performance compared to traditional statistical methods and other machine learning models, achieving higher accuracy and robustness in capturing yield fluctuations. Experiments also showed that our model could adapt to diverse environmental conditions by effectively integrating heterogeneous data sources. Conclusion: This research provides a significant advancement in agricultural yield forecasting by leveraging the capabilities of deep LSTM networks. The outcomes not only improve the accuracy of corn yield predictions at the county level but also offer a scalable approach that can be applied to other crops and regions. These findings have the potential to inform policy-making, guide agricultural practices, and support decision-making in agribusinesses. Keywords: corn yield prediction, LSTM, deep learning, agricultural forecasting, temporal analysis.
