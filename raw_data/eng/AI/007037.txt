Human attribute recognition plays a crucial role in various applications, such as surveillance and person re-identification. This research aims to enhance the accuracy of human attribute recognition by introducing a novel distraction-aware feature learning approach that addresses the common issue of irrelevant background information interfering with recognition tasks.

Methods/Approach: We propose a coarse-to-fine attention mechanism that incorporates distraction-aware feature learning to improve human attribute recognition accuracy. This method employs an initial coarse attention stage to filter out distracting background elements, followed by a fine-grained attention process that focuses on critical attribute details. By adopting a hierarchical attention framework, the model effectively identifies and differentiates useful features from distractions, improving the overall recognition performance.

Results/Findings: Experiments conducted on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in human attribute recognition. The model achieves superior accuracy in recognizing complex attributes under various conditions, highlighting its robustness against noisy and cluttered backgrounds. Comparative analyses also indicate a notable improvement in system performance compared to methods lacking distraction-awareness.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field of human attribute recognition by introducing a novel distraction-aware framework that effectively enhances recognition accuracy through a hierarchical attention mechanism. The implications of this work are substantial for real-world applications where precise human attribute identification is critical, such as intelligent surveillance systems and advanced human-computer interaction interfaces. Future work may explore the adaptation of this approach to other recognition tasks involving complex environmental distractions.

Keywords: Human attribute recognition, distraction-aware feature learning, coarse-to-fine attention, hierarchical attention mechanism, surveillance, person re-identification.
