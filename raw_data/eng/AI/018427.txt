Modern compilers rely heavily on heuristic-based optimization techniques, which often fail to generalize across diverse hardware architectures and software workloads. This paper introduces a novel approach to static neural compiler optimization using deep reinforcement learning (DRL). The objective is to automate and enhance the process of compiler optimization by learning optimal strategies for code transformation and instruction scheduling. We propose a DRL-based framework that integrates a neural network with a compiler's intermediate representation (IR) to dynamically adapt optimization decisions. The model is trained on a diverse set of benchmarks, enabling it to generalize across different hardware targets and application domains. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms traditional heuristic-based methods, achieving significant improvements in execution time and resource utilization. Specifically, the proposed method reduces runtime by up to 15% compared to state-of-the-art compilers. The findings highlight the potential of DRL in bridging the gap between static compiler optimization and dynamic performance tuning. This research contributes to the growing field of AI-driven compiler design, offering a scalable and adaptive solution for modern computing environments.  

**Keywords**: deep reinforcement learning, compiler optimization, static analysis, neural networks, code transformation, instruction scheduling.
