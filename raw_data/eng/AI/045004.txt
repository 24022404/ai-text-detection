This paper addresses the challenge of deep face recognition by proposing a novel approach that disentangles appearance and geometry. The objective is to improve the accuracy and robustness of face recognition systems by separately modeling the appearance and geometric attributes of faces. Our method employs a multi-task learning framework, utilizing a convolutional neural network (CNN) to extract appearance features and a geometric module to capture 3D face structure. The results show that our approach significantly outperforms state-of-the-art face recognition systems, achieving a higher verification accuracy and lower error rate. The key findings indicate that disentangling appearance and geometry leads to more robust and generalizable face representations. This research contributes to the field of computer vision and face recognition, with potential applications in security, surveillance, and identity verification. The novelty of our approach lies in its ability to effectively separate and model the intertwined factors of appearance and geometry, leading to improved face recognition performance. Key keywords: deep face recognition, disentangling, appearance, geometry, convolutional neural networks, computer vision, face verification.
