Few-shot object detection (FSOD) aims to detect novel objects with limited labeled examples, a significant challenge for real-world applications. Existing FSOD methods often struggle with generalization to unseen categories and incremental learning scenarios. This paper introduces a novel framework, addressing both the generalization capability and the incremental adaptability of FSOD. We propose a meta-learning approach that learns to extract more robust and transferable features across different tasks and categories. Furthermore, we incorporate an incremental learning strategy that allows the model to adapt to new categories continuously without catastrophic forgetting. The proposed method utilizes a dynamic prototype generation module and an attention-based feature refinement network. Experiments on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves superior performance compared to existing FSOD methods in both generalized few-shot detection and incremental few-shot learning scenarios. Specifically, we observe significant improvements in detecting novel objects with fewer training examples and a reduced performance degradation when new classes are added incrementally. These results highlight the potential of our approach to enable more practical and adaptable object detection systems.

Keywords: Few-shot object detection, incremental learning, meta-learning, object detection, computer vision, deep learning.
