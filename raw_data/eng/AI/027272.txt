This paper proposes a novel deep learning approach for high-quality image inpainting, leveraging a Pyramid-Context Encoder Network (PCEN) to effectively restore missing regions in images. The objective is to develop an efficient and robust model that can handle complex scenes and varying levels of damage. Our approach employs a multi-scale pyramid structure to capture contextual information at different resolutions, combined with a context encoder to learn long-range dependencies and semantic features. Experimental results demonstrate that our PCEN model outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of visual quality and quantitative metrics, such as peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM). The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a pyramid-context encoding scheme, which enables the model to better capture and propagate contextual information, and the development of a robust training strategy that adapts to various image datasets. Our work has significant implications for applications in image editing, restoration, and generation, and highlights the potential of deep learning techniques for high-quality image inpainting. Key keywords: image inpainting, deep learning, Pyramid-Context Encoder Network, PCEN, computer vision, image restoration.
