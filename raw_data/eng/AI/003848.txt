This paper addresses the challenge of decomposing complex 3D scenes into individual objects without relying on labeled data, a critical task for applications in robotics, augmented reality, and autonomous systems. We propose an unsupervised volume segmentation approach that leverages advanced neural network architectures to identify and separate objects within 3D volumetric data. Our method combines spatial feature extraction with clustering techniques to autonomously detect object boundaries and segment scenes into meaningful components. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive performance compared to supervised methods, with significant improvements in segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency. The proposed framework is robust to varying scene complexities and scales effectively to large datasets. This work contributes a novel, unsupervised solution to 3D scene decomposition, offering a scalable and practical alternative to traditional supervised techniques. The implications of this research extend to applications in 3D reconstruction, scene understanding, and object manipulation, where labeled data is often scarce or unavailable.  

**Keywords**: 3D scene decomposition, unsupervised learning, volume segmentation, neural networks, object detection, 3D reconstruction.
