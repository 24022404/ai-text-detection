Video understanding, traditionally addressed with complex 3D convolutional neural networks or recurrent models, often suffers from high computational costs and limited scalability. This paper investigates the surprising effectiveness of using a simple 2D image classifier for video understanding tasks. Our approach leverages a pre-trained image classification model and applies it frame-by-frame to video sequences. We aggregate the frame-level predictions to generate a video-level classification. Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets such as UCF101 and HMDB51, we demonstrate that our image classifier-based approach achieves competitive or even superior performance compared to more complex video-specific architectures, while significantly reducing computational overhead. The findings suggest that temporal information, while important, might be implicitly captured by individual frames to a greater extent than previously assumed. This work provides a computationally efficient and surprisingly accurate alternative for video understanding, paving the way for deploying video analysis in resource-constrained environments. Keywords: video understanding, image classification, convolutional neural networks, action recognition, deep learning, efficiency.
