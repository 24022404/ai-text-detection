Few-shot learning aims to enable machine learning models to quickly adapt to new tasks with limited labeled data, a capability vital for practical applications where data collection is challenging. This paper addresses the challenge by introducing a meta-learning framework for automated neural architecture search tailored specifically for few-shot learning scenarios. Our approach leverages a meta-learner that jointly optimizes both the architecture and the parameters of neural networks across a diverse set of tasks, encouraging the discovery of architectures with strong generalization ability. We employ an efficient search strategy that considers task-specific adaptation while mitigating computational overhead, making the method scalable to complex datasets. Experimental results on standard few-shot classification benchmarks demonstrate that our meta-learned architectures consistently outperform manually designed baselines and previous architecture search methods, in terms of both accuracy and adaptation speed. The proposed framework significantly advances few-shot learning performance without requiring extensive computational resources, highlighting its potential for real-world deployment where rapid learning from limited data is essential. Keywords: meta-learning, neural architecture search, few-shot learning, automated machine learning, task adaptation.
