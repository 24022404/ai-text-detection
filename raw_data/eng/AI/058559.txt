This paper presents a novel approach to deriving dimension-free generalization bounds for non-linear metric learning. The objective is to establish a theoretical framework that enables the analysis of metric learning algorithms beyond traditional linear settings. We employ a combination of techniques from functional analysis and statistical learning theory to develop a generalization bound that is independent of the dimensionality of the data. Our approach utilizes a non-linear metric learning model, leveraging the strengths of neural networks to learn complex representations. The key findings indicate that our bounds are tighter than existing results, providing a more accurate estimate of the generalization error. The implications of this research are significant, as it enables the application of metric learning to high-dimensional data without the need for explicit dimensionality reduction. Our contributions include the introduction of a new theoretical framework, which has the potential to improve the performance of various machine learning tasks, such as clustering, classification, and recommendation systems. The novelty of our approach lies in its ability to generalize to non-linear settings, making it a valuable tool for real-world applications where data is often complex and high-dimensional. Key keywords: metric learning, non-linear learning, dimension-free generalization bounds, neural networks, statistical learning theory.
