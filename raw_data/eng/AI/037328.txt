Image segmentation is a fundamental task in computer vision, yet achieving robust and accurate results remains challenging due to the inherent ambiguity and complexity of natural images. This paper introduces a novel approach to image segmentation that leverages the diversity of multiple segmentation hypotheses to improve overall performance. Our method generates an ensemble of diverse segmentations using varied parameters and algorithms, effectively capturing different perspectives on the image structure. We then propose a diversity-aware fusion strategy based on a learned weighting scheme, which adaptively combines the individual segmentations based on their quality and complementarity. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms state-of-the-art segmentation methods, particularly in challenging scenarios with complex object boundaries and cluttered backgrounds. The proposed technique offers a robust and effective framework for exploiting diversity in image segmentation, leading to improved accuracy and reliability. Keywords: Image Segmentation, Ensemble Learning, Diversity, Fusion, Computer Vision.
