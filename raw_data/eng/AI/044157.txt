Image inpainting, the task of reconstructing missing or damaged regions in images, remains a challenging problem in computer vision. This paper introduces a novel approach leveraging a hierarchical Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) to generate diverse and structurally coherent inpainted results. Unlike traditional methods that often produce repetitive or overly smooth outputs, our model emphasizes diversity in structure while maintaining high visual fidelity. The proposed hierarchical VQ-VAE architecture captures multi-scale features, enabling the model to learn both global and local structural patterns effectively. By incorporating a diversity-promoting loss function, the approach ensures a wide range of plausible reconstructions for the same input. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing techniques in terms of structural diversity and perceptual quality. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance in generating realistic and varied inpainted images, making it suitable for applications in photo editing, restoration, and content creation. This work contributes to advancing image inpainting by addressing the limitations of structural diversity, offering a robust solution for real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Image inpainting, VQ-VAE, hierarchical modeling, structural diversity, computer vision, generative models.
