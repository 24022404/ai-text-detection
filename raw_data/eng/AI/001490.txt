This paper investigates distributed learning and inference in resource-constrained environments, focusing on the impact of image compression on the performance of machine learning models. We address the challenge of transmitting large image datasets across networks with limited bandwidth by exploring various image compression techniques, including JPEG, JPEG 2000, and WebP, within a distributed learning framework. Our approach involves training deep learning models, specifically convolutional neural networks, on compressed image data distributed across multiple edge devices, followed by aggregating the learned parameters at a central server for global inference. We evaluate the trade-offs between compression ratio, communication efficiency, and model accuracy on benchmark datasets such as CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. Results demonstrate that judicious selection of compression algorithms and parameters can significantly reduce communication costs while maintaining acceptable levels of accuracy. Furthermore, we propose a novel adaptive compression strategy that dynamically adjusts compression levels based on network conditions and the sensitivity of image regions to minimize information loss relevant to the learning task. The findings offer practical guidelines for designing efficient distributed learning systems for image-based applications in IoT, mobile computing, and remote sensing.

Keywords: Distributed Learning, Image Compression, Edge Computing, Deep Learning, Communication Efficiency, Inference, Adaptive Compression.
