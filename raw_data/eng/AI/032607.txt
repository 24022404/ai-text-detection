This research addresses the challenge of efficient and accurate image segmentation, a critical task in computer vision, by leveraging a novel approach that combines the watershed transformation with size-dependent single linkage clustering. The study aims to improve segmentation quality by effectively delineating image regions based on their spatial properties.

Methods/Approach: Our approach begins with the construction of a watershed basin graph that represents the catchment basins of an image. We introduce a size-dependent single linkage clustering algorithm applied to the graph, which accounts for the size of image segments to achieve precise segmentation. This method dynamically adjusts based on region size, in contrast to traditional approaches that may overlook smaller structures or merge disparate regions prematurely.

Results/Findings: The proposed methodology demonstrates significant improvements in segmentation accuracy compared to classical watershed and clustering methods. Experimentation on various benchmark datasets reveals that our technique effectively distinguishes between regions of differing sizes with minimal over-segmentation. The approach efficiently processes complex images, leading to better-defined boundaries and higher segment coherence.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of size-dependent single linkage clustering with watershed basin graphs offers a valuable advancement in image segmentation techniques. Our method's ability to adaptively handle different scales of image features provides a robust solution for applications in medical imaging, remote sensing, and automated inspection systems. The research presents a step forward in achieving more refined image analysis and opens pathways for future exploration in multi-scale segmentation strategies.

Keywords: Image Segmentation, Watershed Transformation, Single Linkage Clustering, Size-Dependent Clustering, Computer Vision, Image Analysis.
