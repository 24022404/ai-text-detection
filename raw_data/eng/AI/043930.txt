This paper introduces Wasserstein Dictionary Learning (WDL), a novel unsupervised non-linear dictionary learning framework leveraging optimal transport (OT) theory. WDL aims to learn a dictionary of representative atoms and their corresponding non-linear encoding from unlabeled data by minimizing the Wasserstein distance between the data distribution and the distribution generated by the learned dictionary. We propose an efficient alternating optimization algorithm to solve the challenging non-convex problem, alternately updating the dictionary atoms and the transport-based encoding coefficients. The algorithm's convergence is analyzed, and its effectiveness is demonstrated through extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets. The results show that WDL outperforms existing dictionary learning methods in terms of reconstruction accuracy and data representation quality, particularly in scenarios with complex, non-linear data structures. The learned dictionaries exhibit improved interpretability and generalization capabilities, opening avenues for applications in clustering, dimensionality reduction, and data generation. Keywords: Dictionary Learning, Optimal Transport, Wasserstein Distance, Unsupervised Learning, Non-linear Representation.
