This paper addresses the challenge of online learning in environments where feedback graphs are uncertain, proposing a novel approach to adapt to such ambiguity. Our objective is to develop an efficient algorithm that can learn from uncertain feedback graphs, where the relationships between nodes are not precisely defined. We employ a probabilistic graph modeling technique, combined with an online learning framework, to handle the uncertainty in the feedback graphs. Our method utilizes a Bayesian update rule to incorporate new information and update the model parameters. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with high uncertainty. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of accounting for uncertainty in feedback graphs and demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed method in real-world applications, such as recommendation systems and social networks. Our contributions include a novel online learning algorithm, uncertainty-aware graph modeling, and empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets, making this work relevant to researchers and practitioners in the fields of machine learning, graph theory, and online learning, with key keywords including online learning, uncertain feedback graphs, probabilistic graph modeling, and Bayesian inference.
