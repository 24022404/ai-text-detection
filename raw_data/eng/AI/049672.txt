Few-shot object detection (FSOD) aims to detect novel objects with limited labeled data, a critical challenge in real-world applications where data scarcity is common. While existing FSOD methods have shown promise, they often struggle with generalization across diverse domains and incremental learning scenarios. This paper proposes a novel framework for generalized and incremental few-shot object detection, addressing these limitations. Our approach integrates a meta-learning strategy with a dynamic feature adaptation mechanism, enabling the model to generalize across unseen domains and adapt to new classes incrementally without catastrophic forgetting. We introduce a lightweight yet effective memory module to retain knowledge of previously learned classes while efficiently incorporating new information. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art FSOD approaches in both cross-domain generalization and incremental learning settings. The proposed framework achieves significant improvements in mean average precision (mAP) while maintaining computational efficiency. Our findings highlight the potential of this approach for real-world applications, such as autonomous driving and robotics, where adaptability and generalization are crucial. This work contributes to advancing the field of FSOD by providing a robust and scalable solution for generalized and incremental learning scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Few-shot object detection, meta-learning, incremental learning, domain generalization, memory module, adaptability.
