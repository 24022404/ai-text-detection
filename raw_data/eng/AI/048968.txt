The objective of this research is to address the challenges associated with transfer learning, particularly the computational inefficiencies and overfitting concerns in adapting pre-trained models to new tasks. We introduce SMILE, a novel method leveraging Self-Distilled MIxup, to enhance the efficiency and robustness of transfer learning processes.

Methods/Approach: SMILE integrates self-distillation, an iterative process where a neural network serves as its teacher for subsequent learning, with mixup, a data augmentation technique that synthesizes new training samples through linear interpolations of existing data points. By combining these two approaches, SMILE facilitates a smoother transition of pre-trained models to new domains with limited labeled data.

Results/Findings: Our experiments across several benchmark datasets demonstrate that SMILE significantly improves the performance and stability of transfer learning models. Compared to traditional fine-tuning methods, SMILE achieves higher accuracy and reduced overfitting, while also requiring less computational resources. Notably, SMILE excels in scenarios with limited data availability, showcasing its potential for practical applications in resource-constrained settings.

Conclusion/Implications: SMILE offers a promising advancement in the field of transfer learning, providing a more effective and efficient means of adapting models to new tasks. This approach not only enhances performance but also broadens the applicability of pre-trained models to diverse domains. The innovations presented by SMILE have the potential to influence future research and development in machine learning, particularly in contexts where computational and data resources are limited.

Keywords: Transfer Learning, Self-Distillation, Mixup, Data Augmentation, Machine Learning, Efficiency, Robustness, Model Adaptation.
