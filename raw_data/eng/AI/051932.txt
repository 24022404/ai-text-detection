**Abstract:**

Understanding the decision-making process of deep learning models for 3D point cloud data is essential for their adoption in safety-critical and real-world applications. This paper presents a novel approach for generating interpretable explanations of 3D point cloud model predictions using gradient-based methods. We first define a framework that adapts established interpretability techniques—such as saliency mapping and gradient-weighted feature attribution—to the unique structure and high-dimensionality of point cloud data. Through comprehensive experiments on benchmark 3D object classification datasets, we demonstrate that our method can successfully identify influential points and geometric regions that drive model decisions. Compared to traditional black-box and occlusion-based explainability approaches, our gradient-based explanations offer a significant improvement in both computational efficiency and granularity of insight. Furthermore, we examine the robustness of our explanations in the presence of noise and adversarial perturbations, highlighting their reliability and practical value. Our findings advance the interpretability of 3D neural networks, paving the way for more transparent and trustworthy AI systems in robotics, autonomous vehicles, and augmented reality. Key contributions include the adaptation of gradient-based techniques to point clouds, a thorough evaluation on standard datasets, and analysis of explainability reliability. Keywords: 3D Point Clouds, Explainability, Gradient-Based Methods, Saliency Maps, Deep Learning, Model Interpretability, Feature Attribution.
