In the domain of affective computing, the ability to accurately interpret human emotions from wearable data is a significant challenge due to the diversity and complexity of signals across different modalities and datasets. This paper addresses the problem of robust emotion recognition by exploring unsupervised multi-modal representation learning. We propose an innovative framework that leverages multi-corpus wearable data to enhance the understanding of affective states. The approach utilizes advanced unsupervised learning algorithms to process and integrate data from various sensors, including physiological and inertial measurement units, without the need for labeled training samples. By employing a novel dimensionality reduction technique paired with multi-modal fusion strategies, our method captures intricate correlations across modalities, significantly improving the reliability of emotion predictions. Our experimental results, conducted on several comprehensive and varied datasets, demonstrate superior performance in terms of accuracy and generalization when compared to existing supervised and semi-supervised methods. This work contributes a scalable and flexible solution for affective computing applications, offering meaningful insights for healthcare, human-computer interaction, and personal well-being monitoring. Key innovations include the scalable integration of heterogeneous data sources and the advancement of autonomous learning capabilities, positioning this research at the forefront of affective multi-modal analysis. The implications of this study extend to potential applications in personalized emotional analytics and real-time emotion tracking through low-cost wearable technologies.
