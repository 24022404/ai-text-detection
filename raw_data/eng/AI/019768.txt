Graph generation is a fundamental challenge in information technology, with applications in chemistry, social network analysis, and circuit design. This paper introduces GraphVAE, a novel approach for the generation of small graphs utilizing the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) framework. The objective is to learn robust latent representations capable of capturing the complex structure and diversity of graph data, enabling the effective generation of valid and meaningful new graphs. Our method encodes graphs into continuous latent spaces and employs deep neural networks to reconstruct adjacency and feature matrices, allowing seamless generation and interpolation. Experimental results on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that GraphVAE achieves superior performance in generating small graphs when compared to traditional random graph models and baseline generative neural networks, with improvements in validity, uniqueness, and diversity of generated structures. The findings suggest that the GraphVAE model is effective for structured data generation and opens avenues for its application in molecular design, network synthesis, and automated reasoning. Key contributions include a scalable end-to-end framework, novel training techniques for handling permutation invariance, and comprehensive evaluations of graph quality metrics. Keywords: graph generation, variational autoencoder, deep generative models, structured data, small graphs, machine learning.
