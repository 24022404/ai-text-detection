Abstract:

Detecting human actions accurately in videos is a critical challenge in computer vision, with applications ranging from surveillance to human-computer interaction. This paper proposes a novel approach, named the Three Branches architecture, for action detection that leverages richer and more diverse feature representations. The method employs a multi-branch neural network structure, where each branch is specialized to extract complementary information: spatial appearance, temporal motion, and semantic context. Features from all branches are dynamically fused to form a unified and discriminative representation of actions. Extensive experiments are conducted on well-known action recognition benchmarks, demonstrating that the Three Branches model achieves superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods. Results show significant improvements in both detection accuracy and robustness to challenging scenarios such as occlusion and background clutter. The proposed framework highlights the importance of multi-faceted feature extraction and offers a scalable solution that can be integrated into existing video analysis pipelines. Our findings suggest that joint modeling of spatial, temporal, and semantic cues is vital for advancing action detection technology. Keywords: action detection, video analysis, feature fusion, multi-branch neural network, spatial-temporal modeling, computer vision.
