Abstract:

Understanding the underlying structure of complex networks is fundamental in various domains such as social media, biological systems, and information technology. A prevalent property observed in many real-world networks is the scale-free nature, characterized by a power-law degree distribution. This paper presents a novel method for learning scale-free networks by introducing a dynamic node-specific degree prior within network inference algorithms. Unlike traditional approaches that enforce a uniform global degree constraint, our methodology adapts the degree prior dynamically for each node, allowing for heterogeneity reflective of realistic scale-free characteristics. We develop an efficient optimization framework that integrates the dynamic prior into the network learning process, enabling accurate recovery of both global and local topological structures. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in reconstructing networks with scale-free properties, achieving higher accuracy in degree distribution and improved robustness to noise. The proposed framework is scalable and can be readily applied to large-scale network data. Our contributions advance the state-of-the-art in network learning by providing a more flexible and effective mechanism for modeling heterogeneous node connectivity. Keywords: scale-free networks, network inference, node-specific prior, degree distribution, optimization, complex systems, graph learning.
