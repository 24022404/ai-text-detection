Image-to-image translation is a fundamental task in computer vision, aiming to map images from one domain to another while preserving essential content. This paper introduces a novel approach leveraging Group-wise Deep Whitening-and-Coloring Transformation (GDWCT) to enhance the quality and flexibility of image translation. The proposed method addresses the limitations of existing techniques by explicitly modeling the statistical relationships between source and target domains through group-wise transformations. GDWCT decomposes the image features into groups, applies whitening to decorrelate features within each group, and then recolors them to align with the target domain. This process ensures more accurate and semantically consistent translations. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that GDWCT outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of visual quality, domain alignment, and computational efficiency. The approach is particularly effective for tasks requiring fine-grained control over style and content, such as artistic style transfer and domain adaptation. The results highlight the potential of GDWCT as a robust framework for image-to-image translation, offering significant improvements in both performance and interpretability. This work contributes to advancing the field by providing a scalable and efficient solution for complex image translation tasks.  

**Keywords**: Image-to-image translation, whitening-and-coloring transformation, domain adaptation, style transfer, deep learning, computer vision.
