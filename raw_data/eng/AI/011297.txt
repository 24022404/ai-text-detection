Extreme weather events pose significant challenges to global safety, infrastructure, and ecosystems, necessitating advanced tools for their detection and analysis. This paper introduces *ExtremeWeather*, a large-scale climate dataset designed to facilitate semi-supervised detection, localization, and understanding of extreme weather phenomena. The dataset comprises high-resolution satellite imagery, meteorological data, and annotated labels for events such as hurricanes, floods, and heatwaves, covering diverse geographical regions and temporal scales. Leveraging a semi-supervised learning framework, we propose a novel deep learning model that combines convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with graph-based representations to enhance spatial and temporal feature extraction. Our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in event detection and localization, outperforming existing methods by 12% in precision and 8% in recall. Additionally, the model provides interpretable insights into the underlying meteorological patterns driving extreme weather, enabling better understanding and prediction. The *ExtremeWeather* dataset and proposed framework offer significant contributions to climate science, disaster management, and AI-driven environmental monitoring, paving the way for more resilient and adaptive systems in the face of climate change.  

**Keywords**: extreme weather, semi-supervised learning, climate dataset, deep learning, event detection, localization, satellite imagery.
