This paper investigates the phenomenon of benign overfitting in the context of ridge function estimation, a fundamental problem in statistical learning and high-dimensional data analysis. Specifically, we provide a finite sample analysis to understand under what conditions over-parameterized ridge regression models can achieve low generalization error despite perfectly fitting noisy training data. Our approach leverages theoretical tools from empirical process theory and high-dimensional probability to derive non-asymptotic error bounds for the estimation of ridge functions. We analyze the effects of sample size, noise variance, model complexity, and regularization parameter on the generalization performance, highlighting the regimes where benign overfitting occurs. Experimental results on synthetic datasets validate our theoretical findings, demonstrating that certain over-parameterized models preserve statistical efficiency and avoid the pitfalls of classical overfitting. This work advances the understanding of the interplay between model complexity and generalization in modern machine learning and offers practical insights for designing algorithms that harness over-parameterization in high-dimensional settings. Keywords: benign overfitting, ridge regression, finite sample analysis, high-dimensional statistics, generalization error, over-parameterization.
