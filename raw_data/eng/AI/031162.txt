This paper presents a novel approach to 3D moving object detection inspired by the visual systems of Drosophila, leveraging point cloud data for robust and efficient scene analysis. The primary objective is to enhance the accuracy and responsiveness of object detection in dynamic environments, with applications in autonomous vehicles, robotics, and intelligent surveillance systems. Drawing inspiration from the compound eyes and motion perception mechanisms of Drosophila, we propose a bio-inspired detection framework that processes point clouds captured by LiDAR or depth sensors. The methodology integrates a multi-perspective motion analysis algorithm, mimicking Drosophila’s optic flow processing, to effectively discriminate moving objects from static backgrounds in cluttered scenes. Extensive experiments on public and custom datasets demonstrate that our method achieves superior detection rates and robustness against noise and sparse data, outperforming conventional 3D object detection techniques. The results validate that the Drosophila-inspired model not only improves detection precision but also offers fast real-time performance suitable for embedded systems. This work contributes a biologically-guided, scalable solution for 3D moving object detection in point cloud environments, opening new avenues for nature-inspired perception in intelligent machines. Keywords: Drosophila-inspired, 3D object detection, point cloud, bio-inspired algorithm, motion perception, autonomous systems.
