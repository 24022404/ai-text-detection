This paper introduces Alchemy, a novel framework for meta-reinforcement learning that leverages structured task distribution to enhance learning efficiency and adaptability. The objective is to develop an approach that enables agents to learn how to learn from a diverse set of tasks, facilitating rapid adaptation to new, unseen tasks. Alchemy achieves this through a hierarchical task representation, which allows for the discovery of underlying patterns and relationships between tasks. Our method employs a combination of reinforcement learning and meta-learning algorithms to optimize task distribution and selection. Experimental results demonstrate that Alchemy outperforms existing meta-reinforcement learning methods, achieving significant improvements in learning speed and transferability. The key findings highlight the importance of structured task distribution in meta-reinforcement learning, enabling agents to learn more effectively and efficiently. This research contributes to the development of more adaptable and generalizable reinforcement learning systems, with potential applications in areas such as robotics, game playing, and autonomous decision-making. Key keywords: meta-reinforcement learning, task distribution, hierarchical representation, reinforcement learning, adaptability.
