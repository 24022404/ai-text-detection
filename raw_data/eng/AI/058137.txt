Continual learning in neural networks is challenged by catastrophic forgetting, where previously acquired knowledge is overwritten by new information. This paper introduces a novel rectification-based knowledge retention (RKR) strategy to address this limitation. Our approach leverages adaptive rectification modules to selectively revise network representations, enabling effective integration of new knowledge while preserving performance on earlier tasks. We systematically design the rectification process to identify and correct knowledge drift during incremental updates, employing dynamic weight adjustment and feature rectification to maintain model stability. Comprehensive experiments on benchmark continual learning datasets demonstrate that RKR consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy retention and forward transfer, achieving significant reductions in forgetting across various multi-task scenarios. The proposed method is model-agnostic and seamlessly integrates with existing architectures, requiring minimal computational overhead. Our findings highlight the potential of rectification-based mechanisms for robust lifelong learning, paving the way for more reliable and scalable artificial intelligence systems in dynamic environments. Key keywords: continual learning, knowledge retention, catastrophic forgetting, neural networks, rectification, lifelong learning.
