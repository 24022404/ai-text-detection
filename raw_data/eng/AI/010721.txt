Latent variable graphical models are powerful tools for representing complex dependencies among observed variables by introducing unobserved, or latent, variables. However, learning these models from data is computationally challenging, often requiring iterative global optimization procedures. This paper introduces a novel sequential local learning (SLL) approach for estimating latent graphical models. Instead of optimizing a global objective function, SLL sequentially estimates local structures around each variable, leveraging conditional independence tests and neighborhood selection techniques. This localized approach significantly reduces the computational burden, enabling efficient learning on high-dimensional datasets. We demonstrate the effectiveness of SLL through experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets, showing that it achieves comparable or superior accuracy to existing global methods while significantly reducing computation time. The SLL framework offers a scalable and efficient alternative for learning latent graphical models, with potential applications in areas such as gene regulatory network inference and social network analysis.

Keywords: Latent Graphical Models, Structure Learning, Conditional Independence, Neighborhood Selection, Sequential Learning, High-Dimensional Data.
