The objective of this study is to enhance interactive video object segmentation in dynamic and uncontrolled environments, referred to as "in the wild," by developing a novel framework for frame recommendation. Interactive video object segmentation is crucial for various applications such as video editing, augmented reality, and autonomous systems. Our approach leverages machine learning models to learn the optimal frames for user interaction, allowing for improved segmentation accuracy and reduced user input. We propose a robust algorithm that evaluates frame importance based on contextual features and segment-level information, which guides the recommendation process. Experimental results demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing techniques by achieving higher segmentation accuracy and efficiency across diverse video datasets. This innovation reduces the effort required from users while maintaining high-quality outputs, paving the way for broader application in real-world scenarios. The findings highlight the effectiveness of learning-based frame recommendation in advancing the field of interactive video segmentation. Key implications of this research include the deployment of more intuitive video editing tools and the support of real-time interactive systems. Essential keywords include video object segmentation, frame recommendation, machine learning, and interactive systems.
