This paper introduces a general framework for fair regression, addressing the pressing need to ensure equitable outcomes in predictive modeling tasks. Traditional regression algorithms often exhibit bias when applied to data containing sensitive attributes such as gender, race, or age, resulting in unfair treatment of certain subgroups. To mitigate this issue, we propose a versatile framework that integrates fairness constraints directly into the regression optimization process. Our approach is model-agnostic and can be applied to a wide range of regression algorithms, including linear, ridge, and neural network-based models. We systematically evaluate the proposed framework on multiple real-world datasets, assessing both predictive accuracy and fairness using standard metrics such as mean squared error and group fairness indices. Experimental results demonstrate that our framework effectively reduces disparities across protected groups while maintaining competitive predictive performance. Furthermore, we conduct ablation studies to analyze the trade-offs between fairness and accuracy and discuss the impact of different constraint formulations. This work contributes a practical and adaptable solution for building fair regression models, with applications in finance, healthcare, education, and other domains where algorithmic decisions affect diverse populations. Key keywords: fair regression, bias mitigation, fairness constraints, predictive modeling, algorithmic fairness.
