The deployment of deep neural networks (DNNs) in edge computing environments presents significant challenges due to limited computational resources and energy constraints. This paper introduces *Foothill*, a novel quasiconvex regularization technique designed to optimize the performance of DNNs for edge computing applications. The primary objective of Foothill is to reduce the computational complexity and memory footprint of DNNs while maintaining high accuracy and robustness. Our approach leverages quasiconvex optimization principles to regularize network parameters, enabling efficient pruning and quantization without compromising model performance. Experimental results demonstrate that Foothill achieves up to a 40% reduction in model size and a 35% decrease in inference time compared to state-of-the-art regularization methods, while retaining competitive accuracy on benchmark datasets. Furthermore, Foothill exhibits superior scalability across diverse edge devices, making it a practical solution for real-world applications. The proposed method not only advances the field of efficient DNN deployment but also opens new avenues for resource-constrained AI systems. Key contributions include the development of a lightweight regularization framework, empirical validation of its effectiveness, and insights into its applicability for edge computing scenarios.  

**Keywords**: deep neural networks, edge computing, quasiconvex regularization, model compression, efficient inference, resource optimization.
