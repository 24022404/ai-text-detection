The interpretability of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) is crucial for building trust and understanding their decision-making processes. Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) is a widely used technique for visualizing the regions of an input image that are important for a CNN's prediction. However, Grad-CAM often produces noisy and low-resolution heatmaps, hindering its effectiveness. This paper introduces a lightweight method to enhance the interpretability and explainability of Grad-CAM for classification networks. Our approach leverages a guided filtering technique combined with a novel normalization strategy to refine the Grad-CAM heatmaps, reducing noise and sharpening the focus on salient features. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method generates more visually coherent and accurate heatmaps compared to standard Grad-CAM and other existing enhancement techniques, without significantly increasing computational overhead. Furthermore, quantitative evaluations using established metrics confirm the improved fidelity and localization accuracy of the refined heatmaps. This lightweight enhancement significantly improves the usability of Grad-CAM for diagnosing network behavior and understanding image classification decisions.
Keywords: Explainable AI, Interpretability, Grad-CAM, Convolutional Neural Networks, Visualization, Deep Learning.
