This paper introduces ALBA, a novel reinforcement learning-based framework for video object segmentation. The objective of this study is to address the challenge of accurately segmenting objects across video frames, particularly in the presence of occlusions, dynamic backgrounds, and complex motion patterns. ALBA formulates the segmentation task as a sequential decision-making process, where an agent learns to delineate object masks by interacting with video data and receiving feedback through a carefully designed reward mechanism. Unlike traditional supervised approaches that rely heavily on large annotated datasets, ALBA leverages reinforcement learning to adaptively refine segmentation boundaries over time, demonstrating resilience to annotation scarcity and frame-to-frame inconsistencies. Experimental results conducted on benchmark datasets show that ALBA achieves superior segmentation accuracy and temporal consistency compared to state-of-the-art deep learning methods, especially in challenging scenarios. The proposed framework also demonstrates efficient generalization to unseen object categories and video domains. In conclusion, ALBA advances video object segmentation by integrating reinforcement learning strategies, offering a promising direction for future research and applications in areas such as video surveillance, autonomous driving, and augmented reality. Keywords: video object segmentation, reinforcement learning, deep learning, temporal consistency, object tracking, computer vision.
