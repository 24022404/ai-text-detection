This paper introduces a novel zero-shot learning framework for recognizing emotions from body gestures. The framework addresses the challenge of limited labeled data across diverse emotional expressions and gesture variations. Our approach leverages a generalized embedding space, learned from both visual gesture features and semantic emotion descriptions, to facilitate knowledge transfer from seen to unseen emotion classes. Specifically, we employ a multi-modal contrastive learning objective to align gesture representations with corresponding emotion word embeddings. This allows the model to predict emotion labels for new gestures without requiring any labeled examples for those specific emotions. We evaluate our framework on benchmark datasets for emotion recognition from body gestures, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing zero-shot and few-shot methods. Results show that our approach effectively generalizes to unseen emotions, achieving significant improvements in recognition accuracy and robustness. Key contributions include the development of a generalized embedding space, the introduction of a novel contrastive learning scheme, and a comprehensive evaluation demonstrating the efficacy of the framework for zero-shot emotion recognition from body gestures. Keywords: Zero-shot learning, emotion recognition, body gestures, contrastive learning, multi-modal embedding.
