This paper addresses the challenges associated with Multi-label Few-Zero-shot Learning (ML-FZSL) by proposing a novel framework that leverages knowledge aggregated from multiple label graphs. The principal objective is to enhance classification performance when faced with limited or no labeled data in multi-label settings. Our approach employs a combination of graph theory and machine learning to construct label relationships that facilitate transfer learning across diverse tasks. We introduce a unique algorithm that integrates label graph embeddings with few- and zero-shot learning techniques, allowing for efficient knowledge transfer and representation learning. The results demonstrate significant improvements in classification accuracy on benchmark datasets compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly in scenarios underscored by scarce labeled instances. This research contributes a transformative perspective that emphasizes the utility of graph-based knowledge in enriching learning paradigms and mitigating data limitations. Our findings suggest that this framework not only broadens the applicability of ML-FZSL in real-world settings but also opens avenues for future exploration into graph-enhanced learning strategies. 

Keywords: Multi-label learning, Few-shot learning, Zero-shot learning, Knowledge aggregation, Graph theory, Transfer learning.
