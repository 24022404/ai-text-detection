This paper addresses the challenge of preserving fine details and edges in image processing tasks, such as denoising and reconstruction, where traditional methods often struggle. We propose a novel approach based on Weighted Nonlocal Total Variation (WNTV), which enhances the conventional nonlocal total variation framework by incorporating adaptive weighting schemes. The proposed method leverages the structural similarity between image patches to assign weights, enabling better preservation of textures and edges while effectively reducing noise. Our approach is implemented using an efficient optimization algorithm, ensuring computational feasibility for large-scale images. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that WNTV outperforms existing methods in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM), particularly in scenarios with high noise levels. The findings highlight the robustness and versatility of WNTV, making it a promising tool for applications in medical imaging, remote sensing, and computer vision. This work contributes to the advancement of image processing techniques by introducing a more adaptive and effective framework for handling complex image structures.  

**Keywords**: Image processing, nonlocal total variation, weighted regularization, denoising, edge preservation, optimization.
