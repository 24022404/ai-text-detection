This paper explores the concept of representing videos as a sequence of visual words, inspired by the success of language models in natural language processing. Our objective is to investigate the effectiveness of applying transformer-based architectures to video understanding, treating each frame as a 16x16 word embedding. We propose a novel approach that leverages a combination of convolutional neural networks and self-attention mechanisms to learn spatiotemporal features from videos. Our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on several video classification benchmarks, demonstrating the potential of this paradigm for video analysis. The results show that our model can effectively capture complex patterns and relationships in videos, outperforming traditional methods. This research contributes to the growing field of multimodal learning, highlighting the promise of adapting language models to video understanding. Key contributions include the introduction of a video-to-word embedding framework and the demonstration of its efficacy in video classification tasks, with potential applications in areas such as video search, recommendation systems, and autonomous vehicles. Relevant keywords: video understanding, transformer models, multimodal learning, computer vision, deep learning.
