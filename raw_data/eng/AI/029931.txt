This paper explores the impact of increased dropout rates in the pool5 layer of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to enhance object detection performance. The study addresses the challenge of preventing overfitting and improving the generalization capabilities of CNN models in object detection tasks.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel approach that systematically applies augmented dropout techniques specifically to the pool5 feature maps of pre-trained CNN architectures. By experimenting with various dropout configurations, our method seeks to strike a balance between the suppression of overfitting and the retention of crucial feature information. The approach is evaluated through extensive experiments on established object detection benchmarks, using leading object detection frameworks to ensure robust validation.

Results/Findings: Our findings demonstrate that increasing dropout rates within the pool5 layer leads to significant improvements in object detection accuracy. The experiments reveal enhanced generalization across diverse datasets, yielding a higher mAP (mean Average Precision) compared to models without the adapted dropout strategy. Additionally, the approach shows competitive performance against state-of-the-art dropout implementations in existing object detection models.

Conclusion/Implications: The research presents a compelling case for the use of augmented dropout strategies in advanced layers of CNNs, particularly pool5, to boost object detection efficacy. These insights contribute to the ongoing development of more robust and generalizable neural network models. The proposed technique has potential applications in real-world scenarios where object detection reliability is critical, such as autonomous driving, video surveillance, and robotic vision systems.

Keywords: Dropout, Pool5, Feature Maps, Object Detection, Convolutional Neural Networks, Overfitting, Generalization, mAP, Neural Network Models.
