Object detection, a critical task in computer vision, often requires balancing two objectives: accurate classification and precise localization. Traditional approaches typically treat these tasks separately, leading to suboptimal performance and inefficiencies. This paper introduces a novel ranking-based, balanced loss function that unifies classification and localization into a single optimization framework. The proposed method leverages a ranking mechanism to prioritize high-confidence predictions while ensuring accurate bounding box regression. By integrating these objectives, the loss function inherently balances their contributions, addressing the common issue of misalignment between classification scores and localization accuracy. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, such as COCO and PASCAL VOC, demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both detection accuracy and localization precision. The results highlight the effectiveness of the unified loss function in improving model robustness and generalization. This work contributes a scalable and efficient solution for object detection, with potential applications in autonomous driving, surveillance, and medical imaging. Key innovations include the ranking-based mechanism and the seamless integration of classification and localization, offering a new direction for future research in object detection.  

**Keywords**: object detection, loss function, classification, localization, ranking mechanism, computer vision.
