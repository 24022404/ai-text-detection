Neural Architecture Search for Lightweight Non-Local Networks

Abstract:  
The growing demand for efficient deep learning models in real-time applications has intensified interest in designing lightweight architectures that maintain high performance. This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing non-local neural networks, which capture long-range dependencies but are often computationally intensive, by leveraging Neural Architecture Search (NAS). Our objective is to automatically discover novel lightweight non-local network structures that strike an optimal balance between accuracy and efficiency. We propose a specialized NAS framework that integrates candidate non-local modules into the search space, guided by constraints on model size and computational cost. The search process employs a combination of reinforcement learning and latency-aware objectives to ensure practical deployment on resource-constrained devices. Experimental evaluations conducted on benchmark image recognition datasets demonstrate that the resulting architectures achieve significant reductions in parameter count and inference time, with minimal sacrifice in model accuracy compared to conventional non-local networks. Our findings highlight the effectiveness of automated design in constructing high-performing, efficient networks suitable for mobile and edge applications. This work advances the field by providing a systematic approach for generating state-of-the-art lightweight non-local models and establishes new baselines for efficient attention-based architectures.  
Keywords: Neural Architecture Search, non-local networks, lightweight models, deep learning, attention mechanisms, model efficiency, mobile deployment.
