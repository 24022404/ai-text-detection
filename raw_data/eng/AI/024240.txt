Nighttime haze and glow significantly degrade the quality of images captured in low-light conditions, posing challenges for computer vision applications such as surveillance, autonomous driving, and image enhancement. This paper addresses the problem of nighttime haze and glow removal by proposing a novel deep learning-based approach using a Deep Dilated Convolutional Network (DDCN). The proposed model leverages dilated convolutions to capture multi-scale contextual information, enabling effective removal of haze and glow artifacts while preserving fine details in the image. The network is trained on a synthetic dataset generated using physically-based rendering techniques, ensuring robust performance across diverse real-world scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate that the DDCN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both qualitative and quantitative metrics, including peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM). The model also exhibits superior generalization capabilities when tested on real-world nighttime images. The findings highlight the potential of the proposed approach for enhancing nighttime image quality, with applications in computer vision systems operating in challenging lighting conditions. This work contributes to the field by introducing a scalable and efficient solution for nighttime haze and glow removal, paving the way for improved performance in low-light imaging tasks.  

**Keywords**: nighttime image enhancement, haze removal, glow removal, deep learning, dilated convolutional networks, low-light imaging.
