This research addresses the challenge of domain adaptation in object detection systems for autonomous driving, focusing on enhancing the accuracy and robustness of such systems across different environmental conditions. The primary objective is to develop a domain adaptation framework that effectively integrates pixel-level and feature-level transformations to bridge the gap between source and target domains. Our approach employs a novel multi-level adaptation strategy that leverages Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for pixel-level adaptation, ensuring alignment in visual appearance, while incorporating a feature-level adaptation module based on adversarial learning to harmonize high-dimensional feature representations. Experimental results demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques in terms of detection accuracy across challenging domains, such as varied lighting conditions and diverse urban landscapes. The proposed framework enhances the reliability of autonomous driving systems by providing a more consistent and accurate object detection capability, crucial for real-world deployment. This work contributes to the advancement of autonomous vehicle technology by offering a domain-adaptive solution that is both effective and scalable, paving the way for broader practical applications. Key keywords include domain adaptation, object detection, autonomous driving, Generative Adversarial Networks, and feature transformation.
