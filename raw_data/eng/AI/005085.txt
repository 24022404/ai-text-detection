Understanding molecular structure-property relationships is a fundamental challenge in chemistry and materials science, with significant implications for drug discovery and material design. This paper introduces a novel deep learning framework, the Attention- and Gate-Augmented Graph Convolutional Network (AG-GCN), to accurately predict molecular properties by leveraging the inherent graph structure of molecules. The proposed model integrates attention mechanisms and gating functions into a graph convolutional network, enabling it to capture both local and global structural features while prioritizing critical molecular substructures. We evaluate AG-GCN on benchmark datasets, including quantum mechanical and physicochemical properties, and demonstrate its superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods. The model achieves significant improvements in prediction accuracy, particularly for complex properties influenced by long-range molecular interactions. Furthermore, we provide interpretability insights through attention weights, revealing the model's ability to identify chemically meaningful substructures. The results highlight the potential of AG-GCN as a powerful tool for accelerating molecular property prediction and guiding experimental design. This work contributes to advancing deep learning applications in molecular science by introducing a robust and interpretable framework for structure-property relationship modeling.  

**Keywords**: molecular property prediction, graph convolutional networks, attention mechanisms, deep learning, interpretability, molecular structure-property relationships.
