This paper introduces DyStaB, a novel unsupervised framework for object segmentation that leverages dynamic-static bootstrapping to address the challenges of identifying and segmenting objects in complex visual scenes without labeled data. The proposed approach combines dynamic motion cues with static appearance features to iteratively refine object masks, enabling robust segmentation in diverse environments. DyStaB employs a two-stage process: first, it extracts dynamic regions by analyzing temporal consistency across video frames, and second, it refines these regions using static appearance models to enhance segmentation accuracy. The framework is evaluated on benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing unsupervised methods, with significant improvements in precision and recall. Key contributions include a self-supervised bootstrapping mechanism that dynamically adapts to varying scene complexities and a lightweight architecture that ensures scalability. The results highlight DyStaB's potential for applications in autonomous systems, video analysis, and augmented reality, where accurate object segmentation is critical. This work advances the state of the art in unsupervised segmentation by bridging the gap between motion-based and appearance-based approaches, offering a versatile and efficient solution for real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: unsupervised segmentation, object detection, dynamic-static bootstrapping, motion analysis, self-supervised learning, video processing.
