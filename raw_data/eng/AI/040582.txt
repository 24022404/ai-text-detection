The study introduces a novel approach to classify sparse coral images utilizing deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Sparse coral classification presents significant challenges due to limited data availability and complex visual characteristics, which necessitate new methodologies for accurate recognition and analysis.

Methods: The proposed method employs a tailored deep CNN architecture optimized for processing sparse coral datasets. Techniques such as data augmentation, transfer learning, and hyperparameter tuning are applied to refine the model's capability in recognizing diverse coral species from minimal input data effectively. The CNN framework is designed to enhance feature extraction and improve classification accuracy by leveraging pre-trained model weights and fine-tuning specific network layers.

Results: Experiments conducted on real-world sparse coral datasets demonstrate the model's superior performance, achieving high classification accuracy compared to traditional machine learning models and alternative CNN architectures. The approach significantly reduces the misclassification rate, highlighting its efficiency in dealing with sparse datasets. Quantitative assessments reveal improvements in metrics such as precision, recall, and F1-score.

Conclusion: This research contributes a significant advancement in marine biology applications, offering a robust tool for coral classification in conditions with limited data input. The findings suggest potential integrations into automated marine monitoring systems, facilitating better conservation and study of coral ecosystems. The methodology presents a scalable solution adaptable to other sparse classification problems across different domains, underscoring the versatility of deep learning approaches in ecological data analysis.

Keywords: Coral classification, deep convolutional neural networks, sparse datasets, transfer learning, marine biology, automated monitoring.
