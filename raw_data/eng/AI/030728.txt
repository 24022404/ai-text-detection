Depth estimation is a critical task in computer vision, with applications ranging from autonomous driving to augmented reality. This paper introduces AdaBins, a novel approach for monocular depth estimation that leverages adaptive binning to improve accuracy and robustness. Unlike traditional methods that rely on fixed binning strategies, AdaBins dynamically adjusts the bin widths based on the input image, enabling finer granularity in depth prediction for complex scenes. The proposed method employs a deep neural network architecture that integrates a transformer-based module to learn adaptive bin distributions, coupled with a lightweight decoder for efficient depth map generation. Experiments on benchmark datasets, such as NYU Depth V2 and KITTI, demonstrate that AdaBins achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency. The adaptive binning mechanism not only enhances depth estimation in challenging scenarios but also provides interpretable insights into the model's decision-making process. This work contributes a scalable and effective solution for monocular depth estimation, with potential applications in robotics, 3D reconstruction, and scene understanding.  

**Keywords**: depth estimation, adaptive binning, monocular vision, transformer, computer vision, deep learning.
