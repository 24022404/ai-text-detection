This research addresses the growing need for accurate and early detection of breast cancer through the development of an efficient evaluation metric using Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs). Our aim is to enhance the reliability of screening processes by incorporating a confidence measure, which provides clinicians with a better understanding of diagnostic certainty.

Methods/Approach: We propose an innovative confidence measure-based evaluation metric specifically designed to leverage the probabilistic outputs of Bayesian Neural Networks. BNNs are utilized for their ability to model uncertainty in predictions, thus enhancing the interpretability of screening results. The methodology involves the implementation of a probabilistic approach to assess predictions, allowing for a quantifiable confidence level in the network’s output, which is then evaluated across diverse datasets.

Results/Findings: The empirical analysis demonstrates that our proposed metric significantly improves the assessment of screening accuracy by accounting for the uncertainty in predictions. Compared to traditional deterministic models, the Bayesian approach exhibits enhanced performance in identifying high-risk cases, with improved sensitivity and specificity metrics. Our evaluation metric was tested on multiple breast cancer screening datasets and showed superior results in providing reliable confidence scores that correlate with diagnostic accuracy.

Conclusion/Implications: This study contributes to the field by introducing a novel evaluation metric that effectively integrates confidence measures in breast cancer screening, thereby offering a robust tool for clinical decision-making. The Bayesian Neural Network-based method not only improves diagnostic reliability but also aids in prioritizing patients for further diagnostic investigation, demonstrating significant potential for real-world applications in medical imaging and oncology.

Keywords: Bayesian Neural Networks, breast cancer screening, evaluation metric, confidence measure, probabilistic modeling, uncertainty quantification, diagnostic accuracy.
