Hypergraphs offer a powerful tool for modeling complex relationships in data, where interactions involve more than two entities. This paper introduces novel hypergraph neural network architectures: Hypergraph Convolution and Hypergraph Attention, designed to effectively capture high-order dependencies. The Hypergraph Convolution (HGConv) layer aggregates node features based on hyperedge membership, employing a learnable weight matrix to control the contribution of each hyperedge. Complementarily, the Hypergraph Attention (HGAtt) layer learns adaptive weights for each hyperedge based on the relevance of its constituent nodes, allowing for selective information propagation. We demonstrate the efficacy of our proposed models on various hypergraph benchmark datasets, including node classification and hypergraph link prediction tasks. Empirical results show that HGConv and HGAtt outperform existing hypergraph learning methods, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and F1-score. Further analysis reveals that the attention mechanism in HGAtt allows the model to focus on the most informative hyperedges, leading to better performance in complex hypergraph structures. These advancements contribute to the growing field of hypergraph representation learning and offer a promising avenue for modeling intricate relational data in domains such as social networks, citation networks, and biological networks. Keywords: Hypergraph, Convolutional Neural Networks, Attention Mechanism, Graph Neural Networks, Representation Learning.
