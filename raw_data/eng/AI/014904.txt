This paper investigates the relationship between self-attention mechanisms and convolutional layers, two fundamental components widely used in modern deep learning architectures. While self-attention has gained prominence in tasks such as natural language processing and image recognition, its connection to convolutional operations remains underexplored. We aim to bridge this gap by analyzing the structural and functional similarities between these two approaches. Through a systematic comparison, we demonstrate that self-attention can be interpreted as a generalization of convolutional layers, capable of dynamically adapting to local and global patterns in data. We propose a unified framework that highlights the conditions under which self-attention reduces to convolutional operations and vice versa. Experimental results on image classification tasks validate our theoretical insights, showing that hybrid models leveraging both mechanisms achieve competitive performance while offering greater flexibility. Our findings provide a deeper understanding of the interplay between self-attention and convolution, paving the way for more efficient and interpretable neural network designs. This research contributes to the broader discourse on model architecture optimization, with potential applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and beyond.  

**Keywords**: self-attention, convolutional layers, deep learning, neural networks, image classification, model interpretability.
