This paper proposes an innovative approach to image super-resolution, leveraging attention-based multi-reference learning to enhance the quality of low-resolution images. The objective is to develop a model that can effectively utilize multiple reference images to improve the super-resolution process. Our method employs a deep neural network architecture, incorporating attention mechanisms to selectively focus on relevant reference images and extract useful features. The model is trained using a novel loss function that combines reconstruction and perception losses to optimize the super-resolution results. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art single-reference and multi-reference image super-resolution methods, achieving significant improvements in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio and structural similarity index. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of attention-based multi-reference learning and the development of a robust training framework, which can be applied to various image super-resolution applications, including image and video enhancement, surveillance, and medical imaging. Key keywords: image super-resolution, attention mechanisms, multi-reference learning, deep neural networks, computer vision.
