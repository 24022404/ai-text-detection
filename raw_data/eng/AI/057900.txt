**Abstract**

The rapid emergence of antimicrobial multidrug resistance (MDR) in intensive care units (ICUs) represents a significant challenge to public health and patient management. This study aims to improve the early identification of MDR acquisition by developing a novel framework that leverages time series kernel methods combined with dimensionality reduction techniques. Patient health records, including longitudinal clinical and microbiological measurements, were encoded as time series data. We employed time series kernel algorithms to capture complex temporal patterns associated with MDR acquisition, followed by dimensionality reduction methods such as principal component analysis (PCA) and t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) to enhance data interpretability and reduce computational complexity. Experimental evaluations were conducted on a real-world ICU dataset, demonstrating that our approach outperforms standard machine learning baselines in terms of accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity for MDR prediction. The integration of kernel-based time series analysis with dimensionality reduction enables effective identification of latent patterns and risk factors predictive of MDR, providing valuable insights for clinical decision-making. Our findings highlight the potential for advanced data-driven techniques to support antimicrobial stewardship and guide targeted interventions in critical care settings. The results suggest that this methodology can be adapted for other time-sensitive clinical prediction tasks. Keywords: time series analysis, kernel methods, dimensionality reduction, antimicrobial resistance, ICU, multidrug resistance, machine learning.
