Abstract:

Object detection in aerial images presents unique challenges due to the diverse scales, orientations, and densely packed arrangements of objects within complex backgrounds. To address the limitations of existing datasets—which often lack sufficient annotation diversity and real-world complexity—we introduce DOTA, a large-scale benchmark dataset specifically designed for object detection in aerial imagery. DOTA comprises over 280,000 annotated instances across 15 common object categories, collected from a wide range of urban and rural environments. Each object is labeled with oriented bounding boxes to more accurately capture object rotations and spatial configurations inherent in aerial perspectives. The dataset supports multiple detection tasks, including single-category and multi-category detection, and is compatible with state-of-the-art detection algorithms. Through comprehensive experimental evaluations, we demonstrate that DOTA enables rigorous benchmarking of detection models, revealing current algorithmic limitations and guiding future research. Our findings indicate that DOTA significantly improves model generalizability to challenging real-world scenarios compared to prior datasets. By providing extensive diversity and fine-grained annotations, DOTA serves as a valuable resource for advancing research and practical applications in remote sensing, surveillance, and geospatial analysis. 

Keywords: object detection, aerial images, dataset, oriented bounding boxes, remote sensing, benchmark, computer vision.
