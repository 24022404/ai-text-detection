Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for unsupervised visual representation learning, yet its effectiveness heavily relies on the choice of similarity metrics. This paper introduces a novel approach by leveraging Deep Bregman Divergence (DBD) as a generalized similarity measure for contrastive learning. Unlike traditional metrics such as cosine similarity, DBD captures complex, non-linear relationships in high-dimensional feature spaces, enabling more robust and discriminative representations. We propose a deep learning framework that integrates DBD into the contrastive loss function, optimizing feature embeddings through a combination of neural networks and Bregman divergence principles. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in unsupervised representation learning, outperforming existing approaches in downstream tasks such as image classification and object detection. The results highlight the potential of DBD to enhance the generalization and transferability of learned features. This work contributes a new perspective on similarity metrics in contrastive learning, offering a flexible and scalable solution for improving visual representation learning in diverse applications.  

**Keywords**: Contrastive Learning, Deep Bregman Divergence, Unsupervised Representation Learning, Visual Embeddings, Similarity Metrics, Deep Learning.
