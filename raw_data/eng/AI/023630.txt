Image segmentation is a crucial task in computer vision, often challenged by the need for accuracy and computational efficiency. This paper introduces "Dam Burst," a novel region-merging-based image segmentation method designed to enhance segmentation performance across diverse images.

Methods/Approach: The proposed technique leverages a region-merging framework that begins with an initial over-segmentation, employing a watershed algorithm to identify potential segment boundaries. Subsequent merging of regions is guided by a proximity function that evaluates color and spatial consistency, ensuring segments are merged based on meaningful relationships rather than mere proximity. This approach systematically reduces noise and enhances segment definition without compromising computational resources.

Results/Findings: Experiments conducted on benchmark image datasets demonstrate that Dam Burst outperforms traditional region-merging segmentation methods, achieving higher accuracies and improved boundary delineation. Comparisons with existing techniques show that Dam Burst offers a superior balance between granularity and segmentation clarity, particularly in complex scenes. The method's ability to maintain performance with varying image resolutions highlights its robustness.

Conclusion/Implications: The Dam Burst method provides a significant contribution to the field of image segmentation, offering a scalable and efficient solution that reduces over-segmentation issues while maintaining computational efficiency. Its adaptability to different image datasets makes it a promising tool for applications ranging from medical imaging to autonomous driving systems. Future work could explore integrating this technique with deep learning frameworks to further enhance segmentation accuracy.

Keywords: image segmentation, region-merging, Dam Burst, watershed algorithm, computer vision, segmentation performance, noise reduction.
