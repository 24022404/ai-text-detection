Estimating the effects of continuous-valued interventions is a critical challenge in causal inference, particularly in scenarios where interventions are not binary but span a continuous range. This paper introduces a novel approach leveraging Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to model and estimate the causal effects of such interventions. The proposed method, termed Continuous Intervention GAN (CI-GAN), employs a dual-network architecture to generate counterfactual outcomes by simulating the distribution of potential responses under varying intervention levels. Unlike traditional methods that rely on strong parametric assumptions, CI-GAN learns the underlying data distribution non-parametrically, enabling more accurate estimation in complex, high-dimensional settings. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that CI-GAN outperforms existing approaches in terms of precision and robustness, particularly when the relationship between interventions and outcomes is non-linear. The results highlight the potential of GAN-based frameworks in advancing causal inference for continuous interventions, with applications in healthcare, economics, and policy evaluation. This work contributes to the growing body of research at the intersection of machine learning and causal inference, offering a scalable and flexible solution for estimating intervention effects in real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Causal Inference, Continuous Interventions, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Counterfactual Estimation, Machine Learning, Non-parametric Methods.
