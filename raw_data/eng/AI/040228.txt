This paper revisits Adam, a widely-used optimization algorithm in the training of deep learning models, by examining it from the perspective of weighted past gradients. The goal is to enhance the understanding and effectiveness of the algorithm through a novel lens that potentially addresses some of the limitations observed in existing implementations.

Methods/Approach: We propose a re-interpretation of Adam by introducing a modified framework that incorporates a weighted approach to past gradients, providing a refined mechanism for gradient-based optimization. The study involves the adaptation of the traditional Adam algorithm to integrate this perspective, along with an analysis of the mathematical foundations that underpin the proposed modifications.

Results/Findings: Through extensive experiments across various benchmark datasets and neural network architectures, the modified Adam algorithm demonstrates significant improvements in convergence speed and stability compared to the original formulation. Our results highlight the advantages of the weighted gradient perspective, showing enhanced performance in scenarios where traditional Adam might struggle, such as sparse gradients and large-scale models.

Conclusion/Implications: The insights derived from this research offer a new direction for optimization strategies in machine learning, presenting a more robust alternative to conventional techniques. Our weighted past gradients perspective not only contributes a deeper theoretical understanding of Adam but also opens avenues for developing more adaptive and efficient optimization algorithms in deep learning. This work has implications for improving the training of complex models, potentially impacting applications in artificial intelligence, natural language processing, and computer vision.

Keywords: Adam optimization, deep learning, weighted gradients, convergence, neural networks, machine learning, algorithm enhancement.
