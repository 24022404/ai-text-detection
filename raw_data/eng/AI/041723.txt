This paper introduces a novel deep reinforcement learning (DRL) framework for molecular optimization, aiming to efficiently discover molecules with desired properties. The approach utilizes a graph-based representation of molecules, enabling direct manipulation and modification of molecular structures. We employ a policy gradient method, specifically Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), to train an agent that sequentially adds or removes atoms and bonds to optimize molecular properties. The reward function is carefully designed to guide the agent towards generating molecules with high target property scores while maintaining chemical validity and synthetic accessibility. Experimental results demonstrate that our DRL-based method outperforms existing genetic algorithms and traditional optimization techniques in generating molecules with improved drug-likeness and binding affinity against a target protein. Furthermore, the generated molecules exhibit higher structural diversity compared to those obtained through other methods. This work contributes a practical and efficient DRL approach for de novo molecular design, potentially accelerating the discovery of novel drug candidates and materials. Keywords: Deep Reinforcement Learning, Molecular Optimization, Drug Discovery, Proximal Policy Optimization, De Novo Design.
