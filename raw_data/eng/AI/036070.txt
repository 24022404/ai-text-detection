Traffic forecasting is a critical challenge in urban planning and intelligent transportation systems, particularly under varying data availability conditions. This paper explores the effectiveness of transfer learning and online learning as alternative approaches to address the limitations of traditional methods when data is scarce or non-stationary. The study proposes a hybrid framework that leverages pre-trained models from related domains (transfer learning) and adapts to real-time data streams (online learning) to improve forecasting accuracy. We evaluate the framework using diverse datasets, including urban traffic networks with varying levels of data completeness and temporal dynamics. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms conventional models, particularly in scenarios with limited historical data or sudden shifts in traffic patterns. Key findings highlight the trade-offs between model adaptability, computational efficiency, and prediction accuracy. The study provides practical insights into selecting appropriate learning strategies based on data availability and application requirements. This research contributes to advancing traffic forecasting methodologies by offering a flexible and scalable solution for real-world deployment.  

**Keywords**: Traffic forecasting, transfer learning, online learning, data scarcity, adaptive models, intelligent transportation systems.
