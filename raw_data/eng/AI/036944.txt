This paper investigates the challenges and solutions associated with distributed learning and inference on compressed image data, a scenario increasingly common in edge computing and resource-constrained environments. The primary objective is to explore effective methods for conducting collaborative training and real-time prediction when images are transmitted in compressed formats, as typically occurs in distributed Internet of Things (IoT) systems or federated learning setups. We propose a novel framework that integrates compression-aware neural network architectures with optimized data transmission protocols to minimize bandwidth usage while preserving learning accuracy. The methodology involves evaluating various image compression techniques—such as JPEG and WebP—in conjunction with distributed deep learning models, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and federated averaging algorithms. Extensive experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets to assess the trade-offs between image compression rates, communication efficiency, and model performance. Results demonstrate that our approach significantly reduces communication overhead, with less than 5% loss in model accuracy compared to traditional uncompressed distributed learning, thus enabling scalable machine learning applications in bandwidth-limited settings. The study concludes that distributed learning and inference with compressed images is both practical and efficient, offering a pathway for widespread adoption in edge AI, intelligent surveillance, and real-time data analytics. Key contributions include the development of compression-resilient training strategies and comprehensive performance analysis, advancing the state-of-the-art in distributed machine learning. Keywords: distributed learning, compressed images, federated learning, edge computing, inference, bandwidth optimization, convolutional neural networks.
