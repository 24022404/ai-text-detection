The field of image super-resolution has witnessed significant advancements with the advent of neural networks, but achieving high-fidelity image enhancement remains a challenge. This paper introduces RankSRGAN, a novel approach that leverages generative adversarial networks (GANs) complemented with a ranking mechanism to enhance image super-resolution outcomes.

Methods: RankSRGAN integrates a sophisticated GAN architecture with a ranker module designed to distinguish between high-quality and low-quality images. The generator network is responsible for producing high-resolution images from low-resolution inputs, while the discriminator assesses their plausibility. In parallel, the ranker network evaluates pairs of generated images, guiding the training process to prioritize perceptual quality.

Results: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that RankSRGAN outperforms existing super-resolution methods, both qualitatively and quantitatively. The network achieves superior results in terms of visual appeal and objective measures such as the Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM). Comparisons with other state-of-the-art models highlight RankSRGAN's ability to preserve finer details and textures while minimizing artifacts.

Conclusion: This research introduces an innovative direction in the domain of image super-resolution by incorporating a ranking criterion into the GAN framework. RankSRGAN not only advances the capabilities of image restoration technologies but also offers insights into the utility of ranking mechanisms within deep learning paradigms. Its application potential spans various fields requiring high-quality image enhancement, such as medical imaging, satellite photography, and digital media.

Keywords: RankSRGAN, generative adversarial networks, image super-resolution, ranker module, perceptual quality, PSNR, SSIM.
