Human activity recognition (HAR) and fall detection are critical tasks in healthcare and assistive technologies, with applications ranging from elderly care to rehabilitation. This paper proposes a novel Fourier domain feature approach to enhance the accuracy and robustness of HAR and fall detection systems. By transforming time-series sensor data into the frequency domain using Fourier transforms, we extract discriminative features that capture the periodic and non-periodic patterns of human activities. Our method leverages these features to train a lightweight machine learning model, which is optimized for real-time performance on resource-constrained devices. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves superior accuracy compared to traditional time-domain methods, with a 12% improvement in fall detection precision and a 9% increase in overall activity recognition rates. Furthermore, the system exhibits robustness to noise and variations in sensor placement, making it suitable for practical deployment. The findings highlight the potential of frequency-domain analysis in advancing HAR systems, offering a scalable and efficient solution for real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Human activity recognition, fall detection, Fourier transform, frequency domain, machine learning, sensor data analysis.
