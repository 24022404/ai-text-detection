This paper addresses the challenge of learning discriminative prototypes for time-series data, a critical task in applications such as activity recognition, anomaly detection, and speech processing. Traditional methods often struggle with temporal misalignments and variations in time-series data, leading to suboptimal prototype representations. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel approach that integrates Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) into a prototype learning framework. Our method leverages DTW's ability to align sequences with temporal distortions, enabling the extraction of robust and discriminative prototypes. We introduce an optimization algorithm that jointly learns prototypes and alignment paths, ensuring that the prototypes capture essential temporal patterns while minimizing intra-class variability. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of classification accuracy and prototype interpretability. The proposed framework not only advances the state-of-the-art in time-series analysis but also provides a versatile tool for applications requiring temporal pattern recognition. This research contributes to the broader field of machine learning by offering a scalable and effective solution for learning discriminative prototypes in the presence of temporal variability.  

**Keywords**: Dynamic Time Warping, time-series analysis, prototype learning, temporal alignment, discriminative models.
