The rapid advancement of deep neural networks (DNNs) has significantly enhanced visual feature learning, yet the reliance on large-scale labeled datasets remains a critical bottleneck. This paper presents a comprehensive survey of self-supervised learning (SSL) techniques for visual feature extraction, focusing on methods that leverage unlabeled data to reduce dependency on annotations. We systematically categorize existing approaches into generative, contrastive, and predictive frameworks, analyzing their underlying principles, architectures, and performance across various benchmarks. Key findings reveal that SSL methods achieve competitive results compared to supervised counterparts, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. Furthermore, we highlight recent innovations, such as cross-modal SSL and hybrid approaches, which demonstrate improved generalization and robustness. The survey concludes with a discussion on open challenges, including scalability, domain adaptation, and interpretability, while emphasizing the potential of SSL to revolutionize computer vision applications such as object detection, image segmentation, and video analysis. This work aims to provide researchers and practitioners with a structured understanding of SSL advancements and inspire future developments in unsupervised visual representation learning.  

**Keywords**: self-supervised learning, deep neural networks, visual feature extraction, contrastive learning, generative models, computer vision.
