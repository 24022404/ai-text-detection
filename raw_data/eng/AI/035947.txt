This paper addresses the challenge of recognizing historical ciphered manuscripts, a task complicated by the scarcity of labeled data and the unique characteristics of historical scripts. We propose a few-shot learning approach to accurately identify and classify ciphered texts with minimal training examples. Leveraging a convolutional neural network (CNN) combined with a meta-learning framework, our method adapts to new cipher types efficiently by learning transferable features from limited data. Experiments were conducted on a curated dataset of historical manuscripts, including both monoalphabetic and polyalphabetic ciphers. Results demonstrate that our approach achieves superior recognition accuracy compared to traditional machine learning methods, with an average improvement of 15% in classification performance. Furthermore, the model exhibits robustness to variations in script styles and degradation levels, making it suitable for real-world archival applications. This research contributes a novel solution to the field of historical document analysis, offering a scalable and efficient tool for deciphering and preserving cultural heritage. Key innovations include the integration of meta-learning for few-shot scenarios and the development of a generalized feature extraction pipeline for diverse cipher types. Keywords: few-shot learning, historical manuscripts, cipher recognition, meta-learning, convolutional neural networks, document analysis.
