This paper introduces DeepV2D, a novel approach to estimate depth from monocular videos by leveraging differentiable structure from motion. The objective is to accurately recover 3D scene geometry from 2D video sequences, addressing the long-standing challenge of depth estimation in computer vision. Our method employs a deep learning framework that integrates structure from motion principles, enabling end-to-end training and optimization. By combining convolutional neural networks with differentiable pose estimation, DeepV2D achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in depth accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenes with complex motion and occlusions. The proposed approach has far-reaching implications for applications such as 3D reconstruction, autonomous driving, and augmented reality. Key contributions include a differentiable structure from motion module, a novel depth estimation architecture, and a large-scale dataset for video-based depth estimation. Relevant keywords: depth estimation, structure from motion, differentiable rendering, monocular video, 3D reconstruction, computer vision, deep learning.
