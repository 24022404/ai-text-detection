This paper explores the application of reinforcement learning (RL) to the complex task of soccer dribbling. The primary objective is to develop an autonomous agent capable of efficiently navigating a soccer field while maintaining control of the ball. We propose a novel reward function that encourages both forward progress and ball retention, alongside a deep Q-network (DQN) architecture tailored for continuous state and action spaces. The agent is trained within a simulated soccer environment, and its performance is evaluated based on dribbling speed, success rate, and distance traveled. Experimental results demonstrate that the RL-trained agent significantly outperforms hand-engineered control policies, achieving higher dribbling speeds and maintaining ball control for longer durations. This research contributes a practical RL framework for soccer dribbling, paving the way for more sophisticated autonomous soccer agents. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Soccer, Dribbling, Deep Q-Network, Autonomous Agents, Simulation.
