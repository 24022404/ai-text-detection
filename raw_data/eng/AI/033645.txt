This paper introduces a novel approach to inverse reinforcement learning, termed learner-aware teaching, which incorporates both preferences and constraints to improve the teaching process. The objective is to develop a framework that enables an teacher agent to adaptively teach a learner agent by providing personalized demonstrations, taking into account the learner's preferences and constraints. Our approach utilizes a combination of inverse reinforcement learning and constraint-based optimization to generate informative and tailored teaching strategies. The results show that our method outperforms existing inverse reinforcement learning algorithms in terms of teaching efficiency and learner satisfaction. Key findings include the ability to adapt to diverse learner preferences and constraints, and to balance the trade-off between exploration and exploitation in the teaching process. The implications of this research are significant, as it has the potential to enhance the effectiveness of human-computer interaction, autonomous systems, and educational technologies. Our contributions include the development of a learner-aware teaching framework, which can be applied to various domains, such as robotics, healthcare, and education, and the introduction of a new paradigm for inverse reinforcement learning that prioritizes learner-centric teaching. Keywords: inverse reinforcement learning, learner-aware teaching, preferences, constraints, personalized teaching, autonomous systems, human-computer interaction.
