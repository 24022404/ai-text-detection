This paper investigates human visual perception of synthetic faces generated by advanced generative adversarial networks (GANs). The objective is to understand whether observers can reliably distinguish between real and GAN-generated faces, and to identify the perceptual features that influence these judgments. We conducted a series of psychophysical experiments where participants were presented with images of real and synthetic faces and asked to rate their realism. Eye-tracking data was collected to analyze gaze patterns and attentional biases. Results indicate that participants exhibit a limited ability to differentiate between real and synthetic faces, with accuracy rates only slightly above chance in some conditions. Analysis of eye-tracking data reveals that participants fixate on specific facial features, such as the eyes and mouth, and that subtle artifacts in these regions can influence realism judgments. These findings have implications for understanding the potential of synthetic faces in various applications, as well as for informing the development of more realistic and trustworthy AI-generated content. Keywords: synthetic faces, GANs, visual perception, realism, eye-tracking, artificial intelligence.
