Remote heart rate (HR) measurement using video is gaining traction due to its non-contact nature and potential for various applications. However, existing methods often require extensive hand-crafted feature engineering and are sensitive to environmental variations. This paper introduces AutoHR, an end-to-end deep learning framework that leverages neural architecture search (NAS) to automatically discover optimal network structures for robust remote HR measurement. AutoHR employs a novel search space tailored for spatio-temporal feature extraction from facial videos, incorporating diverse convolutional and recurrent operations. The framework integrates a differentiable neural architecture search algorithm to efficiently explore the search space and identify high-performing architectures. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that AutoHR achieves state-of-the-art performance, surpassing existing methods with significantly reduced manual effort. Furthermore, ablation studies validate the effectiveness of the NAS strategy and the discovered network architectures. AutoHR provides a strong baseline for future research in remote HR measurement, showcasing the power of automated machine learning in addressing the challenges of this domain. Keywords: Remote Heart Rate Measurement, Neural Architecture Search, Deep Learning, Spatio-Temporal Feature Extraction, Video Processing.
