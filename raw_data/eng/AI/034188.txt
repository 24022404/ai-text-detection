This paper explores a novel approach to learning likelihood functions using conditional normalizing flows, a powerful class of deep generative models capable of capturing complex data distributions. The objective of the study is to develop a flexible and scalable method for estimating conditional likelihoods, which are crucial in a range of applications including probabilistic inference, anomaly detection, and simulation-based modeling. We present a framework where normalizing flows are conditioned on auxiliary information or context variables, allowing for precise modeling of conditional data distributions. The proposed method leverages invertible neural networks to map data to a latent space while preserving the ability to compute exact likelihoods efficiently. Comprehensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that conditional normalizing flows outperform traditional likelihood estimation techniques, especially in high-dimensional settings and for multimodal data. The results show improvements in sample quality, likelihood calibration, and computational efficiency. This work advances the state-of-the-art in likelihood-based modeling by introducing a general and adaptable technique, paving the way for enhanced performance in machine learning tasks involving complex conditional dependencies. Keywords: conditional normalizing flows, likelihood estimation, deep generative models, invertible neural networks, probabilistic modeling.
