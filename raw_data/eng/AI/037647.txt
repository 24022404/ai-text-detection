This paper addresses the challenge of achieving accurate and robust estimation of motion and orientation in monocular visual-inertial odometry (VIO) systems, crucial for applications in autonomous navigation and augmented reality. We propose a novel method for online initialization and extrinsic spatial-temporal calibration, enhancing the performance of VIO systems without the need for pre-calibrated environments.

Methods/Approach: Our approach utilizes a dynamic algorithm designed to continuously update the initialization and calibration of VIO systems. It involves an innovative combination of iterative optimization and probabilistic modeling to estimate both the spatial and temporal parameters between the camera and inertial measurement unit (IMU). The method operates in real-time, allowing seamless calibration and adjustment of the system as environmental conditions change.

Results/Findings: The proposed method demonstrates significant improvements in accuracy and reliability over traditional VIO systems. Experimental results show that our algorithm enhances motion tracking precision and stability across various scenarios, including challenging dynamic environments. Comparative analysis with existing techniques highlights a reduction in drift and increased robustness to disturbances, contributing to more reliable autonomous systems.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of online initialization and calibration for VIO systems presents a substantial advancement, offering improved performance and adaptability. This research provides valuable insights for the development of more autonomous and flexible navigation systems, with potential applications in robotics, unmanned aerial vehicles, and mixed reality devices. By enabling robust VIO without reliance on fixed calibrations, our method opens new avenues for scaling and deploying autonomous technologies in diverse real-world situations.

Keywords: monocular visual-inertial odometry, online initialization, spatial-temporal calibration, adaptive algorithm, autonomous navigation.
