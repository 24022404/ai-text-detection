Pedestrian attribute recognition and localization are crucial for various applications, including intelligent surveillance and autonomous driving. This paper addresses the challenge of learning discriminative mid-level features for these tasks under weak supervision. We propose a novel framework that leverages only image-level attribute annotations to simultaneously learn attribute classifiers and pedestrian bounding box detectors. Our approach incorporates a multiple instance learning (MIL) strategy to handle the ambiguity of weak supervision, effectively selecting relevant image regions for attribute prediction. Furthermore, we introduce a spatial attention mechanism to focus on salient regions within the predicted bounding boxes, enhancing the robustness of attribute recognition. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance compared to fully supervised approaches, while significantly reducing annotation costs. The learned mid-level features also exhibit strong transferability to other related tasks. Keywords: Weakly-supervised learning, pedestrian attribute recognition, pedestrian detection, multiple instance learning, spatial attention.
