The proliferation of deepfake technology has underscored the need for robust and generalizable face forgery detection mechanisms. This study introduces Face X-ray, a novel approach designed to accurately and effectively detect manipulated facial content across a variety of deepfake algorithms.

Methods/Approach: Face X-ray leverages a deep convolutional neural network (CNN) model specifically trained to identify blending artifacts that are characteristic of facial forgeries. By analyzing these subtle discrepancies, Face X-ray can distinguish between authentic and forged images, regardless of the specific deepfake generation method employed. The model utilizes a broad dataset with diverse forgery techniques to enhance generalization and cross-method detection capabilities.

Results/Findings: Empirical evaluations demonstrate that Face X-ray achieves superior performance in detecting face forgeries, boasting improved accuracy and robustness over existing single-method detection systems. The approach is validated across several benchmark datasets, showing consistent detection rates against a wide spectrum of face forgery techniques, including those it was not explicitly trained on.

Conclusion/Implications: Face X-ray introduces a new paradigm in the detection of facial forgeries by focusing on the universal inconsistencies inherent in blend regions of forged content. This research contributes significantly to the field by offering a more general and adaptable tool for deepfake detection, with potential applications in digital media verification, online security, and forensics. By providing a scalable solution, Face X-ray could become an integral component in safeguarding the authenticity of visual content in the digital age.

Keywords: Face forgery detection, deepfake, convolutional neural network, Face X-ray, digital forensics, authenticity verification.
