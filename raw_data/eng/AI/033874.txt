Automatic sleep staging is crucial for diagnosing sleep disorders, but current deep learning models often lack interpretability, hindering clinical adoption. This paper introduces SLEEPER, a novel interpretable sleep staging framework that leverages prototypes derived from expert-defined rules. SLEEPER uses these prototypes to represent characteristic features of each sleep stage, allowing for transparent decision-making. The model employs a contrastive learning approach to embed polysomnography data points close to their corresponding prototype while pushing them away from prototypes of other stages. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that SLEEPER achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art deep learning models, while offering significantly improved interpretability. Specifically, we show how the learned prototypes align with existing clinical knowledge and enable clinicians to understand the rationale behind each sleep stage classification. This work bridges the gap between accurate sleep staging and clinical understanding, facilitating the development of trustworthy AI systems for sleep medicine. Keywords: Sleep staging, interpretability, prototypes, contrastive learning, expert rules, polysomnography.
