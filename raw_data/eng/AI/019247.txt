**Abstract**

This paper presents CI-Net, a novel deep learning framework designed to jointly address the tasks of semantic segmentation and depth estimation by leveraging contextual information within images. The motivation stems from the complementary nature of semantic and depth cues for scene understanding, yet most existing approaches treat them independently or with limited interaction. CI-Net introduces a context-aware module that dynamically fuses multi-level features across spatial and semantic dimensions, enabling the network to better capture inter-task dependencies. The architecture employs a shared encoder and separate decoders for semantic segmentation and depth prediction, with cross-task attention mechanisms enhancing information flow and consistency. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that CI-Net significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both segmentation accuracy and depth estimation quality. Notably, the results show improved object boundary delineation and depth map coherence, particularly in complex urban scenes. The proposed approach offers an efficient and unified solution for multi-task vision applications, facilitating more robust scene interpretation for domains such as autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality. Key contributions include the integration of contextual feature fusion for joint learning, empirical validation of inter-task benefits, and advancement of multi-task learning efficiency in computer vision. Keywords: multi-task learning, semantic segmentation, depth estimation, contextual information, deep neural networks, scene understanding.
