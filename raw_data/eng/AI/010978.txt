This paper proposes HA-CCN, a novel Hierarchical Attention-based Crowd Counting Network, designed to accurately estimate crowd densities in highly congested scenes. The objective is to address the challenging problem of crowd counting, where existing methods often struggle with varying crowd densities, occlusions, and cluttered backgrounds. Our approach leverages a hierarchical attention mechanism to selectively focus on regions of interest and weigh their importance, enabling the network to capture both local and global contextual information. The HA-CCN model is trained on a large-scale crowd counting dataset and achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods by a significant margin. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in handling diverse crowd scenarios, including dense crowds, varying lighting conditions, and complex backgrounds. The key contributions of this research lie in its ability to adaptively attend to relevant regions and its robustness to crowd density variations, making it a valuable tool for applications such as crowd management, public safety, and urban planning. Key keywords: crowd counting, hierarchical attention, deep learning, computer vision.
