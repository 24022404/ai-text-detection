This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently merging information from multiple modalities in multimodal neural networks. Traditional approaches often encounter scalability issues and increased computational complexity when integrating diverse data types. Our research introduces the concept of "attention bottlenecks" as a novel solution to streamline the fusion process, aiming to enhance both performance and resource efficiency.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a specialized architecture that employs attention bottlenecks to manage the flow of information between modalities. This architecture leverages a selective attention mechanism, reducing the dimensionality of data streams through key feature extraction before fusion. By doing so, we minimize redundancy and focus computational resources on the most relevant features, allowing for more effective cross-modal interaction.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our attention bottleneck approach significantly outperforms existing multimodal fusion techniques in terms of accuracy and computational resource requirements. We tested the model across various datasets involving text, image, and audio inputs, where it consistently achieved higher precision and reduced inference time compared to traditional models. Comparative analyses further reveal improved scalability of our model in larger, high-dimensional data scenarios.

Conclusion/Implications: The implementation of attention bottlenecks provides a scalable and efficient pathway for multimodal fusion, offering substantial improvements in processing speed and predictive accuracy. This approach holds potential applications in fields requiring real-time data fusion, such as autonomous systems, augmented reality, and multimedia content analysis. Our findings encourage further exploration of advanced attention mechanisms to address the expanding complexities of multimodal tasks.

Key Keywords: multimodal fusion, attention bottlenecks, neural networks, scalability, computational efficiency, cross-modal interaction.
