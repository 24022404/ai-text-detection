Stereo matching is a fundamental task in computer vision, critical for applications such as 3D reconstruction and autonomous navigation. However, the performance of stereo matching models often degrades when applied to new domains due to domain shifts in data distribution. This paper introduces a novel Geometry-Aware Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (GA-UDA) framework designed to address this challenge. The proposed approach leverages geometric consistency as a self-supervised signal to align feature representations across domains without requiring labeled target data. Specifically, GA-UDA incorporates a geometry-aware loss function that enforces consistency between disparity maps and depth cues derived from the input stereo pairs. Additionally, a domain-invariant feature extraction module is introduced to reduce discrepancies between source and target domains. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that GA-UDA significantly outperforms existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods, achieving state-of-the-art results in cross-domain stereo matching. The framework’s ability to generalize across diverse environments highlights its potential for real-world applications, particularly in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or unavailable. This work contributes to advancing domain adaptation techniques in computer vision by integrating geometric priors, offering a robust and scalable solution for stereo matching in unconstrained environments.  

**Keywords**: Stereo matching, unsupervised domain adaptation, geometric consistency, 3D reconstruction, computer vision.
