This paper addresses the challenge of action detection in video sequences, a critical task in computer vision with applications in surveillance, human-computer interaction, and video analysis. Traditional methods often rely on limited feature representations, which can hinder performance in complex scenarios. To overcome this limitation, we propose *Three Branches*, a novel framework that leverages richer feature representations by integrating spatial, temporal, and contextual information. Our approach employs a multi-branch architecture, where each branch is specifically designed to capture distinct aspects of the input data: spatial appearance, motion dynamics, and semantic context. By combining these features through a fusion mechanism, the model achieves a more comprehensive understanding of actions. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that *Three Branches* outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with occlusions or varying lighting conditions. The results highlight the importance of multi-modal feature integration for action detection. This work contributes to the field by introducing a scalable and efficient framework that can be adapted to various real-world applications, paving the way for future research in advanced video understanding systems.  

**Keywords**: action detection, multi-branch architecture, feature fusion, video analysis, computer vision.
