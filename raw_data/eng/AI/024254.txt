**Abstract:**  
Monocular depth estimation has emerged as a crucial component in computer vision applications, particularly for indoor environments where accurate structural understanding is essential for robotics, augmented reality, and scene reconstruction. This paper introduces **MonoIndoor**, a novel self-supervised framework tailored for monocular depth estimation specifically in indoor settings. The objective of this study is to address the challenges posed by diverse lighting, cluttered scenes, and limited texture that typically undermine the performance of existing depth estimation models trained primarily on outdoor datasets. MonoIndoor leverages domain-specific augmentation strategies, refined photometric loss functions, and adaptive depth normalization techniques to enhance learning from unlabelled indoor data. Extensive experiments on benchmark indoor datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves superior depth accuracy and robustness compared to leading self-supervised and supervised baselines. The results highlight significant improvements in depth prediction error metrics, particularly in regions with occlusions and reflective surfaces. Our findings establish effective practices for improving self-supervised monocular depth estimation in challenging indoor environments. MonoIndoor paves the way for robust deployment of monocular depth estimation models in real-world indoor applications, and its modular design enables straightforward integration with various computer vision pipelines.  
**Keywords:** monocular depth estimation, self-supervised learning, indoor environments, computer vision, photometric loss, scene understanding, depth normalization.
