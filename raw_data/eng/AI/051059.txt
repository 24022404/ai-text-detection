Partitioning problems such as clustering, community detection, and data segmentation are critical in numerous information technology applications, yet their effectiveness is often constrained by the choice of metric used to measure similarity or dissimilarity between data points. This paper introduces a novel large-margin metric learning framework designed specifically for partitioning tasks. The proposed approach learns a data-driven distance metric that maximizes the separation between different partitions while minimizing the distance within the same partition, thus enhancing the quality and interpretability of the resulting partitions. Our method leverages a large-margin criterion, which systematically incorporates supervised or semi-supervised information to guide the metric learning process, and employs an efficient optimization algorithm suitable for large-scale datasets. Experimental results on multiple benchmark datasets, including those from image analysis and network science, demonstrate significant improvements in clustering accuracy, modularity, and stability compared to conventional methods using standard metrics. Furthermore, the framework exhibits robust generalization across diverse partitioning scenarios, highlighting its potential for a broad range of applications in data mining and machine learning. This work contributes a flexible and effective solution for metric learning in partitioning problems, opening new avenues for improved data organization and analysis. 

Keywords: metric learning, partitioning, large-margin, clustering, distance metric, community detection, data segmentation, machine learning.
