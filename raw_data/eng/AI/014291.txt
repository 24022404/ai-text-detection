The objective of this study is to address the challenges associated with sampling strategies in 3D Point Cloud Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), which are pivotal for accurate 3D object generation. Current approaches often overlook the intricacies of effective sampling, leading to suboptimal performance in representation and generation quality. We propose a novel sampling method specifically designed for 3D point cloud rendering within the GAN framework. By leveraging spatial-temporal coherence and adaptive point selection, our method enhances the efficacy of data representation and improves the network's generative capabilities. Our experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in fidelity and diversity of generated 3D models compared to traditional sampling techniques, as evidenced by quantitative metrics such as Chamfer Distance and Earth Mover's Distance. Key findings illustrate how optimized sampling contributes to better convergence and stability in training GANs for 3D applications. This research offers new insights into the intersection of sampling methodologies and 3D GAN architectures, presenting broader implications for enhancing generative models in fields such as virtual reality, autonomous navigation, and computer-aided design. Our contributions lay the foundation for future advancements in 3D point cloud processing, driving innovation in generative modeling and its application across diverse technological landscapes. Keywords: 3D Point Cloud, Sampling Strategy, Generative Adversarial Networks, 3D Object Generation, Spatial-Temporal Coherence.
