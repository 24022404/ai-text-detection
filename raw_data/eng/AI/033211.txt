Causal discovery is a fundamental challenge in understanding complex systems, particularly in domains where interventions are costly or impractical. This paper introduces a novel framework for collaborative causal discovery using atomic interventions, enabling multiple agents to jointly infer causal relationships with minimal intervention efforts. The proposed approach leverages distributed learning techniques to share knowledge across agents while preserving data privacy and reducing redundancy in interventions. We present a scalable algorithm that integrates atomic interventions with causal graph inference, ensuring efficient and accurate discovery even in high-dimensional settings. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing approaches in terms of both accuracy and intervention efficiency, particularly in scenarios with limited observational data. The framework’s robustness is validated across synthetic and real-world datasets, showcasing its applicability in fields such as healthcare, finance, and autonomous systems. This work contributes a significant step toward collaborative and resource-efficient causal discovery, offering practical insights for deploying causal inference in distributed environments.  

**Keywords**: Causal discovery, atomic interventions, collaborative learning, distributed systems, causal inference, high-dimensional data.
