This paper presents a novel Multi-Modal Graph Neural Network (MM-GNN) designed for joint reasoning over visual content and scene text in complex image understanding tasks. The objective is to bridge the gap between visual features and text embedded within scenes, which has traditionally been treated separately, leading to suboptimal comprehension and reasoning. Our proposed framework constructs a unified graph representation, where nodes correspond to detected objects and extracted scene text entities, and edges capture both intra- and inter-modal relationships. By employing message passing within this multimodal graph structure, the model effectively fuses heterogeneous information, enabling more accurate interpretation and cross-modal inference.

We evaluate MM-GNN on multiple benchmark datasets covering visual question answering and image-text reasoning. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing unimodal and late-fusion baselines, especially in scenarios where semantic understanding depends on context provided by both vision and text modalities. The MM-GNN achieves improved accuracy in answering complex queries that require understanding of object-scene interactions as well as scene text semantics.

Our findings highlight the benefit of early and explicit multi-modal fusion using graph neural networks for joint reasoning. The proposed MM-GNN framework provides a scalable and generalizable solution for a range of applications in automated image analysis, intelligent transportation systems, and assistive technologies. These contributions underscore the importance of graph-based methods and multi-modal learning for advancing multi-source scene understanding. 

Keywords: multi-modal learning, graph neural networks, scene text, vision-language reasoning, image understanding, cross-modal fusion.
