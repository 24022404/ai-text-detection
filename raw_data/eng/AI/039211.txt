Face anti-spoofing is a critical challenge in biometric security systems, aimed at distinguishing genuine users from fraudulent attempts using spoofing attacks such as printed photos, videos, or 3D masks. This paper proposes a novel approach to enhance motion cues for face anti-spoofing by leveraging a hybrid Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architecture. The CNN extracts spatial features from facial images, while the LSTM captures temporal dynamics and motion patterns, enabling robust detection of spoofing attempts. The proposed model is trained and evaluated on benchmark datasets, including CASIA-SURF and Replay-Attack, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing methods. Experimental results show significant improvements in accuracy and robustness, particularly in detecting video-based attacks. The integration of motion cues with deep learning techniques offers a promising direction for advancing face anti-spoofing systems, with potential applications in secure authentication, surveillance, and access control. This research contributes to the field by introducing a hybrid architecture that effectively combines spatial and temporal information, addressing key limitations in current anti-spoofing approaches.  

**Keywords**: Face anti-spoofing, CNN, LSTM, motion cues, biometric security, deep learning.
