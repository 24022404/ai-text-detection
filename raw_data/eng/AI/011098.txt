This paper addresses the problem of discrete-time mean field control in systems with environment states, where a large number of agents interact with each other and their surroundings. The objective is to develop a control strategy that optimizes the behavior of the agents while taking into account the impact of the environment on their dynamics. We propose a novel approach based on mean field theory, which models the collective behavior of the agents as a single entity. Our method uses a discrete-time model to capture the dynamics of the system and incorporates environment states to account for external factors. The results show that our approach can effectively optimize the behavior of the agents and improve the overall performance of the system. Key findings include the development of a scalable and efficient algorithm for solving the mean field control problem, as well as numerical simulations demonstrating the effectiveness of our approach in various scenarios. The contributions of this research include the introduction of a new framework for discrete-time mean field control with environment states, which has potential applications in fields such as smart grids, transportation systems, and social networks. Relevant keywords: mean field control, discrete-time systems, environment states, optimal control, multi-agent systems.
