Domain generalization (DG) aims to develop models that perform well on unseen target domains, a challenging task when training data differs significantly from real-world scenarios. This paper introduces a novel approach, Probably Approximately Correct Domain Generalization (PAC-DG), designed to explicitly handle unexpected domain shifts. We formulate DG as a learning problem where the goal is to minimize the expected risk across all possible target domains, even those drastically different from the training distribution. Our method incorporates a risk estimation framework based on PAC learning theory, allowing the model to anticipate and adapt to unforeseen variations. We achieve this by learning a distribution over domain shifts and optimizing for worst-case performance within a bounded uncertainty set. Experimental results on several benchmark DG datasets demonstrate that PAC-DG significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, particularly when evaluated on highly divergent target domains. The proposed approach offers a robust and theoretically grounded framework for developing generalizable models capable of handling unexpected domain shifts, advancing the field of domain generalization. Keywords: Domain Generalization, PAC Learning, Robustness, Generalization Error, Domain Shift.
