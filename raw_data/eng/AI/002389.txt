Human parsing, the task of segmenting human images into semantically meaningful parts, is a critical challenge in computer vision with applications in virtual reality, fashion, and healthcare. This paper introduces a novel self-learning framework with a rectification strategy to address the limitations of existing methods, particularly in handling noisy or incomplete annotations. The proposed approach leverages a dual-branch network architecture, where one branch generates pseudo-labels for unlabeled data, while the other refines these labels through a rectification module to ensure consistency and accuracy. The rectification strategy dynamically corrects errors in pseudo-labels by incorporating spatial and semantic context, significantly improving the robustness of the model. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including LIP and PASCAL-Person-Part, demonstrate that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing approaches by 2.3% in mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU). Furthermore, the framework exhibits strong generalization capabilities, making it suitable for real-world scenarios with limited labeled data. This research contributes a scalable and efficient solution for human parsing, with potential applications in personalized virtual try-on systems and medical image analysis.  

**Keywords**: Human parsing, self-learning, rectification strategy, pseudo-labeling, semantic segmentation, computer vision.
