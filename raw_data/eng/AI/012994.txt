Fine-grained visual recognition requires distinguishing between highly similar categories, often necessitating large and diverse image datasets. Web-sourced images present an attractive solution for expanding datasets, but are frequently marred by label noise and varying image quality, which degrades model performance. This paper introduces a novel framework for fine-grained visual recognition that intelligently exploits web images by systematically eliminating noisy samples and leveraging hard samples—images that are challenging to classify, yet informative for model training. Our method employs a two-stage process: first, a robust sample selection mechanism filters out noisy images by analyzing prediction consistency and feature distribution; second, an adaptive hard sample mining strategy identifies and prioritizes challenging instances to enhance discriminative learning. Experimental evaluations conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements over baseline and state-of-the-art approaches, achieving higher classification accuracy and generalization capability. The proposed approach not only maximizes the utility of readily available web images but also provides a scalable pathway for improving fine-grained recognition systems. These findings have broad implications for applications in biodiversity monitoring, product identification, and other domains requiring precise visual discrimination. Keywords: fine-grained recognition, web images, noise elimination, hard sample mining, deep learning, computer vision.
