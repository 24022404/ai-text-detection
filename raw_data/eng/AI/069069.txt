This paper investigates the global optima of kernelized adversarial representation learning, a crucial aspect of deep learning that has garnered significant attention in recent years. The objective is to develop a comprehensive understanding of the optimization landscape of kernelized adversarial models, which are widely used for unsupervised representation learning tasks. We employ a novel approach that combines kernel methods with adversarial training, leveraging the strengths of both paradigms to learn robust and informative representations. Our method utilizes a customized kernel function that captures the underlying structure of the data, allowing for more effective exploration of the optimization space. The results show that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of representation quality and robustness, with significant improvements in downstream tasks such as classification and clustering. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of carefully designing the kernel function and the adversarial training process to achieve global optima. This work contributes to the advancement of representation learning and has important implications for applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and recommender systems, with relevant keywords including kernel methods, adversarial training, representation learning, and deep learning.
