This paper addresses the challenge of 3D face reconstruction from single images by employing a novel approach that utilizes color photometric stereo with uncalibrated near point lights. The aim is to enhance the accuracy and reliability of facial reconstruction in scenarios where lighting conditions are not predefined or controlled.

Methods/Approach: We propose a methodology that leverages color photometric stereo, using the variations in color channels to estimate surface normals and depth without pre-calibration of the light sources. By integrating uncalibrated near point lights, our approach significantly reduces the dependency on precise lighting setup, facilitating practical applications in real-world environments.

Results/Findings: The proposed method demonstrates superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art techniques, achieving higher fidelity in the reconstruction of facial features. Our experiments showcase the system's capability to reconstruct fine details and contours accurately, even with inconsistent or near point light sources. We present quantitative evaluations and visual assessments that underscore the robustness and efficiency of our approach.

Conclusion/Implications: This study contributes a significant advancement in 3D face reconstruction technology, offering a feasible solution for applications requiring high-quality facial modeling without controlled lighting. The implications of this research extend to areas such as digital human representation, enhanced virtual reality experiences, and improved security systems. By reducing the reliance on calibrated lighting, our method paves the way for more versatile and accessible 3D reconstruction techniques.

Keywords: 3D face reconstruction, photometric stereo, uncalibrated lighting, near point lights, facial modeling, computer vision.
