This paper explores the application of model-based reinforcement learning (RL) in contextual decision processes, with a focus on Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) bounds and comparative analysis against model-free approaches. Our objective is to investigate the potential of model-based RL in achieving exponential improvements in learning efficiency and decision-making accuracy. We propose a novel model-based RL framework that leverages probabilistic modeling and planning to optimize decision-making in complex, dynamic environments. Our approach utilizes a combination of Bayesian inference and stochastic optimization to learn an accurate model of the environment and compute optimal policies. The results demonstrate that our model-based RL method achieves significant reductions in sample complexity and regret compared to state-of-the-art model-free methods, with exponential improvements in certain scenarios. The key findings highlight the importance of incorporating model-based reasoning in RL for contextual decision processes, enabling more efficient and effective learning in real-world applications. Our research contributes to the development of more robust and scalable RL algorithms, with potential applications in areas such as personalized recommendation systems, autonomous systems, and healthcare decision support. Key keywords: model-based RL, contextual decision processes, PAC bounds, reinforcement learning, Bayesian inference, stochastic optimization.
