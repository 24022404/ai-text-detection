Pedestrian trajectory prediction is crucial for autonomous driving, robotics, and crowd analytics, requiring models to accurately anticipate human movement while accounting for uncertainty. However, existing deep learning-based approaches often generate unrealistic or out-of-distribution (OOD) trajectories, limiting their reliability in real-world applications. To address this challenge, we propose MG-GAN, a novel Multi-Generator Generative Adversarial Network designed to enhance trajectory prediction by mitigating OOD samples.

Our approach employs multiple specialized generators, each capturing different motion patterns and social interactions, ensuring diverse yet plausible trajectory predictions. A discriminator-based selection mechanism evaluates generated samples, suppressing implausible trajectories while refining the model’s understanding of real-world pedestrian movement. Additionally, we incorporate a context-aware feature extractor to encode spatial-temporal dependencies, further improving prediction accuracy and robustness.

Extensive experiments on benchmark pedestrian trajectory datasets demonstrate that MG-GAN significantly reduces OOD predictions while outperforming state-of-the-art methods in terms of trajectory accuracy and social compliance. These results highlight the effectiveness of multi-generator architectures in improving generative trajectory models for safety-critical applications.

Keywords: Pedestrian Trajectory Prediction, Generative Adversarial Networks, Multi-Generator Models, Out-of-Distribution Detection, Autonomous Systems, Spatial-Temporal Modeling.
