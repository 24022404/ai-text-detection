Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have achieved remarkable success in image recognition, often relying on data augmentation techniques to improve generalization. This paper investigates whether and how CNNs internally encode information about the specific data augmentations applied during training. We introduce a novel methodology to probe the learned representations of CNNs, analyzing the consistency and predictability of activations in response to differently augmented versions of the same input image. Our approach involves training CNNs with various augmentation pipelines and then examining the learned feature space using representational similarity analysis and targeted probing classifiers. Results reveal that CNNs do encode information about applied augmentations, albeit in a complex and non-linear manner. Specifically, certain layers exhibit sensitivity to specific augmentation types, while others appear more invariant. Furthermore, we demonstrate that decoding augmentation parameters from the learned representations is possible to a certain extent, suggesting that augmentation information is implicitly stored within the network's weights. These findings shed light on the inner workings of CNNs and offer insights into the design of more robust and data-efficient learning strategies. Keywords: Convolutional Neural Networks, Data Augmentation, Representation Learning, Probing, Image Recognition.
