This study investigates the application of undersampling and bagging techniques on decision tree models to predict extubation readiness in extremely preterm infants through the analysis of cardiorespiratory behavior. Premature infants often require protracted mechanical ventilation, and premature extubation can lead to severe complications. Accurate prediction of extubation readiness is therefore crucial.

Methods: We applied decision tree-based machine learning algorithms augmented with undersampling to address class imbalance, coupled with bagging to enhance model stability and predictive performance. Physiological data, including heart rate, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation, were collected from a cohort of extremely preterm infants. Feature selection and preprocessing techniques were utilized to optimize the data input for model training and evaluation.

Results: Our models demonstrated significant improvements in prediction accuracy over traditional methods, reducing false positives and increasing sensitivity and specificity. The integration of undersampling and bagging techniques effectively balanced the dataset and provided robust performance across various test scenarios. Comparative analysis with standard decision tree approaches showed a marked increase in predictive accuracy, particularly in identifying infants truly ready for extubation.

Conclusion: The research illustrates the potential of machine learning techniques in critical neonatal care, offering a reliable method to assess extubation readiness in extremely preterm infants. The enhanced model not only contributes to improved clinical decision-making but also holds the potential to optimize care strategies and patient outcomes. These findings advocate for the integration of advanced data-driven approaches in neonatal intensive care units, paving the way for further innovations in pediatric healthcare technologies.

Keywords: decision trees, undersampling, bagging, extubation readiness, extremely preterm infants, cardiorespiratory behavior, machine learning, neonatal care.
