Panoptic image segmentation, which unifies instance and semantic segmentation, is a critical task in computer vision with applications in autonomous systems, medical imaging, and robotics. However, existing methods often struggle with segmenting unfamiliar objects and splitting them into meaningful parts, limiting their adaptability in dynamic environments. This paper introduces the *Generator Evaluator-Selector Net (GESNet)*, a novel framework designed to address these challenges. GESNet combines a generative model for proposing segmentation hypotheses, an evaluator network to assess their quality, and a selector mechanism to choose the most accurate segmentation outputs. The framework leverages advanced deep learning techniques, including attention mechanisms and multi-task learning, to enhance its ability to handle unfamiliar objects and decompose them into coherent parts. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that GESNet outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both segmentation accuracy and adaptability to unseen object categories. The proposed approach achieves a 5.2% improvement in panoptic quality (PQ) and shows significant gains in part segmentation tasks. The implications of this research extend to real-world applications, such as improved scene understanding for autonomous vehicles and enhanced object manipulation in robotics. GESNet represents a significant step forward in addressing the limitations of current segmentation methods, offering a robust and scalable solution for complex visual understanding tasks.  

**Keywords**: Panoptic segmentation, unfamiliar object segmentation, part decomposition, deep learning, computer vision, GESNet.
