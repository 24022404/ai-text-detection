This paper addresses the challenge of estimation bias in Q-learning, a widely used reinforcement learning algorithm. Traditional Q-learning often suffers from overestimation bias, which can lead to suboptimal policies and reduced performance in complex environments. To mitigate this issue, we propose Maxmin Q-learning, a novel approach that leverages multiple Q-value estimators to control and reduce estimation bias. The method employs a maxmin strategy, where the minimum value among multiple estimators is used to update the Q-function, effectively balancing exploration and exploitation while minimizing overestimation. We evaluate the proposed algorithm on a range of benchmark environments, including discrete and continuous control tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that Maxmin Q-learning significantly reduces estimation bias and outperforms standard Q-learning and other baseline methods in terms of policy quality and convergence speed. The findings highlight the robustness and scalability of the approach, making it a promising solution for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and other domains requiring reliable reinforcement learning. This work contributes to the ongoing efforts to improve the stability and efficiency of reinforcement learning algorithms, offering a practical and theoretically grounded method for bias control.  

**Keywords**: Q-learning, reinforcement learning, estimation bias, maxmin strategy, policy optimization, autonomous systems.
