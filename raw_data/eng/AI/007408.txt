This paper addresses the challenge of content-based image retrieval (CBIR) for 3D brain MRI scans, focusing on disease-oriented image embedding and scanner standardization. The variability in MRI acquisition protocols across different scanners often hinders the effectiveness of CBIR systems, particularly in medical applications. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel framework that integrates pseudo-scanner standardization with deep learning-based image embedding. Our approach leverages a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture to generate disease-specific embeddings, enabling efficient retrieval of clinically relevant images. Additionally, a pseudo-scanner standardization module is introduced to normalize image features across diverse scanner types, ensuring robustness and generalizability. Experiments conducted on a large-scale 3D brain MRI dataset demonstrate significant improvements in retrieval accuracy compared to existing methods. The proposed framework achieves a 15% increase in precision and a 20% reduction in retrieval time, highlighting its potential for enhancing diagnostic workflows and supporting clinical decision-making. This research contributes to the field of medical imaging by providing a scalable and effective solution for CBIR in heterogeneous MRI datasets, with potential applications in disease diagnosis, treatment planning, and medical research.  

**Keywords**: 3D brain MRI, content-based image retrieval, disease-oriented embedding, pseudo-scanner standardization, deep learning, medical imaging.
