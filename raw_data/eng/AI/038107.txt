This paper addresses the critical challenge of high variance in policy gradient methods within multi-agent reinforcement learning environments. The objective is to systematically analyze and characterize the variance arising in multi-agent policy gradient algorithms, which significantly impacts learning stability and convergence. We propose a novel theoretical framework that decomposes the sources of variance in multi-agent policy gradients, considering factors such as agent interactions, reward structures, and policy parameterizations. Furthermore, we introduce variance reduction techniques specifically tailored for multi-agent scenarios, including coordinated baselines and adaptive credit assignment strategies. Through a series of benchmark experiments on cooperative and competitive multi-agent tasks, our approach demonstrates substantial improvements in sample efficiency, stability, and overall performance compared to standard multi-agent policy gradient methods. The findings offer the first comprehensive treatment of variance issues in this domain, providing both theoretical insights and practical solutions. Our work advances the understanding of multi-agent policy optimization and lays the foundation for more robust and scalable learning in complex, dynamic systems such as autonomous robotics, distributed control, and multi-agent simulations. Keywords: multi-agent systems, policy gradient, variance reduction, reinforcement learning, sample efficiency.
