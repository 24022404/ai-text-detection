Change captioning aims to automatically generate natural language descriptions that precisely and comprehensively describe differences between pairs of images. A core challenge in this task arises when images are captured from different viewpoints, leading to significant geometric variations that can hinder change detection and caption generation. This paper presents a novel Viewpoint-Adapted Matching Encoder (VAME) designed to robustly handle change captioning under such conditions. The proposed method integrates viewpoint-invariant feature extraction with an adaptive attention-based matching mechanism, allowing the encoder to align semantically related regions between image pairs despite pose disparities. Extensive experiments are conducted on publicly available change captioning datasets with both static and viewpoint-altered image sets. Results demonstrate that VAME significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art models in both qualitative and quantitative evaluations, exhibiting higher accuracy and fluency in generated captions. The findings suggest that explicitly modeling viewpoint adaptation within the matching process not only boosts change detection performance but also improves the interpretability and relevance of the generated descriptions. This approach holds promise for real-world applications such as robotic visual inspection, surveillance, and autonomous navigation, where viewpoint changes are inevitable. Keywords: change captioning, viewpoint adaptation, matching encoder, attention mechanism, image captioning, visual change detection.
