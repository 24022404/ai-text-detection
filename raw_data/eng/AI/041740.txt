Lesion segmentation in medical imaging, particularly in CT scans, is a critical task for accurate diagnosis and treatment planning. However, obtaining pixel-level annotations for training deep learning models is time-consuming and labor-intensive. This paper addresses the challenge of weakly-supervised lesion segmentation, where only image-level labels are available, by proposing a novel co-segmentation approach. Our method leverages the inherent similarities between multiple CT scans to jointly segment lesions across images, reducing the reliance on extensive manual annotations. We introduce a dual-branch network architecture that combines global context and local detail extraction, enhanced by a co-attention mechanism to improve lesion localization. The model is trained using a combination of image-level classification loss and region-based consistency loss, ensuring robust performance even with limited supervision. Experimental results on publicly available CT datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive segmentation accuracy compared to fully-supervised methods, while significantly reducing annotation effort. The proposed framework not only advances weakly-supervised learning in medical imaging but also offers practical implications for deploying automated lesion detection systems in clinical settings. Key contributions include the integration of co-segmentation with attention mechanisms and a novel loss function tailored for weakly-supervised scenarios. This work paves the way for more efficient and scalable solutions in medical image analysis.  

**Keywords**: Weakly-supervised learning, lesion segmentation, CT scans, co-segmentation, medical imaging, deep learning.
