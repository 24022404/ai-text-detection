Medical image segmentation is a critical task in healthcare, enabling precise diagnosis and treatment planning. However, the scarcity of annotated medical data and the need for domain-specific expertise pose significant challenges. This paper addresses these issues by proposing a novel semi-supervised few-shot learning framework tailored for medical image segmentation. The approach leverages a small set of labeled data combined with a larger pool of unlabeled data to train a robust segmentation model. We introduce a dual-branch architecture that integrates a self-supervised feature extraction module with a few-shot learning mechanism, enabling the model to generalize effectively across diverse medical imaging datasets. Experimental results on benchmark datasets, including MRI and CT scans, demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior segmentation accuracy compared to existing state-of-the-art approaches, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. The framework also exhibits strong adaptability to new medical imaging modalities, making it a promising solution for real-world clinical applications. This research contributes to advancing medical image analysis by reducing dependency on extensive labeled datasets while maintaining high segmentation performance.  

**Keywords**: Medical image segmentation, semi-supervised learning, few-shot learning, self-supervised learning, MRI, CT scans.
