Variational inference (VI) provides a computationally efficient alternative to Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods for Bayesian inference. However, traditional VI methods often rely on restrictive parametric assumptions, leading to inaccurate posterior approximations. This paper introduces a novel variational inference framework that minimizes the Fisher divergence between the approximate and true posterior distributions. Our approach leverages the Riemannian structure induced by the Fisher information metric to guide the optimization process, allowing for more flexible and accurate approximations. We develop a practical algorithm for computing the Fisher divergence and its gradients, enabling efficient optimization. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our method on several benchmark problems, including Bayesian logistic regression and Gaussian mixture models. Empirical results show that our approach achieves significantly improved accuracy compared to standard VI methods, while maintaining computational efficiency. This work contributes a new perspective on variational inference, offering a powerful tool for approximate Bayesian computation.
**Keywords:** Variational Inference, Fisher Divergence, Bayesian Inference, Approximate Inference, Optimization.
