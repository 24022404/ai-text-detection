This paper addresses the challenge of modeling complex, uncertain environments by learning approximate stochastic transition models, a cornerstone task in areas such as robotics, reinforcement learning, and simulation. We propose a novel framework that utilizes deep learning techniques to capture the probabilistic dynamics underlying sequential data. Our approach integrates a variational inference mechanism with neural network architectures to efficiently approximate the transition probability distributions, enabling robust predictions in non-deterministic systems. Extensive experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets and synthetic environments to evaluate model accuracy and computational efficiency. Results demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms traditional deterministic and basic probabilistic models in terms of predictive performance and scalability. Furthermore, we analyze the impact of uncertainty estimation on downstream tasks such as planning and decision-making. The findings validate the proposed model’s ability to generalize across diverse stochastic processes, highlighting its utility for real-world applications requiring reliable long-term forecasts. This work contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in learning stochastic dynamics by offering a scalable, data-driven modeling solution. Key keywords: stochastic transition models, approximate inference, deep learning, probabilistic modeling, reinforcement learning.
