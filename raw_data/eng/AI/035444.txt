This paper addresses the challenging problem of image degradation caused by haze and glow effects in nighttime scenes, which significantly impairs visibility and the performance of computer vision systems. The proposed approach utilizes a deep dilated convolutional network specifically designed for effective removal of both haze and glow artifacts from night-time images. Our model leverages dilated convolutions to capture multi-scale contextual information, enabling enhanced feature extraction and the preservation of fine image details. The network is trained on a comprehensive dataset of synthetic and real-world nighttime images, simulating a wide range of haze densities and glow intensities. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms traditional dehazing techniques and recent deep learning-based approaches in both qualitative visualization and quantitative metrics, such as Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM). The system not only restores image clarity but also improves subsequent tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation in night settings. This work contributes a novel, robust, and end-to-end framework for nighttime haze and glow removal, with broad applicability in surveillance, autonomous driving, and low-light photography. Key keywords include night time dehazing, glow removal, deep learning, dilated convolutional neural networks, image restoration, and computer vision.
