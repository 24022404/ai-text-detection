This paper explores the concept of graph coarsening, a technique that simplifies complex networks while preserving their essential properties. The objective is to investigate the applications of graph coarsening in both scientific computing and machine learning, with a focus on improving computational efficiency and model accuracy. Our approach involves developing a novel coarsening framework that leverages algebraic multigrid methods and graph neural networks to reduce the dimensionality of large-scale graphs. The results show that our method outperforms existing coarsening techniques in terms of clustering quality and computational speed. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in various domains, including image segmentation, node classification, and graph-based recommendation systems. The key contributions of this research include a unified framework for graph coarsening, a new set of coarsening algorithms, and a comprehensive evaluation of their performance in machine learning tasks. Our work has significant implications for the development of more efficient and scalable graph-based models, and paves the way for further research in graph coarsening and its applications in AI, machine learning, and scientific computing. Key keywords: graph coarsening, scientific computing, machine learning, graph neural networks, algebraic multigrid methods.
