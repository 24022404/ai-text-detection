The de Bruijn graph Gk is notable for being Eulerian, as each vertex has exactly two incoming and two outgoing edges, enabling the construction of a longest path that traverses every edge exactly once with a total length of 2k. On the other hand, the (k, s)-RdB graph Gk,s lacks this Eulerian property because the in-degree and out-degree of its vertices can vary between one and two, which may prevent the existence of a path covering all edges.
