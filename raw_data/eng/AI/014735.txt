Super resolution neural networks have become essential tools for enhancing image quality in a wide array of information technology applications, including medical imaging, surveillance, and multimedia processing. However, the choice of loss function significantly influences the effectiveness and generalizability of these models. This paper proposes a novel adaptive loss function framework for super resolution neural networks, leveraging convex optimization techniques to dynamically adjust loss components during training. Our approach identifies an optimal balance between perceptual fidelity and reconstruction accuracy by formulating the loss adaptation process as a convex optimization problem, thereby ensuring global convergence and computational efficiency. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that neural networks trained with the proposed adaptive loss function achieve superior performance in both quantitative metrics, such as Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), and qualitative visual assessments, outperforming conventional fixed loss functions and existing adaptive strategies. The adaptive framework also exhibits robust performance across diverse network architectures and degradation scenarios. This research introduces a principled, optimization-driven methodology for loss function design, offering a significant advancement for the field of image super resolution and paving the way for its application in broader image enhancement tasks. Key keywords: super resolution, neural networks, adaptive loss function, convex optimization, image enhancement, deep learning.
