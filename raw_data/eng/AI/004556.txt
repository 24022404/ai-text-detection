This paper explores an end-to-end computer vision approach for the multi-class classification of blood cells, aiming to enhance diagnostic accuracy and efficiency. We propose a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, optimized for feature extraction and classification directly from microscopic blood cell images, eliminating the need for manual feature engineering. The proposed model was trained and evaluated on a dataset of blood cell images belonging to multiple classes, including erythrocytes, leukocytes, and platelets, further categorized by subtypes and abnormalities. Our results demonstrate a classification accuracy of 95% on the test set, surpassing traditional methods that rely on handcrafted features. The end-to-end approach significantly reduces processing time and complexity, enabling rapid and automated blood cell analysis. This study contributes a robust and scalable solution for computer-aided diagnosis in hematology, with potential applications in point-of-care testing and resource-limited settings.

**Keywords:** Blood Cell Classification, Convolutional Neural Networks, Computer Vision, Medical Diagnosis, Deep Learning, Hematology, Automated Analysis
