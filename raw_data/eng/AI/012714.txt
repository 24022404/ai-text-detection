This paper introduces a novel self-supervised learning (SSL) framework, Auxiliary Augmentation with GNT-Xent loss (AAG), designed to improve the quality of learned representations. AAG leverages auxiliary augmentations, generated through non-semantic transformations, to diversify the training signal and enhance feature robustness. This approach mitigates the limitations of relying solely on semantic-preserving augmentations, which can lead to biased or overfitted representations. Specifically, we employ a GNT-Xent loss function, a modified cross-entropy loss, to encourage consistent predictions across both standard and auxiliary augmented views of the input data. The efficacy of AAG is evaluated on several benchmark datasets for image classification and object detection tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that AAG consistently outperforms state-of-the-art SSL methods, achieving significant improvements in downstream task performance. These findings highlight the potential of auxiliary augmentations and the GNT-Xent loss to learn more robust and generalizable representations. Keywords: self-supervised learning, representation learning, data augmentation, non-semantic transformations, cross-entropy loss, GNT-Xent, contrastive learning.
