This paper introduces TernaryNet, a novel deep learning framework designed to accelerate inference in medical 3D segmentation tasks without relying on Graphics Processing Units (GPUs). The objective is to address the computational intensity and memory requirements of traditional deep models, hindering their deployment on resource-constrained devices. TernaryNet leverages sparse and binary convolutions to significantly reduce computational complexity and memory usage. Our approach involves a hybrid convolutional design, combining the benefits of sparse and binary weights to achieve efficient inference. Experimental results demonstrate that TernaryNet outperforms state-of-the-art models in terms of inference speed and accuracy, while requiring substantially less computational resources. Notably, TernaryNet achieves real-time performance on medical 3D segmentation tasks using only Central Processing Units (CPUs), making it an attractive solution for clinical applications where GPU availability is limited. The key contributions of this work include the development of a novel ternary convolutional layer, an efficient pruning strategy, and a scalable framework for medical image segmentation. By enabling fast and accurate deep model inference on CPUs, TernaryNet has the potential to broaden the accessibility of advanced medical imaging techniques, particularly in resource-constrained environments. Keywords: medical 3D segmentation, sparse convolutions, binary neural networks, deep model inference, CPU acceleration.
