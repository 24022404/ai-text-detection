Continual learning, a critical area in artificial intelligence, aims to enable models to learn sequentially from non-stationary data streams without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. Despite significant progress, the evaluation of continual learning scenarios remains inconsistent, often leading to inflated claims of performance improvements. This paper re-evaluates existing continual learning scenarios and proposes a refined categorization framework to better align experimental setups with real-world applications. We emphasize the importance of establishing strong baselines, demonstrating that many existing approaches fail to outperform simple yet robust methods when evaluated under standardized conditions. Through extensive experiments, we identify key factors influencing continual learning performance, including task similarity, data distribution shifts, and memory constraints. Our findings reveal that current benchmarks often underestimate the capabilities of baseline models, highlighting the need for more rigorous evaluation protocols. This work contributes a systematic approach to scenario categorization and underscores the value of strong baselines in driving meaningful advancements in continual learning research.  

**Keywords**: continual learning, artificial intelligence, evaluation frameworks, strong baselines, task categorization, non-stationary data streams.
