The objective of this study is to explore the use of Graph Isomorphism Networks (GIN) for analyzing functional connectivity in resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) data. Traditional methods for rs-fMRI analysis often struggle with accurately capturing the complex, non-linear relationships present in brain connectivity patterns. In this research, we propose a novel approach employing GIN—a neural network model capable of discerning intricate graph structures—to enhance the analysis of functional connectivity in the brain. The method involves constructing brain connectivity graphs from rs-fMRI data and applying GIN to effectively identify and interpret isomorphic structures that represent similar connectivity patterns across subjects. Our findings indicate that GIN offers superior performance in detecting subtle connectivity differences compared to conventional graph analysis techniques. This enhancement is demonstrated through improved classification accuracy in distinguishing between typical and atypical connectivity, proving its robustness across various datasets. The implications of our research suggest that integrating GIN into rs-fMRI analysis pipelines can significantly advance the understanding of brain connectivity and facilitate the discovery of novel biomarkers for neurological disorders. Our study not only highlights the potential of GIN in neuroscience but also contributes to the broader field of graph-based learning methods in functional brain analysis. Keywords: Graph Isomorphism Networks, rs-fMRI, Functional Connectivity, Brain Connectivity, Graph-based Learning.
