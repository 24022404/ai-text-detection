This paper addresses the problem of relative pose estimation between two cameras with unknown radial distortion and scale ambiguity, a common challenge in visual SLAM and structure-from-motion. We propose novel minimal solvers for rectifying images captured by cameras with potentially different radial distortion parameters and experiencing a change of scale. These solvers leverage the epipolar constraint and exploit the geometric relationships between corresponding image points to simultaneously estimate relative pose, radial distortion coefficients, and the scale factor. Our approach avoids the need for initial estimates or iterative refinement, leading to faster and more robust pose estimation. The proposed solvers are evaluated on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly in scenarios with significant radial distortion and scale variations. This work provides a practical solution for robust visual odometry and 3D reconstruction in challenging environments.

**Keywords:** Relative Pose Estimation, Radial Distortion, Minimal Solvers, Scale Ambiguity, Visual SLAM, Structure-from-Motion.
