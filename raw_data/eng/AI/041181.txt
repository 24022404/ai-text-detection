This paper addresses the challenge of recognizing American Sign Language (ASL) fingerspelling in unconstrained, real-world environments, often referred to as "in the wild." The objective is to develop a robust system capable of accurately interpreting dynamic and diverse fingerspelling gestures captured under varying lighting conditions, backgrounds, and camera angles. To achieve this, we propose a novel deep learning-based framework that integrates a multi-modal approach, combining visual and temporal data streams. The system leverages a convolutional neural network (CNN) for spatial feature extraction and a recurrent neural network (RNN) with attention mechanisms to model temporal dependencies in the fingerspelling sequences. We evaluate the proposed method on a newly collected dataset of real-world ASL fingerspelling videos, demonstrating significant improvements in recognition accuracy compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. The results highlight the system's ability to generalize across diverse environmental conditions and user variations. This research contributes to advancing accessibility technologies by providing a more reliable and scalable solution for ASL fingerspelling recognition, with potential applications in real-time communication tools and assistive devices for the deaf and hard-of-hearing community.  

**Keywords**: American Sign Language, fingerspelling recognition, deep learning, multi-modal learning, real-world applications, accessibility technology.
