Accurately predicting the center and scale of objects in digital images is fundamental to numerous computer vision tasks, such as object detection, pose estimation, and image segmentation. Traditional prediction methods often suffer from instability and suboptimal accuracy, particularly when faced with occlusions, varied object orientations, and complex backgrounds. This paper proposes an Adapted Center and Scale Prediction (ACSP) approach designed to enhance both stability and accuracy in these challenging environments. The proposed method introduces a dynamic feature aggregation mechanism that leverages both global and local image contexts, combined with a refined regression model optimized for center and scale estimation. Comprehensive experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets, comparing ACSP with existing state-of-the-art approaches. Results demonstrate consistent improvements in key performance metrics, including average precision and localization consistency, especially in scenarios with high variability. The ACSP framework exhibits robustness against noise and partial visibility, maintaining superior performance across diverse testing conditions. This study’s findings suggest that the adapted prediction strategy not only yields measurable gains in accuracy but also delivers greater reliability for downstream computer vision applications. Key contributions include a novel dynamic feature aggregation mechanism, an optimized regression model, and extensive performance evaluation. Keywords: center prediction, scale estimation, computer vision, robustness, deep learning, feature aggregation, model stability.
