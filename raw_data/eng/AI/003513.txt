Establishing robust correspondences between images is a fundamental challenge in computer vision. This paper introduces a novel approach, Cycle Time Correspondence Learning (CTCL), which leverages the temporal cycle consistency present in video sequences to learn these correspondences without direct supervision. Our method trains a deep neural network to predict pixel-level correspondences across video frames by enforcing the constraint that tracking a point forward and backward in time should return to its origin. Specifically, we utilize a cycle consistency loss function computed directly on the predicted correspondence fields. The CTCL framework demonstrates superior performance in learning accurate and dense correspondences compared to existing self-supervised and unsupervised methods, particularly in challenging scenarios with significant occlusions and non-rigid deformations. Experimental results on both synthetic and real-world video datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in applications such as video object segmentation, motion estimation, and 3D reconstruction. The learned correspondence fields exhibit strong generalization capabilities and can be applied to novel video sequences. Keywords: Correspondence Learning, Cycle Consistency, Self-Supervised Learning, Video Analysis, Optical Flow.
