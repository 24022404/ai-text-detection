This paper investigates the role of entropy in policy optimization within reinforcement learning frameworks. Entropy regularization has emerged as a crucial mechanism for promoting exploration and stabilizing learning, yet its precise impact on policy performance and convergence dynamics remains insufficiently understood. In this study, we conduct a comprehensive analysis of entropy's influence across various policy optimization algorithms, including policy gradient and actor-critic methods. Our approach combines theoretical insight with extensive empirical evaluation on standard benchmark environments, quantifying how different entropy scales affect the trade-off between exploration and exploitation. The results demonstrate that appropriately tuned entropy parameters can significantly enhance policy robustness, improve convergence speed, and mitigate the risk of premature policy convergence to suboptimal behaviors. Furthermore, we identify conditions under which excessive entropy regularization may hinder policy refinement. The findings provide a nuanced understanding of entropy's dual role, informing the design of more effective policy optimization strategies. These insights contribute to advancing state-of-the-art reinforcement learning and have practical implications for domains such as robotics, autonomous systems, and adaptive decision-making. Keywords: reinforcement learning, policy optimization, entropy regularization, exploration-exploitation trade-off, convergence, policy gradient, actor-critic.
