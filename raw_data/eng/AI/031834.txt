This paper addresses the challenge of learning navigation costs in dynamic environments by leveraging semantic observations and demonstration data. Traditional navigation systems often rely on predefined cost maps, which may not adapt well to complex or changing scenarios. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel approach that integrates semantic information from the environment with expert demonstrations to infer navigation costs. Our method employs a deep learning framework to process semantic observations, such as object labels and spatial relationships, and maps them to cost predictions that guide autonomous navigation. The approach is validated through extensive simulations and real-world experiments, demonstrating its ability to generalize across diverse environments and outperform traditional cost-map-based methods. Key findings reveal that incorporating semantic data significantly improves navigation efficiency and adaptability, particularly in unstructured or cluttered spaces. This research contributes a scalable and interpretable solution for cost learning in autonomous systems, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous vehicles, and smart mobility. Keywords: navigation costs, semantic observations, demonstration learning, autonomous systems, deep learning.
