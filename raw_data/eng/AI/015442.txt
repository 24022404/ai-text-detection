Pedestrian detection is a critical task in computer vision, especially for applications like autonomous driving and video surveillance. Existing Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based methods often struggle with occlusions and variations in pedestrian appearance. This paper introduces a novel Semantic Self-Attention CNN (SSA-CNN) to enhance pedestrian detection performance. SSA-CNN incorporates a semantic self-attention mechanism that adaptively learns and emphasizes important features based on semantic context within the image. This allows the network to better handle partial occlusions and variations in pedestrian pose and clothing. The proposed architecture comprises a feature extraction backbone followed by a semantic self-attention module and a detection head. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including Caltech and CityPersons, demonstrate that SSA-CNN achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of detection accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the effectiveness of semantic self-attention in improving pedestrian detection by focusing on relevant features and suppressing noise. Keywords: Pedestrian Detection, Convolutional Neural Network, Self-Attention, Semantic Segmentation, Computer Vision.
