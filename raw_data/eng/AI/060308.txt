In many applications, such as image retrieval and computer vision, computing the Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) is essential for comparing probability distributions. However, the exact computation of EMD is computationally expensive, limiting its use in real-time applications. This paper proposes novel low-complexity, data-parallel approximations to the Earth Mover's Distance, aiming to significantly reduce computational demands while maintaining accuracy.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a set of parallelizable algorithms designed to approximate the EMD with lower complexity. These algorithms leverage data-parallelism to achieve efficient computation by distributing the workload across multiple processing units, making them particularly suitable for modern hardware architectures. Our methods utilize innovative techniques such as reduced dimensionality projections and iterative refinement to improve approximation accuracy while keeping computational overhead low.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the proposed approximations offer substantial reductions in execution time compared to standard EMD computation, without significant loss of accuracy. Our methods achieve up to a 90% decrease in computation time on large-scale datasets, with approximation errors consistently below a minimal threshold. Comparisons against existing approximation techniques highlight our models' superiority in both performance and scalability.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduced low-complexity data-parallel approximations for EMD computation enable practical applications of this metric in real-time and large-scale scenarios. This research contributes an effective balance between speed and accuracy, broadening the potential of EMD in various domains such as machine learning, image analysis, and multimedia database querying. Our approach paves the way for enhanced analysis and processing of complex data structures in real-time environments.

Keywords: Earth Mover's Distance, data-parallelism, low-complexity approximations, probabilistic distance metrics, computational efficiency, real-time applications.
