**Abstract**

This paper introduces the Natural Wake-Sleep Algorithm, a biologically inspired approach to neural network training that mimics the alternating phases of wakefulness and sleep observed in the human brain. The primary objective is to improve both the efficiency and robustness of artificial neural networks by leveraging distinct learning strategies during different operational phases. In the wake phase, the algorithm employs supervised learning to optimize network parameters based on observed input-output pairs, while in the sleep phase, it utilizes unsupervised reconstruction to refine internal representations and reinforce learned features. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the Natural Wake-Sleep Algorithm achieves competitive accuracy with reduced overfitting compared to traditional training techniques, showing particular strength in scenarios with limited labeled data. The key findings highlight enhanced generalization, scalability, and adaptability to various network architectures, including deep and recurrent models. This approach offers significant implications for the development of more resilient and self-organizing artificial intelligence systems, bridging the gap between biological processes and machine learning. Keywords: wake-sleep, neural networks, unsupervised learning, representation learning, biologically inspired algorithms.
