Graph spectral learning methods are powerful tools for analyzing network data, but their computational cost often limits their applicability to large-scale graphs. This paper introduces GRASPEL, a novel algorithm for Graph Spectral Learning at Scale, designed to address this limitation. GRASPEL leverages stochastic optimization techniques and efficient sparse matrix approximations to significantly reduce the memory footprint and computational time required for eigenvector computation. We present a theoretical analysis demonstrating GRASPEL's convergence properties and computational complexity. Empirical evaluations on several large real-world graphs, including social networks and web graphs, show that GRASPEL achieves comparable or superior accuracy to existing spectral learning methods, while exhibiting orders-of-magnitude speedups. The results demonstrate that GRASPEL enables the application of graph spectral learning to previously intractable large-scale datasets, opening new avenues for network analysis and machine learning. Keywords: Graph Spectral Learning, Large-Scale Graphs, Stochastic Optimization, Eigenvector Computation, Network Analysis.
