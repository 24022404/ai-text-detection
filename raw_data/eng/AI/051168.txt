This paper re-examines the realm of continual learning, a paradigm where models learn from a stream of data over time, with the goal of improving their performance on sequential tasks. Our objective is to provide a comprehensive categorization of continual learning scenarios, highlighting the nuances and challenges inherent to each. We employ a multi-faceted approach, combining empirical analysis and theoretical insights to evaluate the efficacy of existing methods. Our results show that simple, well-designed baselines can often match or surpass the performance of more complex, specialized continual learning algorithms. We find that the choice of baseline has a significant impact on the perceived effectiveness of continual learning techniques, and that a more rigorous evaluation protocol is necessary to accurately assess progress in the field. Our research contributes to the development of more robust and generalizable continual learning systems, with potential applications in areas such as autonomous systems, lifelong learning, and edge AI. Key findings and contributions include the introduction of a novel categorization framework, the demonstration of strong baseline performance, and a call to action for more transparent and reproducible evaluation practices in continual learning research, with relevant keywords including continual learning, lifelong learning, and strong baselines.
