This paper addresses the enhancement of object detection systems by integrating uncertainty estimation into bounding box regression, aiming to improve the precision and reliability of object localization in various applications, including autonomous vehicles and surveillance.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel approach to bounding box regression that incorporates uncertainty estimation, utilizing a probabilistic framework to predict not only the location and dimensions of bounding boxes but also the associated confidence levels. Our model leverages a modified convolutional neural network architecture to output both mean and variance parameters for bounding box predictions, providing a comprehensive understanding of prediction reliability.

Results/Findings: Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets reveal that our method significantly enhances object detection performance compared to traditional deterministic approaches. The incorporation of uncertainty allows for more robust handling of ambiguous scenarios, resulting in superior mean Average Precision (mAP) scores and improved handling of overlapping or occluded objects. Furthermore, our approach demonstrates better adaptability to varying environmental conditions and dataset biases.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of uncertainty into bounding box regression presents a substantial advancement in object detection accuracy and reliability. This research has significant implications for developing more trustworthy and interpretable AI systems in safety-critical domains, such as autonomous driving and intelligent surveillance systems. The proposed method sets a new standard for future studies aiming to incorporate uncertainty in object detection models, paving the way for innovations in AI-driven detection and recognition tasks.

Keywords: Object Detection, Bounding Box Regression, Uncertainty Estimation, Neural Networks, Autonomous Vehicles, Surveillance, Mean Average Precision (mAP).
