Trajectory prediction is a critical challenge in understanding and modeling social interactions, particularly in dynamic environments such as crowded spaces or autonomous systems. This paper introduces the *Recursive Social Behavior Graph (RSBG)*, a novel framework designed to enhance the accuracy of trajectory prediction by capturing the recursive nature of human social behaviors. The RSBG model integrates graph-based representations with recursive neural networks to dynamically model interactions between individuals and their evolving social contexts. By leveraging spatiotemporal data, the framework recursively updates the graph structure to reflect real-time behavioral changes, enabling more precise predictions. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that RSBG outperforms existing methods, achieving significant improvements in prediction accuracy and robustness. The proposed approach not only advances the state-of-the-art in trajectory prediction but also offers practical applications in areas such as autonomous navigation, crowd management, and human-robot interaction. Key contributions include the recursive modeling of social behaviors, a scalable graph-based architecture, and enhanced interpretability of interaction dynamics. Keywords: trajectory prediction, social behavior modeling, recursive neural networks, graph-based learning, spatiotemporal analysis.
