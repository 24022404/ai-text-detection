This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing energy efficiency in snake-like robotic locomotion through the application of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL). The objective is to develop a gait policy that minimizes energy consumption while maintaining effective movement across diverse terrains. We propose a novel DRL-based framework that trains a neural network to learn energy-efficient locomotion strategies by interacting with a simulated snake robot model. The approach leverages a reward function designed to balance energy expenditure and movement efficiency, enabling the robot to adapt its gait dynamically. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed policy achieves significant energy savings compared to traditional gait control methods, with improvements of up to 30% in energy efficiency across flat, uneven, and obstacle-laden surfaces. The findings highlight the potential of DRL in enhancing the autonomy and sustainability of robotic systems, particularly in applications requiring prolonged operation in resource-constrained environments. This research contributes to the advancement of energy-aware robotics and provides a foundation for future work on adaptive locomotion strategies for complex robotic systems.  

**Keywords**: Snake robotics, Deep Reinforcement Learning, energy efficiency, locomotion gait, adaptive control, robotic autonomy.
