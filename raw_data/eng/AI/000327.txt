Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have shown vulnerability to adversarial attacks, raising concerns about their reliability in security-sensitive applications. This paper introduces a novel adversarial immunization framework to enhance the certifiable robustness of GNNs against node injection attacks. Our method strategically perturbs the graph structure during training by injecting carefully crafted adversarial nodes designed to minimize the certified radius of neighboring benign nodes. We formulate the adversarial node injection as a bi-level optimization problem, where the inner level seeks to maximize the reduction in certified radius and the outer level optimizes the GNN parameters. We propose an efficient approximation to solve this problem, leveraging gradient-based techniques. Empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our adversarial immunization significantly improves the certifiable robustness of GNNs compared to state-of-the-art defense mechanisms, without sacrificing clean accuracy. The results highlight the effectiveness of adversarial training for certifiable graph defense and offer a promising direction for developing robust GNNs.

Keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Adversarial Attacks, Certifiable Robustness, Graph Defense, Adversarial Training, Node Injection.
