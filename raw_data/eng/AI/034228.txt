This research presents LSENet, a novel deep learning framework designed to enhance the detection of multi-class ocean fronts by incorporating both locational and seasonal information. Ocean fronts are critical oceanographic features influencing marine ecosystems and climate patterns. Accurate detection of these fronts is essential for various applications, including environmental monitoring and marine resource management.

Methods/Approach: LSENet is developed using a hybrid neural network architecture that integrates convolutional layers with attention mechanisms to process satellite imagery data. The network uniquely incorporates geographical location and seasonal variations as additional input features, allowing the model to adapt to region-specific and time-specific patterns. This framework enables more accurate and robust front detection across different oceanic regions and seasons.

Results/Findings: The proposed LSENet model demonstrates superior performance in detecting ocean fronts compared to traditional methods and existing deep learning models. Evaluation results show a significant increase in detection accuracy, with improvements in both precision and recall metrics. The model's adaptability to a range of seasonal and locational variations was validated across diverse oceanic datasets, highlighting its robustness.

Conclusion/Implications: LSENet represents a substantial advancement in ocean front detection methodologies by effectively integrating locational and seasonal data, setting a new benchmark for accuracy and applicability. The research provides valuable insights into the influence of contextual factors in environmental data analysis. Its application extends to enhancing marine ecosystem studies, improving climate models, and supporting sustainable marine resource management. This framework serves as a step forward in the development of intelligent and context-aware systems for oceanographic applications.

Keywords: LSENet, ocean front detection, deep learning, convolutional network, location awareness, seasonality, satellite imagery, environmental monitoring.
