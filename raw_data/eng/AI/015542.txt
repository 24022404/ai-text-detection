Understanding and interpreting groups of data points in low-dimensional representations is a crucial challenge in modern data analysis, particularly with the widespread use of dimensionality reduction techniques such as t-SNE, PCA, and UMAP in visualization and exploratory analysis. This paper addresses the problem of systematically explaining why specific groups of points form clusters or patterns in low-dimensional embeddings. We propose a novel framework that leverages feature attribution, cluster analysis, and class-based interpretation to identify and quantify the key factors that contribute to group formation in reduced-dimensional spaces. Our method integrates advanced clustering algorithms with interpretable machine learning models to generate human-understandable explanations for groupings, providing insights into the underlying high-dimensional data structure. Experimental results on benchmark image, text, and biological datasets demonstrate that our approach produces meaningful, accurate explanations that align with known data labels and domain-relevant features. We further compare our method against existing explanation and interpretation techniques, showing improved relevance and clarity in the identified explanatory features. This research enhances the transparency and interpretability of dimensionality reduction results, supporting better decision-making in tasks such as data exploration, anomaly detection, and knowledge discovery. Keywords: dimensionality reduction, clustering, data visualization, interpretability, feature attribution, t-SNE, UMAP, PCA.
