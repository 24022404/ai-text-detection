Action recognition in video sequences is a fundamental problem in computer vision with applications in surveillance, robotics, and human-computer interaction. Current approaches often rely on single-stream architectures, limiting their ability to capture diverse and complementary features. This paper introduces "Three Branches," a novel action recognition framework designed to extract richer feature representations by employing three distinct branches: a spatial branch focused on appearance, a temporal branch capturing motion dynamics, and a context branch modeling long-range dependencies. Each branch is implemented using convolutional neural networks tailored to its specific task. We evaluate our approach on several benchmark datasets, including UCF101 and HMDB51, demonstrating significant improvements in accuracy compared to state-of-the-art single-stream and two-stream methods. The experimental results highlight the effectiveness of integrating multi-faceted features for robust action recognition. Our findings suggest that capturing spatial, temporal, and contextual cues in parallel provides a more comprehensive understanding of human actions, leading to enhanced performance.
Keywords: Action Recognition, Video Analysis, Convolutional Neural Networks, Multi-Stream Networks, Deep Learning.
