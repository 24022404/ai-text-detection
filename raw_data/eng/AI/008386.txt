This paper addresses the challenge of monocular 3D object detection, which traditionally relies on extrinsic parameters, creating limitations in flexibility and efficiency. Our research introduces a novel approach that eliminates the need for these extrinsic parameters, improving the adaptability of 3D object detection systems in real-world scenarios.

Methods/Approach: We propose a pioneering algorithm that leverages a monocular camera setup to accomplish 3D object detection without dependency on extrinsic calibration data. Our model incorporates deep learning techniques and advanced data processing to perform intrinsic parameter estimation directly from image sequences. This intrinsic-only framework utilizes innovative machine learning algorithms to enhance spatial perception and dimensional accuracy.

Results/Findings: The proposed approach was evaluated using standard benchmarks and datasets, demonstrating competitive performance and robustness compared to traditional parameter-dependent methods. Notably, our system achieved enhanced detection accuracy and processing speed, maintaining high precision in diverse environments. Additionally, the approach was tested under varied lighting and spatial conditions, affirming its reliability and versatility.

Conclusion/Implications: Our extrinsic parameter-free approach marks a significant advancement in monocular 3D object detection, offering a more practical and adaptable solution for applications in autonomous vehicles, augmented reality, and robotic navigation. By removing the reliance on extrinsic parameters, the system facilitates smoother integration into existing platforms and reduces the complexity of 3D detection tasks, suggesting impactful contributions to the field and providing a path towards more resilient and scalable 3D perception systems.

Keywords: Monocular 3D detection, extrinsic parameters, intrinsic parameter estimation, deep learning, autonomous vehicles, spatial perception.
