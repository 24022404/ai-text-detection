Understanding and predicting path choice behavior is fundamental in domains such as transportation, robotics, and network analysis. This paper presents a comprehensive tutorial on recursive models for analyzing and forecasting path choice decisions. We begin by outlining the primary challenges in modeling the sequential and hierarchical nature of path selection in decision-making environments. The tutorial introduces the theoretical underpinnings of recursive choice models, detailing their mathematical structure and the rationale behind treating path selection as a series of embedded decisions. We systematically describe methodologies for parameter estimation, model calibration, and simulation, highlighting commonly used algorithms such as Expectation-Maximization and dynamic programming techniques. Practical examples illustrate how recursive models capture dependencies and behavioral patterns more effectively than traditional, non-recursive approaches. Experimental results, using both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrate the superior predictive performance and interpretability offered by recursive frameworks compared to conventional models. We also discuss best practices, potential pitfalls, and guidelines for implementing recursive models in various application domains. The tutorial concludes with an overview of future research directions and the potential for integrating recursive models with machine learning and artificial intelligence techniques for enhanced behavioral prediction. Keywords: recursive models, path choice behavior, behavioral modeling, decision analysis, prediction, transportation systems, network analysis.
