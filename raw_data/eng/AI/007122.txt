This paper investigates the stabilization of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) through spectral normalization, a technique that has been widely adopted to improve the training stability of GANs. Our objective is to provide an in-depth analysis of the underlying mechanisms that make spectral normalization effective in stabilizing GANs. We employ a combination of theoretical and empirical methods to understand how spectral normalization influences the dynamics of GAN training. Our results show that spectral normalization helps to control the Lipschitz constant of the discriminator, which in turn prevents the explosion of gradients during training. We also propose improvements to the existing spectral normalization technique, including a novel regularization method that further enhances the stability of GANs. Our experiments demonstrate that the proposed approach leads to more stable training and improved image quality in various GAN architectures. The key contributions of this work include a deeper understanding of the role of spectral normalization in GANs and the development of a more robust and efficient training method. Our research has important implications for the development of stable and reliable GANs, with potential applications in image and video generation, data augmentation, and style transfer. Key keywords: GANs, spectral normalization, stability, regularization, deep learning, generative models.
