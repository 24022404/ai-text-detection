Image restoration tasks, such as denoising, deblurring, and super-resolution, require methods that effectively capture both local structures and long-range dependencies within images. This paper presents a novel approach utilizing Residual Non-local Attention Networks (RNAN) to address these challenges in image restoration. Our model integrates non-local attention mechanisms within a residual network architecture, enabling the efficient extraction of global contextual information while preserving critical local details. The proposed network employs an attention-guided framework to adaptively focus on informative regions, enhancing feature representation and reducing the influence of noise and artifacts. Extensive experiments were conducted on widely used image restoration datasets, demonstrating that RNAN achieves superior quantitative and qualitative results compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. Notably, our approach delivers improvements in peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM) across multiple restoration tasks. The experimental analyses further illustrate the effectiveness of the non-local attention modules in capturing complex image dependencies. These findings highlight the potential of residual non-local attention frameworks for various image enhancement applications, including photographic restoration, medical imaging, and remote sensing. 

Keywords: image restoration, non-local attention, residual networks, deep learning, image denoising, super-resolution, image deblurring
