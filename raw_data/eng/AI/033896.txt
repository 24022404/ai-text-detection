Action recognition in video data is a critical task in computer vision, with applications ranging from surveillance to human-computer interaction. This paper introduces a novel Hierarchical Multi-scale Attention Network (HMAN) designed to improve the accuracy and robustness of action recognition by leveraging multi-scale feature extraction and attention mechanisms. The proposed approach addresses the challenge of capturing both fine-grained details and global context in complex video sequences. HMAN employs a hierarchical architecture that processes spatial and temporal information at multiple scales, enabling the model to focus on relevant regions and time intervals through self-attention mechanisms. Experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets such as UCF101 and HMDB51, demonstrating that HMAN achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight the effectiveness of the multi-scale attention framework in handling diverse action patterns and occlusions. This research contributes a scalable and interpretable solution for action recognition, with potential applications in real-time video analysis and intelligent systems. Key innovations include the integration of hierarchical feature learning and adaptive attention, which enhance the model's ability to generalize across varied datasets. Keywords: action recognition, multi-scale attention, hierarchical networks, video analysis, computer vision.
