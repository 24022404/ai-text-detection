This paper addresses the challenge of zero-shot learning in the domain of 3D point cloud objects, aiming to extend its applicability beyond traditional object classifications. Zero-shot learning (ZSL) is a paradigmatic advancement that enables models to recognize unseen categories based on knowledge transferred from seen classes, thereby reducing the dependency on extensive labeled datasets. Our approach involves developing a novel neural architecture that leverages spatial geometric features and semantic embeddings to facilitate effective ZSL on 3D point clouds. Utilizing a combination of graph convolutional networks and a semantic alignment mechanism, the model captures intricate relationships within and across object categories. This approach enables the system to generalize from known to novel classes, demonstrating superior performance over conventional models in terms of classification accuracy on benchmark datasets. Additionally, our research explores the scalability of the model to various applications, including robotic perception and augmented reality, highlighting its robust adaptability. The findings indicate significant improvements in the recognition of unseen 3D objects and suggest promising directions for future research in extending zero-shot capabilities to more complex and diverse data forms. This work not only sets a new benchmark for ZSL in 3D environments but also opens avenues for further investigation into more dynamic and adaptable AI systems in real-world scenarios. Key keywords include zero-shot learning, 3D point clouds, semantic embeddings, graph convolutional networks, and object recognition.
