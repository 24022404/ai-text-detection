This paper presents a novel approach utilizing Graph Attention Networks (GATs) for enhancing microwave imaging, specifically targeting the detection and analysis of brain anomalies. Microwave imaging has emerged as a promising non-invasive technique for medical diagnostics, and this study aims to improve its precision and reliability by integrating advanced machine learning models.

Methods/Approach: Our approach employs Graph Attention Networks, a state-of-the-art deep learning framework that extends traditional graph convolutional networks by incorporating attention mechanisms. This enables the model to dynamically focus on the most informative parts of the input data, thereby enhancing feature extraction and representation. The model is trained on synthetic and real-world datasets to ensure robustness and generalization in detecting anomalies within brain tissues.

Results/Findings: The proposed GAT-based method demonstrates superior performance over conventional imaging techniques and baseline deep learning models, as evidenced by increased accuracy and reduced false positive rates in anomaly detection tasks. The system successfully identifies diverse types of anomalies, showcasing its adaptability and effectiveness across different scenarios. Comparative analysis reveals that our model achieves a notable improvement in imaging clarity and anomaly localization.

Conclusion/Implications: This study underscores the potential for Graph Attention Networks to revolutionize microwave imaging applications in medical diagnostics. The enhanced capability to accurately detect brain anomalies opens avenues for improved clinical outcomes and highlights the interdisciplinary benefits of combining advanced AI with traditional imaging techniques. Our findings suggest promising future applications in real-time medical diagnostics and treatment monitoring, heralding a new era of intelligent health diagnostics.

Keywords: Graph Attention Network, microwave imaging, brain anomaly detection, attention mechanism, medical diagnostics, machine learning, deep learning, non-invasive imaging.
