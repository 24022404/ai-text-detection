Skeleton-based human action recognition has gained prominence due to its robustness against variations in appearance and background. However, the vulnerability of these systems to adversarial attacks remains a significant concern. This paper investigates the susceptibility of skeleton-based action recognition models to carefully crafted adversarial perturbations. We propose a novel adversarial attack method, termed "Joint-Perturbation Attack (JPA)," which strategically perturbs the 3D joint coordinates of human skeletons to mislead action classification. JPA optimizes perturbations by considering the kinematic relationships between joints, enhancing attack stealthiness and transferability. We evaluate JPA against state-of-the-art skeleton-based action recognition models on benchmark datasets, including NTU-RGB+D and Kinetics Skeleton. Experimental results demonstrate that JPA significantly degrades the performance of these models, achieving high attack success rates with minimal perturbation magnitudes. Furthermore, we analyze the transferability of adversarial examples generated by JPA across different model architectures. Our findings highlight the critical need for developing robust defense mechanisms to safeguard skeleton-based action recognition systems against adversarial threats. Keywords: Adversarial Attack, Human Action Recognition, Skeleton Data, Deep Learning, Security, Robustness.
