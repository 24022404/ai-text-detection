Medical image segmentation is a critical task in healthcare, where deep learning models have shown remarkable success. However, the uncertainty in model predictions, often influenced by variability in human annotations, remains a significant challenge. This study investigates the impact of individual rater style on the uncertainty estimates of deep learning models in medical imaging segmentation. We propose a framework that quantifies the variability in annotations across multiple raters and evaluates its effect on model confidence and performance. Using a dataset of annotated medical images, we train a state-of-the-art deep learning segmentation model and analyze its uncertainty metrics under different rater styles. Our results demonstrate that individual rater biases significantly influence model uncertainty, leading to inconsistent predictions in regions with high annotation variability. Furthermore, we introduce a novel uncertainty calibration method that incorporates rater-specific information to improve model reliability. The findings highlight the importance of accounting for rater variability in medical imaging tasks and provide actionable insights for developing more robust and trustworthy segmentation models. This work contributes to advancing the interpretability and reliability of deep learning systems in clinical applications, with potential implications for improving diagnostic accuracy and patient outcomes.  

**Keywords**: medical imaging, deep learning, segmentation, uncertainty estimation, rater variability, annotation bias, model calibration.
