Knowledge Graphs (KGs) are widely used for representing structured information, but their utility is often hindered by noise and errors in the data. This paper addresses the challenge of error detection in noisy KGs by proposing a novel approach that leverages graph embeddings guided by path-ranking methods. The objective is to enhance the accuracy of error detection by incorporating relational paths that capture contextual dependencies within the graph. Our method integrates path-ranking algorithms with graph embedding techniques to identify inconsistencies and anomalies in the KG structure. We evaluate the proposed approach on benchmark datasets, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art methods. Results demonstrate significant improvements in error detection accuracy, with our method outperforming existing techniques in identifying both structural and semantic errors. The findings highlight the effectiveness of combining path-based reasoning with graph embeddings for robust error detection. This research contributes to the advancement of KG quality assurance, offering a scalable and interpretable solution for real-world applications in domains such as recommendation systems, semantic search, and data integration. Key innovations include the integration of path-ranking mechanisms into embedding frameworks and the development of a unified model for error detection. Keywords: Knowledge Graphs, Graph Embeddings, Path-Ranking, Error Detection, Noise Reduction, Semantic Analysis.
