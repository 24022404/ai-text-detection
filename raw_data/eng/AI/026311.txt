Transformer-based architectures have shown remarkable success in natural language processing and are increasingly explored in computer vision. This paper investigates the application of transformers to object detection, addressing limitations of existing convolutional neural network (CNN)-based methods. We propose a novel transformer-based object detection framework that leverages self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies between image regions, enabling more accurate and context-aware object localization and classification. Our approach replaces the traditional CNN backbone with a transformer encoder, followed by a lightweight detection head. Experimental results on the COCO dataset demonstrate that our transformer-based detector achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art CNN-based detectors, while offering advantages in terms of architectural simplicity and the ability to model global context. Furthermore, we analyze the impact of different transformer configurations and attention mechanisms on detection accuracy. This research provides insights into the potential of transformers for object detection and opens avenues for future exploration in end-to-end object detection pipelines. Keywords: object detection, transformer, self-attention, computer vision, deep learning.
