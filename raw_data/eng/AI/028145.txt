This paper presents a novel approach for predicting Remaining Useful Life (RUL) of complex systems by leveraging time series embeddings generated through Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs). The objective is to improve the accuracy and reliability of RUL estimations, which are essential for predictive maintenance and asset management in industries such as manufacturing, transportation, and energy. Our method utilizes sequences of sensor measurements to capture temporal dependencies and operational patterns, transforming raw time series data into informative embeddings using long short-term memory (LSTM) based architectures. These embeddings serve as robust feature representations for a downstream regression model tasked with RUL prediction. We validate our approach on publicly available benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in prediction accuracy and robustness compared to traditional feature engineering and other machine learning baselines. The findings indicate that time series embeddings derived from RNNs effectively encapsulate complex temporal dynamics, enabling earlier and more precise estimation of equipment failure. This research contributes to the field by introducing an efficient and scalable framework for RUL prediction that minimizes manual feature extraction while maximizing predictive performance. Keywords: Remaining Useful Life, RUL prediction, time series embeddings, recurrent neural networks, LSTM, predictive maintenance.
