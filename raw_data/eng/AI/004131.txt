Reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms often face challenges in balancing exploration and exploitation, particularly in environments with large state-action spaces. This paper addresses the problem of achieving optimal regret bounds in RL by focusing on posterior sampling, a Bayesian approach that leverages uncertainty to guide decision-making. We propose a novel posterior sampling-based RL algorithm and analyze its worst-case regret bounds in episodic Markov decision processes (MDPs). Our approach integrates posterior updates with efficient exploration strategies, ensuring robust performance across diverse environments. Theoretical analysis demonstrates that the proposed algorithm achieves a worst-case regret bound of \( O(\sqrt{HSAT}) \), where \( H \) is the horizon length, \( S \) is the number of states, \( A \) is the number of actions, and \( T \) is the total number of time steps. This result matches the lower bound for RL in MDPs, indicating the algorithm's near-optimality. Empirical evaluations on benchmark environments further validate the algorithm's effectiveness, showcasing its ability to outperform existing methods in terms of cumulative regret and sample efficiency. The findings highlight the potential of posterior sampling as a scalable and theoretically grounded solution for RL, with implications for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and decision-making under uncertainty.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement learning, posterior sampling, regret bounds, Markov decision processes, exploration-exploitation tradeoff.
