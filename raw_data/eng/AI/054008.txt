Visual Speech Recognition (VSR) aims to decode speech by analyzing lip movements. A major challenge lies in recognizing new classes (words or phrases) without retraining, known as zero-shot learning. This paper introduces a novel approach leveraging Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to synthesize visual representations of unseen classes. Our method employs a conditional GAN, trained on seen classes, to generate realistic lip movement sequences conditioned on phonetic embeddings of new classes. These synthetic samples are then used to augment the training data, enabling a classifier to recognize unseen classes. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our GAN-based approach significantly improves zero-shot VSR performance compared to existing methods. Specifically, we achieve a relative improvement of X% in accuracy for unseen classes. The generated visual data effectively bridges the gap between seen and unseen classes, enhancing the generalization capability of VSR systems. Our work provides a promising direction for developing more flexible and adaptable VSR systems. Keywords: Visual Speech Recognition, Zero-shot Learning, GAN, Generative Model, Lip Reading.
