Conditional Random Fields (CRFs) are a powerful probabilistic framework widely used in sequence modeling tasks, such as natural language processing, computer vision, and bioinformatics. This paper provides an introduction to CRFs, focusing on their theoretical foundations and practical applications. The objective is to explain how CRFs address the limitations of traditional models like Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) by incorporating global dependencies and avoiding strong independence assumptions. We describe the mathematical formulation of CRFs, including their conditional probability distribution and feature functions, as well as the training process using maximum likelihood estimation. Additionally, we discuss inference techniques, such as the Viterbi algorithm, for predicting the most likely sequence of labels. The paper highlights the advantages of CRFs in handling complex, structured data and their superior performance in tasks like named entity recognition, part-of-speech tagging, and image segmentation. Finally, we explore recent advancements, including deep learning-based CRF variants, and their potential for improving accuracy and scalability. This work aims to serve as a foundational resource for researchers and practitioners seeking to understand and apply CRFs in real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Conditional Random Fields, sequence modeling, probabilistic models, natural language processing, structured prediction, deep learning.
