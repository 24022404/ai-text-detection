Abstract  
We present a pillar-based object detection framework tailored for real-time perception in autonomous driving. Unlike conventional voxel- or point-based approaches, our method partitions 3D LiDAR point clouds into evenly spaced vertical pillars and applies a novel Pillar Feature Encoding (PFE) module to aggregate geometric and intensity information within each pillar. Encoded pillar features are projected onto a 2D pseudo-image and processed through a lightweight convolutional backbone to learn multi-scale spatial representations. A single-stage detection head then predicts oriented 3D bounding boxes and class scores for vehicles, pedestrians, and cyclists.  

Evaluated on the KITTI benchmark, our model attains 82.4% mean average precision (mAP) on the moderate difficulty split while running at 62 frames per second on a single GPU—approximately twice the inference speed of leading voxel-based detectors without sacrificing accuracy. Ablation studies demonstrate that the PFE module and 2D CNN architecture each contribute significant gains in both detection performance and computational efficiency.  

By unifying efficient pillar encoding with fast convolutional processing, our approach achieves a favorable balance between high detection accuracy and low latency. This makes it well suited for deployment in safety-critical autonomous vehicles, where real-time 3D object detection is paramount.  

Keywords: pillar-based object detection, LiDAR perception, autonomous driving, real-time 3D detection, convolutional neural network
