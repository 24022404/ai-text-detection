The objective of this research is to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) by introducing the BRP-NAS framework, which leverages Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) for accurate predictive modeling. Traditional NAS methods often suffer from extensive computational burdens due to the need to evaluate numerous architectures. Our approach aims to address this issue by employing GCNs to capture the structural information of neural architectures, thus enabling a prediction-based NAS that efficiently identifies optimal architectures with reduced computational cost. The methodology involves the encoding of neural architectures into graph representations and utilizing GCNs to predict their performance, guiding the search process towards more promising architectures. Our results demonstrate that BRP-NAS outperforms conventional NAS techniques by significantly reducing search time while maintaining competitive model accuracy. Additionally, experiments show that our approach enhances the capability of NAS to explore a wider architectural space, leading to novel and effective neural designs. The conclusions drawn from this study highlight the potential of GCN-integration in NAS processes, offering a scalable solution for fast and precise architecture search that can be applied to a variety of machine learning tasks. This research contributes to the field by enhancing NAS efficiency and expanding its applicability, promising advancements in the development of resource-efficient AI models. Keywords: Neural Architecture Search, Graph Convolutional Networks, BRP-NAS, predictive modeling, machine learning optimization.
