This research addresses the challenge of eliminating undesirable information from self-supervised representations, a common issue in contrastive learning frameworks. The objective of this study is to enhance the quality of learned representations by specifically reducing the influence of irrelevant or misleading features.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel approach called Conditional Contrastive Learning (CCL), which incorporates side information as a condition during the representation learning process. This method systematically differentiates between desirable and undesirable data attributes, refining the model's ability to focus on relevant features. The CCL framework employs a dual-branch contrastive loss function, effectively penalizing unwanted features while reinforcing the positive attributes essential for robust representation.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that Conditional Contrastive Learning significantly improves the quality of self-supervised representations across various datasets and benchmarks. Our approach shows superior capability in filtering out noise and irrelevant information compared to traditional contrastive learning models. This leads to enhanced performance in downstream tasks such as image classification and information retrieval, exhibiting notable improvements over baseline methods.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed Conditional Contrastive Learning framework advances the field of self-supervised learning by offering a reliable solution for removing undesirable information from learned representations. This research contributes to developing more precise and focused representations that can be effectively utilized in a wide range of applications, including computer vision and natural language processing, thereby broadening the scope and efficacy of self-supervised learning paradigms. Keywords: Self-supervised Learning, Contrastive Learning, Representation Learning, Conditional Contrastive Learning, Noise Reduction.
