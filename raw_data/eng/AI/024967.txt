This paper introduces a novel method for generating saliency maps in Atari agents by leveraging attention mechanisms, effectively providing "free-lunch" saliency without requiring additional computational overhead or modifications to the agent's architecture. We extract attention weights from existing attention modules within deep reinforcement learning (DRL) agents, repurposing them as visual explanations of the agent's decision-making process. Our approach demonstrates that these attention-based saliency maps can effectively highlight relevant game elements contributing to the agent's actions. We evaluate our method on a diverse set of Atari games, demonstrating its ability to identify key objects and regions crucial for successful gameplay. Furthermore, we compare the quality of our attention-based saliency maps with gradient-based saliency methods, revealing comparable or superior performance in terms of localization and relevance. The proposed technique offers a computationally efficient and easily implementable approach for interpreting and understanding the behavior of DRL agents in complex environments, facilitating improved debugging, and potential insights for curriculum learning strategies. Keywords: Saliency, Attention, Atari, Reinforcement Learning, Explainable AI, Deep Learning.
