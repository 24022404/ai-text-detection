This paper addresses the challenge of 3D face reconstruction using color photometric stereo with uncalibrated near point lights. Traditional methods often rely on calibrated lighting setups, which limit their practicality in real-world scenarios. The objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient approach for reconstructing 3D facial geometry without requiring precise light source calibration. We propose a novel algorithm that leverages color photometric stereo to estimate surface normals and depth information from images captured under varying, uncalibrated near point light conditions. Our method integrates a self-calibration process to infer light source positions and intensities, enabling accurate 3D reconstruction. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves high reconstruction accuracy, outperforming existing methods in terms of both computational efficiency and robustness to lighting variability. The findings highlight the potential of this technique for applications in biometrics, virtual reality, and facial animation, where unconstrained lighting environments are common. This work contributes to advancing 3D reconstruction technologies by eliminating the need for controlled lighting setups, making the process more accessible and scalable.  

**Keywords**: 3D face reconstruction, photometric stereo, uncalibrated lighting, surface normals, self-calibration, near point lights.
