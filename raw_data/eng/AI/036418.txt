This paper presents LumiPath, a novel approach to achieving real-time, physically-based rendering on embedded devices, addressing the challenge of balancing high-quality graphics with the computational constraints inherent in such hardware. The demand for realistic visualization in mobile applications and AR/VR systems necessitates more efficient rendering solutions.

Methods/Approach: LumiPath leverages optimized rendering algorithms designed to exploit the capabilities of modern embedded GPUs, in conjunction with real-time processing techniques. We employ a hybrid rendering pipeline that integrates adaptive sampling, efficient light transport mechanisms, and hardware-accelerated rasterization to enhance performance while maintaining photorealistic quality.

Results/Findings: Our experimental evaluation demonstrates that LumiPath can render complex scenes at interactive frame rates, achieving performance comparable to desktop systems with significantly higher power consumption. Benchmarks show improvements in frame rendering time by up to 40% in comparison to existing mobile rendering solutions while preserving visual fidelity. Additionally, LumiPath effectively scales to different hardware architectures and supports a variety of lighting conditions.

Conclusion/Implications: LumiPath represents a significant advancement in the field of real-time rendering for embedded systems, expanding the potential for immersive applications in mobile and low-power environments. By optimizing the rendering pipeline, this research paves the way for more accessible AR/VR experiences and high-fidelity graphics on portable devices. Future work could explore the integration of machine learning techniques to further enhance rendering accuracy and performance adaptability. Key Keywords: real-time rendering, physically-based rendering, embedded devices, GPU optimization, mobile graphics, AR/VR.
