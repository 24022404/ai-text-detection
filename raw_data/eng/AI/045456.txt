The increasing reliance on black box machine learning models in high-stakes decision-making, such as healthcare, criminal justice, and finance, has raised significant concerns regarding transparency, accountability, and trust. While post-hoc explanation methods have been proposed to interpret these models, they often fail to provide reliable or actionable insights, potentially leading to flawed decisions. This paper argues for a paradigm shift: instead of explaining opaque models, we advocate for the use of inherently interpretable models in high-stakes scenarios. We present a comprehensive framework for designing and evaluating interpretable models, emphasizing their ability to provide clear, transparent, and auditable decision-making processes. Through empirical evaluations across multiple domains, we demonstrate that interpretable models can achieve competitive performance while offering superior transparency and reliability compared to black box alternatives. Our findings highlight the critical importance of prioritizing interpretability in high-stakes applications, ensuring that decisions are not only accurate but also understandable and justifiable. This research contributes to the growing discourse on ethical AI by providing practical guidelines for adopting interpretable models in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: interpretable models, black box models, high-stakes decisions, transparency, ethical AI, machine learning.
