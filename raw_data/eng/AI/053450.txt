This paper addresses the challenge of transfer reinforcement learning (TRL) in environments where critical contextual information is unobserved or partially observed. Traditional reinforcement learning approaches often assume fully observable states, limiting their effectiveness when environmental contexts—and their variations—are hidden or unavailable. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel TRL framework that leverages latent variable modeling to infer unobserved contextual factors, enabling the transfer of learned knowledge across related tasks with differing or hidden contexts. Our approach integrates context inference into the policy learning process, utilizing a combination of probabilistic modeling and deep reinforcement learning techniques. Experimental evaluations on benchmark environments demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms baseline TRL algorithms in terms of adaptability, sample efficiency, and overall performance in unseen target domains. The results indicate robust policy transfer capabilities even in the presence of substantial context uncertainty. This work contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in reinforcement learning by providing a practical solution for TRL scenarios where contextual information is not fully accessible, opening up new applications in robotics, personalized systems, and real-world adaptive control. Keywords: transfer reinforcement learning, unobserved context, latent variable modeling, policy generalization, adaptability, deep learning.
