Object detection remains a fundamental task in computer vision, with recent emphasis on balancing detection accuracy and computational efficiency. This paper introduces a novel Feature Selective Anchor-Free (FSAF) module designed to enhance single-shot object detection frameworks. The proposed approach eliminates the reliance on predefined anchor boxes, instead employing a dynamic feature selection mechanism that adaptively assigns object instances to the most suitable feature map levels. By integrating the FSAF module into popular single-stage detectors, our method enables the model to learn spatial and semantic characteristics without anchor constraints. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves superior detection accuracy and robustness, particularly for objects with varying scales and aspect ratios, compared to traditional anchor-based and anchor-free detectors. Additionally, the FSAF module incurs minimal computational overhead, facilitating its adoption in real-time detection scenarios. The key contributions of this work include the design of an adaptive feature selection strategy, a fully anchor-free architecture compatible with existing detection frameworks, and empirical validation demonstrating improved performance. This research advances the effectiveness and practicality of single-shot object detectors and offers promising directions for future developments in real-time computer vision applications. Keywords: object detection, anchor-free, feature selection, single-shot detection, computer vision, real-time detection.
