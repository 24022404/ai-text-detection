This paper addresses the challenge of effective information propagation in Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), which are pivotal for tasks involving graph-structured data. Standard approaches often struggle with diverse node types and edge relationships, limiting their ability to capture complex interactions in heterogeneous graphs.

Methods: We propose a novel framework that incorporates heterogeneous aggregations to enhance the propagation process within GNNs. Our approach dynamically adjusts aggregation operations based on node and edge types, utilizing a tailored combination of network architectures and attention-based mechanisms to efficiently integrate information from heterogeneous sources.

Results: Experiments demonstrate that our heterogeneous aggregation framework significantly outperforms traditional GNN models on a diverse set of benchmarks, including node classification, link prediction, and graph classification tasks. Performance gains indicate a notable increase in accuracy and computational efficiency. Moreover, the framework exhibits superior scalability to larger and more complex graph datasets compared to existing methods.

Conclusion: This research introduces a crucial advancement in GNN technology by effectively leveraging node and edge heterogeneity, resulting in improved information propagation. The proposed approach broadens the applicability of GNNs to a wider range of real-world problems involving complex and varied graph structures. Future work may explore extending this method to other graph-related applications, such as social network analysis and recommendation systems.

Keywords: Graph Neural Network, information propagation, heterogeneous graphs, node classification, link prediction, scalability, attention mechanisms.
