This paper explores the application of the Sugeno integral, a non-linear aggregation operator, in the context of binary classification using machine learning. The objective is to investigate whether the Sugeno integral can enhance classification performance by effectively combining multiple decision criteria or features. We propose a novel framework that integrates the Sugeno integral into a machine learning pipeline, leveraging its ability to model interactions between features without requiring explicit probabilistic assumptions. The approach is evaluated on benchmark datasets, comparing its performance against traditional methods such as logistic regression and support vector machines. Experimental results demonstrate that the Sugeno integral-based model achieves competitive accuracy, particularly in scenarios where feature interactions are complex and non-linear. Furthermore, the model exhibits interpretability advantages, as the Sugeno integral provides insights into the relative importance of features and their interactions. This work contributes to the growing body of research on alternative aggregation techniques in machine learning, offering a promising direction for improving classification tasks in domains where interpretability and non-linear feature relationships are critical. Keywords: Sugeno integral, binary classification, machine learning, non-linear aggregation, interpretability.
