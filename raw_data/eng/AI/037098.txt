Coral reef ecosystems are critically important for marine biodiversity but face numerous threats that necessitate efficient, automated monitoring solutions. This paper presents a sparse coral classification framework utilizing deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs), addressing the challenge of accurately identifying coral species in datasets characterized by limited, imbalanced, or sparsely annotated image samples. The proposed approach employs a deep CNN architecture tailored to handle sparse input data, incorporating data augmentation and class-balancing techniques to enhance training effectiveness. Extensive experiments were conducted on underwater coral datasets, demonstrating that the proposed method outperforms traditional machine learning classifiers and baseline CNN models, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. The results indicate significant improvements in classification accuracy, robustness to noise, and generalizability across different reef environments. Our findings suggest that the integration of sparsity-aware deep learning significantly enhances automated coral identification, offering valuable implications for marine conservation initiatives and ongoing large-scale reef monitoring programs. Keywords: sparse data, coral classification, deep convolutional neural networks, image analysis, marine monitoring, biodiversity.
