Synthetic data offers a scalable solution for training depth estimation models, but performance often lags when applied to real-world images due to domain shift. This paper introduces Domain Decluttering, a novel image simplification technique designed to reduce the discrepancy between synthetic and real image distributions. Our method selectively removes complex background elements and irrelevant objects from synthetic images, focusing the model's attention on essential depth cues and scene structure. We evaluate our approach by training a depth estimation network on decluttered synthetic data and testing it on real-world datasets. Results demonstrate significant improvements in depth estimation accuracy compared to models trained on standard synthetic data and other domain adaptation techniques. Furthermore, our approach reduces overfitting to synthetic artifacts, leading to better generalization. Domain Decluttering offers a practical strategy for bridging the synthetic-to-real gap in depth estimation, enhancing the effectiveness of synthetic training data. Keywords: Depth Estimation, Domain Adaptation, Synthetic Data, Domain Shift, Image Simplification, Computer Vision.
