This research addresses the critical challenge of enhancing the accuracy of nanopore base calling, a pivotal task in DNA sequencing where the goal is to determine the sequence of nucleotides from the electrical signal patterns produced by nanopore sensors. Traditional base calling methods often suffer from substantial error rates due to the complex and variable nature of these signals.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel dynamic pooling strategy in the base calling process to improve the interpretation of nanopore data. The approach involves utilizing adaptive pooling layers within the base calling model that can dynamically adjust to the variability seen in nanopore signal data. This method leverages advanced neural network architectures that incorporate dynamic pooling techniques, allowing the model to better capture and process signal variations effectively and efficiently.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate significant improvements in base calling accuracy when employing the dynamic pooling method compared to traditional static approaches. The proposed technique yields an average accuracy increase of X% across various datasets, showcasing superior performance in handling both standard and challenging signal patterns. Furthermore, our method outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques in terms of speed and fidelity, especially in scenarios involving noisy or low-quality signals.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of dynamic pooling in nanopore base calling represents a substantial advancement in the field of DNA sequencing accuracy. This innovation not only enhances the reliability of genomic data analysis but also opens new pathways for its application in clinical genomics and personalized medicine. The flexibility and enhanced performance of this approach have the potential to set a new standard for base calling technologies in the sequencing industry.

Keywords: nanopore sequencing, base calling accuracy, dynamic pooling, neural networks, DNA sequencing, signal variability.
