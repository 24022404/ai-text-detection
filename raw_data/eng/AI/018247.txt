This paper presents a novel gray-box inference framework for structured Gaussian process (GP) models, addressing the need for scalable and interpretable probabilistic modeling in complex data scenarios. Traditional GP models are limited by high computational demands and difficulties in incorporating domain knowledge, while black-box methods often sacrifice interpretability for flexibility. Our approach bridges this gap by enabling users to integrate partial structural information—such as known functional relationships or constraints—into the inference process, thereby leveraging both data-driven learning and expert knowledge. We develop an efficient inference algorithm that exploits the partially specified model structure to reduce computational complexity and improve estimation accuracy. Experimental evaluations on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed gray-box method achieves superior predictive performance and uncertainty quantification compared to standard black-box GP approaches, especially in cases with sparse or structured data. The results highlight the framework’s capability to generalize across various structured modeling tasks in fields such as time series analysis, spatial data modeling, and sensor networks. This work contributes a flexible and interpretable inference paradigm for Gaussian process modeling, with significant implications for applications requiring both prediction accuracy and model transparency. Key keywords: Gaussian processes, structured models, gray-box inference, probabilistic modeling, uncertainty quantification, interpretability, scalable algorithms.
