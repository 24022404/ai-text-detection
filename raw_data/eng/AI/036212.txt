This paper addresses the challenge of learning discriminative prototypes in the context of time-series classification using Dynamic Time Warping (DTW). The objective is to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of prototype-based learning methods by leveraging the strengths of DTW to measure similarities between time-series data. We propose an innovative approach that combines DTW with a prototype learning framework, facilitating the identification of representative examples that capture the underlying patterns within classes. Our method operates by dynamically adjusting prototype representations to better accommodate local variations in time-series sequences. 

Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms traditional techniques on various benchmark datasets, showcasing improved classification accuracy and robustness against noise and distortions in the data. The findings indicate that the integration of DTW not only helps in achieving higher performance metrics but also enriches the interpretability of the learned prototypes. 

In conclusion, this research contributes to the field of time-series analysis by providing a novel framework for learning prototypes that dynamically adapt to input sequences. The implications of our findings suggest practical applications in domains such as speech recognition, bioinformatics, and activity recognition, where accurate time-series classification is essential. 

Keywords: Dynamic Time Warping, time-series classification, discriminative prototypes, prototype learning, machine learning.
