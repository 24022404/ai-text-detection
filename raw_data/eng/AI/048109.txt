This paper presents a novel approach for enhancing residential load control in smart grids by leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for automatic state-time feature extraction within reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks. Traditional RL-based demand response strategies often rely on manually engineered features, which limit scalability and adaptability to diverse and dynamic household environments. In this study, we propose an end-to-end architecture that integrates CNNs to autonomously learn complex temporal and state-dependent patterns from raw time-series data representing household energy consumption, environmental conditions, and device states. The extracted features are then utilized by a deep RL agent to optimize load control policies with the objective of minimizing energy costs and improving grid stability. Experimental results on a simulated residential microgrid demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms conventional RL approaches using manual feature engineering, achieving better energy savings and load shifting performance while maintaining user comfort. Furthermore, the proposed framework exhibits strong generalization across varying residential scenarios and appliance configurations. The findings highlight the potential of deep feature learning in advancing intelligent energy management systems. Keywords: convolutional neural networks, reinforcement learning, state-time feature extraction, residential load control, smart grid, energy management, deep learning.
