This paper addresses the challenge of person re-identification, a critical task in surveillance and security applications. The objective is to develop an effective method for distinguishing between individuals in non-overlapping camera views. To achieve this, we propose a novel Cluster Loss function that enhances the discriminative power of deep neural networks. Our approach leverages clustering principles to optimize the embedding space, allowing for more accurate and efficient person re-identification. The Cluster Loss is designed to minimize the distance between features of the same identity while maximizing the distance between features of different identities. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our method, outperforming state-of-the-art approaches in terms of accuracy and robustness. The key findings highlight the importance of clustering-based loss functions in person re-identification, enabling more reliable and efficient tracking of individuals across multiple cameras. This research contributes to the advancement of person re-identification techniques, with potential applications in intelligent surveillance, security, and access control systems, and is relevant to keywords such as person re-identification, deep learning, clustering, and computer vision.
