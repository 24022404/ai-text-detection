Complex networks, characterized by intricate relational structures, are often analyzed using low-rank matrix representations to facilitate efficient computation and reveal underlying organizational principles. This paper investigates the fundamental limitations of such low-rank approximations for triangle-rich networks, a prevalent feature in many real-world systems. We demonstrate, both theoretically and empirically, that networks exhibiting a high density of triangles inherently resist accurate low-rank representation. Our approach combines spectral analysis with graph-theoretic arguments to establish a lower bound on the rank required to capture the essential structural properties of these networks. Specifically, we show that the reconstruction error incurred by low-rank approximations grows significantly with increasing triangle density. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world networks, including social networks and collaboration networks, validate our theoretical findings. These results suggest that alternative representation methods, such as non-linear embeddings or specialized graph kernels, may be necessary for effective analysis of triangle-rich complex networks. This work has implications for network compression, community detection, and link prediction tasks.
Keywords: Complex Networks, Low-Rank Approximation, Triangle Density, Spectral Analysis, Graph Theory, Network Representation.
