The NIPS 2016 Workshop on Representation Learning in Artificial and Biological Neural Networks (MLINI 2016) aimed to bridge the gap between computational approaches and biological insights into neural processing. The objective was to foster an interdisciplinary dialogue to enhance our understanding of representation learning in both artificial and biological neural systems. The workshop featured a diverse range of methodologies, including advanced machine learning models such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), alongside biologically inspired frameworks. These approaches were explored to uncover common principles and unique aspects of representation across different neural architectures. Key findings highlighted significant parallels in representation learning processes between artificial networks and neural substrates found in biological systems. The workshop also showcased novel contributions demonstrating improved model performance through biologically inspired enhancements, as well as insights into neural plasticity and learning strategies. Overall, MLINI 2016 underscored the potential for cross-pollination between artificial intelligence and neuroscience fields, suggesting novel paths for enhancing machine learning algorithms by leveraging biological principles. Potential applications stemming from this research could advance autonomous systems, neuroinformatics, and cognitive computing. Keywords: representation learning, neural networks, artificial intelligence, neural processing, biologically inspired models.
