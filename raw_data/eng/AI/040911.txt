The rapid growth of deep neural networks has led to significant advancements in various applications, but their increasing complexity poses challenges in terms of computational efficiency and deployment on resource-constrained devices. This paper introduces *Teacher-Class Network (TCN)*, a novel neural network compression mechanism designed to reduce model size and computational overhead while maintaining high performance. The proposed approach leverages a hierarchical knowledge distillation framework, where a single "teacher" network transfers knowledge to multiple "student" networks, each specialized in distinct subtasks. By decomposing the learning process into modular components, TCN achieves efficient model compression without sacrificing accuracy. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that TCN outperforms traditional compression techniques, such as pruning and quantization, in terms of both model size reduction and inference speed. Furthermore, the method exhibits robustness across diverse domains, including image classification and natural language processing. The results highlight the potential of TCN as a scalable solution for deploying lightweight yet powerful neural networks in real-world applications. Key contributions include the hierarchical distillation architecture, task-specific student specialization, and a comprehensive evaluation framework. This work paves the way for more efficient and adaptable neural network designs in resource-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: Neural network compression, knowledge distillation, model efficiency, hierarchical learning, lightweight models.
