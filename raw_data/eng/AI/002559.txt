This paper introduces a novel framework for disentangling controllable and uncontrollable factors of variation in complex environments. Our approach leverages active interaction with the world to identify and isolate these factors. We propose an agent that learns to manipulate environmental variables and observes the resulting changes, thereby distinguishing between factors that respond predictably to its actions (controllable) and those that vary independently (uncontrollable). We employ a deep reinforcement learning architecture coupled with an information-theoretic objective to encourage the agent to learn disentangled representations. Experimental results in simulated environments demonstrate that our method effectively separates controllable and uncontrollable factors, leading to improved downstream task performance in prediction and planning. Specifically, the learned representations enable more accurate predictions of future states and more robust planning in the face of uncontrollable environmental changes. These findings suggest that active interaction is a powerful mechanism for learning disentangled representations of the world. Keywords: Disentanglement, Representation Learning, Reinforcement Learning, Controllable Factors, Uncontrollable Factors, Active Learning.
