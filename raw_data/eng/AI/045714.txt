Co-salient object detection (CoSOD) aims to identify common and prominent objects across multiple related images, a task with important applications in computer vision and multimedia analysis. However, conventional approaches often struggle to effectively leverage the shared characteristics among image groups, leading to limited accuracy and robustness. This paper introduces a novel group collaborative learning framework for Co-salient object detection that jointly explores inter-image relationships and intra-image contextual information. Our method employs a collaborative attention mechanism to enhance feature representation, enabling the model to explicitly capture group-level semantic similarities while preserving unique image-specific features. Extensive experiments on widely used CoSOD benchmarks demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in both quantitative metrics and qualitative results. Moreover, the proposed framework exhibits strong adaptability to diverse object categories and varying group settings. The findings highlight the benefits of collaborative learning in group scenarios, offering valuable insights for future research in object detection and multi-image analysis. Key contributions include the introduction of a group-based collaborative mechanism and a comprehensive evaluation that underscores the effectiveness and generalizability of our approach. Keywords: co-salient object detection, group collaborative learning, attention mechanism, computer vision, multi-image analysis.
