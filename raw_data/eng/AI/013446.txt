The study of scale-free networks, characterized by their power-law degree distributions, is pivotal in understanding complex systems across various domains. This paper addresses the challenge of learning such networks by introducing a novel approach that leverages dynamic node-specific degree priors. The proposed method aims to enhance the accuracy and scalability of network inference by incorporating prior knowledge about node degrees, which adapts dynamically during the learning process. We present a probabilistic framework that integrates these priors into a Bayesian inference model, enabling the estimation of network structures with improved precision. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of both reconstruction accuracy and computational efficiency. The findings highlight the significance of incorporating node-specific degree information in network learning tasks, offering a robust solution for applications in social networks, biological systems, and recommendation systems. This research contributes to the advancement of network inference techniques by providing a scalable and adaptive framework for learning scale-free networks, with potential implications for large-scale data analysis and system modeling.  

**Keywords**: scale-free networks, degree distribution, Bayesian inference, network learning, dynamic priors, node-specific modeling.
