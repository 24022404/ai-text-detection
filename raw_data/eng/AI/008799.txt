This paper investigates the application of metareasoning for dynamic configuration in modular software systems. The research addresses the challenge of optimizing system performance through on-the-fly adjustments, leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) combined with rich contextual representations.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework that integrates RL with a metareasoning layer, allowing the software system to analyze and adapt its configuration dynamically. The framework employs contextual representations to capture diverse system states and environments, facilitating informed decision-making processes. Our approach emphasizes the adaptation capability of the system in real-time, enhancing its responsiveness to changes in operating conditions.

Results/Findings: Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms traditional static configuration techniques, leading to enhanced system efficiency and robustness. The integration of RL with rich contextual representations enables the system to learn optimal configuration strategies on-the-fly, resulting in improved adaptability and performance across various test scenarios. Comparative analysis shows notable performance gains over existing methods, underscoring the effectiveness of our approach.

Conclusion/Implications: The incorporation of metareasoning in modular software systems, through the use of RL and contextual representations, presents a tangible advancement in software adaptability and efficiency. Our findings contribute a significant step toward autonomous systems that can self-optimize without human intervention, potentially transforming how modular systems are designed and managed. This research paves the way for future exploration in dynamic configuration and self-adaptive software solutions.

Keywords: metareasoning, modular software systems, on-the-fly configuration, reinforcement learning, contextual representations, system optimization, dynamic adaptation.
