This paper addresses the challenge of learning relational representations from structured data, a critical task in domains such as knowledge graph reasoning and symbolic AI. We propose a novel approach that leverages auto-encoding logic programs to capture complex relational patterns and dependencies. Unlike traditional methods that rely on fixed feature representations, our framework dynamically encodes relational structures into a latent space, enabling the reconstruction of logical rules and relationships. The proposed model combines probabilistic logic programming with neural auto-encoders, allowing for both interpretability and scalability. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of relational inference accuracy and rule extraction efficiency. Key findings reveal that the model effectively handles noisy and incomplete data, making it suitable for real-world applications. The results highlight the potential of integrating symbolic reasoning with neural architectures, offering a promising direction for advancing relational learning. This work contributes a robust framework for relational representation learning, with implications for areas such as knowledge base completion, automated reasoning, and explainable AI.  

**Keywords**: relational learning, auto-encoding, logic programs, symbolic AI, knowledge representation, neural-symbolic integration.
