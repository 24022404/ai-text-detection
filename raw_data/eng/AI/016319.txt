Color constancy, the ability to perceive consistent colors regardless of illumination, remains a challenging problem in computer vision. This paper introduces a novel approach, the Bag of Color Features (BoCF), for robust color constancy. Unlike traditional methods that rely on low-level image statistics or explicit illuminant estimation, BoCF leverages a statistical representation of color distributions in images. Our method first extracts a diverse set of color features, including RGB, normalized RGB, and opponent color spaces. These features are then quantized into a visual vocabulary, forming a "bag" of color features. The illuminant color is estimated based on the statistical mode of the BoCF representation. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed BoCF approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing statistical and learning-based color constancy algorithms, particularly under challenging illuminations. The BoCF representation provides a robust and efficient means to capture the underlying color characteristics of a scene, leading to improved color constancy and potentially benefiting various vision applications.
Keywords: Color Constancy, Color Features, Illumination Estimation, Image Processing, Computer Vision.
