Objective: Zero-shot learning (ZSL) aims to recognize unseen classes by leveraging auxiliary semantic information, yet existing methods often rely on global features or complex region proposals that overlook fine-grained local cues. This paper introduces a simple and effective framework for localized attribute representations that enhances discriminative power while retaining model interpretability.

Methods: We propose Localized Attribute Encoding (LAE), which partitions convolutional feature maps into semantically meaningful regions and learns attribute detectors directly on these local patches. Each detector is trained to predict the presence of a semantic attribute (e.g., “striped,” “curved beak”) within its region. Local predictions are aggregated via max-pooling to form a compact attribute vector for each image. During inference, class prototypes—constructed from class-level attribute vectors—are matched against image attribute encodings using a cosine similarity score.

Results: Experiments on three standard ZSL benchmarks (AwA2, CUB, SUN) demonstrate that LAE outperforms state-of-the-art global-feature baselines by 5.2–8.7% in zero-shot classification accuracy, while achieving competitive generalized ZSL performance. Ablation studies confirm that region partitioning and local attribute supervision both contribute significantly to accuracy gains, and visualizations highlight improved localization of discriminative attributes.

Conclusion: The proposed LAE framework delivers a lightweight, end-to-end trainable solution for zero-shot learning that harnesses localized semantics without external region proposals. By combining simplicity, interpretability, and strong empirical gains, our method offers a practical step toward more robust recognition of unseen categories.  
Keywords: zero-shot learning, localized attribute representation, convolutional features, semantic attributes, visual interpretability.
