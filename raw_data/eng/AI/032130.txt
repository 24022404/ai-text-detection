Smartphone photo scanning has become a ubiquitous tool for digitizing physical documents, yet real-world challenges such as lighting variations, shadows, and perspective distortions often degrade scan quality. This paper introduces *Deep Photo Scan*, a novel semi-supervised learning framework designed to address these degradation issues effectively. The proposed approach leverages a hybrid model combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and generative adversarial networks (GANs) to enhance image quality while preserving critical details. By utilizing a small labeled dataset alongside a large pool of unlabeled real-world images, the model achieves robust performance across diverse degradation scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in image clarity, edge sharpness, and color accuracy compared to existing methods, with a 15% increase in perceptual quality metrics. The framework also introduces a lightweight post-processing module optimized for mobile deployment, ensuring real-time performance on resource-constrained devices. This research contributes to advancing smartphone-based document digitization by offering a scalable, efficient solution that bridges the gap between supervised and unsupervised learning paradigms. The proposed method has broad applications in mobile computing, digital archiving, and augmented reality, paving the way for more reliable and accessible photo scanning technologies.  

**Keywords**: smartphone photo scanning, semi-supervised learning, image enhancement, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), generative adversarial networks (GANs), mobile computing.
