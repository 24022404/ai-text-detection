Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have achieved remarkable success in representation learning on graph-structured data. However, GNNs often operate primarily in either the spectral or spatial domain, leading to limitations in capturing both global and local graph properties effectively. This paper introduces a novel GNN architecture, Spectral-Spatial Bridging Network (SSBN), designed to seamlessly integrate spectral and spatial information. SSBN employs a multi-head attention mechanism to adaptively weigh and combine features learned through spectral graph convolutions and spatial message passing. We evaluate SSBN on several benchmark graph datasets, including node classification and graph classification tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that SSBN consistently outperforms state-of-the-art GNN models, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and robustness. Specifically, SSBN shows enhanced performance in capturing long-range dependencies and handling noisy graph structures. The proposed approach offers a promising direction for designing more powerful and versatile GNNs by effectively bridging the gap between spectral and spatial domains. Keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Spectral Graph Convolution, Spatial Message Passing, Graph Representation Learning, Multi-Head Attention.
