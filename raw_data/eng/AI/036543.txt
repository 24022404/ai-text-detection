This paper addresses the challenge of detecting building changes in remote sensing images, an essential task for urban development monitoring, disaster assessment, and land-use management. We propose a novel Dual Task Constrained Deep Siamese Convolutional Network Model specifically designed to improve accuracy and robustness in building change detection. The proposed approach leverages a Siamese network architecture to process bi-temporal remote sensing images, incorporating dual task constraints that simultaneously optimize for change localization and semantic classification. This dual-task strategy enforces complementary learning, allowing the model to better distinguish subtle changes and reduce false positives associated with shadow or seasonal variations. 

To evaluate the effectiveness of our method, extensive experiments were conducted on publicly available remote sensing datasets, comparing the performance with state-of-the-art change detection frameworks. The results demonstrate significant improvements in both precision and recall metrics for building change detection, with our model achieving superior robustness across diverse urban scenarios. Our ablation studies further highlight the contribution of the dual task constraints to the overall performance and generalization capability. 

In conclusion, our Dual Task Constrained Deep Siamese Convolutional Network Model presents a substantial advancement for automated, scalable, and reliable building change detection in remote sensing images. Potential applications include urban planning, environmental monitoring, and rapid response to natural disasters. 

Keywords: building change detection, remote sensing, deep Siamese network, dual task constraints, urban monitoring, convolutional neural networks, semantic segmentation.
