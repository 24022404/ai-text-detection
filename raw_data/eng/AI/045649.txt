This paper explores the application of potential-based reward shaping (PBRS) to enhance reinforcement learning (RL) performance in the Sokoban environment. Sokoban, a challenging puzzle game requiring strategic box manipulation, presents sparse and delayed rewards, hindering effective learning. We introduce a novel PBRS approach that leverages a potential function based on the Manhattan distance between the current game state and the solved state, guiding the RL agent towards desirable intermediate states. Experiments demonstrate that PBRS significantly accelerates learning compared to standard RL methods with only sparse rewards. Furthermore, we analyze the impact of different potential function designs on learning speed and final performance. The results indicate that a carefully designed potential function can substantially improve sample efficiency and lead to the discovery of more optimal solutions in complex, reward-sparse environments like Sokoban. This research provides insights into effective reward shaping strategies for solving challenging RL problems with combinatorial state spaces.
Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Reward Shaping, Sokoban, Potential Functions, Sparse Rewards, Game AI.
