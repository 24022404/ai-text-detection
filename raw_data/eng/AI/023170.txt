This paper presents an end-to-end computer vision framework for the multi-class classification of blood cells, aimed at supporting automated diagnosis in clinical settings. The objective of the study is to address challenges inherent in distinguishing between various blood cell types, such as red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets, which are critical for accurate disease identification and treatment planning. We propose a deep learning-based approach utilizing convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to automatically extract and analyze features from microscopic blood cell images. The dataset consists of labeled images drawn from diverse patient samples, ensuring robustness and real-world applicability. Our methodology incorporates data augmentation and transfer learning to enhance model performance and generalizability across multiple classes. The results demonstrate high classification accuracy, outperforming traditional machine learning techniques and prior vision-based models, with particular improvements seen in handling class imbalances and visually similar cell types. The proposed system also offers end-to-end automation, reducing the need for manual intervention in the diagnostic workflow. In conclusion, our approach provides a scalable and accurate solution for blood cell classification, with potential applications in digital pathology, laboratory automation, and early disease detection. Key contributions include a novel integration of deep learning architectures, a comprehensive evaluation on clinically relevant datasets, and practical insights for deploying computer vision systems in healthcare environments. Keywords: blood cell classification, computer vision, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, medical diagnosis, automation, digital pathology.
