This paper investigates the capabilities and limitations of time-lagged autoencoders for discovering slow modes in dynamical systems. Time-lagged autoencoders have emerged as a promising tool for dimensionality reduction and feature extraction in complex systems. We analyze the performance of these autoencoders in identifying the dominant slow modes, which govern the long-term behavior of such systems. Our approach involves training autoencoders on time series data generated from several model dynamical systems with varying degrees of complexity and nonlinearity. We evaluate the accuracy of the learned low-dimensional embeddings in capturing the true slow modes, comparing the results against established methods like Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) and Principal Component Analysis (PCA). We find that time-lagged autoencoders can effectively identify slow modes in systems with linear or weakly nonlinear dynamics. However, performance degrades significantly in highly nonlinear systems, particularly when the time scales are not well-separated. We identify key limitations related to hyperparameter tuning and network architecture, proposing guidelines for optimal application. These results provide insights into the applicability of time-lagged autoencoders for analyzing complex dynamical systems, highlighting both their potential and constraints.

**Keywords:** Time-lagged Autoencoders, Dynamical Systems, Slow Mode Discovery, Dimensionality Reduction, Nonlinear Dynamics, Machine Learning.
