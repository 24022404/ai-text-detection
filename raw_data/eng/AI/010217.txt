Image captioning aims to generate descriptive text for images, a task commonly addressed using encoder-decoder architectures. This paper investigates the role of scene graphs as a structured image representation within such architectures. While recent trends favor end-to-end trained models that implicitly learn relationships, we argue that explicit scene graph representations can offer advantages in terms of interpretability, controllability, and accuracy, particularly in complex scenes. We present a novel image captioning model that leverages scene graphs extracted using a hybrid approach, combining bottom-up object detection with top-down relationship prediction. Our model incorporates graph convolutional networks to refine node and edge embeddings, enabling a more nuanced understanding of the scene. Experimental results on the COCO dataset demonstrate that our scene graph-based approach achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art attention-based models, especially when evaluated on captions requiring relational reasoning. Furthermore, we show that manipulating the scene graph allows for controlled caption generation, offering a degree of interpretability absent in purely data-driven models. This work highlights the continued relevance of scene graphs as a valuable tool for image captioning and suggests avenues for future research in structured visual representations.
Keywords: Image Captioning, Scene Graphs, Graph Convolutional Networks, Relational Reasoning, Computer Vision.
