Lifelong reinforcement learning (LRL) represents an emerging paradigm in artificial intelligence that aims to enable agents to continually acquire, retain, and transfer knowledge across a diverse range of tasks. This paper investigates the fundamental challenges and design principles of lifelong reinforcement learning systems, focusing on their ability to address issues such as catastrophic forgetting, efficient knowledge transfer, and scalable memory management. We present a comparative evaluation of several state-of-the-art LRL architectures, including modular policy frameworks and memory-augmented networks, within both simulated and real-world multi-task environments. Our experimental methodology encompasses a series of benchmarks designed to assess task retention, adaptation speed, and resource efficiency. The results reveal that integrating task-aware memory mechanisms and regularization techniques significantly enhances the agent's capability to preserve learned skills while adapting to novel experiences. Moreover, we discuss the trade-offs between system complexity and learning flexibility, highlighting scenarios where specific LRL strategies yield optimal outcomes. The insights gained from this study contribute to the development of more robust and generalizable reinforcement learning agents suited for dynamic, lifelong applications. Keywords: lifelong reinforcement learning, continual learning, knowledge transfer, memory management, catastrophic forgetting, AI agents.
