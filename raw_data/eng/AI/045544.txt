Deep learning segmentation methods have achieved state-of-the-art performance in cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), enabling improved diagnostics and patient care. However, the widespread clinical adoption of these methods is hindered by concerns regarding their trustworthiness, particularly in terms of reliability, generalizability, and interpretability. This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the trustworthiness of deep learning-based cardiac MRI segmentation by proposing a comprehensive framework that integrates uncertainty estimation, out-of-distribution detection, and explainable AI (XAI) techniques. The proposed approach employs a convolutional neural network architecture with Bayesian inference to quantify model confidence, alongside an anomaly detector to identify inputs that deviate from the training distribution. Furthermore, gradient-based visualization techniques are incorporated to provide interpretable insights into the model’s predictions. Experimental evaluation on multi-center, multi-vendor cardiac MRI datasets demonstrates improved segmentation accuracy, reduced failure modes on anomalous data, and increased transparency for end-users compared to standard black-box models. These findings suggest that our multi-faceted framework not only boosts the safety and reliability of deep learning segmentation for cardiac MRI but also fosters greater clinical confidence in AI-assisted diagnostic tools. The proposed solutions contribute to the advancement of trustworthy medical AI, paving the way for more robust integration of deep learning models in routine cardiac imaging workflows. Keywords: deep learning, segmentation, cardiac MRI, trustworthiness, uncertainty estimation, explainable AI, out-of-distribution detection.
