Abstract:

Color constancy is a fundamental challenge in computer vision, crucial for ensuring consistent color perception in varying illumination conditions. This paper introduces a novel Cascading Convolutional Color Constancy (C4) framework designed to enhance the robustness and accuracy of color correction in digital images. The proposed method employs a cascade of convolutional neural networks (CNNs), where each stage progressively refines the estimated scene illumination and corrects color biases. Unlike traditional single-stage models, our cascading approach allows for the integration of both global context and local image details, resulting in improved estimation accuracy. Comprehensive experiments are conducted on benchmark color constancy datasets, demonstrating that C4 consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of mean angular error and visual fidelity of corrected images. The results also indicate that the cascading design significantly mitigates error accumulation and adapts well to diverse lighting scenarios. The contributions of this research include the development of an end-to-end trainable architecture, enhanced feature extraction strategies for illumination estimation, and a thorough analysis of performance gains attributed to cascading. Our findings suggest that the C4 framework offers a promising solution for real-world applications such as digital photography, autonomous vehicles, and augmented reality.  
Keywords: color constancy, convolutional neural network, cascading models, illumination estimation, computer vision.
