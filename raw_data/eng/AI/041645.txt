The rapid growth of data in modern applications necessitates learning systems capable of adapting to new tasks and environments without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised lifelong learning, where models must continuously learn from unlabeled data streams while retaining past knowledge. We propose a novel Deep Bayesian Unsupervised Lifelong Learning (DBULL) framework that leverages Bayesian neural networks to dynamically adapt to new data distributions while preserving prior learning. The approach combines variational inference with a memory-augmented architecture to enable probabilistic reasoning and mitigate catastrophic forgetting. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that DBULL achieves superior performance in clustering and representation learning tasks compared to existing unsupervised lifelong learning methods. The framework also exhibits robustness to distribution shifts and scalability to large-scale datasets. Key contributions include a probabilistic model for lifelong learning, an efficient memory mechanism for knowledge retention, and a scalable training algorithm. This work advances the state-of-the-art in unsupervised lifelong learning, offering potential applications in autonomous systems, anomaly detection, and adaptive AI.  

**Keywords**: unsupervised learning, lifelong learning, Bayesian neural networks, catastrophic forgetting, variational inference, memory-augmented models.
