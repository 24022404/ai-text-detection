This paper challenges the conventional dichotomy between feature-based and shape-based approaches in time series classification, proposing a novel framework that integrates the strengths of both paradigms. Our objective is to improve the accuracy and robustness of time series classification models by leveraging the complementary information encoded in features and shapes. We employ a hybrid approach that combines traditional feature extraction methods with advanced shape-based techniques, including elastic measures and shapelets. Our results show that the proposed framework outperforms state-of-the-art methods on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in classification accuracy and reduced sensitivity to noise and outliers. The key findings of this study highlight the importance of reconciling the feature-shape dichotomy and provide new insights into the representation and analysis of time series data. Our research contributes to the development of more effective and efficient time series classification models, with potential applications in fields such as finance, healthcare, and environmental monitoring. Key keywords: time series classification, feature extraction, shape-based methods, hybrid approach, elastic measures, shapelets.
