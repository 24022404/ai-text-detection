Neural Architecture Search (NAS) has emerged as a pivotal method for automating the design of neural network architectures, providing significant improvements in model performance across various tasks. This paper investigates the role of supernets in enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of NAS processes. Our objective is to address the challenges in traditional NAS approaches that are computationally intensive and time-consuming. We introduce a novel method that employs a supernet to encapsulate a diverse set of architectures within a single overarching model. The approach leverages shared parameters and weights among sub-networks, significantly reducing the search space and computational cost. Through a comprehensive evaluation, our findings demonstrate that the use of a supernet not only accelerates the search process but also consistently yields competitive or superior architectures in comparison to standalone NAS methods. Moreover, our results highlight improvements in training efficiency and final model performance across benchmark datasets. The implications of this research suggest that supernets can serve as a transformative tool in NAS, providing a scalable and efficient pathway to obtaining state-of-the-art neural architectures. This work contributes by optimizing the NAS pipeline and enabling broader accessibility to high-performance models within shorter timeframes. Key keywords include Neural Architecture Search, supernet, model efficiency, and architecture optimization.
