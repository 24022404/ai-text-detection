Offline reinforcement learning (RL) offers a promising avenue for training agents from previously collected datasets, avoiding costly and potentially dangerous online exploration. However, a key challenge in offline RL is the distributional shift between the offline data and the agent's policy, leading to overestimation of out-of-distribution actions and overly conservative policies. This work investigates methods to reduce the conservativeness inherent in existing offline RL algorithms. We propose a novel approach that dynamically adjusts the conservatism level based on the uncertainty of value function estimates. Specifically, our method incorporates a learned uncertainty estimate into the regularized Bellman update, reducing conservatism in regions with high uncertainty while maintaining safety in regions with sufficient data coverage. Empirical results on benchmark offline RL datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves significant performance improvements compared to state-of-the-art conservative offline RL algorithms, without sacrificing stability. This adaptive conservatism offers a more efficient and less restrictive learning process, leading to policies that better utilize the available offline data. Keywords: Offline Reinforcement Learning, Conservatism, Uncertainty Estimation, Distributional Shift, Policy Optimization.
