This paper addresses the challenge of leveraging unstructured narrated instruction videos for unsupervised learning in computer vision and natural language processing. The objective is to develop a framework that can automatically extract meaningful visual and textual representations from such videos without requiring labeled data. We propose a novel unsupervised approach that combines multi-modal feature extraction, temporal alignment, and self-supervised learning techniques to align visual sequences with corresponding spoken instructions. Our method employs a transformer-based architecture to model the temporal dependencies between video frames and audio transcripts, enabling the system to learn semantic correspondences between visual actions and narrated steps. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing unsupervised methods in tasks such as action recognition, step localization, and cross-modal retrieval. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets, showcasing its ability to generalize across diverse instructional domains. This research contributes to advancing unsupervised learning in multi-modal settings, with potential applications in automated video understanding, robotics, and educational technologies. Key innovations include the integration of temporal alignment with self-supervised learning and the ability to handle noisy, real-world video data. Keywords: unsupervised learning, narrated videos, multi-modal learning, transformer models, self-supervised learning, video understanding.
