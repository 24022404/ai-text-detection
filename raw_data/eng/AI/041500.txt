The paper addresses the challenge of efficient feature selection in Factored Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), which is crucial for enhancing decision-making efficiency in complex environments. Traditional methods often require large sample sizes to identify relevant features, posing computational challenges.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel algorithmic approach that leverages model-based reinforcement learning to achieve sample-efficient feature selection for Factored MDPs. This approach utilizes a structured representation of the environment to identify and exploit relevant features with significantly reduced data requirements. Our method incorporates techniques from graph theory and probabilistic modeling to enhance the selection process.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our proposed method significantly outperforms existing techniques in terms of sample efficiency and computational cost. We provide a comprehensive evaluation using benchmark problems, which shows a reduction in required samples by up to 50% compared to conventional approaches. Our findings indicate robust performance improvements, particularly in high-dimensional state spaces, illustrating the adaptability and efficiency of the model.

Conclusion/Implications: This research provides crucial insights into efficient feature selection in Factored MDPs. By reducing the sample size requirements, our method opens new possibilities for deploying reinforcement learning solutions in real-time decision-making systems where data collection is expensive or time-consuming. The contributions of this study can significantly impact applications in robotics, automated planning, and intelligent system design, where efficient learning from limited data is paramount.

Keywords: Factored MDPs, Feature Selection, Sample Efficiency, Reinforcement Learning, Graph Theory, Probabilistic Modeling, Decision-Making Systems.
