This paper addresses the challenge of melanoma screening by leveraging deep learning techniques to facilitate knowledge transfer between medical imaging modalities. The objective is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of melanoma detection, reducing the reliance on expert dermatologists. Our approach utilizes a novel transfer learning framework, enabling the adaptation of pre-trained convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to dermatoscopic images. The methodology involves fine-tuning the CNNs on a large dataset of annotated images, followed by evaluation on a separate test set. The results demonstrate significant improvements in melanoma detection accuracy, outperforming state-of-the-art methods. Key findings include the effectiveness of knowledge transfer in reducing the need for large annotated datasets and the potential for real-time melanoma screening. The conclusion highlights the importance of this research in enhancing melanoma detection capabilities, with implications for clinical practice and patient outcomes. The study contributes to the advancement of deep learning in medical imaging, particularly in the context of skin cancer diagnosis, with relevant keywords including melanoma screening, deep learning, knowledge transfer, convolutional neural networks, and dermatoscopy.
