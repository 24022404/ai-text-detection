**Abstract**

Reasoning effectively over large and complex theories remains a core challenge in artificial intelligence and automated theorem proving. Traditional approaches often rely on imitation learning, where models are trained to replicate human or algorithmic reasoning steps. In this paper, we present a novel learning framework that enables models to reason in large theories without explicit imitation. Our method leverages a combination of self-supervised objectives and scalable neural architectures to encourage autonomous discovery of reasoning strategies. We introduce an environment that presents intricate logical problems drawn from extensive formal theories and demonstrate that our approach allows agents to develop robust reasoning abilities without access to expert demonstrations. Experimental results show that our models outperform imitation-based baselines in both accuracy and generalization across multiple theory domains. Furthermore, analysis of the learned strategies reveals the emergence of interpretable and transferable reasoning patterns. These findings suggest that endowing models with the capacity for unsupervised reasoning can lead to more flexible and powerful automated reasoning systems. Our work opens new avenues for the development of AI capable of independently learning to reason in complex, real-world domains.

*Keywords: automated reasoning, unsupervised learning, theorem proving, large theories, self-supervised learning, explainable AI*
