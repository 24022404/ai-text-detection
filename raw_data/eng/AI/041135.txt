This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing complex, non-convex functions defined on matrix manifolds, a problem frequently encountered in machine learning, computer vision, and signal processing. Traditional stochastic gradient descent (SGD) methods often struggle with the geometric constraints inherent to such manifolds, leading to suboptimal convergence and performance. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel class of Riemannian adaptive stochastic gradient algorithms that extend adaptive optimization techniques, such as Adam and RMSprop, to matrix manifolds. Our approach leverages the intrinsic geometric structure of the manifold to ensure efficient and stable optimization. The proposed algorithms incorporate adaptive learning rates and momentum, enabling faster convergence and improved robustness to ill-conditioned problems. Through extensive experiments on tasks such as low-rank matrix completion and principal component analysis, we demonstrate that our methods outperform existing Riemannian optimization techniques in terms of convergence speed and solution accuracy. The results highlight the practical significance of our approach, particularly in applications requiring high-dimensional data processing. This work contributes to the growing body of research on geometric optimization by providing a scalable and adaptive framework for solving complex optimization problems on matrix manifolds.  

**Keywords**: Riemannian optimization, matrix manifolds, adaptive stochastic gradient descent, machine learning, geometric optimization.
