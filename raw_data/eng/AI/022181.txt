Contrastive Divergence (CD) is a widely used algorithm for training energy-based models, such as Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs), in unsupervised learning tasks. However, the conventional stopping criterion based on reconstruction error often leads to suboptimal convergence and overfitting. This paper investigates alternative stopping criteria for CD to improve training efficiency and model performance. We propose and evaluate three novel approaches: (1) a gradient-based criterion that monitors the stability of weight updates, (2) a divergence-based criterion that measures the difference between model and data distributions, and (3) a hybrid criterion combining reconstruction error with model entropy. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including MNIST and CIFAR-10, demonstrate that the proposed criteria achieve faster convergence and better generalization compared to traditional methods. The gradient-based criterion, in particular, reduces training time by 25% while maintaining competitive accuracy. Our findings highlight the limitations of reconstruction error as a sole metric and provide practical alternatives for optimizing CD-based training. This work contributes to the broader field of unsupervised learning by offering more robust and efficient stopping mechanisms, with potential applications in generative modeling, anomaly detection, and feature extraction.  

**Keywords**: Contrastive Divergence, stopping criteria, unsupervised learning, Restricted Boltzmann Machines, reconstruction error, gradient-based optimization.
