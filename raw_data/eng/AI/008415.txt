The objective of this study is to develop and evaluate a computer vision-based system for the multi-class classification of blood cells, aimed at improving diagnostic accuracy and efficiency in hematology. The approach leverages a deep learning model, specifically a convolutional neural network (CNN), to automate the classification process across multiple cell types. The dataset comprises pre-processed images of blood samples, which serve as input to train and validate the model. The results demonstrate that the proposed CNN achieves a high classification accuracy, outperforming traditional methods and showing robustness across diverse specimen samples. The system effectively distinguishes between different classes of blood cells, including red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets, with precision and recall metrics indicating superior performance. The implementation of this automated classification system suggests significant potential in clinical diagnostics, reducing the need for manual examination and offering a scalable solution for large volume analysis. This research contributes to the field by presenting an end-to-end computer vision solution that not only enhances diagnostic outcomes but also serves as a foundational platform for further innovations in automated medical imaging. Keywords: deep learning, convolutional neural network, blood cell classification, computer vision, diagnostic automation.
