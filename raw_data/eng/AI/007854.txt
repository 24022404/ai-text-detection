This paper addresses the challenge of accurately segmenting causal motion in videos where both the objects and the camera are in motion. Traditional motion segmentation techniques often struggle with distinguishing foreground objects from the background when the camera itself is moving. Our research proposes a novel probabilistic model designed specifically to handle these complexities.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a probabilistic framework that integrates motion estimation and visual cues to effectively separate dynamic foreground objects from stationary or moving backgrounds. The model utilizes Bayesian inference to account for uncertainties and leverages a combination of optical flow and motion history to predict causal motion segments accurately. This approach is further enhanced by incorporating prior knowledge of potential motion patterns within the scene.

Results/Findings: Our model was evaluated on a diverse set of video sequences captured from moving cameras, demonstrating superior performance in motion segmentation compared to traditional methods such as optical flow-based or background subtraction techniques. The proposed model achieved higher accuracy in identifying foreground objects and exhibited robustness in varying lighting conditions and complex motion paths.

Conclusion/Implications: The research presents a significant advancement in the field of video analysis, particularly in environments involving dynamic camera systems. The probabilistic model not only improves the reliability of causal motion segmentation but also opens avenues for its application in domains such as autonomous navigation, surveillance, and augmented reality. This study highlights the importance of integrating probabilistic reasoning with motion cues to enhance the segmentation process, offering a foundation for future exploration in automated video content analysis.

Key Keywords: motion segmentation, moving camera, probabilistic model, causal motion, Bayesian inference, optical flow, video analysis.
