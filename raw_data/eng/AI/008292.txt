Temporal Difference (TD) learning is a fundamental approach in reinforcement learning, widely used for value function approximation. However, traditional TD methods often struggle with slow convergence and inefficiency in environments with sparse or delayed rewards. This paper introduces *Source Traces*, a novel mechanism designed to enhance the efficiency and stability of TD learning algorithms. The proposed approach leverages a dynamic trace mechanism that identifies and prioritizes critical states (sources) influencing future rewards, enabling more effective credit assignment. We present a theoretical analysis of Source Traces, demonstrating its convergence properties and improved sample efficiency compared to conventional eligibility traces. Empirical evaluations on benchmark reinforcement learning tasks, including gridworld and Atari environments, show that Source Traces significantly reduce training time while achieving higher cumulative rewards. The results highlight the potential of Source Traces to advance TD learning in complex, real-world applications, particularly in scenarios with long-term dependencies and sparse rewards. This work contributes a scalable and interpretable solution to the challenges of temporal credit assignment, offering a promising direction for future research in reinforcement learning.  

**Keywords**: Temporal Difference Learning, Reinforcement Learning, Source Traces, Credit Assignment, Sample Efficiency, Sparse Rewards.
