Deep learning has achieved remarkable success in various domains; however, its effectiveness on tabular data, a prevalent data type in many real-world applications, is often debated. This paper investigates the performance of deep learning models compared to traditional machine learning algorithms on tabular datasets. We evaluated several deep learning architectures, including Multi-Layer Perceptrons and attention-based models, alongside established methods like XGBoost and Random Forests across a diverse set of tabular datasets. Our results demonstrate that while deep learning can be competitive, it frequently underperforms compared to gradient boosting methods, especially when data is limited or feature engineering is crucial. This study highlights the importance of considering traditional algorithms as strong baselines for tabular data and emphasizes that deep learning is not always the optimal solution. We provide insights into scenarios where traditional methods excel and offer recommendations for practitioners choosing algorithms for tabular data problems.
Keywords: Tabular Data, Deep Learning, Machine Learning, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, Random Forests, Model Comparison.
