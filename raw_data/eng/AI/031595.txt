Surface defect detection is a critical task in manufacturing and quality control, yet the scarcity of defective samples often hinders the development of robust automated systems. This paper addresses the challenge of generating realistic defective samples for training deep learning models by leveraging Neural Style Transfer (NST) techniques. We propose a novel approach that simulates surface defects by transferring defect patterns from a limited set of real defective samples onto defect-free surfaces, creating diverse and realistic training data. Our method integrates a pre-trained convolutional neural network (CNN) with a style transfer algorithm to ensure high-quality defect synthesis while preserving the structural integrity of the target surfaces. Experimental results demonstrate that models trained with our simulated defective samples achieve superior performance in automatic defect segmentation tasks, outperforming traditional data augmentation methods. The proposed approach not only reduces the dependency on large annotated datasets but also enhances the generalization capability of defect detection systems. This work contributes to advancing automated quality inspection in industrial applications, offering a scalable and cost-effective solution for defect data generation.  

**Keywords**: Surface defect detection, Neural Style Transfer, defect simulation, automatic segmentation, deep learning, data augmentation.
