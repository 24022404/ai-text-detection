This paper addresses the challenge of generating realistic video sequences from static images, a task with significant implications for video editing, animation, and augmented reality. The objective is to develop a model capable of inferring plausible motion trajectories and augmenting still images into fluid motion videos, focusing on producing diverse and high-quality outputs. 

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel approach utilizing conditional Invertible Neural Networks (cINNs) to achieve stochastic image-to-video synthesis. This method leverages cINNs to model the probability distribution of possible video frames conditioned on a given image. By employing a forward-backward consistency mechanism, our approach ensures the synthesis of coherent and visually appealing video sequences. The model is trained on a large dataset comprising diverse image-video pairs to capture a wide range of motions and scenes effectively.

Results/Findings: Our approach significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of both diversity and realism of the generated videos. Quantitative evaluation demonstrates that the synthesized videos achieve higher scores in terms of perceptual quality metrics, such as structural similarity (SSIM) and peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), compared to baseline models. Moreover, qualitative assessments reveal that our model produces a wide array of plausible motion trajectories from a single input image, reflecting the inherent variability of real-world video sequences.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed stochastic image-to-video synthesis framework represents a significant advancement in the field of video generation, offering a robust solution for creating dynamic content from static images. This research contributes to the growing body of work on conditional generative models and cINNs, paving the way for future applications in creative industries and automated video production tools. Keywords: Image-to-Video Synthesis, Stochastic Process, Conditional Invertible Neural Networks, Video Generation, Motion Trajectories.
