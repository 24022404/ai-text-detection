This paper addresses the challenge of deriving generalization guarantees in nonlinear metric learning without dependence on data dimensionality. Existing metric learning approaches often require strong assumptions on data structure or impose dimension-related constraints, making them impractical for high-dimensional applications such as image retrieval, natural language processing, and bioinformatics. We propose a novel theoretical framework that establishes dimension-free generalization bounds for a broad class of nonlinear metric learning algorithms, leveraging advances in Rademacher complexity and covering numbers. Our analysis encompasses both kernel-based and neural network-based metric learning models, demonstrating that their capacity to generalize is governed by intrinsic algorithmic properties rather than the ambient feature space size. Experimental evaluation on benchmark datasets validates the tightness of our bounds and the superior performance of nonlinear metrics in high-dimensional regimes compared to traditional linear methods. Our findings offer significant insights for the design of scalable and robust metric learning systems in data-rich environments. Keywords: metric learning, generalization bounds, kernel methods, neural networks, high-dimensional data, nonlinear models, Rademacher complexity.
