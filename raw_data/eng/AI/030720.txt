Self-supervised learning (SSL) has shown great promise in learning representations from unlabeled data. However, current SSL methods often struggle with capturing fine-grained details and local context. To address this, we introduce CASTing (Contextual Alignment with Semantic Target), a novel self-supervised learning framework that leverages localization as a pretext task to enhance representation learning. CASTing encourages the model to predict the location of specific image regions while simultaneously learning global representations. This is achieved through a contextual alignment loss that enforces consistency between local feature embeddings and global image representations. Our experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that CASTing significantly improves the quality of learned representations, leading to substantial gains in downstream tasks such as image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation. Specifically, CASTing outperforms state-of-the-art SSL methods, particularly when transferring to tasks requiring precise localization information. The results highlight the importance of incorporating localization as an auxiliary task to improve the robustness and transferability of self-supervised representations. Keywords: Self-supervised learning, representation learning, localization, pretext task, contextual alignment.
