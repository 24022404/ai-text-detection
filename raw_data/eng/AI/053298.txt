This paper introduces a novel approach to scene retrieval by abstracting scene content into semantic representations. Current methods often rely on low-level visual features or object-level recognition, which can be sensitive to variations in viewpoint, lighting, and occlusion. To address these limitations, we propose a system that first extracts objects and their relationships within a scene, then abstracts this information into a graph-based representation capturing the high-level semantic structure. This abstract representation is then used to perform similarity matching against a database of scene graphs. We evaluate our method on a benchmark dataset of indoor and outdoor scenes, demonstrating that our approach achieves superior retrieval accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly in cases where visual features are unreliable. The key contribution lies in the ability to search scenes based on their abstract semantic content, enabling more robust and flexible scene understanding. Keywords: Scene Retrieval, Semantic Abstraction, Graph Representation, Scene Understanding, Image Search.
