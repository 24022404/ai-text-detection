Continual learning enables artificial intelligence systems to learn and adapt from a continuous stream of data. However, these systems often struggle with catastrophic forgetting, where previously acquired knowledge is lost upon learning new information. This paper introduces a novel rectification-based knowledge retention method to address this challenge and enhance continual learning.

Methods/Approach: Our approach involves a strategic rectification mechanism that modifies the learning process to preserve acquired knowledge while integrating new information effectively. This method utilizes a combination of memory-efficient knowledge retention techniques and adaptive rectification strategies, enabling the model to dynamically adjust learning priorities based on the importance of retained knowledge. The architecture seamlessly integrates with existing neural networks, ensuring broad applicability across diverse learning environments.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our rectification-based method significantly outperforms traditional continual learning approaches in mitigating catastrophic forgetting. The proposed system maintains a higher retention accuracy across multiple datasets and exhibits substantial improvements in knowledge transfer capabilities. Comparative analyses with state-of-the-art methods highlight increased performance and reduced computational overhead, making our approach robust and efficient.

Conclusion/Implications: The rectification-based knowledge retention method presents an effective solution to the challenges of continual learning, offering significant advancements in memory preservation and operational efficiency. This research contributes a scalable and adaptable framework that can be applied to various AI systems, enhancing their ability to learn continuously without compromising prior knowledge. Future work will explore extending this approach to more complex learning scenarios and automated retention optimization.

Keywords: continual learning, catastrophic forgetting, knowledge retention, AI systems, adaptive rectification, neural networks.
