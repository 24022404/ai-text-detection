This paper addresses the challenge of predicting healthcare costs by uncovering hidden patterns within patient multivariate time series data. Traditional methods often struggle with the inherent complexity and high dimensionality of real-world clinical datasets, which can obscure essential signals needed for accurate predictions. To tackle this problem, we propose an end-to-end framework leveraging Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to automatically extract salient features from multivariate temporal records, such as patient vitals, laboratory results, and medication histories. Our approach involves preprocessing raw electronic health records, structuring them into time-series inputs, and training a deep CNN model to learn complex temporal dependencies without manual feature engineering. Experiments are conducted on a large-scale healthcare dataset, and the performance of our method is benchmarked against conventional machine learning algorithms. Results demonstrate that the CNN-based model significantly outperforms baseline methods in terms of prediction accuracy and robustness to data irregularities. The findings highlight the effectiveness of CNNs in capturing both short-term and long-term temporal patterns critical for healthcare cost estimation. In conclusion, this study provides a novel, scalable solution for healthcare analytics, with practical implications for risk stratification, resource allocation, and policy planning. Key contributions include the adaptation of CNN architectures for multivariate time series in healthcare and a comprehensive evaluation of their predictive capabilities. Keywords: convolutional neural networks, multivariate time series, healthcare cost prediction, electronic health records, deep learning, feature extraction.
