This paper investigates the resilience of autonomous vehicle object category detection systems to universal adversarial perturbations. The objective is to analyze the vulnerability of these systems to subtle, yet malicious, input manipulations that can compromise their ability to accurately detect and classify objects. A deep learning-based approach is employed, utilizing a state-of-the-art object detection model and evaluating its performance under various universal adversarial perturbation attacks. The results show that even small perturbations can significantly degrade the detection accuracy, highlighting the need for robustness enhancements. Key findings indicate that the model's performance degrades by up to 30% under certain attack scenarios, emphasizing the importance of developing more resilient object detection systems. The research contributes to the understanding of adversarial vulnerabilities in autonomous vehicles and paves the way for the development of more secure and reliable object category detection systems, with potential applications in areas such as autonomous driving, robotics, and computer vision. Keywords: autonomous vehicles, object detection, universal adversarial perturbations, deep learning, robustness, computer vision, adversarial attacks.
