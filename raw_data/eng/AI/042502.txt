This paper introduces GATSBI, a novel framework for simulating realistic and interactive agent behaviors within complex 3D environments, focusing on spatio-temporal object interaction. GATSBI employs a generative agent architecture that learns to predict and execute context-aware actions based on perceived object affordances and environmental dynamics. We present a multi-modal learning approach that integrates visual and semantic information to enable agents to dynamically adapt their behavior to changing circumstances. Experiments conducted in a simulated city environment demonstrate GATSBI's ability to generate coherent and plausible agent activities, including navigation, object manipulation, and social interaction. Compared to existing agent simulation methods, GATSBI exhibits superior performance in terms of behavioral realism, environmental responsiveness, and computational efficiency. The proposed framework has significant implications for a wide range of applications, including urban planning, autonomous robotics, and interactive virtual environments.

Keywords: Generative Agents, Spatio-temporal Interaction, 3D Simulation, Object Affordances, Multi-modal Learning, Behavior Modeling.
