The objective of this research is to explore and advance the application of transformer models for image representation and processing within the field of computer vision. Traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have dominated visual tasks; however, the emergence of transformers for textual data suggests potential for innovative image processing approaches. We propose a novel framework, Visual Transformers (ViTs), which utilizes token-based image representation to enable effective image processing and analysis. The methodology involves segmenting images into a series of smaller patches, treating each patch as a token analogous to words in text processing. These tokens are then processed through a transformer architecture, allowing for global context and long-range dependency capturing that CNNs typically miss. Through comprehensive experiments, we demonstrate that Visual Transformers achieve state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmark datasets, surpassing conventional CNNs in accuracy and efficiency, particularly on tasks requiring detailed scene understanding and object detection. Key findings include improved scalability and adaptability across varied image resolutions and complexities. This research underscores the implications of adopting transformer models in computer vision, paving the way for future studies to extend this approach to other visual tasks. Our contributions highlight the potential transformations in image processing paradigms, supported by promising quantitative results and broad applicability in areas like medical imaging, autonomous driving, and real-time video analysis. Keywords: Visual Transformers, image representation, computer vision, token-based processing, global context, scalability.
