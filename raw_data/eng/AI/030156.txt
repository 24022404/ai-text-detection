Contrastive Divergence (CD) is a widely used algorithm for training energy-based models, particularly in machine learning tasks like image recognition and natural language processing. Determining appropriate stopping criteria for CD training is crucial for achieving optimal model performance and avoiding overfitting. Traditionally, the reconstruction error is used as a primary indicator for convergence. This paper investigates alternative stopping criteria for CD training, exploring their effectiveness compared to the reconstruction error. We propose and evaluate several novel metrics based on the free energy difference and the gradient norm of the model parameters. Through experiments on benchmark datasets, we demonstrate that these alternative criteria can provide more robust and efficient stopping signals, leading to improved generalization performance and reduced training time. Specifically, monitoring the convergence of the free energy difference offers a more stable indication of learning progress, while the gradient norm provides insights into parameter stability. These findings suggest that relying solely on reconstruction error may be suboptimal, and alternative stopping criteria can significantly enhance the training process of energy-based models. Keywords: Contrastive Divergence, Stopping Criteria, Reconstruction Error, Energy-Based Models, Machine Learning, Free Energy, Gradient Norm, Convergence.
