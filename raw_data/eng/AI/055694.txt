This paper presents RECS, a novel approach to robust graph embedding that utilizes connection subgraphs for enhanced representation learning. While existing graph embedding techniques often struggle with noisy structures and fail to capture complex connectivity, RECS addresses these challenges by systematically extracting subgraphs centered around key connections within the original network. These connection subgraphs preserve localized topological features and enable the generation of high-quality node embeddings, even in the presence of perturbations or missing links. We propose a dedicated algorithm for identifying relevant subgraphs and integrating their structure into the embedding process. Through experiments on several benchmark datasets, RECS demonstrates significant improvements in downstream tasks such as node classification, link prediction, and community detection compared to state-of-the-art methods. Results show increased robustness to noise and superior preservation of structural properties. Our findings suggest that leveraging connection-centric subgraphs is an effective strategy for robust and generalizable graph representation learning, with implications for applications in social networks, biological systems, and recommendation engines. Keywords: graph embedding, robustness, connection subgraphs, network representation, node classification, link prediction.
