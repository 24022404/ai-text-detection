This paper introduces a novel approach to manipulating attributes within natural scene images by leveraging a hallucination-based framework. Our objective is to provide users with fine-grained control over scene characteristics, such as lighting, weather conditions, and object presence, without requiring extensive manual editing. We propose a generative model that first hallucinates missing or desired attributes conditioned on the original scene, followed by a refinement stage to ensure photorealistic integration. The method employs a cascaded architecture consisting of a variational autoencoder for attribute embedding and a generative adversarial network for image synthesis. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves superior performance compared to existing image editing techniques, producing high-quality, visually plausible scene modifications. Quantitative evaluations using standard image quality metrics and user studies confirm the effectiveness of our method in manipulating scene attributes while preserving overall scene context. This research contributes a powerful tool for creative image editing and scene understanding. Keywords: image manipulation, scene hallucination, generative models, GAN, variational autoencoder, image editing.
