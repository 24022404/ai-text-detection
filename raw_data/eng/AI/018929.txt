Medical image segmentation using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) is crucial for computer-aided diagnosis and treatment planning. However, designing effective CNN architectures for this task remains challenging, often requiring extensive manual experimentation. This paper presents an automated approach for designing CNN architectures tailored for medical image segmentation. Our method employs a Neural Architecture Search (NAS) strategy guided by a multi-objective optimization algorithm to explore the architectural search space. The objectives include segmentation accuracy, measured by Dice score, and computational efficiency, quantified by the number of parameters. We evaluate our method on two publicly available medical imaging datasets: one for brain tumor segmentation and another for cardiac structure segmentation. Results demonstrate that the automatically designed CNNs achieve comparable or superior segmentation performance compared to manually designed architectures, while significantly reducing the design effort and computational cost. The discovered architectures exhibit unique structural characteristics adapted to the specific imaging modalities, suggesting the potential for domain-specific architectural innovations. This work contributes to the development of more efficient and accurate medical image segmentation pipelines, ultimately improving clinical workflows.

**Keywords:** Medical Image Segmentation, Convolutional Neural Networks, Neural Architecture Search, Automated Design, Deep Learning.
