This paper introduces a novel approach to zero-shot skeleton-based action recognition by leveraging syntactic guidance within generative embeddings. The objective is to address the challenge of recognizing unseen actions without requiring labeled data for those specific actions. Our method employs a generative model conditioned on action descriptions parsed into syntactic dependency trees. This syntactic information guides the generation of action embeddings, enabling the model to learn relationships between actions and their descriptions more effectively. Specifically, we use a graph convolutional network to encode the syntactic structure of action descriptions and integrate this information into a variational autoencoder framework. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing zero-shot action recognition methods. We observe improvements in recognition accuracy, particularly for actions with complex relationships or nuanced descriptions. The syntactic guidance proves crucial for disentangling semantic variations and improving generalization to novel actions. The key contribution lies in the effective fusion of syntactic knowledge with generative embeddings for enhanced zero-shot learning in action recognition. Keywords: Zero-shot learning, action recognition, skeleton data, generative models, syntactic parsing, graph convolutional networks.
