This paper investigates the \(\ell_p\)-norm robustness of ensemble learning methods, specifically focusing on decision stumps and decision trees, which are widely utilized in machine learning for their interpretability and effectiveness. The primary objective of this work is to analyze and quantify how robust these ensemble models are to adversarial perturbations measured in various \(\ell_p\)-norms. We propose a novel theoretical framework for assessing the adversarial vulnerability of both individual stumps, trees, and their ensembles under different \(p\)-norm constraints. To complement our theoretical analysis, we conduct comprehensive empirical evaluations across multiple datasets, ranging from synthetic benchmarks to real-world data, and compare the robustness of ensembles of stumps, shallow trees, and deeper trees. Our findings show that ensemble stumps, while offering strong resistance to \(\ell_\infty\)-norm attacks, exhibit varying robustness profiles across other \(\ell_p\) settings, and that the structural properties of trees play a critical role in adversarial resilience. Additionally, we identify specific design choices and aggregation strategies that can further enhance robustness. The insights derived from this study provide valuable guidelines for practitioners seeking to deploy robust tree-based models in security-sensitive applications. Keywords: \(\ell_p\)-norm, adversarial robustness, decision stumps, decision trees, ensemble methods, machine learning security.
