In the era of data-driven machine learning, fine-tuning pre-trained models has become a standard approach for adapting large-scale models to specific tasks. However, this method often struggles when applied to small datasets, leading to overfitting and suboptimal performance. This paper introduces a novel modular approach to learning on small data, which goes beyond traditional fine-tuning by leveraging task-specific modules that can be dynamically integrated into pre-trained models. The proposed method involves decomposing the learning process into reusable components, enabling efficient adaptation with minimal data while preserving the generalization capabilities of the base model. Experiments across multiple domains, including natural language processing and computer vision, demonstrate that our approach outperforms conventional fine-tuning techniques in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly in low-data regimes. The modular framework also offers interpretability and flexibility, allowing for seamless integration of domain-specific knowledge. This research contributes a scalable and practical solution for small-data learning, with potential applications in areas such as personalized AI, medical diagnostics, and resource-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: small-data learning, modular learning, fine-tuning, pre-trained models, machine learning, AI adaptation.
