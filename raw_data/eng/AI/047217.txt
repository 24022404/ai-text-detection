Autoencoders have emerged as a powerful technique for representation learning, enabling the extraction of salient features from complex data. This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of autoencoders, encompassing fundamental principles, diverse learning task case studies, explainability techniques, and current challenges. We begin by outlining the core architecture and training methodologies of various autoencoder variants, including sparse, denoising, and variational autoencoders. Subsequently, we investigate the application of autoencoders across different learning tasks, such as dimensionality reduction, anomaly detection, and image generation, providing empirical evaluations and comparative analyses. We further explore methods for enhancing the explainability of learned representations, addressing the "black box" nature of these models. Finally, we discuss existing limitations and open research questions, including robustness to adversarial attacks, scalability to high-dimensional data, and interpretability of latent spaces. This work provides a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners seeking a deeper understanding of autoencoders and their potential in various applications. Keywords: Autoencoders, Representation Learning, Unsupervised Learning, Dimensionality Reduction, Explainability, Anomaly Detection, Deep Learning.
