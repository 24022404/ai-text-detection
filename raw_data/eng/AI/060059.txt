Understanding the decision-making processes of complex systems, particularly in software and artificial intelligence, is critical for ensuring transparency and trust. This paper addresses the challenge of interpreting programs as black-box explanations, where the internal logic of a system is opaque. We propose a novel framework that leverages program synthesis techniques to generate human-readable, symbolic representations of black-box behaviors. Our approach combines static analysis and dynamic execution traces to construct interpretable programmatic explanations that approximate the functionality of the target system. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our method achieves high fidelity in replicating black-box behavior while maintaining interpretability, outperforming existing explanation techniques in terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight the potential of program synthesis as a powerful tool for explainable AI and software analysis, offering insights into how complex systems can be made more transparent and accountable. This work contributes to the growing field of explainability by bridging the gap between black-box systems and human-understandable explanations, with applications in debugging, verification, and regulatory compliance.  

**Keywords**: program synthesis, black-box systems, explainable AI, interpretability, symbolic representations, software analysis.
