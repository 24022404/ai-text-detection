This paper introduces a novel unsupervised learning approach based on competing hidden units, designed to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of neural network training. The objective is to develop a system that can automatically discover and represent complex patterns in data without prior knowledge of the underlying structure. Our method employs a competitive mechanism among hidden units, which encourages the network to learn diverse and informative features. The approach utilizes a modified autoencoder architecture, where the competition is induced through a regularization term that penalizes redundant representations. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms traditional unsupervised learning methods in terms of feature quality and clustering performance. The proposed technique has significant implications for applications such as dimensionality reduction, anomaly detection, and generative modeling. Key contributions of this work include the introduction of a competitive learning mechanism, which enhances the robustness and generalizability of the learned representations. Relevant keywords: unsupervised learning, competing hidden units, neural networks, autoencoders, feature learning, dimensionality reduction.
