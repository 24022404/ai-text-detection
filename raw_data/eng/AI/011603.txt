Electrocardiogram (ECG) arrhythmia classification systems play a crucial role in automated diagnosis. However, the robustness of these systems against adversarial attacks remains largely unexplored. This paper investigates the vulnerability of ECG arrhythmia classifiers to adversarial examples by introducing ECGadv, a novel method for generating adversarial ECG signals. ECGadv leverages a generative adversarial network (GAN) to create imperceptible perturbations that, when added to genuine ECG signals, cause misclassification by state-of-the-art arrhythmia classification models. We demonstrate that ECGadv can successfully mislead deep learning-based classifiers with high success rates while maintaining clinically plausible ECG morphology, validated through expert physician review. Our findings reveal a significant security risk in current ECG analysis systems, highlighting the need for robust algorithm design and defenses against adversarial attacks in critical healthcare applications. This work contributes a practical method for evaluating and improving the security of ECG-based diagnostic tools. Keywords: ECG, adversarial attack, arrhythmia classification, deep learning, GAN, security, healthcare.
