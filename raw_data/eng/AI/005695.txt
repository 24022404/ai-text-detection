Pedestrian detection is a critical task in computer vision with applications in autonomous driving, surveillance, and robotics. This paper addresses the challenge of accurately detecting pedestrians in complex environments, where factors such as occlusion, varying lighting conditions, and diverse pedestrian appearances pose significant difficulties. We propose a novel approach based on deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) to improve detection accuracy and robustness. Our method leverages a multi-scale feature extraction framework combined with a region proposal network (RPN) to enhance the detection of pedestrians at different scales and orientations. Additionally, we introduce a context-aware loss function to better handle occluded and partially visible pedestrians. The proposed model is trained and evaluated on widely used pedestrian detection datasets, including Caltech Pedestrian and CityPersons. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of both precision and recall. The model achieves a significant reduction in false positives while maintaining high detection rates, particularly in challenging scenarios. This research contributes to the advancement of pedestrian detection systems by providing a robust and scalable solution that can be integrated into real-world applications. The proposed framework has the potential to enhance safety and efficiency in autonomous systems and urban surveillance.  

**Keywords**: pedestrian detection, deep convolutional neural networks, multi-scale feature extraction, region proposal network, context-aware loss function, autonomous systems.
