This paper introduces a novel unsupervised learning framework, Tracking by Animation (TBA), for multi-object tracking. TBA addresses the challenge of learning robust and accurate trackers without relying on manually annotated data. Our approach leverages animation as a self-supervisory signal, training an attentive tracking model to predict object motion and appearance changes across video frames. The core of TBA is an attention mechanism that selectively focuses on relevant object features, enabling robust tracking in cluttered scenes and under occlusions. We employ a differentiable rendering module to synthesize realistic animations from the predicted object states, creating a closed-loop training environment. Through extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets, we demonstrate that TBA learns effective multi-object trackers that outperform existing unsupervised methods and achieve competitive performance compared to supervised approaches. Key findings include the model's ability to generalize to unseen environments and handle complex object interactions. This work contributes a new paradigm for unsupervised tracking, reducing the reliance on labeled data and paving the way for more scalable and adaptable tracking systems. Keywords: Multi-Object Tracking, Unsupervised Learning, Attention Mechanism, Animation, Self-Supervision, Computer Vision.
