Graph representation learning aims to embed graph-structured data into a low-dimensional space while preserving essential topological properties. This paper introduces a novel approach, Kernel-Regularized Graph Representation Learning (KRGRL), designed to enhance the "Gestalt" perception of graphs, promoting representations that capture global structural coherence and relationships. KRGRL leverages kernel methods to implicitly define and regularize the embedding space, guiding the learning process to favor solutions aligned with intuitive Gestalt principles such as proximity, similarity, and continuity. Specifically, we employ a kernel function to measure the similarity between nodes based on their graph context and incorporate a regularization term that penalizes deviations from these kernel-defined relationships. Experiments on various benchmark datasets, including social networks and biological networks, demonstrate that KRGRL outperforms existing graph embedding techniques in tasks such as node classification and link prediction. Furthermore, visualization of the learned embeddings reveals that KRGRL generates more structured and interpretable representations, reflecting improved Gestalt perception. The proposed method offers a new perspective on graph representation learning by explicitly incorporating Gestalt principles, leading to more robust and insightful graph embeddings. Keywords: Graph Representation Learning, Kernel Methods, Graph Embedding, Gestalt Principles, Machine Learning, Network Analysis.
