This paper introduces YuruGAN, a novel generative framework designed to synthesize Yuru-Chara mascots—regional Japanese characters—using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) optimized for small and diverse datasets. Recognizing the scarcity and high variability of Yuru-Chara training images, we propose a clustering-based data preprocessing method that groups mascot images by visual similarity and semantic features. This clustering enables more stable GAN training and preserves unique stylistic elements inherent to Yuru-Chara designs. Our approach incorporates data augmentation and tailored loss functions to further mitigate the challenges posed by limited data availability. Experimental results demonstrate that YuruGAN outperforms baseline GAN models in both image fidelity and creativity, as judged by Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and human evaluation. Qualitative analysis reveals that YuruGAN effectively captures the playful and distinctive spirit of authentic Yuru-Chara mascots while generating novel character designs suitable for branding and cultural promotion. The proposed method extends the utility of GANs for style-rich image domains with limited data and provides a blueprint for automated mascot generation in content creation industries. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, Yuru-Chara, Mascot Design, Small Dataset, Clustering, Data Augmentation, Image Synthesis.
