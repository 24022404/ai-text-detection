Graph embedding techniques have become essential for analyzing complex networks in various domains, such as social networks, recommendation systems, and biological networks. However, existing methods often struggle with robustness, particularly when dealing with noisy or incomplete graph structures. This paper introduces RECS (Robust Graph Embedding Using Connection Subgraphs), a novel framework designed to enhance the robustness and accuracy of graph embeddings. RECS leverages connection subgraphs, which capture localized structural patterns, to generate embeddings that are resilient to noise and missing data. The proposed approach employs a combination of graph sampling and deep learning to learn node representations that preserve both global and local topological properties. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that RECS outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of embedding quality and robustness, achieving significant improvements in downstream tasks such as node classification and link prediction. The framework's ability to handle noisy and sparse graphs makes it particularly suitable for real-world applications where data quality is often suboptimal. This research contributes to the advancement of graph representation learning by providing a scalable and robust solution that can be applied across diverse domains.  

**Keywords**: graph embedding, connection subgraphs, robustness, deep learning, node classification, link prediction.
