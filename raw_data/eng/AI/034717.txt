**Abstract**

Cervical cancer remains a leading cause of mortality among women worldwide, necessitating reliable and efficient diagnostic tools. This study presents a convolutional neural network (CNN)-based approach for automated classification of cervical cancer in whole-slide histopathology images. The primary objective is to develop a robust model capable of distinguishing between normal, pre-cancerous, and cancerous tissue samples with high accuracy and efficiency. The proposed method utilizes a deep CNN architecture optimized for the analysis of high-resolution, gigapixel whole-slide images, incorporating data augmentation and patch-based analysis to address challenges such as class imbalance and morphological variability. Comprehensive experiments were conducted on a publicly available cervical histopathology dataset to evaluate the model's performance. The results demonstrate that our CNN-based system achieves a classification accuracy of 92.5%, outperforming traditional machine learning approaches and previously reported deep learning models in terms of both precision and recall. These findings indicate that the proposed method can provide pathologists with a powerful tool for rapid and accurate interpretation of histopathology slides, enabling earlier detection and improved management of cervical cancer. This research highlights the potential of deep learning techniques, specifically CNNs, for advancing digital pathology and supporting clinical decision-making processes. Keywords: cervical cancer, convolutional neural networks, whole-slide images, histopathology, deep learning, cancer classification, digital pathology.
