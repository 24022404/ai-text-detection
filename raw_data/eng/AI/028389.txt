Adversarial attacks present significant challenges to the robustness and reliability of deep learning-based object detection systems, particularly in image and video domains. This paper investigates the development of transferable adversarial attack methods capable of compromising multiple state-of-the-art object detectors across both static images and continuous video streams. We propose a novel framework that generates adversarial perturbations optimized not only for individual models but also to maintain high effectiveness across diverse detection architectures, including both two-stage and single-stage detectors. Our approach employs a combination of gradient-based attack strategies and temporal consistency constraints to ensure the generated adversarial examples remain imperceptible yet effective over consecutive video frames. Experimental results demonstrate that our attacks achieve a higher transfer rate and maintain consistent effectiveness when evaluated against a variety of leading image and video object detection models, surpassing existing baseline attack methods in terms of both transferability and visual stealth. These findings underscore the pressing need for enhanced defense mechanisms and provide valuable insights into the vulnerabilities of contemporary detection systems. Our work contributes a transferable attack paradigm and comprehensive evaluation strategy, advancing the understanding of adversarial risks in computer vision. 

Keywords: adversarial attacks, transferability, object detection, image processing, video analysis, deep learning, model robustness.
