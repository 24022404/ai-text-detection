This paper investigates the sample complexity of episodic fixed-horizon reinforcement learning (RL). We analyze the number of episodes required to learn a near-optimal policy within a specified accuracy level. Our approach leverages techniques fromProbably Approximately Correct (PAC) learning and explores the trade-offs between exploration and exploitation in finite Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). We derive novel bounds on the sample complexity, demonstrating how it scales with the horizon length, number of states, actions, and the desired accuracy. The analysis provides tighter guarantees compared to existing results under certain conditions, specifically for MDPs with deterministic transitions. Empirical results on benchmark RL environments validate the theoretical findings, showcasing improved sample efficiency. This work contributes to a better understanding of the fundamental limits of reinforcement learning and provides practical guidelines for designing efficient RL algorithms. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Sample Complexity, Episodic RL, PAC Learning, Markov Decision Processes.
