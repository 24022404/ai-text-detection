In this study, we tackle the challenge of inverse problems using the innovative framework of invertible neural networks (INNs). Inverse problems, common in fields such as physics, image processing, and medical imaging, involve deducing underlying causes from observed data, often under conditions of noise and incomplete information. The purpose of this research is to explore the potential of INNs in providing robust and efficient solutions to these complex problems. We employ a novel approach that leverages the inherently reversible nature of these networks, enabling effective mapping between data and desired latent representations. Our approach is tested on several benchmark datasets relevant to different inverse problem scenarios, demonstrating superior performance in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency compared to traditional methods and other neural network architectures. Key findings reveal that INNs can more accurately reconstruct inputs and provide reliable confidence assessments, thanks to their capacity to model bijective transformations. This research contributes a significant advancement in solving inverse problems, showcasing the versatility and power of INNs in complex system analysis. The implications of our work extend to enhancements in areas such as seismic data interpretation, medical image reconstruction, and astrophysical signal analysis. Our findings suggest that the application of INNs holds great promise for future advancements in a wide spectrum of scientific and engineering challenges. Keywords: inverse problems, invertible neural networks, neural architecture, data reconstruction, computational efficiency.
