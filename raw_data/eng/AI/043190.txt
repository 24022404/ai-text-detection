Point cloud registration is a fundamental task in 3D computer vision, with applications ranging from robotics to augmented reality. However, achieving robust and efficient alignment of point clouds remains challenging due to noise, outliers, and partial overlaps. This paper introduces **PointNetLK**, a novel approach that combines the strengths of PointNet and the Lucas-Kanade (LK) algorithm for point cloud registration. The proposed method leverages PointNet's ability to extract global features from unordered point sets and integrates them into an iterative LK framework to optimize alignment. Unlike traditional methods that rely on handcrafted features or iterative closest point (ICP) algorithms, PointNetLK learns feature representations directly from raw point clouds, enabling robustness to noise and partial data.  

Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that PointNetLK achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The method outperforms existing techniques in scenarios with significant noise and partial overlaps, showcasing its robustness. Furthermore, PointNetLK is computationally efficient, making it suitable for real-time applications. The key contribution of this work lies in the seamless integration of deep learning with classical optimization techniques, offering a new perspective on point cloud registration. This research opens avenues for further exploration in learning-based 3D vision tasks and has potential applications in autonomous navigation, 3D reconstruction, and medical imaging.  

**Keywords**: Point cloud registration, PointNet, Lucas-Kanade algorithm, 3D computer vision, deep learning, feature extraction.
