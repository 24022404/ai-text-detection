This paper introduces Evolving-Graph Gaussian Processes (EGGP), a novel framework for modeling data that is structured over dynamic graphs, where both the graph topology and node attributes may change over time. Traditional Gaussian Processes (GPs) have proven effective for regression and classification on static data but face challenges when applied to temporally varying graph domains. Our approach extends standard GP models by integrating evolving-graph kernels, enabling flexible, non-parametric representation of time-dependent structural patterns in graph-structured data. We formalize the EGGP framework, propose efficient algorithms for scalable inference, and demonstrate its effectiveness using both synthetic and real-world datasets from domains such as social network analysis and sensor networks. Experimental results show that EGGP significantly outperforms existing static or sequence-based GP methods in predictive accuracy and adaptability to evolving structures. Our findings highlight the potential of EGGP to advance the analysis of dynamic graph data in various Information Technology applications, including anomaly detection, forecasting, and recommendation systems. Keywords: Gaussian Processes, evolving graphs, dynamic networks, kernel methods, graph machine learning, temporal modeling.
