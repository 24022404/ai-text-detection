Human Activity Recognition (HAR) has gained significant attention due to its applications in healthcare, fitness tracking, and smart environments. However, the lack of comprehensive datasets incorporating multi-modal sensor data limits the development of robust and generalizable models. This paper introduces **MEx**, a novel multi-modal dataset designed to advance research in HAR. MEx integrates data from wearable sensors, cameras, and environmental sensors, capturing a wide range of physical exercises performed by diverse participants in real-world settings.  

To construct MEx, we employed a systematic data collection protocol, ensuring high-quality annotations and synchronization across modalities. We propose a multi-modal fusion framework leveraging deep learning techniques, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), to effectively combine and analyze heterogeneous data streams. Our experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming single-modal approaches by 12.3% in accuracy and 15.7% in F1-score.  

The MEx dataset and our fusion framework provide a foundation for future research in multi-modal HAR, enabling the development of more accurate and adaptive systems. The dataset is publicly available, fostering collaboration and innovation in the field. This work contributes to the growing body of research on multi-modal sensing and highlights the importance of diverse data sources for improving activity recognition systems.  

**Keywords**: Human Activity Recognition, Multi-modal Dataset, Wearable Sensors, Deep Learning, Sensor Fusion, MEx.
