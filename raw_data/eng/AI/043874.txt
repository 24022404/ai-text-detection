The design of optical thin films is a critical task in enhancing the performance of optical devices such as lenses, mirrors, and filters. Traditional design methods often rely on trial-and-error, limiting efficiency and innovation. This research aims to introduce a novel reinforcement learning approach to optimize the design process of optical thin films, minimizing manufacturing constraints and improving optical characteristics.

Methods/Approach: We developed a reinforcement learning framework that leverages a state-of-the-art neural network architecture to iteratively refine thin-film designs. The model is trained to predict optimal layer thickness and material composition by maximizing specific performance metrics such as reflectance, transmittance, and bandwidth. To handle the multi-objective nature of thin-film design, we incorporated a reward system that encourages not only precision in optical properties but also manufacturability and cost-effectiveness.

Results/Findings: The proposed method significantly outperforms traditional optimization techniques in both speed and design quality. Our approach achieved higher accuracy in desired optical properties with a reduction in computation time by approximately 30%. Comparative analyses demonstrated improvements in reflectance and transmittance control, notably in complex multi-layer designs, which were challenging for conventional methods.

Conclusion/Implications: This research presents a pioneering application of reinforcement learning in the optical thin-film field, offering a robust alternative to conventional design strategies. The approach has potential for broad applications across various optical devices, promising advancements in industries such as telecommunications, photovoltaics, and medical imaging. Future work will focus on expanding the model's applicability to a wider range of materials and scaling the approach for industrial-level applications.

Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Optical Thin-Film Design, Neural Networks, Multi-Objective Optimization, Optical Properties.
