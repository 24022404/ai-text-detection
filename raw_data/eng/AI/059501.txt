Skeleton-based action recognition has become a prominent method in computer vision, leveraging skeletal data to achieve robust human action classification. However, security concerns arise as these systems can be susceptible to adversarial attacks, which subtly perturb input data to mislead recognition models. This paper aims to systematically investigate the adversarial vulnerability of skeleton-based action recognition models. We introduce a comprehensive evaluation framework that examines the robustness of various state-of-the-art neural architectures—including Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)—against different types of adversarial attacks specifically tailored for skeletal sequences. Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, we analyze the effectiveness of both white-box and black-box attack strategies, such as fast gradient sign method (FGSM) and iterative attacks, highlighting significant drops in recognition accuracy under adversarial conditions. The results reveal critical insights into model weaknesses and the transferability of adversarial examples across different architectures. Furthermore, we evaluate several defense mechanisms, including adversarial training and input sanitization, assessing their capacity for improving robustness. Our findings underscore the urgent need for developing more secure and reliable skeleton-based action recognition models, and provide actionable guidance for future research in constructing adversarially resilient systems. Keywords: skeleton-based action recognition, adversarial attacks, robustness, Graph Convolutional Networks, security, neural networks.
