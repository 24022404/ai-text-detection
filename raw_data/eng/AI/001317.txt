Sparse phase retrieval has gained significant importance in various fields, such as signal processing and optical imaging, where the challenge lies in recovering a signal with missing phase information, particularly when the signal is sparse. This paper introduces a novel approach to efficiently solve the sparse phase retrieval problem using a stochastic alternating minimizing method.

Methods/Approach: The proposed method integrates stochastic optimization with an alternating minimization framework, effectively tackling the inherent non-convexity of the phase retrieval problem. By leveraging sparsity constraints, the algorithm iteratively refines the solution through alternating updates, reducing computational complexity and enhancing scalability. The stochastic component optimizes the selection of subproblems in each iteration, further improving convergence speed and accuracy.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the proposed stochastic alternating minimizing method significantly outperforms existing techniques in terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency. Benchmarks across synthetic and real-world datasets reveal improvement in recovery fidelity, especially in highly sparse scenarios. Comparisons with state-of-the-art methods underscore its robustness and effectiveness in maintaining high-quality reconstruction under varying noise levels.

Conclusion/Implications: This research presents a substantial advancement in the field of phase retrieval by introducing a method that balances accuracy and efficiency through a novel integration of stochastic optimization and alternating minimization. The contributions have promising implications for applications requiring high-dimensional signal recovery with minimal phase information. Future work may explore further enhancements through hybrid techniques or extending this approach to other inverse problems in signal processing and imaging.

Keywords: sparse phase retrieval, stochastic optimization, alternating minimization, signal processing, non-convex optimization, signal recovery, computational efficiency.
