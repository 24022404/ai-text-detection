Color constancy, the ability to perceive consistent colors under varying illumination, is a critical challenge in computer vision and image processing. This paper introduces a novel approach, the *Bag of Color Features* (BoCF), to address color constancy by leveraging local color distributions within an image. The proposed method extracts a diverse set of color features from image patches, capturing both global and local color statistics, and aggregates them into a unified representation. Unlike traditional methods that rely on global assumptions or handcrafted features, BoCF adapts to the inherent variability of natural scenes, offering a more robust and flexible solution. We evaluate the proposed approach on standard datasets, including the Gehler-Shi and NUS datasets, and demonstrate its superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods. Experimental results show that BoCF achieves significant improvements in angular error metrics, highlighting its effectiveness in handling complex illumination conditions. The method’s computational efficiency and scalability further underscore its practical applicability in real-world scenarios, such as digital photography and autonomous systems. This work contributes a novel perspective to color constancy research, emphasizing the importance of local color feature aggregation for accurate illumination estimation.  

**Keywords**: Color constancy, Bag of Color Features, illumination estimation, computer vision, image processing.
