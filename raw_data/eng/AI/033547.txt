This paper introduces Wasserstein Dictionary Learning, a novel unsupervised non-linear dictionary learning approach based on optimal transport theory. The objective is to learn a dictionary that captures the underlying structure of complex data distributions, enabling efficient and accurate representation of high-dimensional data. Our method leverages the Wasserstein distance to measure the dissimilarity between data distributions, allowing for the discovery of non-linear relationships and patterns. The approach utilizes a unique combination of optimal transport and dictionary learning techniques to learn a compact and informative dictionary. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method in learning dictionaries that outperform state-of-the-art techniques in terms of representation accuracy and computational efficiency. The proposed framework has significant implications for various applications, including image and signal processing, data compression, and machine learning. Key contributions of this research include the introduction of optimal transport theory to dictionary learning, enabling the handling of non-linear data relationships, and the development of a computationally efficient algorithm for large-scale data sets. Relevant keywords: optimal transport, dictionary learning, unsupervised learning, non-linear dimensionality reduction, Wasserstein distance.
