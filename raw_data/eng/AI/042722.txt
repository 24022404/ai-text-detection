Link prediction in scene text is a critical task for understanding relationships between textual elements in complex visual environments. This paper introduces NENET, a novel edge learnable network designed to address the challenges of link prediction in scene text by leveraging advanced graph-based learning techniques. The proposed approach focuses on modeling the relationships between text instances as edges in a graph, enabling the network to learn contextual dependencies and spatial arrangements effectively. NENET employs a combination of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for feature extraction and graph neural networks (GNNs) for edge representation learning, ensuring robust performance across diverse text layouts and orientations. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that NENET outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art performance in link prediction tasks. The network’s ability to generalize across varying text densities and complex backgrounds highlights its potential for real-world applications, such as document analysis, scene understanding, and automated text extraction systems. This research contributes to the field by introducing a scalable and adaptable framework for link prediction, paving the way for future advancements in scene text analysis.  

**Keywords**: Link prediction, scene text analysis, graph neural networks, edge learning, text relationship modeling, NENET.
