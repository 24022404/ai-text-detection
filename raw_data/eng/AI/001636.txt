This paper introduces a novel Multimodal Dual Attention Memory (MDAM) framework for Video Story Question Answering (VideoQA), aimed at enhancing the machine's understanding of complex audio-visual narratives. The proposed approach integrates both visual and textual modalities through a specialized dual attention mechanism, which independently and jointly attends to relevant aspects of video frames and corresponding linguistic information, such as subtitles or dialogue. The dual attention is coupled with a memory module that effectively captures temporal dependencies and cross-modal interactions, enabling the model to reason over sequential story elements. Extensive experiments on benchmark VideoQA datasets demonstrate that MDAM achieves superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, particularly in scenarios requiring long-term reasoning and contextual understanding. The results indicate notable improvements in answer accuracy and robustness across diverse question types. This research contributes a flexible and scalable solution to multimodal question answering tasks, with potential applications in automated video content analysis, intelligent tutoring systems, and human-computer interaction. Key innovations include the design of the dual attention architecture and seamless multimodal integration, which collectively advance the capabilities of VideoQA systems. 

Keywords: VideoQA, multimodal learning, dual attention, memory networks, temporal reasoning, video story understanding, deep learning.
