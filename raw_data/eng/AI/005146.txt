Predicting molecular properties from their structures is crucial in drug discovery and materials science. Traditional quantitative structure-property relationship (QSPR) methods often struggle with complex molecular representations. This paper introduces a novel attention- and gate-augmented graph convolutional network (AG-GCN) to deeply learn molecular structure-property relationships. The AG-GCN incorporates an attention mechanism to selectively aggregate information from neighboring atoms and a gating mechanism to control the flow of information between graph convolutional layers, allowing for more effective feature extraction. We evaluated the AG-GCN on several benchmark datasets, including those for predicting solubility, toxicity, and bioactivity. Results demonstrate that AG-GCN consistently outperforms existing GCN-based and traditional QSPR methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance in most tasks. Ablation studies confirm the importance of both the attention and gating mechanisms. The AG-GCN provides a powerful and interpretable framework for molecular property prediction, facilitating the design of novel molecules with desired characteristics. Keywords: Graph Convolutional Network, Molecular Property Prediction, Attention Mechanism, Gating Mechanism, Deep Learning, Cheminformatics.
