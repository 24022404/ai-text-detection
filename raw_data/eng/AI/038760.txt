This paper explores the potential of leveraging human instructions to enhance generalization in reinforcement learning (RL) agents. The objective is to develop a framework that enables RL agents to learn from human guidance, improving their ability to adapt to new, unseen environments. Our approach involves incorporating human-provided instructions into the RL framework, allowing agents to learn from demonstrations and feedback. We employ a novel algorithm that combines reinforcement learning with imitation learning, enabling agents to generalize more effectively to new situations. Experimental results demonstrate that our method significantly improves the generalization performance of RL agents, outperforming traditional RL approaches. The key findings suggest that human instructions can provide valuable insights, guiding the agent's exploration and learning process. Our research contributes to the development of more robust and adaptable RL agents, with potential applications in areas such as robotics, game playing, and autonomous systems. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, human-in-the-loop, imitation learning, generalization, AI agents.
