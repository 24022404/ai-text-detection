Time series classification (TSC) has long been dominated by a debate between feature-based and shape-based approaches, often treated as mutually exclusive paradigms. This paper challenges this false dichotomy by proposing a unified framework that leverages the strengths of both methodologies. We introduce a hybrid model that integrates feature extraction techniques with shape-based similarity measures, enabling a more comprehensive representation of time series data. Our approach employs advanced machine learning algorithms to dynamically balance the contributions of features and shape, optimizing classification accuracy. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms traditional single-paradigm approaches, achieving state-of-the-art performance in terms of accuracy and robustness. The findings highlight the importance of combining feature and shape information for effective TSC, offering new insights into the design of classification systems. This research contributes to the field by bridging the gap between feature-based and shape-based methods, paving the way for more versatile and accurate time series analysis tools.  

**Keywords**: Time series classification, feature extraction, shape-based methods, hybrid models, machine learning, dynamic optimization.
