Semantic image segmentation, a crucial task in computer vision, benefits significantly from deep learning. However, designing effective deep neural networks for this task remains challenging. Neural Architecture Search (NAS) offers a promising solution by automating this design process. This paper introduces Densely Connected Neural Architecture Search (DCNAS), a novel NAS framework specifically tailored for semantic image segmentation. DCNAS employs a densely connected search space, promoting information flow and feature reuse across different layers. This approach enables the discovery of architectures that effectively capture both local and global contextual information, critical for accurate segmentation. We evaluate DCNAS on benchmark datasets, including Cityscapes and PASCAL VOC 2012, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing NAS-based and manually designed segmentation models. Specifically, DCNAS achieves a significant improvement in mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) while maintaining computational efficiency. The results highlight the effectiveness of the densely connected search space in discovering high-performing segmentation architectures. DCNAS offers a practical and efficient approach to automated neural architecture design for semantic image segmentation, reducing the reliance on manual expertise and accelerating the development of advanced vision systems.
Keywords: Neural Architecture Search, Semantic Image Segmentation, Deep Learning, Dense Connectivity, Automation.
