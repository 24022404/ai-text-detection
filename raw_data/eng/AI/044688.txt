Generalized Zero-shot Learning (GZSL) aims to recognize objects from both seen and unseen classes by leveraging semantic representations, such as attributes or textual descriptions. However, existing methods often struggle with the domain shift problem, where the model biases toward seen classes during inference. To address this challenge, we propose a novel Two-Level Adversarial Visual-Semantic Coupling (TAVSC) framework. At the first level, a visual-semantic alignment module ensures that visual features are tightly coupled with their corresponding semantic embeddings. At the second level, an adversarial learning mechanism is introduced to minimize the distribution gap between seen and unseen classes, enabling better generalization. Our approach employs a generative adversarial network (GAN) to synthesize realistic visual features for unseen classes, enhancing the model's ability to handle unseen data. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including CUB, SUN, and AWA2, demonstrate that TAVSC achieves state-of-the-art performance in GZSL settings. The results show significant improvements in harmonic mean accuracy, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed two-level coupling strategy. This work contributes to advancing zero-shot learning by addressing the domain shift problem and providing a robust framework for real-world applications where unseen classes are prevalent.  

**Keywords**: Generalized Zero-shot Learning, Adversarial Learning, Visual-Semantic Coupling, Domain Shift, GAN, Feature Synthesis.
