This paper introduces a novel approach for learning pedestrian trajectory representations using an Actor-Critic Sequence-to-Sequence Autoencoder (AC-Seq2Seq-AE). The objective is to develop a robust and informative trajectory representation suitable for various applications, including anomaly detection, behavior prediction, and trajectory clustering. The proposed AC-Seq2Seq-AE leverages a sequence-to-sequence autoencoder architecture for trajectory reconstruction, enhanced by an actor-critic reinforcement learning framework to optimize the latent space representation. The actor network guides the encoder to produce representations that are beneficial for downstream tasks, while the critic network evaluates the quality of the reconstructed trajectories. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the AC-Seq2Seq-AE outperforms existing methods in trajectory reconstruction accuracy and downstream task performance. Specifically, the learned representations enable more effective anomaly detection and trajectory clustering compared to traditional autoencoder-based approaches. The findings suggest that the actor-critic framework significantly improves the quality and utility of trajectory representations learned by sequence-to-sequence autoencoders. Keywords: Pedestrian Trajectory, Representation Learning, Sequence-to-Sequence, Autoencoder, Actor-Critic, Reinforcement Learning.
