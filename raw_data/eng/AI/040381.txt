This paper addresses the challenge of adapting anchorless object detection models from synthetic to real-world images, a crucial problem in computer vision. The objective is to develop an unsupervised domain adaptation approach that enables accurate object detection in real images without requiring labeled real-world data. Our method leverages a novel combination of adversarial training and self-supervised learning to align the feature distributions between synthetic and real images. The approach utilizes a domain-invariant feature extractor and a detection head that can effectively detect objects in real images. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. The key findings show that our method outperforms existing unsupervised domain adaptation techniques, with significant improvements in object detection accuracy. This research contributes to the development of more robust and generalizable object detection systems, with potential applications in autonomous driving, surveillance, and robotics. The proposed approach highlights the importance of domain adaptation in computer vision, particularly in scenarios where labeled real-world data is scarce or unavailable. Key keywords: unsupervised domain adaptation, anchorless object detection, synthetic-to-real translation, computer vision, deep learning.
