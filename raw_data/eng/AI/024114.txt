This paper introduces a novel second-order optimization framework tailored for Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (Neural ODEs), addressing the computational inefficiencies and convergence challenges associated with first-order methods. The proposed approach leverages second-order derivative information to enhance optimization stability and accelerate training convergence. Specifically, we develop a scalable and memory-efficient algorithm that approximates the Hessian matrix using quasi-Newton techniques, enabling faster gradient updates while maintaining computational tractability. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed optimizer achieves superior convergence rates and improved model performance compared to state-of-the-art first-order methods. Furthermore, the framework exhibits robustness across diverse Neural ODE architectures, including those applied to time-series prediction and continuous-depth models. The findings highlight the potential of second-order optimization in advancing the scalability and efficiency of Neural ODEs, paving the way for broader applications in machine learning and scientific computing.  

**Keywords**: Neural ODEs, second-order optimization, quasi-Newton methods, convergence acceleration, continuous-depth models.
