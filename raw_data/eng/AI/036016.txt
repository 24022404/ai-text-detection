This paper addresses the challenge of model calibration in the context of long-tailed object detection and instance segmentation, where the imbalanced distribution of object classes can significantly impact model accuracy and reliability. Traditional object detection models face difficulties when dealing with underrepresented classes, leading to skewed predictions and suboptimal performance.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel calibration approach to tackle this issue, incorporating advanced techniques such as re-weighted loss functions and adaptive balancing mechanisms. Our method integrates seamlessly with existing detection frameworks, enhancing their ability to handle class imbalance. The approach focuses on adjusting the model's confidence scores post-training to improve the accuracy of predictions for minority classes.

Results/Findings: Through comprehensive experiments on benchmark datasets, our calibrated model demonstrates notable improvements in both accuracy and fairness across all object classes, particularly enhancing the recognition of rare instances. Comparative analysis with state-of-the-art methods shows a significant increase in performance metrics, including mean Average Precision (mAP) and instance segmentation quality, validating the efficacy of the proposed calibration technique.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field of object detection by providing a robust solution for long-tailed distributions, a common scenario in real-world applications. The implications of enhanced model calibration extend to various domains such as autonomous driving, wildlife monitoring, and security systems, where accurate detection of rare objects is critical. Our approach not only enhances model performance but also ensures more equitable treatment across object classes, paving the way for more reliable AI systems.

Keywords: model calibration, long-tailed distribution, object detection, instance segmentation, class imbalance, adaptive balancing.
