This paper introduces a novel approach to graph representation learning, addressing the limitations of existing contrastive learning methods. Traditional models often neglect the significance of node similarity at varying distances within the graph structure. Our proposed method, Distance-wise Graph Contrastive Learning (DGCL), aims to capture the nuanced relational information by emphasizing distance-dependent node embeddings.

Methods/Approach: The DGCL framework systematically integrates distance information into the contrastive learning paradigm for graphs. We employ a novel contrastive loss function that dynamically recalibrates node similarity based on their relative distances. This strategy ensures that embeddings accurately reflect both local and global graph structures. We utilize an innovative combination of graph neural networks (GNNs) and distance-aware augmentation strategies to enhance representation quality.

Results/Findings: Our experimental evaluations demonstrate that DGCL significantly outperforms state-of-the-art graph representation learning models across multiple benchmark datasets. Our method achieves superior performance in node classification, clustering, and link prediction tasks, with notable improvements in capturing long-range dependencies and structural nuances. Comparative analysis reveals that DGCL is both robust and generalizable, maintaining high accuracy even on large-scale and complex graphs.

Conclusion/Implications: The DGCL approach offers a transformative shift in graph contrastive learning by intelligently incorporating distance metrics into the learning process. Our contributions include a scalable and effective solution for enhanced graph representations, with potential applications in network analysis, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems. By bridging the gap between local and global graph perceptions, DGCL sets a new standard in the efficient extraction of meaningful graph features. Key keywords include graph representation learning, contrastive learning, graph neural networks, and distance-aware embeddings.
