This paper revisits the paradigm of parameter sharing in multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL), a prominent approach aimed at improving coordination and reducing computational complexity in large agent systems. The research explores the effectiveness and limitations of parameter sharing techniques and assesses their impact on the performance and scalability of MADRL frameworks.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel framework that enables dynamic parameter sharing among agents based on their roles and environment dynamics, utilizing a combination of transfer learning and adaptive strategy selection. This approach is benchmarked against traditional fixed parameter sharing schemes to evaluate enhancements in flexibility and efficiency. In particular, our method leverages advanced neural architecture to facilitate communication and collaboration among agents, ensuring optimal learning strategies are dynamically adopted.

Results/Findings: The proposed framework demonstrates significant improvements in both training speed and performance outcomes across a variety of simulated environments. Compared to existing baseline MADRL techniques, our dynamic parameter sharing strategy enhances coordination among agents and adapts more efficiently to changing environmental conditions. Notably, results indicate reduced convergence time and improved scalability, especially in complex, multi-faceted tasks involving numerous agents.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field of multi-agent systems by providing a scalable and adaptive parameter sharing strategy that surpasses the limitations of conventional approaches. The findings underscore the potential applications of the proposed framework in diverse scenarios, such as autonomous vehicles, resource allocation, and collaborative robotics. Our work advances the understanding of parameter sharing mechanics in MADRL, paving the way for more robust and efficient agent-based solutions.

Keywords: Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning, Parameter Sharing, Dynamic Framework, Transfer Learning, Scalability, Coordination.
