This paper addresses the challenge of self-supervised scene flow estimation by introducing an adversarial metric learning approach. The objective is to improve the accuracy of scene flow estimation by learning to distinguish between trustworthy and untrustworthy neighboring pixels. Our method employs a novel adversarial framework that pits a scene flow estimator against a discriminator, which attempts to identify incorrect estimates. The estimator is trained to fool the discriminator, resulting in more robust and accurate scene flow predictions. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, outperforming state-of-the-art self-supervised methods by a significant margin. Our contributions include a new adversarial metric learning formulation and a robust scene flow estimation model that can handle complex and dynamic scenes. The proposed method has potential applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and computer vision, and highlights the importance of careful consideration of neighboring pixel relationships in scene flow estimation. Key keywords: self-supervised learning, scene flow estimation, adversarial metric learning, computer vision, robotics.
