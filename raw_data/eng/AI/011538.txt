This paper addresses the challenge of meta-learning transferable representations in scenarios where only a single target domain is available for adaptation. Traditional meta-learning approaches often rely on multiple target domains to generalize effectively, limiting their applicability in real-world settings where data is scarce. We propose a novel framework that leverages a combination of self-supervised learning and domain adaptation techniques to enable robust representation transfer with minimal target data. Our method employs a meta-optimization strategy that iteratively refines the representation space, ensuring adaptability to the target domain while preserving generalizability. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing meta-learning methods in single-target-domain scenarios, achieving significant improvements in classification accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the potential of our framework for applications in domains such as healthcare and robotics, where labeled data is often limited. This work contributes to advancing meta-learning by providing a practical solution for transfer learning in data-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: meta-learning, transfer learning, domain adaptation, self-supervised learning, single-target domain, representation learning.
