Estimating causal effects from observational data is a fundamental challenge across various domains. This paper introduces a transfer learning framework to improve the estimation of causal effects using neural networks, particularly when target datasets are small or biased. We propose a novel approach that leverages knowledge learned from related source datasets with known causal structures to enhance the performance of neural network estimators on the target dataset. Specifically, we pre-train a neural network on the source dataset to capture invariant relationships and then fine-tune it on the target dataset using a modified loss function that incorporates causal constraints. We evaluate our approach on synthetic and semi-synthetic datasets, demonstrating that it significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of estimation accuracy and robustness, especially when the target dataset suffers from limited sample size or confounding bias. Our findings suggest that transfer learning can effectively improve causal effect estimation by leveraging external knowledge and reducing the reliance on large, unbiased target datasets, opening up opportunities for more reliable causal inference in real-world applications.

Keywords: Transfer Learning, Causal Inference, Neural Networks, Causal Effect Estimation, Observational Data, Bias Reduction.
