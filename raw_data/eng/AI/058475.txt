Smart home systems rely heavily on activity recognition to enable context-aware automation and enhance user comfort and security. However, the adoption of advanced machine learning techniques in this domain often results in black-box models with limited transparency, hindering user trust and system adaptability. This paper introduces a novel framework for explainable activity recognition in smart home environments, aiming to balance high predictive accuracy with interpretability. The proposed approach leverages a combination of sensor data fusion, feature selection, and interpretable machine learning models, such as decision trees and attention-enabled neural networks, to transparently identify and explain daily activities within household settings. Extensive experiments were conducted using real-world datasets collected from multi-modal sensors deployed across various smart home scenarios. Results demonstrate that the explainable models achieve competitive recognition accuracy compared to state-of-the-art deep learning methods while offering clear, human-understandable justifications for their predictions. The framework supports real-time operation and provides actionable explanations that enhance user confidence, facilitate debugging, and enable personalization of smart home behaviors. The findings suggest that integrating explainability into activity recognition not only maintains system performance but also promotes broader acceptance and effective use of smart home technologies. Keywords: explainability, activity recognition, smart home systems, interpretable machine learning, sensor fusion, user trust.
