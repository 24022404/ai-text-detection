This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the resolution of brain Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) images, crucial for accurate diagnosis and detailed analysis. We propose a novel super-resolution method leveraging overcomplete dictionaries and nonlocal similarity. Our approach utilizes an overcomplete dictionary learned from high-resolution training data to represent image patches. To enhance reconstruction accuracy, we incorporate nonlocal self-similarity, identifying similar patches within the low-resolution image to constrain the high-resolution reconstruction. Experimental results on a dataset of brain MRI images demonstrate that our method achieves superior performance compared to state-of-the-art super-resolution techniques, both quantitatively in terms of PSNR and SSIM, and qualitatively with improved visual detail. The proposed method offers a valuable tool for improving the clarity and diagnostic utility of brain MRI images, potentially leading to more accurate diagnoses and treatment planning. Keywords: Super-resolution, MRI, Brain Imaging, Overcomplete Dictionary, Nonlocal Similarity, Image Reconstruction.
