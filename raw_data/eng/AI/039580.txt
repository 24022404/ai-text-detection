Causality has emerged as a critical area of study in machine learning (ML), offering the potential to enhance model interpretability, robustness, and decision-making capabilities. This paper explores the integration of causal reasoning into ML frameworks, addressing the limitations of traditional correlation-based approaches. We propose a novel methodology that combines causal inference techniques with supervised and unsupervised learning models to identify and leverage cause-effect relationships within complex datasets. Our approach employs directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) and counterfactual analysis to disentangle causal dependencies, enabling more accurate predictions and actionable insights. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in model performance across various benchmarks, particularly in scenarios involving confounding variables and spurious correlations. The findings highlight the potential of causality-aware ML systems to advance applications in healthcare, finance, and autonomous systems. This research contributes to the growing body of work on causal ML by providing a scalable and interpretable framework for integrating causality into modern AI pipelines.  

**Keywords**: causality, machine learning, causal inference, directed acyclic graphs, counterfactual analysis, interpretability, AI systems.
