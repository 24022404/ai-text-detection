This paper presents a comprehensive benchmarking study of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) algorithms for continuous control tasks. The objective is to evaluate and compare the performance of state-of-the-art DRL methods, including policy gradient, actor-critic, and deep deterministic policy gradient algorithms. We employ a range of benchmark environments, such as robotic arm manipulation and locomotion tasks, to assess the algorithms' ability to learn complex control policies. Our results show that while all algorithms demonstrate impressive performance, significant differences exist in their sample efficiency, stability, and ability to handle high-dimensional action spaces. We identify key factors influencing algorithmic performance, including exploration strategies, network architectures, and hyperparameter tuning. The findings of this study have important implications for the development of autonomous systems, robotics, and other applications requiring continuous control. Our benchmarking framework and results provide a foundation for future research in DRL, highlighting the need for more efficient, robust, and generalizable algorithms. Key keywords: deep reinforcement learning, continuous control, benchmarking, policy gradient, actor-critic, robotic arm manipulation, locomotion tasks.
