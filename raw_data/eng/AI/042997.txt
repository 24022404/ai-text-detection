Understanding and predicting system dynamics under varying contextual conditions is a critical challenge in fields such as robotics, autonomous systems, and predictive modeling. This paper addresses the problem of optimal data collection for training context-conditional dynamics models, which aim to accurately predict system behavior across diverse environmental and operational contexts. We propose a novel framework that leverages active learning and information-theoretic principles to identify the most informative data points for model training, ensuring robust performance across a wide range of scenarios. Our approach combines Bayesian optimization with context-aware sampling strategies to minimize data collection costs while maximizing model generalization. Experimental results demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms traditional random sampling and other baseline approaches in terms of prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. The proposed framework not only enhances the reliability of context-conditional dynamics models but also provides a scalable solution for real-world applications where data acquisition is resource-intensive. This work contributes to advancing the field of context-aware modeling by offering a systematic and efficient methodology for data collection, with potential applications in autonomous systems, industrial automation, and adaptive control systems.  

**Keywords**: context-conditional models, data collection, active learning, Bayesian optimization, predictive modeling, autonomous systems.
