Knowledge Graphs (KGs) are widely used for representing structured information, but reasoning over them remains a significant challenge due to their inherent complexity and sparsity. This paper introduces **Query2box**, a novel framework for reasoning over KGs in vector space using **box embeddings**. The primary objective of this work is to enable efficient and interpretable reasoning over complex logical queries, such as conjunctions, disjunctions, and existential quantifiers, by leveraging geometric representations. Query2box represents entities and queries as hyper-rectangles (boxes) in a continuous vector space, allowing for intuitive and scalable reasoning.  

The proposed approach employs a neural model to learn embeddings for entities and queries, enabling the system to handle multi-hop reasoning tasks effectively. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that Query2box outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and scalability, particularly for complex queries involving multiple logical operations. The framework also provides interpretable results by visualizing the geometric relationships between queries and entities.  

The key contribution of this research lies in its ability to bridge the gap between symbolic reasoning and neural-based approaches, offering a robust and efficient solution for querying KGs. Query2box has potential applications in areas such as recommendation systems, question answering, and semantic search, where reasoning over structured data is critical.  

**Keywords**: Knowledge Graphs, Box Embeddings, Logical Reasoning, Vector Space Models, Neural Networks, Query Processing.
