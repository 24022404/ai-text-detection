In the context of Neural Architecture Search (NAS), selecting an optimal architecture is crucial for enhancing model performance and efficiency. This paper aims to address the challenges of architecture selection within Differentiable NAS by introducing a novel approach that re-evaluates the decision-making process involved. Our method leverages a refined search strategy that incorporates advanced sampling techniques and adaptive learning rates to enhance the exploration of architecture landscapes. This approach is designed to optimize the balance between exploration and exploitation, reducing the chances of suboptimal convergence on local minima. The experimental results demonstrate that our framework outperforms existing differentiable NAS methods in terms of both computational efficiency and the accuracy of derived models across standard benchmarks, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. The findings suggest that our method can efficiently discover high-performance architectures with reduced computational resources. Our contributions include the introduction of a more robust architecture selection criterion and a scalable framework applicable to a variety of NAS scenarios. This research paves the way for more efficient NAS processes, potentially benefiting a wide range of applications, from autonomous vehicles to real-time data analysis. Future work will explore dynamic adaptation mechanisms further to enhance the adaptability of architecture selection in diverse computational environments. Keywords: Differentiable NAS, architecture selection, neural networks, computational efficiency, CIFAR-10, ImageNet.
