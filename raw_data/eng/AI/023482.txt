Abstract:

The growing complexity of machine learning (ML) models demands efficient compilation frameworks to optimize both model performance and resource utilization across diverse hardware platforms. Existing graph optimizers within ML compilers often suffer from limited transferability, as their optimization rules or strategies are designed for specific frameworks or hardware vendors. This paper introduces a novel approach for developing transferable graph optimizers that can be seamlessly integrated across various ML compiler infrastructures. Our method leverages a modular design and intermediate representation (IR)-agnostic transformation techniques, enabling optimizations to be abstracted from underlying graph structures. We implemented our approach within prominent ML compilers and conducted extensive experiments on representative workloads, including natural language processing and computer vision models. Results demonstrate that our transferable optimizers achieve comparable or superior performance to native, framework-specific optimizers, while significantly reducing development and adaptation effort when targeting new platforms. The proposed framework promotes rapid deployment of optimizations and facilitates cross-pollination of optimization strategies among different compiler ecosystems. Our contributions pave the way toward more unified and scalable ML compilation pipelines, with implications for accelerating innovation in AI deployment. Keywords: machine learning compiler, graph optimizer, transferability, intermediate representation, compiler infrastructure, optimization strategies, AI deployment.
