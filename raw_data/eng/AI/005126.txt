Zero-shot learning (ZSL) aims to enable models to recognize unseen classes by leveraging semantic descriptions, such as attributes or textual embeddings, without requiring labeled examples. However, existing ZSL methods often struggle with domain shift and overfitting to seen classes, limiting their generalization capabilities. This paper introduces a novel approach, Class Description Regularization (CDR), which enhances zero-shot learning by explicitly regularizing the model to align with class-specific semantic descriptions during training. CDR integrates a regularization term into the loss function, ensuring that the learned feature representations are semantically consistent with the class descriptions, thereby improving generalization to unseen classes. We evaluate our approach on benchmark datasets, including AWA2, CUB, and SUN, and demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art ZSL methods. Experimental results show that CDR achieves superior performance in both conventional and generalized zero-shot learning settings, with notable gains in accuracy and robustness. The proposed method not only addresses the domain shift problem but also provides a flexible framework for incorporating diverse semantic information. This work contributes to advancing zero-shot learning by offering a simple yet effective regularization technique that can be easily integrated into existing ZSL pipelines.  

**Keywords**: Zero-shot learning, class description regularization, domain shift, semantic alignment, generalization, unseen classes.
