Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have shown promise in modeling complex data distributions, but often struggle with heavy-tailed data due to limitations in capturing extreme values. This paper introduces Pareto GAN, a novel approach that extends the representational power of GANs to effectively model heavy-tailed distributions. Pareto GAN incorporates a Pareto tail fitting mechanism within the discriminator, enabling it to better distinguish between real and generated data in the tails of the distribution. This enhances the generator's ability to produce samples that accurately reflect the tail behavior. Experimental results on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that Pareto GAN significantly outperforms existing GAN variants in capturing heavy-tailed characteristics, as measured by metrics such as tail index estimation and goodness-of-fit tests. The improved modeling of heavy-tailed distributions enables more realistic data generation and can benefit downstream tasks sensitive to tail behavior. Pareto GAN offers a valuable tool for applications involving data with extreme values, such as finance, climate modeling, and anomaly detection.
Keywords: GAN, generative adversarial networks, heavy-tailed distributions, Pareto distribution, deep learning, data generation.
