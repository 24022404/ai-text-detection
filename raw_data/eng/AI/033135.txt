This paper addresses the challenge of semantic colorization in digital images, focusing on the accurate and context-aware assignment of colors to grayscale or pixelated inputs. The objective is to develop a robust framework that leverages semantic understanding to produce realistic and visually coherent colorized outputs. To achieve this, we propose a novel deep learning-based approach that integrates convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with semantic segmentation techniques. Our method utilizes a multi-stage pipeline, where semantic labels are first predicted to guide the colorization process, ensuring that colors are applied in a contextually meaningful manner. The model is trained on a diverse dataset of annotated images, enabling it to generalize across various domains and scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in colorization accuracy and visual quality compared to existing methods, particularly in complex scenes with multiple objects and textures. The proposed approach also exhibits enhanced robustness to pixelation artifacts, making it suitable for applications in image restoration and enhancement. This research contributes to the field of computer vision by introducing a semantically aware colorization framework that bridges the gap between low-level pixel manipulation and high-level contextual understanding. Potential applications include digital art, historical image restoration, and automated image processing pipelines.  

**Keywords**: semantic colorization, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, image restoration, semantic segmentation, pixelation.
