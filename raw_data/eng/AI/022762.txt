Gaze estimation is a critical task in human-computer interaction, with applications ranging from assistive technologies to user behavior analysis. However, existing methods often struggle with cross-person generalization due to significant individual differences in eye anatomy and gaze behavior. This paper introduces **The Story in Your Eyes (TSYE)**, a novel individual-difference-aware model designed to improve cross-person gaze estimation accuracy. Our approach leverages a multi-branch neural network architecture that explicitly models person-specific features while maintaining a shared representation for generalizability. The model incorporates adaptive feature extraction and domain adaptation techniques to address variability in eye shapes, head poses, and environmental conditions. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that TSYE achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods by 12.3% in cross-person gaze estimation accuracy. Furthermore, ablation studies highlight the contributions of individual components, such as the adaptive feature fusion module and the domain alignment mechanism. The results suggest that TSYE not only enhances gaze estimation robustness but also provides a scalable framework for personalized human-computer interaction systems. This work advances the field by offering a practical solution to the challenges of individual variability, paving the way for more accurate and inclusive gaze-based applications.  

**Keywords**: Gaze estimation, cross-person generalization, individual differences, adaptive feature extraction, domain adaptation, human-computer interaction.
