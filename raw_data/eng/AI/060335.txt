System throughput metrics—such as the median, 97.5th percentile, 99th percentile, average, and standard deviation—provide deep insights into the operational characteristics of modern network or application systems. The median value gives a sense of the most common experience, revealing what a ‘typical’ request might encounter under normal circumstances. Examining higher percentiles like the 97.5th and 99th illuminates the experiences of the slowest requests, which is crucial for understanding worst-case scenarios and ensuring quality of service even at the edge cases. Average throughput, meanwhile, informs about the overall system efficiency, reflecting how well the infrastructure can serve requests in aggregate. The standard deviation further enriches this picture by quantifying variability and consistency, helping to distinguish between systems that perform reliably and those with sporadic performance spikes. By systematically monitoring all these metrics across various connection types, administrators can more accurately optimize system performance, promptly identify and address potential bottlenecks, and plan strategically for scaling resources to anticipate and meet rising user demand.
