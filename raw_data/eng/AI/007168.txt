Accurate people counting in retail environments is essential for optimizing operations, enhancing customer experiences, and enabling data-driven business decisions. This paper proposes LRCN-RetailNet, a novel recurrent neural network architecture tailored for precise people counting in complex retail scenarios characterized by occlusions, dynamic crowds, and varied lighting conditions. The model combines a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) backbone for robust spatial feature extraction with a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) layer to capture temporal dependencies between video frames. We evaluate LRCN-RetailNet on several publicly available retail surveillance datasets as well as a newly curated dataset with annotated sequences from actual retail stores. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms traditional detection-based methods and baseline neural architectures, achieving higher accuracy with reduced false positives and improved handling of overlapping individuals. The architecture’s ability to leverage sequential information enables effective differentiation between individual and group movements, further enhancing counting reliability. This work establishes a foundation for smarter retail analytics and offers potential applications in occupancy monitoring, queue management, and resource allocation. Keywords: people counting, recurrent neural networks, LSTM, computer vision, retail analytics, deep learning.
