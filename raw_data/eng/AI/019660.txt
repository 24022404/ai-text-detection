This paper addresses the problem of depth estimation in multi-view settings, a critical component for applications in 3D reconstruction and autonomous navigation. Current depth estimation methods often struggle with accurately capturing depth information across views, especially in dynamic environments with varying temporal elements.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel algorithm, Epipolar Spatio-Temporal Networks (ESTN), designed to leverage multi-view geometry and temporal consistency for improved depth estimation. Our approach integrates epipolar geometry constraints with a spatio-temporal network architecture, enabling the effective combination of spatial and temporal information from multiple views. The ESTN model is trained using a specialized loss function that enforces consistency across temporal sequences, enhancing the robustness of depth predictions.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our ESTN model outperforms existing methodologies in both accuracy and computational efficiency. Comparative evaluations conducted on standard datasets show substantial improvements in depth accuracy, particularly in complex scenes with moving objects and challenging lighting conditions. Our approach also exhibits superior performance in maintaining temporal coherence across video frames, which is a notable advancement in the field.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of Epipolar Spatio-Temporal Networks marks a significant step forward in multi-view depth estimation, providing a powerful tool for 3D scene understanding. The proposed method's ability to maintain consistency and accuracy across varying conditions makes it particularly suitable for real-time applications in autonomous vehicles and augmented reality systems. These findings contribute to the advancement of depth estimation techniques and highlight the potential for further research into spatio-temporal integration in computer vision. 

Keywords: Multi-view depth estimation, epipolar geometry, spatio-temporal networks, 3D reconstruction, autonomous navigation, temporal coherence.
