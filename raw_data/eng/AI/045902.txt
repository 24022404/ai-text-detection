Object detection and pose estimation are critical tasks in computer vision, with applications ranging from robotics to augmented reality. This paper presents a comparative study of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for jointly addressing these tasks, aiming to identify the most effective architectures and methodologies. We evaluate several state-of-the-art CNN-based models, including single-stage and multi-stage approaches, focusing on their ability to simultaneously detect objects and estimate their poses in complex environments. Our experiments are conducted on benchmark datasets such as COCO and Pascal3D+, using metrics like mean Average Precision (mAP) and pose estimation accuracy. Results demonstrate that multi-stage models, particularly those integrating attention mechanisms, achieve superior performance in both detection and pose estimation tasks compared to single-stage counterparts. Additionally, we highlight the trade-offs between computational efficiency and accuracy, providing insights into model selection for real-time applications. This study contributes to the field by offering a comprehensive analysis of CNN-based approaches, identifying key factors influencing performance, and proposing directions for future research. The findings have practical implications for developing robust vision systems in dynamic and resource-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: Convolutional Neural Networks, object detection, pose estimation, computer vision, attention mechanisms, real-time systems.
