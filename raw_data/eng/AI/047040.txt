This paper addresses the challenge of accurately forecasting long-term trends in time series data, a critical task in fields such as finance, climatology, and operations management. Traditional forecasting models often struggle to capture nonlinear patterns and complex temporal dependencies inherent in long-term data. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel approach utilizing Self-Organizing Maps (SOMs), a type of unsupervised neural network known for their powerful clustering and visualization capabilities. Our method involves training SOMs to detect underlying structures and intrinsic relationships within multivariate time series, enabling more robust trend identification over extended horizons. We evaluate the performance of the proposed approach against established forecasting techniques using benchmark datasets from diverse application domains. Experimental results demonstrate that SOM-based models outperform conventional methods in detecting and predicting long-term trends, showing increased accuracy and stability, particularly in highly nonlinear and nonstationary environments. This study highlights the potential of self-organizing neural architectures for advancing the state of time series analysis, offering a scalable and interpretable solution for extracting actionable insights from complex temporal data. Key contributions include the integration of SOMs into the long-term forecasting framework and empirical evidence of their superior trend detection capabilities. Keywords: time series forecasting, long-term trends, Self-Organizing Maps, neural networks, unsupervised learning, nonlinear dynamics.
