The Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) is a widely used metric for measuring the dissimilarity between two distributions, with applications in image processing, machine learning, and data analysis. However, its computational complexity often limits its practicality, especially for large-scale datasets. This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently approximating EMD while maintaining accuracy, focusing on low-complexity, data-parallel solutions. We propose a novel approximation framework that leverages parallel computing architectures to significantly reduce computational overhead. Our approach combines optimized histogram binning techniques with a lightweight iterative algorithm, enabling scalable and efficient EMD computation. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves a balance between accuracy and speed, outperforming traditional EMD implementations by up to 40% in runtime without compromising on precision. Furthermore, the proposed framework is highly adaptable to modern GPU and multi-core CPU environments, making it suitable for real-world applications. This work contributes a practical, scalable solution for EMD approximation, opening new possibilities for its use in large-scale data analysis and machine learning tasks.  

**Keywords**: Earth Mover's Distance, data-parallel computing, low-complexity algorithms, histogram binning, GPU acceleration, machine learning.
