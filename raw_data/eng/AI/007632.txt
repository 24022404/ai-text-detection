This paper addresses the challenge of multi-camera association, a crucial task in various computer vision applications. The objective is to develop an efficient and accurate method for associating objects across multiple cameras with non-overlapping fields of view. To achieve this, we propose a novel approach that leverages localization techniques to establish correspondences between objects detected in different cameras. Our method utilizes a combination of visual features and spatial information to compute the likelihood of association between objects. The results show significant improvement in association accuracy and efficiency compared to existing methods. Key findings include the ability to handle large camera networks and robustness to variations in object appearance and motion. The proposed approach has important implications for various applications, including surveillance, tracking, and autonomous systems. By highlighting the benefits of integrating localization with multi-camera association, this research contributes to the development of more effective and scalable computer vision systems, with relevant keywords including multi-camera tracking, object association, localization, and computer vision.
