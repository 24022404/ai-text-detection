Human parsing, the process of segmenting and classifying parts of a human body in images, is a challenging problem due to the variability in human poses, appearances, and occlusions. This paper presents a novel self-learning framework with a rectification strategy to improve human parsing accuracy. The objective of our research is to address the limitations in existing models which often require extensive labeled datasets and struggle with noisy inputs. Our approach leverages a self-learning mechanism that autonomously refines its parsing over iterations. Coupled with a rectification strategy, the model effectively identifies and corrects errors in segmentation outputs even when initial predictions are imprecise. We employ a robust deep learning architecture enhanced with adversarial networks that facilitate this iterative refinement process. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in parsing accuracy across several benchmark datasets, with our model outperforming existing methods in both accuracy and efficiency. The proposed strategy not only reduces the dependency on labeled data but also enhances model robustness against variable input conditions. Our findings suggest broader applications in fields such as autonomous driving, augmented reality, and surveillance systems, where precise human parsing is critical. Through this research, we contribute a unique approach that advances the capabilities of human parsing models, offering scalable and effective solutions in computer vision. Keywords: human parsing, self-learning, rectification strategy, segmentation, deep learning, adversarial networks.
