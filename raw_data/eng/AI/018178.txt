The proliferation of sophisticated video face manipulation techniques, such as deepfakes, poses significant challenges to digital security and information integrity. Our research addresses the urgent need for robust and reliable methods to detect these manipulated videos, ensuring the protection of individuals and institutions against potential misuse.

Methods/Approach: This study introduces an innovative approach leveraging an ensemble of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to enhance the detection accuracy of video face manipulations. By integrating multiple CNN architectures, we capitalize on their unique strengths to improve model generalization and robustness. Each CNN in the ensemble is optimized with diverse input preprocessing techniques and network configurations to capture subtle artifacts introduced during face manipulation processes.

Results/Findings: The proposed ensemble method demonstrates superior performance compared to individual CNN models and existing state-of-the-art detectors. Evaluation on benchmark datasets reveals a notable increase in detection accuracy, with our method achieving precision and recall rates that surpass current standards. Moreover, the system shows strong resilience to various manipulation techniques and compressions, maintaining high performance across different video formats and resolutions.

Conclusion/Implications: Our research presents a significant advancement in the field of video manipulation detection, offering a practical and scalable solution for real-time applications. The ensemble of CNNs not only enhances detection accuracy but also provides a framework adaptable to emerging manipulation techniques. This work paves the way for future studies to refine and build upon our approach, aiming at securing digital media against evolving threats. Key Keywords: Deepfake Detection, Convolutional Neural Networks, Ensemble Learning, Video Analysis, Media Security.
