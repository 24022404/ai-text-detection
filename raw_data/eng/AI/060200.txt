This paper introduces a novel approach to language inference using multi-head automata trained via reinforcement learning. The objective is to develop an efficient and scalable method for inferring formal languages from limited data. Our method employs a multi-head automaton, allowing parallel exploration of different grammar rules, and optimizes its behavior using a reinforcement learning framework. This framework rewards automata that correctly classify strings as belonging or not belonging to the target language. We evaluated our approach on a range of benchmark language inference tasks, demonstrating superior performance compared to traditional automata learning algorithms and achieving comparable results with neural network based approaches, especially in scenarios with sparse training data. Key findings indicate that the multi-head architecture coupled with reinforcement learning enables effective exploration of the search space, leading to more accurate language inference. This research contributes a new perspective on grammar induction, offering potential applications in compiler design, program synthesis, and natural language processing. Keywords: Language Inference, Automata Learning, Reinforcement Learning, Multi-head Automaton, Grammar Induction.
