This paper presents an in-depth analysis of autoencoders for representation learning, a crucial aspect of deep learning. The objective is to explore the fundamentals, applications, and challenges of autoencoders in various learning tasks. We employ a range of autoencoder architectures, including variational autoencoders and denoising autoencoders, to demonstrate their effectiveness in dimensionality reduction, anomaly detection, and feature learning. Our results show that autoencoders can learn compact and informative representations, outperforming traditional methods in several case studies. Furthermore, we investigate the explainability of autoencoders, highlighting techniques to interpret and visualize the learned representations. Despite their potential, autoencoders pose challenges, such as training instability and mode collapse, which we discuss and address through proposed solutions. This research contributes to the understanding of autoencoders in representation learning, providing insights into their capabilities, limitations, and applications in areas like computer vision, natural language processing, and recommender systems. Key aspects of this work include autoencoder architectures, representation learning, deep learning, explainability, and dimensionality reduction, making it a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners in the field of artificial intelligence and machine learning.
