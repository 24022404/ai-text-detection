Graph Convolutional Neural Networks (GCNs) have demonstrated significant potential in various graph-based learning tasks. However, training deeper GCNs remains a challenge due to issues such as over-smoothing and gradient vanishing. This paper investigates the impact of different training strategies on the performance of deeper GCN architectures. We propose a novel training regime incorporating layer-wise learning rate adjustment and a specialized regularization technique to mitigate over-smoothing. Our approach enables the successful training of GCNs with significantly increased depth, leading to improved performance on node classification and graph classification benchmarks. Empirical results on datasets such as Cora, Citeseer, and PubMed demonstrate that our training strategy unlocks the potential of deeper GCNs, achieving state-of-the-art results compared to existing shallow and deep GCN models. The findings suggest that careful training procedure design is crucial for realizing the benefits of depth in GCNs, offering new avenues for exploring more complex graph representations. Keywords: Graph Convolutional Networks, Deep Learning, Graph Neural Networks, Training Strategies, Over-smoothing, Regularization.
