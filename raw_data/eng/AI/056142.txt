This paper addresses the challenge of 3D object detection from RGB-D data, focusing on the development of a transferable semi-supervised approach. The objective is to improve detection accuracy while reducing the need for large amounts of labeled training data. Our method leverages a combination of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and graph-based techniques to learn robust features from RGB-D data. The approach involves a semi-supervised framework that utilizes both labeled and unlabeled data to enhance model generalizability. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. Key findings include improved detection accuracy, especially in scenarios with limited labeled data, and the ability to transfer knowledge across different domains and datasets. The proposed approach contributes to the field of 3D object detection by providing a flexible and efficient solution for real-world applications, such as robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented reality. Our research highlights the importance of semi-supervised learning and transferability in 3D vision tasks, with relevant keywords including RGB-D data, 3D object detection, semi-supervised learning, transfer learning, and deep learning.
