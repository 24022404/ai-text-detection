This paper addresses the challenge of generating "Doppelganger Graphs"—graph structures that closely resemble a given source graph in terms of key structural properties, yet remain distinctly different to prevent direct identification or one-to-one mapping. The objective is to facilitate applications such as privacy-preserving data sharing, graph anonymization, and robust benchmarking for graph algorithms. We propose a novel framework that systematically analyzes the target graph’s features, including degree distribution, clustering coefficient, and centrality measures, and then generates a new graph preserving these characteristics while introducing controlled alterations at both local and global levels. The methodology employs a hybrid approach that combines probabilistic graph generation models with iterative optimization techniques to balance resemblance and distinction metrics. Experimental evaluations on social, biological, and communication network datasets demonstrate that the generated doppelganger graphs maintain high fidelity in structural resemblance (over 95% similarity in key metrics) while achieving quantifiable distinction measures, outperforming standard random and synthetic graph generation baselines. Our findings reveal the practical utility of Doppelganger Graphs in scenarios requiring graph data obfuscation without compromising analytical utility. This work provides a foundation for further research in secure graph generation, privacy-aware analytics, and adversarial benchmarking. Key keywords: doppelganger graph, graph generation, graph similarity, anonymization, privacy-preserving, network analysis.
