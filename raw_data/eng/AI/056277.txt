This paper investigates the extent to which Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) encode data augmentations, a crucial aspect of deep learning that enhances model robustness and generalization. Our objective is to analyze the impact of various data augmentation techniques on the internal representations learned by CNNs. We employ a range of methods, including visualization techniques and feature importance analysis, to examine the encoding of augmented data in CNNs. Our results show that CNNs do indeed encode data augmentations, but the extent of encoding varies depending on the type and intensity of augmentation. Notably, our findings indicate that CNNs tend to prioritize learning augmentations that are most relevant to the task at hand. This research contributes to the understanding of how CNNs process and represent augmented data, with implications for the design of more effective data augmentation strategies and improved model generalization. Key keywords: CNNs, data augmentations, deep learning, representation learning, computer vision.
