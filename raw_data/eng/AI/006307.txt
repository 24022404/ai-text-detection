This paper addresses the challenge of stochastic image-to-video synthesis, a critical task in computer vision with applications in video generation, simulation, and content creation. We propose a novel framework leveraging conditional invertible neural networks (cINNs) to model the inherent uncertainty and variability in video sequences from a single input image. Unlike deterministic approaches, our method captures the probabilistic nature of video synthesis, enabling the generation of diverse and realistic video outputs. The cINN architecture is trained on a large-scale dataset to learn the conditional distribution of video frames given an input image, ensuring both temporal coherence and visual fidelity. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of diversity, quality, and computational efficiency. Key metrics, including Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) and perceptual quality scores, highlight significant improvements over state-of-the-art techniques. The proposed framework also exhibits robustness across various domains, such as natural scenes and human motion. This work contributes to advancing stochastic video generation by introducing a scalable and interpretable model, with potential applications in augmented reality, autonomous systems, and creative media production.  

**Keywords**: image-to-video synthesis, conditional invertible neural networks (cINNs), stochastic modeling, video generation, computer vision.
