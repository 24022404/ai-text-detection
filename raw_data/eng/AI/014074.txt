In the realm of video surveillance and security system enhancement, cross-domain person re-identification remains a significant challenge due to variations in camera angles, lighting, and environmental conditions. This study introduces an innovative approach, the End-to-End Domain Adaptive Attention Network (EADAAN), specifically designed to address these challenges in cross-domain scenarios.

Methods/Approach: The proposed EADAAN employs a robust domain adaptive strategy, utilizing attention mechanisms to dynamically adjust and focus on critical features across domains. By integrating domain adaptation techniques and leveraging attention networks, the model learns transferable representations that are robust to domain shifts without requiring extensive labeled target domain data.

Results/Findings: Extensive experimentation across multiple datasets demonstrates that the EADAAN significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and robustness. Key findings illustrate its superior adaptability to new environments, achieving higher person re-identification rates even in the presence of substantial domain discrepancies.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of the EADAAN marks a pivotal advancement in the field of cross-domain person re-identification by effectively narrowing the domain gap through an attention-driven framework. This approach not only enhances the accuracy of person re-identification in diverse domain settings but also reduces the dependence on large annotated datasets, paving the way for broader applications in security and surveillance systems. Key keywords include person re-identification, domain adaptation, attention network, cross-domain, and surveillance.
