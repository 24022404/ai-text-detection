**Abstract**

Reward shaping has emerged as an effective method to accelerate reinforcement learning by providing additional guidance to agents beyond primary task rewards. However, inappropriate shaping rewards can mislead learning processes and limit policy optimality. This paper introduces a novel learning-based framework for reward shaping, wherein agents automatically learn to utilize shaping rewards effectively during training. Our approach leverages meta-learning techniques to adaptively integrate shaping signals, allowing agents to discern useful auxiliary rewards from detrimental ones. We evaluate our method on several benchmark environments in both discrete and continuous action spaces, comparing its performance to traditional hand-crafted shaping strategies and baseline reinforcement learning algorithms. Experimental results demonstrate that agents equipped with our adaptive reward shaping mechanism achieve faster convergence and superior task performance, while maintaining robustness against poorly designed shaping signals. The findings highlight the advantages of learning to utilize shaping rewards dynamically, offering improved generalization across unseen tasks. This work advances the development of autonomous reward design and has potential applications in complex, real-world reinforcement learning scenarios. **Keywords:** reward shaping, reinforcement learning, meta-learning, adaptive agents, policy optimization.
