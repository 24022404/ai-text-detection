Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI) provides rich, high-dimensional data for understanding brain activity, yet accurately modelling the complex spatio-temporal relationships inherent in this data remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces a Dynamic Adaptive Spatio-temporal Graph Convolution (DAST-GC) framework designed to effectively capture both spatial and temporal dependencies in fMRI signals. The proposed approach constructs dynamic graphs that adaptively reflect region-wise brain connectivity, where graph convolutional layers are combined with temporal modelling strategies to jointly process neural activity across both dimensions. Our method automatically learns the evolving patterns of connectivity, avoiding the limitations of fixed or static graph structures commonly used in prior work. Extensive experiments conducted on benchmark fMRI datasets demonstrate that DAST-GC outperforms existing graph-based and deep learning methods in brain state classification and anomaly detection tasks, achieving higher accuracy and improved robustness to noise. The results highlight the capability of dynamic graph convolutional networks to more precisely characterize neural spatio-temporal dynamics. This research advances the modelling of fMRI data, providing deeper insights into brain function and supporting potential clinical and cognitive neuroscience applications. Keywords: fMRI, spatio-temporal modelling, dynamic graph convolution, adaptive networks, brain connectivity, neural data analysis.
