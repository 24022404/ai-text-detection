Distributed learning has become a cornerstone for scaling machine learning models across large datasets and computational resources. However, achieving efficient scalability while maintaining model accuracy remains a significant challenge, particularly when using large batch sizes and asynchronous updates. This paper investigates the critical parameters that influence the performance of scalable distributed learning systems under these conditions. We propose a systematic framework to analyze the trade-offs between batch size, update frequency, and communication overhead in distributed environments. Our approach leverages a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical evaluation to identify optimal configurations for minimizing training time without compromising model convergence. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our framework achieves up to a 30% reduction in training time compared to traditional synchronous methods, while maintaining competitive accuracy. Furthermore, we highlight the importance of adaptive learning rate strategies and gradient compression techniques in mitigating the challenges of large-batch training. The findings provide actionable insights for designing scalable distributed learning systems, particularly in resource-constrained environments. This research contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in distributed machine learning by offering a practical guide for balancing efficiency and performance in large-scale training scenarios.  

**Keywords**: distributed learning, large batch training, asynchronous updates, scalability, machine learning, optimization.
