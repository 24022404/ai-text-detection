This paper presents TDIOT, a novel approach for deep video object tracking that leverages target-driven inference to enhance tracking accuracy and efficiency. The objective is to address the challenges of object tracking in complex and dynamic environments, where existing methods often struggle with occlusions, scale variations, and appearance changes. Our approach employs a deep learning-based framework that integrates a target-driven module to focus on the object of interest, while suppressing background distractions. The method utilizes a combination of convolutional neural networks and recurrent neural networks to model the temporal and spatial relationships between objects. Experimental results demonstrate that TDIOT outperforms state-of-the-art tracking methods, achieving significant improvements in terms of precision, recall, and speed. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a target-driven inference mechanism, which enables more accurate and robust object tracking, and the development of a lightweight and efficient tracking architecture. The proposed approach has potential applications in various fields, including surveillance, autonomous driving, and robotics, and can be extended to other computer vision tasks, such as object detection and segmentation. Key keywords: deep learning, object tracking, target-driven inference, computer vision, video analysis.
