This paper addresses the challenge of transferring robot learning from simulation to real-world environments, focusing on tasks with variable length input sequences. A novel approach is presented that combines domain randomization with a recurrent neural network (RNN) architecture capable of processing inputs of varying lengths. The method utilizes a curriculum learning strategy during simulation training, gradually increasing the complexity and variability of the simulated environment to enhance robustness. The trained policy is then transferred to a physical robot without further fine-tuning. Experimental results demonstrate successful transfer on a manipulation task involving stacking objects with differing sizes and arrangements. Compared to fixed-length input methods, the proposed approach exhibits improved generalization and performance in real-world scenarios, achieving a 30% increase in task completion rate. This work contributes a practical solution for deploying robot learning models trained in simulation to real-world applications, particularly those involving dynamic and unstructured environments.

**Keywords:** Sim-to-Real Transfer, Robot Learning, Domain Randomization, Recurrent Neural Networks, Variable Length Inputs, Curriculum Learning, Manipulation.
