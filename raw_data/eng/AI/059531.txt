This paper presents a novel approach for real-time dense 3D reconstruction by integrating Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) with multi-spectral photometric stereo. The objective is to address the limitations of existing 3D reconstruction methods, which often struggle with accuracy and computational efficiency in dynamic environments. Our proposed method leverages the strengths of SLAM for robust pose estimation and scene mapping, combined with multi-spectral photometric stereo to enhance surface detail recovery under varying lighting conditions. A hybrid framework is introduced, where SLAM provides the global structure, and photometric stereo refines local geometry by exploiting multi-spectral data to infer surface normals and albedo. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed system achieves real-time performance with significantly improved reconstruction accuracy compared to traditional methods. Key findings include a 25% reduction in geometric error and a 30% improvement in texture fidelity, validated on benchmark datasets. The integration of multi-spectral photometric stereo also enables robust performance in challenging lighting scenarios, such as low-light or high-contrast environments. This research contributes a scalable and efficient solution for applications in robotics, augmented reality, and autonomous navigation, where real-time dense 3D reconstruction is critical. The proposed framework highlights the potential of combining SLAM with advanced photometric techniques to push the boundaries of 3D perception systems.  

**Keywords**: SLAM, photometric stereo, 3D reconstruction, multi-spectral imaging, real-time systems, surface normals, robotics, augmented reality.
