This paper proposes RRLFSOR, a novel self-supervised learning strategy designed to enhance the efficiency and performance of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs). The objective is to address the limitations of existing supervised methods, which often rely on large amounts of labeled data. RRLFSOR leverages a robust representation learning framework, combining graph reconstruction and feature smoothing techniques to learn informative node representations. The approach utilizes a multi-task learning paradigm, where the model is trained to reconstruct the graph structure and preserve local node features simultaneously. Experimental results demonstrate that RRLFSOR outperforms state-of-the-art self-supervised methods on several benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in node classification and graph clustering tasks. The proposed strategy contributes to the development of more efficient and effective GCNs, with potential applications in social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics. Key contributions include the introduction of a robust self-supervised learning framework, improved node representation learning, and enhanced graph convolutional network performance. Relevant keywords: Graph Convolutional Networks, Self-Supervised Learning, Representation Learning, Graph Reconstruction, Feature Smoothing.
