Accurate classification of Local Climate Zones (LCZs) is critical for urban climate studies and sustainable city planning. However, the lack of high-quality training samples often limits the performance of deep learning models in LCZ classification tasks. This study addresses this challenge by developing a robust framework for generating high-quality training samples tailored to the unique urban and rural landscapes of Korea. We propose a multi-stage approach that integrates satellite imagery, geographic information systems (GIS), and domain expert validation to ensure the reliability and representativeness of the training data. A deep convolutional neural network (CNN) is then trained using these samples to classify LCZs across diverse regions in Korea. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in classification accuracy compared to existing methods, with a particular emphasis on distinguishing complex urban structures and rural areas. The proposed framework not only enhances the performance of LCZ classification models but also provides a scalable solution for other regions with similar challenges. This research contributes to advancing urban climate studies by enabling more precise and reliable LCZ mapping, which is essential for informed decision-making in urban planning and climate adaptation strategies.  

**Keywords**: Local Climate Zones (LCZs), deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), satellite imagery, urban climate, Korea, training sample generation.
