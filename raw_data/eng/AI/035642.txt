Instance segmentation on 3D point clouds is a critical task in computer vision, enabling applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality. However, existing approaches often rely on task definitions and evaluation metrics that may not fully capture the complexities of real-world scenarios. This paper rethinks the task formulation and evaluation framework for 3D instance segmentation, proposing a more robust and practical paradigm. We introduce a novel methodology that leverages advanced deep learning architectures, including graph neural networks and transformer-based models, to better handle the sparse and unstructured nature of point cloud data. Our approach emphasizes the importance of semantic consistency and spatial coherence in segmentation results, addressing limitations in current methods. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in segmentation accuracy and robustness, outperforming state-of-the-art techniques by a notable margin. Furthermore, we propose a revised set of evaluation metrics that better align with real-world performance requirements, such as handling occlusions and varying point densities. The findings of this study provide valuable insights for future research and practical applications, highlighting the need for task and metric redefinition to advance the field of 3D instance segmentation.  

**Keywords**: 3D point clouds, instance segmentation, deep learning, graph neural networks, transformer models, evaluation metrics.
