This study explores the application of transfer learning techniques for melanoma detection, focusing on the ISIC 2017 Skin Lesion Classification Challenge. The objective is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of automated skin lesion classification, a critical task in early melanoma diagnosis. We employ state-of-the-art deep learning architectures, including pre-trained convolutional neural networks (CNNs) such as ResNet and Inception, fine-tuned on the ISIC 2017 dataset. The approach leverages transfer learning to adapt models trained on large-scale image datasets to the specific domain of dermatological images, addressing challenges such as limited labeled data and class imbalance. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in classification performance, achieving competitive accuracy and sensitivity metrics compared to baseline methods. The proposed method outperforms traditional approaches by effectively capturing intricate patterns in skin lesion images, reducing false positives, and enhancing diagnostic reliability. This research highlights the potential of transfer learning in medical image analysis, offering a scalable and efficient solution for melanoma detection. The findings contribute to advancing computer-aided diagnosis systems, with implications for improving early detection rates and patient outcomes in dermatology.  

**Keywords**: Transfer learning, melanoma detection, skin lesion classification, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, ISIC 2017, medical image analysis.
