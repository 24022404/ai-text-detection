This paper introduces Ego-CNN, a novel approach to graph representation learning that leverages distributed, egocentric representations to detect critical structures in complex networks. The objective is to develop a method that can effectively identify key nodes and patterns in graphs, which is crucial for various applications such as social network analysis, traffic forecasting, and molecular biology. Ego-CNN employs a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture that operates on egocentric representations of graphs, where each node is represented by its local neighborhood. This approach enables the model to capture subtle patterns and relationships between nodes, leading to improved performance in critical structure detection. Experimental results demonstrate that Ego-CNN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in detecting critical structures, such as clusters, communities, and bottlenecks, in various real-world graphs. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of egocentric representations, the development of a distributed CNN architecture, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in critical structure detection. This work has significant implications for graph-based applications, including network analysis, recommendation systems, and graph-based machine learning. Key keywords: graph representation learning, egocentric representations, convolutional neural networks, critical structure detection, graph analysis.
