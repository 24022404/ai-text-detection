Time-lagged autoencoders (TLAEs) have emerged as a promising approach for identifying slow modes in dynamical systems, which are critical for understanding long-term system behavior. This study investigates the capabilities and limitations of TLAEs in extracting slow modes from high-dimensional, nonlinear systems. We propose a systematic framework to evaluate TLAE performance, focusing on their ability to disentangle slow and fast dynamics under varying noise levels and system complexities. Using synthetic and real-world datasets, we compare TLAEs with traditional methods such as time-lagged independent component analysis (TICA) and dynamic mode decomposition (DMD). Our results demonstrate that TLAEs excel in capturing nonlinear slow modes, particularly in systems with intricate temporal dependencies. However, we identify key limitations, including sensitivity to hyperparameter choices and challenges in scaling to ultra-high-dimensional data. The findings highlight the potential of TLAEs for applications in molecular dynamics, climate modeling, and other fields requiring robust slow mode discovery. This work provides actionable insights for optimizing TLAE architectures and paves the way for future advancements in dynamical systems analysis.  

**Keywords**: Time-lagged autoencoders, slow mode discovery, dynamical systems, nonlinear dynamics, dimensionality reduction, machine learning.
