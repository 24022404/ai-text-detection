Spectral clustering is a widely used technique for uncovering community structure in graphs; however, its effectiveness diminishes when applied to sparse graphs, which are common in real-world networks. This paper presents a unified framework for spectral clustering specifically designed to address the unique challenges posed by sparse graphs, including noise, degree heterogeneity, and disconnected components. The proposed framework leverages advanced normalization schemes, robust spectral embeddings, and regularization methods to improve cluster detection accuracy in low-degree regimes. Comprehensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms existing spectral clustering algorithms in terms of clustering quality and computational efficiency, particularly on large-scale sparse networks. The framework also provides theoretical guarantees for cluster recovery under sparse conditions. These results highlight the potential for our unified method to be adopted in a wide range of applications, such as social network analysis, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems, where data naturally exhibits sparsity. Keywords: spectral clustering, sparse graphs, community detection, graph algorithms, network analysis, robust embeddings.
