Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for solving complex decision-making problems, yet the choice of learning rate policies remains a critical challenge in achieving optimal performance. This paper addresses the problem of designing adaptive learning rate policies that enhance the efficiency and stability of RL algorithms. We propose a novel framework that dynamically adjusts learning rates based on the agent's performance and environmental feedback, leveraging a combination of gradient-based optimization and meta-learning techniques. Our approach is evaluated on a range of benchmark environments, including discrete and continuous control tasks, and demonstrates significant improvements in convergence speed and final performance compared to traditional fixed and decay-based learning rate strategies. The results indicate that our method achieves up to a 30% reduction in training time while maintaining or surpassing the accuracy of state-of-the-art RL algorithms. This research contributes to the broader field of RL by providing a robust and scalable solution for learning rate adaptation, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and real-time decision-making scenarios. The proposed framework is generalizable and can be integrated into existing RL pipelines, offering a practical advancement for both researchers and practitioners.  

**Keywords**: reinforcement learning, learning rate policies, adaptive optimization, meta-learning, decision-making, autonomous systems.
