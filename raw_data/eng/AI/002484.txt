Skeleton-based action recognition has gained significant attention in the field of computer vision due to its robustness in modeling human movements. However, existing Graph Neural Network (GNN) approaches often rely on first-order node relationships, which can limit the ability to capture complex spatial and temporal dependencies inherent in human skeletal data. This paper proposes a novel method for fusing higher-order features within GNN architectures to enhance the representation of joint interactions for skeleton-based action recognition tasks. Our approach constructs higher-order adjacency matrices that model not only direct connections between joints but also multi-joint relationships, enabling the network to learn more comprehensive structural patterns. We design a hierarchical feature fusion module that systematically integrates information from various graph orders into unified node embeddings. Extensive experiments conducted on widely used benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves superior accuracy compared to state-of-the-art techniques, particularly in recognizing intricate and ambiguous actions. The results highlight the effectiveness of incorporating higher-order graph features for improved recognition performance. This work advances the development of robust action recognition systems and offers valuable insights for future research in graph-based human activity analysis. 

Keywords: skeleton-based action recognition, graph neural networks, higher-order features, spatial-temporal modeling, human activity analysis, feature fusion.
