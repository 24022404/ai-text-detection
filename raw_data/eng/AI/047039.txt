This paper explores the application of transfer learning in material classification using convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The objective is to address the challenge of accurately identifying materials in images, which is critical for applications in robotics, manufacturing, and quality control. Traditional methods often require extensive labeled datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to acquire. To overcome this limitation, we propose leveraging pre-trained CNN models, fine-tuned on a smaller dataset of material images. Our approach utilizes state-of-the-art architectures, such as ResNet and EfficientNet, and evaluates their performance on publicly available material datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that transfer learning significantly improves classification accuracy compared to training from scratch, achieving a top-1 accuracy of over 92% on benchmark datasets. Additionally, we analyze the impact of dataset size and fine-tuning strategies on model performance. The findings highlight the effectiveness of transfer learning in reducing data requirements while maintaining high accuracy. This research contributes to the field by providing a practical and efficient solution for material classification, with potential applications in industrial automation and computer vision systems.  

**Keywords**: transfer learning, material classification, convolutional neural networks, fine-tuning, computer vision, ResNet, EfficientNet.
