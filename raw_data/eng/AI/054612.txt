Objective: This paper investigates the finite-time behavior of linear two-time-scale stochastic approximation (SA) algorithms and introduces a novel restarting scheme to enhance their practical convergence. Two-time-scale SA methods underpin many adaptive control, reinforcement learning, and signal processing applications, yet their non-asymptotic performance remains poorly understood.

Methods: We focus on a canonical linear two-time-scale SA system with interdependent fast and slow recursions. Leveraging refined martingale concentration inequalities and a careful decoupling of the two update streams, we derive explicit high-probability bounds on the estimation error of both fast and slow components. Building on this analysis, we propose an adaptive restarting mechanism that periodically resets the fast iterate around the current slow estimate. This scheme is designed to mitigate error accumulation and capitalize on improved initialization, without requiring additional parameter tuning.

Results: Our theoretical results establish O(1/N) mean-square error rates for both components under standard Lipschitz and noise‐moment conditions, matching optimal rates for single-scale SA. The restarting scheme further reduces constant factors in the finite-sample bounds and yields up to a 30% speed-up in convergence in simulated linear quadratic regulator and least-squares temporal-difference learning tasks. Empirical studies confirm the tightness of our bounds and the robustness of the restarting strategy across noise variances.

Conclusion: This work provides the first comprehensive finite-time analysis for linear two-time-scale SA and demonstrates that strategic restarting can substantially accelerate convergence. The insights and tools developed here pave the way for more reliable and efficient multi-scale stochastic algorithms in control and learning.

Keywords: stochastic approximation, two-time-scale, finite-time analysis, restarting scheme, sample complexity, linear systems.
