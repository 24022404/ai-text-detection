This paper addresses the challenge of improving the convergence speed of algorithms used in Expectation Propagation (EP), a prominent method for approximate Bayesian inference. Traditional implementations of EP can be computationally demanding and slow to converge, posing limitations in practical applications, particularly in large-scale data environments.

Methods/Approach: We propose novel algorithmic strategies that enhance the convergence rate of EP. These strategies involve developing fast convergent algorithms that leverage adaptive learning rates and incorporate variance reduction techniques. Our approach ensures stability while maintaining the approximation accuracy inherent in EP methods.

Results/Findings: Experiments demonstrate that the proposed algorithms significantly reduce convergence times compared to existing methods, with no loss in accuracy. We benchmark our algorithms against standard EP implementations using various synthetic and real-world datasets. The results show consistent performance improvements, achieving faster convergence by up to 50% in some cases.

Conclusion/Implications: The newly developed fast convergent algorithms for EP provide a robust solution for practitioners seeking efficient Bayesian inference, particularly in scenarios involving large datasets or complex models. This advancement holds potential for broader applications in machine learning, data science, and AI, where rapid and precise probabilistic inference is crucial. Key insights from this work could lead to further enhancements in approximate inference techniques.

Keywords: Expectation Propagation, Bayesian Inference, Fast Convergent Algorithms, Variance Reduction, Adaptive Learning Rates, Machine Learning, Probabilistic Inference.
