The objective of this research is to develop an efficient method for gesture recognition in RGB videos by leveraging human body keypoints and dynamic time warping (DTW). Gesture recognition has significant implications in fields such as human-computer interaction and assistive technology, where accurate and real-time interpretation of gestures is critical.

Methods/Approach: We employ a novel approach that utilizes human body keypoints extracted from RGB video frames as the foundational data for recognizing gestures. The keypoints, which provide a detailed representation of the body's posture, are tracked over time to capture movement dynamics. Dynamic time warping, a well-established algorithm for measuring the similarity between temporal sequences, is then applied to align and compare these sequences against gesture templates.

Results/Findings: Our method demonstrates a high level of accuracy in recognizing a diverse set of gestures across varying conditions. Experimental results indicate a significant improvement in recognition rates compared to traditional image-based approaches. The system maintains robust performance even with occlusion or variations in lighting, highlighting its applicability in real-world scenarios. Furthermore, our approach shows remarkable efficiency in computational time, making it suitable for real-time applications.

Conclusion/Implications: This research advances the field of gesture recognition by introducing a method that combines body keypoints and dynamic time warping for precise and efficient recognition in RGB videos. The findings suggest potential applications in interactive gaming, virtual reality, and assistive devices for individuals with disabilities. By enabling more intuitive and natural interactions with technology, this work contributes to the evolution of user-friendly interfaces.

Key Keywords: Gesture Recognition, RGB Videos, Human Body Keypoints, Dynamic Time Warping, Human-Computer Interaction, Real-Time Applications.
