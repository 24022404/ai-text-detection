Predicting the trajectories of agents in dynamic environments is a crucial challenge with significant applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and surveillance. This paper introduces a novel approach called the Recursive Social Behavior Graph (RSBG) for trajectory prediction, designed to effectively capture complex social interactions among multiple agents over time. The proposed method models agents as nodes within a dynamic graph, where edges represent social relationships and influence. Our key innovation lies in the recursive update mechanism that allows the system to iteratively refine the representation of agent interactions by leveraging historical behavior and context information. 

To evaluate the effectiveness of RSBG, we implemented it with state-of-the-art recurrent neural network architectures and conducted experiments on widely used pedestrian trajectory datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that our model achieves higher prediction accuracy and better handles crowded scenarios when compared to existing baseline methods. Notably, the recursive mechanism improves the system's ability to anticipate future movements by dynamically adapting to changing group behaviors. 

In summary, this work provides a robust and scalable framework for multi-agent trajectory forecasting by incorporating recursive social context modeling. The proposed RSBG establishes a new benchmark for socially-aware trajectory prediction and shows promising potential for deployment in real-world intelligent transportation and human-robot interaction systems.

Keywords: trajectory prediction, social behavior, graph neural networks, recursive modeling, multi-agent systems, autonomous navigation.
