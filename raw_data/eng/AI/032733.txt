Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have become a dominant approach for representation learning on graphs, but their expressive power, or ability to distinguish different graph structures, remains a critical concern. This paper investigates the expressive power of GNNs, focusing on designing provably powerful architectures. We introduce a novel GNN framework, theoretically grounded in the Weisfeiler-Leman (WL) test, demonstrating its ability to achieve comparable expressive power to the 1-WL algorithm, a widely recognized benchmark for graph isomorphism testing. Our method enhances standard GNN message passing with learnable structural features and a refined aggregation scheme. We prove that the proposed framework can distinguish a broader range of graph structures than standard GNNs. Empirical evaluations on various graph classification benchmarks demonstrate that our architecture achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing GNNs in distinguishing complex graph structures while maintaining computational efficiency. This work contributes a theoretically sound and practically effective GNN architecture with provable expressive power, advancing the field of graph representation learning.
Keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Expressive Power, Weisfeiler-Leman Test, Graph Representation Learning, Graph Isomorphism.
