This paper proposes a novel approach to video action classification, leveraging mutual modality learning to enhance the accuracy and robustness of existing methods. The objective is to improve the understanding and recognition of human actions in videos by effectively fusing and learning from multiple modalities, such as RGB, optical flow, and audio. Our approach employs a multi-stream framework, where each stream focuses on a specific modality, and a mutual learning mechanism is designed to facilitate the exchange of knowledge between different modalities. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art approaches on several benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in classification accuracy. The key findings highlight the importance of mutual modality learning in capturing complementary information from different modalities, leading to a more comprehensive understanding of video actions. This research contributes to the field of video analysis and has potential applications in areas such as surveillance, human-computer interaction, and autonomous systems, with relevant keywords including video action classification, mutual modality learning, multi-stream framework, and deep learning.
