Dynamic dispatching is a critical process in various real-time systems, such as transportation networks, manufacturing, and cloud computing, where the allocation of resources must adapt to rapidly changing environments. Recently, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has emerged as a promising approach to address the complexity of dynamic dispatching by enabling agents to learn optimal strategies directly from high-dimensional data. This paper investigates the key challenges associated with applying DRL in dynamic dispatching scenarios. We systematically analyze issues related to environment non-stationarity, large state-action spaces, reward sparsity, and the need for real-time decision-making. To evaluate these challenges, we implement several state-of-the-art DRL algorithms, including Deep Q-Networks and Policy Gradient methods, within representative dynamic dispatching environments. Our experimental results highlight significant performance bottlenecks caused by delayed feedback, high variance in learning, and difficulties in generalizing learned policies across diverse operational contexts. We further discuss practical limitations such as sample inefficiency and scalability, as well as strategies for improving robustness, including model-based enhancements and hybrid approaches. The findings provide actionable insights for researchers and practitioners seeking to leverage DRL for dynamic dispatching, emphasizing the importance of tailored algorithm designs and efficient computational frameworks. Keywords: Deep Reinforcement Learning, dynamic dispatching, real-time decision-making, resource allocation, non-stationary environments.
