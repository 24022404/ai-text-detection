This paper introduces a novel deep reinforcement learning (DRL) framework for automated classification of 3D MRI brain volumes, leveraging automatically extracted labels from clinical reports. The objective is to reduce the reliance on manual annotation, which is time-consuming and expensive. The proposed approach utilizes a convolutional neural network (CNN) to process MRI volumes and a reinforcement learning agent to optimize classification performance based on extracted labels. A key innovation is the automated label extraction process from clinical reports using natural language processing (NLP) techniques, eliminating manual labeling efforts. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed DRL framework achieves comparable, and in some cases superior, classification accuracy compared to traditional supervised learning methods trained on manually annotated data. Specifically, the system achieves a 10% improvement in F1-score in detecting Alzheimer's Disease compared to a baseline CNN model. The findings suggest that DRL with automated label extraction can significantly improve the efficiency and scalability of medical image analysis. Keywords: Deep Reinforcement Learning, MRI, Brain Volume Classification, Automated Label Extraction, Clinical Reports, Medical Image Analysis, Alzheimer's Disease.
