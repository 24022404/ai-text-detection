Graph-structured data is pervasive in various domains, making effective representation learning on graphs a critical research focus within information technology and artificial intelligence. This paper explores a novel approach to representation learning on graphs, leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize the extraction of informative features for downstream tasks. We propose an RL-based framework where an agent sequentially selects node neighborhoods to aggregate, dynamically adapting its strategy based on feedback from the learning process. The approach is evaluated on several benchmark graph datasets, demonstrating enhanced performance in tasks such as node classification and link prediction compared to traditional graph embedding methods. Experimental results indicate that the reinforcement learning-driven model effectively captures both local and global structural information, leading to more robust and expressive node representations. This research highlights the potential of integrating RL techniques with graph neural networks to advance representation learning on graphs. The proposed method offers improved adaptability and generalization, with significant implications for applications in social network analysis, recommendation systems, and biological network modeling. Keywords: Representation learning, graph neural networks, reinforcement learning, node classification, graph embeddings.
