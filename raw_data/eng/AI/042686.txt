This paper addresses the challenge of extracting meaningful low-dimensional representations and identifying metastable clusters from high-dimensional time series data, a critical task in understanding complex dynamical systems. We propose a novel framework that combines dimensionality reduction techniques with unsupervised clustering to uncover latent structures in the data. Our approach leverages a combination of autoencoders for efficient state embedding and spectral clustering to detect metastable states, which are crucial for characterizing long-term system behavior. The proposed method is evaluated on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating superior performance in terms of clustering accuracy and computational efficiency compared to existing techniques. Results indicate that our framework effectively captures the underlying dynamics and transitions between metastable states, providing valuable insights for applications in fields such as molecular dynamics, climate modeling, and financial forecasting. The key contributions of this work include a scalable and interpretable model for state embedding, a robust clustering algorithm tailored for metastable state detection, and a comprehensive evaluation framework. This research advances the state-of-the-art in time series analysis and offers practical tools for analyzing complex systems with high-dimensional data.  

**Keywords**: time series analysis, dimensionality reduction, metastable clusters, autoencoders, spectral clustering, dynamical systems.
