Affect analysis in real-world settings presents a significant challenge due to the inherent variability and complexity of human behavior. This paper introduces a novel approach to multi-modal affect analysis utilizing standardized data within subjects collected in the wild. Our method addresses the limitations of traditional approaches by leveraging personalized baseline modeling to normalize affective responses across diverse individuals and contexts. We employ a combination of physiological signals (ECG, EDA), facial expressions, and self-reported emotions, standardized against each participant's own baseline, to train a robust affect recognition model. Results demonstrate a significant improvement in classification accuracy compared to models trained on non-standardized data, particularly in discerning subtle emotional nuances. The findings suggest that personalized standardization enhances the reliability and generalizability of affect recognition systems in uncontrolled environments. This research contributes to the development of more accurate and ecologically valid affect-aware technologies for applications in mental health monitoring, personalized human-computer interaction, and behavioral science.

Keywords: Affect analysis, multi-modal data, standardization, in-the-wild, personalized modeling, emotion recognition.
