Action recognition in videos is a challenging task due to the complex temporal dynamics and subtle variations within actions. This paper introduces a novel Temporal Distinct Representation Learning (TDRL) framework designed to capture discriminative temporal features for improved action recognition. TDRL employs a two-branch network: a temporal aggregation branch that captures long-range dependencies and a temporal difference branch that focuses on subtle motion changes between consecutive frames. A contrastive learning objective is introduced to maximize the feature distinctiveness between different action classes and minimize the variance within the same class in the temporal domain. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including HMDB51 and UCF101, demonstrate that TDRL achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods by a significant margin. The results highlight the effectiveness of learning distinct temporal representations for robust action recognition. The proposed framework offers a promising direction for future research in video understanding and human-computer interaction.

**Keywords:** Action Recognition, Temporal Modeling, Representation Learning, Contrastive Learning, Video Understanding.
