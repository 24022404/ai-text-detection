Image super-resolution (SR) aims to reconstruct high-resolution (HR) images from their low-resolution (LR) counterparts. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have shown promise in generating visually appealing SR images, but often suffer from artifacts and unnatural textures. This paper introduces RankSRGAN, a novel GAN-based architecture for SR that incorporates a ranker network to improve perceptual quality. Our approach trains a discriminator to not only distinguish between real HR images and SR images, but also to rank the relative realism of different SR outputs. This ranking mechanism guides the generator to produce more perceptually plausible and visually consistent SR images. We evaluate RankSRGAN on benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in perceptual metrics compared to state-of-the-art SR methods. The results show that RankSRGAN generates SR images with finer details and fewer artifacts, leading to more visually pleasing results. The inclusion of a ranker within the GAN framework offers a promising direction for enhancing the perceptual quality of SR images. Keywords: Super-Resolution, Generative Adversarial Networks, Ranking, Image Reconstruction, Deep Learning.
