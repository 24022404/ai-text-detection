This paper addresses the challenge of leveraging offline data for meta-reinforcement learning (meta-RL) while maintaining adaptability to new tasks. Traditional meta-RL methods often require extensive online interaction, which can be impractical in real-world scenarios. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel framework that combines offline meta-reinforcement learning with online self-supervision. Our approach utilizes a pre-trained meta-RL model on offline datasets and introduces an online self-supervision mechanism to fine-tune the model with minimal interaction. This mechanism enables the model to adapt to new tasks by generating pseudo-labels and refining its policy through self-generated feedback. Experiments on benchmark environments demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance compared to fully online meta-RL approaches while significantly reducing the need for real-time interaction. The results highlight the potential of our framework for applications where offline data is abundant but online exploration is costly or constrained. This work contributes to advancing meta-RL by bridging the gap between offline learning and online adaptability, offering a practical solution for real-world deployment.  

**Keywords**: Meta-reinforcement learning, offline learning, self-supervision, adaptation, pseudo-labels, policy refinement.
