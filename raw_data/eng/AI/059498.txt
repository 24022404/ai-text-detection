The proliferation of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in various applications raises significant privacy concerns, particularly regarding membership inference attacks, which aim to determine whether a specific data instance was included in the model's training set. This paper introduces privGAN, a novel approach designed to safeguard GANs from such attacks while maintaining minimal computational overhead.

Methods/Approach: PrivGAN incorporates a privacy-preserving mechanism within the traditional GAN framework. Our approach leverages a two-fold strategy: the integration of noise perturbation techniques during the training phase and an adaptive privacy adjustment model that fine-tunes the privacy-utility trade-off. Unlike conventional methods that may significantly degrade model performance, privGAN maintains the integrity and quality of the generated outputs.

Results/Findings: Through comprehensive experiments across benchmark datasets, privGAN demonstrates a substantial reduction in the success rate of membership inference attacks compared to existing defenses, achieving this with negligible impact on the GAN's output quality. Performance metrics indicate that our method consistently delivers robust privacy protection while preserving the fidelity of synthetic data.

Conclusion/Implications: PrivGAN offers a cost-effective and efficient solution to enhancing the privacy of GANs without compromising their performance, making it a promising candidate for secure GAN deployment in sensitive applications. The innovative blend of noise perturbation and adaptive tuning provides a scalable way to mitigate privacy risks, paving the way for broader adoption of GANs in environments where data privacy is paramount.

Keywords: GANs, membership inference attacks, privacy-preserving, noise perturbation, adaptive privacy, synthetic data, model security.
