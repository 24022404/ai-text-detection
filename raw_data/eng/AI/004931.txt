This paper introduces Vispi, a novel system for automatic visual perception and interpretation of chest X-rays. The objective is to develop an accurate and efficient method for detecting abnormalities in chest X-rays, addressing the need for improved diagnostic tools in medical imaging. Vispi utilizes a deep learning approach, leveraging a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture to analyze X-ray images and identify potential issues. The system is trained on a large dataset of annotated chest X-rays, enabling it to learn patterns and features associated with various abnormalities. Results show that Vispi achieves high accuracy in detecting conditions such as pneumonia, nodules, and fractures, outperforming existing methods. The system's performance is evaluated using metrics such as sensitivity, specificity, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC). Vispi has significant implications for clinical practice, enabling rapid and accurate diagnosis of chest X-rays, and potentially improving patient outcomes. Key contributions include the development of a robust and generalizable CNN architecture, and the creation of a large annotated dataset for chest X-ray analysis. Relevant keywords: deep learning, chest X-rays, medical imaging, computer-aided diagnosis, CNN, visual perception.
