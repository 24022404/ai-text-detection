Remote heart rate measurement has emerged as a critical area of research, particularly for its applications in telemedicine and fitness monitoring. Existing methods face challenges in achieving high accuracy and reliability across diverse environments. This paper introduces AutoHR, an end-to-end baseline model developed to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of remote heart rate monitoring through an innovative neural searching approach.

Methods/Approach: AutoHR leverages neural architecture search (NAS) techniques to automatically design an optimized neural network tailored for heart rate estimation from video data. The model processes facial video clips to detect subtle pulsations, translating them into reliable heart rate measurements. The neural searching framework ensures the model is dynamically adjusted to various conditions without extensive manual tuning, which is typical in conventional methods.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate AutoHR's superior performance against current state-of-the-art approaches, showing improved accuracy and robustness across different lighting conditions and motion scenarios. The model consistently outperforms traditional methods in experiments conducted on multiple benchmark datasets, reflecting its capability to adaptively learn effective feature representations.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of AutoHR marks a significant advancement in the field of remote heart rate monitoring by providing a robust, efficient, and scalable solution. Its novel use of neural searching not only simplifies the model design process but also significantly boosts performance, making it a valuable tool for deploying real-time heart rate monitoring systems in diverse environments. This research paves the way for future exploration into automated neural network design in biomedical applications.

Keywords: remote heart rate measurement, neural architecture search, facial video processing, telemedicine, AutoHR, end-to-end model.
