Zero-shot object detection (ZSD) is a challenging task that aims to detect objects from unseen categories without labeled training data. This paper introduces GTNet, a novel Generative Transfer Network designed to address the limitations of existing ZSD approaches. GTNet leverages a generative framework to synthesize high-quality feature representations for unseen classes by transferring knowledge from seen categories. The proposed model integrates a dual-branch architecture, combining a generative adversarial network (GAN) with a transfer learning mechanism to enhance feature generalization and reduce domain gaps. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that GTNet achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of mean average precision (mAP) and robustness to domain shifts. The key contributions of this work include a novel feature synthesis strategy, an effective transfer learning framework, and a comprehensive evaluation of GTNet's performance across diverse datasets. This research advances the field of zero-shot learning by providing a scalable and efficient solution for real-world object detection tasks, with potential applications in autonomous systems, surveillance, and robotics.  

**Keywords**: Zero-shot object detection, generative adversarial networks, transfer learning, feature synthesis, domain adaptation.
