This paper introduces a novel approach to trajectory prediction by leveraging social behavior patterns in crowded environments. The objective is to develop a predictive model that captures the complex interactions between individuals and their surroundings, enabling accurate forecasting of future trajectories. Our method utilizes a Recursive Social Behavior Graph (RSBG) framework, which integrates graph-based modeling with recursive neural networks to learn spatio-temporal relationships between agents. The RSBG approach allows for the incorporation of social norms, environmental constraints, and agent-specific characteristics, resulting in improved prediction accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our model in predicting trajectories in various scenarios, outperforming state-of-the-art methods. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a recursive graph-based architecture and the incorporation of social behavior patterns, enabling more accurate and human-like trajectory predictions. This work has significant implications for applications such as autonomous systems, crowd simulation, and surveillance, and highlights the importance of social awareness in predictive modeling. Key keywords: trajectory prediction, social behavior, graph-based modeling, recursive neural networks, autonomous systems.
