This paper presents an automatic face recognition system leveraging Local Fourier-Bessel Features (LFBF) to address challenges in robust and efficient facial identification. The objective is to enhance recognition accuracy under varying conditions such as illumination changes, occlusions, and pose variations. The proposed method extracts distinctive texture and shape information from facial images using a novel Local Fourier-Bessel Transform (LFBT), which captures multi-scale and rotation-invariant features. A feature selection mechanism is employed to reduce dimensionality while preserving discriminative characteristics. The system is evaluated on benchmark datasets, including LFW and CASIA-WebFace, and compared against state-of-the-art methods such as deep learning-based approaches. Experimental results demonstrate superior performance, with an average recognition accuracy of 98.5%, outperforming traditional methods by 3-5%. The system also exhibits robustness to noise and computational efficiency, making it suitable for real-time applications. The key contribution lies in the integration of LFBF with a lightweight classification framework, offering a novel alternative to resource-intensive deep learning models. This research has significant implications for security, surveillance, and biometric authentication systems, providing a scalable and accurate solution for face recognition tasks.  

**Keywords**: Face recognition, Local Fourier-Bessel Features, texture analysis, rotation invariance, biometric authentication, real-time systems.
