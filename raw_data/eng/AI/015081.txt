Graph-based data structures are increasingly critical in modeling complex systems, such as social networks, biological interactions, and dynamic communication systems. However, traditional graph-based models often struggle to capture the temporal evolution and uncertainty inherent in such systems. This paper introduces *Evolving-Graph Gaussian Processes* (EGGPs), a novel framework that extends Gaussian Processes (GPs) to model dynamic graphs with evolving structures and attributes. The proposed approach leverages a probabilistic framework to represent both the graph topology and node features as time-varying functions, enabling robust predictions and uncertainty quantification. We employ a combination of kernel-based learning and graph neural networks to efficiently capture spatiotemporal dependencies. Experimental results on real-world datasets demonstrate that EGGPs outperform state-of-the-art methods in tasks such as link prediction, node classification, and anomaly detection, particularly in scenarios with incomplete or noisy data. The framework’s ability to adapt to evolving graph structures and provide interpretable uncertainty estimates makes it a valuable tool for applications in network analysis, recommendation systems, and beyond. This work contributes a scalable and flexible solution for modeling dynamic graphs, addressing key challenges in modern graph-based machine learning.  

**Keywords**: Gaussian Processes, dynamic graphs, spatiotemporal modeling, uncertainty quantification, graph neural networks, evolving structures.
