The objective of this research is to address the challenges in video object segmentation by introducing a novel framework named ALBA, which leverages reinforcement learning to enhance segmentation accuracy and adaptability. Traditional video object segmentation approaches often struggle with dynamic environments and varied object appearances. ALBA utilizes a reinforcement learning model to intelligently adapt segmentation strategies based on the visual context of each video frame. The approach involves training an agent using reward mechanisms that favor precision and continuity in object boundary delineation across frames. Our findings demonstrate that ALBA significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency, particularly in complex scenarios with occlusions and rapidly changing backgrounds. The integration of reinforcement learning presents a flexible solution that dynamically adjusts the segmentation process, offering improved performance without the need for extensive manual tuning. ALBA's innovative application of reinforcement learning in video segmentation represents a substantial contribution to the field, with potential applications in automated video editing, surveillance, and augmented reality. Keywords for this study include video object segmentation, reinforcement learning, adaptive strategies, and dynamic environments.
