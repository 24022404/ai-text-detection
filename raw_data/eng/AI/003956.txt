Energy harvesting sensors offer a promising solution for sustainable and autonomous sensing, but their intermittent energy supply presents a significant configuration challenge. This paper addresses the problem of dynamically optimizing sensor parameters to maximize performance under fluctuating energy availability. We propose a novel reinforcement learning (RL) framework that autonomously learns optimal sensor configurations based on real-time environmental conditions and energy harvesting patterns. Our approach utilizes a deep Q-network (DQN) agent trained to select sensor operating modes that balance energy consumption with data acquisition quality. We evaluate the proposed RL-based configuration method through extensive simulations using real-world energy harvesting data traces and compare it with traditional rule-based and static configuration strategies. Results demonstrate that the RL agent significantly improves sensor uptime, data throughput, and overall system efficiency. The findings highlight the potential of reinforcement learning for adaptive power management in energy harvesting sensor networks, paving the way for more robust and efficient deployments. Keywords: Energy Harvesting, Sensors, Reinforcement Learning, Power Management, Optimization, Autonomous Systems.
