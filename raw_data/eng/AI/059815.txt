This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised 3D geometry learning by leveraging holistic 3D motion understanding from monocular video sequences. Traditional methods often rely on supervised learning with labeled datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to produce. In contrast, our approach introduces a novel unsupervised framework that learns depth, camera pose, and scene motion directly from raw pixel data without requiring ground truth annotations. The proposed method integrates a multi-task learning architecture, combining depth estimation, ego-motion prediction, and object motion understanding into a unified model. By exploiting temporal consistency and geometric constraints across frames, our system achieves robust 3D scene reconstruction and motion segmentation. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in depth accuracy and motion estimation compared to state-of-the-art unsupervised methods. The framework’s ability to generalize across diverse scenes and dynamic objects highlights its potential for applications in autonomous navigation, augmented reality, and robotics. This work contributes to advancing unsupervised learning in 3D vision by emphasizing the importance of holistic motion understanding and pixel-level optimization.  

**Keywords**: unsupervised learning, 3D geometry, motion understanding, depth estimation, monocular video, scene reconstruction.
