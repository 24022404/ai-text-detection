This paper investigates a novel multi-armed bandit problem incorporating both feedback graphs and switching costs, addressing limitations of traditional bandit algorithms in networked decision-making scenarios. We introduce a framework where the learner receives feedback based on a predefined graph structure, capturing dependencies between arms, and incurs a cost for switching between arms. We propose a modified Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) algorithm, termed Graph-Switching UCB (GS-UCB), designed to balance exploration, exploitation, and minimization of switching costs while leveraging feedback graph information. Theoretical analysis provides a regret bound for GS-UCB, demonstrating its efficiency in learning optimal policies. Empirical evaluations on synthetic and real-world datasets, including a personalized recommendation system, show that GS-UCB outperforms standard bandit algorithms and their extensions, particularly when feedback is sparse and switching costs are significant. The findings highlight the importance of considering feedback structures and switching costs in bandit problems, paving the way for more effective decision-making in complex networked environments.

**Keywords:** Multi-armed bandits, feedback graphs, switching costs, online learning, UCB algorithm, regret analysis.
