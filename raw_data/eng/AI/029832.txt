This paper addresses the challenge of uncertainty quantification in deep neural networks (DNNs) by introducing a principled approach to construct PAC (Probably Approximately Correct) confidence sets through calibrated prediction. The primary objective is to provide reliable confidence estimates for DNN outputs, ensuring that the true label is included in the predicted set with a user-specified probability. Our method leverages modern calibration techniques, such as temperature scaling, in combination with nonconformity scores derived from model outputs, to generate confidence sets that are both informative and statistically rigorous. Extensive experiments on standard image classification benchmarks demonstrate that our approach yields confidence sets with near-nominal coverage and reduced ambiguity compared to existing baseline methods. Furthermore, we empirically show that the proposed calibration framework improves both the interpretability and trustworthiness of DNN predictions, particularly in safety-critical and high-stakes applications. This work contributes a scalable and theoretically grounded technique for uncertainty quantification in deep learning, facilitating the deployment of AI systems with enhanced reliability. Keywords: PAC confidence sets, deep neural networks, uncertainty quantification, model calibration, predictive reliability.
