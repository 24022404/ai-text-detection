This paper addresses the challenge of maintaining model performance in sparse neural networks during training, where traditional pruning methods often suffer from reduced plasticity and irreversible loss of critical connections. We propose a novel approach that integrates neuroregeneration-inspired mechanisms into sparse training to dynamically recover and optimize pruned connections, thereby enhancing pruning plasticity. Our method leverages a biologically inspired framework that selectively regenerates pruned weights based on their potential contribution to model performance, ensuring a balance between sparsity and learning capacity. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly improves the accuracy and robustness of sparse models compared to existing pruning techniques, achieving state-of-the-art results with up to 90% sparsity. The proposed method also exhibits superior scalability and efficiency, making it suitable for resource-constrained environments. This work contributes to advancing sparse training methodologies by introducing a biologically inspired mechanism that enhances plasticity and performance, with potential applications in edge computing and large-scale AI deployments.  

**Keywords**: sparse training, neural network pruning, neuroregeneration, pruning plasticity, model efficiency, edge AI.
