This paper addresses the challenge of domain adaptation for object detection in autonomous driving, where models trained on one dataset often fail to generalize well to another. Our objective is to develop a robust domain adaptation approach that enables accurate object detection across different environments and conditions. We propose a novel method that combines pixel and feature level adaptation techniques, leveraging the strengths of both to achieve superior performance. Our approach utilizes a dual-branch architecture, where one branch focuses on aligning the pixel-level distributions of the source and target domains, while the other branch adapts the feature-level representations to reduce the domain shift. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art domain adaptation techniques, achieving significant improvements in object detection accuracy on various autonomous driving datasets. The key contributions of our research include a novel dual-branch adaptation architecture, a feature-level alignment loss function, and a comprehensive evaluation framework for assessing domain adaptation performance in object detection. Our work has important implications for the development of reliable and efficient autonomous driving systems, and can be applied to various computer vision tasks that involve domain adaptation and object detection, including autonomous vehicles, robotics, and surveillance. Key keywords: domain adaptation, object detection, autonomous driving, pixel-level adaptation, feature-level adaptation, deep learning.
