**Abstract**

Accurate nighttime satellite imagery is essential for applications ranging from urban planning to environmental monitoring, yet its acquisition is often hampered by sensor limitations and high costs. This paper introduces NightVision, a novel framework for generating high-quality nighttime satellite images from readily available infra-red (IR) observations. Our approach leverages deep neural networks, specifically a generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture, to translate IR data into realistic visible-spectrum nighttime imagery. The model is trained on a diverse dataset that includes paired IR and real nighttime satellite images, enabling it to learn complex mappings between modalities. Experimental results demonstrate that NightVision produces visually coherent and spatially accurate images, outperforming baseline image translation methods in both perceptual quality and structural similarity metrics. Additionally, the framework effectively reconstructs urban and rural lighting patterns and is robust across different geographical regions and weather conditions. The proposed method significantly reduces the dependency on costly nighttime sensors while expanding the potential for nighttime Earth observation analytics. NightVision has important implications for remote sensing, disaster response, and urban development, offering a scalable solution for generating consistent and reliable nighttime satellite imagery from IR data. 

**Keywords:** nighttime satellite imagery, infra-red observations, image translation, generative adversarial networks, remote sensing, deep learning
