This paper addresses the challenge of inefficient experience reuse in reinforcement learning (RL), which often hinders sample efficiency and limits the scalability of RL algorithms. We propose a novel experience reuse method based on policy residual representation, enabling an agent to leverage prior experiences more effectively across different tasks and environments. Our approach decomposes policies into residual components, which capture task-specific adaptations relative to a shared base policy. By representing experiences in terms of these residuals, our method enables the flexible transfer and adaptation of learned behaviors, even when task dynamics differ. We implement the proposed framework within standard off-policy RL algorithms and evaluate its performance on a suite of continuous control benchmarks. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in sample efficiency and convergence speed compared to baseline experience replay and transfer methods. The findings suggest that residual policy representation provides a more generalizable and modular approach to experience reuse, leading to accelerated learning and improved generalization. This research offers important insights for developing scalable RL systems capable of transferring knowledge across diverse environments. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, experience reuse, policy residual representation, sample efficiency, transfer learning, continual learning.
