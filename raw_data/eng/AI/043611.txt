This paper addresses the challenge of accurately classifying gliomas using MRI data. The focus is on enhancing the performance of neural network models by incorporating explanation-guided training, thereby improving decision-making transparency and model reliability in medical imaging applications.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel extension to traditional neural network training by integrating explanation-guided techniques that leverage human-interpretable insights to direct model learning. Our approach utilizes post-hoc interpretation methods to identify regions of interest in MRI scans that significantly influence model predictions. These insights are then used to tailor the training process, ensuring the network focuses on clinically relevant features.

Results/Findings: The implementation of explanation-guided training resulted in a noticeable improvement in model performance metrics, including accuracy, precision, and sensitivity, when applied to glioma classification tasks. Our approach demonstrated superior results compared to conventional training methodologies, showcasing its potential as a robust tool for computer-aided diagnosis. The trained model also showed increased interpretability, contributing to more reliable and understandable AI-driven medical evaluations.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of explanation-guided training into neural networks for glioma classification marks a significant step forward in medical imaging. This approach not only enhances classification accuracy but also aligns model predictions with clinical reasoning. The findings underscore the potential of leveraging human-interpretable insights to refine AI systems, paving the way for improved collaboration between AI technology and healthcare professionals. Keywords include neural networks, explanation-guided training, glioma classification, MRI data, medical imaging.
