The geologic storage of carbon dioxide (CO₂) is a critical strategy for mitigating climate change, yet accurately monitoring and predicting CO₂ behavior in subsurface reservoirs remains a significant challenge. This study addresses the problem of uncertainty quantification in CO₂ storage by proposing a novel Bayesian inversion framework integrated with generative models. The objective is to enhance the accuracy of subsurface CO₂ plume predictions while accounting for inherent uncertainties in geological parameters. We employ a generative adversarial network (GAN) to model complex geological structures and combine it with a Bayesian inference approach to iteratively update predictions based on observed data. The proposed method is evaluated using synthetic datasets and real-world geological scenarios, demonstrating its ability to outperform traditional inversion techniques in terms of prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. Key findings reveal that the integration of generative models with Bayesian methods significantly reduces uncertainty in plume localization and improves the reliability of long-term storage assessments. This research contributes a scalable and robust framework for CO₂ storage monitoring, offering practical implications for carbon capture and storage (CCS) projects. The approach is particularly valuable for optimizing injection strategies and ensuring the safe containment of CO₂ in subsurface reservoirs.  

**Keywords**: Bayesian inversion, generative models, CO₂ storage, uncertainty quantification, subsurface monitoring, GAN, carbon capture and storage.
