This paper introduces *Points2Vec*, a novel unsupervised framework for object-level feature learning from 3D point clouds. The objective of this research is to address the challenge of extracting meaningful and discriminative features from unstructured point cloud data without relying on labeled datasets. Traditional supervised methods often require extensive annotations, which are costly and time-consuming to obtain. To overcome this limitation, *Points2Vec* leverages a self-supervised learning approach that utilizes geometric and spatial relationships within point clouds to learn robust feature representations. The proposed method employs a graph-based neural network architecture combined with a contrastive learning strategy to capture both local and global structural information. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that *Points2Vec* achieves competitive performance in object classification and segmentation tasks, outperforming existing unsupervised methods and approaching the accuracy of supervised baselines. The results highlight the framework's ability to generalize across diverse 3D shapes and its potential for applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality. This work contributes to the advancement of unsupervised learning in 3D vision by providing a scalable and efficient solution for feature extraction from point clouds, reducing the dependency on labeled data while maintaining high performance.  

**Keywords**: Point clouds, unsupervised learning, feature extraction, 3D vision, contrastive learning, graph neural networks.
