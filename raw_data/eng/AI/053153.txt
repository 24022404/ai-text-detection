Graph matching, a fundamental problem in computer science, seeks to find a correspondence between the nodes of two graphs based on their structural similarity. This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently computing approximate graph matchings, particularly in scenarios where finding the exact solution is computationally prohibitive. We present a novel algorithm that leverages linear programming relaxation and a specialized assignment strategy to achieve near-optimal assignments in linear time. Unlike traditional approaches with quadratic or higher complexity, our method scales efficiently with large graphs. The algorithm's performance is evaluated on synthetic and real-world graph datasets, demonstrating significant speed improvements compared to existing approximate graph matching techniques while maintaining competitive matching accuracy. Experimental results reveal that our algorithm consistently achieves high-quality matchings, approaching the performance of more computationally intensive methods. This linear-time approach enables graph matching to be applied in time-critical applications and on very large datasets, such as social network analysis, computer vision, and bioinformatics. Key words: Graph Matching, Assignment Problem, Linear Time Algorithm, Approximate Matching, Optimization, Graph Similarity.
