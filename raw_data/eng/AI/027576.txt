**Abstract**

Hierarchical agglomerative clustering (HAC) is widely used for discovering data groupings, but traditional approaches often disregard the natural or original order of elements, which is crucial in domains such as time series analysis, genomics, and preference ranking. This paper introduces an order preserving hierarchical agglomerative clustering (OP-HAC) method that retains the sequential order of data while constructing the hierarchical clustering dendrogram. Our approach modifies traditional agglomerative strategies by restricting candidate cluster merges to adjacent clusters, thereby ensuring the global order is maintained throughout the clustering process. We present both an efficient algorithm for OP-HAC and a theoretical analysis of its computational complexity. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets, including temporal event streams and sorted lists, demonstrate that OP-HAC identifies meaningful clusters consistent with the underlying sequential structure, outperforming standard HAC when order preservation is essential. The proposed method also enhances interpretability in ordered data scenarios and reduces computational overhead by limiting merge possibilities. OP-HAC presents a significant advancement for clustering applications where structural ordering is intrinsic, with potential applications in time series segmentation, biological sequence analysis, and rank-based data mining. **Keywords:** hierarchical clustering, order preservation, agglomerative clustering, sequential data, time series, data mining.
