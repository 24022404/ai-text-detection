Deep neural networks (DNNs) have achieved remarkable performance in various domains, yet their lack of transparency hinders trust and widespread adoption. This paper introduces a novel adversarial approach to explain DNN predictions by generating minimal input perturbations that significantly alter the network's output. Our method, termed Adversarial Explanation Generation (AEG), crafts adversarial examples specifically designed to reveal the salient features influencing the DNN's decision-making process. AEG leverages a gradient-based optimization strategy guided by a loss function that balances perturbation magnitude and prediction change. Experiments on image classification tasks demonstrate that AEG produces more concise and interpretable explanations compared to existing methods, highlighting crucial input regions. Furthermore, we show that AEG can identify potential biases and vulnerabilities in DNNs. The proposed approach offers a valuable tool for understanding and improving the reliability of deep learning models, fostering greater trust and accountability.

**Keywords:** Deep Neural Networks, Explainable AI, Adversarial Examples, Interpretability, Machine Learning, Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI).
