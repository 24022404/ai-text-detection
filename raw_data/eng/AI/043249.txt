This paper addresses the challenge of first-person action-object detection, a critical task in understanding human interactions from egocentric perspectives. The objective is to accurately identify and localize actions and their associated objects in real-world scenarios captured by wearable cameras. To tackle this problem, we introduce EgoNet, a novel deep learning framework specifically designed for egocentric vision tasks. EgoNet leverages a multi-stream architecture that integrates spatial, temporal, and contextual features to capture the dynamic relationships between actions and objects. The model is trained on a large-scale egocentric dataset, incorporating self-supervised learning techniques to enhance feature representation. Experimental results demonstrate that EgoNet outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both action and object detection accuracy, achieving a 12% improvement in mean average precision (mAP) on benchmark datasets. Furthermore, the framework exhibits robust performance in diverse real-world environments, highlighting its practical applicability. The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to model the intricate dependencies between actions and objects in egocentric videos, paving the way for advancements in applications such as augmented reality, assistive technologies, and human-robot interaction. Keywords: egocentric vision, action-object detection, deep learning, EgoNet, wearable cameras.
