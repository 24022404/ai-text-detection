Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) have achieved state-of-the-art performance across various tasks, but their vulnerability to adversarial attacks raises significant concerns. This paper addresses the problem of quantifying the robustness of DNNs against such attacks. We propose a novel probabilistic robustness analysis framework based on Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) learning theory. Our approach estimates the probability that a DNN is robust within a specified radius around a given input, providing a rigorous, data-driven measure of robustness. The method avoids computationally expensive verification techniques by leveraging PAC learning to efficiently sample and analyze the network's behavior. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach can effectively estimate robustness probabilities, providing valuable insights into the security of DNNs. Furthermore, we show that our method can be used to compare the robustness of different DNN architectures and training strategies. The findings contribute to a better understanding of DNN robustness and enable the development of more secure and reliable AI systems. Keywords: Deep Neural Networks, Robustness, Adversarial Attacks, PAC Learning, Probabilistic Analysis, Verification.
