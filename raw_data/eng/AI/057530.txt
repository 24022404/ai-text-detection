Fine-tuning pre-trained language models has become a cornerstone of modern natural language processing (NLP), yet the underlying reasons for its effectiveness remain poorly understood. This paper investigates the role of intrinsic dimensionality in explaining the success of fine-tuning across diverse NLP tasks. We hypothesize that the effectiveness of fine-tuning stems from the low-dimensional structure of the parameter space, which allows models to adapt efficiently to new tasks with minimal updates. To test this, we employ techniques from dimensionality estimation to analyze the intrinsic dimensionality of pre-trained language models during fine-tuning. Our experiments reveal that fine-tuning primarily operates within a low-dimensional subspace, enabling rapid adaptation while preserving the model's generalization capabilities. Furthermore, we demonstrate that intrinsic dimensionality correlates with task complexity and model performance, providing a theoretical foundation for optimizing fine-tuning strategies. These findings offer new insights into the mechanisms of transfer learning in NLP and suggest practical guidelines for improving fine-tuning efficiency. This research contributes to the broader understanding of language model adaptation and highlights the importance of intrinsic dimensionality in designing effective fine-tuning protocols.  

**Keywords**: intrinsic dimensionality, language models, fine-tuning, transfer learning, natural language processing, parameter efficiency.
