This paper addresses the challenge of detecting anomalies in complex datasets by proposing a novel approach to learning ensembles of anomaly detectors on synthetic data. The objective is to improve the accuracy and robustness of anomaly detection systems by leveraging the diversity of multiple detectors trained on artificially generated data. Our method employs a combination of generative models and ensemble learning techniques to create a robust anomaly detection framework. The approach involves generating synthetic data that mimics the patterns and structures of real-world data, and then training an ensemble of anomaly detectors on this synthetic data. Our results show that the proposed approach outperforms state-of-the-art anomaly detection methods, particularly in scenarios where real-world training data is limited or noisy. The use of synthetic data enables the creation of more diverse and robust ensembles, leading to improved detection accuracy and reduced false alarm rates. This research contributes to the development of more effective anomaly detection systems, with potential applications in areas such as cybersecurity, healthcare, and finance, and highlights the importance of synthetic data in enhancing the performance of machine learning models, including those based on autoencoders, one-class SVM, and isolation forests. Key keywords: anomaly detection, synthetic data, ensemble learning, generative models, machine learning, robustness, accuracy.
