Color constancy, the ability to perceive consistent object colors despite varying illumination, remains a challenging problem in computer vision. This paper introduces a novel Cascading Convolutional Color Constancy (CCCC) network designed to improve the accuracy and robustness of color constancy algorithms. Our method utilizes a cascade of convolutional neural networks, where each stage refines the illuminant estimation based on the output of the preceding stage. This architecture allows for hierarchical feature extraction and iterative error correction. We train the CCCC network end-to-end using a large-scale dataset of diverse scenes and illuminations. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our CCCC network outperforms state-of-the-art color constancy methods, achieving significantly lower angular error and improved color reproduction accuracy. Furthermore, we show that the cascaded architecture contributes to better generalization across different datasets and lighting conditions. The proposed method offers a robust and accurate solution for color constancy, with potential applications in various computer vision tasks such as image enhancement, object recognition, and autonomous navigation.

Keywords: Color Constancy, Convolutional Neural Networks, Cascaded Architecture, Illumination Estimation, Color Correction, Computer Vision.
