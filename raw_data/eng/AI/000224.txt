This paper explores the integration of differential privacy (DP) techniques with federated learning (FL) frameworks in environments constrained by limited communication bandwidth. The primary objective is to address the growing concerns regarding user data confidentiality in decentralized machine learning, while simultaneously mitigating the practical challenges associated with frequent data exchanges between clients and central servers. We propose a novel approach that combines adaptive communication strategies with differential privacy mechanisms to optimize the trade-off between privacy guarantees and system efficiency. The proposed model introduces an enhanced aggregation protocol, which selectively transmits compressed and privatized updates, reducing communication overhead without significantly compromising model accuracy. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves strong privacy protection and competitive learning performance, outperforming traditional FL approaches under tight communication budgets. The results suggest that robust privacy preservation and efficient communication can coexist in federated learning systems, enabling scalable and trustworthy deployment in resource-limited applications. This work contributes new insights into privacy-preserving distributed learning and highlights practical solutions for real-world adoption. Keywords: federated learning, differential privacy, communication efficiency, privacy-preserving machine learning, distributed systems.
