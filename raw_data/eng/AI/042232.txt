Fair machine learning (ML) models are increasingly deployed in financial services, yet the long-term sustainability of fairness remains a challenge. This paper investigates the often-overlooked hidden technical debts associated with maintaining fairness in these systems. We identify key categories of technical debt, including data dependencies, fairness metric instability, and the complexity of fairness interventions. Through empirical analysis of real-world financial datasets and model deployments, we demonstrate how these debts can accumulate over time, leading to model degradation and unintended discriminatory outcomes. We propose a framework for proactively managing fairness-related technical debt, incorporating automated monitoring, version control for fairness configurations, and continuous evaluation strategies. Our findings highlight the importance of addressing technical debt to ensure the responsible and equitable application of ML in finance, fostering trust and mitigating potential risks to vulnerable populations. Keywords: Fair Machine Learning, Technical Debt, Financial Services, Algorithmic Bias, Responsible AI, Model Monitoring.
