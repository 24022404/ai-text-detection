This paper explores the application of persistent homology, a topological data analysis (TDA) technique, to characterize and classify medical images, with a focus on lung and brain cancers. The objective is to leverage the inherent topological features of medical imaging data to improve diagnostic accuracy and provide a novel framework for image analysis. Persistent homology captures the shape and structure of data by identifying persistent topological features, such as connected components, loops, and voids, across multiple scales. We propose a computational pipeline that extracts these features from 2D and 3D medical images, including CT and MRI scans, and employs machine learning models for classification. Our approach is evaluated on publicly available datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in distinguishing between cancerous and non-cancerous tissues. Results indicate that topological features provide complementary information to traditional image analysis methods, achieving competitive classification accuracy. The study highlights the potential of persistent homology as a powerful tool for medical image analysis, offering insights into the structural patterns associated with cancer. This work contributes to the growing field of TDA in healthcare, paving the way for further exploration of topological methods in medical diagnostics and personalized treatment planning.  

**Keywords**: Persistent homology, topological data analysis, medical imaging, lung cancer, brain cancer, machine learning, CT scans, MRI scans.
