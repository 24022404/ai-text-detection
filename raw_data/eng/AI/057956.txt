This paper addresses the challenge of video object segmentation, where the goal is to accurately segment objects of interest from videos with minimal supervision. The objective is to develop a one-shot weakly supervised approach that can learn to segment objects from a single annotated frame. Our method leverages a novel combination of convolutional neural networks and graph-based models to effectively propagate object information across frames. The approach utilizes a weak supervision signal, in the form of image-level labels, to learn a robust and generalizable representation of objects. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets with significant reductions in annotation requirements. The key findings highlight the importance of incorporating temporal context and weak supervision in video object segmentation. This research contributes to the development of more efficient and scalable video analysis systems, with potential applications in areas such as autonomous driving, surveillance, and healthcare. Key keywords: one-shot learning, weakly supervised learning, video object segmentation, convolutional neural networks, graph-based models.
