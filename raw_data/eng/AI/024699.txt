Time series forecasting is a critical task in information technology, with applications spanning finance, healthcare, transportation, and energy management. This paper addresses the theoretical foundations and algorithmic advancements in time series forecasting. We present a comprehensive analysis of existing predictive models, including autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA), exponential smoothing, and state-of-the-art machine learning approaches such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks. Building on these, we introduce novel algorithms that combine classical statistical techniques with deep learning architectures to enhance prediction accuracy and adaptability to various types of temporal data. Extensive experiments on diverse benchmark datasets demonstrate that our hybrid models outperform traditional and contemporary forecasting methods across multiple evaluation metrics, including mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean square error (RMSE). Our theoretical analysis further provides insights into the stability, convergence, and generalizability of the proposed algorithms. The results highlight the advantages of integrating domain knowledge with advanced computational models, offering robust solutions for real-world time series forecasting challenges. This work contributes new perspectives and practical tools for improving forecasting systems, and lays the groundwork for future research in the development of interpretable and scalable prediction algorithms. Key keywords include time series forecasting, prediction algorithms, ARIMA, LSTM, hybrid models, machine learning, and temporal data analysis.
