Pedestrian trajectory prediction is crucial for autonomous driving and social robotics, yet remains challenging due to the inherent uncertainty and multi-modality of human motion. Existing generative adversarial network (GAN)-based methods often suffer from generating out-of-distribution (OOD) samples, leading to unrealistic or unsafe predictions. This paper introduces MG-GAN, a novel multi-generator GAN architecture designed to mitigate the OOD problem in pedestrian trajectory prediction. MG-GAN employs multiple generators, each specializing in capturing different aspects of pedestrian behavior, coupled with a shared discriminator. A diversity loss function encourages the generators to produce diverse and complementary trajectory predictions. Furthermore, we introduce a novel OOD detection mechanism integrated within the discriminator to penalize generators producing unrealistic trajectories. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that MG-GAN significantly reduces the occurrence of OOD samples compared to state-of-the-art methods, leading to improved prediction accuracy and robustness. Specifically, MG-GAN achieves a 15% reduction in OOD predictions while maintaining competitive performance in average displacement error and final displacement error. The proposed architecture offers a promising approach for enhancing the reliability and safety of trajectory prediction systems. Keywords: Trajectory Prediction, GAN, Generative Adversarial Networks, Out-of-Distribution Detection, Autonomous Driving, Pedestrians.
