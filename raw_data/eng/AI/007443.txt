Abstract:  
Deep learning has emerged as a transformative technology in medical image processing, offering unprecedented capabilities for image classification, segmentation, detection, and diagnostic support. This paper provides a comprehensive overview of current deep learning techniques applied to various medical imaging modalities, including MRI, CT, X-ray, and ultrasound. We examine state-of-the-art convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and generative adversarial networks (GANs), highlighting their architectures, performance, and adaptability to medical datasets. Key challenges identified include data scarcity, class imbalance, annotation reliability, interpretability, and the need for robust validation. Comparative analyses demonstrate that deep learning methods consistently outperform traditional image processing algorithms, particularly in feature extraction and automation efficiency. However, issues related to explainability, model generalization, and integration into clinical workflows persist as major obstacles. The paper discusses future directions, such as federated learning, self-supervised models, and explainable AI, which hold promise for enhancing both accuracy and trustworthiness in clinical applications. Our findings underscore the potential of deep learning to revolutionize medical image analysis, while emphasizing the necessity for interdisciplinary collaboration and ethical considerations in the deployment of these advanced technologies.  
Keywords: deep learning, medical image processing, convolutional neural network, image segmentation, explainable AI, federated learning, clinical applications.
