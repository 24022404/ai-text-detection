Wavelet Networks (WNs) represent a novel approach to scale-equivariant learning directly from raw waveforms, addressing the limitations of traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in handling multi-scale data. This paper introduces a framework that leverages wavelet transforms to enable neural networks to learn scale-invariant features efficiently. The proposed method integrates wavelet-based filters into the network architecture, allowing for the direct processing of raw waveform data without the need for pre-processing or fixed-scale feature extraction. By design, WNs inherently capture scale-equivariant representations, making them particularly suited for applications such as audio signal processing, time-series analysis, and biomedical signal classification.  

The approach is evaluated on benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance in tasks requiring multi-scale feature extraction compared to conventional CNNs and other state-of-the-art methods. Experimental results highlight the robustness of WNs in handling varying scales and their ability to generalize across different domains. The findings suggest that WNs offer a significant advancement in scale-equivariant learning, with potential applications in real-time signal processing and adaptive systems. This work contributes to the growing field of neural network architectures by introducing a scalable and efficient method for learning directly from raw waveforms, paving the way for future research in multi-scale data analysis.  

**Keywords**: Wavelet Networks, scale-equivariant learning, raw waveforms, multi-scale feature extraction, neural networks, signal processing.
