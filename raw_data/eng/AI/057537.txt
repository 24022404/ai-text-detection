This paper addresses the challenge of determining stopping criteria in Contrastive Divergence (CD), a widely used algorithm for training Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs). The traditional approach relies on the reconstruction error, which can be misleading and lead to suboptimal model performance. We propose alternative stopping criteria that leverage the underlying principles of CD, including the contrastive divergence objective and the model's log-likelihood. Our approach employs a combination of metrics, such as the log-likelihood of the training data and the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the model's distribution and the empirical data distribution. Experimental results demonstrate that our proposed criteria outperform the traditional reconstruction error-based approach, leading to improved model generalization and faster convergence. The contributions of this research include the development of novel stopping criteria for CD, which can be applied to various deep learning models and applications, such as dimensionality reduction, feature learning, and generative modeling. Key keywords: Contrastive Divergence, Restricted Boltzmann Machines, Stopping Criteria, Deep Learning, Unsupervised Learning.
