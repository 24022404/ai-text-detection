This paper presents a novel approach to collaborative causal discovery, leveraging atomic interventions to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of causal structure learning. The objective is to develop a framework that enables multiple agents to collectively discover causal relationships in complex systems. Our method employs a combination of probabilistic graphical models and intervention-based learning to identify causal dependencies. The approach utilizes atomic interventions, which are minimal and targeted interventions that provide maximal information about the causal structure. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our collaborative framework, showing improved performance in recovering causal graphs compared to existing methods. The key findings highlight the benefits of collaborative learning and atomic interventions in causal discovery, with implications for applications in fields such as healthcare, finance, and social sciences. Our research contributes to the development of more accurate and efficient causal discovery methods, with potential applications in decision-making and policy intervention. Key keywords: causal discovery, collaborative learning, atomic interventions, probabilistic graphical models, intervention-based learning.
