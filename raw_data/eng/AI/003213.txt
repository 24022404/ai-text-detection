This paper investigates the performance capabilities of Vision Transformers (ViTs) compared to Residual Networks (ResNets) in image classification tasks, particularly in scenarios without pretraining or the use of strong data augmentations. The aim is to explore the efficiency and effectiveness of ViTs under minimal preprocessing conditions.

Methods/Approach: We conducted a series of experiments using both Vision Transformers and ResNets on multiple standard image datasets. Unlike traditional approaches that rely heavily on pretraining and elaborate data augmentation techniques to enhance model performance, our approach evaluates these architectures in their raw form. The study involves training from scratch and applying only basic image transformations during the training phase.

Results/Findings: The results reveal that Vision Transformers can outperform Residual Networks without the need for extensive pretraining or strong data augmentation strategies. ViTs demonstrated superior generalization capabilities across diverse datasets, achieving higher accuracy rates compared to ResNets in the majority of cases. Our analysis indicates that the flexibility in the attention mechanism of ViTs contributes significantly to their robustness when faced with varied data distributions and limited preprocessing.

Conclusion/Implications: This research highlights a significant shift in the landscape of image classification architectures, showcasing the potential of Vision Transformers in scenarios where computationally intensive preprocessing techniques are not feasible. This has profound implications for deploying efficient and high-performing models in environments with limited resources. Our findings suggest a path forward for harnessing the power of Transformers in computer vision tasks, opening opportunities for further exploration into optimization and deployment strategies for ViTs. 

Keywords: Vision Transformers, Residual Networks, Image Classification, Pretraining, Data Augmentation, Attention Mechanism, Deep Learning.
