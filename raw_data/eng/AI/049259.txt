In the realm of distributed machine learning, improving communication efficiency is paramount for enhancing system performance and scalability. This paper investigates an alternative to the conventional error feedback mechanism, which is commonly used in distributed learning paradigms to reduce communication overhead. We introduce a novel approach that optimizes gradient compression techniques, reducing the volume of data exchanged between distributed nodes without compromising the accuracy of the learning models. Our method employs adaptive quantization strategies and sparsification algorithms to achieve significant reductions in communication requirements. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our approach not only maintains model accuracy similar to existing error feedback mechanisms but also substantially decreases communication latency and resource consumption. The findings suggest that our proposed model can be seamlessly integrated into existing distributed learning frameworks, such as federated learning systems, to enhance their operational efficiency. This advancement in communication-efficient distributed learning has the potential to improve the deployment of machine learning models in resource-constrained environments, providing valuable contributions to the field. Key areas of innovation include adaptive quantization, gradient sparsification, and efficient data exchange protocols in distributed systems.
