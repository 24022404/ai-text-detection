Abstract  
This paper introduces the concept of Semantic Adversarial Examples (SAEs), a new class of inputs that deceive machine learning models by altering high-level semantic attributes rather than adding imperceptible noise. Our objective is to investigate how controlled modifications to an image’s or text’s meaning—such as changing object orientation, color semantics, or syntactic structures—can trigger misclassification in state-of-the-art classifiers. We propose a two-stage framework: first, a semantic transformation module leverages generative networks (for images) or dependency-guided rewriting (for text) to produce candidate variants that preserve human-interpretable meaning; second, an adversarial selection module evaluates these variants against the target model to identify those that maximize prediction error while maintaining semantic coherence. Experimental results on benchmark image datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet) and text corpora (SST-2 sentiment analysis) demonstrate that SAEs achieve attack success rates exceeding 85%, with changes readily understood by humans but unrecognized by defended models. Compared to pixel-level and token-level attacks, our approach yields more transferable adversarial examples and exposes vulnerabilities in recent robustness schemes. We conclude that incorporating semantic perturbations into evaluation and defense strategies is crucial for building truly robust AI systems. Our contributions include the formalization of SAEs, an efficient generation pipeline, and extensive empirical insights into their impact on modern classifiers.  

Keywords: Semantic Adversarial Examples; Adversarial Attacks; Model Robustness; Generative Networks; Deep Learning.
