This paper introduces Neural Predictive Belief Representations (NPBR), a novel framework for modeling and predicting belief states in dynamic environments. The objective of this research is to address the challenge of accurately representing and updating belief states in complex systems, such as autonomous agents or decision-making algorithms, where uncertainty and partial observability are prevalent. To achieve this, we propose a deep learning-based approach that leverages recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and attention mechanisms to encode and predict belief states over time. The model is trained on simulated environments with varying levels of uncertainty, enabling it to generalize across different scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate that NPBR outperforms traditional methods, such as Bayesian filters, in terms of prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. Furthermore, the framework shows robustness in handling noisy and incomplete data, making it suitable for real-world applications. The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to provide a scalable and adaptive solution for belief state prediction, which has significant implications for fields such as robotics, reinforcement learning, and human-computer interaction. Keywords: belief state prediction, neural networks, uncertainty modeling, attention mechanisms, autonomous systems.
