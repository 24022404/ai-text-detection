This paper addresses the challenge of simultaneous manipulation of directional lighting and facial expressions in digital faces, a task of significant importance in computer graphics and visual effects. Traditional methods often struggle to disentangle these components effectively without compromising the quality and realism of the final render.

Methods/Approach: We introduce LEGAN, a novel framework that utilizes Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to independently adjust lighting and expressions in facial imagery. Our approach leverages human perceptual judgments to guide the disentanglement process, ensuring that the adjustments maintain high visual fidelity and consistency with human perceptual expectations. The framework employs a dual-path architecture that separately learns representation for lighting and expression, utilizing self-supervised learning techniques to improve disentanglement accuracy.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that LEGAN outperforms existing methods in both qualitative and quantitative measures. The framework accurately and independently modifies lighting and expressions, preserving the natural semantics of the face. When validated against a dataset of diverse facial images, LEGAN consistently achieved superior results compared to state-of-the-art approaches in both naturalness and realism, indicating its robustness in different lighting conditions and emotional expressions.

Conclusion/Implications: LEGAN presents a significant advancement in the field of 3D face manipulation, offering a powerful tool for applications in gaming, animated movie production, and virtual reality. The method's ability to leverage human perceptual judgments opens new avenues for further research in perception-driven AI and enhances user interaction experiences. By disentangling and precisely controlling lighting and expressions, LEGAN paves the way for more sophisticated and realistic digital human renderings.

Keywords: LEGAN, facial expression manipulation, directional lighting, generative adversarial networks, human perceptual judgments, disentanglement, computer graphics.
