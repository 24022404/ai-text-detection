This paper introduces a novel approach to single-image super-resolution based on Dual-State Recurrent Networks (DSRN). The objective is to enhance the spatial resolution and perceptual quality of low-resolution images, addressing challenges in detail recovery and efficient computation. The proposed DSRN architecture incorporates two recurrent states operating in parallel: one focuses on capturing long-term contextual information from the input, while the other specializes in refining high-frequency details. By enabling information flow and interaction between these states through recurrent connections, our model effectively balances global context aggregation with local feature enhancement. Extensive experiments are conducted on several benchmark image datasets, demonstrating that the DSRN consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art super-resolution methods in terms of both quantitative metrics, such as Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), and perceptual visual quality. Moreover, the dual-state design achieves these improvements with competitive computational efficiency, making it suitable for real-time and resource-constrained applications. Our findings highlight the benefits of recurrent dual-state modeling for high-quality image reconstruction, paving the way for more effective solutions in image enhancement and related computer vision tasks. 

Keywords: image super-resolution, dual-state recurrent network, deep learning, image enhancement, computer vision.
