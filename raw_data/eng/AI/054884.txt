This paper presents EgoNet, a novel deep learning framework designed for first-person action-object detection in egocentric video data. The primary objective is to accurately identify objects with which a user interacts during diverse activities from a first-person viewpoint, addressing a critical need in applications such as robotics, augmented reality, and human-computer interaction. EgoNet leverages a dual-stream convolutional neural network that simultaneously processes visual cues from both the appearance of the scene and the spatial context relative to the observer’s hands and gaze. By integrating spatial priors and action cues, the model effectively distinguishes between background objects and those actively manipulated by the user. Extensive experiments on benchmark egocentric datasets demonstrate that EgoNet achieves superior detection accuracy and robustness compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, particularly in cluttered and dynamic environments. The results highlight EgoNet’s ability to generalize across various action-object classes and activity domains. This research advances the field of action-object detection by enabling more precise and context-aware understanding of human actions from the first-person perspective, with promising implications for wearable computing, assistive technologies, and interactive AI systems.

Keywords: egocentric vision, action-object detection, deep learning, convolutional neural network, EgoNet, human-computer interaction, wearable computing
