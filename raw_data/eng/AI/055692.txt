Video completion, the task of filling in missing regions of a video, remains a challenging problem due to complex motion and dynamic textures. This paper introduces Onion-Peel Networks (OPN), a novel deep learning architecture designed specifically for robust and coherent video completion. OPN iteratively refines the completed video by peeling away and filling in successive layers, inspired by the structure of an onion. Our method employs a 3D convolutional encoder-decoder network at each layer to capture spatiotemporal dependencies. A key innovation is the adaptive attention mechanism within each layer that focuses on reconstructing missing regions based on contextual information from both the available video frames and the previously completed layers. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that OPN significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both qualitative visual quality and quantitative metrics. The layered completion strategy effectively handles large missing regions and complex occlusions, resulting in more temporally consistent and visually plausible video completions. This work contributes a new approach to video completion that leverages layered processing and adaptive attention for improved reconstruction accuracy and temporal coherence. Keywords: Video Completion, Deep Learning, 3D Convolutional Networks, Attention Mechanism, Inpainting.
