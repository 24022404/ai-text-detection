Continuous blood pressure (BP) monitoring is critical for early detection and management of cardiovascular diseases. Traditional methods often rely on invasive techniques or require specialized hardware, limiting their scalability and accessibility. This paper proposes a novel approach to estimate continuous BP using photoplethysmography (PPG) signals through a federated learning (FL) framework. The objective is to develop a robust, privacy-preserving model that leverages distributed data without centralizing sensitive patient information. Our method employs a deep neural network (DNN) architecture trained on PPG data from multiple decentralized sources, ensuring data privacy and security while maintaining model accuracy. The FL approach is evaluated on a diverse dataset, demonstrating competitive performance compared to centralized training methods. Results indicate that the proposed model achieves high accuracy in systolic and diastolic BP estimation, with mean absolute errors of 4.2 mmHg and 2.8 mmHg, respectively. Furthermore, the FL framework shows resilience to data heterogeneity across different devices and populations. This research contributes to the advancement of non-invasive BP monitoring by introducing a scalable, privacy-aware solution with potential applications in remote healthcare and wearable technology. Key innovations include the integration of FL with PPG-based BP estimation and the development of a lightweight DNN optimized for edge devices. Keywords: blood pressure estimation, photoplethysmography, federated learning, deep neural networks, remote healthcare, wearable technology.
