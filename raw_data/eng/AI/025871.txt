The objective of this paper is to address the challenge of rendering realistic atmospheric clouds in computer graphics using advanced neural network techniques. Traditional methods for simulating cloud radiance are often computationally expensive and lack the efficiency needed for dynamic and interactive applications. We propose a novel approach called Deep Scattering, which leverages radiance-predicting neural networks to improve the efficiency and accuracy of cloud rendering. Our method utilizes a hierarchical neural architecture that models the scattering properties of clouds, trained on high-dimensional radiance data. The neural networks are crafted to predict the scattering of light within cloud structures, thus enabling efficient computations that significantly reduce rendering times while maintaining high fidelity. Our results demonstrate that Deep Scattering outperforms existing cloud rendering techniques in quality and speed, offering a new benchmark for real-time cloud simulation. This advancement provides significant contributions to visual effects in gaming and film, virtual reality experiences, and meteorological simulations. By integrating state-of-the-art deep learning models with atmospheric physics, this research opens new avenues for the realistic and resource-efficient rendering of voluminous cloud formations. Key keywords include neural networks, atmospheric rendering, deep learning, radiance prediction, cloud simulation.
