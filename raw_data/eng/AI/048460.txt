This paper addresses the challenge of efficient inference in structured Gaussian process (GP) models, which are widely used in domains such as spatial statistics, machine learning, and temporal data analysis. Traditional white-box and black-box inference methods either require extensive computational resources or lack robustness when dealing with complex structured data.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel gray-box inference framework that strikes a balance between interpretability and computational efficiency. This framework leverages the intrinsic structure of Gaussian process models by incorporating structured prior knowledge while maintaining the flexibility to adapt to new data. Our approach integrates techniques from variational inference and sparse Gaussian processes, enhancing scalability without sacrificing accuracy.

Results/Findings: Empirical evaluations demonstrate that our gray-box inference method consistently outperforms traditional inference techniques on several benchmark datasets. The proposed method achieves substantial reductions in computational time and resource usage, with significant improvements in both predictive accuracy and model interpretability. Comparisons with existing methods highlight our approach's robustness in capturing data dependencies and providing reliable uncertainty estimates.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a novel inference technique that enhances the utility of Gaussian process models across various applications, enabling more efficient handling of large-scale and structured datasets. By offering a scalable and interpretable inference method, our framework opens up new possibilities for deploying GP models in real-world scenarios where both computational efficiency and model transparency are critical. Key Keywords: Gaussian process, gray-box inference, structured models, variational inference, sparse Gaussian processes, efficiency, interpretability.
