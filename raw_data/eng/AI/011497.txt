Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have demonstrated significant success in learning from graph-structured data by effectively capturing node and edge relationships. However, traditional GNN architectures often face limitations in information propagation due to overly homogeneous aggregation schemes, which can hinder the effective integration of diverse relational patterns inherent in real-world graphs. This paper proposes a novel framework that enhances information propagation in GNNs through heterogeneous aggregations. By introducing multiple aggregation mechanisms tailored to various node and edge types, the approach enables richer and more discriminative feature representations. Extensive experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets in domains such as citation networks and social graphs. The experimental results indicate notable improvements in node classification accuracy, convergence speed, and robustness compared to conventional GNN models employing uniform aggregation. The proposed heterogeneous aggregation framework also demonstrates flexibility and scalability when applied to large and complex heterogeneous graphs. These findings suggest that leveraging diverse aggregation strategies can significantly boost the representational power of GNNs, providing new opportunities for applications in recommendation systems, knowledge graphs, and bioinformatics. Key keywords: graph neural networks, information propagation, heterogeneous aggregation, node classification, representation learning.
