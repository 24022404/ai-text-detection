This paper introduces RicciNets, a novel approach to pruning high-performance neural networks using Ricci flow, a geometric technique for optimizing network architecture. The objective is to develop an efficient method for reducing computational complexity while preserving model accuracy. Our approach utilizes Ricci flow to analyze the curvature of the network's loss landscape, identifying redundant connections and neurons that can be safely removed. We employ a curvature-guided pruning strategy, which prioritizes the removal of parameters with low curvature, resulting in a more efficient and accurate network. Experimental results demonstrate that RicciNets outperforms state-of-the-art pruning methods, achieving significant reductions in parameter count and computational cost while maintaining competitive accuracy on benchmark datasets. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of Ricci flow as a novel tool for network pruning and the development of a curvature-guided pruning strategy. Our approach has important implications for the deployment of neural networks in resource-constrained environments, such as edge devices and mobile applications, and highlights the potential of geometric techniques for optimizing neural network architecture. Key keywords: neural network pruning, Ricci flow, curvature analysis, high-performance computing, efficient AI models.
