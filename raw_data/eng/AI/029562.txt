This paper addresses the challenge of data efficiency in reinforcement learning (RL) by introducing a novel pretraining approach for representations. The objective is to develop a method that enables RL agents to learn effective policies with minimal interaction with the environment. Our approach utilizes a combination of self-supervised learning and representation learning to pretrain embeddings that capture relevant features of the environment. We employ a contrastive learning framework to train these representations, which are then fine-tuned using RL algorithms. Our results show that pretraining representations significantly improves the data efficiency of RL agents, achieving comparable or better performance than state-of-the-art methods with substantially less environment interaction. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of representation learning in RL and demonstrate the potential of pretraining as a means to improve data efficiency. This work contributes to the development of more efficient and effective RL methods, with potential applications in areas such as robotics and autonomous systems. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, representation learning, pretraining, data efficiency, contrastive learning.
