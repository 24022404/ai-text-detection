This paper introduces FD-GAN, a novel Face-Demorphing Generative Adversarial Network designed to restore the facial image of an accomplice from morphed images. Morphing attacks, which combine facial features of multiple individuals, pose significant security risks in identity verification systems. The objective of this research is to develop a robust solution capable of reversing the morphing process and reconstructing the original accomplice's face with high fidelity. FD-GAN employs a dual-path generator architecture combined with a discriminative network to disentangle and reconstruct facial features from morphed inputs. The model is trained on a diverse dataset of morphed and non-morphed facial images, leveraging adversarial learning to enhance reconstruction accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate that FD-GAN outperforms existing methods in terms of visual quality and structural similarity, achieving a 15% improvement in reconstruction accuracy compared to state-of-the-art techniques. The proposed approach also shows robustness against various morphing techniques and noise levels. This work contributes to the field of biometric security by providing a reliable tool for detecting and reversing morphing attacks, with potential applications in passport verification, border control, and forensic investigations. Keywords: face demorphing, generative adversarial networks, biometric security, image reconstruction, morphing attacks.
