This research addresses the challenge of accurately identifying trees located outside of closed-canopy forests using medium-resolution satellite imagery. While dense forests are often the focus of remote sensing studies, trees existing in agricultural, urban, and semi-urban landscapes play crucial roles in ecological balance and carbon sequestration, yet tend to be underrepresented in such analyses.

Methods/Approach: We developed a comprehensive global method that leverages medium-resolution satellite imagery to identify and map trees outside of traditional forested areas. The approach integrates advanced image processing techniques and machine learning algorithms specially tailored to delineate individual trees or small clusters in diverse landscapes. By optimizing the combination of spectral analysis and AI-driven feature recognition, our model offers a scalable solution that is applicable across varying geographic regions and environmental conditions.

Results/Findings: Our method demonstrated high accuracy in detecting trees in non-forest environments, with performance metrics showing significant improvement over existing models that typically focus on high-resolution data. Comparative analyses across multiple climatic zones and terrain types revealed that our algorithm consistently outperforms traditional techniques, particularly in areas with sparse vegetation.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings of this study provide critical insights into the potential of medium-resolution satellite imagery as a viable tool for global tree inventory efforts beyond dense forest applications. This work contributes to enhancing our understanding of green cover distribution, facilitating better resource management, and informing environmental policy frameworks. The method's scalability underscores its applicability for extensive ecological monitoring and carbon accounting across different regions. Key keywords include remote sensing, tree mapping, medium-resolution imagery, machine learning, ecological monitoring.
