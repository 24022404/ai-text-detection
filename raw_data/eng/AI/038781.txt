Accurate traffic forecasting is crucial for intelligent transportation systems, yet its performance heavily relies on the availability and quality of historical data. This paper investigates the effectiveness of transfer learning (TL) and online learning (OL) techniques for traffic forecasting under varying data availability scenarios. We evaluate different TL strategies for adapting models trained on data-rich source domains to data-scarce target domains. Furthermore, we explore OL algorithms to continuously update forecasting models as new traffic data becomes available, addressing non-stationary traffic patterns. Our results demonstrate that TL can significantly improve forecasting accuracy in data-scarce scenarios, but careful selection of source domains and TL methods is crucial to avoid negative transfer. OL techniques, particularly those employing adaptive learning rates, effectively capture evolving traffic dynamics and outperform static models. A comparative analysis reveals the trade-offs between TL and OL under different data conditions, highlighting potential pitfalls and providing guidelines for their effective deployment. This work contributes to a better understanding of how to leverage TL and OL to enhance traffic forecasting accuracy and robustness in real-world transportation networks. Keywords: Traffic Forecasting, Transfer Learning, Online Learning, Data Availability, Time Series Analysis.
