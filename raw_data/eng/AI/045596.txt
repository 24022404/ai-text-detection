The objective of this research is to explore the application of potential-based reward shaping in the puzzle game Sokoban, a challenging problem for reinforcement learning (RL) agents due to its combinatorial complexity and sparse rewards. We propose a novel approach utilizing potential-based reward shaping to enhance the learning efficiency of RL agents navigating Sokoban environments. Our method involves designing potential functions that incorporate game-specific heuristics, effectively guiding agents by providing additional context and incentives toward achieving the optimal solution. The experimental results demonstrate that this approach significantly improves the learning speed and performance of agents compared to baseline RL methods without reward shaping. Specifically, our method achieves higher success rates across various Sokoban levels and reduces the number of steps required to complete puzzles. These findings suggest that potential-based reward shaping can serve as a critical mechanism for improving agent performance in complex RL tasks with sparse reward structures. This research contributes to the field by providing an effective strategy for solving similar complex puzzles, potentially extending its applications to other domains requiring advanced problem-solving capabilities. Keywords: reinforcement learning, reward shaping, Sokoban, potential function, puzzle solving.
