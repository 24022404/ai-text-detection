Cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) is widely used in medical imaging due to its efficient three-dimensional reconstruction capabilities. However, reducing the number of projection views in sparse-view CBCT, often necessary for minimizing radiation exposure and acquisition time, leads to severe image artifacts that can compromise diagnostic quality. This paper proposes an adversarial learning-based framework for artifact reduction in sparse-view CBCT reconstruction. The proposed method employs a convolutional neural network (CNN) paired with a generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture to effectively learn and suppress complex artifacts while preserving critical anatomical details. The adversarial framework leverages a discriminator to distinguish between artifact-free and artifact-laden reconstructions, guiding the generator towards producing higher-quality images. Extensive experiments on simulated and real CBCT datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves significant improvements over traditional iterative reconstruction algorithms and standard deep learning methods, as measured by common image quality metrics such as PSNR and SSIM. Visual assessment further confirms the model's ability to restore fine structural details and reduce streaking and blurring artifacts. The proposed adversarial sparse-view CBCT artifact reduction technique holds promise for enhancing clinical CBCT applications by enabling high-quality imaging with fewer projections. Keywords: Sparse-view CBCT, artifact reduction, deep learning, GAN, adversarial learning, medical imaging, low-dose reconstruction.
