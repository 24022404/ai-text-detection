Video completion, the task of filling in missing or corrupted regions in video sequences, remains a challenging problem in computer vision. This paper introduces *Onion-Peel Networks* (OPN), a novel deep learning framework designed to address video completion with improved accuracy and efficiency. The proposed approach leverages a hierarchical, layer-by-layer processing mechanism inspired by the structure of an onion, where each layer refines the completion process by progressively reconstructing finer details. OPN employs a combination of spatial and temporal attention mechanisms to ensure coherent and context-aware video inpainting, even for complex scenes with dynamic motion. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that OPN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both visual quality and computational efficiency. Key contributions include a unique multi-scale feature extraction strategy and a lightweight architecture that reduces inference time without compromising performance. The framework shows significant potential for applications in video editing, restoration, and augmented reality. Keywords: video completion, deep learning, attention mechanisms, video inpainting, Onion-Peel Networks.
