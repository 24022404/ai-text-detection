The objective of this research is to address the challenge of accurately predicting human trajectories in dynamic environments by leveraging social interactions. We introduce a novel Recursive Social Behavior Graph (RSBG) framework, designed to effectively capture and model the complex social interactions among individuals. The proposed approach employs a graph-based representation where nodes represent individuals, and edges encapsulate social behaviors and interactions over time. Our model recursively integrates social cues to improve predictive accuracy, using advanced machine learning techniques to dynamically adjust to evolving social contexts. Experimental results demonstrate that RSBG outperforms existing trajectory prediction models by a notable margin in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The findings of this study indicate that incorporating recursive social behavior significantly enhances the model's ability to anticipate future trajectories in settings with high interaction complexity, such as crowded pedestrian areas or autonomous vehicle navigation. The RSBG framework's adaptability and robust performance highlight its potential applicability to a wide range of fields including autonomous systems, robotics, and urban planning. This research underlines the importance of considering social dynamics in trajectory prediction, offering valuable insights and tools for future advancements in intelligent systems. Key keywords include trajectory prediction, social behavior modeling, recursive graph, and machine learning.
