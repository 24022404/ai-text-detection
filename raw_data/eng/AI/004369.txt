Accurately predicting corn yields at the county level is crucial for informed decision-making in agriculture, impacting resource allocation, supply chain management, and economic forecasting. This paper investigates the application of deep Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models for forecasting corn yields at the county level, leveraging historical weather data, soil characteristics, and planting information. We propose a novel architecture incorporating multiple LSTM layers to capture complex temporal dependencies within agricultural datasets. Our experiments across various counties demonstrate that the deep LSTM model outperforms traditional statistical methods and simpler machine learning algorithms, achieving a significant reduction in prediction error, up to 15% improvement in Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) compared to benchmark models. These findings highlight the potential of deep learning techniques to enhance agricultural forecasting accuracy, providing valuable insights for farmers, policymakers, and agribusinesses. This approach offers a scalable and adaptable solution for yield prediction across diverse geographical regions.
**Keywords:** Corn Yield Prediction, LSTM, Deep Learning, Time Series Forecasting, Agriculture, County Level.
