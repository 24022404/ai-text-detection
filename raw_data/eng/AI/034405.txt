**Abstract**

Image-to-image translation seeks to convert an input image from one domain into a corresponding image in another domain while preserving essential structural details. Traditional approaches often struggle to maintain both style consistency and content integrity, particularly when bridging domains with significant appearance discrepancies. In this work, we propose a novel group-wise deep whitening-and-coloring transformation (GDWCT) framework to enhance image-to-image translation tasks. By integrating group-wise feature processing into the deep whitening-and-coloring transformation pipeline, our approach effectively aligns feature distributions between source and target domains at multiple semantic levels. We implement GDWCT within an end-to-end neural network architecture, facilitating flexible feature manipulation without manual crafting or domain-specific heuristics. Experimental evaluations on several benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves superior translation quality compared to existing baselines, delivering images with improved fidelity, style adaptation, and reduced visual artifacts. Notably, GDWCT consistently outperforms standard whitening-and-coloring networks by offering more granular and robust feature alignment. Our findings suggest that group-wise transformation is a promising strategy for diverse image-to-image translation applications, including style transfer, domain adaptation, and conditional image synthesis. 

**Keywords:** image-to-image translation, deep learning, whitening-and-coloring transformation, feature alignment, style transfer, group-wise processing, generative models
