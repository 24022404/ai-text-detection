This paper introduces SSD-GAN, a novel Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) architecture designed to improve the realism of generated images by focusing on both spatial and spectral domain characteristics. The objective is to address limitations in existing GANs that often fail to capture fine-grained details and realistic spectral properties, leading to artifacts and unrealistic textures. The proposed SSD-GAN incorporates a spectral discriminator alongside the conventional spatial discriminator. The spectral discriminator leverages Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to analyze and penalize inconsistencies in the frequency domain, guiding the generator to produce images with more authentic spectral signatures. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that SSD-GAN generates images with significantly improved visual quality, reduced artifacts, and more realistic textures compared to state-of-the-art GAN models. Quantitative evaluations, including Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and spectral metrics, confirm the superiority of SSD-GAN. The results highlight the importance of spectral domain analysis in GAN training and open new avenues for generating high-fidelity images for various applications.
Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, Spectral Analysis, Image Generation, Deep Learning, Discrete Cosine Transform, Image Quality.
