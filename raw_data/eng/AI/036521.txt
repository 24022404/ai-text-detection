Anomaly detection in images is crucial for various applications, including medical diagnosis, industrial quality control, and surveillance. This paper compares the performance of supervised and unsupervised deep learning methods for image anomaly detection. We investigate several state-of-the-art techniques, including a supervised convolutional neural network (CNN) trained on labeled anomalous and normal images, and unsupervised methods such as autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs) trained only on normal images. The models were evaluated on benchmark datasets with varying anomaly types and complexities, using metrics such as AUC, precision, and recall. Our results demonstrate that while supervised methods generally achieve higher accuracy when sufficient labeled data is available, unsupervised methods exhibit strong performance and robustness in scenarios with limited or no labeled anomaly data. Specifically, we found that GAN-based approaches effectively identify subtle anomalies due to their ability to learn the underlying distribution of normal images. The study highlights the trade-offs between the two approaches, providing guidance for selecting the most appropriate method based on data availability and application requirements. Keywords: Anomaly Detection, Deep Learning, Supervised Learning, Unsupervised Learning, Image Analysis, CNN, Autoencoder, GAN.
