Variational Continual Learning (VCL) addresses the challenge of training models on sequentially arriving data while mitigating catastrophic forgetting. This paper introduces a novel approach to improve VCL performance by enhancing the variational approximation and understanding its limitations. Our method integrates a task-aware prior and a dynamic capacity allocation mechanism within the variational inference framework. This allows for better retention of past knowledge and more efficient learning of new tasks. We empirically evaluate our approach on several benchmark continual learning datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in average accuracy and backward transfer compared to existing VCL methods. Furthermore, we provide a theoretical analysis of the forgetting dynamics in VCL, identifying key factors that contribute to performance degradation. The analysis suggests that optimizing the trade-off between plasticity and stability is crucial for effective continual learning. Keywords: Continual Learning, Variational Inference, Catastrophic Forgetting, Bayesian Learning, Neural Networks.
