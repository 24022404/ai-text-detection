The accurate identification of plant species through leaf recognition is a critical task in botany, agriculture, and environmental monitoring. This paper addresses the challenge of automating leaf recognition by leveraging the power of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). The proposed approach focuses on extracting discriminative features from leaf images using a CNN-based architecture, which is trained on a diverse dataset of leaf samples. The methodology involves preprocessing the images, designing a CNN model optimized for feature extraction, and employing a classification layer to predict species. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior accuracy compared to traditional feature-based techniques, with a significant reduction in computational complexity. The model’s performance is evaluated on benchmark datasets, showcasing its robustness and generalizability across different plant species. This research contributes to the field by introducing a novel CNN-based feature extraction framework that enhances the efficiency and accuracy of leaf recognition systems. The findings have practical implications for applications such as biodiversity conservation, precision agriculture, and automated plant classification.  

**Keywords**: Leaf recognition, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), feature extraction, plant species identification, image classification.
