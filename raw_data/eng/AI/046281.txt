Inverse problems, which involve inferring hidden causes from observed effects, are a fundamental challenge in fields such as image processing, medical diagnostics, and physics. Traditional deep learning methods often struggle with the ambiguities and high-dimensional mapping intrinsic to inverse problems. This paper investigates the application of invertible neural networks (INNs) to improve both the interpretability and accuracy of inverse problem solutions. INNs are designed to provide bijective mappings, enabling precise reconstruction of input data from outputs and facilitating the modeling of inverse processes. We present a comprehensive analysis of INN architectures, including their ability to capture uncertainty, retain information, and outperform conventional networks in handling ill-posed problems. Through experiments on benchmark datasets related to tomographic image reconstruction and signal source localization, our results demonstrate that INNs offer robust performance, yielding superior reconstruction quality and credible uncertainty quantification when compared to standard feedforward and autoencoder-based methods. We further discuss the implications of INNs for real-world inverse problem applications, highlighting enhanced generalizability, improved reliability, and potential integration into existing data analysis pipelines. This study underscores the promise of invertible neural networks as a powerful tool for advancing inverse problem-solving in information technology and related domains.

Keywords: inverse problems, invertible neural networks, uncertainty quantification, image reconstruction, deep learning, bijective mapping.
