Adder neural networks (AdderNets) offer significant computational efficiency by replacing energy-intensive multiplications with additions. However, training AdderNets effectively remains challenging, often resulting in accuracy degradation compared to conventional convolutional neural networks (CNNs). This paper introduces Kernel Based Progressive Distillation (KBPD), a novel training strategy designed to enhance the performance of AdderNets. KBPD leverages kernel methods to progressively transfer knowledge from a pre-trained CNN teacher network to the AdderNet student network. Specifically, the kernel alignment loss function guides the AdderNet to mimic the feature map activation patterns of the CNN, focusing on preserving crucial spatial relationships. This progressive distillation process avoids sudden shifts in the student network's learning trajectory, leading to more stable and effective training. Experimental results on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet datasets demonstrate that KBPD significantly improves the accuracy of AdderNets, closing the performance gap with CNNs. Furthermore, KBPD showcases improved energy efficiency over standard training methods for AdderNets. The proposed method offers a promising approach to deploy efficient AdderNets in resource-constrained environments.

**Keywords:** Adder Neural Networks, Knowledge Distillation, Kernel Methods, Convolutional Neural Networks, Energy Efficiency, Deep Learning.
