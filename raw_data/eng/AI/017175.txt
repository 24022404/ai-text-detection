This paper investigates the behavior of deep neural networks (DNNs) under imbalanced training scenarios, focusing on the phenomenon of minority collapse. The objective is to understand how class imbalance affects the learning dynamics and performance of DNNs, particularly in the context of minority classes. To achieve this, we introduce the Layer-Peeled Model (LPM), a simplified yet insightful framework that captures the essential dynamics of DNN training. Using LPM, we analyze the gradient flow and feature representations of minority and majority classes, revealing a critical transition point where minority classes collapse—losing their discriminative power—while majority classes dominate the model's predictions. Our findings demonstrate that minority collapse is a direct consequence of imbalanced training data and can significantly degrade model performance on underrepresented classes. Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, we validate the theoretical insights provided by LPM and propose a novel regularization technique to mitigate minority collapse. The results show improved generalization and fairness in imbalanced classification tasks. This work contributes to a deeper understanding of DNN training dynamics and offers practical solutions for addressing class imbalance in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: deep neural networks, minority collapse, imbalanced training, Layer-Peeled Model, regularization, fairness, classification.
