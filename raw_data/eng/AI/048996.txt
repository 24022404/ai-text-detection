This paper explores the intersection of machine learning (ML) and control theory, addressing the challenge of integrating data-driven ML techniques with traditional model-based control systems. The objective is to enhance the adaptability and efficiency of control systems in dynamic and uncertain environments. We propose a hybrid approach that combines reinforcement learning (RL) with optimal control strategies, leveraging the strengths of both paradigms. The methodology involves training RL agents using simulated environments derived from control theory models, followed by real-world validation. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in system performance, including faster convergence, reduced error rates, and enhanced robustness compared to conventional methods. The findings highlight the potential of this approach in applications such as autonomous vehicles, robotics, and industrial automation. This research contributes to the growing body of work on intelligent control systems, offering a novel framework that bridges the gap between data-driven and model-based methodologies. Key innovations include the integration of RL with model predictive control (MPC) and the development of a scalable training pipeline. The implications of this work extend to the broader adoption of AI-driven solutions in real-time control systems, paving the way for more adaptive and resilient technologies.  

**Keywords**: machine learning, control theory, reinforcement learning, optimal control, model predictive control, autonomous systems.
