This paper investigates the theoretical foundations and limitations of adversarial training for predictive tasks, focusing on the deterministic case. The objective is to analyze the effectiveness of adversarial training in enhancing model robustness and generalization. We employ a mathematical approach to examine the underlying mechanisms of adversarial training, exploring its relationship with regularization techniques and margin theory. Our analysis reveals that, in the deterministic setting, adversarial training can be viewed as a form of robust optimization, which leads to improved model performance on unseen data. However, we also identify fundamental limitations, including the potential for over-regularization and the sensitivity of adversarial training to hyperparameter tuning. The results of our theoretical analysis are supported by empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets, demonstrating the trade-offs between model robustness and accuracy. This research contributes to the understanding of adversarial training, highlighting its potential benefits and limitations, and provides insights for the development of more effective and efficient training methods for predictive tasks, with key applications in areas such as machine learning, deep learning, and AI model development.
