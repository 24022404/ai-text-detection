This paper presents a novel approach to document layout analysis, incorporating human-in-the-loop feedback to improve the accuracy and robustness of layout extraction. The objective is to develop a system that can effectively identify and classify document elements, such as text, images, and tables, in a variety of document formats. Our approach utilizes a combination of computer vision and machine learning techniques, leveraging user feedback to refine the layout analysis model. The system is trained on a large dataset of annotated documents, and user corrections are incorporated to adapt the model to new document types and layouts. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in layout extraction accuracy, outperforming state-of-the-art methods. The proposed system has important implications for document understanding, information retrieval, and digital archiving, enabling more efficient and effective processing of large document collections. Key contributions include the development of a human-in-the-loop framework for document layout analysis, and the demonstration of improved performance through user feedback and model adaptation. Relevant keywords: document layout analysis, human-in-the-loop, computer vision, machine learning, document understanding.
