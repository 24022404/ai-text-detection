Outlier detection plays a critical role in ensuring the reliability and robustness of decision-making systems, particularly within the context of multi-armed bandit (MAB) problems commonly found in online recommendation, adaptive experimentation, and real-time optimization. This paper presents a novel, generic framework for outlier detection in multi-armed bandit settings, addressing the limitations of existing methods that often lack flexibility and robustness across diverse environments. The proposed approach integrates statistical modeling and adaptive learning within the MAB paradigm to effectively identify anomalous arms—those that deviate significantly from expected reward distributions—without prior knowledge of arm behavior. Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our method accurately detects outliers and mitigates their negative impact on cumulative reward and arm selection strategies. Comparative analysis reveals that our framework outperforms traditional MAB algorithms and specialized outlier detectors in terms of detection accuracy, computational efficiency, and versatility. The solution is scalable, model-agnostic, and readily adaptable to various MAB applications, supporting continuous online monitoring and dynamic policy adjustment. These findings advance the field by introducing a unified and effective approach to outlier detection in bandit systems, with direct implications for improving performance and reliability in adaptive learning and automated decision processes. Key keywords: multi-armed bandit, outlier detection, anomaly detection, adaptive learning, online algorithms, reward distribution, robustness.
