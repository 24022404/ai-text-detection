Abstract:

This paper explores the integration of cinematic rendering, a technique that generates photorealistic visualizations from medical imaging data, with deep learning to enhance the performance of deep neural networks in medical image analysis. The primary objective is to address the challenge of limited and variable-quality annotated data by leveraging highly detailed and anatomically accurate images produced through cinematic rendering. We present a novel approach where state-of-the-art convolutional neural networks are fine-tuned on rendered images derived from computed tomography (CT) scans, offering a more naturalistic and diverse representation of medical conditions. Empirical evaluation is conducted on classification and segmentation tasks using both synthetic cinematic images and traditional grayscale inputs. Our results demonstrate that networks trained with cinematic renderings outperform baseline models, exhibiting improved accuracy, robustness to noise, and better generalization to unseen datasets. The findings indicate that photorealistic augmentation not only compensates for data scarcity but also enhances feature extraction from complex anatomical structures. This research underscores the potential of combining advanced rendering techniques with deep learning to create more effective and reliable medical image analysis tools, with implications for diagnostics, surgical planning, and AI-driven decision support. 

Keywords: deep learning, cinematic rendering, medical imaging, neural networks, transfer learning, photorealistic augmentation, image analysis, CT scans.
