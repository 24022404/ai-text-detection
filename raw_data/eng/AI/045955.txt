This paper addresses the challenge of identifying active market participants—such as institutions, individual traders, or automated agents—based solely on observed financial time series data. We propose a novel framework leveraging adversarial calibration of multi-agent simulators to uncover the composition and behavior of participants driving market dynamics. Our approach constructs a multi-agent market simulator, parameterizing distinct trading strategies and participant profiles, and utilizes an adversarial learning mechanism to iteratively calibrate the simulator against real market data. Through this process, the simulator learns to reproduce time series statistics that closely match those observed, thereby revealing plausible configurations of market actors. Experimental evaluation on synthetic and real-world financial data demonstrates that our method achieves superior accuracy in participant identification compared to conventional clustering and regression-based time series analysis techniques. Furthermore, we analyze the robustness and interpretability of the discovered agents, showing that the calibrated simulator not only fits statistical features but also yields meaningful insights into strategic market behavior. The results highlight the potential of adversarially-calibrated multi-agent models for market structure analysis, regulatory supervision, and algorithmic trading strategy design. Keywords: time series analysis, multi-agent simulation, adversarial learning, market participant discovery, financial data, agent-based modeling, simulator calibration.
