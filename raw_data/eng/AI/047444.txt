Person re-identification (Re-ID) across different domains remains a challenging task due to significant variations in lighting, viewpoints, and backgrounds. This paper proposes an End-to-End Domain Adaptive Attention Network (EDAAN) to address cross-domain person Re-ID by effectively aligning feature distributions and enhancing discriminative feature learning. The proposed framework integrates a domain adaptation module with an attention mechanism to minimize domain discrepancies while focusing on salient regions of person images. Specifically, EDAAN employs adversarial training to align source and target domain features, coupled with a multi-scale attention network to capture fine-grained details and spatial dependencies. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including Market-1501, DukeMTMC-reID, and MSMT17, demonstrate that EDAAN achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in cross-domain scenarios. The results highlight the effectiveness of the attention mechanism in improving feature robustness and the domain adaptation module in reducing inter-domain gaps. This work contributes a novel, scalable solution for real-world applications such as surveillance and security, where domain shifts are prevalent.  

**Keywords**: Person re-identification, domain adaptation, attention mechanism, adversarial learning, cross-domain learning.
