In this paper, we address the challenge of text recognition within images by proposing a novel unsupervised learning approach that leverages spelling as a mechanism for reading. The objective is to develop a system capable of accurately recognizing and interpreting textual content without the need for extensive labeled datasets, which are often scarce and costly to obtain. Our approach employs a combination of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to autonomously learn character representations and spelling patterns from unannotated text images. We conducted extensive experiments on multiple datasets, demonstrating that our model outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of recognition accuracy and efficiency. The findings reveal that leveraging spelling not only enhances performance but also improves the model's ability to generalize across diverse text fonts and backgrounds. This research contributes significant insights into unsupervised learning paradigms and text recognition technology, with potential applications in augmented reality, mobile scanning, and accessibility tools. Our approach represents a step forward in creating adaptable learning systems that can facilitate reading comprehension in varied contexts, highlighting the potential for future advancements in unsupervised machine learning for visual text analytics. 

Keywords: unsupervised learning, text recognition, spelling, neural networks, computer vision.
