This paper addresses the challenge of generating realistic and reliable process event streams and time series data, which are essential for developing and evaluating information systems, process mining algorithms, and data-driven applications. We propose a neural network-based framework that leverages deep learning techniques to model and synthesize event-driven and temporal data with complex dependencies and sequential patterns. The approach combines recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and attention mechanisms to capture long-range temporal correlations and varying event inter-arrival times typical in process event logs and time series. Extensive experiments were conducted using diverse benchmark datasets from manufacturing, finance, and IT system monitoring domains. Results demonstrate that the proposed model outperforms traditional synthetic data generators and baseline machine learning techniques in terms of accuracy, fidelity, and variability of generated data. Furthermore, the generated event streams preserve the statistical and temporal characteristics necessary for downstream analytics and process discovery tasks. This work provides a scalable solution for generating high-quality process event streams and time series, enabling more robust benchmarking, synthetic data generation, and privacy-preserving analytics across multiple IT domains. Keywords: process event streams, synthetic data generation, neural networks, time series, deep learning, process mining, data augmentation.
