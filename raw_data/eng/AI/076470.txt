This paper proposes a novel approach to video representation learning, focusing on the decomposition of long short view features via contrastive learning. The objective is to enhance the understanding and representation of complex video data by disentangling short-term and long-term features. Our method employs a contrastive learning framework that leverages both spatial and temporal information to learn distinctive and informative video representations. The approach utilizes a multi-scale feature decomposition strategy, allowing for the effective capture of both short-term motions and long-term dependencies within videos. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our proposed method, achieving state-of-the-art performance on various video understanding benchmarks. The key findings highlight the importance of decomposing long short view features for improved video representation learning. This research contributes to the advancement of video analysis and understanding, with potential applications in areas such as action recognition, video summarization, and autonomous systems. Key keywords: contrastive learning, video representation, feature decomposition, long short view, video understanding.
