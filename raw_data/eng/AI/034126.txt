Face Anti-Spoofing (FAS) is crucial for securing face recognition systems against presentation attacks. While existing methods primarily focus on spatial features, temporal dynamics indicative of genuine liveness are often overlooked. This paper proposes a novel approach to enhance motion cues for FAS by integrating a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) with a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network. The CNN extracts frame-level spatial features, while the LSTM models temporal dependencies to capture subtle motion patterns indicative of spoofing attacks. We introduce a motion magnification technique to further amplify these subtle cues, improving the LSTM's ability to distinguish between real and fake faces. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including CASIA-SURF CeFA and Replay-Attack, demonstrate that our CNN-LSTM architecture significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods. The results highlight the importance of incorporating motion cues for robust FAS, with our approach achieving a 5% improvement in Average Classification Error Rate (ACER) compared to existing spatial-based methods. This research contributes a more effective and robust FAS solution, enhancing the security of face recognition systems.
Keywords: Face Anti-Spoofing, CNN, LSTM, Motion Cues, Deep Learning, Presentation Attack Detection.
