Unsupervised visual representation learning is critical for leveraging the vast amount of unlabeled video data. This paper introduces a novel approach for learning visual representations by tracking patches within video sequences. The method focuses on predicting the future location of randomly sampled image patches, forcing the network to learn spatio-temporal relationships and motion patterns. A Siamese network architecture is employed, trained with a contrastive loss function to distinguish between correctly and incorrectly tracked patches. Experimental results on benchmark video datasets demonstrate that the learned representations achieve competitive performance on downstream tasks, including action recognition and object detection, compared to existing unsupervised and self-supervised methods. Notably, our approach excels in capturing fine-grained motion cues, leading to improved accuracy in action classification tasks. Key innovations include a dynamic patch selection strategy and a robust occlusion handling mechanism. This work contributes a scalable and effective method for unsupervised visual representation learning, paving the way for more efficient video understanding models. Keywords: Unsupervised Learning, Visual Representation Learning, Video Analysis, Patch Tracking, Siamese Networks, Action Recognition.
