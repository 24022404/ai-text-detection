This research addresses the problem of change-point detection in time series data, a critical task for identifying structural changes that may impact system performance, decision-making, and analysis in various domains. Traditional methods often rely on indirect approaches that do not efficiently handle the complexity of real-world datasets.

Methods/Approach: Our approach leverages direct density ratio estimation to generalize change-point detection techniques, offering a more robust and precise methodology. By utilizing this innovative technique, we eliminate the need for explicit probability density estimation, thus reducing complexity and enhancing computational efficiency. This method identifies changes in the data distribution directly from time series observations, without assumptions about the underlying data distribution.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our approach across multiple benchmark datasets, showcasing improved performance in detecting change-points with higher accuracy compared to traditional methods. Specifically, our method significantly reduces false detection rates and enhances the sensitivity to actual structural changes within the data.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed method provides a novel contribution to the field of time series analysis by enhancing the accuracy and reliability of change-point detection through direct density ratio estimation. This advancement holds potential applications in various industries, including finance, healthcare, and environmental monitoring, where timely and accurate detection of changes can lead to better decision-making processes. The generalization of this technique can further catalyze development in real-time monitoring systems and anomaly detection frameworks.

Key Keywords: Change-Point Detection, Time Series, Direct Density Ratio Estimation, Structural Changes, Data Distribution, Anomaly Detection.
