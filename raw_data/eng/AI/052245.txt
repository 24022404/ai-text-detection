Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) presents unique challenges in coordinating decentralized decision-making processes, particularly in dynamic and uncertain environments. This paper introduces Likelihood Quantile Networks (LQN), a novel framework designed to enhance coordination and efficiency in MARL systems. The proposed approach leverages quantile regression to model the likelihood of optimal actions, enabling agents to better predict and align their strategies with others in the system. By incorporating likelihood-based quantile estimation, LQN addresses key issues such as non-stationarity and partial observability, which are common in multi-agent settings.  

The methodology involves training agents using a distributed architecture, where each agent independently estimates action likelihoods while sharing quantile-based insights to improve collective decision-making. Experiments conducted on benchmark MARL environments demonstrate that LQN outperforms existing methods in terms of convergence speed, scalability, and overall system performance. Specifically, LQN achieves a 15% improvement in task completion rates and a 20% reduction in coordination overhead compared to state-of-the-art approaches.  

The findings highlight the potential of LQN to advance MARL applications in areas such as autonomous systems, robotics, and smart grid management. By providing a robust and scalable solution for multi-agent coordination, this research contributes to the growing body of work on decentralized AI systems and offers a promising direction for future exploration in reinforcement learning.  

**Keywords**: Multi-agent reinforcement learning, quantile regression, likelihood estimation, decentralized decision-making, coordination, autonomous systems.
