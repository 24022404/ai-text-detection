This paper presents a unified framework for understanding explicit and implicit feature maps of graph kernels, addressing the long-standing problem of disparate representations in graph-based machine learning. Our approach leverages a novel mathematical formulation to reconcile the two paradigms, providing a clear and concise methodology for analyzing and comparing graph kernels. We employ a combination of algebraic and geometric techniques to derive a comprehensive theory that elucidates the relationships between explicit and implicit feature maps. Our results demonstrate that this unifying view enables the development of more efficient and effective graph kernel algorithms, with significant improvements in computational complexity and predictive performance. The implications of this research are far-reaching, with potential applications in graph-based clustering, classification, and regression tasks, particularly in domains such as social network analysis, bioinformatics, and computer vision. Key contributions include a deeper understanding of the interplay between explicit and implicit feature maps, as well as the introduction of novel graph kernel constructions that exploit this unified perspective. Relevant keywords: graph kernels, feature maps, machine learning, graph-based algorithms, kernel methods.
