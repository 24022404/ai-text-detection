This paper presents a novel approach to image super-resolution by leveraging a Frequency Domain Neural Network (FDNN), aimed at accelerating reconstruction while maintaining high visual quality. The core objective is to address the computational bottlenecks of traditional spatial domain deep learning methods, which often involve redundant processing and substantial memory requirements. The proposed FDNN transforms input images into the frequency domain, enabling the model to efficiently capture and enhance high-frequency details essential for sharp image reconstruction. Our architecture integrates adaptive frequency filtering and multi-scale feature extraction to effectively reconstruct high-resolution images from low-resolution counterparts. We conducted extensive experiments on standard image super-resolution benchmarks, comparing our method with leading spatial domain convolutional neural networks. Results demonstrate that our FDNN achieves comparable or superior quantitative performance in terms of Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), while significantly reducing inference time and computational cost. The findings highlight the potential of frequency domain processing for scalable and real-time image enhancement tasks. This research contributes a new direction for deep learning-based super-resolution, opening avenues for fast and efficient deployment in resource-constrained environments.  
Keywords: image super-resolution, frequency domain neural network, deep learning, real-time processing, high-frequency enhancement, computational efficiency.
