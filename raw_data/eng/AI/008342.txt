Abstract:  
Textual editing of images—modifying visual content based on natural language instructions—has emerged as a compelling task at the intersection of computer vision and natural language processing. This paper addresses the challenge of accurately aligning user-provided text with corresponding visual regions in images, enabling intuitive and fine-grained manipulations. We propose a novel multimodal affinity learning framework that jointly models the semantic relationships between textual descriptions and image components. Our approach leverages a cross-modal attention mechanism that dynamically computes affinities between word-level features and image segments, facilitating precise localization and editing actions. The method is trained on a curated dataset comprising diverse textual-image pairs, enabling robust generalization to unseen instructions and image types. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our model significantly outperforms existing baselines on multiple benchmarks, achieving improved fidelity in both semantic alignment and editing accuracy. The results further indicate enhanced user controllability and naturalness in generated edits, reflecting the effectiveness of our affinity-driven approach. Our contributions advance the state-of-the-art in multimodal understanding and lay a strong foundation for practical applications in intelligent image editing, creative content generation, and human-computer interaction.  
Keywords: multimodal learning, text-to-image editing, affinity modeling, cross-modal attention, computer vision, natural language processing
