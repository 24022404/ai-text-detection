This paper addresses the challenge of imbalanced corporate credit ratings by introducing a novel contrastive pre-training approach. The objective is to improve the accuracy and robustness of credit rating models in the presence of class imbalance, where the majority of companies have high credit ratings and only a few have low ratings. Our method leverages contrastive learning to pre-train a model on a large dataset of corporate financial statements, learning to distinguish between similar and dissimilar companies. The pre-trained model is then fine-tuned on a smaller labeled dataset of credit ratings, demonstrating significant improvements in predictive performance. Our results show that the contrastive pre-training approach outperforms traditional methods, particularly for companies with low credit ratings. The key contributions of this research include the application of contrastive learning to imbalanced credit rating datasets and the development of a more robust and generalizable credit rating model. This study has important implications for risk management and lending decisions in the financial sector, and highlights the potential of contrastive pre-training for addressing class imbalance in other domains. Key keywords: contrastive learning, corporate credit ratings, class imbalance, pre-training, financial risk management.
