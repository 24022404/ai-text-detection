This paper presents a novel approach to self-supervised learning, focusing on the development of pretext-invariant representations. The objective is to learn robust and generalizable features that are not biased towards specific pretext tasks, but rather capture the underlying structure of the data. Our method employs a unique combination of contrastive learning and adversarial training to achieve this goal. By leveraging a pretext-invariant loss function, our model learns to extract features that are invariant to different pretext tasks, resulting in improved performance on downstream tasks. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, outperforming existing self-supervised learning methods on several benchmark datasets. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of pretext-invariant representations in self-supervised learning, with potential applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and other areas of artificial intelligence. Our contributions include a new perspective on self-supervised learning, a novel loss function, and a state-of-the-art approach to learning pretext-invariant representations, with relevant keywords including self-supervised learning, pretext-invariant representations, contrastive learning, and adversarial training.
