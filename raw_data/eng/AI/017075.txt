This paper introduces CityLearn, a novel framework designed to standardize research in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) for demand response and urban energy management. The objective is to address the complexity of urban energy systems by developing a unified platform that enables the evaluation and comparison of different MARL algorithms and strategies. CityLearn utilizes a modular architecture, incorporating realistic models of buildings, energy grids, and weather patterns, to simulate the dynamic interactions between multiple agents in urban environments. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of CityLearn in facilitating the development of scalable and adaptive MARL solutions, outperforming traditional methods in reducing energy consumption and peak demand. The key findings highlight the importance of standardized evaluation metrics and scenarios in MARL research, enabling fair comparisons and accelerating progress in the field. CityLearn contributes to the advancement of urban energy management by providing a widely applicable and extensible framework, with potential applications in smart cities, grid management, and sustainable development. Relevant keywords: multi-agent reinforcement learning, demand response, urban energy management, smart cities, energy efficiency.
