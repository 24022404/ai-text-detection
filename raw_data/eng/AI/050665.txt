This paper addresses the challenge of action recognition and domain adaptation in 3D body skeletons using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). The objective is to develop a robust framework that can accurately recognize human actions and adapt to new environments with limited labeled data. Our approach utilizes kernel feature maps to extract relevant features from 3D skeleton data, which are then fed into a CNN-based architecture for action recognition. We also propose a supervised domain adaptation method that leverages kernel feature maps to align the source and target domains, resulting in improved adaptation performance. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. The key findings of this research include the importance of kernel feature maps in capturing discriminative information from 3D skeletons and the benefits of supervised domain adaptation in reducing the gap between source and target domains. This work contributes to the development of more accurate and adaptable action recognition systems, with potential applications in human-computer interaction, healthcare, and surveillance. Key keywords: CNN, action recognition, domain adaptation, 3D body skeletons, kernel feature maps.
