Understanding user behavior and preferences is critical for enhancing personalized recommendations in online platforms. Traditional recommendation systems often rely on explicit feedback, such as ratings, which can be sparse and unreliable. This paper addresses the challenge of leveraging implicit feedback, such as clicks and browsing patterns, to model sequential user behaviors and capture both long-term and short-term preferences. We propose a novel deep neural network architecture that integrates temporal dynamics and hierarchical attention mechanisms to effectively process sequential data. The model is designed to learn from implicit interactions, enabling it to predict user preferences more accurately over time. Experiments conducted on real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of recommendation accuracy and user engagement metrics. The results highlight the model's ability to adapt to evolving user interests and provide actionable insights for improving personalized recommendations. This research contributes to the advancement of recommendation systems by bridging the gap between implicit and explicit feedback, offering a scalable solution for real-world applications in e-commerce, streaming platforms, and social media.  

**Keywords**: implicit feedback, sequential behavior modeling, deep neural networks, recommendation systems, long-short term preferences, attention mechanisms.
