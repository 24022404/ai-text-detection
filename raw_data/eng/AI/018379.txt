Distribution inference, where an adversary aims to infer properties of a training dataset given access to a trained model, poses a significant risk to data privacy. This paper formalizes the distribution inference problem within a rigorous statistical framework, defining novel metrics to quantify the risk associated with different inference attacks. We introduce a new attack strategy leveraging hypothesis testing based on likelihood ratios, demonstrating its effectiveness against various machine learning models, including those trained with differential privacy. We derive theoretical bounds on the sample complexity required for successful distribution inference, highlighting the factors influencing vulnerability. Empirical evaluations on image classification and natural language processing tasks validate our theoretical findings, showing that even moderately sized models can leak sensitive information about the training data distribution. Our results provide a practical framework for assessing and mitigating distribution inference risks, contributing to the development of more privacy-preserving machine learning techniques. Keywords: distribution inference, privacy, machine learning, adversarial attacks, hypothesis testing, differential privacy.
