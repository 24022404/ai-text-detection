Generating labeled graphs is a fundamental challenge with applications spanning drug discovery, social network analysis, and knowledge graph construction. Existing graph generation models often struggle with scalability and computational efficiency, especially when dealing with large, complex graphs with rich node and edge labels. This paper introduces GraphGen-Redux, a novel recurrent neural network architecture designed for fast and lightweight labeled graph generation. GraphGen-Redux employs a sequential node-and-edge generation process conditioned on previously generated elements, enabling efficient parallelization and reduced memory footprint. The model incorporates a customized attention mechanism to capture long-range dependencies within the graph structure. Experimental results on benchmark graph datasets demonstrate that GraphGen-Redux achieves significant speedups in generation time and improved scalability compared to state-of-the-art methods, while maintaining competitive graph quality. Furthermore, ablation studies validate the effectiveness of the proposed attention mechanism and parallelization strategy. GraphGen-Redux offers a practical solution for generating large-scale labeled graphs, opening avenues for broader adoption in resource-constrained environments.
Keywords: Graph Generation, Recurrent Neural Networks, Labeled Graphs, Scalability, Parallelization, Attention Mechanism.
