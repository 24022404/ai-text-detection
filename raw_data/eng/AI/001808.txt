The CAMELS project (Cosmological and Astrophysical Magnetohydrodynamics Environment and Learning Simulations) aims to explore the connection between cosmological parameters and astrophysical processes through a suite of large-volume, diverse hydrodynamical simulations. This work introduces the CAMELS Multifield Dataset, comprised of over 4,000 simulations generated with distinct cosmological and astrophysical parameters using the AREPO and GIZMO codes. We leverage this dataset to train machine learning models, specifically convolutional neural networks, to predict fundamental cosmological parameters, such as the matter density and the amplitude of the power spectrum, directly from different simulated observational data, including weak lensing maps, Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect maps, and X-ray temperature maps. Our results demonstrate the feasibility of accurately inferring cosmological parameters from these multifield datasets, achieving competitive results with traditional methods. Furthermore, we investigate the degeneracy between cosmological and astrophysical parameters, identifying the most influential astrophysical processes affecting the inference accuracy. This approach provides a powerful tool for understanding the complex interplay between cosmology and astrophysics, paving the way for improved constraints on the universe's fundamental parameters from upcoming observational surveys. Keywords: Cosmology, Astrophysics, Machine Learning, Simulations, Parameter Estimation, Multifield Data.
