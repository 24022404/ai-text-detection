This paper introduces HarperValleyBank, a novel domain-specific spoken dialog corpus designed to facilitate research in spoken language understanding and dialog systems. The objective of this work is to provide a comprehensive dataset that captures the nuances of human-computer interactions in a banking domain, enabling the development of more accurate and effective spoken dialog systems. Our approach involves collecting and annotating a large corpus of spoken dialogs between customers and banking representatives, utilizing a combination of crowdsourcing and expert annotation methods. The resulting corpus comprises over 10,000 dialogs, each annotated with intent labels, slot values, and dialog acts. Our evaluation demonstrates the corpus's potential to improve spoken language understanding models, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark tasks. The HarperValleyBank corpus contributes to the field by providing a unique resource for training and testing spoken dialog systems, with applications in virtual assistants, customer service chatbots, and voice-controlled interfaces. Key features of the corpus include its domain specificity, large size, and high-quality annotations, making it an invaluable resource for researchers and developers in the field of spoken language processing, dialog systems, and human-computer interaction.
