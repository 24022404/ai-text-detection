This paper introduces RECS, a novel approach to robust graph embedding that leverages connection subgraphs to enhance the representation of complex networks. The objective is to develop a method that can effectively capture the structural properties of graphs and improve the robustness of graph embeddings against noise and missing data. RECS achieves this by constructing connection subgraphs that preserve the local and global topology of the original graph, and then learning node representations using a graph neural network. Experimental results demonstrate that RECS outperforms state-of-the-art graph embedding methods in terms of node classification, link prediction, and graph clustering tasks, especially in the presence of noisy or missing data. The key contributions of RECS lie in its ability to handle incomplete and noisy graphs, and its scalability to large-scale networks. This research has important implications for various applications, including social network analysis, recommendation systems, and network biology, where robust graph representations are crucial. Key keywords: graph embedding, connection subgraphs, graph neural networks, robust representation learning, network analysis.
