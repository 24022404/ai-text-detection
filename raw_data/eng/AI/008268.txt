The paper addresses the issue of sparsity bias in training neural networks, particularly when zero imputation is used as a default method for handling missing data. Zero imputation, while convenient, can introduce biases that negatively affect model performance and predictive accuracy.

Methods/Approach: We propose alternative strategies for managing sparsity in neural network datasets, focusing on strategies that better preserve underlying data distributions and relationships. The techniques explored include mean imputation, k-nearest neighbors imputation, and a novel adaptive imputation approach that leverages data patterns learned through neural network architectures.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that the alternative imputation methods significantly enhance the performance of neural networks compared to zero imputation. Specifically, the adaptive imputation method shows improved predictive accuracy, reducing error rates by up to 15% across various benchmark datasets. A thorough comparison with conventional imputation techniques showcases the superior capability of the proposed methods in mitigating sparsity bias.

Conclusion/Implications: This research highlights the limitations of zero imputation in neural network training and emphasizes the importance of selecting appropriate imputation methods to correct sparsity bias. By advancing imputation techniques, our work opens avenues for more robust and accurate neural network models, with potential applications in domains heavily reliant on incomplete datasets, such as healthcare and finance. These findings encourage further exploration into adaptive imputation methods to enhance neural network training paradigms.

Keywords: sparsity bias, zero imputation, neural networks, adaptive imputation, data distributions, predictive accuracy, machine learning.
