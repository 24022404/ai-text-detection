Continual learning (CL) aims to enable models to learn new tasks sequentially without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. This paper addresses inconsistencies and ambiguities in the current landscape of CL scenario definitions. We propose a refined categorization of CL scenarios, distinguishing between task-incremental, domain-incremental, and class-incremental learning based on clear data and task boundary definitions. Furthermore, we demonstrate that many existing CL methods are not rigorously compared against strong, well-tuned baselines. Through extensive experiments across several benchmark datasets and using a unified evaluation protocol, we show that simple regularization techniques and replay strategies, when optimized, can achieve competitive or superior performance compared to more complex CL algorithms. Our findings underscore the importance of establishing robust baselines for future CL research and advocate for a more standardized and rigorous evaluation framework. This work provides valuable insights for researchers and practitioners in the field, promoting more meaningful progress in continual learning. Keywords: Continual Learning, Catastrophic Forgetting, Baselines, Evaluation, Benchmarking, Incremental Learning.
