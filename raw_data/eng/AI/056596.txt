Abstract:

Accurate detection of small objects in thermal images is crucial for various applications, including surveillance, wildlife monitoring, and search and rescue operations. However, the low resolution and distinct characteristics of thermal imagery present significant challenges for existing object detection algorithms. This paper proposes an enhanced approach for identifying small objects in thermal images by leveraging the Single-Shot Detector (SSD) architecture. Our method integrates specialized data augmentation techniques and multi-scale feature extraction to improve sensitivity to small object features without compromising real-time performance. Extensive experiments were conducted on public thermal image datasets, demonstrating that the proposed model outperforms baseline SSD and other state-of-the-art detectors in terms of precision, recall, and mean average precision (mAP) metrics, particularly for small-sized objects. The results highlight the effectiveness and robustness of our approach in challenging thermal imaging conditions. The proposed detection framework offers practical benefits for real-world thermal vision systems, facilitating improved situational awareness and automated analysis in safety-critical environments. Keywords: small object detection, thermal images, Single-Shot Detector, deep learning, computer vision, feature extraction.
