This paper addresses the challenge of reversible image merging, a critical task in low-level machine vision applications such as image compression, data hiding, and secure communication. The objective is to develop a method that allows multiple images to be merged into a single composite image while ensuring the original images can be fully reconstructed without loss of information. To achieve this, we propose a novel reversible image merging framework based on a combination of wavelet transforms and adaptive pixel mapping. The approach leverages the multi-resolution properties of wavelets to preserve image details and employs an adaptive mapping algorithm to optimize the merging process. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves high-quality image reconstruction with minimal distortion, outperforming existing techniques in terms of both visual fidelity and computational efficiency. The framework also supports scalability, enabling the merging of multiple images with varying resolutions. This research contributes to the advancement of reversible image processing techniques, offering potential applications in secure data transmission, medical imaging, and multimedia storage. Key innovations include the integration of wavelet-based decomposition with adaptive mapping, ensuring robustness and efficiency in low-level machine vision tasks. Keywords: reversible image merging, wavelet transform, adaptive pixel mapping, low-level machine vision, image reconstruction.
