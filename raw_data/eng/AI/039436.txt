Accurate 3D object detection is critical for autonomous driving and robotics, yet achieving robust performance remains challenging due to the limitations of individual sensor modalities. This paper introduces **CLOCs (Camera-LiDAR Object Candidates Fusion)**, a novel framework designed to effectively fuse object detection candidates from camera and LiDAR sensors for improved 3D object detection. Unlike traditional fusion methods that rely on raw sensor data, CLOCs operates on the output of independent 2D and 3D detection networks, leveraging their complementary strengths. The proposed approach employs a lightweight, learnable fusion network that combines confidence scores and spatial information from both modalities to generate refined detection results. Experiments on the KITTI dataset demonstrate that CLOCs significantly enhances detection accuracy, particularly for challenging cases such as distant or occluded objects, while maintaining computational efficiency. Compared to state-of-the-art methods, CLOCs achieves superior performance in terms of precision and recall, with minimal additional computational overhead. The framework’s modular design allows seamless integration with existing detection pipelines, making it a practical solution for real-world applications. This work highlights the potential of late-stage fusion strategies in multi-sensor perception systems and provides a foundation for future advancements in 3D object detection.  

**Keywords**: 3D object detection, sensor fusion, LiDAR, camera, autonomous driving, multi-modal perception, CLOCs.
