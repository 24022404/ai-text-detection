Deepfake technology has brought profound implications for information integrity and security, necessitating effective attribution methods to discern authenticity. This paper addresses the problem of identifying and attributing deepfakes, which are synthetic media depicting realistic but manipulated content, often used maliciously. We propose a novel framework that leverages advanced machine learning algorithms, specifically convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and generative adversarial networks (GANs), to detect subtle artifacts indicative of deepfakes. Our approach surpasses traditional forensic techniques by incorporating feature extraction and identity verification processes specifically tailored for deepfake recognition. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in accuracy and robustness against adversarial attacks compared to existing methods. Furthermore, our system's scalability and real-time processing capability suggest its applicability in various real-world scenarios, including social media monitoring and digital forensics. The research contributes to the burgeoning field of synthetic media detection by providing a comprehensive solution for deepfake attribution. Our findings underscore the importance of developing sophisticated technological defenses as a means to uphold digital authenticity and trust. Keywords: Deepfake, Attribution, Convolutional Neural Networks, Generative Adversarial Networks, Digital Forensics.
