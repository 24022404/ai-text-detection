The sim-to-real transfer of robot learning has emerged as a critical challenge in deploying robotic systems in real-world environments. This paper addresses the problem of enabling robots to generalize learned behaviors from simulation to reality, particularly when dealing with variable-length inputs, such as sensor data or task-specific sequences. We propose a novel framework that leverages adaptive neural architectures and domain randomization techniques to bridge the gap between simulated and real-world dynamics. Our approach introduces a dynamic input processing mechanism capable of handling inputs of varying lengths, ensuring robust performance across diverse scenarios. Through extensive experiments, we demonstrate that our method achieves significant improvements in transferability, outperforming existing approaches in terms of task success rates and adaptability. The results highlight the framework's ability to generalize across different environments and tasks, even with limited real-world training data. This research contributes to advancing the field of robot learning by providing a scalable solution for sim-to-real transfer, with potential applications in industrial automation, healthcare robotics, and autonomous systems.  

**Keywords**: sim-to-real transfer, robot learning, variable-length inputs, domain randomization, adaptive neural networks, robotics.
