This paper proposes a novel approach to training Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) by integrating self-labeling and self-attention mechanisms. The objective is to improve the stability and efficiency of GAN training, addressing the longstanding challenges of mode collapse and unstable convergence. Our method employs a self-labeling technique to generate informative labels for the discriminator, while a self-attention module enables the generator to focus on relevant features and produce more realistic samples. Experimental results demonstrate that our unified framework outperforms existing GAN training methods, achieving superior image quality and diversity. The key findings include improved inception scores, reduced training time, and enhanced robustness to hyperparameter settings. Our approach contributes to the advancement of GAN research, offering a promising direction for applications in image synthesis, data augmentation, and AI-generated content. The proposed methodology highlights the importance of self-supervised learning and attention mechanisms in deep generative models, with potential implications for the development of more sophisticated AI models. Key keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, Self-Labeling, Self-Attention, Deep Learning, Image Synthesis.
