This paper investigates the cross-sensor portability of neural network architectures designed for LiDAR-based semantic labeling, a critical task in autonomous driving and robotics applications. The primary objective is to assess how well state-of-the-art deep learning models, originally trained on data from one LiDAR sensor, generalize to data from different sensor types with varying resolutions, fields of view, and noise profiles. To this end, we benchmark several widely adopted neural network architectures, including those specialized for point cloud and range image representations, using standardized datasets from multiple LiDAR sensors. The evaluation considers both quantitative metrics, such as mean Intersection over Union (mIoU), and qualitative assessments of semantic segmentation performance. Our results reveal significant performance variations across sensors and highlight model architectures that demonstrate superior adaptability and robustness to sensor-specific characteristics. Furthermore, we explore architectural design choices and domain adaptation techniques that improve cross-sensor generalization without requiring extensive retraining. The findings provide valuable insights for developing more portable and resilient LiDAR semantic labeling systems, ultimately contributing to safer and more reliable perception in heterogeneous sensor environments. Key keywords: LiDAR, semantic segmentation, neural networks, cross-sensor portability, point cloud, domain adaptation, autonomous driving.
