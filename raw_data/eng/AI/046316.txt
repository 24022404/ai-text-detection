Understanding the universe’s fundamental parameters is central to advancing modern cosmology. This paper introduces the CAMELS Multifield Dataset, a comprehensive suite of simulated cosmological data tailored for the application of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) in parameter inference. The objectives are twofold: to provide a large, diverse collection of synthetic maps spanning multiple fields—such as dark matter, gas, and stars—and to enable robust training and evaluation of AI-based models for extracting key cosmological parameters, including the matter density, Hubble constant, and amplitude of fluctuations. We detail the generation of the multifield dataset using state-of-the-art hydrodynamical and N-body simulations, and the curation of labeled samples ideal for supervised and unsupervised learning techniques. Benchmarks are established using deep neural networks and convolutional architectures to infer cosmological parameters, demonstrating improved accuracy over traditional statistical approaches, particularly in the presence of complex baryonic effects. Our findings highlight the significance of multifield data in reducing parameter degeneracies and enhancing the sensitivity of AI algorithms. The CAMELS Multifield Dataset sets a new standard for open-access cosmological AI benchmarks, empowering the community to develop, compare, and validate advanced machine learning models for astrophysical discovery and precision cosmology. Keywords: cosmology, artificial intelligence, machine learning, CAMELS, multifield dataset, parameter inference, deep learning, simulation, astrophysics.
