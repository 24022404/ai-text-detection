This paper addresses the challenge of weakly supervised semantic segmentation, where the goal is to segment images into meaningful regions using limited supervision. Our objective is to develop a novel approach that leverages classifiers to improve segmentation accuracy. We propose a modified cue-based method that utilizes classifiers to generate high-quality pseudo-labels, which are then used to train a segmentation model. Our approach employs a two-stage framework, where the first stage involves training a classifier to predict object presence, and the second stage uses the classifier's predictions to guide the segmentation process. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing weakly supervised semantic segmentation approaches, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. The key contributions of our research include the development of a novel classifier-based framework, the introduction of modified cues to improve pseudo-label quality, and the demonstration of the effectiveness of our approach in real-world applications. Our work has significant implications for computer vision tasks, particularly in scenarios where annotated data is scarce. Keywords: weakly supervised semantic segmentation, classifiers, modified cues, pseudo-labels, computer vision.
