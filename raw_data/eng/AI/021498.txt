Deep transfer learning has emerged as a powerful approach for leveraging pre-trained models to address challenges in medical image analysis, where labeled datasets are often limited. This study investigates the underlying mechanisms of deep transfer learning, focusing on how knowledge from large-scale natural image datasets can be effectively transferred to medical imaging tasks. We propose a systematic framework to analyze the impact of transfer learning on model performance, emphasizing the role of feature extraction, fine-tuning strategies, and domain adaptation. Using a diverse set of medical imaging datasets, including X-rays, MRIs, and CT scans, we evaluate the effectiveness of transfer learning across various architectures, such as ResNet and EfficientNet. Our results demonstrate that transfer learning significantly improves model accuracy, particularly in scenarios with limited training data, while also reducing computational costs. Furthermore, we identify key factors influencing transferability, such as the similarity between source and target domains and the depth of fine-tuning. The findings provide actionable insights for optimizing transfer learning in medical imaging, enabling more robust and efficient diagnostic tools. This research contributes to advancing the understanding of transfer learning mechanisms and offers practical guidelines for its application in healthcare, with potential implications for improving disease detection and patient outcomes.  

**Keywords**: Deep transfer learning, medical imaging, feature extraction, fine-tuning, domain adaptation, ResNet, EfficientNet.
