Offline reinforcement learning (RL) aims to learn optimal policies from previously collected, static datasets without further environment interaction. This work addresses the challenge of partial coverage in offline RL, where the dataset may not fully represent the state-action space, leading to overestimation and poor generalization. We introduce a pessimistic model-based approach grounded in Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) learning theory. Our method constructs a lower-confidence bound on the model's predicted reward and transition dynamics. By optimizing policies with respect to this pessimistic model, we mitigate the risks associated with out-of-distribution actions. We derive novel PAC bounds demonstrating the sample complexity of learning near-optimal policies under partial coverage. Furthermore, we propose a practical algorithm based on posterior sampling, allowing for efficient exploration of the pessimistic model space. Empirical results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, significantly outperforming existing offline RL methods in scenarios with limited data coverage. This work contributes to the theoretical understanding and practical development of robust offline RL algorithms.

**Keywords:** Offline Reinforcement Learning, Pessimistic Planning, PAC Learning, Partial Coverage, Model-based RL, Posterior Sampling.
