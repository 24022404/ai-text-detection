The advent of Transformer models has revolutionized natural language processing and other sequential data tasks. However, their application to graph-structured data remains limited due to inherent differences in data representation. This paper introduces GraphiT, a novel approach to incorporate graph structures within Transformer architectures, aiming to enhance their performance on tasks involving graph-based data.

Methods/Approach: GraphiT leverages a graph representation mechanism that encodes the structural information of graphs directly into the Transformer model. By integrating graph adjacency and node features into the attention mechanism, GraphiT enables the effective processing of both local and global graph contexts. This approach is evaluated on tasks such as node classification and link prediction, capitalizing on multi-head attention to learn from diverse graph properties.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that GraphiT outperforms state-of-the-art graph neural networks and Transformer-based models in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency on several benchmark datasets. The model's enhanced ability to capture intricate graph patterns leads to significant improvements in prediction tasks while maintaining scalability.

Conclusion/Implications: GraphiT represents a significant advancement in encoding graph structures within Transformer models, providing a potent tool for graph-based machine learning applications. Its success opens new avenues for research in domains where graph data is prevalent, such as social network analysis, molecule modeling, and recommendation systems, offering promising potential for future advancements in graph-aware AI technologies.

Key Keywords: GraphiT, Transformers, graph structure, attention mechanism, node classification, link prediction, graph neural networks.
