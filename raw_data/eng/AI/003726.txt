This paper proposes a novel approach to graph representation learning, introducing Stochastic Graphlet Embedding (SGE), a method designed to capture the complex structural properties of graphs. The objective is to develop an efficient and scalable technique for embedding graphlets, which are small, informative subgraphs, into a low-dimensional vector space. Our approach leverages stochastic processes to sample and embed graphlets, allowing for the preservation of crucial topological information. We employ a combination of graph convolutional networks and stochastic gradient descent to optimize the embedding process. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of SGE in various graph classification and clustering tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a stochastic graphlet sampling strategy and a novel embedding architecture, enabling the application of SGE to large-scale graph datasets. This work has significant implications for graph-based applications, such as social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics, and highlights the potential of stochastic graphlet embedding for improving the performance and interpretability of graph machine learning models. Key keywords: graph representation learning, stochastic graphlet embedding, graph convolutional networks, graph classification, clustering.
