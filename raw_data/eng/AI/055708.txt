Neural Architecture Search (NAS) automates the design of neural networks, but existing methods often suffer from high computational costs and limited exploration of the architecture space. This paper introduces L^NAS, a novel NAS framework that employs continuous-action reinforcement learning to efficiently optimize neural architectures. Unlike discrete action space methods, L^NAS utilizes a continuous action space, enabling finer-grained control over architectural parameters and more effective exploration. The framework learns a policy that maps network performance to architectural modifications, guiding the search process. We evaluate L^NAS on image classification tasks using the CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that L^NAS achieves state-of-the-art performance with significantly reduced search costs compared to existing NAS techniques. Specifically, L^NAS discovers architectures with superior accuracy and lower latency. The continuous action space enables the discovery of novel architectural motifs not easily accessible with discrete NAS methods. These findings highlight the potential of continuous-action reinforcement learning for efficient and effective neural architecture optimization. Keywords: Neural Architecture Search, Reinforcement Learning, Continuous Action Space, Deep Learning, Automation.
