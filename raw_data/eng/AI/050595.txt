This paper addresses the challenge of learning robust and discriminative representations in unsupervised settings by introducing a novel framework that combines adversarial learning with contrastive optimal transport. The objective is to enhance representation quality by leveraging the complementary strengths of adversarial training and optimal transport theory, enabling the model to capture both fine-grained and global structures in data. We propose a dual-objective approach where adversarial networks generate challenging samples to improve representation robustness, while contrastive optimal transport aligns the learned representations with a structured latent space, ensuring discriminative power. The method is evaluated on benchmark datasets for unsupervised representation learning, demonstrating superior performance in downstream tasks such as classification and clustering compared to state-of-the-art methods. Experimental results reveal significant improvements in representation quality, with our approach achieving higher accuracy and better generalization across diverse datasets. The proposed framework offers a scalable and efficient solution for unsupervised representation learning, with potential applications in domains such as computer vision, natural language processing, and bioinformatics. This work contributes to advancing the field by bridging adversarial learning and optimal transport, providing a new perspective on representation learning.  

**Keywords**: representation learning, adversarial learning, optimal transport, contrastive learning, unsupervised learning, deep learning.
