Reinforcement learning (RL) often suffers from high sample complexity, hindering its applicability in data-scarce environments. This paper investigates pretraining representation learning models to improve the data efficiency of downstream RL tasks. We propose a novel pretraining approach that combines contrastive learning with masked autoencoding, enabling the model to learn robust and generalizable features from unlabeled environment interactions. Our method, Representation Pretraining for Efficient Reinforcement Learning (RepErl), first learns a state representation via contrastive learning to capture essential environment dynamics. Subsequently, a masked autoencoder is employed to further refine the representation by predicting masked state components, enhancing the learned features' completeness and robustness. We evaluate RepErl on a suite of challenging benchmark tasks, demonstrating significant improvements in sample efficiency and asymptotic performance compared to state-of-the-art RL algorithms and alternative pretraining strategies. Results show that RepErl reduces the sample complexity by up to 50% while achieving comparable or superior performance. This work highlights the potential of representation pretraining to accelerate RL, offering a pathway to deploy RL agents effectively with limited data. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Representation Learning, Pretraining, Contrastive Learning, Masked Autoencoding, Data Efficiency.
