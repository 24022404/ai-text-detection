Message passing inference (MPI) algorithms are widely used in various fields, including computer vision and probabilistic graphical models. This paper investigates the interpretability of messages exchanged during MPI by leveraging deep learning techniques. We propose a novel framework that learns a latent representation of messages, allowing us to analyze the information content embedded within them. Our approach uses a variational autoencoder (VAE) to encode messages into a lower-dimensional space, followed by a supervised learning component to predict relevant variables from the encoded messages. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our method can effectively extract meaningful features from MPI messages. We also show that the learned representations can be used to improve the performance of MPI algorithms by identifying and filtering out irrelevant or noisy messages. Our findings provide insights into the inner workings of MPI and open avenues for developing more robust and efficient inference algorithms. Keywords: Message Passing Inference, Deep Learning, Variational Autoencoder, Interpretability, Probabilistic Graphical Models.
