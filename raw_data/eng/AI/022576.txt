This paper introduces a novel method for learning low-dimensional state embeddings and identifying metastable clusters directly from time series data. The objective is to reduce the complexity of high-dimensional time series by representing them in a lower-dimensional space while preserving essential dynamical information. Our approach combines a time-lagged autoencoder with a clustering algorithm to simultaneously learn embeddings and identify metastable states. Specifically, the autoencoder learns a low-dimensional representation that captures the temporal dependencies in the data, while the clustering algorithm groups similar states in the embedded space. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our method on synthetic datasets and real-world molecular dynamics trajectories, showing that it accurately identifies metastable states and captures the underlying dynamics with significantly reduced dimensionality. The results indicate improved performance compared to traditional dimensionality reduction and clustering techniques. This method provides a powerful tool for analyzing complex time series data in various scientific domains, including molecular dynamics, climate science, and financial modeling. Keywords: time series analysis, dimensionality reduction, metastable states, clustering, autoencoders, state embeddings.
