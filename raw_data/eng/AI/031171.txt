Early quantum key distribution (QKD) protocols, notably BB84 and E91, have transitioned from theoretical proposals to practical implementations in real-world communication networks since around 2005. These protocols have demonstrated secure quantum communication over increasingly long distances, reaching up to 1,000 kilometers using fiber optic cables. However, this progress is fundamentally constrained by photon losses inherent to optical fibers, which severely limit the range and scalability of terrestrial QKD systems. To overcome these distance limitations and enable secure communications over intercontinental or even global scales, researchers have turned to satellite-based QKD as a promising solution. A prominent example is the Micius satellite, which has performed pioneering quantum communication experiments between space and ground stations. Despite such advancements, satellite QKD presents its own set of formidable technical challenges, including severe signal attenuation during transmission through Earth's atmosphere and rapidly changing environmental conditions that can introduce noise and instability. Addressing these challenges is critical for the future development of global-scale quantum-secure communication networks.
