Sequence labeling is a fundamental task in natural language processing with applications in part-of-speech tagging, named entity recognition, and information extraction. This paper introduces Embedded-State Latent Conditional Random Fields (ESLCRF), a novel probabilistic model that extends traditional Conditional Random Fields (CRFs) by incorporating latent state representations learned within an embedding space. Our approach combines the strengths of CRFs in capturing sequential dependencies with the representational power of distributed embeddings to model complex relationships between input tokens. The ESLCRF model learns latent states associated with each input token, which are then used to inform the transition probabilities within the CRF. This allows the model to capture finer-grained contextual information than standard CRFs. We evaluate ESLCRF on several benchmark sequence labeling datasets, including the CoNLL 2000 chunking task and the CoNLL 2003 named entity recognition task. Experimental results demonstrate that ESLCRF achieves significant improvements in F1-score compared to baseline CRFs and other state-of-the-art sequence labeling models. The learned latent state embeddings provide interpretable insights into the model's decision-making process. The proposed ESLCRF offers a powerful and flexible framework for sequence labeling, with potential applications in various NLP tasks. Keywords: sequence labeling, conditional random fields, latent variables, embeddings, natural language processing.
