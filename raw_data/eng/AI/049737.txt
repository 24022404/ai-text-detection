Fine-tuning pre-trained language models has become a standard approach for adapting them to downstream tasks. However, the underlying reasons for its effectiveness remain an active area of research. This paper investigates the hypothesis that the intrinsic dimensionality of the fine-tuning landscape plays a crucial role in explaining this phenomenon. We employ a minimal random reparameterization approach to estimate the intrinsic dimensionality required for effective fine-tuning across a diverse set of tasks and models. Our results demonstrate a strong correlation between the estimated intrinsic dimensionality and the performance achieved via fine-tuning. Specifically, we find that successful fine-tuning can be achieved within a surprisingly low-dimensional subspace. Furthermore, our analysis reveals that the intrinsic dimensionality is influenced by factors such as model architecture and the nature of the downstream task. These findings suggest that the effectiveness of language model fine-tuning stems from the existence of low-dimensional, easily navigable subspaces within the high-dimensional parameter space. This understanding opens avenues for more efficient fine-tuning techniques and improved model generalization. Keywords: Language Models, Fine-tuning, Intrinsic Dimensionality, Optimization, Transfer Learning.
