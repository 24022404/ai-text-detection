This paper introduces a novel Periphery-Fovea Multi-Resolution Driving Model (PFM-DM) inspired by human visual attention mechanisms to enhance autonomous driving systems. The objective is to address the challenge of efficiently processing high-resolution visual data while maintaining real-time performance and safety. The proposed model mimics the human visual system by dividing the input into two streams: a high-resolution foveal region for critical details and a lower-resolution peripheral region for broader context. A lightweight attention-guided mechanism dynamically allocates computational resources based on the saliency of objects and road conditions. The model is trained and evaluated on a large-scale driving dataset, incorporating diverse weather, lighting, and traffic scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate that PFM-DM achieves a 15% improvement in object detection accuracy and a 20% reduction in computational latency compared to traditional single-resolution approaches. Furthermore, the model exhibits robust performance in edge cases, such as detecting pedestrians in low-visibility conditions. The findings highlight the potential of integrating human-inspired attention mechanisms into autonomous systems, paving the way for safer and more efficient self-driving technologies. Keywords: autonomous driving, human attention, multi-resolution model, foveal-peripheral vision, computational efficiency.
