Neural Architecture Search (NAS) has emerged as a powerful tool for automating the design of deep learning models, with Differentiable NAS (DNAS) offering a computationally efficient alternative to traditional methods. However, the architecture selection process in DNAS remains suboptimal, often leading to models that underperform or lack robustness. This paper addresses the limitations of current DNAS approaches by proposing a novel framework that rethinks architecture selection through a multi-objective optimization lens. Our method integrates a dynamic weighting mechanism to balance model performance, computational efficiency, and generalization capabilities during the search phase. We introduce a gradient-based pruning strategy that prioritizes architectures with high potential while discarding suboptimal candidates early in the process. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing DNAS methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency. Furthermore, the proposed framework exhibits improved scalability and adaptability across diverse tasks, including image classification and object detection. This work contributes to the advancement of NAS by providing a more robust and flexible solution for architecture selection, paving the way for broader applications in resource-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: Neural Architecture Search, Differentiable NAS, Multi-Objective Optimization, Gradient-Based Pruning, Deep Learning, Model Efficiency.
