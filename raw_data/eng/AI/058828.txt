This paper introduces *Dam Burst*, a novel region-merging-based image segmentation method designed to address the challenges of over-segmentation and computational inefficiency in traditional approaches. The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and scalable segmentation technique that accurately delineates objects in complex images while minimizing computational overhead. The proposed method leverages a dynamic region-merging strategy inspired by the natural phenomenon of dam bursts, where regions are iteratively merged based on adaptive similarity thresholds and boundary strength metrics. By incorporating a hierarchical merging process and an efficient data structure, *Dam Burst* significantly reduces the computational complexity compared to existing methods. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves superior segmentation accuracy, particularly in scenarios with high variability in texture and intensity. Furthermore, *Dam Burst* outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both precision and processing speed, making it suitable for real-time applications such as medical imaging, remote sensing, and autonomous systems. The key contribution of this work lies in its innovative merging mechanism, which balances accuracy and efficiency, offering a practical solution for large-scale image analysis tasks. This research advances the field of image segmentation by providing a scalable and effective tool for diverse applications in computer vision and beyond.  

**Keywords**: image segmentation, region merging, computational efficiency, boundary strength, hierarchical merging, real-time processing.
