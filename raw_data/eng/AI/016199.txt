This paper explores the theoretical foundations of product embedding, a crucial component in e-commerce machine learning systems. The objective is to investigate how product embedding captures complex relationships between products, enabling accurate recommendations and personalized customer experiences. We employ a multi-faceted approach, combining graph-based methods and neural networks to learn dense vector representations of products. Our results demonstrate significant improvements in recommendation accuracy and scalability, outperforming traditional collaborative filtering methods. Key findings highlight the importance of incorporating side information, such as product categories and attributes, to enhance embedding quality. Theoretical analyses provide insights into the role of embedding dimensions, graph structure, and neural network architectures in shaping the performance of product embedding models. This research contributes to the development of more effective and efficient e-commerce machine learning systems, with potential applications in recommendation systems, product search, and customer segmentation. Relevant keywords include product embedding, e-commerce, machine learning, graph neural networks, and recommendation systems.
