This research addresses the challenge of accurately determining the maturity of peanuts, which is critical for optimizing harvest time and maximizing yield. Traditional methods of assessing peanut maturity are labor-intensive and subjective. The objective of this study is to develop an automated approach utilizing hyperspectral imagery to reliably classify the maturity of peanuts.

Methods/Approach: Our method harnesses the capabilities of hyperspectral imaging, which captures detailed spectral information over a range of wavelengths. We engineered a classification model that processes this rich spectral data to identify distinct maturity stages of peanuts. Key technologies utilized include advanced image processing techniques and machine learning algorithms designed for spectral data analysis.

Results/Findings: The study demonstrates that our hyperspectral imagery-based approach outperforms conventional visual inspection methods in classifying peanut maturity. Experiments conducted show high accuracy and reliability, with our model achieving superior performance metrics across different maturity stages. Comparison with existing methods reveals significant improvement in precision and consistency.

Conclusion/Implications: This work provides valuable insights into the application of hyperspectral imagery for agricultural practices. Our research presents a novel and efficient solution for peanut maturity classification, which can potentially revolutionize current agricultural monitoring practices. The results suggest significant implications for precision agriculture, offering a scalable and objective tool that can enhance productivity and decision-making for peanut producers. Key keywords include hyperspectral imagery, peanut maturity, classification model, machine learning, and precision agriculture.
