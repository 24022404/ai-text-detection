Image restoration is a fundamental problem in computer vision and graphics, particularly relevant for tasks such as denoising, deblurring, and artifact removal. Traditional deep neural networks for image restoration rely on standard activation functions, which often fail to fully exploit spatial information crucial for high-quality reconstructions. In this paper, we present xUnit, a novel spatial activation function designed to enhance image restoration performance while maintaining computational efficiency. The proposed xUnit learns spatially adaptive activation maps, enabling the model to selectively emphasize salient features and suppress irrelevant noise across image regions. Our approach integrates xUnit into various restoration architectures and optimizes the activation parameters through end-to-end training. Experimental evaluations on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that xUnit outperforms conventional activation methods in terms of both quantitative metrics (such as PSNR and SSIM) and visual fidelity, achieving superior results with reduced model complexity. Furthermore, ablation studies confirm the effectiveness and adaptability of xUnit in diverse image restoration scenarios. This work highlights the importance of spatially aware activations and provides a scalable solution for improving the efficiency and accuracy of learning-based image restoration systems. Key keywords include spatial activation function, image restoration, deep learning, image denoising, and computational efficiency.
