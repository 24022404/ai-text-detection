Facial Expression Recognition (FER) is a crucial task in human-computer interaction, enabling machines to understand and respond to human emotions. This paper explores the effectiveness of using pre-trained Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) features for FER. We leverage features extracted from CNNs pre-trained on large-scale image datasets like ImageNet, transferring the learned representations to the FER task. Specifically, we investigate the performance of different CNN architectures, including VGG16, ResNet50, and InceptionV3, by employing their intermediate layer outputs as feature vectors for training a Support Vector Machine (SVM) classifier. Experimental results on benchmark datasets, such as FER-2013 and CK+, demonstrate that the pre-trained CNN features achieve competitive accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods that involve training CNNs from scratch. Furthermore, our approach significantly reduces training time and computational resources. The findings suggest that pre-trained CNN features offer a powerful and efficient solution for FER, especially when limited labeled data is available. This transfer learning technique provides a robust and easily adaptable method for facial expression analysis in various applications.
Keywords: Facial Expression Recognition, Convolutional Neural Networks, Transfer Learning, Pre-trained Models, Feature Extraction, ImageNet, Emotion Recognition.
