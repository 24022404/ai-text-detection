This paper presents a novel approach to image segmentation using size-dependent single linkage clustering of a watershed basin graph. The objective is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of image segmentation by leveraging the topological properties of the watershed transform. Our method involves constructing a graph where each node represents a watershed basin, and edges connect adjacent basins based on their spatial proximity and size similarity. We then apply single linkage clustering to group basins into segments, using a size-dependent criterion to control the clustering process. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms traditional watershed-based segmentation methods, particularly in images with complex textures and varying object sizes. The proposed technique achieves a balance between over- and under-segmentation, and its computational efficiency makes it suitable for large-scale image analysis applications. Key contributions of this research include the introduction of size-dependent clustering and the integration of graph-based methods with the watershed transform, enabling more accurate and robust image segmentation. Relevant keywords: image segmentation, watershed transform, single linkage clustering, graph-based methods, computer vision.
