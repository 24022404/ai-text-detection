Accurate forecasting of aggregated renewable generation is crucial for maintaining grid stability and optimizing energy distribution. This paper investigates the interpretability of neural network models applied to this forecasting task. We employ a hybrid approach combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for feature extraction from time series data and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks for capturing temporal dependencies. To enhance interpretability, we integrate attention mechanisms and layer-wise relevance propagation (LRP). Results demonstrate that our model achieves state-of-the-art forecasting accuracy compared to traditional statistical methods and other machine learning approaches. Furthermore, the attention weights and LRP scores provide insights into the key input features and time steps driving the forecasts, revealing the importance of specific weather patterns and historical generation data. This enhanced interpretability allows energy system operators to better understand and trust the model predictions, leading to improved decision-making and grid management. The findings contribute to the development of more reliable and transparent renewable energy forecasting systems.
Keywords: renewable energy forecasting, neural networks, interpretability, attention mechanisms, layer-wise relevance propagation, time series analysis.
