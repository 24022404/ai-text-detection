This paper addresses the challenge of modeling neuron point processes in the presence of unknown unknowns, a critical issue in neuroscience and computational biology. Traditional methods often fail to account for unobserved or unpredictable factors, leading to inaccurate representations of neural activity. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel data-driven approach that leverages advanced machine learning techniques to infer hidden dynamics and improve the perception of neuron point processes. Our method integrates probabilistic modeling with deep learning architectures to capture complex dependencies and uncertainties in neural data. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate significant improvements in predictive accuracy and robustness compared to existing methods. The proposed framework not only enhances the understanding of neural dynamics but also provides a scalable solution for applications in brain-computer interfaces and neuroprosthetics. This research contributes to the field by introducing a flexible, data-driven methodology capable of handling unknown unknowns, paving the way for more reliable and interpretable models of neural activity.  

**Keywords**: neuron point process, unknown unknowns, data-driven modeling, machine learning, neural dynamics, probabilistic modeling.
