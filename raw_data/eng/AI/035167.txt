This paper addresses the challenge of modeling neuron point processes with unknown unknowns, a critical problem in neuroscience. Our objective is to develop a data-driven approach that can accurately perceive and predict neural activity in the presence of unobserved variables. We propose a novel framework that leverages advanced machine learning techniques, including Bayesian inference and stochastic processes, to identify and characterize the underlying patterns in neural data. Our method uses a non-parametric approach to model the point process, allowing for flexible and adaptive estimation of the underlying intensity function. The results show that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of predictive accuracy and robustness to unknown unknowns. Our findings have important implications for the analysis and interpretation of neural data, and contribute to the development of more accurate and reliable models of neural activity. Key contributions include the introduction of a new class of models for neuron point processes, and the demonstration of the effectiveness of data-driven approaches in addressing the challenges of unknown unknowns. Relevant keywords: neuron point process, unknown unknowns, Bayesian inference, stochastic processes, machine learning, neural data analysis.
