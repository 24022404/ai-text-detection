This paper introduces R4Dyn, a novel approach to self-supervised monocular depth estimation of dynamic scenes using radar data. The objective is to improve depth estimation accuracy in scenarios where traditional methods struggle, such as in the presence of moving objects or changing environments. Our method leverages radar sensors to provide complementary information to monocular cameras, enabling the estimation of depth maps in dynamic scenes. We propose a fusion framework that combines radar and camera data, using a self-supervised learning strategy to train a deep neural network. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of R4Dyn, achieving state-of-the-art performance in dynamic scene depth estimation. Our approach outperforms existing methods, particularly in scenes with significant motion or occlusions. The contributions of this research include the introduction of radar data to self-supervised depth estimation, a novel fusion architecture, and a comprehensive evaluation framework. Key applications of R4Dyn include autonomous driving, robotics, and surveillance systems, where accurate depth estimation of dynamic scenes is crucial. Relevant keywords: monocular depth estimation, self-supervised learning, radar sensors, dynamic scenes, computer vision, autonomous systems.
