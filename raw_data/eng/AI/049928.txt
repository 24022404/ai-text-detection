This research addresses the challenge of learning efficient, interpretable representations for planning tasks in environments characterized by causal dependencies. The primary objective is to develop a framework that integrates causality into generative models to enhance plannable representation learning.

Methods/Approach: We introduce the Causal InfoGAN, a novel approach that combines InfoGAN's ability to learn disentangled representations with causality-focused modeling. By embedding causal structures within the architecture, the Causal InfoGAN promotes the learning of representations that facilitate informed decision-making in dynamic systems. The model is trained using a modified adversarial process that incorporates causal disentanglement, ensuring that the learned representations encapsulate the environment's causal relationships.

Results/Findings: The proposed Causal InfoGAN demonstrates superior performance in capturing relevant causal features compared to traditional learning frameworks. Our experimental results show that the representations learned by Causal InfoGAN improve success rates in various planning tasks, including robotic navigation and strategic game environments, by providing more robust and interpretable planning strategies. Furthermore, the model's capability to generalize across different tasks highlights its versatility and effectiveness in diverse application domains.

Conclusion/Implications: This study contributes to the field of representation learning by introducing a causality-oriented generative model that enhances plannability and interpretability. The Causal InfoGAN offers significant advancements in understanding and exploiting causal relationships within environments, providing a foundation for future research in task-oriented generative modeling and planning. Potential applications range from autonomous robotics to complex decision-making systems, benefiting fields that require a deep understanding of causality for improved performance.

Keywords: Causal InfoGAN, plannable representations, generative models, causal disentanglement, representation learning, planning tasks, autonomous systems.
