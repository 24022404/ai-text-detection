Semantic image segmentation, a crucial task in computer vision, demands models that can accurately delineate object boundaries and efficiently process complex scene structures. This paper introduces DCNAS: Densely Connected Neural Architecture Search, a novel framework designed to automatically discover high-performing neural network architectures for semantic image segmentation. The proposed approach integrates dense connectivity into the architecture search space, enabling more effective feature reuse and improved gradient flow among network layers. Leveraging an efficient gradient-based neural architecture search (NAS) algorithm, DCNAS systematically explores a rich variety of densely connected building blocks tailored for segmentation tasks. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that architectures discovered by DCNAS achieve superior segmentation accuracy and generalization compared to state-of-the-art NAS and manually designed models. Moreover, the resulting models exhibit reduced computational complexity and parameter count, underscoring their practicality for real-world applications. The contributions of this work include the introduction of dense connectivity into the NAS paradigm for segmentation, empirical validation of enhanced performance, and insights into the architecture patterns preferred for semantic segmentation. These findings highlight the potential of DCNAS to set new standards in automated model design for pixel-level image understanding.  
Keywords: Semantic segmentation, neural architecture search, dense connectivity, deep learning, computer vision.
