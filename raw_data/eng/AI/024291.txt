This paper introduces a novel Bayesian Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (BMAML) approach designed to enhance the adaptability and uncertainty quantification of meta-learning models. BMAML integrates Bayesian principles into the MAML framework, enabling the learning of task-specific posteriors over model parameters rather than point estimates. Our method employs a variational inference scheme for efficient approximation of the posterior, allowing for better generalization to new tasks and improved robustness to overfitting. Experiments on few-shot image classification benchmarks, including Mini-ImageNet and tiered-ImageNet, demonstrate that BMAML achieves superior performance compared to traditional MAML and other meta-learning algorithms, especially in low-data regimes. Furthermore, we show that BMAML provides well-calibrated uncertainty estimates, enabling more informed decision-making in downstream applications. The proposed approach offers a significant advancement in meta-learning by providing a principled way to incorporate uncertainty and improve generalization.
Keywords: Meta-Learning, Bayesian Methods, Model-Agnostic, Variational Inference, Few-Shot Learning.
