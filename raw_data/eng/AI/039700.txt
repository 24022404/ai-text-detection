This paper addresses the challenge of forecasting time series systems with unknown clusters of forecast tasks, where traditional methods often struggle due to the lack of prior knowledge about task groupings. We propose a novel framework that learns predictive leading indicators to improve forecasting accuracy in such scenarios. The approach leverages unsupervised learning techniques to automatically identify clusters of forecast tasks based on their underlying patterns, followed by the extraction of leading indicators that are predictive across these clusters. A key innovation lies in the integration of clustering and indicator learning, enabling the model to adapt to diverse and previously unseen task structures. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing approaches in terms of forecasting accuracy and robustness. The framework’s ability to generalize across unknown task clusters makes it particularly valuable for applications in domains such as finance, healthcare, and supply chain management, where task groupings are often unclear or dynamic. This research contributes a scalable and adaptive solution for time series forecasting, offering significant practical implications for systems with heterogeneous and evolving forecast tasks.  

**Keywords**: time series forecasting, predictive leading indicators, unsupervised clustering, forecast task adaptation, heterogeneous systems.
