This research addresses the challenge of multi-person 3D pose estimation in environments where severe occlusion occurs. Traditional methods struggle with accurately predicting poses due to partial visibility of individuals, which can significantly impact applications in surveillance, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework integrating Gated Convolutional Networks tailored for occlusion scenarios. This approach leverages a gating mechanism to dynamically prioritize visible cues while suppressing occluded regions in the input data. The model is trained on synthetic datasets augmented with controlled occlusion patterns to enhance robustness, and it is adapted for real-world scenarios through transfer learning.

Results/Findings: Our method demonstrates superior performance in handling occlusions compared to state-of-the-art techniques. Experiments on benchmark datasets show significant improvements in pose estimation accuracy and reduced error rates, particularly in crowded scenes where occlusions are prevalent. Qualitative and quantitative assessments reveal that our approach consistently retains pose estimation reliability under diverse occlusion conditions.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of Gated Convolution presents a substantial advancement in the field of 3D pose estimation for multiple persons under severe occlusions. Our findings suggest that this method can improve real-time applications across various domains, including augmented reality, sports analytics, and smart surveillance systems. This research contributes a pivotal tool that enhances the robustness and applicability of 3D pose estimation technologies.

Keywords: 3D pose estimation, severe occlusion, multi-person tracking, Gated Convolution, human pose analysis, computer vision.
