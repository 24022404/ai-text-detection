This paper presents a novel deep unsupervised learning framework for 3D point cloud analysis that leverages graph topology inference and spectral filtering. The objective is to address the challenges of capturing complex geometric structures and local-global relationships within unstructured 3D point data, without relying on labeled datasets. Our approach first infers the underlying graph topology of the point cloud, dynamically constructing local neighborhood graphs to represent spatial correlations. Subsequently, we apply spectral graph filtering to extract multi-scale geometric features, enhancing the robustness and descriptiveness of learned representations. Extensive experiments on benchmark 3D point cloud datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method for tasks such as shape classification, object clustering, and unsupervised segmentation. The results show significant improvements over existing unsupervised baselines, especially in capturing subtle structural details and improving feature discrimination. This framework offers a scalable and flexible solution for 3D data understanding in domains such as robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented reality, where labeled data is scarce or unavailable. Keywords: 3D point cloud, deep learning, graph topology, spectral filtering, unsupervised learning, geometric representation.
