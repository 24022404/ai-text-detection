Weakly supervised semantic segmentation (WSSS) aims to achieve pixel-level segmentation using only image-level labels, reducing the need for costly annotations. However, existing methods often underutilize the potential of classifiers, leading to suboptimal segmentation performance. This paper introduces a novel approach that fully leverages classifiers to generate high-quality pseudo-labels for WSSS by modifying traditional cues. Our method enhances the classifier's ability to identify discriminative regions and refine localization maps through a combination of attention mechanisms and iterative refinement processes. We propose a modified cue generation framework that integrates class activation maps (CAMs) with spatial and contextual information, improving the accuracy of pseudo-labels. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of segmentation accuracy and robustness. The proposed method achieves significant improvements in mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) scores, highlighting its effectiveness in bridging the gap between weakly supervised and fully supervised segmentation. This work contributes to advancing WSSS by demonstrating the untapped potential of classifiers and providing a scalable solution for real-world applications with limited annotation resources.  

**Keywords**: weakly supervised semantic segmentation, class activation maps, pseudo-labels, attention mechanisms, image-level supervision, segmentation refinement.
