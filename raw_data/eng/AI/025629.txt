This paper presents TDIOT: Target-driven Inference for Deep Video Object Tracking, a novel framework designed to improve the robustness and efficiency of object tracking in video sequences. The objective of this work is to address the limitations of conventional deep tracking algorithms, particularly in handling long-term occlusions, appearance variations, and background clutter. TDIOT leverages a target-driven approach, where a target-specific feature extractor is combined with an inference module that dynamically adapts to the evolving appearance of the object of interest throughout the video. The proposed architecture integrates deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with attention mechanisms to selectively focus on discriminative target features while suppressing distractors. Extensive experiments were conducted on multiple benchmark datasets, demonstrating that TDIOT achieves superior tracking accuracy and real-time performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. Key findings reveal that the target-driven design significantly enhances adaptability to challenging scenarios, such as abrupt motion or partial occlusions. In conclusion, TDIOT provides a significant advancement in deep video object tracking by introducing a flexible and effective target-driven inference strategy. The proposed framework holds promise for various applications, including intelligent surveillance, autonomous vehicles, and human-computer interaction. Keywords: video object tracking, deep learning, target-driven inference, convolutional neural networks, visual attention.
