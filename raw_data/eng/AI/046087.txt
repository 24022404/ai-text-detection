This paper investigates a novel approach to video generation by posing the question of whether objects in video can be treated akin to words in natural language processing. The research aims to explore the potential of leveraging transformer architectures, commonly employed in large language models, for generative video tasks.

Methods/Approach: We introduce the Generative Video Transformer (GVT), a model that conceptualizes objects within a video as a sequence of "visual words." Our approach utilizes a transformer network framework to capture and generate temporal and spatial dependencies in video sequences, akin to text processing in language models. The model is trained on extensive video datasets to learn the dynamics and interactions of objects over time.

Results/Findings: Results demonstrate that the GVT outperforms existing methods in generating coherent and contextually relevant video sequences, showcasing improved spatial and temporal consistency. Comparative analyses with state-of-the-art video generation techniques reveal that our model achieves superior performance in terms of video quality and object continuity, validating the viability of treating objects as analogous to words.

Conclusion/Implications: The adoption of transformer architectures for video generation opens promising avenues in generative models, highlighting the versatility and potential of using object-word analogies. This research contributes to the growing field of video synthesis and expands the applications of transformer models beyond text and image domains. Potential applications range from content creation and entertainment to educational tools and beyond, marking a significant step forward in the field of AI-driven video generation.

Keywords: Generative Video Transformer, Video Generation, Transformer Architecture, Visual Words, AI Models, Object Continuity, Temporal Dynamics.
