Understanding and disentangling controllable and uncontrollable factors of variation is a critical challenge in machine learning and artificial intelligence, particularly for applications requiring robust and interpretable models. This paper presents a novel framework that leverages interactive learning with the environment to separate these factors effectively. By designing an agent capable of actively interacting with its surroundings, we enable the identification and isolation of controllable factors—those that can be influenced by the agent’s actions—from uncontrollable factors, which remain invariant to such interactions. Our approach integrates reinforcement learning with representation learning, utilizing a disentangled latent space to model these distinct factors. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing techniques in both synthetic and real-world datasets, achieving superior disentanglement performance and improved downstream task accuracy. The framework’s ability to generalize across diverse environments highlights its potential for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and adaptive AI. This work contributes a significant step toward building more interpretable and adaptable AI systems by explicitly addressing the interplay between controllable and uncontrollable factors of variation.  

**Keywords**: disentangled representation, interactive learning, reinforcement learning, controllable factors, uncontrollable factors, adaptive AI.
