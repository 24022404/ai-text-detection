Point cloud analysis is critical in various applications, including 3D reconstruction, autonomous navigation, and object recognition. Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) is a widely used technique for point cloud downsampling and feature extraction due to its simplicity and efficiency in providing uniform coverage. However, FPS can be suboptimal in preserving local geometric details and capturing intricate structures. This paper introduces a novel sampling strategy, Adaptive Density-Aware Sampling (ADAS), that goes beyond traditional FPS by incorporating local density estimation to guide the sampling process. ADAS prioritizes regions with high geometric complexity and varying point densities, leading to improved feature representation. We evaluate ADAS on standard benchmark datasets for point cloud classification and segmentation tasks, demonstrating its superior performance compared to FPS and other state-of-the-art sampling methods. Our experiments show that ADAS enhances the accuracy and robustness of point cloud analysis, particularly in scenarios with non-uniform point distributions. The proposed approach offers a valuable alternative to FPS, enabling more effective point-wise analysis and improved performance in downstream tasks. Keywords: Point Cloud, Sampling, Farthest Point Sampling, Density Estimation, 3D Reconstruction, Point-wise Analysis, Geometric Features.
