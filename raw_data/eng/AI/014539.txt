The identification of orphaned data points in large datasets is a critical challenge in data analysis, particularly in domains such as healthcare and genomics. This paper introduces a novel approach, Corresponding Projections for Orphan Screening (CPOS), designed to efficiently detect and classify orphaned data points in high-dimensional datasets. The proposed method leverages a combination of dimensionality reduction techniques and adaptive clustering algorithms to map data points into a lower-dimensional space, where orphaned instances are more easily identifiable. CPOS employs a multi-stage projection framework that ensures robustness against noise and outliers while maintaining computational efficiency. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that CPOS outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and scalability, achieving a 15% improvement in orphan detection rates. Furthermore, the method is shown to be highly adaptable to various data types, including structured and unstructured data. The implications of this research extend to applications in anomaly detection, data cleaning, and precision medicine, where identifying orphaned data points can significantly enhance decision-making processes. This work contributes a scalable and effective solution to the problem of orphan screening, offering a valuable tool for researchers and practitioners in data-intensive fields.  

**Keywords**: Orphan screening, dimensionality reduction, adaptive clustering, anomaly detection, high-dimensional data, data cleaning.
