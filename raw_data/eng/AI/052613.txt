This paper explores the concept of free energy in the context of sequential decision making, with a focus on developing generalized optimality equations. The objective is to provide a novel framework for understanding and optimizing decision-making processes in complex, dynamic environments. We employ a combination of information-theoretic and game-theoretic approaches to derive a set of equations that characterize optimal behavior in terms of free energy minimization. Our method involves the use of approximate inference techniques and stochastic optimization algorithms to solve the resulting equations. The results show that our framework can capture a wide range of decision-making scenarios, including those involving partial observability, non-stationarity, and multi-agent interactions. Key findings include the derivation of a free energy bound on the expected cumulative reward, as well as the development of a novel policy iteration algorithm that leverages this bound to improve decision-making performance. The implications of this research are significant, as it provides a new perspective on the optimization of sequential decision-making processes and has potential applications in fields such as artificial intelligence, robotics, and autonomous systems. Keywords: free energy, sequential decision making, generalized optimality equations, approximate inference, stochastic optimization, artificial intelligence, decision theory.
