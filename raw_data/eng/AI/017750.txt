This paper addresses the challenge of multi-modal affect analysis in real-world scenarios, focusing on the integration of standardized data within subjects to improve emotion recognition accuracy. The objective is to develop a robust framework that leverages diverse data modalities, such as facial expressions, voice, and physiological signals, to analyze human emotions in naturalistic settings. We propose a novel approach that combines deep learning techniques with domain adaptation methods to handle the variability and noise inherent in "in-the-wild" data. Our methodology involves preprocessing and aligning multi-modal data streams, followed by feature extraction and fusion using advanced neural network architectures. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in emotion classification accuracy compared to existing methods, with a particular emphasis on cross-subject generalization. The findings highlight the effectiveness of standardized data integration and the potential of our approach for applications in human-computer interaction, mental health monitoring, and personalized user experiences. This research contributes to the field by providing a scalable and adaptable framework for multi-modal affect analysis, paving the way for more accurate and context-aware emotion recognition systems.  

**Keywords**: multi-modal affect analysis, emotion recognition, deep learning, domain adaptation, in-the-wild data, human-computer interaction.
