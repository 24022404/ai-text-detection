This paper introduces CoCoNets, a novel approach to learning continuous 3D scene representations using contrastive learning. The objective is to enable efficient and effective encoding of complex scenes into compact, informative representations. To achieve this, we propose a continuous contrastive learning framework that leverages a combination of 3D convolutional neural networks and transformer-based architectures. Our method learns to represent 3D scenes as continuous functions, allowing for flexible and efficient querying of scene information. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of CoCoNets in various 3D scene understanding tasks, including scene reconstruction, object recognition, and semantic segmentation. Compared to existing methods, CoCoNets achieves state-of-the-art performance while requiring significantly less memory and computational resources. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to learn continuous, hierarchical representations of 3D scenes, enabling a wide range of applications in fields such as robotics, computer vision, and virtual reality. Key keywords: 3D scene representation, contrastive learning, continuous functions, transformer architectures, computer vision, robotics.
