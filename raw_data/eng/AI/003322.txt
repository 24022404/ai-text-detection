Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have achieved significant success in modeling complex data distributions; however, their effectiveness is limited when applied to data characterized by heavy-tailed distributions, which frequently arise in fields such as finance, network traffic analysis, and natural language processing. This paper introduces Pareto GAN, a novel extension of the standard GAN framework specifically designed to enhance the representational power and robustness of GANs for heavy-tailed distributions. Our approach incorporates a Pareto-inspired loss function and tailored architecture modifications to better capture the statistical properties of heavy-tailed data. Extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that Pareto GAN consistently outperforms existing GAN variants in modeling tail events, achieving superior accuracy in density estimation and sample generation for distributions with significant skewness and kurtosis. The proposed model maintains the flexibility of GANs while providing substantial improvements in handling rare and extreme events. These findings suggest that Pareto GAN offers valuable advancements for applications requiring reliable modeling of heavy-tailed behaviors, such as risk assessment and anomaly detection. Key contributions include the introduction of the Pareto GAN architecture, empirical validation across multiple benchmarks, and detailed analysis of its suitability for heavy-tailed data. Keywords: GAN, heavy-tailed distribution, Pareto, generative models, density estimation, deep learning, anomaly detection.
