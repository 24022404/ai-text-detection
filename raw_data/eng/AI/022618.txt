This paper addresses the challenge of personalized glycemic control in septic patients, a critical aspect of intensive care management. We propose a novel framework combining representation learning and reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize insulin dosage and minimize glycemic variability. The approach leverages a deep learning model to learn patient-specific representations from historical clinical data, capturing individual physiological responses to insulin. These representations are then used to train a personalized RL agent that dynamically adjusts insulin infusion rates. We evaluate our method on a real-world dataset of septic patients, demonstrating significant improvements in glycemic control metrics, including reduced time in hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia, compared to standard clinical protocols and existing RL-based approaches. The results indicate that our representation learning-enhanced RL framework can effectively personalize glycemic control strategies, leading to improved patient outcomes. This work contributes a data-driven approach to personalized medicine and provides a pathway for developing intelligent decision support systems in critical care. Keywords: Glycemic Control, Sepsis, Reinforcement Learning, Representation Learning, Personalized Medicine, Intensive Care.
