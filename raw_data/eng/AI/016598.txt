The Information Bottleneck (IB) principle has emerged as a fundamental framework for understanding information compression and representation learning in machine learning. This paper investigates the learnability of representations under the IB framework, focusing on the theoretical and practical aspects of optimizing the trade-off between compression and relevance. We propose a novel approach to analyze the learnability of IB-based models by leveraging recent advancements in optimization techniques and neural network architectures. Our method introduces a scalable algorithm that efficiently approximates the IB objective, enabling the training of deep models with improved generalization capabilities. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves superior performance in terms of both compression efficiency and task accuracy compared to existing methods. Furthermore, we provide theoretical insights into the conditions under which IB-based representations are learnable, offering a deeper understanding of the interplay between model complexity and data structure. The findings of this study have significant implications for applications in domains such as unsupervised learning, feature extraction, and interpretable AI. Keywords: Information Bottleneck, representation learning, compression, optimization, neural networks, generalization.
