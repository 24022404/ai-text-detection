Adversarial training has emerged as a robust defense against adversarial attacks in classification tasks. However, its efficacy in predictive tasks, particularly within deterministic settings, remains underexplored. This paper presents a theoretical analysis of adversarial training applied to deterministic prediction problems. We derive closed-form solutions for adversarially trained linear models under specific deterministic data distributions, revealing inherent limitations in their ability to generalize beyond the training data's adversarial perturbations. Our analysis demonstrates that adversarial training in deterministic scenarios can lead to overfitting to the adversarial noise, resulting in degraded performance on clean, unseen data. Furthermore, we show that the benefits of adversarial training diminish as the deterministic structure of the data increases. These findings highlight the importance of considering data characteristics when applying adversarial training and suggest alternative regularization techniques may be more suitable for deterministic predictive tasks. Keywords: adversarial training, deterministic prediction, generalization, adversarial robustness, linear models, theoretical analysis.
