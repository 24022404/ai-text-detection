Video-based person re-identification (Re-ID) aims to match pedestrian tracklets across disjoint camera views. Existing methods often struggle with high computational costs and memory requirements when modeling long-range dependencies in videos. To address these limitations, we propose a Spatially and Temporally Efficient Non-local Attention Network (STEN) for video-based person Re-ID. STEN incorporates a novel factorized non-local block that decouples spatial and temporal attention, significantly reducing computational complexity. Furthermore, we introduce a memory-efficient implementation of the attention mechanism. Our approach effectively captures crucial spatio-temporal relationships between video frames while maintaining scalability. Experiments on widely used datasets, including MARS and DukeMTMC-VideoReID, demonstrate that STEN achieves competitive or superior Re-ID accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods, with significantly reduced computational overhead and memory usage. The proposed network offers a practical and efficient solution for large-scale video-based person Re-ID, enabling its deployment in real-world surveillance systems. Keywords: Person Re-identification, Video Analysis, Non-local Attention, Spatio-temporal Modeling, Deep Learning, Computer Vision.
