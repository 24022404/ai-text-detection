This paper introduces a novel Learner-aware Teaching (LaT) framework that leverages Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) to optimize teaching strategies. LaT addresses the challenge of designing effective curricula by incorporating learner preferences and pedagogical constraints. Unlike traditional IRL approaches that focus solely on replicating expert demonstrations, our method elicits preference feedback from learners regarding different learning paths and incorporates these preferences into the reward function. Furthermore, we enforce pedagogical constraints, such as prerequisite knowledge and concept dependencies, to ensure a structured and coherent learning experience. We formulate the teaching problem as a constrained optimization problem and develop an efficient algorithm to solve it. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world educational datasets demonstrate that LaT significantly outperforms baseline teaching methods in terms of learner engagement, knowledge acquisition, and adherence to pedagogical principles. The framework offers a promising avenue for developing personalized and effective learning experiences in various educational domains. Keywords: Inverse Reinforcement Learning, Teaching, Curriculum Design, Preference Learning, Constrained Optimization, Learner Modeling.
