Estimating causal effects from observational data is a fundamental challenge across various scientific disciplines. This paper introduces Graph Intervention Networks (GIN), a novel framework that leverages graph neural networks to model and estimate causal effects under complex causal structures. GIN learns node representations that encode both observational and interventional distributions, enabling accurate estimation of treatment effects even in the presence of confounding. The proposed method employs a specialized graph neural network architecture coupled with an intervention module to simulate counterfactual outcomes. We evaluate GIN on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its superior performance compared to state-of-the-art causal effect estimation methods. Results show that GIN achieves significant improvements in accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with high-dimensional data and complex causal dependencies. The framework provides a valuable tool for researchers seeking to understand and quantify causal relationships from observational data. Key words: Causal Inference, Causal Effect Estimation, Graph Neural Networks, Intervention, Counterfactual Reasoning, Observational Data.
