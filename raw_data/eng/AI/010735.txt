This paper presents Graph Filtration Learning, a novel framework designed to enhance the representation and analysis of complex graph structures. It addresses the limitations of existing graph learning methodologies in capturing multi-scale topological features essential for tasks like node classification, link prediction, and community detection.

Methods/Approach: The proposed approach leverages the concept of graph filtration, which systematically decomposes a graph into a sequence of subgraphs captured via varying edge weight thresholds. By integrating topological data analysis with machine learning techniques, the framework learns hierarchical representations that preserve crucial structural information across different scales. The architecture is powered by a tailored neural network model that enhances the learning of topological features, providing an efficient pathway for comprehensive graph analysis.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that Graph Filtration Learning significantly outperforms traditional graph convolutional networks and other state-of-the-art graph representation models. Evaluations on various benchmark datasets confirm improved accuracy and robustness in key graph analysis tasks. The method further exhibits strong generalization capabilities when applied to graphs varying in domain and complexity.

Conclusion/Implications: This research introduces a transformative approach to graph learning, emphasizing the importance of multi-scale topological features in understanding complex networks. The findings suggest potential applications in diverse fields such as social network analysis, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems, where understanding intricate relationships is crucial. The innovations presented pave the way for future exploration into more advanced, topology-focused graph representation learning frameworks.

Keywords: Graph Filtration Learning, graph representation, topological data analysis, node classification, machine learning, neural networks, graph convolutional networks.
