This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the adaptability and generalization of reinforcement learning (RL) agents by combining offline meta-reinforcement learning with online self-supervision. The objective is to enable agents to efficiently learn new tasks with minimal interaction data by leveraging prior offline experience while continually improving through online adaptation. We propose a novel framework that first utilizes a diverse offline dataset to train a meta-policy capable of rapid adaptation across different environments. During online deployment, the agent employs self-supervised auxiliary objectives—such as representation consistency and predictive modeling—without reliance on additional human-labeled data, enabling ongoing refinement of both policy and internal state representations. Experimental results on a suite of benchmark continuous control tasks indicate significant gains in sample efficiency, adaptation speed, and final performance compared to conventional offline RL and meta-RL methods. Notably, our approach demonstrates robust generalization to unseen tasks and effective utilization of self-generated supervisory signals in dynamic, real-world settings. This research highlights the synergy between offline meta-learning and online self-supervision, offering a practical pathway for developing RL agents with enhanced autonomy and resilience. Keywords: offline reinforcement learning, meta-reinforcement learning, self-supervised learning, policy adaptation, sample efficiency, generalization.
