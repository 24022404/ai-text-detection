The inverse design of inorganic materials, which involves identifying optimal material compositions and structures for specific properties, remains a significant challenge in materials science. Traditional methods often struggle with the vast and complex chemical space, leading to inefficiencies in exploration and discovery. This paper proposes a novel approach leveraging Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to efficiently sample the chemical space for inverse design. The proposed GAN-based framework generates realistic and diverse material candidates by learning the underlying distribution of known inorganic materials. A discriminator network evaluates the generated samples, ensuring their validity and relevance to the target properties. The method is validated using a comprehensive dataset of inorganic materials, demonstrating its ability to produce chemically feasible and property-optimized candidates. Results show that the GAN-based approach outperforms traditional sampling techniques in terms of computational efficiency and diversity of generated materials. Furthermore, the framework enables the discovery of previously unexplored material compositions with desirable properties, showcasing its potential for accelerating materials innovation. This work highlights the transformative role of GANs in addressing the challenges of chemical space exploration and provides a robust tool for the inverse design of inorganic materials.  

**Keywords**: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), inverse design, inorganic materials, chemical space, materials discovery, machine learning.
