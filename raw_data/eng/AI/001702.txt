This paper addresses the challenge of deriving dimension-free generalization bounds for non-linear metric learning, a crucial area in machine learning for tasks such as similarity search and clustering. We introduce a novel theoretical framework leveraging the concept of the covering number to analyze the generalization performance of non-linear metric learning algorithms. Unlike traditional approaches that depend on the dimensionality of the input space, our bounds are independent of the input dimension, offering improved guarantees in high-dimensional settings. We apply our framework to analyze several popular non-linear metric learning methods, including those based on kernel methods and neural networks. Our theoretical results are validated through experiments on benchmark datasets, demonstrating the practical relevance and tightness of our dimension-free bounds. The findings provide insights into the generalization capabilities of non-linear metric learning and offer a valuable tool for designing more robust and scalable algorithms.

**Keywords:** Metric Learning, Generalization Bounds, Non-Linear Models, Dimension-Free Bounds, Kernel Methods, Neural Networks, Covering Number.
