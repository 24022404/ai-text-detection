This paper presents Gaze360, a novel system for robust and accurate gaze estimation in unconstrained, real-world environments. The objective is to address the limitations of existing gaze estimation methods that often require controlled settings or specialized hardware. Our approach utilizes a deep convolutional neural network trained on a large-scale, diverse dataset of gaze annotations captured in "the wild." We employ a multi-stage architecture incorporating appearance-based and geometry-based features to achieve high precision. The system is designed to be physically unconstrained, operating effectively across a wide range of head poses, lighting conditions, and user demographics. Experimental results demonstrate that Gaze360 significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods on challenging benchmark datasets, exhibiting a substantial reduction in gaze estimation error. This advancement enables new applications in human-computer interaction, augmented reality, and behavioral analysis. Keywords: Gaze estimation, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, human-computer interaction, computer vision.
