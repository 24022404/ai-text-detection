This paper investigates the relationship between intrinsic dimensionality and the effectiveness of fine-tuning language models. The objective is to understand why fine-tuning large language models leads to significant performance gains on downstream tasks. We employ a novel approach, analyzing the intrinsic dimensionality of language model representations to explain the success of fine-tuning. Our method utilizes techniques from differential geometry and information theory to estimate the intrinsic dimensionality of model embeddings. The results show that fine-tuning adapts the model's representation to better align with the intrinsic dimensionality of the task, leading to improved performance. Key findings indicate that models with higher intrinsic dimensionality benefit more from fine-tuning, and that this dimensionality is closely related to the model's ability to capture nuanced semantic relationships. Our research contributes to the understanding of language model fine-tuning, highlighting the importance of intrinsic dimensionality in explaining its effectiveness. The study has implications for the development of more efficient and effective fine-tuning methods, particularly in applications involving large language models, such as natural language processing and AI-powered text analysis, with relevant keywords including language models, fine-tuning, intrinsic dimensionality, and representation learning.
