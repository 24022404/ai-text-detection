This paper introduces a novel approach to learning directed exploration strategies, aiming to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of exploration in complex environments. Our objective is to develop an agent that can adaptively balance exploration and exploitation, leading to better performance in tasks with sparse rewards. We propose a new framework that combines reinforcement learning with intrinsic motivation, enabling the agent to learn directed exploration strategies through self-supervised learning. Our method utilizes a hybrid approach, incorporating both model-based and model-free techniques to guide the exploration process. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in several benchmark environments, achieving significant improvements in cumulative rewards and exploration efficiency. The key contributions of this research include the development of a flexible and scalable framework for learning directed exploration strategies, and the introduction of a new intrinsic motivation mechanism that encourages the agent to explore novel and informative states. Our work has important implications for applications in robotics, game playing, and other areas where efficient exploration is crucial, and highlights the potential of combining reinforcement learning with intrinsic motivation to achieve more effective and efficient exploration strategies, with relevant keywords including reinforcement learning, intrinsic motivation, exploration-exploitation trade-off, and directed exploration.
