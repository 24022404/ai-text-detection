**Abstract:**

Person image generation remains a challenging task in computer vision due to the need for high-fidelity synthesis and adaptability to diverse poses, clothing, and backgrounds. This paper introduces MUST-GAN, a novel Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework designed for self-driven person image generation through Multi-level Statistics Transfer. Unlike conventional methods that rely solely on global feature alignment or paired data, MUST-GAN leverages both global and local statistical features learned from source images to guide the synthesis process, enabling realistic texture rendering and intricate attribute preservation. The proposed approach employs a multi-scale architecture where hierarchical statistical representations are extracted and transferred across different network levels to enhance generative consistency and semantic control. Experimental evaluations on standard person image datasets demonstrate that MUST-GAN surpasses existing state-of-the-art models in terms of image quality, structural coherence, and diversity, as measured by both quantitative metrics and human perceptual studies. Furthermore, ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of the multi-level statistics transfer mechanism in capturing fine-grained details and improving synthesis robustness. The advancements presented in this work offer significant implications for applications such as virtual try-on, animation, and identity-preserving image editing. 

**Keywords:** person image generation, generative adversarial network, multi-level statistics transfer, self-driven synthesis, image synthesis, computer vision
