This paper presents an adversarial approach to explaining the predictions of deep neural networks, addressing the pressing need for transparency and interpretability in complex machine learning models. Our objective is to develop a method that can provide insights into the decision-making process of deep neural networks, thereby enhancing trust and reliability in their predictions. We propose a novel framework that leverages adversarial examples to identify the most influential input features contributing to a network's predictions. Our approach utilizes a generative model to craft adversarial examples that are used to perturb the input data, allowing us to analyze the resulting changes in the network's output. The results show that our method can effectively identify the most critical input features and provide meaningful explanations for the network's predictions. Our approach has significant implications for the development of more transparent and trustworthy AI systems, particularly in high-stakes applications such as healthcare and finance. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a novel adversarial approach for explainability, the development of a generative model for crafting adversarial examples, and the demonstration of the method's effectiveness in explaining the predictions of deep neural networks. Keywords: deep neural networks, explainability, adversarial examples, transparency, interpretability, AI models.
