This paper introduces a novel image enhancement technique that allows standard digital cameras to capture high-quality images in near-complete darkness, termed "Dark Flash Normal Camera" (DFNC). Our method leverages a rapid, low-intensity flash sequence synchronized with high ISO settings to acquire a series of noisy images. These images are then processed using a custom-designed denoising algorithm based on deep learning, specifically a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture optimized for low-light conditions. The CNN is trained on a large dataset of paired noisy/clean images generated through a physics-based rendering pipeline simulating dark environments. Experimental results demonstrate that DFNC significantly outperforms traditional single-flash photography and existing low-light enhancement methods, producing images with reduced noise, improved color accuracy, and enhanced detail. The system is implemented using readily available hardware and software, making it accessible for widespread adoption. This research contributes a practical and effective solution for high-quality imaging in extremely low-light conditions, expanding the capabilities of standard cameras for various applications.

**Keywords:** Low-light imaging, image enhancement, deep learning, denoising, convolutional neural networks, flash photography.
