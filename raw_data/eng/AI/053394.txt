This research addresses the challenge of interpreting groups of points in low-dimensional representations, a process critical for understanding complex datasets produced by dimensionality reduction techniques. The objective is to develop a systematic approach to explain and visualize these groups effectively, enhancing the comprehension of multi-dimensional data in fields like machine learning and data analysis.

Methods/Approach: The study introduces a novel framework that integrates clustering algorithms with interpretative models to identify and explain groups within low-dimensional spaces. Techniques such as t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) and uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) are employed to transform high-dimensional data into a reduced form. The framework then applies interpretable machine learning techniques to articulate the characteristics and behaviors of clustered points.

Results/Findings: The proposed method successfully identifies distinct group patterns and offers clear and intuitive explanations of their formations. Evaluation on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrates the framework's superiority in producing meaningful insights compared to traditional approaches. Furthermore, comparisons with established methods indicate significant improvements in interpretability and user understanding of complex datasets.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field by providing a robust tool for data scientists to explore and understand low-dimensional data landscapes. The potential applications are vast, ranging from enhancing exploratory data analysis to improving model transparency in machine learning workflows. The methodology fosters better decision-making processes by offering clearer data representations, thus bridging the gap between data complexity and human interpretability.

Keywords: Low-dimensional representations, dimensionality reduction, clustering algorithms, data interpretability, t-SNE, UMAP.
