Object detection and pose estimation are critical tasks in computer vision, with applications ranging from robotics to augmented reality. This paper presents a comprehensive survey on joint object detection and pose estimation using monocular vision, addressing the challenges of accurately identifying objects and determining their spatial orientation from a single image. The objective is to explore the integration of these two tasks into a unified framework, leveraging advancements in deep learning and convolutional neural networks (CNNs). We review state-of-the-art methods, including end-to-end architectures, multi-task learning approaches, and hybrid models that combine detection and pose estimation. Key techniques such as anchor-based detection, keypoint prediction, and geometric reasoning are analyzed for their effectiveness in improving accuracy and computational efficiency. The survey highlights the trade-offs between model complexity and performance, comparing various approaches on benchmark datasets such as KITTI and COCO. Results demonstrate that joint models often outperform sequential methods, achieving higher precision in both detection and pose estimation tasks. This work concludes with insights into future research directions, emphasizing the potential of leveraging transformer-based architectures and self-supervised learning to further enhance performance. The findings contribute to the development of robust vision systems capable of real-time object understanding in dynamic environments.  

**Keywords**: object detection, pose estimation, monocular vision, deep learning, multi-task learning, computer vision.
