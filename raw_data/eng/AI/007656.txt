Optical flow estimation is a fundamental problem in computer vision, with applications ranging from motion analysis to video processing. Despite significant progress, accurately estimating optical flow remains challenging due to complex motion patterns and occlusions. This paper introduces FlowNet 2.0, an advanced deep learning framework that builds upon its predecessor, FlowNet, to achieve state-of-the-art performance in optical flow estimation. The proposed approach integrates multiple innovations, including a cascaded architecture, improved training strategies, and a novel loss function designed to handle large displacements and fine details. FlowNet 2.0 leverages end-to-end training with synthetic and real-world datasets, enabling robust generalization across diverse scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate that FlowNet 2.0 outperforms existing methods on benchmark datasets such as Sintel and KITTI, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and computational efficiency. The framework also exhibits superior performance in handling challenging cases, such as fast motion and textureless regions. This work not only advances the state of optical flow estimation but also provides insights into the design of deep networks for complex vision tasks. FlowNet 2.0’s contributions pave the way for enhanced applications in autonomous driving, video stabilization, and action recognition.  

**Keywords**: Optical flow, deep learning, FlowNet 2.0, motion estimation, computer vision, cascaded architecture.
