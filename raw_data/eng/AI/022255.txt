This paper presents a novel approach to neural architecture search (NAS) using graph-based methods and operation embeddings. The objective is to efficiently explore the vast space of possible neural network architectures to find optimal designs for specific tasks. Our method represents neural networks as graphs, where nodes denote operations and edges signify connections between them. We leverage operation embeddings to capture the semantic meaning of different operations, enabling the search process to focus on the most promising architectures. The search algorithm utilizes a reinforcement learning framework to navigate the graph space, guided by a reward function that balances performance and computational cost. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing NAS methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency, particularly on large-scale image classification tasks. The key contributions of this work include the introduction of operation embeddings to NAS and the development of a graph-based search algorithm that effectively exploits these embeddings. Our research has significant implications for the automated design of neural networks, enabling faster and more accurate model development for a wide range of applications, including computer vision and natural language processing. Key keywords: neural architecture search, graph-based methods, operation embeddings, reinforcement learning, automated machine learning.
