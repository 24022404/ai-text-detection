Affective computing, which aims to understand and respond to human emotions, benefits significantly from multi-modal data. This paper introduces an unsupervised multi-modal representation learning approach tailored for affective computing using wearable sensor data collected across multiple corpora. The proposed method leverages contrastive learning to fuse representations from physiological signals (e.g., heart rate, skin conductance) and contextual information, without relying on labeled emotion data. We demonstrate that our approach learns robust and generalized affective representations across diverse datasets. Evaluation on benchmark emotion recognition tasks reveals that the learned representations achieve comparable or superior performance compared to supervised methods, particularly in cross-corpus settings. The findings suggest that unsupervised multi-modal learning is a promising avenue for developing affective computing systems that can generalize across different populations and environments. This research contributes to the field by providing a novel and effective technique for learning affective representations from unlabeled wearable data, enabling the development of more robust and adaptable emotion recognition systems.

Keywords: Affective Computing, Multi-Modal Learning, Unsupervised Learning, Wearable Sensors, Emotion Recognition, Representation Learning, Contrastive Learning.
