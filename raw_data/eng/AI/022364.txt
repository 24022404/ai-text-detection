The objective of this research is to investigate whether Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), a prominent technology in artificial content creation, leave discernible artificial fingerprints in their outputs. With the increasing prevalence of GAN-generated media, detecting the origins of digital content has become crucial for ensuring authenticity and mitigating misinformation. Our approach involves a comprehensive analysis of digital artifacts produced by various GAN architectures, employing advanced forensic techniques to identify consistent patterns or markers indicative of non-human generation. We implemented a robust detection model, leveraging state-of-the-art machine learning algorithms, to automatically pinpoint these artificial fingerprints in images and videos. The results demonstrate that specific types of GANs do indeed leave identifiable patterns, which can be consistently detected and distinguished from human-created content. This finding not only provides valuable insight into the nature of GAN-generated media but also contributes significantly to the development of automated tools for digital media authentication. The implications of this study are far-reaching, offering a foundation for enhancing security protocols in digital forensics and supporting efforts to combat the spread of deepfakes and other fabricated digital content. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, artificial fingerprints, digital forensics, media authentication.
