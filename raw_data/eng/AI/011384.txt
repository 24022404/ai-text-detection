This paper introduces Deep Ordinal Reinforcement Learning (DORL), a novel framework designed to address the challenges of decision-making in environments with ordinal reward structures. Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) methods often struggle with sparse or hierarchical rewards, limiting their effectiveness in complex, real-world scenarios. The objective of this research is to develop a robust approach that leverages ordinal relationships between rewards to improve learning efficiency and policy performance. We propose a deep learning-based model that integrates ordinal regression with RL, enabling the agent to infer and exploit the inherent ordering of rewards. The approach is evaluated on a series of benchmark tasks, including hierarchical goal-reaching and sparse-reward environments. Experimental results demonstrate that DORL outperforms conventional RL methods in terms of convergence speed and final policy quality, achieving up to a 30% improvement in cumulative reward. Furthermore, the framework exhibits superior generalization capabilities, making it suitable for applications such as robotics, autonomous systems, and resource management. The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to bridge the gap between ordinal learning and RL, offering a scalable and interpretable solution for decision-making in structured environments. Keywords: reinforcement learning, ordinal regression, deep learning, decision-making, hierarchical rewards.
