Image stitching, the process of combining multiple overlapping images into a seamless panorama, is a fundamental task in computer vision with applications ranging from virtual reality to medical imaging. This paper presents a novel and robust method for image stitching that addresses common challenges such as parallax, motion blur, and varying exposure. Our approach introduces a new feature matching algorithm based on a modified SIFT descriptor coupled with a robust outlier rejection scheme using a spatially adaptive RANSAC. Furthermore, we propose a dynamic blending technique that minimizes ghosting artifacts and compensates for exposure differences between images. Experimental results on a diverse dataset demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art techniques in terms of stitching accuracy, robustness to image noise, and visual quality. The proposed system achieves a 20% reduction in alignment error and a 15% improvement in structural similarity index measure (SSIM) compared to existing methods. The contributions of this work include a novel feature matching strategy, a robust outlier removal process, and an adaptive blending algorithm, providing a significant advancement in image stitching technology.
**Keywords:** Image stitching, Panorama, Feature matching, SIFT, RANSAC, Image blending, Computer vision.
