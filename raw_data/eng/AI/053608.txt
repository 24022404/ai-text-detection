Graph-structured data is ubiquitous in various domains, from social networks to biological systems, making subgraph recognition a critical task for extracting meaningful patterns. This paper introduces the *Graph Information Bottleneck (GIB)* framework, a novel approach for subgraph recognition that balances the trade-off between information compression and predictive accuracy. The GIB framework leverages information-theoretic principles to identify the most informative subgraphs while minimizing redundant or irrelevant information. We propose a computationally efficient algorithm based on graph neural networks (GNNs) to optimize the GIB objective, enabling robust subgraph recognition in large-scale graphs. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of both accuracy and interpretability, achieving state-of-the-art performance in tasks such as molecular property prediction and social network analysis. The GIB framework not only advances the theoretical understanding of subgraph recognition but also offers practical implications for applications in drug discovery, recommendation systems, and anomaly detection. Key contributions include the integration of information bottleneck principles with graph-based learning and the development of a scalable algorithm for real-world graph analysis. Keywords: graph neural networks, subgraph recognition, information bottleneck, graph-structured data, interpretability.
