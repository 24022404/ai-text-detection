**Abstract**

Multi-frame image super-resolution (MISR) aims to reconstruct high-resolution images by fusing information from multiple low-resolution observations, overcoming the inherent limitations of single-frame approaches. This paper introduces a novel double sparse multi-frame image super resolution framework that leverages sparsity priors both in the spatial and temporal dimensions to enhance the reconstruction quality and robustness. Our method first applies a sparse coding strategy to model the underlying high-frequency structures within each frame, followed by a second-stage sparsity-driven fusion across frames to capture inter-frame dependencies and complementary details. Extensive experiments conducted on standard MISR datasets demonstrate that our double sparse approach consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM), while also maintaining lower computational complexity. The results validate the effectiveness of enforcing dual sparsity constraints, which lead to improved detail preservation and artifact reduction. This research provides new insights into sparsity-based MISR algorithms and offers a robust solution for applications in remote sensing, video surveillance, and medical imaging where high-quality image reconstruction from multiple frames is critical. Keywords: multi-frame super resolution, image reconstruction, double sparsity, sparse coding, image enhancement, MISR.
