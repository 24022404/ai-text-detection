Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have achieved remarkable success in various computer vision tasks, but their computational and memory demands often hinder deployment in resource-constrained environments. This paper addresses the critical question of whether retraining is necessary after pruning deep CNN networks to achieve efficiency without sacrificing performance. We propose a systematic evaluation of pruning methods, focusing on their impact on model accuracy, computational efficiency, and retraining requirements. Our approach includes structured and unstructured pruning techniques, combined with an analysis of layer sensitivity and sparsity patterns. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that certain pruning strategies can achieve significant model compression (up to 70% reduction in parameters) with minimal accuracy loss, even without retraining. Furthermore, we identify scenarios where retraining is essential to recover performance, particularly in highly compressed models. The findings provide practical insights for deploying efficient CNN models in real-world applications, balancing the trade-offs between model size, computational cost, and accuracy. This work contributes to the growing body of research on model optimization, offering a framework for decision-making in pruning and retraining processes.  

**Keywords**: Deep Learning, Convolutional Neural Networks, Model Pruning, Efficiency, Retraining, Model Compression.
