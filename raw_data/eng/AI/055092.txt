In the rapidly evolving field of machine learning, deploying large-scale neural networks is resource-intensive and often impractical for edge devices with limited computational power. This paper addresses this challenge by introducing a method for compressing adversarial networks while maintaining performance and robustness.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel adversarial network compression framework that combines pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation techniques. This approach leverages sparsity induced by adversarial training and optimizes network architectures through a systematic reduction in the number of parameters and computational complexity, without significant loss of accuracy.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that the proposed method achieves a substantial reduction in model size, achieving up to 70% compression compared to the original networks, while preserving over 95% of the adversarial accuracy. Comparisons with existing network compression techniques indicate superior performance in both compression ratio and preservation of adversarial robustness.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field by offering a scalable solution for deploying adversarial networks on resource-constrained devices. The implications are significant for enhancing the accessibility of robust AI models in real-time applications such as mobile and embedded systems. Our results underline the potential for adversarial network compression methods to transform the landscape of edge computing, enabling broader deployment and efficiency.

Keywords: adversarial networks, network compression, pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, edge computing, model efficiency.
