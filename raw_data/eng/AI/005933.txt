The interpretability of artificial neural networks (ANNs) has emerged as a critical challenge in the field of artificial intelligence (AI), particularly as these models grow in complexity and are increasingly deployed in high-stakes applications. This paper presents a comprehensive survey of existing approaches, techniques, and frameworks aimed at enhancing the interpretability of ANNs. We begin by defining the key challenges associated with understanding and explaining the decision-making processes of neural networks, including issues related to transparency, trust, and accountability. Next, we categorize and analyze a wide range of interpretability methods, such as feature attribution, model simplification, and post-hoc explanation techniques, while highlighting their strengths and limitations. Our findings reveal that while significant progress has been made, there remains a need for more robust and scalable solutions that balance interpretability with model performance. The survey concludes by identifying open research questions and proposing future directions, including the integration of interpretability into the design of neural architectures and the development of standardized evaluation metrics. This work aims to provide researchers and practitioners with a structured overview of the field, fostering advancements in the development of interpretable and trustworthy AI systems.  

**Keywords**: artificial neural networks, interpretability, explainable AI, feature attribution, model transparency, post-hoc explanations.
