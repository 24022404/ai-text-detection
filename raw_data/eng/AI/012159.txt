This paper addresses the challenge of scene searching by developing a method that abstracts individual objects within a scene to improve search efficiency and accuracy. The aim is to enhance the ability to search through complex environments by focusing on abstract representations rather than detailed specifics of each object.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel scene abstraction algorithm that utilizes a combination of computer vision techniques and semantic segmentation to represent objects within a scene at a higher abstraction level. The approach involves creating a hierarchical abstraction model that categorizes and represents objects based on shared attributes, reducing the complexity of scene interpretation.

Results/Findings: Our method was tested on a variety of scene datasets and demonstrated significant improvements in search performance. The abstraction model enabled faster processing times and increased search accuracy when compared to traditional object-specific search methodologies. Quantitative results show that our approach reduces computational overhead by up to 30%, while maintaining or improving the precision-recall metrics for scene retrieval tasks.

Conclusion/Implications: The research presents an effective strategy for enhancing scene searching by abstracting object details, which has broad implications for applications in autonomous systems, digital content management, and augmented reality environments. This abstraction-based approach provides a scalable solution to the growing complexity of scene analysis in both real-time and large-scale image databases. Future work could explore further refinements in abstraction techniques and their integration into advanced AI-driven systems.

Keywords: scene abstraction, semantic segmentation, computer vision, search efficiency, hierarchical model, object representation.
