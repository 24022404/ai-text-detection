Q-learning, a widely used reinforcement learning algorithm, often suffers from overestimation bias, leading to suboptimal policies. This paper introduces Maxmin Q-learning, a novel approach designed to mitigate this bias. Maxmin Q-learning modifies the standard Q-learning update rule by using the minimum Q-value estimate across multiple independently learned Q-functions, instead of the maximum, to reduce the impact of overestimated values. We evaluate Maxmin Q-learning on a suite of benchmark control tasks, comparing its performance against standard Q-learning and Double Q-learning. Empirical results demonstrate that Maxmin Q-learning significantly reduces overestimation bias and achieves improved performance, particularly in environments with stochastic transitions and sparse rewards. The reduction in estimation bias leads to more stable and reliable learning, enhancing the applicability of Q-learning in complex control problems. This method offers a practical and effective way to improve the performance of Q-learning without introducing significant computational overhead.
