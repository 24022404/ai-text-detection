This study addresses the challenge of enhancing driving experiences by tailoring climate control systems to the individual preferences of drivers. The research explores how personalized climate settings can be mined and implemented in intelligent driving assistants to increase comfort and satisfaction, improving overall driver safety and performance.

Methods/Approach: We employed machine learning algorithms to analyze and predict drivers' climate preferences based on historical data, behavior patterns, and environmental conditions. Our model leverages both supervised learning for initial preference modeling and reinforcement learning for real-time adjustments. The system was integrated into an assistant driving platform, allowing dynamic personalization of in-vehicle climate settings.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our personalized climate control system achieves a significant improvement in driver comfort levels compared to traditional static settings. The model showed an accuracy rate of over 85% in matching drivers' preferred climate settings and adapted swiftly to changing preferences. Comparative analysis indicates superior performance against baseline methods without personalization features.

Conclusion/Implications: Our research highlights the potential of combining personalized data mining with driving assistance systems to provide customized user experiences. The implications extend beyond user comfort, suggesting enhancements in user satisfaction and potential reductions in distraction-related incidents. This approach can be adapted to other in-car personalization technologies, paving the way for the development of intelligent, user-centric vehicular environments.

Keywords: personalized climate control, assistant driving, machine learning, driver comfort, in-vehicle systems, data mining, user preferences.
