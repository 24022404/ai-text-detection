**Abstract:**

Graph topology inference is a critical task in machine learning, with applications spanning social network analysis, biological systems, and sensor networks. Despite significant advancements in algorithms for graph reconstruction and topology learning, the absence of standardized benchmarks hinders fair evaluation and comparison of new methods. This paper presents a comprehensive suite of benchmarks specifically designed for graph topology inference in machine learning contexts. Our benchmarks encompass a diverse set of synthetic and real-world graph datasets, evaluation metrics, and testing protocols suitable for a wide range of algorithms, including both classical statistical approaches and recent deep learning techniques. We conduct an extensive experimental study, analyzing the performance of existing state-of-the-art graph inference models using the proposed benchmarks. The results provide new insights into the strengths and limitations of different algorithmic families, highlighting the impact of graph size, sparsity, noise, and underlying structure on inference accuracy. Our findings underscore the need for robust, consistent evaluation strategies and facilitate more transparent development in the field. The benchmark suite is made publicly available to support the community in advancing research on graph learning, enabling reproducibility and encouraging the development of novel graph topology inference methods. **Keywords:** graph topology inference, benchmarking, machine learning, graph neural networks, evaluation protocols, reproducibility.
