Zero-shot image recognition (ZSL) aims to recognize unseen object classes by transferring knowledge from seen classes, leveraging semantic relationships between visual and semantic spaces. This paper introduces a novel Graph-based Visual-Semantic Entanglement Network (GVSE-Net) to explicitly model complex semantic correlations and enhance visual-semantic feature entanglement for improved ZSL performance. GVSE-Net constructs a semantic graph to capture inter-class relationships and employs a graph neural network to propagate semantic information across nodes, generating enhanced semantic embeddings. Furthermore, a visual-semantic entanglement module is proposed to facilitate fine-grained feature interaction and alignment. This module adaptively learns the dependencies between visual and semantic features, promoting a more discriminative joint embedding space. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that GVSE-Net outperforms state-of-the-art ZSL methods. The proposed approach achieves significant improvements in both conventional ZSL and generalized ZSL settings, showcasing the effectiveness of graph-based semantic modeling and visual-semantic entanglement for knowledge transfer. Keywords: Zero-shot learning, graph neural networks, visual-semantic embedding, image recognition, knowledge transfer.
