Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have emerged as a powerful framework for generating high-quality synthetic data across various domains. However, evaluating the performance of GANs remains a significant challenge due to the lack of standardized and universally accepted metrics. This study conducts an empirical analysis of existing evaluation metrics for GANs, aiming to identify their strengths, limitations, and suitability for different applications. We systematically compare metrics such as Inception Score (IS), Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), and Precision-Recall for Generative Models (PR), among others, using a diverse set of datasets and GAN architectures. Our findings reveal that no single metric comprehensively captures all aspects of GAN performance, and the choice of metric significantly impacts the perceived quality of generated outputs. Furthermore, we propose a hybrid evaluation framework that combines multiple metrics to provide a more holistic assessment of GANs. The results highlight the importance of context-aware metric selection and offer practical guidelines for researchers and practitioners. This work contributes to the ongoing discourse on GAN evaluation by providing empirical insights and recommendations for improving the reliability and interpretability of GAN assessments.  

**Keywords**: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), evaluation metrics, Inception Score (IS), Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), synthetic data, hybrid evaluation framework.
