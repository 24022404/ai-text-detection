This paper presents a novel approach to unsupervised geometry learning, leveraging holistic 3D motion understanding to extract meaningful geometric information from images. The objective is to develop a system that can accurately infer 3D structure from 2D observations without requiring explicit supervision. Our method employs a unique combination of computer vision and machine learning techniques, utilizing a transformer-based architecture to model complex 3D motions and reason about the underlying geometry. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance in unsupervised 3D reconstruction and geometry estimation tasks. Key findings include the ability to accurately recover 3D shapes, camera poses, and scene layouts from monocular video sequences. The proposed framework has significant implications for various applications, including robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented reality, where accurate 3D understanding is crucial. By emphasizing the importance of every pixel in the input images, our work contributes to the development of more robust and generalizable 3D vision systems, with potential applications in fields such as scene understanding, object recognition, and 3D reconstruction. Relevant keywords: unsupervised learning, 3D reconstruction, geometry estimation, transformer architecture, computer vision, machine learning.
