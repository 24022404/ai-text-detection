The increasing adoption of machine learning (ML) in financial services has raised concerns about fairness and ethical implications, particularly in high-stakes decision-making processes such as credit scoring and loan approvals. This paper investigates the hidden technical debts associated with ensuring fairness in ML models deployed in financial systems. We identify and analyze key challenges, including biases in training data, trade-offs between fairness and model performance, and the long-term maintenance costs of fairness-aware algorithms. To address these issues, we propose a systematic framework that integrates fairness constraints into the ML pipeline while minimizing technical debt. Our approach leverages fairness-aware optimization techniques and model interpretability tools to ensure compliance with regulatory standards and ethical guidelines. Experimental results on real-world financial datasets demonstrate that our framework achieves a balance between fairness and predictive accuracy, outperforming traditional fairness-agnostic models. The findings highlight the importance of proactive management of technical debt in fairness-focused ML systems, offering actionable insights for practitioners in the financial sector. This research contributes to the growing discourse on ethical AI by providing a scalable and sustainable solution for fair ML deployment in financial services.  

**Keywords**: Fair Machine Learning, Technical Debt, Financial Services, Bias Mitigation, Ethical AI, Model Interpretability.
