Object detection in aerial images presents unique challenges due to the complex backgrounds, varying scales, and orientations of objects. This paper introduces DOTA (Dataset for Object Detection in Aerial Images), a large-scale dataset designed to advance research in this domain. DOTA comprises over 2,800 high-resolution aerial images, annotated with more than 188,000 instances across 15 object categories, including vehicles, ships, and buildings. The dataset addresses key limitations of existing datasets by providing diverse scenes, high-quality annotations, and a wide range of object scales and orientations.  

To evaluate the effectiveness of DOTA, we benchmarked several state-of-the-art object detection algorithms, including Faster R-CNN, YOLOv3, and RetinaNet. Our experiments reveal that current methods struggle with small objects and complex backgrounds, highlighting the need for specialized approaches tailored to aerial imagery. We propose a novel multi-scale detection framework that leverages context-aware feature fusion and adaptive anchor generation, achieving a significant improvement in detection accuracy compared to baseline models.  

The contributions of this work include the release of DOTA, a comprehensive evaluation of existing methods, and a new detection framework that sets a strong baseline for future research. DOTA is expected to serve as a valuable resource for advancing object detection in aerial images, with potential applications in urban planning, environmental monitoring, and disaster response.  

**Keywords**: Aerial imagery, object detection, dataset, multi-scale detection, deep learning, DOTA.
