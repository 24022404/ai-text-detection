This paper addresses the challenge of personalizing climate control systems in vehicles to enhance driver comfort and energy efficiency. The objective is to develop a data-driven approach to mine and predict individual climate preferences based on driving behavior, environmental conditions, and user feedback. To achieve this, we propose a machine learning framework that integrates sensor data from in-car systems, including temperature, humidity, and user interactions, with external weather data. The framework employs clustering techniques to identify patterns in user preferences and a predictive model to adapt climate settings dynamically. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed system achieves a 15% improvement in user satisfaction compared to traditional static climate control systems, while reducing energy consumption by 10%. The findings highlight the potential of personalized climate control to improve driving experiences and contribute to sustainable energy usage. This research provides a foundation for future advancements in intelligent vehicle systems and human-centric AI applications.  

**Keywords**: climate control, personalization, machine learning, driver comfort, energy efficiency, intelligent vehicles.
