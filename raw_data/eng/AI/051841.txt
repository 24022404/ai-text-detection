This paper addresses the challenge of removing undesirable information—such as sensitive attributes or dataset biases—from learned data representations in self-supervised learning frameworks. We propose Conditional Contrastive Learning (CCL), a novel approach that extends standard contrastive learning by conditioning the representation learning process to explicitly suppress specified unwanted features. The method incorporates a conditional objective that guides the neural network to disentangle and minimize the encoding of user-defined undesirable information, while still preserving task-relevant content. We evaluate CCL on benchmark image and text datasets where the removal of confounding attributes, including demographic information and spurious correlations, is critical. Experimental results demonstrate that CCL achieves superior performance in mitigating attribute leakage compared to traditional self-supervised and adversarial approaches, without sacrificing downstream task accuracy. The proposed framework thus enables more robust, fair, and privacy-preserving representations, with broad implications for applications in bias-aware AI, secure machine learning, and ethical data analysis. Keywords: self-supervised learning, contrastive learning, representation learning, information removal, attribute disentanglement, bias mitigation, privacy-preserving AI.
