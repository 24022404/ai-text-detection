Training neural networks on sparse datasets often involves imputing missing values, with zero imputation being a commonly adopted approach due to its simplicity. However, this method introduces a sparsity bias that can significantly degrade model performance and generalization. This paper investigates the limitations of zero imputation and proposes a novel framework to correct sparsity bias in neural network training. Our approach leverages adaptive imputation techniques combined with sparsity-aware regularization to mitigate the adverse effects of missing data. Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, we demonstrate that our method outperforms traditional zero imputation, achieving superior accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the importance of addressing sparsity bias explicitly, offering insights into more effective data preprocessing strategies for machine learning applications. This research contributes to advancing the understanding of handling sparse data in neural networks, with implications for domains such as healthcare, finance, and recommendation systems.  

**Keywords**: sparsity bias, zero imputation, neural networks, adaptive imputation, missing data, regularization, machine learning.
