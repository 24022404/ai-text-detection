This paper addresses the challenge of recognizing new, unseen classes in visual speech recognition, a crucial aspect of human-computer interaction. Our objective is to develop a zero-shot learning approach that leverages Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to learn new classes without requiring labeled training data. We propose a novel GAN-based framework that generates synthetic visual speech samples for unseen classes, enabling our model to learn robust features and improve recognition accuracy. Our approach utilizes a combination of adversarial training and self-supervised learning to generate high-quality synthetic samples that mimic real-world visual speech patterns. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, achieving significant improvements in recognition accuracy for unseen classes compared to traditional zero-shot learning approaches. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of GANs for zero-shot learning in visual speech recognition, and the development of a robust and generalizable framework for recognizing new classes without labeled data. Our work has important implications for applications such as speech recognition, lip reading, and human-computer interaction, and highlights the potential of GANs for improving the accuracy and robustness of visual speech recognition systems. Key keywords: GANs, zero-shot learning, visual speech recognition, adversarial training, self-supervised learning.
