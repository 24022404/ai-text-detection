**Abstract:**

Accurate and robust 3D object detection is critical for autonomous driving and intelligent sensing systems. While current approaches often rely on either camera or LiDAR data independently, each presents inherent limitations in terms of spatial resolution or semantic richness. This paper introduces CLOCs, a novel Camera-LiDAR Object Candidates fusion framework designed to integrate the complementary strengths of 2D image and 3D point cloud modalities for enhanced 3D object detection. The proposed method first generates candidate object proposals separately from camera and LiDAR streams using state-of-the-art detection networks. Subsequently, a fusion module aligns and aggregates these proposals, leveraging advanced confidence scoring and spatial consistency checks to refine detections and suppress false positives. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that CLOCs achieves superior detection accuracy, outperforming single-sensor and conventional early or late fusion strategies, especially in challenging scenarios involving occlusion or limited visibility. Our results highlight the effectiveness of multi-modal data fusion and the importance of candidate-level integration for reliable scene understanding. CLOCs establishes a robust foundation for future research in multi-sensor 3D perception, with potential applications in autonomous vehicles, robotics, and urban surveillance. 

**Keywords:** 3D object detection, sensor fusion, LiDAR, camera, multi-modal integration, autonomous driving, deep learning
