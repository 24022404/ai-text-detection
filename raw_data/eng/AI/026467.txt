This paper introduces RetCL, a novel selection-based approach for retrosynthesis that leverages contrastive learning to predict the most suitable reactants for a given product. The objective is to improve the efficiency and accuracy of retrosynthetic analysis, a crucial step in organic synthesis planning. Our approach employs a contrastive learning framework to learn the representations of reactants and products, enabling the model to identify the most relevant reactants for a target molecule. The results demonstrate that RetCL outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and diversity of predicted reactants. Key findings include improved performance on benchmark datasets and the ability to generalize to unseen molecules. The contributions of this research lie in its innovative application of contrastive learning to retrosynthesis, offering a promising direction for future developments in computer-aided synthesis planning. This work has significant implications for the field of organic chemistry, particularly in the design and optimization of synthetic routes. Relevant keywords: retrosynthesis, contrastive learning, organic synthesis, reactant selection, computer-aided synthesis planning.
