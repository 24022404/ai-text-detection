This paper addresses the challenge of spatial mapping in complex environments, where traditional methods often struggle with nonstationary data and computational inefficiency. We propose a novel approach that combines Gaussian Processes (GPs) with nonstationary Fourier features to enhance spatial modeling accuracy and scalability. The method leverages the flexibility of GPs for probabilistic modeling while incorporating nonstationary Fourier features to capture spatially varying patterns effectively. Our approach introduces an adaptive kernel design that dynamically adjusts to local data characteristics, enabling more precise mapping in heterogeneous environments. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in both prediction accuracy and computational efficiency compared to existing stationary and nonstationary GP-based methods. The proposed framework is validated on real-world datasets, including urban terrain and environmental monitoring scenarios, showcasing its robustness and applicability. This research contributes to advancing spatial mapping techniques by offering a scalable and adaptive solution for nonstationary data, with potential applications in autonomous navigation, environmental modeling, and geospatial analysis.  

**Keywords**: Gaussian Processes, nonstationary Fourier features, spatial mapping, adaptive kernels, probabilistic modeling, geospatial analysis.
