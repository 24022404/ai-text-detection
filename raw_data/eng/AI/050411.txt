This paper introduces Query Twice, a novel meta-learning approach for video summarization, which leverages dual mixture attention to improve the accuracy and efficiency of summarization models. The objective is to develop a method that can effectively capture the most informative and representative frames from a video, given the complexity and variability of video content. Our approach employs a meta-learning framework that combines two attention mechanisms, allowing the model to query the video data twice and refine its understanding of the key frames. The results show that Query Twice outperforms state-of-the-art video summarization methods, achieving significant improvements in terms of summary quality and computational efficiency. The proposed method has important implications for applications such as video analysis, surveillance, and content creation, where efficient and accurate video summarization is crucial. Key contributions of this research include the introduction of dual mixture attention and a meta-learning framework for video summarization, which enables the model to adapt to new, unseen videos and improve its performance over time. Relevant keywords: video summarization, meta-learning, attention mechanisms, deep learning, computer vision.
