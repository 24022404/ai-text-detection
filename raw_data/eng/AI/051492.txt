The objective of this research is to develop an advanced method for retrosynthesis, a critical task in computational chemistry, by introducing a novel approach known as RetCL. Retrosynthesis involves predicting the necessary set of precursor molecules for a given target molecule, a process vital for drug discovery and chemical manufacturing. We propose a selection-based approach using contrastive learning techniques to enhance prediction accuracy and efficiency. RetCL leverages a contrastive learning framework to distinguish and prioritize relevant precursor candidates by embedding both target and potential precursor molecules in a latent space where similar chemical structures are closer, allowing for efficient selection. Our methodology includes training a neural network model on a newly curated large-scale dataset, resulting in a significant improvement in retrosynthesis performance compared to state-of-the-art methods. The results indicate superior prediction accuracy and reduced computational time, validating RetCL's effectiveness. The implications of this study suggest that the integration of contrastive learning into retrosynthesis tasks could streamline the chemical synthesis processes, enable the discovery of novel compounds, and provide a robust tool for the AI-driven transformation of synthetic chemistry. Overall, RetCL offers a groundbreaking contribution to the field by addressing the limitations of previous models and providing a more intuitive, scalable solution. Key keywords include retrosynthesis, contrastive learning, selection-based approach, computational chemistry, and chemical synthesis.
