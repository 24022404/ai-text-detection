This paper addresses the challenge of inverse problems, which entail inferring unknown causes from observed effects, a crucial task in fields such as computer vision and computational imaging. Traditional approaches often rely on supervised learning, necessitating large labeled datasets, which may not always be practical. Methods: We propose an innovative unsupervised method leveraging Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to effectively solve inverse problems. By utilizing GANs, our model can learn to generate plausible solutions from observed data without requiring labeled examples for training. The adversarial training mechanism refines the generator to produce outputs that convincingly resemble real-world data, optimizing the inverse problem solution process. Results: Extensive experiments demonstrate that our approach not only surpasses baseline supervised methods in benchmark inverse problem tasks but also significantly reduces the need for extensive labeled datasets. Our unsupervised GAN-based model exhibits competitive performance with less computational overhead and establishes new state-of-the-art results in synthetic and real-world scenarios. Conclusion: The introduction of GANs into the realm of inverse problem-solving marks a substantial advancement, offering a robust alternative to conventional supervised techniques. This research contributes a scalable, efficient framework with wide-ranging implications for computational tasks requiring inverse problem-solving, thereby broadening the landscape of applications where data scarcity is a constraint. Key keywords include inverse problems, unsupervised learning, generative adversarial networks, computational imaging, and computer vision.
