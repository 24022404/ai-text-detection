Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning (MADRL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for solving complex decision-making tasks in collaborative and competitive environments. However, the scalability and efficiency of MADRL systems are often hindered by the challenges of parameter sharing among agents. This paper revisits the concept of parameter sharing in MADRL, aiming to address its limitations and explore novel strategies to enhance learning performance. We propose a hybrid approach that dynamically adjusts parameter sharing based on agent-specific task requirements and environmental dynamics. Our method integrates adaptive mechanisms to balance shared knowledge and individual learning, ensuring robust performance across diverse scenarios. Through extensive experiments on benchmark environments, we demonstrate that our approach significantly improves convergence speed and final task performance compared to traditional fixed parameter-sharing strategies. The results highlight the importance of context-aware parameter sharing in MADRL and provide insights into its potential applications in real-world multi-agent systems, such as autonomous vehicles, robotics, and smart grid management. This work contributes to the ongoing advancement of MADRL by offering a scalable and efficient framework for parameter sharing, paving the way for more adaptable and intelligent multi-agent systems.  

**Keywords**: Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning, Parameter Sharing, Adaptive Learning, Scalability, Autonomous Systems.
