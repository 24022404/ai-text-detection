Clinical time series data offers a rich source for predictive modeling in healthcare, yet challenges such as data heterogeneity and the need for robust generalization across diverse clinical tasks remain. This paper introduces a novel multitask learning framework designed to leverage shared representations across multiple clinical prediction tasks using time series data. We employ a deep learning architecture based on recurrent neural networks, adapted to handle irregularly sampled time series and incorporating attention mechanisms for improved feature extraction. The framework is evaluated on a comprehensive benchmark consisting of five distinct clinical prediction tasks, including mortality prediction, diagnosis classification, and length-of-stay estimation, using real-world electronic health records. Results demonstrate that our multitask learning approach consistently outperforms single-task models, achieving significant improvements in predictive accuracy and calibration. Furthermore, we provide an in-depth analysis of task relatedness and the benefits of knowledge transfer across different clinical endpoints. This work establishes a valuable benchmark for evaluating multitask learning algorithms on clinical time series data and highlights the potential of shared representation learning for advancing predictive healthcare. Keywords: Multitask Learning, Clinical Time Series, Electronic Health Records, Deep Learning, Benchmarking, Predictive Healthcare.
