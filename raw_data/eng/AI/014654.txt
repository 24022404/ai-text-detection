This paper proposes a novel approach to person re-identification, focusing on improving the accuracy of matching individuals across different cameras and environments. The objective is to develop an effective method that enhances the discriminative power of deep learning models in this domain. We introduce the Support Neighbor Loss (SNL) function, a new loss mechanism designed to optimize the learning process by emphasizing the importance of hard negative samples and supportive neighbors. Our approach leverages the SNL to learn more robust and generalizable features, which are crucial for person re-identification tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that the SNL outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving significant improvements in rank-1 accuracy and mean Average Precision (mAP) on several benchmark datasets. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of the SNL concept, its integration into a deep learning framework, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in person re-identification. This work has important implications for surveillance, security, and smart city applications, where accurate person re-identification is essential. Keywords: person re-identification, deep learning, Support Neighbor Loss, computer vision, surveillance.
