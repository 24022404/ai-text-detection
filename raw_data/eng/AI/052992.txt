Dimensionality reduction (DR) techniques are widely used to visualize and analyze high-dimensional data, but interpreting their results remains challenging. This paper introduces a novel framework that leverages contrastive learning to enhance the analysis of DR outcomes. The objective is to provide a more interpretable and insightful representation of reduced-dimensional data by emphasizing meaningful contrasts between data points. Our approach integrates contrastive learning with traditional DR methods, enabling the identification of key patterns and relationships that might otherwise be overlooked. We evaluate the framework on multiple datasets, demonstrating its ability to improve the interpretability of DR results while maintaining computational efficiency. Comparative experiments show that our method outperforms existing approaches in highlighting significant data structures and reducing noise. The findings suggest that contrastive learning can serve as a powerful tool for supporting DR analysis, offering new insights into complex datasets. This work contributes to the field by bridging the gap between dimensionality reduction and interpretability, with potential applications in data visualization, clustering, and exploratory data analysis.  

**Keywords**: dimensionality reduction, contrastive learning, interpretability, data visualization, exploratory data analysis.
