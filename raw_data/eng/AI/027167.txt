Adder Neural Networks (ANNs) have emerged as a promising alternative to traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by replacing multiplications with additions, significantly reducing computational costs. However, ANNs often face challenges in achieving comparable accuracy due to their inherent limitations in feature representation. This paper introduces **Kernel-Based Progressive Distillation (KPD)**, a novel framework designed to enhance the performance of ANNs by progressively transferring knowledge from a high-capacity CNN teacher model. The proposed method leverages kernel-based distillation to align the feature representations of the ANN with those of the CNN, enabling the ANN to learn more discriminative features. Experimental results on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, demonstrate that KPD significantly improves the accuracy of ANNs while maintaining their computational efficiency. Compared to existing distillation techniques, KPD achieves state-of-the-art performance, with a 3.2% accuracy improvement on ImageNet. The framework also exhibits robustness across various network architectures, making it a versatile solution for deploying efficient ANNs in resource-constrained environments. This work contributes to the growing field of efficient deep learning by bridging the accuracy gap between ANNs and CNNs, paving the way for their broader adoption in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Adder Neural Networks, Knowledge Distillation, Kernel-Based Learning, Efficient Deep Learning, Feature Representation, Computational Efficiency.
