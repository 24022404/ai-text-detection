Contrastive representation learning has shown promise in learning visual representations without relying on manual annotations. However, existing methods often struggle to capture fine-grained details and contextual relationships within images, limiting their ability to generalize across diverse datasets. This paper introduces a novel Center-wise Local Image Mixture (CLIM) approach designed to enhance contrastive learning by focusing on local image features and their relationships. CLIM generates new views by mixing local regions centered around key points detected within an image. This mixing process encourages the model to learn more robust and discriminative representations by considering various combinations of local features. We evaluate CLIM on several benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet. The results demonstrate that CLIM consistently outperforms state-of-the-art contrastive learning methods, achieving significant improvements in downstream classification accuracy. Furthermore, we analyze the learned representations and show that CLIM effectively captures fine-grained details and contextual information. Our findings suggest that focusing on local image mixtures is a promising direction for improving contrastive representation learning. Keywords: Contrastive Learning, Self-Supervised Learning, Image Representation, Image Mixing, Local Features.
