Abstract:

Monocular face reconstruction aims to produce accurate 3D facial geometry and rich reflectance properties from a single 2D image, a challenging task critical to applications in virtual reality, animation, biometrics, and human-computer interaction. This paper proposes a novel framework that integrates self-supervised learning with differentiable ray tracing to achieve high-fidelity monocular face reconstruction with detailed geometry and realistic reflectance modeling. Our approach leverages a self-supervised loss formulation, eliminating the need for ground truth 3D data during training and enhancing generalization to in-the-wild images. By embedding differentiable ray tracing into the reconstruction pipeline, we enable precise simulation of complex light interactions, including specular and diffuse reflections, which leads to superior recovery of skin texture, albedo, and facial details. Experimental results on publicly available benchmarks demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art techniques in terms of geometric accuracy and photorealism under diverse lighting and pose conditions. Qualitative analysis further confirms the effectiveness of our system in capturing subtle facial features and producing high-quality reconstructions. This work offers significant advancements in single-image 3D face reconstruction, providing a robust, scalable solution for real-world scenarios. Keywords: monocular face reconstruction, self-supervised learning, ray tracing, 3D geometry, reflectance modeling, photorealistic rendering.
