This paper introduces GRASPEL, a novel framework for graph spectral learning at scale. The objective is to efficiently process and analyze large-scale graph-structured data, addressing the limitations of existing methods in terms of scalability and computational complexity. GRASPEL leverages a combination of advanced graph decomposition techniques and distributed computing architectures to enable the learning of graph spectral representations at unprecedented scales. The approach is based on a scalable graph filtering algorithm, which allows for the efficient computation of graph spectral features in parallel. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of GRASPEL in learning accurate graph spectral representations on large-scale graphs, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency. The proposed framework has significant implications for a wide range of applications, including graph neural networks, recommendation systems, and network analysis. Key contributions of this work include the development of a scalable graph spectral learning algorithm, the introduction of a novel graph decomposition technique, and the demonstration of the framework's effectiveness on large-scale real-world graphs. Relevant keywords: graph spectral learning, scalable graph processing, distributed computing, graph neural networks, network analysis.
