This paper introduces SVDistNet, a novel self-supervised learning framework designed for accurate near-field distance estimation using surround view fisheye cameras. The primary objective of this research is to overcome the challenges posed by conventional supervised distance estimation methods, which often require extensive labeled datasets for training. The proposed method utilizes a unique self-supervised approach that leverages the inherent properties of surround view fisheye images to generate training signals without the need for ground truth distance annotations. By exploiting geometric and photometric consistency between overlapping views, SVDistNet facilitates robust distance estimation even in dynamic and complex scenes. 

The framework employs a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture tailored for the distorted nature of fisheye imagery, enabling effective feature extraction and distance prediction. Experimental results highlight the superiority of SVDistNet, demonstrating significant improvements over traditional methods when applied to real-world driving data. The system's performance is rigorously validated through comparisons with established baseline models, showcasing enhancements in both accuracy and generalization capabilities. 

The implications of this research are profound, providing a scalable and cost-effective solution for distance estimation in autonomous driving and advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS). SVDistNet's ability to function without manual annotation significantly reduces development time and resource requirements, paving the way for broader application in real-time vehicular environments. Keywords for this study include self-supervised learning, fisheye cameras, distance estimation, convolutional neural networks, and autonomous driving.
