This paper explores the development of agents with distinct behaviors, namely prosocial and selfish, for contract negotiation using reinforcement learning. The objective is to investigate how different behavioral models impact negotiation outcomes and relationships between agents. We employ a reinforcement learning approach, utilizing Q-learning and Deep Q-Networks, to train agents with prosocial and selfish tendencies. Our results show that prosocial agents achieve higher joint utilities and more cooperative relationships, while selfish agents prioritize individual gains. The findings have significant implications for the design of autonomous negotiation systems, highlighting the importance of considering agent behavior and its impact on negotiation outcomes. This research contributes to the field of artificial intelligence and multi-agent systems, with potential applications in e-commerce, supply chain management, and conflict resolution. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, contract negotiation, prosocial behavior, selfish behavior, multi-agent systems, autonomous negotiation.
