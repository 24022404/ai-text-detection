This paper introduces a novel Deep Bayesian Unsupervised Lifelong Learning (DBULL) framework designed to address catastrophic forgetting and enhance knowledge transfer in continually learning environments. DBULL leverages a deep neural network architecture integrated with Bayesian nonparametrics to dynamically expand model capacity and infer task-specific latent representations without supervision. Our approach utilizes a hierarchical Dirichlet process mixture model to cluster data streams and adaptively allocate network resources to new tasks. This facilitates the preservation of previously learned knowledge while effectively learning new information. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that DBULL significantly outperforms existing unsupervised lifelong learning methods in terms of both accuracy and forgetting rate. Furthermore, the Bayesian formulation provides inherent uncertainty estimation, enhancing the robustness of the learning process. The DBULL framework offers a promising direction for developing intelligent systems capable of autonomously learning and adapting in dynamic and evolving environments. Keywords: Lifelong Learning, Unsupervised Learning, Bayesian Nonparametrics, Deep Learning, Catastrophic Forgetting, Knowledge Transfer.
