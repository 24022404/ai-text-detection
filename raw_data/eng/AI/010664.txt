Active contours, or snakes, are widely used for image segmentation. However, traditional active contour models often suffer from initialization sensitivity and difficulty in handling topological changes. This paper introduces a novel approach to active contour evolution based on solving Eikonal equations, reformulating the problem of contour propagation as the computation of minimal geodesic paths. Our method utilizes a fast marching algorithm to efficiently compute the distance function from an initial contour. This distance function is then used to guide the evolution of the contour towards desired image features by identifying the shortest path in a cost landscape derived from image gradients. We demonstrate that this approach significantly reduces sensitivity to initial contour placement and naturally handles topological changes such as splitting and merging. Experimental results on synthetic and real images show that our method achieves superior segmentation accuracy and robustness compared to traditional active contour models and level set methods, particularly in images with complex structures and weak boundaries. The proposed method offers a robust and efficient alternative for active contour-based image segmentation.
Keywords: Active Contours, Image Segmentation, Eikonal Equation, Geodesic Paths, Fast Marching Algorithm.
