Temporal Difference (TD) learning is a fundamental algorithm in reinforcement learning, enabling agents to learn value functions from experience. However, standard TD methods often suffer from slow convergence, particularly in environments with delayed rewards. This paper introduces a novel approach that integrates source traces with TD learning to accelerate learning speed and improve overall performance. Our method, Source-TD, maintains eligibility traces originating from significant state transitions, allowing for more efficient credit assignment. We evaluate Source-TD on a range of benchmark reinforcement learning tasks, comparing its performance against standard TD learning and other eligibility trace methods like TD($\lambda$). Results demonstrate that Source-TD achieves significantly faster convergence and higher asymptotic performance in environments with sparse and delayed rewards. Furthermore, we provide a theoretical analysis of Source-TD, demonstrating its convergence properties. The integration of source traces provides a powerful mechanism for improving the efficiency and effectiveness of TD learning, offering a promising direction for future research in reinforcement learning.
Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Temporal Difference Learning, Eligibility Traces, Credit Assignment, Convergence.
