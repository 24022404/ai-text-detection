This paper presents a novel approach to sparse coding and dictionary learning by introducing Confident Kernel Sparse Coding (CKSC), a framework that effectively combines the power of kernel methods with a confidence-guided mechanism for robust data representation. The objective of our research is to address the limitations of traditional sparse coding, particularly in handling non-linear data distributions and dealing with uncertainty in the representation process. Our method leverages kernel functions to implicitly map data into a higher-dimensional feature space, enabling the capture of complex, non-linear relationships. At the core of CKSC lies a confidence estimation model that dynamically adjusts the sparse coding and dictionary update process, promoting more reliable and discriminative representations. Extensive experiments on image classification and signal reconstruction benchmarks demonstrate significant improvements in reconstruction accuracy, classification performance, and robustness to noise compared to state-of-the-art sparse coding and kernel dictionary learning techniques. The proposed CKSC framework is scalable, adaptable to various kernels, and can be seamlessly integrated into existing machine learning pipelines. Our findings suggest that confidence-aware kernel-based dictionary learning offers a promising direction for a wide range of applications in computer vision, signal processing, and information retrieval. Keywords: sparse coding, dictionary learning, kernel methods, confidence estimation, non-linear representation, robust learning.
