This paper addresses the challenge of automatic surface defect segmentation by introducing a novel approach based on Neural Style Transfer (NST) for simulating defective samples. The objective is to enhance the accuracy of defect detection models by generating a diverse set of synthetic defective samples that mimic real-world surface defects. Our method utilizes NST to transfer the style of real defects onto healthy surface images, creating realistic and varied defective samples. The results show that our approach significantly improves the performance of defect segmentation models, especially in cases where real defective samples are scarce. Key findings indicate that the simulated defective samples can effectively augment limited datasets, leading to better generalization and robustness of defect detection systems. The proposed technique has important implications for quality control and inspection in various industries, such as manufacturing and construction, where automatic surface defect detection is crucial. Our research contributes to the development of more accurate and reliable defect segmentation models, highlighting the potential of Neural Style Transfer for simulating realistic surface defects. Keywords: Neural Style Transfer, surface defect segmentation, automatic defect detection, synthetic data generation, quality control.
