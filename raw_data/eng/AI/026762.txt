Image captioning, a critical task in computer vision and natural language processing, aims to generate descriptive and contextually accurate textual summaries of images. While significant progress has been made in improving caption quality, most existing models tend to produce generic and repetitive descriptions, lacking the diversity and richness observed in human-generated captions. This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing diversity in image captioning while maintaining relevance and accuracy. We propose a novel approach that integrates a diversity-aware training mechanism into a transformer-based architecture, leveraging multi-modal embeddings and reinforcement learning to balance diversity and fidelity. Our method introduces a diversity metric that evaluates the variability of generated captions, ensuring that the model captures a broader range of descriptive possibilities. Experimental results on benchmark datasets, such as MS-COCO and Flickr30k, demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art models in terms of both diversity and caption quality, as measured by metrics like CIDEr, SPICE, and a newly proposed diversity score. The findings highlight the importance of diversity in making image captioning systems more human-like and applicable to real-world scenarios, such as assistive technologies and content generation. This work contributes to advancing the field by providing a framework for generating more varied and contextually rich captions, bridging the gap between machine-generated and human-like descriptions.  

**Keywords**: Image captioning, diversity, transformer models, reinforcement learning, multi-modal embeddings, human-like descriptions.
