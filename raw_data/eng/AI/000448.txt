This paper addresses the challenges of traditional deep reinforcement learning (DRL) systems, which often struggle with high computational cost and complex environment dynamics. We propose a novel approach involving a surrogate agent-environment interface to enhance learning efficiency and scalability.

Methods/Approach: Our method involves the development of a surrogate interface that accurately mimics environment responses, allowing the reinforcement learning agent to interact in a simplified yet effective manner. The interface is generated through a combination of neural network architectures and dynamic modeling, which enables the surrogate to predict environment states and outcomes with reduced computational requirements. This surrogate framework is integrated into a standard DRL framework, leveraging policy gradient methods to refine agent behavior while lessening direct environment interactions.

Results/Findings: Experiments demonstrate that the surrogate interface substantially reduces the computational cost of DRL training, achieving similar or improved performance on benchmark tasks compared to conventional approaches. In a series of trials across several standard environments, our surrogate-assisted DRL model shows faster convergence rates and enhanced stability, highlighting its potential for real-time and complex applications.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of a surrogate agent-environment interface represents a significant advancement in DRL methodologies, offering both practical and theoretical contributions. By providing an efficient mechanism for environment interaction, our approach paves the way for scalable DRL applications in scenarios previously deemed too resource-intensive or complex. This method is poised to impact domains such as robotics, autonomous systems, and online decision-making processes.

Keywords: Deep Reinforcement Learning, Surrogate Interface, Neural Networks, Policy Gradient, Computational Efficiency, Scalability, Environment Modeling.
