Monocular depth estimation is crucial for autonomous navigation, yet it struggles in dynamic environments due to occlusions and moving objects. This paper introduces R4Dyn, a novel self-supervised framework leveraging radar data to improve monocular depth estimation in dynamic scenes. R4Dyn uses radar point clouds as a geometric prior to guide the learning process, specifically addressing the challenges posed by independently moving objects. We propose a robust loss function that incorporates radar-derived motion cues to differentiate between static and dynamic regions. This allows the network to learn more accurate depth representations, especially for dynamic objects, without relying on ground truth depth. Experiments on the nuScenes dataset demonstrate that R4Dyn outperforms state-of-the-art self-supervised monocular depth estimation methods in dynamic environments, achieving significant improvements in depth accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the potential of radar as a valuable sensor for self-supervised learning in complex, real-world scenarios. Keywords: Monocular Depth Estimation, Self-Supervised Learning, Radar, Dynamic Scenes, Autonomous Navigation.
