This study investigates the cross-sensor portability of neural network architectures for LiDAR-based semantic labeling, a critical challenge in 3D scene understanding. While deep learning models have shown remarkable success in semantic segmentation, their performance often degrades when applied to data from different LiDAR sensors due to variations in point cloud density, resolution, and noise characteristics. To address this, we evaluate the adaptability of state-of-the-art neural network architectures, including PointNet++, KPConv, and RandLA-Net, across multiple LiDAR datasets captured by distinct sensors. Our approach involves training models on one dataset and testing their generalization capabilities on others, with and without fine-tuning. Experimental results reveal significant performance disparities, highlighting the sensitivity of these architectures to sensor-specific characteristics. We propose a lightweight domain adaptation technique to enhance cross-sensor portability, achieving a 15% improvement in mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) on unseen datasets. This research provides valuable insights into the challenges of deploying LiDAR-based semantic labeling systems in real-world scenarios and offers practical solutions for improving model robustness across diverse sensor environments. The findings contribute to advancing autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality applications by enabling more reliable 3D perception systems.  

**Keywords**: LiDAR, semantic labeling, neural networks, cross-sensor portability, domain adaptation, 3D scene understanding.
