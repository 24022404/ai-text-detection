This paper introduces ICON, a novel framework for learning regular maps in the context of geometric deep learning by leveraging the principle of inverse consistency. The objective is to address the challenge of constructing smooth and invertible correspondences between complex geometric domains, a fundamental problem in computer vision, 3D reconstruction, and medical imaging. ICON formulates the map learning process as a bi-directional optimization problem that explicitly enforces inverse consistency, ensuring that the forward and backward mappings are mutually consistent and regularized. The approach combines neural network-based mapping functions with a consistency loss, enabling the resulting maps to be smooth, continuous, and robust to noise or perturbations. Extensive experiments on various benchmarks—including synthetic and real-world datasets—demonstrate that ICON significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy, regularity, and invertibility of the learned maps. The proposed framework also exhibits enhanced generalization to unseen data and complex shapes. In conclusion, ICON provides a reliable and scalable solution for learning regularity-preserving maps, with broad applicability in areas such as shape matching, non-rigid registration, and morphable model construction. Key contributions include the development of the inverse consistency-driven optimization scheme and empirical evidence of improved map quality. Keywords: geometric deep learning, inverse consistency, map regularity, shape correspondence, 3D reconstruction, neural networks.
