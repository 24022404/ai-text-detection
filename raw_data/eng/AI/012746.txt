This paper introduces a novel approach to synthesizing realistic face photographs from simple line drawings, enabling attribute-level control over the generated images. Our method leverages a generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture, incorporating a novel attribute conditioning mechanism that allows users to manipulate specific facial features, such as age, gender, and expression, directly from the line drawing input. The network consists of a generator that translates the line drawing into a photorealistic image and a discriminator that distinguishes between real and synthesized images. Crucially, attribute control is achieved by integrating attribute vectors into both the generator and discriminator, guiding the synthesis process. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach produces high-quality face photographs that faithfully reflect the input line drawing while accurately reflecting the desired attribute modifications. Quantitative evaluations and user studies confirm the superior performance of our method compared to existing line drawing to face synthesis techniques, particularly in terms of attribute controllability and image realism. This work contributes a practical tool for image editing, face generation, and potentially artistic expression. Keywords: Face synthesis, Line drawing, Generative adversarial networks, Attribute control, Image generation.
