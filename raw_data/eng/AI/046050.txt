This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised video object segmentation (UVOS) by introducing a novel approach that leverages instance embedding transfer. In UVOS, the objective is to automatically identify and segment distinct objects in video sequences without manual annotations or prior information.

Methods/Approach: We propose a method that utilizes instance embedding transfer, where learned features from pre-trained models on image-centric tasks are adapted to the video domain. The approach employs advanced embedding techniques to capture object-related information across frames, enhancing the segmentation capabilities in dynamic video environments. A key component is the integration of a transformation model that aligns the embeddings with video-specific characteristics, ensuring effective segmentation across varied conditions.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that the proposed instance embedding transfer method significantly improves segmentation accuracy compared to existing unsupervised techniques. Extensive evaluation on benchmark datasets shows an increase in precision and robustness in detecting and segmenting objects, particularly in challenging video scenes with multiple moving entities and occlusions. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse video settings, highlighting its adaptability and effectiveness.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a novel strategy for UVOS by exploiting instance embeddings, showcasing the potential of transfer learning in bridging the gap between image and video understanding. The findings suggest that instance embedding transfer can be a pivotal element in advancing unsupervised video analysis. Future applications could extend to areas such as autonomous navigation, video surveillance, and augmented reality, where real-time object segmentation is crucial.

Keywords: unsupervised video object segmentation, instance embedding transfer, video domain adaptation, segmentation accuracy, transfer learning, video analysis.
