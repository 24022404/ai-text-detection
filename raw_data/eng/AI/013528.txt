**Abstract**

Accurate prediction of future patterns in time series data is fundamental to a wide range of applications, yet traditional loss functions often fail to account for both the temporal and structural characteristics critical to robust forecasting. This paper introduces Shape and Time Distortion Loss (STDL), a novel loss function designed to enhance the training of deep time series forecasting models. Unlike conventional approaches that primarily focus on point-wise errors, STDL integrates measures of both shape similarity and temporal alignment, explicitly penalizing misalignments and deformations in predicted sequences. We incorporate STDL into state-of-the-art deep neural architectures, including convolutional and recurrent models, and evaluate performance on multiple real-world datasets from domains such as finance, energy, and healthcare. Empirical results demonstrate that models trained with STDL consistently outperform those using standard loss functions, achieving improvements in prediction accuracy and preserving essential sequence dynamics. The proposed approach not only advances the accuracy and resilience of time series forecasts but also offers a principled framework for capturing complex dependencies in sequential data. These findings highlight the effectiveness and versatility of STDL, paving the way for improved deployment of deep learning models in critical forecasting tasks. **Keywords:** time series forecasting, deep learning, loss function, shape distortion, temporal alignment, neural networks, predictive modeling.
