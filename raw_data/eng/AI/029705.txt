This study aims to develop a convolutional neural network (CNN) for detecting gaze preferences, a potential indicator of autism spectrum disorder (ASD) in children. Our approach utilizes a deep learning model to analyze eye-tracking data and identify patterns associated with ASD. The CNN is trained on a dataset of eye movements from children with and without ASD, allowing it to learn subtle differences in gaze behavior. Our results show that the model achieves high accuracy in distinguishing between the two groups, with a significant improvement over traditional machine learning methods. The proposed system has important implications for the early diagnosis and intervention of ASD, enabling clinicians to identify potential cases more efficiently. Our research contributes to the growing field of AI-assisted diagnostics, highlighting the potential of CNNs in analyzing complex behavioral data. Key findings include the development of a robust gaze preference detection system, with applications in autism diagnosis, eye-tracking analysis, and deep learning-based healthcare solutions.
