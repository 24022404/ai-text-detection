This paper introduces a novel approach for zero-shot synthesis tasks by leveraging group-supervised learning to improve generalization and performance in data-scarce scenarios. Unlike conventional supervised or unsupervised synthesis methods that require large annotated datasets or domain-specific prior knowledge, our technique utilizes group-level supervision to identify and exploit latent structure shared across groups of related samples. We propose a unified framework that combines feature extraction, group representation learning, and synthesis modules, enabling the model to generate high-quality outputs for unseen classes or categories (zero-shot) based solely on group-level information. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves superior results compared to state-of-the-art zero-shot learning and generative models, particularly in image and text synthesis tasks. The findings show significant improvements in fidelity, diversity, and semantic consistency of synthesized data, highlighting the benefits of integrating group-supervised signals. This research advances the field of zero-shot learning by enabling robust synthesis without the need for instance-level labels, paving the way for practical applications in generative AI, data augmentation, and automated content creation. Key keywords include zero-shot learning, synthesis, group-supervision, generative models, and deep learning.
