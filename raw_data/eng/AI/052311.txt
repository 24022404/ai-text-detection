The detection of prohibited items in X-ray security inspections is a critical task for ensuring public safety. However, occlusions caused by overlapping objects in luggage significantly hinder the accuracy of detection systems. This paper addresses the challenge of occluded prohibited items detection by introducing a novel benchmark dataset specifically designed for X-ray security inspection scenarios. The dataset includes a diverse range of occluded objects, providing a robust foundation for evaluating detection algorithms under realistic conditions. To tackle the occlusion problem, we propose a De-occlusion Attention Module (DAM), a lightweight yet effective neural network component that enhances the visibility of occluded regions by focusing on contextual features and spatial relationships. The DAM integrates seamlessly with existing object detection frameworks, improving their ability to identify obscured items. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on the proposed benchmark, outperforming traditional methods by a significant margin. The contributions of this work include the introduction of a comprehensive dataset, the development of an innovative de-occlusion module, and a systematic evaluation of its effectiveness in real-world security inspection scenarios. This research provides valuable insights for advancing X-ray security systems and has potential applications in enhancing automated threat detection in high-traffic environments.  

**Keywords**: X-ray security inspection, occluded object detection, de-occlusion, attention mechanism, prohibited items, benchmark dataset.
