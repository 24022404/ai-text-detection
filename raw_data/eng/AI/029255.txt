This paper introduces Fractal Gaussian Networks, a novel sparse random graph model rooted in Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos. The objective is to create a framework that captures the intricate structures and scaling properties observed in real-world networks. Our approach combines the principles of fractal geometry with the stochastic processes of Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos to generate graphs that exhibit self-similarity and long-range dependence. The model utilizes a multiplicative cascade process to assign weights to edges, resulting in a sparse and fractal network topology. Our results show that Fractal Gaussian Networks can effectively replicate the characteristics of real networks, including power-law degree distributions and community structures. The proposed model contributes to the understanding of complex networks by providing a unique perspective on their structural and dynamical properties. With potential applications in network science, epidemiology, and social network analysis, Fractal Gaussian Networks offer a promising tool for modeling and analyzing complex systems. Key aspects of this work include sparse graph modeling, fractal geometry, Gaussian processes, and network science, making it relevant to researchers in these fields.
