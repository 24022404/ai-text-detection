Retrosynthesis, a critical task in organic chemistry, involves identifying precursor molecules for synthesizing target compounds. Traditional methods often rely on rule-based systems or template-based approaches, which can be limited in scalability and generalizability. This paper introduces **RetCL**, a novel selection-based approach for retrosynthesis that leverages **contrastive learning** to enhance the prediction of synthetic routes. The proposed method employs a deep learning framework to encode molecular structures and their relationships, enabling the model to learn meaningful representations for retrosynthetic planning. By contrasting positive and negative pairs of molecular transformations, RetCL effectively captures the underlying chemical logic and improves the accuracy of precursor selection. Experimental results demonstrate that RetCL outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods on benchmark datasets, achieving higher prediction accuracy and robustness. The approach also exhibits strong generalization capabilities, making it applicable to a wide range of chemical compounds. This work contributes to the field of computational chemistry by introducing a scalable and efficient framework for retrosynthesis, with potential applications in drug discovery and materials science.  

**Keywords**: Retrosynthesis, Contrastive Learning, Deep Learning, Molecular Representation, Chemical Synthesis, Computational Chemistry.
