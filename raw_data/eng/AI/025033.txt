This paper introduces a novel approach for time series classification based on learning discriminative prototypes using Dynamic Time Warping (DTW). Existing prototype-based methods often struggle to effectively capture the temporal variability inherent in time series data. Our method addresses this limitation by dynamically adapting the warping path within the DTW calculation to focus on the most discriminative segments of the time series. We propose a differentiable framework that simultaneously learns both the prototypes and the optimal warping paths during the training process. This is achieved through a modified DTW algorithm integrated with a gradient-based optimization scheme. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art classification accuracy compared to traditional DTW-based methods and other prototype learning techniques. The learned prototypes are more representative and robust to noise and variations in time series data. Our framework provides a powerful and interpretable solution for time series classification, with potential applications in areas such as activity recognition, anomaly detection, and medical diagnosis.

**Keywords:** Time Series Classification, Dynamic Time Warping, Prototype Learning, Machine Learning, Differentiable Programming.
