This paper presents a novel global methodology for detecting trees located outside of closed-canopy forests using medium-resolution satellite imagery. The objective of the study is to address the challenge of identifying scattered and isolated trees, which are often overlooked by traditional forest mapping approaches focused on dense, contiguous canopies. Our approach leverages advanced image processing techniques and machine learning algorithms to analyze satellite data with spatial resolutions between 10 and 30 meters. By integrating spectral, textural, and contextual features, the proposed method effectively distinguishes individual trees and small tree groups from surrounding non-forest land covers across diverse geographical regions. The methodology was validated on multiple test sites representing different climatic and land-use conditions, demonstrating high accuracy in detecting trees outside closed forests and outperforming existing classification systems. Key findings reveal substantial tree coverage in agricultural lands, savannas, and urban areas previously underreported in global vegetation assessments. This work enables improved monitoring of ecosystem services, supports biodiversity conservation efforts, and informs land-use policy by providing more comprehensive and spatially explicit data on tree distributions. The proposed technique is scalable, adaptable to evolving satellite platforms, and has significant implications for global environmental monitoring. Keywords: tree detection, satellite imagery, machine learning, medium resolution, land cover mapping, remote sensing, global vegetation assessment.
