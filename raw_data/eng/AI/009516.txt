This paper introduces a novel convolutional decoder architecture designed for point cloud reconstruction, leveraging Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN) to enhance feature representation and reconstruction accuracy. Point clouds, widely used in 3D modeling and computer vision, often suffer from irregular structures and sparse data, posing challenges for traditional reconstruction methods. The proposed approach integrates AdaIN into a convolutional decoder framework, enabling dynamic adaptation of feature normalization based on input conditions, thereby improving the model's ability to capture fine-grained details and geometric structures. The architecture is trained on a diverse dataset of 3D point clouds, and its performance is evaluated against state-of-the-art methods using metrics such as Chamfer Distance and Earth Mover's Distance. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior reconstruction quality, particularly in preserving intricate details and handling sparse inputs. The study highlights the potential of AdaIN-based architectures for advancing point cloud processing, with applications in autonomous driving, augmented reality, and 3D object recognition. This work contributes to the field by introducing a scalable and adaptive solution for point cloud reconstruction, offering a significant improvement over existing techniques.  

**Keywords**: Point clouds, convolutional decoder, Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN), 3D reconstruction, feature representation, geometric modeling.
