**Abstract**

Contrastive representation learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for unsupervised and self-supervised learning, relying on maximizing mutual information (MI) between paired data representations. However, traditional MI estimators often struggle to capture the complex, high-dimensional dependencies present in modern datasets, impacting the performance and interpretability of learned features. This paper introduces a novel framework for decomposed mutual information estimation tailored for contrastive learning. Our approach decomposes global MI objectives into localized, structured components, enabling finer-grained measurement and alignment of relevant dependencies between representation subspaces. We propose a scalable estimation method based on neural networks, integrating these decomposed MI objectives within existing contrastive learning pipelines. Empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets—including vision and text domains—demonstrate that our method yields more informative and discriminative representations, outperforming standard InfoNCE-based models in downstream classification and retrieval tasks. Further analysis reveals that our decomposition framework enhances training stability and provides interpretability benefits by highlighting contribution patterns of subspace features. These findings suggest that refined MI estimation can significantly advance the state-of-the-art in self-supervised learning. Keywords: contrastive learning, mutual information estimation, representation learning, deep learning, self-supervised learning, information decomposition.
