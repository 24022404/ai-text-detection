This paper proposes a novel approach to image super-resolution, dubbed Double Sparse Multi-Frame Image Super Resolution, which leverages the strengths of both sparse representation and multi-frame fusion to achieve high-quality image upsampling. The objective is to address the long-standing challenge of reconstructing high-resolution images from low-resolution counterparts, particularly in scenarios where multiple frames of the same scene are available. Our method employs a double sparse regularization technique, combining the benefits of spatial and temporal sparsity to effectively capture the underlying structures and patterns in the input images. The approach utilizes a deep neural network architecture, incorporating a multi-frame feature extraction module and a sparse representation-based upsampling module. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art single-image and multi-frame super-resolution techniques, achieving significant improvements in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM). The proposed technique has important implications for various applications, including surveillance, robotics, and multimedia processing, where high-resolution imaging is crucial. Key contributions of this work include the introduction of a double sparse regularization framework and the demonstration of its efficacy in multi-frame image super-resolution, with relevant keywords including image super-resolution, sparse representation, multi-frame fusion, and deep learning.
