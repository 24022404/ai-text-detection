**Abstract**

Effective demand response and urban energy management are critical challenges in modern cities, driven by the need to optimize energy usage and integrate renewable resources. This paper introduces CityLearn, a standardized platform designed to advance research in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) for urban energy management scenarios. CityLearn provides a modular environment that simulates realistic urban energy systems, allowing multiple agents—representing individual buildings or zones—to learn and cooperate for demand response tasks. The platform incorporates diverse building profiles, dynamic energy demands, and grid constraints, offering a robust benchmark for evaluating MARL algorithms. Using CityLearn, we assess the performance of various reinforcement learning strategies, highlighting the benefits and limitations of both independent and cooperative agent approaches in reducing peak loads and overall energy costs. Experimental results demonstrate that MARL-based solutions outperform traditional rule-based and single-agent methods, particularly when agents collaborate and share information. CityLearn aims to facilitate reproducibility, comparability, and acceleration of innovations by providing open access to standardized datasets, simulation scenarios, and benchmarking tools. Our work establishes a unified framework for advancing research at the intersection of smart grids, demand response, and artificial intelligence. Key contributions include the development of a flexible testbed for the research community, the introduction of comprehensive evaluation metrics, and insights into scalable cooperative strategies for urban energy optimization.

**Keywords:** CityLearn, multi-agent reinforcement learning, demand response, urban energy management, smart grids, benchmarking, AI optimization.
