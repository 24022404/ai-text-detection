This research addresses the challenge of accurate traffic forecasting in urban environments, acknowledging the variability in data availability due to sensor coverage, data sparsity, and unpredictable events. The study investigates the potential of combining transfer learning and online learning to improve prediction accuracy under different data conditions.

Methods/Approach: We utilized a transfer learning approach to leverage knowledge from traffic datasets of well-monitored areas to improve predictions in less-instrumented regions. Alongside, online learning techniques were integrated to dynamically adapt models to real-time data fluctuations and evolving traffic patterns. The proposed methodology was implemented using neural networks, specifically focusing on optimizing model generalization across diverse datasets.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the hybrid approach significantly outperforms traditional methods in various data availability scenarios, offering enhanced prediction capability. The models exhibited improved performance in regions with sparse data and showcased robust adaptability to sudden traffic changes. The study also identifies critical pitfalls, such as overfitting risks in transfer learning and latency challenges in real-time online model updates.

Conclusion/Implications: This paper highlights the synergistic benefits of combining transfer and online learning for traffic forecasting, providing a flexible and efficient framework suited for dynamic and data-limited environments. These findings suggest potential applications in smart city planning, where accurate traffic forecasting can lead to better resource allocation and congestion management. The research underscores the importance of accommodating data variability, contributing to more resilient and scalable forecasting systems.

Keywords: Traffic forecasting, transfer learning, online learning, data availability, neural networks, smart cities, real-time adaptation, model generalization.
