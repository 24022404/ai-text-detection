Abstract:

Visual Speech Recognition (VSR) has made significant advances with deep learning techniques, yet the challenge remains to recognize out-of-vocabulary or unseen words and phrases, particularly in low-resource scenarios. This paper addresses the problem of zero-shot learning in VSR—the ability to identify new classes that were absent during training—by harnessing the powerful generative capabilities of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). We propose a novel framework that employs GANs to synthesize realistic visual speech features for new, unseen classes using semantic class embeddings as guidance. The generated data is then integrated into a conventional VSR pipeline, enabling robust recognition of previously unobserved words or speakers. Experimental results on benchmark VSR datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly improves classification accuracy for unseen classes compared to traditional methods, while maintaining performance on known classes. The key contributions include an effective GAN-based data generation mechanism for zero-shot class augmentation and an end-to-end architecture seamlessly combining generative and discriminative models. This work highlights the potential of generative deep learning models to advance VSR systems towards more flexible, scalable, and generalizable recognition. Keywords: Visual Speech Recognition, Zero-shot Learning, Generative Adversarial Networks, Data Augmentation, Deep Learning.
