This paper proposes a novel image processing technique based on Weighted Nonlocal Total Variation (WNTV), addressing the challenge of preserving texture and edge details in images. The objective is to develop an efficient method for image denoising, deblurring, and inpainting by incorporating nonlocal weights into the total variation framework. Our approach utilizes a weighted nonlocal means algorithm to compute the weights, which are then used to regularize the total variation minimization problem. The results show that WNTV outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio and visual quality, particularly in textured regions. The key findings highlight the importance of nonlocal weighting in capturing long-range dependencies and preserving image structures. This research contributes to the field of image processing by providing a robust and efficient technique for image restoration, with potential applications in computer vision, medical imaging, and multimedia processing. Key keywords: image processing, total variation, nonlocal means, weighted regularization, image denoising, deblurring, inpainting.
