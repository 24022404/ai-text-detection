This paper introduces an unsupervised methodology for identifying lane-change maneuvers associated with on-ramp merges within naturalistic driving data. The objective is to automatically extract and analyze these critical events to understand driver behavior and improve traffic flow models. Our approach leverages a novel combination of trajectory clustering and Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) to segment driving sequences and identify distinct lane-change patterns without requiring labeled data. The method first clusters vehicle trajectories based on spatial and temporal features, then uses HMMs to model the sequential evolution of these clusters, effectively detecting the onset and completion of lane-change events. Evaluation on a real-world dataset demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed approach in accurately identifying lane-change maneuvers related to on-ramp merging, achieving a precision of 85% and a recall of 80% compared to manual annotation. This automated identification enables large-scale analysis of merging behavior, offering valuable insights for traffic management and autonomous driving system development. Keywords: Lane Change, On-Ramp Merge, Unsupervised Learning, Trajectory Clustering, Hidden Markov Model, Naturalistic Driving Data.
