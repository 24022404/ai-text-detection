This paper presents a novel and general framework for hypercomplex-valued Extreme Learning Machines (HELMs), addressing the limitations of existing learning algorithms restricted to real or complex domains. The objective is to extend the ELM architecture—renowned for its fast learning speed and good generalization capability—to hypercomplex number systems, such as quaternions and octonions, thereby enhancing its proficiency in processing multidimensional data with inherent algebraic structures. The proposed framework formulates the HELM learning paradigm through a unified mathematical model that enables the direct handling of hypercomplex-valued inputs, weights, and activation functions. Key components include the design of appropriate multiplication rules, activation mappings, and an efficient output weight calculation method tailored to hypercomplex arithmetic. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets—spanning signal processing, image recognition, and bioinformatics—demonstrate that HELMs outperform their real- and complex-valued counterparts in accuracy and convergence speed, particularly when modeling structured multi-dimensional signals. The results further highlight the robustness and scalability of the proposed framework across various hypercomplex domains. This research offers significant theoretical and practical contributions by generalizing ELMs to new algebraic settings, opening new opportunities for machine learning applications in multimedia processing, robotics, and biomedical engineering where multidimensional data representation is essential. Key terms: hypercomplex numbers, extreme learning machine, quaternion neural networks, multidimensional data, fast learning algorithms.
