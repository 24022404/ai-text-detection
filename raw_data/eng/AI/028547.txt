This paper addresses the challenges of Bayesian inference in high-dimensional time-series data, where traditional approaches often struggle with computational complexity and the curse of dimensionality. We propose the Orthogonal Stochastic Linear Mixing Model (OSLMM), a novel probabilistic framework designed to efficiently capture complex dependencies in time-series with large feature spaces. OSLMM leverages orthogonal transformations to decouple and compress the observed signals, while a stochastic linear mixing mechanism enables flexible modeling of both temporal and cross-sectional correlations. We integrate scalable variational inference techniques to make posterior estimation tractable, even in settings with thousands of variables and long temporal horizons. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that OSLMM achieves superior accuracy and robustness in forecasting and anomaly detection tasks when compared to existing models such as Vector AutoRegressive (VAR) and Deep State Space Models. Our approach provides notable gains in computational efficiency without sacrificing interpretability. In conclusion, the Orthogonal Stochastic Linear Mixing Model presents a powerful and scalable solution for Bayesian inference in high-dimensional time-series analysis, with potential applications in finance, healthcare, network traffic, and other domains where large-scale temporal data is prevalent. Keywords: Bayesian inference, high-dimensional time-series, orthogonal transformations, stochastic linear mixing, variational inference, time-series forecasting, anomaly detection.
