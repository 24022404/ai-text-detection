Estimating causal effects from observational data is a critical challenge in data-driven decision-making, particularly in fields like healthcare, economics, and social sciences. Traditional methods often rely on strong assumptions or fail to capture complex, high-dimensional relationships in the data. This paper explores the application of transfer learning with neural networks to improve causal effect estimation. We propose a novel framework that leverages pre-trained neural network models to transfer knowledge from related domains, enhancing the accuracy and robustness of causal inference in target domains with limited data. Our approach integrates domain adaptation techniques to mitigate distributional shifts between source and target datasets, ensuring reliable effect estimation. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms conventional approaches, achieving significant improvements in estimation accuracy and generalizability. The results highlight the potential of transfer learning to address key challenges in causal inference, particularly in scenarios with sparse or heterogeneous data. This work contributes to advancing the application of neural networks in causal analysis, offering a scalable and adaptable solution for real-world problems.  

**Keywords**: Causal inference, transfer learning, neural networks, domain adaptation, observational data, causal effect estimation.
