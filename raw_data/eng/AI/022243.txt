This paper explores the capabilities and limitations of time-lagged autoencoders for slow mode discovery in dynamical systems. The objective is to identify and extract slow modes from high-dimensional dynamical systems, which is crucial for understanding and predicting complex behaviors. We employ time-lagged autoencoders, a type of neural network architecture, to learn compact representations of the slow modes. Our approach involves training the autoencoder on time-lagged data to capture the underlying dynamics. The results show that time-lagged autoencoders can effectively discover slow modes in various dynamical systems, including chaotic and stochastic systems. However, our analysis also reveals limitations, such as sensitivity to hyperparameters and potential mode mixing. The conclusions highlight the potential of time-lagged autoencoders for slow mode discovery, while also emphasizing the need for careful tuning and validation. This research contributes to the development of novel methods for analyzing complex dynamical systems, with implications for fields like climate modeling, fluid dynamics, and machine learning. Key keywords: time-lagged autoencoders, slow mode discovery, dynamical systems, neural networks, mode decomposition.
