This paper explores the application of deep learning techniques in medical image analysis, focusing on enhancing the performance of deep neural networks through fine-tuning with photorealistic, cinematic rendered images. The primary objective is to improve diagnostic accuracy and reduce the reliance on large datasets of real patient scans, which are often limited due to privacy concerns and data scarcity. We propose a novel approach leveraging cinematic rendering to generate synthetic medical images that closely resemble real-world scans, subsequently fine-tuning pre-trained deep learning models on this synthetic data. Experiments were conducted using a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture for lung nodule detection. Results demonstrate that fine-tuning with cinematic rendered images significantly improved the CNN's performance, achieving an average increase of 15% in F1-score compared to models trained solely on real data. The study concludes that cinematic rendering offers a viable and effective method for augmenting medical image datasets, enhancing deep learning model accuracy, and potentially accelerating the development of AI-driven diagnostic tools. Keywords: Deep Learning, Cinematic Rendering, Medical Imaging, Fine-tuning, Convolutional Neural Networks, AI, Lung Nodule Detection.
