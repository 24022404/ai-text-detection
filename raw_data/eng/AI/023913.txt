This paper presents RSINet, a novel Rotation-Scale Invariant Network designed to address the challenges in online visual tracking caused by variations in object orientation and size. The primary goal is to enhance the robustness and accuracy of visual object tracking systems by developing a model that effectively handles rotation and scale changes while maintaining real-time performance.

Methods/Approach: RSINet integrates deep learning architectures with advanced feature extraction techniques that inherently account for rotation and scale invariances. The approach leverages convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with a specialized loss function that penalizes scale and orientation prediction errors. Additionally, the model incorporates an adaptive mechanism to update tracking parameters online, ensuring the system aligns with dynamically changing environments.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that RSINet significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art tracking methods on several challenging benchmark datasets. RSINet achieves higher precision and success rates, effectively maintaining object tracking even with rapid orientation shifts and scale variations. The model also shows improved real-time performance, maintaining a high frame-per-second (FPS) rate without sacrificing tracking accuracy.

Conclusion/Implications: The results indicate that RSINet not only advances the field of online visual tracking but also offers practical implications for applications in surveillance, autonomous navigation, and augmented reality. This research introduces a scalable framework that paves the way for more robust and efficient tracking systems capable of operating under complex visual conditions. Future work will explore extending RSINet to multi-object tracking environments and integrating additional contextual information.

Keywords: RSINet, visual tracking, rotation-scale invariant, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, real-time performance.
