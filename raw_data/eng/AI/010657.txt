This paper investigates the theoretical equivalence between Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) time series models and Variational Bayesian (VB) models. The research aims to bridge these two domains, providing a unified framework that could enhance their application in complex time series forecasting.

Methods/Approach: We utilize a mathematical analysis to derive the conditions under which RNN structures can be interpreted as VB models. Our approach involves examining the architecture of RNNs and establishing a variational framework to interpret these networks in probabilistic terms. Specifically, we explore the application of long short-term memory (LSTM) and gated recurrent units (GRU) within this context.

Results/Findings: The study uncovers a direct correspondence between the parameter tuning in RNNs and the inference process in VB models. Our findings demonstrate that the latent variable dynamics in VB models are analogous to the state transition mechanisms in RNNs. Experimental results show that our approach achieves comparable, and in some cases superior, performance in predictive accuracy compared to conventional models, particularly in non-linear and non-stationary time series data.

Conclusion/Implications: The equivalence derived herein offers significant insights into the potential for cross-applicability of techniques between RNN and VB models, opening avenues for novel optimization strategies and hybrid model development. This research contributes to the fields of machine learning and statistical modeling by providing a deeper understanding of the underlying principles governing these models, potentially enhancing their efficacy in real-world applications.

Keywords: Recurrent Neural Networks, Variational Bayesian Models, Time Series Forecasting, LSTM, GRU, Machine Learning, Model Equivalence.
