This paper reexamines the role of softmax and cross-entropy in neural network classifiers, proposing a novel perspective where these components are viewed as estimators of mutual information between input and output variables. Our objective is to enhance the understanding and performance of deep learning models by leveraging this insight. We employ an information-theoretic approach, utilizing the relationship between softmax, cross-entropy, and mutual information to develop a more effective classification framework. Our method involves modifying the traditional softmax and cross-entropy formulation to directly estimate and maximize mutual information, leading to improved classification accuracy and robustness. Experimental results demonstrate the superiority of our approach over conventional methods, particularly in scenarios with complex datasets and noisy labels. The key findings of this research contribute to a deeper understanding of neural network classifiers and their potential as mutual information estimators, with significant implications for applications in image classification, natural language processing, and other areas of artificial intelligence. By highlighting the connection between information theory and deep learning, this work paves the way for further innovations in AI model design and optimization, with relevant keywords including mutual information, softmax, cross-entropy, neural networks, and deep learning.
