The increasing ubiquity of self-driving vehicles necessitates the development of systems capable of understanding complex traffic environments. This research aims to extract traffic primitives, fundamental elements of traffic behavior, directly from naturalistically logged data to enhance the decision-making capabilities of autonomous vehicles.

Methods/Approach: Our approach utilizes a novel combination of machine learning algorithms and data analysis techniques to process large datasets of naturalistic driving logs. By employing unsupervised learning methods, we identify and isolate key traffic primitives such as vehicle trajectories, pedestrian movements, and environmental factors. This technique enables the direct extraction of essential elements without relying on predefined labels, thus providing a more adaptable framework for diverse driving scenarios.

Results/Findings: The proposed method demonstrates significant improvements in accurately detecting and classifying traffic primitives compared to existing approaches. Our system was tested on an extensive dataset of real-world driving logs, showcasing its ability to generalize across different traffic conditions. The results indicate a notable increase in the algorithm's efficiency and robustness, achieving a classification accuracy that surpasses current benchmarks for self-driving technologies.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a pioneering approach to enhancing self-driving vehicle systems by allowing them to learn and adapt to dynamic traffic environments directly from empirical data. The extraction of traffic primitives paves the way for more intelligent, context-aware decision-making processes in autonomous vehicles. The framework's adaptability to various traffic scenarios makes it a valuable asset for advancing the development and safety of self-driving technology. Potential applications include improved navigation systems and enhanced real-time traffic management in smart cities.

Keywords: self-driving vehicles, traffic primitives, naturalistic driving data, machine learning, autonomous systems, unsupervised learning, data analysis.
