Human action recognition (HAR) is a critical task in computer vision and sensor-based applications, with widespread use in healthcare, sports analytics, and surveillance. This paper addresses the challenge of effectively utilizing inertial sensor data for HAR by proposing a novel approach to encode raw sensor data into image representations. Traditional methods often rely on time-series analysis, which can be computationally intensive and less interpretable. Our approach leverages image-based encoding techniques, transforming inertial sensor data (e.g., accelerometer and gyroscope readings) into 2D images that capture temporal and spatial patterns. We employ a convolutional neural network (CNN) to process these encoded images, enabling robust feature extraction and classification. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves superior accuracy compared to conventional time-series approaches, with significant improvements in computational efficiency. The proposed framework also exhibits strong generalization capabilities across diverse action categories. This research contributes to the field by introducing a novel, interpretable, and efficient method for HAR, with potential applications in real-time systems and edge computing.  

**Keywords**: Human action recognition, inertial sensors, image encoding, convolutional neural networks, time-series data, accelerometer, gyroscope.
