Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for solving sequential decision-making problems, with applications ranging from robotics to game theory. This paper explores the implementation and evaluation of RL algorithms using the R programming language, a widely used tool in statistical computing and data analysis. The objective of this study is to demonstrate the feasibility and effectiveness of leveraging R for RL tasks, particularly in scenarios where integration with statistical models and data visualization is critical. We present a systematic approach to implementing key RL algorithms, including Q-learning and policy gradient methods, within the R ecosystem. The performance of these algorithms is evaluated on benchmark environments, such as grid-world and cart-pole systems, with results indicating competitive accuracy and computational efficiency. Furthermore, we highlight the advantages of using R for RL, such as seamless integration with existing statistical workflows and enhanced interpretability through advanced visualization tools. The findings suggest that R is a viable platform for RL research, particularly for interdisciplinary applications that require robust data analysis capabilities. This work contributes to the growing body of research on accessible and versatile tools for RL, offering a novel perspective on the use of R in machine learning.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, R programming, Q-learning, policy gradient, machine learning, statistical computing.
