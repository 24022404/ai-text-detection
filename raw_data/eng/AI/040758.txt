This paper addresses the challenge of domain shift between synthetic and real-world images in the context of depth estimation. Domain shift often leads to decreased performance in models trained on synthetic data when applied to real-world scenarios. Our research seeks to mitigate this issue by introducing a novel approach termed "Domain Decluttering," which simplifies image content to better align synthetic and real environments.

Methods/Approach: We propose a robust method that employs image simplification techniques to reduce visual complexity in both synthetic and real images. By focusing on key structural features rather than detailed textures, the Domain Decluttering process enhances a model's ability to transfer learned depth estimation capabilities across domains. The approach utilizes convolutional neural networks to apply these simplifications efficiently, ensuring minimal computational overhead.

Results/Findings: Evaluation of our method shows a significant improvement in depth estimation accuracy when applied to real-world images, as compared to traditional cross-domain training approaches. Our technique not only enhances the robustness of existing depth estimation models but also reduces the domain gap more effectively than current data augmentation methods. Performance gains were particularly notable in cluttered environments where domain differences are pronounced.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of Domain Decluttering offers a compelling solution to the persistent problem of synthetic-real domain shift in depth estimation tasks. This work contributes to the field by providing a practical and efficient method to improve model transferability, with potential applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality. Our findings encourage further exploration into image simplification for other domain adaptation challenges.

Keywords: Domain Decluttering, Depth Estimation, Domain Shift, Image Simplification, Cross-Domain Training, Synthetic Data, Real-World Images.
