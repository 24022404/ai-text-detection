This paper introduces a novel dataset-level geometric framework designed to optimize ensemble classifiers in machine learning applications. The objective is to address the challenges of diversity and accuracy trade-offs in ensemble systems by representing entire datasets and classifier decisions within a unified geometric space. Our approach models datasets as high-dimensional geometric objects and ensemble classifiers as geometric transformations, allowing systematic exploration of relationships between data structure and ensemble performance. Experimental evaluation was conducted using multiple real-world datasets and various base classifiers, such as decision trees, support vector machines, and k-nearest neighbors. Results demonstrate that the proposed geometric framework enhances the interpretability and effectiveness of ensemble methods, yielding improvements in classification accuracy and robustness compared to traditional voting and blending strategies. The framework also enables the identification of optimal subsets of classifiers based on geometric diversity metrics, reducing computational cost without sacrificing predictive power. Our findings highlight the importance of data geometry in ensemble learning and open new avenues for adaptive, data-driven ensemble design. Keywords: ensemble classifiers, geometric framework, dataset representation, classification, machine learning, classifier diversity, data analysis.
