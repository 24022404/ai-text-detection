The k-Means algorithm is a widely used clustering technique, but its performance is highly sensitive to initial centroid selection and struggles with complex data distributions. This paper introduces a novel approach to enhance k-Means clustering by learning disentangled internal representations of the data prior to clustering. Our method employs a variational autoencoder (VAE) with a carefully designed information bottleneck to extract independent latent factors that capture distinct aspects of the data. By clustering in this disentangled latent space, we mitigate the impact of correlated features and improve cluster separability. We evaluate our approach on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in clustering accuracy and robustness compared to standard k-Means and other dimensionality reduction techniques. Specifically, our method achieves a higher normalized mutual information (NMI) score and a lower Davies-Bouldin index, indicating better cluster quality. These results highlight the potential of disentangled representations for enhancing unsupervised learning algorithms. Keywords: k-Means, Clustering, Disentanglement, Variational Autoencoder, Representation Learning, Unsupervised Learning.
