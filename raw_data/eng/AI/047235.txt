**Abstract**

This paper investigates the novel paradigm of "Learning to Teach," wherein an artificial intelligence (AI) agent is optimized not only to learn but also to autonomously improve the learning processes of other agents or models. The objective is to bridge the gap between traditional machine learning, which focuses solely on student model improvement, and a more holistic framework where both teacher and student models co-evolve. We introduce an innovative meta-learning approach in which a teacher model dynamically generates tailored training strategies, curriculum sequences, or feedback mechanisms based on the student’s current performance and learning trajectory. Experiments are conducted across several benchmark tasks, including image classification, natural language processing, and reinforcement learning environments. Our results demonstrate that student models guided by learned teaching strategies achieve faster convergence, better generalization, and greater robustness compared to those trained with fixed or randomly devised curricula. The proposed framework enables adaptive and automated teacher-student co-optimization, outperforms traditional knowledge distillation and static curriculum learning methods, and is scalable across different AI domains. This study outlines new directions for AI-augmented education systems and adaptive training pipelines in machine learning. **Keywords:** meta-learning, curriculum learning, knowledge distillation, adaptive teaching, teacher-student co-optimization, artificial intelligence.
