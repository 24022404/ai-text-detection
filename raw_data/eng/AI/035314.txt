Image restoration is a critical task in computer vision, aiming to recover high-quality images from degraded inputs. Traditional methods often rely on fixed or handcrafted activation functions, which may limit their adaptability and performance across diverse restoration scenarios. This paper introduces *xUnit*, a novel spatial activation function designed to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of image restoration models. Unlike conventional approaches, *xUnit* dynamically learns spatial activation patterns tailored to the input image, enabling the model to adaptively focus on relevant regions and features. We propose a lightweight yet powerful architecture that integrates *xUnit* into existing convolutional neural networks (CNNs), ensuring minimal computational overhead. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets for tasks such as denoising, deblurring, and super-resolution demonstrate that *xUnit* significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both restoration quality and computational efficiency. The results highlight the potential of *xUnit* to serve as a versatile and scalable solution for real-world image restoration applications. This work contributes a novel activation mechanism that bridges the gap between adaptability and efficiency, paving the way for more robust and resource-efficient restoration models.  

**Keywords**: Image restoration, spatial activation function, convolutional neural networks, adaptive learning, computational efficiency.
