This paper addresses the challenge of accurately forecasting pedestrian movements in crowded environments while keeping computational cost low. We propose SGCN (Sparse Graph Convolution Network), a novel framework that dynamically models social interactions through a sparse adjacency graph. Historical trajectory sequences are first encoded using temporal layers, then mapped onto a graph where edges connect only the most relevant neighbors based on distance and motion similarity. Sparse graph convolution layers propagate contextual information selectively, reducing noise from irrelevant interactions and improving scalability. Extensive experiments on standard benchmarks (ETH/UCY, Stanford Drone) demonstrate that SGCN achieves up to 15% lower average displacement error and 20% lower final displacement error compared to state-of-the-art methods. Moreover, by enforcing sparsity in the interaction graph, our model reduces inference time by nearly 30% without sacrificing accuracy. These results confirm that targeted message passing in graph structures can yield both efficient and precise trajectory predictions. SGCN’s lightweight design makes it well suited for real-time applications in autonomous navigation, crowd monitoring, and robot guidance. Future work will explore adaptive sparsity thresholds and integration with scene semantics to further enhance prediction reliability.  
Keywords: sparse graph convolution, pedestrian trajectory prediction, social interaction modeling, real-time forecasting.
