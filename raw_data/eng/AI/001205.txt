Human pose estimation, a fundamental task in computer vision, suffers from challenges in handling occlusions and complex articulated movements. This paper introduces a novel Knowledge-Guided Deep Fractal Neural Network (KG-DFNN) to address these limitations. Our approach integrates domain knowledge of human anatomy and pose constraints into a deep fractal architecture, enabling robust feature learning and improved pose estimation accuracy. Specifically, we leverage anatomical knowledge to guide the network's attention mechanisms, focusing on informative body joints and their relationships. The fractal network structure facilitates multi-scale feature aggregation, capturing both local and global contextual information. Experimental results on benchmark datasets, including COCO and MPII, demonstrate that KG-DFNN outperforms state-of-the-art methods, particularly in challenging scenarios with occlusions and pose variations. The proposed method achieves a significant improvement in Average Precision (AP) and Average Recall (AR) metrics. These findings highlight the effectiveness of incorporating domain knowledge into deep learning architectures for human pose estimation, paving the way for more reliable and accurate perception systems. Keywords: Human Pose Estimation, Deep Learning, Fractal Networks, Knowledge Integration, Computer Vision.
