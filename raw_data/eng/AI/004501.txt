Gesture recognition is a critical component in advancing human-computer interaction, with wide-ranging applications in gaming, surveillance, and assistive technologies. This paper presents an effective approach for gesture recognition in RGB videos by leveraging human body keypoints and the Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) algorithm. Our method begins by extracting spatiotemporal body keypoints from RGB video frames using a state-of-the-art pose estimation model, representing complex gestures as sequences of keypoints. DTW is then employed to measure the similarity between gesture sequences, accommodating variations in speed and duration. Experiments were conducted on publicly available gesture datasets, and the proposed approach demonstrated robust performance, achieving high recognition accuracy and outperforming traditional feature-based methods. The results suggest that integrating keypoint extraction with DTW provides significant advantages in capturing temporal dynamics and reducing sensitivity to intra-class variability. This research offers a scalable and efficient solution for gesture recognition tasks and paves the way for more natural human-computer interfaces. Key keywords: gesture recognition, RGB video, human body keypoints, dynamic time warping, pose estimation, computer vision, human-computer interaction.
