Estimating the intrinsic dimension (ID) of data is a fundamental problem with applications in machine learning, data analysis, and dimensionality reduction. This paper introduces a novel estimator for ID based on the fractal properties of data manifolds. Our approach, termed FracDim, leverages the scaling behavior of neighborhood distances to infer the underlying dimensionality. Unlike existing methods that rely on strong assumptions about data distribution or require computationally expensive procedures, FracDim offers a computationally efficient and robust alternative. We evaluate FracDim on a variety of synthetic and real-world datasets, including image, text, and biological data. Results demonstrate that FracDim outperforms state-of-the-art ID estimators in terms of accuracy, particularly in high-dimensional spaces and with noisy data. Furthermore, we show how FracDim can be used to improve the performance of dimensionality reduction techniques and anomaly detection algorithms. The proposed method provides a valuable tool for understanding and processing high-dimensional data, offering a practical solution for various data science applications.

Keywords: Intrinsic Dimension Estimation, Fractal Dimension, Manifold Learning, High-Dimensional Data, Dimensionality Reduction.
