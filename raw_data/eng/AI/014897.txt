Objective: This paper addresses online decision-making in stochastic multi-armed bandit problems where the learner receives side observations according to a feedback graph and incurs nontrivial switching costs when changing actions. Traditional bandit algorithms either ignore structured feedback or assume cost-free arm changes, limiting their applicability in real-world settings such as sensor networks, recommendation systems, and financial trading.

Methods: We introduce Graph-SW-UCB, a novel upper‐confidence‐bound method that jointly exploits graph‐structured feedback and controls the frequency of arm switches. At each round, the algorithm selects an arm by balancing exploration bonuses derived from its neighborhood in the feedback graph against a switching penalty. We derive adaptive confidence intervals that account for both direct rewards and side observations, and we design a thresholding scheme to decide when the expected benefit of switching outweighs its cost.

Results: We prove that Graph-SW-UCB achieves a regret bound of order Õ(√αT + SC), where α is the independence number of the feedback graph, T the time horizon, and SC the cumulative switching cost budget. This bound improves on existing results by explicitly incorporating graph structure and cost constraints. Empirical evaluations on synthetic and benchmark datasets demonstrate up to 30% regret reduction compared to state-of-the-art graph‐based and cost‐aware bandit algorithms, especially in sparse graphs and high‐cost regimes.

Conclusion: By unifying feedback‐graph exploitation with switching‐cost management, our approach offers a principled framework for more efficient online learning in structured environments. The proposed algorithm and analysis open new avenues for developing cost‐sensitive bandit methods in applications where both information structure and action costs are critical.

Keywords: Multi‐Armed Bandits; Feedback Graphs; Switching Costs; Regret Minimization; Online Learning
