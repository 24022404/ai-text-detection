Optical Character Recognition (OCR) systems commonly underperform when confronted with ultra-low-resolution printed text images, frequently resulting in significant information loss and transcription errors. This paper presents an end-to-end OCR approach specifically designed for the unique challenges posed by ultra-low-resolution (ULR) text images, where character details are often severely degraded or indistinct. Our method integrates a multi-stage deep learning pipeline that combines advanced image enhancement with context-aware character recognition. First, a super-resolution module, built upon convolutional neural networks (CNNs), reconstructs high-fidelity text images from ULR inputs. Subsequently, a sequence-to-sequence recognition module employing attention mechanisms is utilized to accurately transcribe text, leveraging both visual and linguistic cues. Experimental evaluations on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that our solution achieves significant improvements in character recognition accuracy compared to leading OCR systems and baseline super-resolution techniques, particularly for images with resolutions as low as 16×16 pixels. The results highlight the model's robustness to extreme degradation, as well as its adaptability across different font types and print conditions. Our end-to-end architecture offers a practical solution for digital archiving, document analysis, and mobile-based text recognition applications where image quality is often compromised. This research contributes a novel, unified framework that addresses both enhancement and recognition challenges in the OCR domain for ultra-low-resolution printed texts. Keywords: OCR, ultra-low-resolution images, deep learning, super-resolution, text recognition, end-to-end system.
