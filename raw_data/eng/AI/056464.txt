Nonnegative Matrix Factorization (NMF) is a widely used technique for parts-based, interpretable dimensionality reduction in various domains such as image processing and text mining. However, a critical challenge remains in determining the optimal latent dimensionality—i.e., the number of basis vectors—that best represents the underlying data without overfitting or information loss. This paper introduces a novel neural network-based framework designed to automatically estimate the optimal latent dimensionality in NMF. Our approach leverages an end-to-end trainable model that analyzes the input matrix and predicts the most appropriate rank by evaluating reconstruction quality and complexity trade-offs. We integrate adaptive loss functions and dimensionality-aware regularization to guide the network toward robust dimensionality estimation across diverse datasets. Experimental results show that our neural network consistently outperforms traditional heuristics and statistical criteria, such as the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), both in synthetic and real-world scenarios. The proposed method offers improved scalability and generalizability, demonstrating reliable performance even with noisy or sparse data. This advancement in latent dimensionality determination streamlines the workflow for NMF applications and enhances the model's interpretability and efficiency. Key keywords: Nonnegative Matrix Factorization, latent dimensionality, neural network, dimensionality reduction, model selection, adaptive regularization.
