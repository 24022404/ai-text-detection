Graph representation learning has become a cornerstone in addressing complex relational data across various domains, yet current graph contrastive learning approaches often focus primarily on node-level or global-level similarities, neglecting nuanced structural relationships. This paper introduces Distance-wise Graph Contrastive Learning (DGCL), a novel framework that leverages distance information between node pairs to enhance graph representation quality. The proposed method systematically incorporates distance-aware positive and negative sample selection into the contrastive learning process, enabling the model to capture fine-grained structural semantics within graphs. To evaluate the effectiveness of DGCL, we conducted extensive experiments on multiple benchmark datasets for node classification and link prediction tasks. Results demonstrate that DGCL consistently outperforms state-of-the-art graph neural network and contrastive learning baselines in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios where local and global structures play essential roles. Our findings highlight the importance of distance-based contrast in learning expressive and discriminative graph embeddings. The proposed framework paves the way for improved performance in downstream graph analytics and offers new insights into leveraging structural features for contrastive learning. Key contributions include a principled distance-wise contrastive objective and an efficient sampling strategy, making DGCL highly scalable and adaptable to diverse graph-based applications. Keywords: graph contrastive learning, distance-aware representation, graph neural networks, node classification, structural similarity.
