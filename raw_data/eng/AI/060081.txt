This paper introduces GLEAN (Generative Latent Bank), a novel framework for large-factor image super-resolution that leverages a pre-trained generative model to reconstruct high-quality, high-resolution images from low-resolution inputs. The primary objective of this work is to address the limitations of existing super-resolution methods, which often struggle to recover fine details and realistic textures when upscaling images by large factors. GLEAN achieves this by utilizing a latent bank extracted from a generative adversarial network (GAN), enabling the synthesis of high-resolution images with enhanced perceptual quality and structural fidelity.  

The proposed approach integrates a multi-scale feature extraction mechanism and a latent code optimization process, allowing the model to effectively utilize the rich prior knowledge embedded in the GAN's latent space. Experimental results demonstrate that GLEAN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both quantitative metrics (e.g., PSNR, SSIM) and qualitative visual assessments. Notably, the framework excels in preserving intricate details and generating photorealistic textures, even for challenging real-world images.  

The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to bridge the gap between generative models and super-resolution tasks, offering a robust solution for applications requiring high-quality image reconstruction, such as medical imaging, satellite imagery, and digital restoration. GLEAN's innovative use of a latent bank and its scalability for large-factor super-resolution make it a significant advancement in the field.  

**Keywords**: Image super-resolution, generative adversarial networks (GANs), latent space, large-factor upscaling, perceptual quality, texture synthesis.
