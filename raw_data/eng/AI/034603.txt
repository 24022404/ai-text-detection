Unsupervised graph representation learning aims to encode node information into low-dimensional embeddings. Existing methods often focus on node-level properties, neglecting valuable cluster-level information that can enhance representation quality. This paper introduces Graph InfoClust, a novel approach that leverages cluster assignments to guide the learning process. Graph InfoClust first performs an initial graph clustering to identify groups of related nodes. It then maximizes the mutual information between node embeddings and their corresponding cluster assignments, encouraging embeddings to capture both node-specific features and cluster-level context. We evaluate Graph InfoClust on several benchmark datasets for node clustering and node classification tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms state-of-the-art unsupervised graph embedding methods, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and normalized mutual information. The results highlight the importance of incorporating cluster-level information for effective graph representation learning. Key words: graph representation learning, unsupervised learning, graph clustering, mutual information, node embeddings.
