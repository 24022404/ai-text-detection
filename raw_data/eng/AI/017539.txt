Graph-structured data plays a pivotal role in various applications, from social network analysis to recommendation systems. However, the vulnerability of graph-based models to adversarial attacks remains a critical concern. This paper investigates the robustness of graph neural networks (GNNs) against adversarial perturbations, focusing on how structured data can be manipulated to degrade model performance. We propose a novel adversarial attack framework that leverages gradient-based optimization to identify and perturb critical edges and nodes in the graph structure. Our approach is designed to minimize detectability while maximizing the impact on model predictions. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that even minor perturbations can significantly reduce the accuracy of state-of-the-art GNNs, highlighting their susceptibility to adversarial attacks. Furthermore, we analyze the transferability of these attacks across different GNN architectures, revealing insights into their generalizability. This study underscores the need for robust defenses in graph-based learning systems and provides a foundation for future research in adversarial robustness for structured data. Key contributions include a scalable attack methodology, empirical validation of GNN vulnerabilities, and a discussion on potential mitigation strategies.  

**Keywords**: Graph Neural Networks, Adversarial Attacks, Robustness, Graph-structured Data, Perturbation, Transferability.
