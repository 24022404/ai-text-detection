This paper introduces *LumiPath*, a novel framework designed to enable real-time physically-based rendering (PBR) on resource-constrained embedded devices. The growing demand for high-quality graphics in applications such as augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and mobile gaming has highlighted the limitations of current rendering techniques on low-power hardware. *LumiPath* addresses this challenge by combining an optimized ray-tracing algorithm with a lightweight neural network for efficient light transport simulation. The proposed approach leverages adaptive sampling and a hybrid rendering pipeline to reduce computational overhead while maintaining visual fidelity. Experimental results demonstrate that *LumiPath* achieves up to 40% faster rendering times compared to state-of-the-art methods, with minimal loss in image quality. Furthermore, the framework is shown to be scalable across a range of embedded platforms, including smartphones and IoT devices. The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to deliver real-time PBR on devices with limited processing power, opening new possibilities for immersive graphics in mobile and edge computing environments. This research has significant implications for industries relying on high-quality visualizations, such as gaming, education, and industrial design.  

**Keywords**: physically-based rendering, real-time rendering, embedded systems, ray tracing, neural networks, adaptive sampling, mobile graphics.
