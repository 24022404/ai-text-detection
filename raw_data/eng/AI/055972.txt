This paper addresses the challenge of causal discovery from incomplete data, a crucial problem in various fields of science and engineering. The objective is to develop a novel approach that can effectively learn causal relationships from partially observed data. To achieve this, we propose a method that combines an encoder with reinforcement learning. The encoder is used to learn a compact representation of the data, while the reinforcement learning module is employed to guide the discovery of causal structures. Our approach is able to handle missing data by leveraging the encoder's ability to impute missing values and the reinforcement learning agent's capacity to explore the space of possible causal graphs. The results show that our method outperforms existing state-of-the-art approaches in terms of causal discovery accuracy and robustness to missing data. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a novel encoder-reinforcement learning framework for causal discovery and the demonstration of its effectiveness in handling incomplete data. This work has important implications for applications such as healthcare, finance, and social sciences, where causal discovery from incomplete data is a common challenge. Key keywords: causal discovery, incomplete data, encoder, reinforcement learning, causal graphs, missing data imputation.
