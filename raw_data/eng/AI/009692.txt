This paper introduces Deep Distributed Random Samplings (DDRS), a novel approach to supervised learning that challenges the traditional dominance of Random Forests. The objective is to develop an alternative method that can efficiently handle complex datasets and improve predictive accuracy. Our approach employs a distributed framework, where multiple random samplings are performed in parallel, and the results are aggregated using a deep neural network. This methodology enables the capture of intricate relationships between features and targets, leading to enhanced model performance. Experimental results demonstrate that DDRS outperforms Random Forests on several benchmark datasets, achieving higher accuracy and reduced overfitting. The key findings suggest that DDRS can be a viable alternative to Random Forests, offering improved scalability and flexibility. This research contributes to the development of innovative machine learning techniques, with potential applications in areas such as data mining, image classification, and natural language processing. Key keywords: Deep Learning, Distributed Computing, Random Samplings, Supervised Learning, Alternative to Random Forests.
