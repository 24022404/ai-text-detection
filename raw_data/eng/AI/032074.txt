This paper introduces a novel imaging system, the Dark Flash Normal Camera, designed to capture high-quality images under low-light conditions while minimizing visual disturbance. Traditional flash photography often produces intrusive light, which can be disruptive in sensitive environments. The proposed system leverages a combination of near-infrared (NIR) and ultraviolet (UV) illumination, paired with advanced computational algorithms, to reconstruct normal RGB images without the need for visible light. The approach involves a custom hardware setup and a deep learning-based image reconstruction pipeline that translates NIR and UV data into photorealistic RGB outputs. Experimental results demonstrate that the Dark Flash Normal Camera achieves comparable image quality to conventional flash photography while significantly reducing light pollution. The system’s performance is validated through quantitative metrics such as peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM), as well as qualitative user studies. This research contributes to the field of computational photography by offering a non-intrusive, energy-efficient imaging solution with potential applications in surveillance, wildlife monitoring, and medical imaging. The proposed method represents a significant advancement in low-light imaging, combining hardware innovation with state-of-the-art machine learning techniques.  

**Keywords**: low-light imaging, computational photography, near-infrared, ultraviolet, deep learning, image reconstruction.
