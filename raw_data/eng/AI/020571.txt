**Abstract:**

Few-shot extrapolation remains a significant challenge in reinforcement learning (RL), particularly in environments where agents must generalize beyond limited experiences to handle unseen tasks. This paper addresses the issue by introducing Structured Maximum Entropy Reinforcement Learning (Structured MaxEnt RL), a novel framework designed to enhance few-shot extrapolation capabilities. The approach integrates structured probabilistic modeling with the maximum entropy principle to guide exploration and policy learning, enabling agents to capture essential task structures with minimal data. Unlike conventional RL methods that often overfit to available solutions, our model explicitly encourages learning a diverse set of structured policies, improving generalization to new, related tasks. Experimental results across benchmark environments demonstrate that Structured MaxEnt RL outperforms standard RL and existing few-shot methods, achieving superior performance with reduced sample complexity. Notably, our framework maintains high performance when extrapolating to previously unseen tasks, validating its effectiveness in low-data regimes. This research highlights the limitations of singular policy approaches and emphasizes the benefits of structured diversity for robust few-shot learning. The proposed method offers promising applications in robotics, adaptive control, and AI systems requiring rapid adaptation. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, few-shot learning, extrapolation, maximum entropy, structured policies, generalization.
