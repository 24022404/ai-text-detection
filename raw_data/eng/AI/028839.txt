This paper addresses the challenge of developing regret bounds for kernel-based reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms. The focus is on understanding the performance of these algorithms in continuous state-action spaces, where traditional tabular methods are often infeasible.

Methods: We propose a novel kernel-based RL framework that leverages reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS) to efficiently model and predict state-action values. Our approach involves integrating kernel methods within the actor-critic architecture and employing a provably efficient exploration mechanism. This combination allows the estimation of value functions in a non-parametric manner, suitable for high-dimensional spaces.

Results: We establish theoretical regret bounds for our kernel-based RL algorithm, demonstrating its efficacy and optimality. Specifically, our analysis provides guarantees on the cumulative regret, comparable to the best-performing algorithms in the literature. Experimental results validate the theoretical findings, with our approach showing superior performance in simulated environments, surpassing conventional methods in terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency.

Conclusion: This research introduces significant advancements in the theory and application of kernel-based reinforcement learning. By deriving regret bounds, we provide a critical measure for evaluating the exploration-exploitation trade-off in continuous RL problems. The implications of this work extend to the development of more robust and scalable RL systems, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and adaptive control. Key innovations include the integration of RKHS into RL and establishing performance benchmarks that set a new standard for kernel-based learning models.

Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Kernel Methods, Regret Bounds, Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space, Actor-Critic, Continuous State-Action Spaces.
