Structured Gaussian Process (GP) models provide a powerful framework for capturing complex dependencies in data, yet their computational cost often limits their application to large datasets. This paper introduces a novel gray-box inference approach that combines the strengths of both black-box variational inference (BBVI) and traditional white-box methods. Our method leverages readily available gradient information from the GP's marginal log-likelihood to guide BBVI, leading to more efficient and accurate posterior inference. Specifically, we employ a control variate technique using the analytical gradients to reduce the variance of the stochastic gradient estimates used in BBVI. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach on several benchmark datasets, showing significant improvements in both inference speed and predictive accuracy compared to standard BBVI and other approximate inference techniques. Furthermore, we explore the application of our method to sparse GP models, achieving competitive performance with state-of-the-art methods while requiring less computational resources. The proposed gray-box approach offers a practical solution for scaling structured GP models to larger datasets, enabling their use in a wider range of applications.

**Keywords:** Gaussian Process, Variational Inference, Gray-box Inference, Control Variates, Bayesian Optimization, Machine Learning.
