This paper aims to investigate the current state-of-the-art deep learning techniques for remote sensing applications where limited training data is available. The objective is to identify the gaps and challenges in existing methods and explore potential solutions to improve the performance of deep learning models in such scenarios. We employ a comprehensive review of recent studies, focusing on techniques such as transfer learning, data augmentation, and few-shot learning, to analyze their effectiveness in remote sensing tasks like image classification, object detection, and segmentation. Our analysis reveals that while these methods show promise, there are still significant gaps in their ability to generalize well to new, unseen data. The results highlight the need for more robust and adaptable deep learning architectures that can efficiently learn from limited labeled data. This research contributes to the development of more effective deep learning approaches for remote sensing applications, emphasizing the importance of addressing the challenges associated with limited training data. Key contributions include a thorough review of existing techniques, identification of gaps, and proposals for future research directions, ultimately enhancing the application of deep learning in remote sensing with limited data, and related fields like earth observation, geographic information systems, and environmental monitoring.
