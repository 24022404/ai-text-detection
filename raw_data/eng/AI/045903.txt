This research introduces an innovative method for seismic inverse modeling using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). The study aims to address the limitations of conventional seismic inversion techniques by offering a more robust, efficient, and accurate approach to interpreting subsurface geological features from seismic data.

Methods/Approach: The proposed method leverages the GAN architecture, where a generator is trained to create realistic subsurface models based on input seismic data, and a discriminator evaluates the authenticity of these models. This adversarial training process iteratively enhances the quality of the generated models. The approach is designed to handle complex geological settings and is tested on synthetic and real seismic datasets.

Results/Findings: The GAN-based seismic inverse modeling method demonstrates significant improvements in model accuracy and computational efficiency compared to traditional inversion methods. The results show enhanced resolution of subsurface features and improved prediction accuracy. Comparative analysis with established techniques indicates that our method provides superior modeling capabilities, especially in complex environments.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a novel application of GANs in the field of geophysics, specifically for seismic inverse modeling. By improving the fidelity and efficiency of subsurface interpretations, this approach has the potential to significantly impact exploration and production processes in the oil and gas industry. The advancements presented could pave the way for further exploration of AI-driven solutions in geophysical research.

Keywords: Seismic Inversion, Generative Adversarial Networks, Subsurface Modeling, Geophysics, AI in Geoscience.
