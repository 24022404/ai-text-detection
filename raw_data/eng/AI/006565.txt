This paper presents a novel approach to task-driven video summarization using meta learning. The objective is to develop a model that can efficiently summarize videos based on specific tasks or user preferences. Our method employs a meta learning framework that enables the model to learn a set of generalizable features and adapt to new tasks with limited training data. The approach utilizes a transformer-based architecture and a task-agnostic training strategy to learn a universal video representation. Experimental results demonstrate that our model outperforms state-of-the-art video summarization methods, achieving significant improvements in terms of summary quality and task-specific relevance. The key findings of this research highlight the effectiveness of meta learning in adapting to diverse tasks and user preferences, making it a valuable tool for real-world applications such as video analysis, surveillance, and content creation. Our contributions include a novel meta learning framework for task-driven video summarization, a task-agnostic training strategy, and a comprehensive evaluation of the model's performance on various tasks and datasets. Key keywords: meta learning, video summarization, task-driven, transformer, adaptive learning.
