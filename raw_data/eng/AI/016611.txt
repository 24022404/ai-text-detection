This paper proposes a novel approach to action recognition, focusing on Temporal Distinct Representation Learning (TDRL). The objective is to improve the accuracy of action recognition models by learning distinctive temporal representations from video data. Our method employs a multi-branch neural network architecture that captures both short-term and long-term temporal dependencies. The TDRL approach utilizes a combination of convolutional and recurrent neural network layers to extract robust features from video sequences. Experimental results demonstrate that our model outperforms state-of-the-art methods on several benchmark datasets, including UCF-101 and HMDB-51. The key findings indicate that the proposed TDRL method can effectively capture temporal patterns and relationships, leading to improved action recognition performance. This research contributes to the field of computer vision and has potential applications in areas such as surveillance, human-computer interaction, and robotics. The novelty of our approach lies in its ability to learn distinct temporal representations, enabling more accurate and efficient action recognition. Key keywords: action recognition, temporal representation learning, convolutional neural networks, recurrent neural networks, computer vision.
