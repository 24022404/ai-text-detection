This paper explores the application of Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) to 3D point cloud objects, addressing the challenge of recognizing unseen object categories without prior training data. Traditional 3D object recognition methods rely heavily on labeled datasets, limiting their scalability and adaptability to new classes. To overcome this, we propose a novel framework that leverages semantic embeddings and geometric features extracted from 3D point clouds. Our approach integrates a graph-based neural network to model spatial relationships within point clouds and aligns them with semantic attributes derived from textual descriptions. We evaluate the framework on benchmark datasets, demonstrating its ability to generalize to unseen categories with competitive accuracy. Experimental results show significant improvements over existing methods, achieving a 12% increase in classification accuracy on average. The proposed method not only advances the state-of-the-art in 3D ZSL but also provides a scalable solution for real-world applications, such as autonomous navigation and augmented reality. This research highlights the potential of combining geometric and semantic representations for robust zero-shot learning in 3D domains.  

**Keywords**: Zero-Shot Learning, 3D Point Clouds, Graph Neural Networks, Semantic Embeddings, Object Recognition.
