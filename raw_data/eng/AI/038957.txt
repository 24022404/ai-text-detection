The paper addresses the challenge of environmental sound classification, a critical aspect of audio analysis with applications in surveillance, acoustic scene analysis, and smart environments. Traditional audio-based models often struggle to capture complex sound patterns effectively. This research aims to improve environmental sound classification by leveraging visual domain models, which have demonstrated superior performance in image recognition tasks.

Methods/Approach: We propose ESResNet, a novel approach that adapts ResNet, a well-established visual recognition model, for environmental sound classification. The method involves converting audio signals into spectrograms, treating them as images, and subsequently using the ResNet architecture to classify these images. This approach allows us to harness the powerful feature extraction capabilities of ResNet, optimized for visual data, to enhance audio classification tasks.

Results/Findings: Experiments conducted on benchmark environmental sound datasets demonstrate that ESResNet achieves significantly higher accuracy compared to traditional audio-based models. The model shows robust performance across diverse sound categories, achieving a notable improvement in classification precision and recall. The results underscore the effectiveness of integrating techniques across the visual and audio domains.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings reveal that employing visual domain models like ResNet for environmental sound classification can lead to substantial improvements in accuracy and robustness. ESResNet sets a new benchmark for sound classification performance, offering valuable insights for the development of cross-domain models. The approach highlights the potential for broader applications, including real-time surveillance systems, urban monitoring, and intelligent acoustic environments, benefiting from enhanced sound recognition capabilities.

Keywords: Environmental Sound Classification, ESResNet, ResNet, Spectrograms, Visual Domain Models, Audio Analysis, Cross-Domain Models.
