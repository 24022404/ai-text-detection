Autoregressive models have become a cornerstone in various applications, including natural language processing, time-series forecasting, and generative tasks. However, their reliance on sequential data makes them vulnerable to adversarial attacks that exploit environmental dependencies. This paper investigates the problem of crafting optimal attacks against autoregressive models by strategically manipulating the environment in which they operate. We propose a novel framework that identifies critical environmental factors influencing model predictions and leverages gradient-based optimization techniques to generate adversarial perturbations. Our approach is evaluated on multiple autoregressive architectures, including transformer-based models, across diverse datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that our method significantly degrades model performance, achieving higher success rates in adversarial scenarios compared to existing attack strategies. Furthermore, we analyze the robustness of autoregressive models under varying environmental conditions, providing insights into their vulnerabilities. The findings highlight the importance of developing defense mechanisms tailored to autoregressive systems and offer practical implications for enhancing their security in real-world applications. This work contributes to the growing body of research on adversarial machine learning by addressing a critical gap in understanding and mitigating attacks on autoregressive models.  

**Keywords**: autoregressive models, adversarial attacks, environmental manipulation, gradient-based optimization, model robustness, transformer models.
