This paper introduces RotLSTM, a novel architecture for enhancing memory management in recurrent neural networks by integrating rotational transformations into long short-term memory (LSTM) units. The primary objective is to address the limitations of standard LSTM models in capturing long-range dependencies and mitigating memory bottlenecks, which often impede performance in complex sequential learning tasks such as natural language processing and time series analysis. RotLSTM incorporates a rotation-based memory mechanism within the cell state, enabling more flexible information encoding and improved retention of historical context. Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets spanning language modeling, sequence classification, and signal prediction, RotLSTM demonstrates superior performance compared to conventional LSTM and gated recurrent unit (GRU) models, particularly in tasks requiring long-term information preservation. Key findings highlight enhanced sequence recall, faster convergence, and improved resilience to vanishing gradient problems. The results suggest that the rotational memory mechanism facilitates more efficient communication across time steps, leading to better generalization and robustness. In conclusion, RotLSTM represents a significant advancement in recurrent neural network design, with potential applications in speech recognition, text generation, and adaptive signal processing. Key keywords: RotLSTM, recurrent neural networks, rotational memory, LSTM, long-range dependencies, sequence modeling.
