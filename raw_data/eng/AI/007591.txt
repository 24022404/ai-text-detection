This paper introduces Subject2Vec, a novel generative-discriminative approach for representing a set of image patches as a compact vector. The objective is to learn a dense vector representation that captures the underlying structure of the image patches, enabling efficient comparison and analysis. Our method combines the strengths of generative and discriminative models, utilizing a variational autoencoder to learn a probabilistic representation of the image patches, and a discriminator to refine the vector representation. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of Subject2Vec in image classification, clustering, and retrieval tasks, outperforming existing methods. The key findings highlight the importance of integrating generative and discriminative components in learning vector representations. This research contributes to the development of more accurate and efficient image analysis techniques, with potential applications in computer vision, medical imaging, and robotics. Key keywords: image representation, generative-discriminative models, vector embedding, computer vision, deep learning.
