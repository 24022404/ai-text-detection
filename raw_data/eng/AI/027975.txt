The increasing demand for data privacy in the era of big data has led to the development of various anonymization techniques. Among these, k-anonymity is widely adopted to protect individual identities in datasets. However, traditional k-anonymity methods often distort the underlying data distribution, limiting their utility for downstream analysis. This paper introduces **Distribution-Preserving k-Anonymity (DPkA)**, a novel approach that ensures privacy while preserving the statistical properties of the original dataset. Our method leverages advanced clustering algorithms and distribution-aware anonymization strategies to achieve k-anonymity without compromising data integrity. Experimental results on real-world datasets demonstrate that DPkA outperforms existing methods in maintaining data utility, as measured by statistical similarity metrics, while achieving comparable privacy guarantees. The proposed approach has significant implications for applications requiring both privacy and accurate data analysis, such as healthcare, finance, and social science research. By bridging the gap between privacy preservation and data utility, DPkA represents a critical advancement in the field of data anonymization.  

**Keywords**: k-anonymity, data privacy, distribution preservation, clustering, data utility, anonymization techniques.
