Stochastic graphlet embedding presents a novel approach to representing graph structures in a low-dimensional space, capturing both local and global properties. This research introduces a probabilistic framework for embedding nodes based on the occurrence of graphlets, small connected subgraphs, within their local neighborhoods. Unlike deterministic methods, our stochastic approach samples graphlets, providing robustness against noise and scalability for large graphs. The embedding process is formulated as a stochastic optimization problem, leveraging techniques from spectral analysis and random projection to efficiently approximate the graphlet distribution. Experiments on several real-world networks demonstrate that stochastic graphlet embedding achieves superior performance compared to existing methods in node classification, link prediction, and graph visualization tasks. The learned embeddings effectively capture complex network patterns, revealing insights into node function and network organization. Key innovations include the development of a scalable stochastic sampling strategy and a novel objective function that balances accuracy and computational efficiency. The findings offer a promising direction for analyzing and understanding complex relational data.
Keywords: graph embedding, graphlets, stochastic optimization, network analysis, machine learning.
