This paper investigates the efficient training of machine learning models using Neural Tangent Kernels (NTK) in high-dimensional data settings. The primary objective is to reduce the computational burden associated with kernel methods when dealing with near input sparsity, a common characteristic of real-world datasets. We introduce a novel algorithm that leverages input sparsity to approximate the NTK matrix multiplication in near-linear time, achieving significant speedups compared to traditional quadratic time complexity. Our approach exploits the structure of sparse data to accelerate kernel computations without sacrificing accuracy. Empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method maintains comparable or superior predictive performance to standard NTK methods, while exhibiting substantial runtime improvements, particularly for large-scale, sparse datasets. This work provides a practical and scalable approach to kernel learning, extending its applicability to domains where data sparsity is prevalent. Keywords: Neural Tangent Kernel, Sparsity, Kernel Methods, Machine Learning, High-Dimensional Data, Computational Efficiency.
