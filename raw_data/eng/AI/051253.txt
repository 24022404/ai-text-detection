This paper explores the application of neuroscience principles to enhance the generality and adaptability of reinforcement learning agents. The objective is to develop more versatile agents that can learn from experience and adapt to new situations, mimicking the flexibility of human learning. Our approach integrates insights from neuroscience into the design of reinforcement learning algorithms, incorporating mechanisms such as synaptic plasticity, dopamine-based reward processing, and hierarchical learning. We evaluate our method through a series of experiments, demonstrating improved performance and transferability across diverse environments and tasks. The results show that our neuroscience-inspired agents can learn more efficiently and effectively than traditional reinforcement learning methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance in several benchmarks. Our research contributes to the development of more general and adaptive reinforcement learning agents, with potential applications in areas such as robotics, game playing, and autonomous systems. Key aspects of this work include reinforcement learning, neuroscience, adaptive agents, and generalizability, highlighting the potential for interdisciplinary approaches to advance the field of artificial intelligence.
