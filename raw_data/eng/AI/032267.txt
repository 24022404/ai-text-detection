A universal cycle (U-cycle) of a finite set of combinatorial objects of rank k is defined as a cyclic sequence in which every possible k-length substring uniquely corresponds to each object in the set exactly once. For instance, an order k binary de Bruijn sequence serves as a U-cycle for all binary strings of length k, encompassing every possible arrangement without repetition within the sequence. The study of U-cycles centers on their existence and methods of explicit construction for a variety of combinatorial structures. In particular, Section 2.3.1 highlights significant results concerning U-cycles for permutations, partitions, and subsets of a set consisting of n distinct symbols, a subject originally summarized in the foundational work by Chung, Diaconis, and Graham.
