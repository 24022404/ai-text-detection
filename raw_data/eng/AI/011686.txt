This paper addresses the critical issue of efficiently pruning deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to reduce computational costs while maintaining performance. The objective is to investigate whether retraining is necessary after pruning, and to develop methods that can efficiently prune CNNs without requiring extensive retraining. We propose a novel pruning approach that combines structural and unstructured pruning techniques, leveraging the strengths of both to minimize the loss of accuracy. Our method utilizes a hybrid algorithm that prunes redundant connections and neurons, followed by a fine-tuning process that refines the remaining weights. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves significant reductions in model size and computational complexity, with minimal loss of accuracy, outperforming existing pruning methods. The key findings highlight the importance of careful pruning and fine-tuning strategies, and demonstrate that retraining is not always necessary to achieve optimal performance. This research contributes to the development of efficient deep learning models, with potential applications in edge AI, real-time object detection, and autonomous systems, and is relevant to keywords such as CNN pruning, deep learning, model compression, and efficient AI.
