Although the construction and algorithm for granddaddy sequences have been known since around 1978, the discovery of a specialized decoder, DKRR, did not occur until 2016, thanks to Kociumaka, Radoszewski, and W. Rytter. DKRR is designed for granddaddy sequences of order k, and given any substring v of length k, it can efficiently locate the unique position of v in x. Remarkably, DKRR operates in O(k²log(q)) time on the word-RAM model and in O(k²) time under the unit-cost RAM model, offering significant computational efficiency.
