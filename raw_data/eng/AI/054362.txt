Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) have revolutionized 3D scene reconstruction by synthesizing high-quality novel views from structured input images. However, applying NeRF to unconstrained photo collections, such as those captured in dynamic, real-world environments, remains a significant challenge due to variations in lighting, occlusions, and camera parameters. This paper introduces a novel framework, *NeRF in the Wild*, designed to address these limitations and enable robust 3D reconstruction from unstructured, in-the-wild photo collections. Our approach integrates adaptive appearance modeling and a dynamic scene representation to handle varying illumination and transient objects, ensuring consistent and accurate reconstructions. We evaluate our method on diverse real-world datasets, demonstrating superior performance in reconstructing complex scenes compared to existing NeRF-based techniques. The results highlight the framework's ability to generalize across challenging conditions, making it a promising tool for applications in augmented reality, cultural heritage preservation, and autonomous navigation. This work advances the state-of-the-art in neural rendering by bridging the gap between controlled laboratory settings and real-world, unconstrained environments.  

**Keywords**: Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), 3D reconstruction, unconstrained photo collections, neural rendering, adaptive appearance modeling, dynamic scenes.
