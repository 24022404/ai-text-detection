This paper delves into the optimization landscapes of overcomplete representation learning, a crucial aspect of deep learning that enables models to capture complex patterns in data. The objective is to understand how the optimization process influences the learning of overcomplete representations, which can lead to improved performance in various tasks. We employ a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical experiments to investigate the optimization landscapes of overcomplete representation learning models. Our approach involves analyzing the loss landscapes and the behavior of optimization algorithms in these landscapes. The results show that overcomplete representation learning models exhibit unique optimization landscapes, characterized by a large number of local minima and saddle points. We find that the choice of optimization algorithm and hyperparameters significantly affects the convergence to desirable solutions. Our analysis also reveals that the learned representations exhibit interesting properties, such as sparsity and disentanglement. The conclusions drawn from this study have important implications for the design and training of overcomplete representation learning models, and highlight the need for careful optimization and regularization techniques to unlock their full potential. Key aspects of this research include overcomplete representation learning, optimization landscapes, deep learning, and neural networks.
