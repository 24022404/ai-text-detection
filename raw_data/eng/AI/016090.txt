This paper introduces CAST, a novel self-supervised learning approach that enhances model representations by incorporating localization information. The objective is to improve the quality of self-supervised representations by learning to localize objects within images. Our method employs a multi-task framework that combines self-supervised learning with object localization, leveraging a simple yet effective architecture to learn robust and informative features. Experimental results demonstrate that CAST significantly outperforms state-of-the-art self-supervised methods on various benchmarks, including image classification and object detection tasks. The key findings indicate that learning to localize objects improves the model's ability to capture meaningful semantic information, leading to more accurate and generalizable representations. Our approach has important implications for computer vision applications, enabling the development of more effective and efficient models for real-world tasks. By highlighting the importance of localization in self-supervised learning, CAST contributes to the advancement of representation learning and paves the way for future research in this area, with relevant keywords including self-supervised learning, object localization, representation learning, and computer vision.
