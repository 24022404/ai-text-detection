The primary objective of this paper is to introduce OpenSpiel, a comprehensive framework designed for the development and evaluation of reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms within diverse gaming environments. OpenSpiel aims to address the growing demand for adaptable and scalable platforms that facilitate research in game-based reinforcement learning. 

Methods/Approach: The framework encompasses a wide variety of games, ranging from classic board games to real-time strategy and hidden information games, providing a versatile tool for testing and benchmarking RL algorithms. OpenSpiel is built to support various RL methodologies, including multi-agent learning, self-play, and population-based approaches, while ensuring cross-compatibility with existing RL libraries. 

Results/Findings: Our experimentation with OpenSpiel demonstrates its effectiveness in accommodating state-of-the-art algorithms and generating comprehensive performance evaluations. The framework’s ability to easily integrate with established libraries allows researchers to focus on innovative algorithmic solutions without redundantly implementing basic game environments. Benchmarking results illustrate OpenSpiel's capabilities in offering standardized environments for consistent algorithm comparison.

Conclusion/Implications: OpenSpiel contributes significantly to the field of reinforcement learning by providing a robust, open-source platform that supports extensive game types and flexible algorithm implementation. This fosters a conducive environment for research innovation and collaboration, potentially leading to advancements in artificial intelligence and human-computer interaction through gaming. The framework’s adaptability to new games and algorithms ensures its relevance in future research endeavors. 

Keywords: OpenSpiel, reinforcement learning, gaming environments, multi-agent learning, self-play, benchmarking, open-source framework.
