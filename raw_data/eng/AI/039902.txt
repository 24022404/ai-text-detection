This paper explores the integration of Capsule Networks (CapsNets) into Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) architectures for enhanced image synthesis. While traditional GANs have demonstrated remarkable success in generating realistic images, they often struggle with capturing spatial hierarchies and fine-grained details in complex datasets. To address this limitation, we propose a novel GAN framework that leverages CapsNets to improve feature representation and spatial relationships within generated images. The proposed architecture replaces conventional convolutional layers in the discriminator with CapsNet-based modules, enabling better modeling of part-whole relationships and geometric transformations.  

We evaluate the performance of our approach on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, comparing it against state-of-the-art GAN variants. Experimental results demonstrate that the CapsNet-enhanced GAN achieves superior image quality, as measured by metrics such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM). Additionally, the model exhibits improved robustness to variations in object orientation and scale, highlighting the benefits of CapsNets in generative tasks.  

This research contributes to the advancement of generative models by introducing a novel architecture that bridges the strengths of GANs and CapsNets. The proposed framework has potential applications in areas such as computer vision, medical imaging, and creative content generation, where high-fidelity image synthesis is critical.  

**Keywords**: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Capsule Networks (CapsNets), Image Synthesis, Spatial Hierarchies, Feature Representation, Deep Learning.
