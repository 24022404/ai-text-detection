This paper addresses the challenge of detecting and localizing hue modifications in digital images, a critical task in ensuring image authenticity and integrity. The objective is to develop a robust method capable of identifying subtle color alterations that may be imperceptible to the human eye. To achieve this, we propose a novel approach based on pair matching, which leverages the inherent relationships between pixel pairs to detect inconsistencies in hue distribution. Our method employs a combination of feature extraction and statistical analysis to compare pixel pairs across different regions of an image, enabling precise localization of modified areas. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach, achieving high accuracy in detecting hue modifications across diverse datasets. Compared to existing techniques, our method shows superior performance in handling complex scenarios, such as images with varying lighting conditions and textures. The findings highlight the potential of pair matching as a reliable tool for image forensics, with applications in digital media verification, copyright protection, and cybersecurity. This research contributes a novel and scalable solution to the growing need for advanced image tampering detection technologies.  

**Keywords**: hue modification, image forensics, pair matching, tampering detection, pixel analysis, digital authenticity.
