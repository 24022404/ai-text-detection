Ensemble classifiers have gained significant attention in machine learning due to their ability to improve predictive performance by combining multiple models. However, the selection and optimization of ensemble methods often lack a systematic understanding of dataset-level geometric properties, which can influence their effectiveness. This paper introduces a novel geometric framework designed to analyze and optimize ensemble classifiers at the dataset level. The proposed approach leverages geometric properties such as data distribution, feature space topology, and decision boundary complexity to guide the selection and combination of base classifiers. We evaluate the framework on multiple benchmark datasets, comparing its performance against traditional ensemble methods. Results demonstrate that the geometric framework consistently enhances classification accuracy, particularly in scenarios with imbalanced or high-dimensional data. Furthermore, the framework provides interpretable insights into the relationship between dataset characteristics and ensemble performance. This research contributes a new perspective to ensemble learning, offering a data-driven methodology for improving classifier robustness and adaptability. The findings have practical implications for applications in areas such as healthcare, finance, and computer vision, where accurate and reliable classification is critical.  

**Keywords**: Ensemble classifiers, geometric framework, dataset-level analysis, machine learning, decision boundaries, feature space topology.
