This paper addresses the problem of generating valid Euclidean distance matrices, a crucial task in various fields such as data analysis, machine learning, and computational geometry. The objective is to develop an efficient method for creating matrices that satisfy the properties of a Euclidean distance matrix, including non-negativity, symmetry, and the triangle inequality. Our approach utilizes a combination of linear algebra and optimization techniques to generate valid matrices. The results show that our method can produce a wide range of valid Euclidean distance matrices with varying sizes and properties. Key findings include the ability to control the distribution of distances and the scalability of the method for large matrices. The conclusion highlights the importance of this research for applications such as data clustering, dimensionality reduction, and graph embedding, where valid Euclidean distance matrices are essential. Our contributions include a novel algorithm for generating valid matrices and a thorough analysis of the properties of the resulting matrices, making this research a valuable contribution to the field of computational geometry and machine learning, with relevant keywords including Euclidean distance matrix, linear algebra, optimization, data analysis, and machine learning.
