Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have demonstrated remarkable success in generating realistic images, but often struggle with mode collapse and generating diverse samples. This paper introduces MCL-GAN, a novel GAN architecture that employs multiple specialized discriminators to enhance the diversity and quality of generated images. Each discriminator in MCL-GAN is trained to focus on different feature aspects of the generated and real images, encouraging the generator to produce samples that satisfy a broader range of criteria. We propose a training strategy that balances the contributions of individual discriminators, preventing any single discriminator from dominating the learning process. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that MCL-GAN significantly improves image generation quality and diversity compared to traditional GANs and other state-of-the-art GAN variants. Specifically, MCL-GAN achieves higher Inception Score and lower Frechet Inception Distance (FID) scores, indicating improved image quality and diversity. The use of multiple specialized discriminators offers a promising direction for enhancing GAN performance and addressing the challenges of mode collapse and limited diversity. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, Image Generation, Multiple Discriminators, Mode Collapse, Deep Learning, Artificial Intelligence.
