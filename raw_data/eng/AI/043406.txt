This paper presents a comprehensive survey on intrinsic motivation in reinforcement learning, aiming to understand the mechanisms that drive agents to explore and learn in complex environments. We employ a multidisciplinary approach, combining insights from psychology, neuroscience, and artificial intelligence to investigate the current state of intrinsic motivation in reinforcement learning. Our survey covers various intrinsic motivation methods, including curiosity-driven exploration, novelty search, and empowerment, and discusses their applications in tasks such as robotic control, game playing, and simulated environments. The results of our survey highlight the benefits of intrinsic motivation in improving learning efficiency, promoting exploration, and enhancing overall performance. We conclude that intrinsic motivation is a crucial component of reinforcement learning, enabling agents to develop autonomous learning behaviors and adapt to changing environments. Our research contributes to the development of more sophisticated and human-like learning systems, with potential applications in areas such as autonomous robotics, game development, and educational technologies. Key keywords: intrinsic motivation, reinforcement learning, curiosity-driven exploration, novelty search, empowerment, autonomous learning.
