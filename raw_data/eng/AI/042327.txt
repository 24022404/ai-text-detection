This paper addresses the challenge of transferring robot learning models from simulated environments to real-world applications, focusing on handling variable-length input sequences. The objective of this research is to develop a robust Sim-to-Real transfer approach that accommodates different input lengths, thereby enhancing robotic adaptability in dynamic environments. We employ a novel model that integrates recurrent neural networks (RNN) with attention mechanisms to process and learn from variable-length sequences effectively. This approach not only captures essential temporal patterns but also enhances the model's generalization capabilities. Our experiments demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms traditional fixed-length input models, showing significant improvements in adaptability and performance across varied robotic tasks, such as navigation and manipulation. The results indicate that our technique successfully bridges the sim-to-real gap, reducing training time and computational resources while maintaining high accuracy and efficiency. The implications of this research extend beyond robotics, suggesting potential applications in other domains requiring flexible and reliable transfer learning techniques. By addressing the variability in input lengths, we contribute a scalable solution to improve real-world robotic performance, highlighting advancements in AI and robotics technology. Keywords: Sim-to-Real Transfer, Robot Learning, Variable Length Inputs, Recurrent Neural Networks, Attention Mechanisms, Generalization, Adaptability, Transfer Learning.
