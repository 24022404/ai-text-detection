This paper presents an innovative approach to unsupervised sketch-based image retrieval, addressing the challenge of retrieving relevant images from a database based on free-hand sketches. Our objective is to develop a system that can effectively bridge the gap between sketch and image domains without requiring paired training data. We propose a novel method that leverages adversarial training and attention mechanisms to learn a shared embedding space for both sketches and images. Our approach enables the model to capture subtle features and semantic relationships between sketches and images, facilitating accurate retrieval. Experimental results demonstrate the superiority of our method over existing state-of-the-art techniques, with significant improvements in retrieval precision and recall. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a new unsupervised framework for sketch-based image retrieval and the exploration of attention-based architectures for cross-domain matching. This work has important implications for applications such as image search, artistic design, and multimedia analysis, and paves the way for future research in unsupervised cross-modal retrieval. Key keywords: unsupervised learning, sketch-based image retrieval, adversarial training, attention mechanisms, cross-modal matching.
