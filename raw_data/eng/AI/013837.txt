This paper addresses the problem of causal discovery in linear acyclic models, where the goal is to identify the underlying causal structure from observational data. Our objective is to develop a method that can handle arbitrary distributions, moving beyond traditional Gaussian assumptions. We propose a novel approach that combines independence testing with a recursive causal search algorithm, enabling the discovery of causal relationships in a wide range of scenarios. Our method is evaluated on various synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its ability to accurately recover the causal graph and outperform existing methods. The key findings of this research include the development of a robust and efficient causal discovery algorithm, capable of handling non-Gaussian distributions and latent variables. Our contributions have significant implications for applications in fields such as economics, biology, and social sciences, where causal understanding is crucial. The proposed method advances the state-of-the-art in causal discovery, providing a powerful tool for uncovering causal relationships in complex systems. Key keywords: causal discovery, linear acyclic models, arbitrary distributions, independence testing, recursive causal search.
