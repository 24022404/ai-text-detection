The objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency and reliability of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) systems by integrating them with Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) regions, a technique traditionally used in control theory. Our approach seeks to address the limitations of traditional DRL algorithms, which often struggle with stability and optimality in continuous state-action spaces. By partitioning these spaces into LQR regions, we propose a novel model that leverages the robustness of LQR to guide the exploration and exploitation strategies of DRL agents. The methods involve the use of a hybrid algorithm that dynamically allocates LQR solutions within specific regions, facilitating more effective decision-making by the agent. Our experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in system performance and convergence speed over existing DRL methods, particularly in scenarios featuring complex dynamics and high-dimensional environments. These findings suggest not only a reduction in computational resources but also an increase in the scalability of DRL applications. The research contributes to the field by introducing a framework that merges the precision of LQR with the adaptive potential of DRL, paving the way for more robust deployments in fields ranging from robotics to autonomous vehicles. Potential applications include improved control systems in smart manufacturing and more effective automated trading strategies. Keywords: Deep Reinforcement Learning, Linear Quadratic Regulator, Algorithm Optimization, Control Theory, Autonomous Systems.
