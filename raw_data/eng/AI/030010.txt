Membrane proteins play critical roles in a range of biological processes, yet their structural characterization remains a significant challenge due to their complex environments and limited experimental data. This paper presents a novel computational framework for predicting the architecture of membrane proteins using Conditional Random Fields (CRFs). Our approach leverages sequence-based features and known structural motifs to model inter-residue dependencies, enabling accurate segmentation and identification of transmembrane regions and functional domains. We designed and trained a CRF model on curated datasets of membrane protein structures, optimizing feature selection to capture both local and global sequence patterns. Experimental results demonstrate that our method surpasses traditional machine learning and heuristic-based techniques in accuracy and robustness, particularly for proteins with atypical topologies. The model is further validated on newly released protein structures, highlighting its generalizability and practical value for large-scale proteome annotation. Our research provides new insights into the use of probabilistic graphical models in structural bioinformatics and suggests that CRFs can substantially improve the computational prediction of membrane protein architectures. Potential applications include facilitating high-throughput drug discovery, guiding mutagenesis experiments, and enhancing our fundamental understanding of membrane protein functions. Keywords: membrane protein structure, Conditional Random Fields, transmembrane region prediction, computational biology, sequence analysis, probabilistic graphical models.
