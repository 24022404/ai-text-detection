This paper explores the relationship between self-attention mechanisms and convolutional layers, two fundamental components in deep learning architectures. Our objective is to investigate how these layers interact and influence each other in various neural network designs. We employ a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical experiments to compare the representational capacities of self-attention and convolutional layers. Our approach involves designing and training multiple models with different layer configurations, allowing us to systematically examine the effects of self-attention on convolutional neural networks. Our results show that incorporating self-attention into convolutional architectures can significantly enhance their ability to capture long-range dependencies and improve performance on image classification tasks. We also find that the choice of self-attention mechanism and its placement within the network have a substantial impact on the overall performance. Our study provides new insights into the interplay between self-attention and convolutional layers, highlighting the potential benefits of combining these techniques to create more powerful and efficient deep learning models. Key contributions include a deeper understanding of the complementary strengths of self-attention and convolutional layers, as well as practical guidance for designing more effective neural network architectures. Relevant keywords: self-attention, convolutional neural networks, deep learning, transformer, computer vision.
