This paper addresses the challenge of video super-resolution, where the goal is to enhance the quality of low-resolution videos. Our objective is to develop a self-supervised adaptation framework that can effectively improve the resolution of videos without requiring paired high-resolution training data. We propose a novel approach that leverages temporal information and internal video statistics to adapt a pre-trained super-resolution model to the target video. Our method utilizes a combination of spatial and temporal losses to learn a video-specific enhancement model. Experimental results demonstrate that our self-supervised adaptation approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving significant improvements in video super-resolution quality. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of adapting super-resolution models to the specific characteristics of each video, leading to enhanced visual quality and potential applications in various fields, including video surveillance, entertainment, and virtual reality. Our contributions include a novel self-supervised adaptation framework, a new temporal loss function, and a comprehensive evaluation of video super-resolution methods, with relevant keywords including video super-resolution, self-supervised learning, deep learning, and computer vision.
