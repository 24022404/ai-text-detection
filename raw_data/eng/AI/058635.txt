This paper introduces GazeGAN, a novel unpaired adversarial image generation framework designed to enhance gaze estimation accuracy. The objective is to generate synthetic images that mimic real-world gaze patterns, thereby improving the robustness of gaze estimation models. Our approach leverages a generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture, incorporating a gaze estimation module to guide the generation process. The GAN model is trained on unpaired datasets of face images and corresponding gaze directions, allowing it to learn the underlying gaze patterns and generate realistic synthetic images. Experimental results demonstrate that GazeGAN outperforms state-of-the-art gaze estimation methods, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and robustness. The proposed framework has important implications for various applications, including human-computer interaction, virtual reality, and automotive safety. Key contributions of this research include the introduction of a novel GAN-based approach for gaze estimation and the demonstration of its effectiveness in generating high-quality synthetic images for improved gaze estimation accuracy. Relevant keywords: Gaze Estimation, Generative Adversarial Networks, Unpaired Image Generation, Computer Vision, Human-Computer Interaction.
