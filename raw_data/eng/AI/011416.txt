Accurate estimation of forest biomass is critical for environmental monitoring and carbon stock assessment. This paper addresses the challenge of developing precise forest biomass prediction maps using radar backscatter data. The primary objective is to enhance prediction accuracy by integrating a sequential regression approach with a Conditional Generative Adversarial Network (cGAN).

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel method that leverages the strength of Conditional Generative Adversarial Networks to refine predictions from sequential regression models. Radar backscatter data serves as the primary input to the regression model, which sequentially adjusts its predictions using cGAN to capture complex relationships between the radar signals and biomass quantities. The cGAN architecture is designed to learn and correct prediction errors, providing more reliable biomass estimates.

Results/Findings: The proposed approach was evaluated on forest areas with varying biomass densities. Results demonstrate that our method significantly improves prediction accuracy compared to traditional regression techniques and state-of-the-art machine learning models. Specifically, the integration of cGAN with sequential regression yielded more precise biomass maps, reducing prediction variance and enhancing spatial resolution.

Conclusion/Implications: This research highlights the potential of combining advanced machine learning techniques, specifically Conditional Generative Adversarial Networks, with radar data for environmental applications. The improved accuracy of forest biomass predictions has significant implications for ecological management and climate change studies. Future work could explore expanding this framework to other forms of remote sensing data for broader environmental monitoring applications.

Keywords: Forest biomass, radar backscatter, Conditional Generative Adversarial Network, sequential regression, environmental monitoring, machine learning.
