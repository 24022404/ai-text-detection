Cross-domain adaptation is a crucial challenge in the field of machine learning, particularly when transferring models from a source domain with abundant labeled data to a target domain with limited or no labels. This paper presents Importance Filtered Cross-Domain Adaptation (IFCDA), a novel framework designed to address domain shift by selectively filtering source domain samples based on their relevance and similarity to the target domain. The proposed approach introduces an importance evaluation mechanism that quantifies the contribution of each source data point to the adaptation process. By integrating this filtering stage into standard domain adaptation workflows, IFCDA effectively reduces the negative impact of irrelevant or misleading source data, leading to improved alignment between domains. Extensive experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets across image classification and sentiment analysis tasks, demonstrating that IFCDA consistently outperforms existing domain adaptation methods in terms of accuracy and robustness. The results show substantial gains in target domain performance, particularly in scenarios characterized by significant domain discrepancies. The findings of this research highlight the value of dynamic data selection in cross-domain learning and pave the way for more reliable and efficient transfer of knowledge across diverse real-world applications. Key contributions include the development of the importance filtering mechanism and its seamless integration with popular adaptation strategies. Keywords: cross-domain adaptation, importance filtering, transfer learning, domain shift, source data selection, machine learning.
