This paper proposes a novel approach to few-shot learning, leveraging sparse spatial transformers to enhance model performance and efficiency. The objective is to develop a framework that can effectively learn from limited data, addressing the long-standing challenge of overfitting in few-shot learning scenarios. Our method employs a sparse attention mechanism, allowing the model to focus on the most relevant features and spatial regions, thereby reducing computational complexity and improving adaptability. Experimental results demonstrate that our sparse spatial transformers outperform existing state-of-the-art methods, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and reducing the need for extensive training data. The key findings highlight the importance of sparse attention in few-shot learning, enabling the model to generalize better to new, unseen tasks. This research contributes to the advancement of few-shot learning and has potential applications in various domains, including computer vision and robotics, where data scarcity is a common issue. Key keywords: few-shot learning, sparse spatial transformers, attention mechanisms, transfer learning, meta-learning.
