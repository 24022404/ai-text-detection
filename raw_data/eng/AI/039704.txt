This paper addresses the challenge of effectively combining multiple visual features in content-based image retrieval (CBIR) systems. Current approaches often rely on manually assigned or empirically tuned feature weights, which are suboptimal and require significant effort. We propose a novel automatic feature weight determination method that leverages indexing techniques and pseudo-relevance feedback (PRF). Initially, an inverted index is constructed for each feature, facilitating efficient retrieval of candidate images. Subsequently, PRF is employed to refine the initial retrieval results and dynamically adjust feature weights based on the relevance of retrieved images. The proposed method iteratively updates feature weights, emphasizing features that contribute to retrieving relevant images while down-weighting those that do not. Experimental results on benchmark image datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing feature weighting strategies, achieving higher retrieval accuracy and improved user satisfaction. Key contributions include the automatic adaptation of feature weights based on image content and the efficient integration of indexing and PRF techniques. Keywords: Content-Based Image Retrieval, Feature Weighting, Indexing, Pseudo-Relevance Feedback, Image Retrieval.
