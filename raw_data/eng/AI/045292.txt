This paper explores the application of Fisher divergence as a variational approximation framework for probabilistic inference and optimization tasks. The objective is to address the limitations of traditional divergence measures, such as Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, in capturing complex data distributions and ensuring computational efficiency. We propose a novel approach that leverages Fisher divergence to approximate posterior distributions in high-dimensional spaces, offering improved robustness and scalability. The methodology involves formulating a variational objective function based on Fisher divergence, coupled with gradient-based optimization techniques to minimize the approximation error. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms conventional variational inference techniques in terms of accuracy and convergence speed, particularly in scenarios involving non-Gaussian and multimodal distributions. Furthermore, the approach exhibits significant computational advantages, making it suitable for large-scale applications in machine learning and data science. The findings highlight the potential of Fisher divergence as a versatile tool for probabilistic modeling, with implications for advancing Bayesian inference, generative modeling, and uncertainty quantification in real-world systems. Keywords: Fisher divergence, variational inference, probabilistic modeling, optimization, Bayesian inference, machine learning.
