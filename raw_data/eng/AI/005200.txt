Volumetric medical image segmentation is a critical task in healthcare, enabling precise diagnosis and treatment planning. However, existing methods often struggle with balancing computational efficiency and segmentation accuracy, particularly for high-resolution 3D data. This paper introduces a novel 3D coarse-to-fine framework designed to address these challenges. The proposed approach leverages a hierarchical architecture that first processes low-resolution volumetric data to identify coarse structures and then refines the segmentation at higher resolutions. By integrating multi-scale feature extraction and adaptive attention mechanisms, the framework effectively captures both global context and fine-grained details. Experiments on benchmark medical imaging datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing techniques in terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency. The framework’s ability to handle large-scale 3D data with reduced memory consumption makes it particularly suitable for real-world clinical applications. This research contributes a scalable and robust solution for volumetric medical image segmentation, with potential implications for improving diagnostic workflows and patient outcomes.  

**Keywords**: 3D medical imaging, volumetric segmentation, coarse-to-fine framework, multi-scale feature extraction, adaptive attention, computational efficiency.
