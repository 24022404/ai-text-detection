The increasing reliance on neural networks in critical applications has exposed them to sophisticated adversarial threats, such as Trojan attacks, which manipulate model behavior through malicious triggers. This paper investigates a novel approach to Trojan attacks by exploiting the deep feature space of neural networks through controlled detoxification. The objective is to demonstrate how attackers can embed hidden triggers in the feature space, enabling malicious behavior while maintaining high accuracy on clean inputs. We propose a method that leverages controlled detoxification techniques to subtly alter the feature representations of poisoned samples, ensuring the Trojan remains undetected during standard validation. Our experiments evaluate the effectiveness of this approach across multiple benchmark datasets and neural architectures, demonstrating its ability to achieve high attack success rates with minimal impact on model performance. The results reveal significant vulnerabilities in current defense mechanisms, highlighting the need for more robust feature-space analysis in neural network security. This work contributes to the understanding of advanced adversarial threats and provides insights into developing countermeasures against feature-space-based attacks.  

**Keywords**: Trojan attack, neural networks, deep feature space, controlled detoxification, adversarial machine learning, model security.
