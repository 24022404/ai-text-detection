This paper presents a novel approach to zero-shot skeleton action recognition, leveraging syntactically guided generative embeddings to improve recognition accuracy. The objective is to enable action recognition systems to identify unseen actions without requiring labeled training data. Our method employs a generative model that incorporates syntactic information to learn embeddings that capture the underlying structure of actions. The approach utilizes a transformer-based architecture to encode skeleton sequences into compact representations, which are then used for zero-shot recognition. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. Key findings include improved recognition accuracy and robustness to variations in action sequences. The proposed approach contributes to the advancement of zero-shot learning and action recognition, with potential applications in human-computer interaction, surveillance, and healthcare. Our work highlights the importance of syntactic guidance in generative embeddings for action recognition, paving the way for future research in this area. Keywords: zero-shot learning, skeleton action recognition, generative embeddings, syntactic guidance, transformer architecture.
