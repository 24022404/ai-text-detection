This paper addresses the challenge of accurately inferring multiple human intentions in dynamic environments through a novel approach called Deep Adaptive Multi-Intention Inverse Reinforcement Learning (DAMIRL). The objective is to enhance decision-making systems by better capturing and predicting complex intention patterns.

Methods/Approach: The proposed method, DAMIRL, combines deep learning with inverse reinforcement learning to adaptively model and infer multiple concurrent intentions from observed behavior. By leveraging a hierarchical structure and a multi-intention framework, the model is capable of decomposing and predicting human actions in real-time. The approach integrates neural networks to dynamically adjust and optimize intention inference, thus maintaining robustness across varying scenarios.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that DAMIRL outperforms existing state-of-the-art inverse reinforcement learning techniques in terms of accuracy and adaptability across multiple intention tasks. The system exhibits a high success rate in environments with complex and ambiguous human activities, showing significant improvements in prediction capability and computational efficiency.

Conclusion/Implications: The research provides valuable contributions to the field of reinforcement learning by introducing a deep adaptive framework that effectively handles multi-intention scenarios. This work opens up new possibilities for applications in areas such as autonomous driving, robotics, and human-computer interaction, where understanding and predicting human intents are crucial. The innovative integration of deep learning with inverse reinforcement learning in DAMIRL sets a benchmark for future studies aiming to address similar challenges.

Key Keywords: Deep Learning, Inverse Reinforcement Learning, Multi-Intention Inference, Human Intention Prediction, Adaptive Modeling.
