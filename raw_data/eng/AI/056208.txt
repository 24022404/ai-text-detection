This paper introduces Non-Transferable Learning (NTL), a novel approach for model verification and authorization. Unlike conventional machine learning paradigms that prioritize model portability and generalization, NTL focuses on creating models with inherent resistance to knowledge transfer. The NTL framework employs a combination of adversarial training techniques and specialized regularization methods to generate models that perform optimally on a specific, authorized dataset while exhibiting significantly degraded performance on any other data distribution. We present a theoretical analysis of NTL's properties and demonstrate its effectiveness through experiments on image classification and natural language processing tasks. The results show that NTL models achieve near-perfect accuracy on authorized datasets, while exhibiting a substantial drop in accuracy when applied to unauthorized datasets, even those with similar characteristics. This discrepancy allows for robust model verification and authorization, ensuring that only models trained on approved data are deployed. NTL offers a promising solution to mitigate the risks associated with model theft, unauthorized use, and supply chain vulnerabilities in machine learning systems. Keywords: Non-Transferable Learning, Model Verification, Authorization, Adversarial Training, Machine Learning Security, Data Poisoning.
