This paper introduces a novel approach to policy learning and adaptation using importance weighting. The objective is to improve the efficiency and stability of reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly in scenarios with non-stationary environments or sparse rewards. Our method leverages importance sampling to re-weight experiences from past policies, enabling effective learning from off-policy data. This approach addresses the challenges of distribution shift and sample inefficiency that commonly plague reinforcement learning. We propose an adaptive importance weighting scheme that dynamically adjusts the weights based on the similarity between the current and past policies, mitigating potential instability due to high variance. Experimental results demonstrate that our algorithm outperforms state-of-the-art policy learning methods in various benchmark environments, including continuous control tasks and simulated robotic manipulation. The findings highlight the significant potential of importance weighted policy learning for enabling robust and efficient adaptation in complex, dynamic systems. This research contributes a practical solution for learning effective policies from limited and potentially biased data.

**Keywords:** Reinforcement Learning, Policy Learning, Importance Sampling, Policy Adaptation, Off-Policy Learning, Adaptive Control.
