This paper presents XCI-Sketch, a novel method for extracting and utilizing color information from images to generate colored outlines and sketches. The main objective is to enhance the expressiveness and realism of automated image sketching by integrating significant color cues into the traditionally monochrome sketch generation pipeline. The proposed approach combines advanced edge detection techniques with color segmentation algorithms to accurately capture both structural and chromatic features from input images. We introduce a dual-phase processing pipeline: first, edge maps are computed to delineate salient contours; second, localized color regions are segmented and mapped to corresponding outline segments, resulting in sketches with color-encoded boundaries. The XCI-Sketch system is evaluated on diverse benchmark datasets, demonstrating improved fidelity in capturing original image content and superior visual coherence compared to existing grayscale and traditional colorization methods. Our results indicate that the approach effectively bridges the gap between simplistic outlines and fully rendered color sketches, offering new possibilities for applications in digital illustration, content stylization, and data augmentation for machine learning. The research highlights the advantages of preserving color context within outline representations and establishes a foundation for further exploration in color-aware sketch generation systems.

Keywords: colored outlines, image processing, sketch generation, color extraction, edge detection, color segmentation, digital illustration, computer vision.
