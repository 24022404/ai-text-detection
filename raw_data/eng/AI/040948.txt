This paper presents a novel method for time series forecasting that integrates fuzzy logic with interval index numbers and membership values, optimizing predictive accuracy using support vector machines (SVM). The aim is to address the limitations of traditional forecasting techniques in handling uncertainty and imprecision inherent in real-world data.

Methods/Approach: We propose a hybrid forecasting model that first constructs interval index numbers from historical data to capture the underlying variability. The model utilizes fuzzy sets to assign membership values, enhancing its ability to interpret the uncertainty. This enriched dataset is then used to train a support vector machine, leveraging its robust classification capabilities to improve forecast precision. The system's effectiveness is benchmarked against traditional fuzzy time series approaches.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in forecasting accuracy, with the proposed method outperforming classical fuzzy time series models. Our approach shows superior adaptability to fluctuating datasets and better scalability when applied to larger datasets. Comparative analysis reveals that integrating interval index numbers and membership values with SVM enhances the model's predictive power.

Conclusion/Implications: The study introduces a powerful forecasting tool that bridges fuzzy logic and machine learning, providing enhanced accuracy and versatility in time series forecasting. This approach is applicable to a wide range of domains, including finance, weather prediction, and demand forecasting, where managing uncertainty is crucial. Our research underscores the potential of hybrid models in advancing forecasting methodologies and provides a framework for future exploration and development in predictive analytics.

Keywords: Fuzzy Time Series, Interval Index Number, Membership Value, Support Vector Machine, Forecasting Model, Predictive Analytics.
