This paper proposes GEE, a novel Gradient-based Explainable Variational Autoencoder designed for network anomaly detection. The objective is to develop an effective and interpretable model that identifies anomalies in network traffic while providing insights into the decision-making process. GEE employs a gradient-based approach to optimize the variational autoencoder, enabling the detection of subtle anomalies and improving the model's explainability. The method utilizes a combination of reconstruction error and gradient information to identify anomalous patterns in network data. Experimental results demonstrate that GEE outperforms state-of-the-art anomaly detection methods, achieving higher accuracy and recall rates. The key findings highlight the importance of explainability in anomaly detection, allowing for better understanding and mitigation of potential security threats. This research contributes to the development of more transparent and reliable network anomaly detection systems, with potential applications in cybersecurity and network management. Key keywords: explainable AI, variational autoencoder, network anomaly detection, gradient-based optimization, cybersecurity.
