This paper introduces a novel approach to creating runtime-throttleable neural networks, enabling dynamic adjustment of computational cost based on available resources or performance requirements. We propose a training methodology that embeds fine-grained control mechanisms within the network architecture, allowing for adaptive layer skipping and reduced precision computation at runtime. Our approach leverages a reinforcement learning agent to determine the optimal throttling policy, balancing accuracy and computational efficiency. Experimental results on image classification tasks demonstrate that our method achieves significant reductions in inference time and energy consumption with minimal accuracy loss compared to standard, fixed-size networks. Furthermore, we show that the trained networks can adapt to varying resource constraints without retraining, making them suitable for deployment on resource-constrained devices or in dynamic environments. This work contributes to the development of more efficient and adaptable deep learning systems, with potential applications in mobile computing, embedded systems, and edge AI.

Keywords: Neural Networks, Runtime Throttling, Resource-Aware Computing, Reinforcement Learning, Adaptive Inference, Model Compression.
