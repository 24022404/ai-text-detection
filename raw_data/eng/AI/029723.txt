This paper investigates the implicit bias of algorithms in machine learning, focusing on quantifying and bounding bias through the lens of margin-based analysis. The objective is to provide a theoretical framework for understanding how different optimization algorithms inherently favor certain solutions, particularly in high-dimensional settings. We propose a novel approach that leverages margin theory to establish bounds on the implicit bias of any algorithm, offering a unified perspective applicable to a wide range of optimization techniques. Our methodology involves analyzing the geometric properties of the solution space and deriving conditions under which algorithms converge to solutions with specific margin characteristics. The results demonstrate that the implicit bias can be systematically controlled by adjusting the margin constraints, leading to improved generalization and fairness in model outcomes. Key findings include a comparative analysis of popular algorithms, such as gradient descent and stochastic gradient descent, highlighting their bias tendencies under varying margin conditions. The implications of this research are significant for designing algorithms that promote fairness, reduce unintended biases, and enhance interpretability in machine learning models. This work contributes to the broader understanding of algorithmic behavior and provides actionable insights for developing more equitable AI systems.  

**Keywords**: implicit bias, margin theory, optimization algorithms, fairness in AI, machine learning, generalization, high-dimensional optimization.
