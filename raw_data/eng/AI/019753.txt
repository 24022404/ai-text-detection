This paper explores the integration of quasi-Newton optimization methods within deep learning applications, addressing the challenges of efficient training and convergence in large-scale neural networks. Unlike conventional first-order methods such as stochastic gradient descent (SGD), quasi-Newton approaches utilize second-order information to accelerate convergence, but their applicability to deep learning has traditionally been limited due to computational and memory constraints. To bridge this gap, we propose adaptations of popular quasi-Newton algorithms—specifically, L-BFGS and SR1—to fit the stochastic and high-dimensional nature of deep learning contexts. Our methodology incorporates memory-efficient updates, batch-wise curvature estimation, and adaptive regularization strategies. Experimental results on benchmark datasets including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet demonstrate faster convergence rates and improved generalization performance compared to standard first-order optimizers. Additionally, we analyze the trade-offs in terms of computational overhead and scalability. The findings highlight the feasibility of employing quasi-Newton methods to enhance deep learning training, especially in settings where rapid convergence and robust optimization are critical. This work provides a foundation for further research into second-order techniques in neural network optimization and offers practical insights for deploying quasi-Newton optimizers in real-world deep learning systems. Keywords: deep learning, quasi-Newton methods, L-BFGS, SR1, neural network optimization, second-order optimization, convergence acceleration.
