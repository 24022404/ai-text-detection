Video Scene Graph Generation (VidSGG) aims to model the complex spatiotemporal relationships among objects in video sequences, a critical task for applications such as video understanding and autonomous systems. However, existing methods often struggle with dynamic scene variations and inefficient context aggregation, limiting their performance. This paper introduces a novel framework, **Target Adaptive Context Aggregation (TACA)**, designed to address these challenges. TACA leverages a multi-level attention mechanism to adaptively aggregate contextual information across spatial, temporal, and object-level dimensions, ensuring robust representation learning for diverse video scenes. The proposed approach incorporates a dynamic weighting scheme that prioritizes relevant contexts based on target object interactions, enhancing the model's ability to capture fine-grained relationships. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that TACA outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving significant improvements in relationship prediction accuracy and scene graph quality. The framework's adaptability and scalability make it suitable for real-world applications, including video surveillance and human-computer interaction. This work contributes a novel perspective on context-aware VidSGG, paving the way for more effective video understanding systems.  

**Keywords**: Video Scene Graph Generation, Context Aggregation, Spatiotemporal Modeling, Attention Mechanisms, Video Understanding.
