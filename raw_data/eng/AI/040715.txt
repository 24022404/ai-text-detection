This paper addresses the challenge of image segmentation by proposing a novel approach based on size-dependent single linkage clustering of a watershed basin graph. The objective is to improve segmentation accuracy by effectively grouping regions while preserving fine-grained details in complex images. The proposed method leverages a watershed transform to partition the image into distinct basins, which are then represented as nodes in a graph. A size-dependent single linkage clustering algorithm is applied to merge these nodes, ensuring that regions are grouped based on both spatial proximity and size constraints. This approach overcomes limitations of traditional methods, such as over-segmentation and sensitivity to noise. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior performance compared to existing techniques, particularly in handling images with varying textures and object sizes. The findings highlight the method's robustness and adaptability, making it suitable for applications in medical imaging, remote sensing, and computer vision. This research contributes a scalable and efficient solution to image segmentation, offering significant potential for advancing automated image analysis systems.  

**Keywords**: image segmentation, watershed transform, single linkage clustering, graph-based methods, size-dependent clustering, computer vision.
