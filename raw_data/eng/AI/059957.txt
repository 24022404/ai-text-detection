This paper presents a supervised learning approach for crop-weed classification, leveraging color and texture features to improve agricultural productivity. The objective is to develop an accurate and efficient system for distinguishing between crops and weeds, enabling targeted weed control and reducing herbicide usage. A convolutional neural network (CNN) model is employed, utilizing a dataset of images with diverse crop and weed species. The CNN is trained on a combination of color and texture features, extracted using techniques such as histogram analysis and Gabor filtering. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach, achieving an accuracy of 92% in classifying crops and weeds. The system's performance is compared to traditional machine learning methods, showcasing the superiority of the CNN-based approach. The research contributes to the development of precision agriculture, highlighting the potential of supervised learning and computer vision for automated crop-weed classification. Key applications include autonomous weeding systems, crop monitoring, and sustainable agricultural practices. Relevant keywords: crop-weed classification, supervised learning, convolutional neural networks, color and texture features, precision agriculture, computer vision.
