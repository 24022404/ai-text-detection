Human pose estimation in ancient vase paintings presents unique challenges due to the stylistic and artistic variations inherent in these artifacts. This paper addresses the problem of accurately estimating human poses in such artworks by leveraging perceptually-grounded style transfer learning. We propose a novel approach that integrates style transfer techniques with deep learning-based pose estimation models to adaptively align the artistic style of vase paintings with modern visual representations. Our method employs a two-stage framework: first, a style transfer network transforms the input images to a more interpretable visual domain, and second, a state-of-the-art pose estimation model processes the transformed images to predict human poses. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in pose estimation accuracy compared to baseline methods, achieving a 15% increase in mean average precision (mAP) on a curated dataset of ancient vase paintings. The proposed approach not only enhances the interpretability of artistic depictions but also opens new avenues for analyzing historical artifacts using modern computer vision techniques. This research contributes to the fields of cultural heritage preservation and computer vision by bridging the gap between artistic styles and machine learning models, enabling more accurate and meaningful analysis of ancient artworks.  

**Keywords**: Human pose estimation, style transfer, ancient vase paintings, deep learning, cultural heritage, computer vision.
