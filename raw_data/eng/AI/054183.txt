Understanding visual emotions is a critical challenge in computer vision, with applications ranging from affective computing to human-computer interaction. However, existing datasets often suffer from inherent biases, leading to suboptimal model performance and limited generalizability. This paper addresses the problem of dataset bias in visual emotion recognition by proposing a novel framework that identifies, quantifies, and mitigates such biases. We introduce a multi-modal approach combining deep learning techniques with domain adaptation strategies to disentangle emotion-related features from dataset-specific artifacts. Our method leverages a diverse set of visual datasets and employs adversarial training to reduce bias while preserving emotion-discriminative information. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in cross-dataset generalization, outperforming state-of-the-art methods by up to 15% in accuracy. Furthermore, we provide insights into the sources of bias and their impact on model performance, offering actionable recommendations for future dataset creation and model training. This research contributes to the development of more robust and fair emotion recognition systems, with potential applications in mental health monitoring, personalized content recommendation, and social robotics.  

**Keywords**: visual emotion recognition, dataset bias, domain adaptation, adversarial learning, affective computing, computer vision.
