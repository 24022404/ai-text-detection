Dropout is a widely adopted technique in training deep neural networks, primarily known for its effectiveness in preventing overfitting by randomly deactivating neurons during training. This paper investigates dropout from a novel perspective, analyzing its role as an optimization trick beyond conventional regularization. Our objective is to systematically explore how dropout influences the training dynamics, loss landscape, and convergence behavior of neural networks. We employ both theoretical analysis and empirical studies across various benchmark datasets and network architectures to dissect the interaction between dropout and standard optimization algorithms such as stochastic gradient descent (SGD) and Adam. Key findings reveal that dropout not only enhances generalization but also modifies the optimization trajectory, helping models escape undesirable local minima and saddle points. Furthermore, compared to traditional regularization techniques, dropout accelerates convergence and leads to models with flatter minima, supporting improved robustness and transferability. Our study provides new insights into the underlying mechanisms of dropout, highlighting its dual function as an optimization enhancer and a regularizer. These results suggest practical considerations for incorporating dropout into modern deep learning pipelines, particularly in scenarios with complex loss surfaces or limited data. Keywords: dropout, neural networks, optimization, regularization, deep learning, convergence, loss landscape.
