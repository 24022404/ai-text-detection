This paper investigates the challenges and limitations of representing complex networks, especially those abundant in triangles, through low-rank structures. The study aims to understand whether triangle-rich networks can be accurately captured using low-dimensional representations, which are commonly employed for simplifying network analysis.

Methods/Approach: We employ a theoretical framework to analyze the properties of complex networks characterized by a significant density of triangles. Our approach involves mathematical exploration of network representation feasibility, leveraging concepts from graph theory and dimensionality reduction techniques. The study systematically evaluates the conditions under which low-rank approximations fail to encapsulate the intricate structural features inherent to these networks.

Results/Findings: Our findings demonstrate the inherent challenges in effectively using low-rank representations for networks rich in triangular formations. We establish that the presence of numerous interconnected triads in such networks fundamentally obstructs the creation of low-rank models that accurately represent their complexity. Empirical comparisons with existing methods underscore the observed limitations, highlighting the discrepancies between the actual network structure and its low-rank approximation.

Conclusion/Implications: The research contributes to a nuanced understanding of complex network representation, emphasizing the infeasibility of low-rank approaches for triangle-rich networks. These insights are pertinent for fields such as social network analysis, biological network modeling, and communication systems, where understanding the intricacies of network structure is crucial. The findings urge the development of new representation techniques that can adequately address the limitations of traditional low-rank methodologies, potentially guiding future research into more sophisticated modeling strategies.

Keywords: complex networks, low-rank representation, triangle-rich networks, graph theory, dimensionality reduction, network modeling.
