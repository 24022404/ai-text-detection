This paper introduces LEGAN, a novel approach for disentangled manipulation of directional lighting and facial expressions in images. The objective is to develop a system that can separately control lighting and facial expressions, enabling realistic and flexible image editing. To achieve this, we leverage human perceptual judgments to train a deep learning model that can decouple these two factors. Our method utilizes a generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture, incorporating a disentanglement loss function that ensures the separation of lighting and expression features. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of LEGAN in manipulating directional lighting and facial expressions independently, outperforming existing methods in terms of realism and control. The key findings include the ability to generate high-quality images with desired lighting and expression conditions, as well as the model's capacity to generalize to unseen data. This research contributes to the field of image editing and synthesis, with potential applications in fields such as computer vision, graphics, and human-computer interaction. Key keywords: facial expression manipulation, directional lighting, disentangled representation, generative adversarial networks, human perceptual judgments.
