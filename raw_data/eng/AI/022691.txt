This paper explores the role of supernet in neural architecture search (NAS), a crucial aspect of deep learning that automates the design of neural networks. The objective is to investigate how supernet, a neural network that contains a large number of sub-networks, can efficiently search for optimal architectures. Our approach involves training a supernet and then using its weights to initialize and evaluate a large number of sub-networks, thereby reducing the computational cost associated with NAS. The results show that supernet-based NAS achieves state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets, outperforming traditional NAS methods. Key findings include the ability of supernet to identify high-performing architectures with reduced computational resources and its potential to be applied to various deep learning tasks. The conclusion highlights the significance of supernet in NAS, enabling faster and more efficient architecture search, and its potential applications in areas such as computer vision and natural language processing. Overall, this research contributes to the development of more efficient NAS methods, with key keywords including supernet, neural architecture search, deep learning, and efficient architecture design.
