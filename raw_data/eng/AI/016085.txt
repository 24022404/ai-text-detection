This paper introduces a novel approach to language-guided navigation, addressing the challenge of enabling robots to navigate complex environments based on natural language instructions. Our method leverages cross-modal grounding to establish robust correspondences between linguistic descriptions and visual scene representations, facilitating accurate interpretation of navigational goals. We propose an alternate adversarial learning framework that enhances the robot's ability to generalize to unseen environments and handle ambiguous language instructions. Specifically, the framework alternates between training a generator that produces navigation trajectories and a discriminator that distinguishes between real and generated trajectories, guided by language. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of navigation accuracy and success rate, particularly in challenging and previously unexplored environments. The key contributions of this work include a cross-modal grounding module for improved language understanding and an adversarial learning strategy that promotes robust navigation policies. This research has significant implications for applications such as autonomous robots in warehouses, assistive navigation for the visually impaired, and service robots in dynamic environments.

**Keywords:** Language-guided navigation, Cross-modal grounding, Adversarial learning, Robotics, Natural Language Processing, Computer Vision.
