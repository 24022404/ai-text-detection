This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently performing inference in probabilistic programming, where flexibility and generalization across diverse models are critical. We propose a novel meta-learning framework that automatically learns inference algorithms tailored to families of probabilistic programs. Our approach utilizes a neural meta-learner that, given a definition of a probabilistic program and its observations, generates an inference network capable of rapidly approximating posterior distributions. By leveraging a dataset of varied generative models, the meta-learner acquires transferable inference strategies that generalize to new, unseen probabilistic programs with minimal retraining. Experimental results demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms traditional inference techniques in terms of accuracy and runtime efficiency, particularly on complex and high-dimensional models. Furthermore, ablation studies and comparative benchmarks highlight improvements in posterior estimation and scalability. The proposed framework offers significant advances in the automation and adaptability of inference for probabilistic programming, with potential applications in Bayesian modeling, reinforcement learning, and data-driven decision making. Keywords: meta-learning, probabilistic programming, neural inference, Bayesian inference, automated reasoning, machine learning.
