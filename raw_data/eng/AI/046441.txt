Scene flow estimation, which involves predicting the 3D motion of every point in a scene, is a critical component in various computer vision and robotics applications, such as autonomous driving and augmented reality. Traditional approaches often rely on 2D projections or volumetric representations, limiting their scalability and accuracy when dealing with raw 3D point cloud data. In this paper, we introduce FlowNet3D, a deep neural network specifically designed to learn and predict scene flow directly from unordered 3D point clouds. Our method leverages novel point-by-point feature learning modules and embedding mechanisms to efficiently capture geometric relationships and motion patterns between consecutive 3D scans. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that FlowNet3D achieves state-of-the-art performance, significantly outperforming conventional and recent deep learning-based methods in terms of scene flow accuracy and computational efficiency. Furthermore, the end-to-end architecture enables robust performance under challenging scenarios, including sparse and noisy point clouds. The proposed approach opens new avenues for precise 3D motion understanding in dynamic environments. Key contributions include the introduction of point-based scene flow estimation, architectural innovations for direct processing of point clouds, and comprehensive evaluation demonstrating superior generalization capabilities. Keywords: scene flow, 3D point cloud, deep learning, FlowNet3D, motion estimation, feature learning, neural networks.
