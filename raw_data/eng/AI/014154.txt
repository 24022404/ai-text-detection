This paper presents a novel approach to monocular 3D object detection, eliminating the need for extrinsic parameters. The objective is to accurately detect and localize objects in 3D space using a single camera, without requiring prior knowledge of the camera's pose or calibration. Our method leverages a deep learning-based architecture, combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and geometric constraints to estimate object dimensions, orientation, and location. The approach is validated through extensive experiments on publicly available datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. Key findings include improved accuracy in object detection and 3D localization, as well as enhanced robustness to varying lighting conditions and object occlusions. The proposed technique has significant implications for applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and surveillance, where fast and accurate 3D object detection is crucial. By removing the dependency on extrinsic parameters, our method offers a more flexible and practical solution for monocular 3D object detection, with potential applications in computer vision, machine learning, and 3D reconstruction.
