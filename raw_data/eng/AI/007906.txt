This paper introduces the Encoded Prior Sliced Wasserstein AutoEncoder (EPSWAE), a novel framework designed to enhance the learning of latent manifold representations in high-dimensional data. The objective of this research is to address the limitations of traditional autoencoders in capturing complex data distributions and ensuring robust latent space representations. To achieve this, EPSWAE integrates a sliced Wasserstein distance-based loss function with an encoded prior mechanism, enabling more efficient and stable optimization during training. The proposed method leverages the geometric properties of the sliced Wasserstein distance to improve the alignment between the latent space and the data manifold, while the encoded prior ensures a more structured and interpretable latent representation. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that EPSWAE outperforms existing autoencoder variants in terms of reconstruction accuracy, latent space quality, and generative performance. Furthermore, the framework exhibits superior scalability and computational efficiency, making it suitable for large-scale applications. The findings highlight the potential of EPSWAE for tasks such as dimensionality reduction, anomaly detection, and generative modeling, offering a significant advancement in unsupervised learning techniques.  

**Keywords**: Autoencoders, Sliced Wasserstein Distance, Latent Manifold, Unsupervised Learning, Generative Models, Dimensionality Reduction.
