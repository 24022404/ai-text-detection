This study addresses the challenge of accurately predicting multiphase flow dynamics, a critical problem in fields such as fluid dynamics, energy systems, and chemical engineering. Traditional methods for modeling multiphase flows often rely on computationally expensive simulations or simplified assumptions, limiting their accuracy and scalability. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel deep neural network (DNN) framework designed to predict multiphase flow behavior with high precision and efficiency. Our approach leverages convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to capture spatial and temporal dependencies in flow data, enabling the model to learn complex patterns and interactions between phases. The proposed framework is trained and validated on a comprehensive dataset generated from high-fidelity simulations, covering a wide range of flow regimes and conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that our DNN-based model achieves superior performance compared to traditional methods, with significant improvements in prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. Furthermore, the model exhibits strong generalization capabilities across diverse flow scenarios, highlighting its potential for real-world applications. This research contributes to the advancement of multiphase flow modeling by introducing a data-driven, AI-powered solution that bridges the gap between accuracy and computational feasibility. The proposed framework has broad implications for industries reliant on multiphase flow analysis, offering a scalable and cost-effective alternative to conventional approaches.  

**Keywords**: multiphase flow, deep neural networks, convolutional neural networks, recurrent neural networks, fluid dynamics, AI modeling.
