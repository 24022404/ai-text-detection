This paper introduces *Hue-Net*, a novel deep learning framework for intensity-based image-to-image translation, leveraging differentiable histogram loss functions to enhance the quality and fidelity of translated images. The primary objective of this work is to address the challenge of preserving intensity distributions during image translation tasks, which is critical for applications such as medical imaging, satellite imagery, and artistic style transfer. Traditional methods often struggle to maintain the statistical properties of pixel intensities, leading to artifacts or loss of critical information. *Hue-Net* proposes a unique approach by integrating differentiable histogram matching into the loss function, enabling the model to learn and preserve the intensity distributions of the target domain while performing translation. The framework employs a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture optimized for end-to-end training, combined with a custom histogram loss function that ensures smooth and accurate intensity mapping. Experimental results demonstrate that *Hue-Net* outperforms existing methods in terms of both visual quality and quantitative metrics, such as structural similarity (SSIM) and peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR). The proposed method also shows robustness across diverse datasets, including natural images and specialized domains. This research contributes a significant advancement in image-to-image translation by introducing a differentiable histogram loss, which opens new possibilities for applications requiring precise intensity preservation. Keywords: image-to-image translation, histogram loss, deep learning, intensity preservation, differentiable functions, convolutional neural networks.
