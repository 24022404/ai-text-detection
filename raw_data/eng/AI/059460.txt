This paper addresses the computational challenges associated with training Legendre Memory Units (LMUs), a type of recurrent neural network that excels in processing time-series data. The primary objective is to enhance the efficiency and scalability of LMU training by exploring methods for parallelization.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel parallelization strategy for LMU training that leverages modern multi-core processing architectures. By decomposing the LMU training algorithm into independent tasks, we enable concurrent computations that significantly reduce training time. Our approach involves optimizing data flow and minimizing synchronization overhead, allowing for effective distribution of computational workload across available cores.

Results/Findings: The proposed parallel training method demonstrates substantial improvements in training efficiency, with experiments showing reduced runtime by up to 60% compared to conventional sequential training approaches. Additionally, our method maintains or improves upon the accuracy of the LMU, as evaluated on benchmark time-series datasets, underscoring the capability of our approach to preserve model performance while accelerating training processes.

Conclusion/Implications: Our research contributes to the field of machine learning by offering a scalable training framework for LMUs, potentially enabling their deployment in real-time and resource-constrained environments. The insights gained from this work can be extended to parallelize other recurrent neural networks, paving the way for performance gains in the training of sequential models. Key Keywords: parallel computing, Legendre Memory Units, time-series, recurrent neural networks, scalable training.
