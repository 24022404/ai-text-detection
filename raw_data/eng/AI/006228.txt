This paper addresses the critical challenge of medical image segmentation in situations where labeled data are scarce, which is a common scenario in medical imaging. We propose a novel semi-supervised few-shot learning approach designed to efficiently utilize limited labeled data while leveraging the abundant unlabeled images typically available in medical datasets.

Methods/Approach: Our method integrates a few-shot learning framework with semi-supervised learning techniques, utilizing convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to enhance segmentation performance. We employ a meta-learning strategy to train the model using a small number of annotated examples per class, augmented with a consistency-based regularization scheme that leverages unlabeled data.

Results/Findings: The proposed approach demonstrates superior segmentation accuracy compared to existing methods, particularly in settings with extremely limited labeled data. Experiments conducted on benchmark medical imaging datasets, including MRI and CT scans, reveal significant improvements in pixel-wise accuracy and Dice coefficient over traditional fully-supervised and other few-shot learning methods.

Conclusion/Implications: Our semi-supervised few-shot learning model offers a robust solution for medical image segmentation with minimal annotated data, making it a valuable tool in clinical settings where expert labeling is a costly and time-consuming process. This approach has the potential to be adapted to a wide range of medical imaging tasks and applications, paving the way for more efficient and scalable AI solutions in healthcare. Key keywords include few-shot learning, semi-supervised learning, medical image segmentation, meta-learning, and convolutional neural networks.
