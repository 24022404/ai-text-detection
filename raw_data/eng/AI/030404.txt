This paper explores the advancements in deep reinforcement learning algorithms, aiming to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of agent decision-making in complex environments. We employ a range of techniques, including deep Q-networks, policy gradients, and actor-critic methods, to develop novel algorithms that enhance learning stability and convergence. Our results demonstrate significant improvements in performance compared to traditional reinforcement learning approaches, with our models achieving state-of-the-art results in various benchmark tasks. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a new regularization technique to mitigate overestimation in Q-learning and the development of a robust exploration strategy to handle high-dimensional action spaces. Our work has important implications for applications in robotics, game playing, and autonomous systems, highlighting the potential of deep reinforcement learning to drive innovation in these fields. Key keywords: deep reinforcement learning, Q-learning, policy gradients, actor-critic methods, regularization techniques, exploration strategies, robotics, game playing, autonomous systems.
