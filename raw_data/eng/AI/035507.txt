This paper presents Language2Pose, a novel system designed to forecast human poses from natural language descriptions. The research addresses the challenge of bridging verbal and physical human expression, aiming to enhance interactive applications in fields such as robotics, virtual reality, and animation.

Methods/Approach: Language2Pose leverages advances in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and pose estimation by using a transformer-based architecture to process input text and predict corresponding human movements. The model is trained on a specially curated dataset that aligns descriptive texts with pose sequences, enabling it to generate accurate and contextually appropriate pose forecasts.

Results/Findings: Our evaluation demonstrates that Language2Pose significantly outperforms existing methods in both quantitative and qualitative metrics. The system shows robust performance across varying linguistic inputs and effectively manages complex language cues to predict a range of dynamic poses. Experimental results indicate high fidelity in the generated poses and favorable comparisons with human expectations and motion capture data.

Conclusion/Implications: Language2Pose represents a significant advancement in human-computer interaction by enabling more intuitive communication between humans and machines through natural language. This research contributes to the growing field of NLP-grounded motion prediction and opens new avenues for applications in entertainment, automated systems, and user experience design. The approach provides a foundation for future work on integrating more diverse linguistic inputs and refining model precision and adaptability.

Keywords: natural language processing, pose forecasting, transformer, motion prediction, human-computer interaction, Language2Pose.
