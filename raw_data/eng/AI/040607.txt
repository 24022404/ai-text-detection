**Abstract**

Accurate estimation of dense scene flow is critical for applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and 3D video processing, yet remains a challenging problem due to dynamic environments and occlusions. This paper addresses dense scene flow computation by integrating stereo disparity and optical flow information. We present a novel framework that combines disparity maps from stereo image pairs with optical flow fields estimated between consecutive frames to recover the full 3D motion of each pixel in a scene. The proposed method incorporates robust matching algorithms and consistency checks to handle noise and mismatches in the input data. Experimental evaluation on standard benchmark datasets demonstrates that our approach achieves higher accuracy and temporal consistency compared to conventional methods relying solely on either disparity or optical flow. Furthermore, our framework shows strong performance in complex scenarios with significant motion and depth discontinuities. The results highlight the effectiveness of fusing stereo and motion cues for dense scene flow reconstruction, making this technique promising for real-world visual perception tasks. **Keywords:** Scene flow, stereo disparity, optical flow, 3D motion estimation, computer vision, depth estimation, motion analysis.
