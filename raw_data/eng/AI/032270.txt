The paper introduces BEV-MODNet, a novel approach for detecting moving objects in a bird's eye view (BEV) representation using monocular cameras, aimed at enhancing autonomous driving capabilities. Traditional detection methods in autonomous vehicles typically rely on multiple sensor modalities, which can be costly and complex. This study seeks to simplify the process by utilizing monocular vision systems without compromising accuracy and efficiency.

Methods/Approach: BEV-MODNet employs a deep learning framework specifically designed for monocular camera data to generate a BEV representation of the environment. The proposed model leverages advanced neural network architectures tailored for object detection, incorporating innovative techniques to transform 2D visual data from a single camera into a coherent 3D spatial context. The approach optimizes feature extraction and motion analysis, ensuring accurate detection of moving objects in real-time driving scenarios.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that BEV-MODNet achieves competitive performance compared to existing multi-sensor systems, with a significant reduction in computational and operational overhead. The method excels in accurately identifying and tracking moving objects under various traffic conditions and lighting environments, maintaining a high detection precision and recall rate.

Conclusion/Implications: BEV-MODNet presents a significant advancement for the development of cost-effective, sensor-efficient autonomous driving systems by reducing reliance on extensive sensor arrays. The research contributes a novel methodology for monocular-based BEV object detection, providing a viable path towards scalable and efficient autonomous vehicle technologies. Future applications may extend to various autonomous navigation systems and smart transportation infrastructure, promoting broader adoption and development in the field.

Keywords: BEV-MODNet, monocular camera, bird's eye view, moving object detection, autonomous driving, deep learning, 3D spatial context, sensor efficiency.
