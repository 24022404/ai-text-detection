The design of optimal morphological structures for robotic systems is a critical yet challenging problem, particularly in dynamic and task-agnostic environments. This paper introduces a novel framework for task-agnostic morphology evolution, enabling the automatic generation of adaptable robotic structures without predefined task objectives. Our approach leverages evolutionary algorithms combined with a generative model to explore diverse morphological designs, optimizing for general adaptability and robustness. The proposed method is evaluated through extensive simulations, where evolved morphologies are tested across a range of tasks, including locomotion, manipulation, and obstacle navigation. Results demonstrate that the evolved structures exhibit superior adaptability and performance compared to traditional task-specific designs, achieving an average improvement of 25% in task generalization. Furthermore, the framework highlights the potential for creating versatile robotic systems capable of operating in unpredictable environments. This research contributes to the field of evolutionary robotics by providing a scalable and efficient methodology for morphology optimization, with implications for autonomous robotics, adaptive systems, and AI-driven design.  

**Keywords**: morphology evolution, task-agnostic design, evolutionary algorithms, generative models, robotic adaptability, autonomous systems.
