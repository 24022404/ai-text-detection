The research addresses the challenge of video scene graph generation, which involves analyzing dynamic scenes to infer semantic relationships between objects over time. The primary goal is to enhance the understanding of video content by developing an approach that adaptively aggregates contextual information relevant to specific targets within a video sequence.

Methods: We propose a novel Target Adaptive Context Aggregation (TACA) framework designed to dynamically fuse contextual cues from different video frames, focusing particularly on targeted objects and their evolving interactions. The framework leverages graph neural networks to model interactions and utilizes attention mechanisms to prioritize context relevant to specific scene elements, allowing for more precise representation of relationships across time.

Results: Our empirical analysis demonstrates the superior performance of the TACA framework when benchmarked against state-of-the-art methods in video scene graph generation tasks. The results indicate significant improvements in accuracy and consistency in detecting and contextualizing object relationships within dynamic video environments. Comparative evaluations revealed that TACA outperformed existing models in several standard datasets, both in object relationship detection and overall scene graph generation accuracy.

Conclusion: The research contributes a significant advancement in the field of video scene analysis by introducing a method that efficiently captures and aggregates context-specific information. The TACA framework's capabilities present promising applications in content-based video retrieval, enhanced video surveillance, and automated video content understanding. Furthermore, the framework's adaptability and precision hold potential for future developments in real-time video analytics.

Keywords: Video Scene Graph Generation, Context Aggregation, Graph Neural Networks, Video Content Understanding, Scene Dynamics, Object Relationships.
