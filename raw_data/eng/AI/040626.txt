This paper presents LumiPath, a novel approach to achieving real-time physically-based rendering on embedded devices. The objective is to bridge the gap between high-quality rendering and the limited computational resources of embedded systems. Our method leverages a combination of optimized rendering algorithms and hardware-aware optimizations to enable fast and accurate rendering of complex scenes. We employ a hybrid rendering technique that combines the benefits of rasterization and ray tracing, allowing for efficient rendering of dynamic scenes with accurate lighting and materials. Our results show that LumiPath achieves significant performance improvements over existing rendering methods, with frame rates exceeding 30 FPS on a variety of embedded devices. The implications of this research are substantial, enabling the deployment of high-quality rendering applications on resource-constrained devices, such as virtual reality, augmented reality, and gaming. Key contributions include a novel rendering pipeline, optimized for embedded devices, and a set of hardware-aware optimizations that minimize computational overhead. Relevant keywords: physically-based rendering, embedded devices, real-time rendering, ray tracing, rasterization, computer graphics.
