This paper introduces GATSBI (Generative Agent-centric Spatio-temporal Object Interaction), a novel framework designed to model and simulate complex interactions between agents and objects in dynamic environments. The primary objective of this research is to address the challenge of capturing spatio-temporal dependencies and agent-centric behaviors in multi-agent systems, which are critical for applications such as autonomous robotics, virtual environments, and intelligent transportation systems. GATSBI leverages a generative approach, combining graph neural networks (GNNs) with temporal sequence modeling to represent interactions as a dynamic graph, where nodes correspond to agents and objects, and edges encode their evolving relationships. The framework incorporates a self-supervised learning mechanism to predict future states and interactions, enabling robust performance in scenarios with partial observability and uncertainty. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that GATSBI outperforms existing methods in terms of prediction accuracy and computational efficiency, achieving a 15% improvement in interaction modeling tasks. Furthermore, the framework exhibits strong generalization capabilities across diverse environments, highlighting its potential for real-world deployment. The key contributions of this work include a scalable, agent-centric architecture for spatio-temporal modeling, a novel generative mechanism for interaction prediction, and a comprehensive evaluation showcasing its superiority over state-of-the-art approaches. GATSBI opens new avenues for research in multi-agent systems and provides a foundation for developing more intelligent and adaptive systems in complex environments.  

**Keywords**: Generative modeling, spatio-temporal interactions, agent-centric systems, graph neural networks, multi-agent systems, autonomous systems.
