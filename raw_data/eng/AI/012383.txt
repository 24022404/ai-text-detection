This paper introduces the Generative Video Transformer (GVT), a novel approach to video generation that leverages the transformer architecture by treating objects as fundamental “words.” Instead of operating directly on pixel data, GVT first identifies and represents objects within video frames using a pre-trained object detector. These object representations, along with their spatial and temporal relationships, are then fed into a transformer network to generate subsequent video frames. This object-centric approach allows GVT to model complex object interactions and long-range dependencies more effectively than pixel-based generative models. We demonstrate the effectiveness of GVT on several benchmark video datasets, showing superior performance in terms of visual fidelity, motion coherence, and object consistency. Specifically, GVT achieves a significant improvement in Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) compared to state-of-the-art generative adversarial networks (GANs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs). These results suggest that representing videos as sequences of interacting objects provides a powerful framework for generative video modeling. Keywords: Video generation, transformer, object detection, generative model, deep learning, artificial intelligence.
