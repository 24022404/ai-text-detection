**Abstract:**

Video understanding tasks require effective modeling of both long-term temporal dependencies and short-term motion cues. Traditional approaches often struggle to capture these multi-scale temporal features in a unified manner, leading to suboptimal performance in complex video analysis scenarios. This paper introduces Long Short View Feature Decomposition via Contrastive Video Representation Learning, a novel framework designed to explicitly decompose and learn temporally diverse features from video data. Our method employs a dual-branch architecture: one branch focuses on extracting long-term temporal representations, while the other captures fine-grained short-term dynamics. We propose a contrastive learning paradigm that encourages the integration of complementary information from both branches, facilitating robust video representations. Experimental results on standard video benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing methods in action recognition and temporal localization tasks, particularly in challenging settings with substantial temporal variation. The results highlight the effectiveness of multi-scale feature decomposition and contrastive objectives in enhancing video representation quality. Our framework provides a scalable and generalizable solution for a wide range of video understanding applications, including video retrieval, surveillance, and activity detection.

**Keywords:** contrastive learning, video representation, temporal feature decomposition, multi-scale learning, action recognition, deep learning
