Accurate survival prediction is critical for personalized cancer treatment planning. While machine learning models have shown promise in this area, they often lack reliable uncertainty estimates, hindering clinical adoption. This paper introduces a novel deep learning framework for cancer survival prediction that explicitly quantifies uncertainty. Our approach integrates a modified Cox proportional hazards model with a variational inference technique to estimate both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty. We evaluate our method on several publicly available cancer genomic datasets, demonstrating improved prediction accuracy and well-calibrated uncertainty estimates compared to existing state-of-the-art survival models. The results show that incorporating uncertainty information leads to more robust and reliable survival predictions, offering clinicians a valuable tool for informed decision-making. The framework’s ability to identify high-risk patients with greater confidence has the potential to improve patient outcomes.
Keywords: Cancer survival prediction, uncertainty estimation, deep learning, variational inference, Cox proportional hazards model.
