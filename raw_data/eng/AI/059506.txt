Understanding pedestrian trajectories is critical for applications such as autonomous navigation, urban planning, and crowd behavior analysis. This paper addresses the challenge of learning meaningful representations of pedestrian trajectories to improve prediction accuracy and interpretability. We propose a novel Actor-Critic Sequence-to-Sequence Autoencoder (AC-Seq2SeqAE) framework that leverages reinforcement learning to enhance the encoding and decoding of trajectory sequences. The actor-critic mechanism optimizes the reconstruction process by dynamically adjusting the model's focus on critical trajectory features, while the sequence-to-sequence architecture captures temporal dependencies and spatial patterns. Our approach is evaluated on benchmark pedestrian trajectory datasets, demonstrating superior performance in trajectory prediction and representation quality compared to existing methods. The results show significant improvements in prediction error rates and robustness under varying environmental conditions. The proposed framework not only advances the state-of-the-art in trajectory modeling but also provides a scalable solution for real-world applications. Key contributions include the integration of reinforcement learning with sequence-to-sequence models and the development of a generalized representation learning method applicable to diverse trajectory datasets. Keywords: pedestrian trajectories, representation learning, sequence-to-sequence models, actor-critic, autoencoder, trajectory prediction.
