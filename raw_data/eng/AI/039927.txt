This paper investigates the application of the Local Adaptive Modified Self-Organizing Tree ARTMAP (LAMSTAR) neural network for iris recognition in personal identification systems. The objective is to develop a robust and efficient iris recognition system resilient to variations in illumination, occlusion, and noise. The proposed method utilizes a novel pre-processing pipeline involving iris localization, normalization via Daugman's rubber sheet model, and feature extraction using Gabor filters. The extracted features are then fed into the LAMSTAR neural network for classification. Experimental results on the CASIA-IrisV4 Interval database demonstrate that the LAMSTAR network achieves a recognition accuracy of 99.2%, surpassing the performance of traditional methods such as SVM and other ART-based architectures by 1.5-2%. The findings suggest that LAMSTAR’s adaptive learning capability and hierarchical structure enable it to effectively handle the complexities of iris patterns, making it a promising approach for high-security biometric identification systems. Keywords: Iris Recognition, Biometrics, LAMSTAR, Neural Network, Pattern Recognition, Personal Identification.
