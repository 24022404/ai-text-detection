This paper presents a novel approach for 3D object localization using 2D estimates derived from monocular images, addressing the challenge of accurately determining object position and orientation in 3D space with limited sensor data. Our method leverages advancements in 2D object detection to obtain precise bounding box predictions, which are then combined with geometric constraints and a learned depth estimation network to infer 3D object coordinates. We introduce a multi-stage refinement process that iteratively optimizes the 3D pose, incorporating semantic understanding from the 2D detections to improve accuracy and robustness. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance compared to existing methods, particularly in scenarios with significant occlusion or viewpoint variation. The proposed system exhibits enhanced efficiency and accuracy in 3D object localization, offering significant potential for applications in autonomous navigation, augmented reality, and robotic perception. Keywords: 3D Object Localization, 2D Object Detection, Computer Vision, Monocular Vision, Depth Estimation, Pose Estimation.
