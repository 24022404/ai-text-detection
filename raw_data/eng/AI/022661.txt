Abstract  
Accurate forecasting and causal analysis of multivariate time series are essential in domains ranging from economics to neuroscience, yet classical vector autoregressions (VAR) struggle to capture nonlinear dependencies and complex dynamics. We propose Neural Vector Autoregressions (NVAR), a unified framework that integrates deep neural networks with autoregressive modeling to jointly address forecasting, causality inference, and impulse response estimation. NVAR employs a modular architecture that learns flexible, nonlinear interactions among variables while enforcing sparsity constraints for interpretable Granger-causal structures. To compute impulse responses, we derive a local linearization of the trained network around its operating point, enabling dynamic analysis of system shocks. Extensive experiments on synthetic data and real-world financial and neural recordings demonstrate that NVAR reduces forecasting error by up to 20% compared to linear VAR and kernel-based baselines, and achieves higher precision and recall in detecting causal links. Furthermore, the recovered impulse responses closely match ground-truth dynamics under diverse scenarios. Our results show that NVAR delivers both predictive accuracy and interpretability, offering a powerful tool for time series analysis. This approach opens new avenues for robust decision support in economics, epidemiology, climate science, and other fields that rely on understanding complex temporal interactions.  

Keywords: Neural VAR, multivariate time series forecasting, Granger causality, impulse response analysis, nonlinear dynamics, sparse modeling
