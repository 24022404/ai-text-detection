This paper addresses the challenge of forecasting human-object interaction in first-person videos by proposing a novel approach that jointly predicts motor attention and actions. The objective is to anticipate how a person will interact with objects in their environment, enabling applications such as assistive robotics, human-computer interaction, and video analysis. Our method leverages a deep learning framework that combines visual and sensory cues to predict both the motor attention, which represents the person's focus on specific objects, and the subsequent actions. The results show that our joint prediction model outperforms state-of-the-art methods in forecasting human-object interactions, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and robustness. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a new dataset for first-person human-object interaction forecasting and the development of a novel attention-action prediction framework. This work has important implications for various fields, including artificial intelligence, computer vision, and human-robot interaction, and paves the way for the creation of more intelligent and interactive systems that can anticipate and respond to human behavior. Key keywords: human-object interaction, first-person video, motor attention, action prediction, deep learning, computer vision, artificial intelligence.
