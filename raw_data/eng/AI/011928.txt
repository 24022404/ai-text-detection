Person re-identification (re-ID) is a crucial task in computer vision, focusing on matching individuals across non-overlapping camera views. While deep metric learning has shown significant progress, the effectiveness of loss functions in optimizing embedding discriminability remains a challenge. This paper introduces a novel Cluster Loss designed specifically for person re-identification, aiming to enhance the compactness within identity clusters while maximizing the separation between different identities in the learned feature space. The proposed Cluster Loss is integrated into a deep convolutional neural network and evaluated on standard person re-ID benchmarks. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms widely used loss functions such as triplet loss and softmax loss in terms of accuracy and mean Average Precision (mAP). Furthermore, the Cluster Loss promotes better convergence and more robust feature embeddings, making it suitable for large-scale and real-world re-ID applications. Our method offers new insights into loss function design for re-identification tasks and has potential implications for other metric learning scenarios. Keywords: person re-identification, cluster loss, deep metric learning, feature embedding, computer vision.
