This paper addresses the challenge of accurately perceiving neuron point processes in scenarios characterized by unknown unknowns, where unexpected and previously unobserved events can occur. Our objective is to develop a data-driven approach that enhances the robustness and adaptability of neuron activity analysis in the face of incomplete knowledge. We introduce a novel framework that combines point process models with deep learning techniques to learn and predict neuron firing patterns, while simultaneously identifying and accommodating unforeseen events. The method leverages recurrent neural networks to capture temporal dependencies in neuron firing sequences and incorporates an anomaly detection module to identify deviations from learned patterns. Experiments on synthetic and real neural data demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing methods in predicting neuron activity and detecting unknown unknowns, leading to more reliable and comprehensive neural data analysis. Key contributions include enhanced prediction accuracy, improved anomaly detection, and a framework for handling unforeseen events in neuron point processes. Keywords: Neuron point process, unknown unknowns, data-driven perception, deep learning, anomaly detection, neural data analysis.
