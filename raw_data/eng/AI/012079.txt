Objective: Medical image segmentation often demands extensive annotated data, which is costly and time‐consuming to obtain, particularly for rare conditions. This study introduces a semi‐supervised few‐shot learning framework designed to achieve high segmentation accuracy with minimal labeled examples.  
Methods: We combine meta‐learning with consistency regularization, employing a student–teacher architecture that leverages unlabeled images through pseudo‐label generation and confidence‐based filtering. An attention‐guided feature fusion module aligns support and query representations, while a task‐adaptive loss function balances supervised and unsupervised signals. The model is trained episodically on small labeled subsets (1–5 shots) and large pools of unlabeled scans to mimic real‐world clinical scarcity.  
Results: Experiments on three public benchmarks—abdominal CT, brain MRI, and retinal OCT—demonstrate that our approach achieves average Dice scores of 82.4% in 1‐shot and 88.7% in 5‐shot settings, outperforming state‐of‐the‐art fully supervised baselines by up to 8.3 percentage points. Ablation studies confirm the efficacy of pseudo‐label filtering and attention‐guided fusion, each contributing over 3% improvement in segmentation quality.  
Conclusion: The proposed semi‐supervised few‐shot framework significantly reduces annotation requirements while maintaining competitive performance. Its adaptability to diverse imaging modalities suggests broad applicability in clinical workflows where labeled data are scarce.  
Keywords: semi‐supervised learning, few‐shot learning, medical image segmentation, pseudo‐labeling, meta‐learning.
