This paper addresses the challenge of 3D object detection from RGB-D data, focusing on improving generalization across diverse environments with limited labeled data. We propose a transferable semi-supervised learning framework that leverages both labeled and unlabeled data to enhance detection accuracy while reducing annotation costs. Our approach combines a novel multi-modal feature fusion mechanism with a self-training strategy, enabling the model to effectively utilize depth and color information for robust object localization and classification. The framework employs a teacher-student architecture, where the teacher model generates pseudo-labels for unlabeled data, which are then refined and used to train the student model iteratively. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing supervised and semi-supervised approaches by significant margins. Notably, the proposed framework shows strong transferability, maintaining high accuracy when applied to unseen domains with minimal fine-tuning. The results highlight the potential of our approach for real-world applications, such as autonomous driving and robotics, where labeled data is scarce and environments are highly variable. This work contributes to advancing semi-supervised learning in 3D object detection by introducing a scalable and adaptable solution for cross-domain generalization.  

**Keywords**: 3D object detection, semi-supervised learning, RGB-D data, transfer learning, feature fusion, self-training.
