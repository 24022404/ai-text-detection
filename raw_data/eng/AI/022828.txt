Pedestrian trajectory prediction is a critical challenge in autonomous systems, particularly for applications like self-driving cars and robotics. Traditional methods often struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) samples, leading to unreliable predictions in dynamic environments. This paper introduces MG-GAN, a novel multi-generator generative adversarial network designed to address this limitation. The proposed model leverages multiple generators to capture diverse pedestrian behaviors and prevent OOD samples by enhancing the robustness of trajectory predictions. MG-GAN employs a discriminative framework to evaluate and refine the generated trajectories, ensuring high accuracy and reliability. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that MG-GAN outperforms existing methods in terms of prediction accuracy and robustness, particularly in complex, real-world scenarios. The model achieves a significant reduction in OOD errors, making it a promising solution for real-time applications. This research contributes to the field by providing a scalable and efficient approach to pedestrian trajectory prediction, with potential implications for improving safety and decision-making in autonomous systems.  

**Keywords**: Pedestrian trajectory prediction, generative adversarial networks, out-of-distribution samples, multi-generator models, autonomous systems.
