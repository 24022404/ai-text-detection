This paper presents a novel self-supervised approach to mapping language and noisy real-world videos, enabling machines to watch and learn from unlabelled video data. The objective is to develop a model that can effectively associate linguistic descriptions with corresponding visual content in videos, despite the presence of noise and irrelevant information. Our method leverages a multimodal fusion framework, combining vision and language models to learn a shared representation space. Through extensive experiments, we demonstrate the efficacy of our approach in learning robust and generalizable mappings between language and video, outperforming existing supervised and self-supervised methods. Our results show significant improvements in video retrieval and description generation tasks, with applications in areas such as video search, recommendation systems, and human-computer interaction. The key contributions of this work include the introduction of a self-supervised learning paradigm for multimodal video understanding, and the development of a noise-robust video-language mapping model. Our approach has the potential to unlock the vast amounts of unlabelled video data available, enabling machines to learn from real-world videos and improving the state-of-the-art in video understanding and generation. Key keywords: self-supervised learning, multimodal fusion, video-language mapping, noise-robust learning, computer vision, natural language processing.
