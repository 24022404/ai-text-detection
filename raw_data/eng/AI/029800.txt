This paper presents an empirical evaluation of a Q-Learning algorithm for model-free autonomous soaring in unstructured atmospheric environments. The primary objective is to enable unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to exploit atmospheric updrafts, such as thermals, for energy-efficient flight without relying on predefined environmental models. The proposed method employs Q-Learning, a reinforcement learning algorithm, to dynamically learn optimal soaring strategies from sensor feedback and flight experience. Through extensive simulated flight trials, the performance of the model-free Q-Learning agent is assessed against traditional rule-based and model-driven soaring approaches. Results demonstrate that the Q-Learning algorithm can successfully identify and utilize thermal updrafts, achieving significant improvements in flight endurance and energy conservation. Notably, the system demonstrates adaptability to varying and unpredictable wind fields, outperforming baseline methods in both success rate and cumulative soaring time. The study highlights the practical potential of reinforcement learning for enhancing UAV autonomy in challenging and dynamic atmospheric conditions. These findings offer valuable insights for future developments in autonomous flight, environmental adaptation, and energy-aware UAV operations. Keywords: Q-Learning, reinforcement learning, model-free control, autonomous soaring, unmanned aerial vehicles, atmospheric energy harvesting.
