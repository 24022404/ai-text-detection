The proliferation of colorization techniques has made it increasingly challenging to differentiate between genuinely colorized images and original color photographs. This research aims to address the problem of detecting colorized images with high accuracy and good generalization across diverse datasets.

Methods: We propose utilizing convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to create a robust detection model specifically designed for this task. The approach involves training the CNN on a comprehensive dataset composed of both original and purposely colorized images. The model employs various layers and feature extraction techniques to effectively discern subtle differences that may not be evident to the human eye.

Results: The proposed CNN model outperforms existing methods by achieving a significant improvement in accuracy and generalization capabilities. In our experiments, the model demonstrated a detection accuracy of over 95% across multiple datasets, including real-world images and those generated by different colorization algorithms. Comparative analysis indicates that our model has a lower false positive rate compared to traditional detection techniques.

Conclusion: This study contributes a novel method for detecting colorized images using CNNs, providing a promising tool for applications in digital forensics and media authentication. The research highlights the potential of deep learning techniques in enhancing the accuracy and reliability of image authenticity verification. The model's strong generalization suggests its applicability to a wide range of scenarios, paving the way for future advancements in the field.

Keywords: colorized images, convolutional neural networks, image detection, deep learning, digital forensics, media authentication.
