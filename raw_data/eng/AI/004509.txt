Objective: Variational Continual Learning (VCL) offers a principled Bayesian framework to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in sequential tasks, yet its practical performance and interpretability remain limited. This work aims to both enhance VCL’s accuracy on long task sequences and provide deeper insights into its behavior.

Methods: We introduce two key improvements to the standard VCL approach. First, we augment the variational objective with an importance-weighted regularizer that balances old and new task information more effectively, reducing bias toward recent data. Second, we propose a hierarchical prior structure that dynamically adjusts parameter uncertainty based on task similarity, improving uncertainty calibration. To interpret model behavior, we develop layer-wise forgetting metrics and visualize posterior contraction over tasks. Experiments are conducted on benchmark continual-learning datasets (Permuted MNIST, Split CIFAR-100, and Omniglot), comparing our method against baseline VCL, Elastic Weight Consolidation, and replay-based systems.

Results: Our enhanced VCL achieves a 15–20% reduction in average forgetting and a 10% boost in overall accuracy versus standard VCL, while matching or surpassing replay-based methods without storing raw data. Hierarchical priors yield better-calibrated uncertainty estimates (15% lower expected calibration error), and the proposed interpretability tools reveal consistent parameter importance patterns across tasks.

Conclusion: The proposed improvements make VCL more robust and transparent, offering both empirical gains and actionable insights into posterior dynamics. This work advances the state of Bayesian continual learning and provides a foundation for real-world online adaptation in resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive settings.

Keywords: variational continual learning, Bayesian neural networks, catastrophic forgetting, variational inference, uncertainty calibration, interpretability.
