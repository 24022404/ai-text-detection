Modern computing systems rely heavily on caching mechanisms to optimize performance and reduce latency. However, traditional cache management strategies often struggle to adapt to dynamic workloads and varying access patterns. This paper introduces **RLCache**, a novel approach to automated cache management using **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**. The primary objective of RLCache is to dynamically optimize cache eviction policies in real-time, ensuring efficient resource utilization across diverse workloads.  

The proposed system employs a **deep Q-learning-based RL model** that learns optimal eviction strategies by interacting with the cache environment. Unlike static policies such as LRU or LFU, RLCache adapts to changing access patterns, making it particularly effective for applications with unpredictable or non-stationary workloads. The model is trained using a reward function that balances hit rate, latency, and resource overhead, ensuring a trade-off between performance and computational cost.  

Experimental results demonstrate that RLCache outperforms traditional cache management techniques, achieving up to **15% higher hit rates** and **20% lower latency** in benchmark tests. Furthermore, the system exhibits robust performance across a variety of real-world datasets, including web server logs and database query traces.  

The key contribution of this research lies in the integration of RL into cache management, offering a scalable and adaptive solution for modern computing systems. RLCache has significant implications for applications in cloud computing, content delivery networks, and distributed databases, where efficient caching is critical for performance.  

**Keywords**: Cache Management, Reinforcement Learning, Deep Q-Learning, Adaptive Systems, Performance Optimization.
