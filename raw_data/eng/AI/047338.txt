The early and accurate recognition of pain expression in neonates is crucial for timely management and improved care outcomes. This research addresses the challenge of neonatal pain assessment by leveraging transfer learning techniques to develop an automated, reliable system for recognizing pain expressions from facial cues.

Methods/Approach: The study employs a transfer learning approach utilizing pre-trained deep neural networks to enhance the recognition capabilities of neonatal pain expression from video data. Specifically, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) designed for image classification are fine-tuned on a dataset comprising neonatal facial images exhibiting various expressions of distress and pain. Data augmentation and regularization techniques are employed to improve model generalization and accuracy.

Results/Findings: The proposed model achieved significant improvements in classification accuracy and speed over baseline methods, effectively distinguishing between different levels of pain expressions with high sensitivity and specificity. Comparative analysis with existing methodologies highlights the superior performance of our approach, demonstrating its robustness across diverse neonatal demographic variables.

Conclusion/Implications: This innovative application of transfer learning presents a significant advancement in neonatal care, offering an efficient and non-invasive method for real-time pain assessment. The findings suggest potential integration into clinical settings, enhancing decision support systems for healthcare providers. Future work could extend this framework to broader pediatric pain recognition systems and explore real-time deployment scenarios. Key keywords include neonatal pain, transfer learning, convolutional neural networks, facial expression recognition, and automated healthcare systems.
