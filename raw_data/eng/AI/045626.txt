Weakly supervised instance segmentation (WSIS) is a challenging task in computer vision, as it requires precise object localization and segmentation using only weak supervision, such as image-level labels. This paper introduces **Label-PEnet**, a novel framework that leverages sequential label propagation and enhancement networks to address the limitations of existing WSIS methods. The proposed approach consists of two key components: a **label propagation module** that iteratively refines object boundaries by propagating pixel-level pseudo-labels, and an **enhancement network** that improves segmentation accuracy by learning discriminative features from the propagated labels. Unlike traditional methods that rely on static pseudo-labels, Label-PEnet dynamically updates and enhances labels during training, enabling more accurate instance-level segmentation. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including PASCAL VOC and COCO, demonstrate that Label-PEnet achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing weakly supervised methods in terms of both segmentation accuracy and object localization. The framework’s ability to generalize across diverse datasets highlights its robustness and potential for real-world applications, such as medical imaging and autonomous driving. This work contributes to advancing weakly supervised learning by introducing a dynamic label refinement mechanism, paving the way for more efficient and accurate instance segmentation systems.  

**Keywords**: Weakly supervised learning, instance segmentation, label propagation, enhancement networks, computer vision, pseudo-labels.
