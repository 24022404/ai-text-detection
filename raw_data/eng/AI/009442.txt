This paper introduces a novel unsupervised approach to sketch-based image retrieval (SBIR). Existing SBIR methods often rely on large labeled datasets for training, which are expensive and time-consuming to acquire. To address this limitation, we propose a framework that leverages cycle consistency and generative adversarial networks (GANs) to learn a shared representation space between sketches and images without explicit supervision. The core of our method involves training a cycle-GAN to translate between sketch and image domains, coupled with a contrastive loss that encourages semantic consistency across the domains. Retrieval is performed by projecting both sketches and images into the learned common space and identifying nearest neighbors. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our unsupervised approach achieves competitive retrieval accuracy compared to supervised methods, while significantly reducing the need for labeled data. This work opens new avenues for SBIR in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or unavailable, enabling more practical and scalable applications. Keywords: Sketch-based Image Retrieval, Unsupervised Learning, Generative Adversarial Networks, CycleGAN, Image Retrieval.
