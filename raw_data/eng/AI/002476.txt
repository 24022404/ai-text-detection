Inferential privacy in time-series data is critical for protecting sensitive information against sophisticated inference attacks. This paper investigates composition properties of inferential privacy, focusing on how privacy guarantees degrade or improve as multiple privacy mechanisms are applied sequentially or in parallel. We introduce a novel framework for analyzing composition based on differential privacy and Rényi differential privacy, adapted to account for the temporal dependencies inherent in time-series data. Our methods utilize Gaussian mechanisms and Laplace mechanisms, evaluating their cumulative privacy loss under various composition scenarios. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world time-series datasets demonstrate that naive composition can lead to significant privacy degradation. We propose optimized composition strategies based on privacy budget allocation that demonstrably improve overall privacy guarantees while maintaining data utility. Our findings offer practical guidelines for designing privacy-preserving time-series analysis systems. Keywords: Inferential Privacy, Time-Series Data, Differential Privacy, Composition, Privacy Budget Allocation, Data Security.
