Designing optical thin-film coatings with desired spectral properties is a challenging optimization problem. This paper introduces a novel reinforcement learning (RL) approach to automate and enhance this design process. We formulate the thin-film design problem as a Markov Decision Process, where the agent iteratively modifies layer thicknesses to optimize target reflectance and transmittance spectra. Our method utilizes a deep Q-network (DQN) to learn an optimal policy for layer deposition. The reward function is carefully designed to guide the agent toward achieving specific spectral characteristics while penalizing deviations. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed RL-based method significantly outperforms traditional optimization techniques, achieving improved spectral matching and design efficiency. Furthermore, the learned policies exhibit generalization capabilities, allowing for the rapid design of coatings with varying target spectra. This approach offers a promising avenue for accelerating the development of advanced optical coatings. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Optical Thin-Film Design, Deep Q-Network, Spectral Optimization, Markov Decision Process.
