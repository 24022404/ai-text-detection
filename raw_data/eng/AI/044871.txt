Understanding and modeling the underlying structure of time series data is a fundamental challenge in information technology, particularly for tasks involving prediction, classification, and anomaly detection. This paper introduces a novel framework that simultaneously learns low-dimensional state embeddings and identifies metastable clusters directly from raw time series data. Our approach leverages dimensionality reduction techniques combined with advanced clustering algorithms to distill complex temporal signals into informative and compact representations, capturing both the dynamic evolution and the long-lived (metastable) states within the data. Empirical evaluations on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed model outperforms conventional feature extraction and clustering methods in terms of state interpretability, reconstruction accuracy, and the ability to reveal slow-evolving system behaviors. The results provide a robust foundation for downstream applications such as system monitoring, change-point detection, and model-based control. This research advances the state of the art in time series analysis by offering an integrated solution for dimensionality reduction and metastable state discovery, paving the way for more efficient and interpretable analysis of high-dimensional temporal processes. Key keywords: state embedding, time series, dimensionality reduction, metastable clusters, unsupervised learning, temporal dynamics.
