This paper addresses the challenge of language-guided navigation, where autonomous agents interpret natural language instructions to navigate complex environments. We propose a novel framework that integrates cross-modal grounding with alternate adversarial learning to enhance agents’ understanding and execution of language-based navigation tasks. Our method leverages deep neural networks to align semantic features from textual instructions with visual environmental cues, enabling more accurate decision-making during navigation. The adversarial learning component introduces an alternate training paradigm, fostering a robust interplay between the agent’s policy network and a discriminator that assesses alignment between navigation actions and intended instructions. Comprehensive experiments conducted on benchmark navigation datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms state-of-the-art models in terms of navigation accuracy and robustness, particularly in unseen and ambiguous scenarios. The results highlight the effectiveness of cross-modal grounding in bridging the gap between linguistic inputs and perceptual understanding, while alternate adversarial learning provides enhanced generalization and adaptability. Our contributions advance the field of embodied AI by facilitating more intuitive and reliable language-based interactions within real-world and simulated environments. Keywords: language-guided navigation, cross-modal grounding, adversarial learning, deep learning, embodied AI, natural language processing, visual grounding.
