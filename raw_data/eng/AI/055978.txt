This paper addresses the challenge of time series classification by introducing a novel approach based on topological signature estimates and Sinkhorn divergence. The objective is to develop a robust and efficient method for distinguishing between different time series patterns. Our approach utilizes persistent homology to extract topological features from time series data, which are then compared using Sinkhorn divergence, a variant of the Wasserstein distance. The results show that our method outperforms existing techniques in terms of classification accuracy and computational efficiency. Key findings include the ability to capture subtle topological changes in time series data and to handle high-dimensional datasets. The conclusion highlights the potential of this approach for real-world applications, such as anomaly detection and signal processing. The novelty of our research lies in the combination of topological data analysis and optimal transport theory, providing a new perspective on time series classification. Relevant keywords: time series classification, topological data analysis, Sinkhorn divergence, persistent homology, optimal transport.
