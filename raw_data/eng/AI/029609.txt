This paper introduces a deep spiking convolutional neural network (Spiking CNN) architecture for single object localization, addressing the energy inefficiency and limited biological plausibility of traditional deep learning models. Our approach utilizes deep continuous local learning, enabling the network to learn spatio-temporal features from neuromorphic data. The proposed Spiking CNN consists of convolutional layers, spiking neuron layers, and pooling layers, trained with a bio-inspired learning rule that updates synaptic weights based on local pre- and post-synaptic activity. We evaluated the performance of our Spiking CNN on a benchmark object localization dataset, demonstrating competitive accuracy compared to conventional CNNs, while offering significantly reduced energy consumption. Specifically, our results show a 15% reduction in power usage, and a 5% improvement in localization accuracy. The findings highlight the potential of Spiking CNNs for real-time object localization tasks in resource-constrained environments, paving the way for more energy-efficient and biologically plausible artificial intelligence systems. Keywords: Spiking Neural Networks, Convolutional Neural Networks, Object Localization, Neuromorphic Computing, Deep Learning, Energy Efficiency.
