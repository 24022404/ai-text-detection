Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have demonstrated remarkable success in generating realistic data across various domains. However, estimating the likelihood of data under a GAN remains a significant challenge, hindering quantitative evaluation and comparison with other generative models. This paper introduces a novel approach for likelihood estimation in GANs by leveraging a learned auxiliary network. Our method trains a separate network to approximate the intractable density function implicitly defined by the generator. Specifically, we employ a variational inference framework, optimizing a lower bound on the log-likelihood using the auxiliary network as an approximation to the true posterior. The approach is validated on several benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and CelebA, demonstrating competitive performance in likelihood estimation compared to existing GAN evaluation metrics and other likelihood-based generative models. Furthermore, we explore the use of our likelihood estimates for anomaly detection and model selection. This work provides a practical and effective approach for quantifying the generative capability of GANs, facilitating their wider adoption and application. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, Likelihood Estimation, Variational Inference, Density Estimation, GAN Evaluation.
