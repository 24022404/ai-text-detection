This study introduces Vispi, a novel system for the automatic visual perception and interpretation of chest X-rays, addressing the need for efficient and accurate diagnostic tools in medical imaging. The growing volume of radiological data necessitates advanced techniques to aid radiologists in promptly identifying potential abnormalities.

Methods/Approach: Vispi employs deep learning algorithms, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), to analyze chest X-ray images. The system is trained using a large dataset, incorporating data augmentation and transfer learning to enhance its robustness and accuracy. The framework includes a pre-processing module for noise reduction, followed by feature extraction and classification stages, designed to identify common pathologies such as pneumonia, tuberculosis, and lung nodules.

Results/Findings: The implementation of Vispi demonstrates high accuracy in the detection and classification of chest X-ray anomalies, outperforming existing automated systems. The model achieves a significant improvement in sensitivity and specificity, with a marked reduction in false positives. Comparative analysis with benchmark datasets shows Vispi's superior capability in handling diverse pathological cases.

Conclusion/Implications: Vispi presents a valuable tool for augmenting radiological workflows, offering rapid and reliable analysis of chest X-rays. Its integration with hospital information systems can enhance diagnostic efficiency and accuracy, particularly in under-resourced settings. The system's adaptability to incorporate additional diseases suggests its potential for broader applications in medical imaging diagnostics. Further development could expand its utility to other imaging modalities and complex clinical contexts.

Keywords: Vispi, chest X-rays, medical imaging, deep learning, CNNs, radiology, automated diagnosis, medical diagnostics.
