This research tackles the challenge of hard exploration games, where traditional reinforcement learning methods often struggle due to sparse rewards and complex environments. We aim to enhance the exploration capabilities of agents by leveraging video demonstrations from YouTube, thus bridging the gap between passive observation and active gameplay.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework that integrates a deep learning model with transfer learning techniques to process and learn from publicly available video content. The model extracts meaningful behavior patterns and strategies employed in various gaming scenarios, subsequently translating these observations into actionable insights for reinforcement learning agents. The architecture employs a combination of convolutional neural networks and recurrent neural networks to effectively process sequential video data and align it with game states.

Results/Findings: Our approach demonstrates significant improvements in exploration efficiency and success rate across several challenging exploration games when compared to baseline models without video integration. Empirical results showcase the model's capability to outperform traditional methods in terms of both the speed of learning and the quality of gameplay strategies developed by the agents. The experimental evaluation includes games known for sparse reward structures, highlighting the robustness and adaptability of our proposed framework.

Conclusion/Implications: This study underscores the potential of using readily available video content as a rich resource for training reinforcement learning agents in complex environments. The contributions include a methodology for repurposing online video content for AI learning, presenting a cost-effective and scalable solution for enhancing agent performance. The implications of this research extend beyond gaming, suggesting applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and other domains where effective exploration is crucial. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, video demonstrations, deep learning, transfer learning, exploration games.
