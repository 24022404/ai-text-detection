This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised domain adaptation in stereo matching, a crucial component in many computer vision applications such as autonomous driving and 3D scene reconstruction. The primary objective is to enhance the adaptability and accuracy of stereo matching algorithms when applied to new domains without manual re-calibration.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel geometry-aware framework for unsupervised domain adaptation in stereo matching. Our approach integrates geometric consistency constraints with disparity estimation, facilitating the transfer of knowledge from labeled synthetic datasets to unlabeled real-world data. The framework leverages a geometrically-guided self-supervised learning pipeline, optimizing the model's performance by enforcing structural robustness and reducing domain discrepancies.

Results/Findings: The proposed method significantly improves stereo matching accuracy across various cross-domain scenarios, demonstrating superior performance over existing unsupervised domain adaptation methodologies. Extensive experiments reveal that our geometry-aware approach not only maintains high precision in disparity maps but also enhances the system's adaptability to different environmental conditions, such as variable lighting and scene complexities, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings of this research provide valuable insights into leveraging geometric cues for domain adaptation in stereo matching tasks. The integration of geometric consistency offers a promising avenue for improving cross-domain robustness, enabling more reliable 3D information gathering in diverse real-world applications. This work opens pathways for future research in geometry-aware learning strategies and their applications in other areas of computer vision.

Keywords: stereo matching, unsupervised domain adaptation, geometry-aware, disparity estimation, computer vision, self-supervised learning.
