Objective: Graph coarsening is a fundamental technique for reducing the size of large-scale graphs while preserving their essential structural and spectral properties. Although it has been extensively studied in scientific computing—particularly for multigrid solvers and spectral partitioning—its adoption in modern machine-learning pipelines remains fragmented. This paper aims to bridge that gap by providing a unified treatment of graph coarsening methods and demonstrating their applicability across both domains.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a flexible, multilevel coarsening framework that generalizes classical algebraic multigrid (AMG) coarseners and recent spectral sparsification techniques. Our approach selects node aggregates based on a combination of local edge weights, global spectral importance scores, and feature-based affinities. We further propose an adaptive refinement step that iteratively rebalances coarse graphs to better support downstream learning tasks such as graph neural network (GNN) training and spectral clustering.

Results/Findings: Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets from scientific computing (mesh graphs, finite-element matrices) and machine learning (social networks, citation graphs) show that our method achieves up to 10× reduction in graph size with less than 2% loss in algebraic connectivity. When used as a preprocessing step for GNNs, coarsened graphs yield training speed-ups of 3–5× while matching or improving accuracy compared to full-scale models. In spectral partitioning tasks, our coarsened graphs attain cuts within 1.5% of the optimal.

Conclusion/Implications: By unifying coarsening strategies across disciplines, our work highlights the transferable benefits of multilevel graph reduction. The proposed framework enables scalable scientific simulations and accelerates machine-learning workflows without sacrificing solution quality. This synergy paves the way for more efficient large-scale graph analytics in both traditional numerical methods and emerging AI applications.

Keywords: graph coarsening, multilevel algorithms, spectral sparsification, graph neural networks, scientific computing.
