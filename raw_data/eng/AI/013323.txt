The increasing demand for scalable solutions in distributed machine learning has led to the exploration of large batch sizes and asynchronous updates. This paper aims to investigate the critical parameters that influence the effectiveness of distributed learning systems when scaling with large batches and employing asynchronous update mechanisms.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a framework for examining distributed learning models that focuses on parameter tuning for batch sizes and asynchrony levels. Key aspects include network topology, communication overhead, and synchronization delays. Our study utilizes both theoretical analysis and empirical validation through large-scale simulations and real-world dataset tests.

Results/Findings: Our findings demonstrate that careful tuning of batch size and synchronization frequency can significantly enhance system performance, leading to improved convergence rates and reduced training time. Notably, we identified thresholds for network communication latency where asynchronous updates begin to yield diminishing returns. Additionally, our results show that certain configurations outperform traditional synchronous approaches in distributed settings.

Conclusion/Implications: The insights provided by this research contribute to the broader understanding of distributed learning paradigms and offer practical guidelines for implementing scalable solutions. The framework and results can aid practitioners in optimizing distributed learning architectures to achieve greater efficiency, especially as the demand for training large AI models continues to rise. Keywords such as scalable learning, asynchronous updates, distributed machine learning, large batches, and parameter tuning are crucial for advancing the efficiency of contemporary machine learning systems.
