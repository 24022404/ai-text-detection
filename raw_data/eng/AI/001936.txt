Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have become a cornerstone methodology for image synthesis and other data generation tasks in artificial intelligence. However, the evaluation of GAN output quality remains a significant challenge due to the diversity and limitations of existing assessment metrics. This paper presents an empirical study aimed at systematically comparing and analyzing the most widely used evaluation metrics for GANs, including Inception Score (IS), Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Kernel Inception Distance (KID), and Precision-Recall metrics, among others. We conduct a series of experiments using multiple benchmark datasets and diverse GAN architectures to assess the consistency, robustness, and sensitivity of each metric with respect to variations in GAN outputs. Our findings reveal notable discrepancies in how metrics respond to image quality, diversity, and mode collapse, highlighting strengths and weaknesses unique to each approach. Furthermore, we discuss the correlations between metric scores and human perceptual judgments, providing actionable insights into which metrics align best with qualitative evaluations. The results offer valuable guidance for researchers and practitioners in selecting appropriate metrics for GAN assessment and underscore the need for continued development of more comprehensive evaluation tools. This study contributes to the broader understanding of GAN evaluation and aims to facilitate more reliable benchmarking in future generative model research. Keywords: generative adversarial networks, GAN evaluation, empirical study, Inception Score, Fréchet Inception Distance, performance metrics, image synthesis.
