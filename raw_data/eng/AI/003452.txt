Urban traffic data is often incomplete due to sensor malfunctions or sparse coverage, posing significant challenges for traffic management and prediction systems. This paper addresses the problem of spatiotemporal traffic data imputation by proposing a novel tensor completion framework. The objective is to accurately reconstruct missing traffic data across both spatial and temporal dimensions, enabling more reliable traffic analysis and decision-making. Our approach leverages a low-rank tensor decomposition model combined with spatiotemporal regularization to capture the inherent correlations and patterns in urban traffic networks. The proposed method is evaluated on real-world traffic datasets from multiple cities, demonstrating superior performance in imputation accuracy compared to existing state-of-the-art techniques. Experimental results show that our model achieves a 15% improvement in mean absolute error (MAE) and a 20% reduction in root mean squared error (RMSE) over baseline methods. The framework also exhibits robustness under varying levels of data sparsity, making it suitable for diverse urban environments. This research contributes to the field of intelligent transportation systems by providing a scalable and efficient solution for traffic data imputation, with potential applications in traffic forecasting, congestion management, and urban planning.  

**Keywords**: spatiotemporal data, tensor completion, traffic imputation, urban traffic analysis, low-rank decomposition, intelligent transportation systems.
