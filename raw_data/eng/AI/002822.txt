This paper introduces a novel few-shot learning approach for recognizing characters in historical ciphered manuscripts, addressing the challenge of limited labeled data. Our method leverages a Siamese neural network architecture trained on a small set of known cipher characters to effectively classify unseen cipher symbols. We augment the training data with synthetic transformations to improve robustness to variations in handwriting and image quality. The proposed approach is evaluated on a newly curated dataset of ciphered manuscripts, demonstrating significant improvements in character recognition accuracy compared to traditional machine learning techniques and existing few-shot learning algorithms. Results show that our method achieves high accuracy with as few as five labeled examples per character, enabling the decipherment of previously unreadable historical texts. This work contributes a practical and efficient solution for historical document analysis and cryptology, paving the way for automated decipherment of complex historical codes.

Keywords: Few-shot learning, historical manuscripts, cipher recognition, Siamese networks, data augmentation, cryptology.
