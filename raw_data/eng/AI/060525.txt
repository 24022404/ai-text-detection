Domain Randomization (DR) is a popular technique for training robust models by exposing them to a wide variety of simulated environments. However, manually designing effective randomization distributions can be challenging and sub-optimal. This paper introduces Adversarial Domain Randomization (ADR), a novel approach that automatically learns an optimal randomization strategy through an adversarial game. We employ two networks: a generator that proposes randomization parameters and a discriminator that attempts to distinguish between real and synthetic images produced with those parameters. By iteratively optimizing these networks, ADR identifies randomization distributions that maximize the realism and diversity of synthetic data, thereby improving the transferability of models trained in simulation to the real world. Experiments on several benchmark datasets for object detection and image classification demonstrate that ADR significantly outperforms traditional DR methods and achieves state-of-the-art results in sim-to-real transfer learning. The proposed approach offers a more automated and effective means of leveraging simulation for real-world applications. Keywords: Domain Randomization, Adversarial Training, Sim-to-Real Transfer, Deep Learning, Computer Vision.
