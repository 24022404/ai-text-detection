This paper addresses the challenge of estimating light direction and color from a single image, a critical task in computer vision with applications in augmented reality, 3D reconstruction, and image editing. Traditional methods often rely on complex setups or multiple images, limiting their practicality. To overcome these limitations, we propose a deep regression-based approach that leverages convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to directly predict light direction and color from a single input image. Our method employs a carefully designed network architecture trained on a diverse dataset of synthetic and real-world images, ensuring robustness across varying lighting conditions and scene complexities. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The proposed model also generalizes well to unseen data, making it suitable for real-world applications. This work contributes a novel, end-to-end solution that simplifies light estimation while maintaining high precision, paving the way for more efficient and accessible tools in computer vision and graphics.  

**Keywords**: light estimation, deep regression, single-image analysis, convolutional neural networks, computer vision, augmented reality.
