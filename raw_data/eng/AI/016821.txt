This paper addresses the challenge of accurate causal discovery in complex systems by introducing a novel framework for collaborative causal discovery utilizing atomic interventions. The main objective is to enhance the precision and efficiency of causal structure identification by leveraging the cooperation of multiple agents, each capable of performing targeted, atomic interventions on specific variables within a system. Our proposed approach integrates distributed decision-making algorithms with a mechanism for coordinating interventions, ensuring that redundant or conflicting actions are minimized. Through extensive simulations and evaluations on both synthetic and real-world datasets, we demonstrate significant improvements in causal graph recovery accuracy, reduced intervention costs, and faster convergence compared to conventional single-agent and observational-only methods. The findings reveal that collaborative strategies accelerate the discovery process and enable scalable application to high-dimensional data. This research contributes a robust methodological framework for collaborative causal inference and opens new avenues for real-time experimentation in distributed scientific, industrial, and AI-driven environments. Key keywords: causal discovery, atomic interventions, collaborative learning, causal inference, multi-agent systems, active learning.
