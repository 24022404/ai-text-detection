Seismic inverse modeling is a critical process in geophysical exploration, aiming to reconstruct subsurface structures from seismic data. Traditional methods often face challenges in handling complex geological features and noise, leading to limited accuracy and computational inefficiency. This paper proposes a novel seismic inverse modeling method based on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), leveraging the power of deep learning to address these limitations. The proposed approach employs a GAN-based framework, where a generator network learns to produce high-resolution subsurface models from seismic data, while a discriminator network evaluates the realism of the generated models. The method is trained on a diverse dataset of synthetic and real-world seismic profiles, enabling robust generalization across various geological scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate that the GAN-based approach significantly outperforms conventional methods in terms of reconstruction accuracy, noise resilience, and computational efficiency. The proposed method achieves a 20% improvement in structural similarity metrics and reduces processing time by 40% compared to state-of-the-art techniques. This research contributes to advancing seismic data interpretation by introducing a data-driven, AI-powered solution that enhances the fidelity and efficiency of subsurface imaging. The findings have broad implications for applications in oil and gas exploration, earthquake monitoring, and environmental studies.  

**Keywords**: Seismic inverse modeling, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), deep learning, subsurface imaging, geophysical exploration.
