This paper addresses the challenge of accurately classifying Local Climate Zones (LCZs) in Korea through deep learning techniques. Identifying high-quality training samples is crucial for improving the performance and accuracy of LCZ classification models, which play a significant role in urban planning, environmental monitoring, and climate research.

Methods/Approach: Our approach involves the development of a robust training dataset tailored specifically for the Korean geographic and climatic context. We utilized advanced deep learning architectures and satellite imagery to identify and classify LCZs. The process includes rigorous sample selection criteria to ensure that the training dataset is diverse and representative. Data augmentation techniques were applied to enhance model generalization, and various preprocessing steps were optimized for different deep learning models.

Results/Findings: The results from our experiments indicate a significant improvement in LCZ classification accuracy compared to existing datasets and methods. The tailored dataset and model pipeline achieved higher consistency and precision in classification tasks across various Korean regions. This positive outcome highlights the usefulness of our approach in handling regional climatic variations and urban morphology intricacies.

Conclusion/Implications: Our research presents an effective methodological framework for creating high-quality training samples that enhance the capability of deep learning models in LCZ classification. This study contributes to the field by presenting a model that is adaptable and significantly improves performance over previous methods. The implications of this work extend to urban planning and environmental management practices, where accurate LCZ data can inform better climate adaptation and mitigation strategies. Key Keywords: Local Climate Zone, Deep Learning, Training Dataset, Urban Planning, Environmental Monitoring, Korea.
