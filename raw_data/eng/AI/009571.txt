This paper introduces an Unsupervised Environment Design (UED) framework to address the challenge of achieving robust generalization in reinforcement learning without manually engineered curricula. Our approach couples an adversarial generator that proposes diverse task environments with a learning agent that continuously adapts its policy to meet rising levels of difficulty. Over training, this co-evolutionary process yields emergent complexity: agents develop sophisticated strategies—such as navigation heuristics and tool use—without explicit supervision. We evaluate UED on procedurally generated maze navigation and robotic manipulation benchmarks, demonstrating up to 45% improvement in success rate on held-out tasks compared to standard reinforcement learning and hand-tuned curriculum baselines. Notably, agents trained under UED achieve strong zero-shot transfer to entirely novel environments, reducing adaptation time by 60%. These findings show that unsupervised environment optimization can drive open-ended skill acquisition and immediate generalization. Our contributions include (1) a scalable UED algorithm that balances environment diversity and agent competence, (2) empirical evidence of spontaneously emerging task complexity, and (3) strong zero-shot transfer performance across multiple domains. This work paves the way toward autonomous, self-directed learning systems capable of tackling unseen challenges without human intervention.  
Keywords: Unsupervised Environment Design, Emergent Complexity, Zero-shot Transfer, Reinforcement Learning, Curriculum Learning
