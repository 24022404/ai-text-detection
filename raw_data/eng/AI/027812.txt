This paper presents an unsupervised multi-modal representation learning approach for affective computing, leveraging multi-corpus wearable data to improve emotion recognition and analysis. The objective is to develop a robust and generalizable model that can effectively capture complex emotional patterns from diverse physiological and contextual signals. Our approach employs a novel fusion of deep learning architectures and dimensionality reduction techniques to learn compact and informative representations from multiple wearable datasets. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our method, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines in emotion classification and regression tasks. The proposed framework enables the discovery of latent emotional structures and relationships between different modalities, contributing to a deeper understanding of human affective experiences. This research has significant implications for affective computing, human-computer interaction, and wearable technology applications, highlighting the potential of unsupervised multi-modal learning for enhancing emotional intelligence and well-being. Key contributions include the development of a scalable and adaptable framework for multi-corpus wearable data integration, and the introduction of a novel evaluation protocol for assessing representation learning quality in affective computing contexts. Relevant keywords: affective computing, multi-modal representation learning, wearable technology, unsupervised learning, emotion recognition, human-computer interaction.
