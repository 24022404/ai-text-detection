This paper addresses the challenge of inefficiency in training dense neural networks by exploring a method that promotes model sparsity without sacrificing performance. The central problem tackled is the need for a more efficient training approach that maintains high accuracy and speed.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel technique called Boosted Pruning Plasticity (BPP), which leverages the dynamic re-growth of network connections, termed neuroregeneration, during the training process. BPP enhances the plasticity of the network by selectively pruning redundant weights while concurrently regenerating essential connections as dictated by the evolving network architecture. This method combines the advantages of conventional pruning methods with the adaptability of neuroregeneration to maintain robustness and efficiency.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that the BPP method achieves superior sparsity levels and accuracy compared to traditional dense network training and existing pruning techniques. The training process using BPP showed accelerated convergence times and reduced computational resources while avoiding significant loss in accuracy. The comparative analysis underscores the efficiency of BPP, particularly in large-scale deep learning models where conventional methods struggle with computational demands.

Conclusion/Implications: The research presents important contributions towards more sustainable AI by reducing resource consumption in training neural networks. The proposed BPP method offers a promising solution for efficiently scaling deep learning models without degrading performance, making it applicable to a wide range of AI-driven technologies and applications. Enhanced with neuroregeneration, this approach not only promotes sparsity but also extends the adaptability and lifespan of AI models.

Keywords: Sparse Training, Boosted Pruning Plasticity, Neuroregeneration, Neural Networks, Efficiency, Deep Learning, Model Sparsity.
