This paper presents CLOCs, a novel approach to 3D object detection that leverages the fusion of camera and LiDAR data to generate high-quality object candidates. The objective is to improve the accuracy and robustness of 3D object detection in various environments. Our method employs a deep learning-based framework that combines the complementary strengths of camera and LiDAR sensors to produce a unified representation of object candidates. The approach utilizes a transformer-based architecture to fuse the multimodal data, enabling the model to capture complex relationships between visual and depth features. Experimental results demonstrate that CLOCs outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency, particularly in scenarios with limited lighting or occlusions. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a camera-LiDAR fusion module and a novel loss function that encourages the model to focus on object boundaries and shapes. The proposed method has significant implications for applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and surveillance, where accurate 3D object detection is crucial. Keywords: 3D object detection, camera-LiDAR fusion, transformer, deep learning, multimodal sensing.
