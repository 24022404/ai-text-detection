Image segmentation, a fundamental task in computer vision, remains challenging in scenarios with complex scenes and subtle intensity variations. This paper introduces Dam Burst, a novel region-merging-based image segmentation method designed to address these challenges. Dam Burst leverages an adaptive merging criterion based on local gradient information, mimicking the way water breaches a dam at its weakest point, to guide the segmentation process. Regions are iteratively merged based on the lowest gradient difference along their shared boundary, promoting accurate segmentation while preserving fine details. The method incorporates a dynamic thresholding mechanism that adjusts merging sensitivity according to the homogeneity of neighboring regions, preventing over-segmentation. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that Dam Burst achieves superior segmentation accuracy compared to traditional region-merging techniques and state-of-the-art algorithms, particularly in images with high noise and complex textures. The proposed method offers a robust and efficient approach to image segmentation with applications in medical imaging, object recognition, and autonomous navigation.
Keywords: Image segmentation, region merging, computer vision, gradient-based segmentation, adaptive thresholding.
