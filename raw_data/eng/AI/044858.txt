Reconstructing accurate 3D face shapes from diverse data sources is a fundamental challenge in computer vision, with significant implications for biometrics, animation, and virtual reality applications. This paper introduces "3D Face From X," a novel deep learning framework designed to infer detailed 3D facial geometry from heterogeneous input modalities, including single RGB images, depth maps, and multi-view photographs. Our approach leverages a unified neural architecture that efficiently integrates and adapts to various data types, enabling robust 3D reconstruction even under unconstrained or sparse input scenarios. Through extensive experiments on publicly available benchmarks, we demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques across multiple input conditions, achieving higher geometric fidelity and improved generalization to unseen modalities. The results highlight the framework’s versatility and potential in real-world scenarios where input sources are varied or unpredictable. In conclusion, "3D Face From X" offers an effective and flexible solution for 3D face reconstruction, advancing the field toward more practical and inclusive applications. Keywords: 3D face reconstruction, deep learning, multi-modal input, computer vision, shape estimation, neural networks.
