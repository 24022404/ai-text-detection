Extreme weather events pose significant threats to global infrastructure and human populations. Accurate detection and understanding of these events are crucial for timely response and mitigation efforts. This paper introduces ExtremeWeather, a novel large-scale climate dataset designed to facilitate research in semi-supervised detection, localization, and comprehensive understanding of extreme weather phenomena. The dataset comprises multi-source climate data, including satellite imagery, radar data, and meteorological measurements, annotated with bounding boxes and descriptive labels for various extreme weather events. We leverage a semi-supervised learning framework, combining labeled and unlabeled data, to train robust detection models. Experimental results demonstrate that our models achieve state-of-the-art performance in identifying and localizing extreme weather events, surpassing existing approaches. Furthermore, we explore the use of the dataset for understanding the complex relationships between different climate variables and the occurrence of extreme weather. The dataset and associated models contribute to advancing climate research and improving the ability to predict and respond to extreme weather events. Keywords: climate dataset, extreme weather, semi-supervised learning, object detection, climate change.
