This paper investigates the phenomenon of benign overfitting in ridge function estimation, focusing on finite sample analysis. We analyze the performance of ridge regression in high-dimensional settings where the number of parameters exceeds the sample size, leading to seemingly contradictory behavior where a model that perfectly fits the training data still generalizes well. Our approach utilizes spectral analysis and concentration inequalities to derive finite sample error bounds that explicitly characterize the trade-off between the regularization parameter, the sample size, and the dimensionality of the feature space. The results demonstrate that benign overfitting can occur under specific conditions related to the eigenvalue decay of the covariance matrix and the smoothness of the underlying ridge function. Furthermore, we provide insights into the optimal choice of the regularization parameter to minimize the generalization error in the overparameterized regime. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the generalization capabilities of ridge regression in modern machine learning applications and offer practical guidelines for parameter tuning.

Keywords: Benign Overfitting, Ridge Regression, Finite Sample Analysis, High-Dimensional Statistics, Generalization Error.
