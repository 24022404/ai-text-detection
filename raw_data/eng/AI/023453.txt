Semantic segmentation models are vulnerable to adversarial attacks and domain shifts, limiting their deployment in real-world applications. This paper introduces a novel data augmentation technique inspired by the "painting-by-numbers" concept to enhance the robustness of these models. Our method decomposes images into simplified, segmented regions and then recombines these regions with variations in color palettes, textures, and styles. This process effectively expands the training data with diverse, yet semantically consistent, synthetic samples. We evaluated our approach on benchmark datasets like Cityscapes and ADE20K, demonstrating significant improvements in segmentation accuracy under various adversarial attacks and cross-domain scenarios. Specifically, our models exhibited a 5-10% increase in mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) compared to baseline models and other augmentation strategies when faced with unseen noise and style variations. The proposed technique is simple to implement, computationally efficient, and offers a practical solution for improving the reliability of semantic segmentation models in challenging environments. This work contributes a robust training strategy for semantic segmentation, bridging the gap between theoretical model performance and real-world applicability. Keywords: Semantic Segmentation, Robustness, Data Augmentation, Adversarial Attacks, Domain Adaptation, Painting-by-Numbers.
