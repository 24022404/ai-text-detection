Cervical cancer diagnosis using whole-slide images (WSIs) is a challenging task due to the high resolution and complex tissue structures. This paper introduces a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based approach for automated cervical cancer classification in WSIs. We propose a novel pipeline involving WSI preprocessing, patch extraction, and a deep CNN model for feature learning and classification. Specifically, we employ a multi-resolution patch extraction strategy to capture both local and global contextual information. The CNN architecture is fine-tuned using a large dataset of WSIs with corresponding diagnostic labels. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in cervical cancer classification, surpassing existing methods in terms of accuracy and F1-score. The proposed method offers a promising tool for assisting pathologists in accurate and efficient cervical cancer diagnosis, potentially improving patient outcomes. Key words: Cervical Cancer, Whole-Slide Images, Convolutional Neural Network, Histopathology, Deep Learning, Classification.
