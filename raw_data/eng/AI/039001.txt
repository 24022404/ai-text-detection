Understanding the functional connectivity of the human brain using resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) is a critical challenge in neuroscience and computational biology. This study explores the application of Graph Isomorphism Networks (GINs) to analyze rs-fMRI data, leveraging their ability to capture structural similarities in graph-based representations of brain connectivity. The objective is to improve the classification and interpretation of functional brain networks by addressing the limitations of traditional graph neural networks (GNNs) in distinguishing non-isomorphic graphs. We propose a novel framework that integrates GINs with rs-fMRI data preprocessing techniques to model brain connectivity as a graph, where nodes represent brain regions and edges denote functional correlations. Our approach is evaluated on publicly available rs-fMRI datasets, demonstrating superior performance in classifying brain states and identifying connectivity patterns compared to conventional GNNs and other baseline methods. The results highlight the effectiveness of GINs in capturing subtle topological differences in brain networks, offering new insights into the underlying mechanisms of brain function. This research contributes to the advancement of graph-based machine learning techniques in neuroimaging, with potential applications in diagnosing neurological disorders and understanding brain dynamics.  

**Keywords**: Graph Isomorphism Network, rs-fMRI, functional connectivity, graph neural networks, brain network analysis, neuroimaging.
