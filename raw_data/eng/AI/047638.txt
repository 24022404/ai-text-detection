Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for decision-making in complex environments, with applications ranging from robotics to game playing. However, the lack of standardized evaluation methodologies poses challenges in comparing and advancing RL algorithms. This paper presents a comprehensive survey of benchmarking frameworks designed for RL, aiming to address the need for consistent and reproducible evaluation practices. We analyze the key components of these frameworks, including environment design, performance metrics, and scalability considerations. Additionally, we compare the strengths and limitations of existing frameworks, highlighting their suitability for different RL tasks and domains. Our findings reveal significant gaps in current benchmarking practices, such as the underrepresentation of real-world scenarios and the need for more robust evaluation metrics. The survey concludes with recommendations for future research directions, emphasizing the development of unified benchmarking standards to accelerate progress in RL. This work contributes to the RL community by providing a structured overview of existing frameworks and offering insights into improving evaluation methodologies.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Benchmarking Frameworks, Evaluation Metrics, Reproducibility, Scalability, Performance Analysis.
