Zero-shot learning (ZSL) aims to classify instances from classes that have not been observed during the training phase, relying on auxiliary information, such as class descriptions, to bridge the gap between seen and unseen categories. This paper addresses the limitations of existing ZSL approaches that often fail to effectively incorporate class descriptions, leading to suboptimal performance.

Methods: We introduce a novel framework for zero-shot learning, termed Class Description Regularization (CDR). This approach integrates class semantic vectors and leverages a strategic regularization technique to enhance the alignment between visual and semantic spaces. By embedding class descriptions directly into the learning process, our method effectively mitigates the semantic gap and improves generalization to unseen classes.

Results: Our empirical evaluations demonstrate that the proposed CDR method achieves superior performance on multiple benchmark datasets, including Caltech-UCSD Birds, Animals with Attributes, and SUN Attribute. We observe significant improvements over state-of-the-art techniques, with notable gains in accuracy and robustness across varied zero-shot learning scenarios. Moreover, our analysis shows that CDR not only improves model precision but also enhances interpretability by aligning visual attributes more closely with semantic labels.

Conclusion: The introduction of Class Description Regularization represents a significant advancement in zero-shot learning, offering a robust mechanism to harness the potential of class descriptions. This research provides novel insights into bridging seen and unseen domains, contributing to the broader field of machine learning and pattern recognition. Future work may extend this framework to other related tasks, such as few-shot learning and domain adaptation.

Keywords: Zero-shot learning, Class Description Regularization, semantic gap, unseen classes, attribute alignment, machine learning, pattern recognition.
