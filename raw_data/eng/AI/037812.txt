Camouflaged object detection (COD) remains a challenging task due to the strong similarity between target and background textures and the low contrast of concealed regions. This work introduces a novel deep learning framework, called DTAF-Net, that explicitly models texture-aware features to improve COD performance. First, we propose a Texture Attention Module (TAM) that adaptively emphasizes subtle textural cues by integrating multi-scale wavelet-based texture maps with backbone feature maps. Second, a bi-directional feature fusion strategy is employed to combine coarse semantic information and fine-grained texture details across different resolution levels. Third, a refinement head guided by high-response texture regions further suppresses background noise and sharpens object boundaries.  
We evaluate DTAF-Net on three widely used COD benchmarks—CAMO, CHAMELEON, and COD10K—demonstrating consistent gains over state-of-the-art methods. Quantitatively, our approach achieves up to 4.2% improvement in F-measure and a 15% reduction in mean absolute error (MAE). Qualitative results indicate more accurate delineation of deeply concealed objects in complex scenes such as foliage, urban rubble, and animal camouflage.  
The integration of dedicated texture modeling into a deep COD network delivers significant advantages in detecting low-contrast, high-confusability targets. This texture-aware design can be extended to related tasks such as medical anomaly detection and remote-sensing analysis.  
Keywords: Camouflaged Object Detection, Texture Attention Module, Deep Learning, Multi-Scale Feature Fusion, Wavelet Transform.
