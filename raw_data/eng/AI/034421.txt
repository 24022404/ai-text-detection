Distribution inference risks have emerged as a critical concern in machine learning, particularly in scenarios where models trained on sensitive data inadvertently reveal information about the underlying data distribution. This paper formalizes the concept of distribution inference risks, providing a rigorous framework to quantify and analyze the extent to which machine learning models expose properties of their training data distributions. We propose a novel methodology that leverages statistical techniques and adversarial learning to estimate these risks systematically. Our approach evaluates the vulnerability of various machine learning models under different training conditions and data distributions. Experimental results demonstrate that distribution inference risks vary significantly across model architectures and training regimes, with certain models exhibiting higher susceptibility to such attacks. The findings highlight the importance of incorporating distribution inference risk assessments into the design and deployment of machine learning systems, particularly in privacy-sensitive applications. This work contributes to the broader field of trustworthy AI by providing tools and insights to mitigate unintended information leakage, thereby enhancing the security and privacy of machine learning models.  

**Keywords**: distribution inference, privacy risks, machine learning, adversarial learning, data leakage, trustworthy AI.
