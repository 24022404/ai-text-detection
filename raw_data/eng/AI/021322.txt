Snow accumulation on images poses a significant challenge for computer vision tasks in outdoor environments, often degrading scene visibility and affecting the performance of downstream algorithms. This paper presents DesnowNet, a context-aware deep learning network specifically designed to address the problem of snow removal from images. The proposed model leverages a multi-scale convolutional architecture that incorporates contextual information, enabling effective identification and removal of snow artifacts across varying sizes and densities. By integrating attention mechanisms and residual connections, DesnowNet adapts to the spatial distribution of snow and preserves underlying image details. Extensive experiments conducted on benchmark and real-world snow-covered datasets demonstrate that DesnowNet consistently outperforms existing methods in both qualitative and quantitative assessments, achieving superior restoration of scene clarity and structural integrity. The findings highlight DesnowNet’s robustness and efficiency in real-time image enhancement applications, offering substantial improvements for tasks such as autonomous driving, surveillance, and outdoor photography. The key contributions of this work include a tailored architecture for context-aware snow detection, improved restoration quality, and generalizability across diverse environmental conditions. Keywords: image restoration, snow removal, deep learning, context-aware networks, attention mechanism, computer vision.
