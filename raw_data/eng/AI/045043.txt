This paper explores a novel approach to image representation and processing by adapting the transformer architecture, traditionally used in natural language processing, to the domain of computer vision. We introduce a visual transformer model that treats images as sequences of visual tokens, enabling the application of self-attention mechanisms to capture global dependencies and contextual relationships within the image. Our method involves partitioning images into patches, which are then embedded into a high-dimensional space to serve as input tokens for the transformer. We demonstrate the efficacy of this token-based image representation across various computer vision tasks, including image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation. Experimental results on benchmark datasets show that our visual transformer achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art convolutional neural networks, while offering advantages in terms of capturing long-range dependencies and model scalability. Key contributions include a novel image tokenization strategy, an efficient transformer architecture tailored for visual data, and comprehensive evaluations demonstrating its effectiveness in various computer vision applications. Keywords: Visual Transformer, Image Tokenization, Self-Attention, Computer Vision, Deep Learning.
