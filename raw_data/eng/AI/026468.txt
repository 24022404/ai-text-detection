This paper presents a novel approach to multi-instance pose estimation, leveraging the capabilities of transformers to develop an end-to-end trainable model. The objective is to accurately estimate the poses of multiple instances of objects within an image, addressing the challenges of instance overlap and occlusion. Our method employs a transformer-based architecture that integrates both detection and pose estimation tasks, enabling the model to learn instance-specific features and spatial relationships. The results demonstrate significant improvements in pose estimation accuracy and efficiency, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods. Key findings include the ability to handle complex scenes with multiple instances and the model's robustness to varying levels of occlusion. The proposed approach contributes to the field of computer vision, offering a powerful tool for applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and surveillance. By highlighting the effectiveness of transformers in multi-instance pose estimation, this research paves the way for further innovations in AI-powered vision systems, with relevant keywords including transformer, pose estimation, multi-instance detection, and computer vision.
