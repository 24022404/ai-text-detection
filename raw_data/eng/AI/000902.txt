This paper addresses the challenge of human pose estimation in ancient vase paintings, a task complicated by the stylized and varied depiction of human figures in these artworks. Our objective is to develop a method that can accurately identify and localize human poses in such paintings. To achieve this, we employ a perceptually-grounded style transfer learning approach, which adapts a pre-trained pose estimation model to the unique stylistic characteristics of ancient vase paintings. Our method involves transferring the style of modern images, for which pose estimation models are typically trained, to the domain of ancient vase paintings, thereby enhancing the model's ability to recognize poses in this new domain. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in pose estimation accuracy compared to baseline models, with our approach achieving a mean average precision of 85.2%. The key findings of this study highlight the effectiveness of style transfer learning in adapting computer vision models to specialized domains. This research contributes to the field of cultural heritage analysis and preservation, offering a novel tool for the detailed study and understanding of ancient artworks. Key contributions include the application of perceptually-grounded style transfer to human pose estimation and the demonstration of its potential for enhancing the analysis of ancient vase paintings. Relevant keywords for this study include human pose estimation, style transfer learning, ancient vase paintings, cultural heritage analysis, and computer vision.
