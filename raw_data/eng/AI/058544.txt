This paper addresses the challenge of achieving reliable performance in continuous control tasks where the underlying model of the environment is uncertain or inaccurately specified, a common scenario in real-world applications. We propose a robust reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to accommodate model misspecification by integrating uncertainty estimation and adaptive policy optimization. Our approach leverages distributional RL techniques coupled with adversarial domain randomization to expose the agent to a diverse set of simulated dynamics during training, enabling the learned policy to generalize beyond initially assumed models. Experimental evaluation across a suite of continuous control benchmarks demonstrates that our method consistently outperforms baseline RL algorithms when subjected to various forms of model inaccuracy, such as changes in physical parameters or unexpected disturbances. Notably, our robust RL agent maintains high stability and performance without requiring explicit knowledge of the true environment during training. These results underscore the framework’s potential for real-world deployments in robotics and autonomous systems, where model uncertainty is inevitable. Key contributions include a unified formulation for robust policy learning under model misspecification, a principled training methodology, and comprehensive empirical analysis. Keywords: reinforcement learning, continuous control, model misspecification, robustness, domain randomization, policy optimization.
