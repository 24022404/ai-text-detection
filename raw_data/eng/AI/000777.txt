This paper addresses the challenge of gaze estimation in realistic environments, where individuals are free to move and interact naturally. Current methods often depend on controlled conditions, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios. Our research introduces Gaze360, a novel approach to estimating gaze direction under unrestricted, "in-the-wild" conditions.

Methods/Approach: We present a deep learning model that leverages a diverse dataset collected from dynamic and uncontrolled settings, capturing a wide range of head poses and gaze directions. The model utilizes a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture that is designed to handle the variability and complexity inherent in natural environments, while maintaining high precision and computational efficiency.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that Gaze360 achieves state-of-the-art accuracy in gaze estimation across varied and unconstrained scenarios. Comparative analysis with existing methods shows significant performance improvements, especially in challenging conditions with occlusions, extreme angles, and low-resolution imagery. This is facilitated by the model's robustness to diverse lighting and positional variations.

Conclusion/Implications: Gaze360 represents a significant advancement in gaze tracking technology, expanding its applicability to a range of practical applications, from virtual reality and augmented reality systems to advanced human-computer interaction interfaces. The flexibility and accuracy of this approach open new avenues for research and development in environments where physical constraints previously hindered innovation. Key keywords include gaze estimation, deep learning, convolutional neural network, in-the-wild conditions, and human-computer interaction.
