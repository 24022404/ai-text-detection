Ocean front detection is a critical task in marine science, with applications in climate monitoring, fisheries management, and navigation. However, existing methods often struggle to account for the spatial and temporal variability of ocean fronts, particularly across different seasons and geographic regions. This paper introduces LSENet, a novel Location and Seasonality Enhanced Network designed to improve multi-class ocean front detection by incorporating geographic location and seasonal patterns into the detection process. LSENet leverages a hybrid deep learning architecture that integrates convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with spatial-temporal attention mechanisms to capture the dynamic nature of ocean fronts. The model is trained on a comprehensive dataset of satellite imagery and oceanographic data, annotated with front locations across multiple seasons. Experimental results demonstrate that LSENet outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of detection accuracy and robustness, achieving a 12% improvement in F1-score across diverse oceanic regions. The inclusion of location and seasonality features enables the model to adapt to varying environmental conditions, making it a versatile tool for real-world applications. This research contributes to advancing oceanographic studies by providing a more accurate and adaptable framework for ocean front detection, with potential implications for climate modeling and marine resource management.  

**Keywords**: Ocean front detection, deep learning, spatial-temporal modeling, attention mechanisms, satellite imagery, marine science.
