This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently training Legendre Memory Units (LMUs), a class of recurrent neural networks known for their ability to model long-term dependencies in sequential data. While LMUs have shown promise in applications such as time-series forecasting and natural language processing, their training process is computationally intensive, limiting scalability. To overcome this, we propose a novel parallelization strategy that leverages distributed computing frameworks to accelerate LMU training. Our approach decomposes the LMU's memory and computation components, enabling concurrent processing across multiple GPUs or nodes. We evaluate the proposed method on benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant reductions in training time without compromising model accuracy. Comparative analysis with existing sequential training methods highlights the scalability and efficiency of our approach, particularly for large-scale datasets. The results suggest that parallelized LMU training can unlock new possibilities for real-time and high-throughput applications. This work contributes to the broader field of neural network optimization by providing a scalable solution for training memory-augmented models, with potential implications for advancing AI systems in domains requiring long-term sequential reasoning.  

**Keywords**: Legendre Memory Unit, parallel computing, recurrent neural networks, distributed training, time-series modeling, scalability.
