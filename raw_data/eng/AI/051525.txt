This paper addresses the critical need for improving the interpretability and explainability of classification networks, particularly focusing on enhancing the Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) technique. The primary goal is to develop a light-weight method that provides clearer insights into the decision-making processes of deep learning models without imposing significant computational overhead.

Methods/Approach: We propose an innovative approach that leverages a refined version of the Grad-CAM, integrating a novel weighting scheme to more accurately highlight the regions of input data that influence model predictions. Our method involves optimizing the activation maps through a streamlined process, ensuring minimal interference with the model's performance while maximizing interpretative clarity.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our method significantly improves the visualization quality of Grad-CAM by producing more focused and meaningful activation maps, thereby aiding in the identification of critical input regions. Comparative analysis shows a marked improvement over traditional Grad-CAM outputs in both visual clarity and computational efficiency across various benchmark classification models.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed method enhances the interpretability and explainability of classification networks, offering an effective tool for researchers and practitioners to understand and trust AI-driven decisions. The lightweight nature of our approach ensures broad applicability to different models without requiring extensive modifications. This advancement has potential implications for fields where model transparency is crucial, such as healthcare and autonomous systems.

Keywords: Grad-CAM, interpretability, explainability, classification networks, deep learning, activation maps, model transparency.
