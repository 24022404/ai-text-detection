This paper investigates the role of geometric information in multiclass classification tasks and its implications for data-driven intelligence. We propose a novel framework that analyzes both coarse-grained and fine-grained geometric properties inherent within the data distribution to enhance classification accuracy and robustness. Our method leverages topological data analysis (TDA) for coarse-scale structure extraction and incorporates manifold learning techniques for capturing fine-scale geometric details. This multi-scale approach allows for a more comprehensive representation of the underlying data geometry compared to traditional methods. Experimental results on several benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves superior classification performance, particularly in scenarios with high data dimensionality and complex class boundaries. Furthermore, we show how the extracted geometric features can be used to interpret and understand the decision-making process of the classifier, providing insights into the data-driven intelligence embedded within the model. Key findings include the identification of persistent homology features that significantly correlate with classification accuracy and the demonstration that fine-grained manifold structures are crucial for distinguishing between closely related classes. The implications of this research extend to various applications, including image recognition, medical diagnosis, and anomaly detection, where geometric understanding of data is paramount. Keywords: Multiclass Classification, Geometric Information, Topological Data Analysis, Manifold Learning, Data-Driven Intelligence.
