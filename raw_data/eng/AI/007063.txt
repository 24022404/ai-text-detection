Medical image segmentation is a critical task in healthcare, enabling precise diagnosis and treatment planning. However, designing effective Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architectures for this purpose often requires extensive expertise and manual tuning. This paper addresses the challenge by proposing an automated approach to design CNN architectures tailored for medical image segmentation. Our method leverages a neural architecture search (NAS) framework, which explores a diverse search space of convolutional operations, skip connections, and upsampling strategies to optimize segmentation performance. The proposed approach is evaluated on multiple medical imaging datasets, including MRI and CT scans, and demonstrates superior accuracy compared to manually designed architectures and existing NAS-based methods. Key findings reveal that the automatically generated architectures achieve higher Dice scores and improved boundary delineation, particularly in complex anatomical structures. The results highlight the potential of automated CNN design to reduce human effort while enhancing segmentation quality. This research contributes to the field by introducing a scalable and efficient NAS framework for medical imaging, paving the way for broader applications in clinical settings.  

**Keywords**: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Medical Image Segmentation, Neural Architecture Search (NAS), Automated Design, MRI, CT Scans.
