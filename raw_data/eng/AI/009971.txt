This paper introduces COMISR, a novel video super-resolution approach that leverages compression artifacts to enhance video quality. The objective is to develop an efficient method for upsampling compressed videos, addressing the limitations of existing super-resolution techniques that often neglect the impact of compression on video content. COMISR employs a deep learning-based framework that incorporates compression-informed features, allowing the model to adapt to various compression standards and bitrates. Experimental results demonstrate that COMISR outperforms state-of-the-art video super-resolution methods, achieving significant improvements in peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM). The proposed approach has important implications for video streaming and storage applications, enabling high-quality video reconstruction while reducing the need for excessive bandwidth and storage resources. Key contributions of this research include the development of a compression-aware video super-resolution model, the introduction of a novel compression-informed feature extraction module, and the demonstration of the approach's effectiveness in real-world video enhancement scenarios. Relevant keywords: video super-resolution, compression artifacts, deep learning, video enhancement, PSNR, SSIM.
