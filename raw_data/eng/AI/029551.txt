Super-resolution (SR) networks have shown remarkable performance in reconstructing high-resolution (HR) images from low-resolution (LR) inputs. However, the internal mechanisms driving these networks remain largely opaque, hindering trust and interpretability. This paper introduces a novel approach for interpreting SR networks by generating local attribution maps. Our method leverages gradient-based techniques to identify which LR pixels most significantly influence the reconstruction of specific HR pixels. We propose a modified backpropagation strategy that accounts for the unique characteristics of SR architectures, such as skip connections and multi-scale processing. Through quantitative evaluation and visual analysis on benchmark datasets, we demonstrate that our attribution maps accurately highlight relevant LR features and reveal important network decision-making processes. Specifically, our approach identifies spatial correlations between LR and HR image patches, providing insights into how SR networks learn to recover fine details. Furthermore, we show how these attribution maps can be used to diagnose failure cases and identify potential biases within the SR model. This work contributes a valuable tool for understanding and improving the reliability of super-resolution networks. Keywords: Super-Resolution, Explainable AI, Interpretability, Attribution Maps, Deep Learning, Image Reconstruction.
