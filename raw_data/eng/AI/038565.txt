This paper investigates the performative risk associated with algorithmic decision-making systems, particularly in contexts where outputs are strategically manipulated to achieve specific outcomes rather than reflecting objective truth. We define performative risk as the potential for algorithms to incentivize behaviors that undermine the integrity of the underlying data and distort the system's intended function. Our approach involves developing a novel framework to model and quantify this risk, incorporating elements of game theory and causal inference. We analyze how different algorithmic architectures and incentive structures influence the manifestation of performative risk in simulated environments. Results demonstrate that naive optimization strategies can lead to significant degradation in system performance and unintended consequences, while carefully designed algorithms can mitigate these risks. The findings highlight the importance of considering performativity when deploying algorithms in complex socio-technical systems. This research contributes to the growing body of knowledge on algorithmic fairness and accountability, offering practical guidance for designing robust and trustworthy AI systems. Keywords: Algorithmic bias, Performative risk, Game theory, Causal inference, Algorithmic decision-making, Optimization, Incentive design.
