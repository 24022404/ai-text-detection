Manifold learning, a critical area in machine learning, aims to uncover low-dimensional structures embedded in high-dimensional data. This paper explores the potential of deep neural networks (DNNs) to serve as locality-sensitive hash (LSH) functions for manifold learning tasks. We propose a novel approach where DNNs are trained to map high-dimensional data points into a lower-dimensional space while preserving local geometric relationships. By leveraging the inherent nonlinearity and adaptability of DNNs, our method achieves superior locality sensitivity compared to traditional LSH techniques. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach significantly improves nearest-neighbor search accuracy and computational efficiency. Furthermore, we provide theoretical insights into why DNNs can effectively approximate LSH functions under specific conditions. The findings highlight the versatility of DNNs beyond conventional applications, offering a promising direction for scalable and efficient manifold learning. This work contributes to advancing both deep learning and manifold learning by bridging their theoretical and practical aspects, with potential applications in data compression, dimensionality reduction, and large-scale data retrieval.  

**Keywords**: manifold learning, deep neural networks, locality-sensitive hashing, dimensionality reduction, nearest-neighbor search.
