This paper introduces a novel approach to enhance the sample efficiency and stability of model-based reinforcement learning by leveraging model confidence. We propose Masked Model-based Actor-Critic (MMAC), a framework that selectively trusts the learned environment model based on its predicted uncertainty. MMAC employs a masking mechanism, informed by the model's confidence, to dynamically blend model-based and model-free learning. Regions where the model exhibits high confidence are utilized for efficient policy improvement via model-based rollouts, while regions with low confidence rely on model-free learning to ensure robustness. Our experiments on benchmark continuous control tasks demonstrate that MMAC significantly outperforms state-of-the-art model-based and model-free algorithms in terms of sample efficiency and asymptotic performance. Furthermore, we provide an ablation study to analyze the impact of different confidence measures and masking strategies. The results indicate that dynamically adapting the trust in the model based on its confidence leads to more stable and efficient learning, offering a promising direction for deploying reinforcement learning in complex and uncertain environments. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Model-Based Learning, Actor-Critic, Uncertainty, Confidence, Sample Efficiency.
