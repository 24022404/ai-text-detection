This paper introduces AAG, a novel self-supervised representation learning approach that leverages auxiliary augmentation techniques in conjunction with a customized GNT-Xent loss function. The objective is to enhance the quality and robustness of learned representations without relying on labeled data. AAG's methodology involves generating multiple augmented views of the input data and employing a contrastive learning framework to maximize the agreement between these views. The GNT-Xent loss function plays a crucial role in this process, as it facilitates the discovery of more discriminative and generalizable features. Experimental results demonstrate that AAG outperforms existing state-of-the-art self-supervised learning methods, achieving superior performance on various downstream tasks such as image classification and object detection. The key contributions of AAG lie in its ability to effectively harness auxiliary augmentation strategies and its innovative loss function design, which collectively contribute to more accurate and robust representation learning. This research has significant implications for the development of AI models, particularly in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or unavailable, highlighting the importance of self-supervised learning, contrastive learning, and representation learning in the broader context of machine learning and deep learning. Relevant keywords include self-supervised learning, representation learning, auxiliary augmentation, GNT-Xent loss, contrastive learning, and deep learning.
