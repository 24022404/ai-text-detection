This paper addresses the challenge of manual-label dependency in 3D object detection, a critical bottleneck in developing robust and scalable perception systems for autonomous vehicles and robotics. We propose a novel framework that leverages an open-source simulator to generate synthetic, high-fidelity 3D data, eliminating the need for costly and time-intensive manual labeling. Our approach integrates domain randomization techniques to bridge the sim-to-real gap, ensuring the synthetic data's applicability to real-world scenarios. We employ a state-of-the-art 3D detection model trained exclusively on simulator-generated data and evaluate its performance on benchmark datasets. Experimental results demonstrate competitive accuracy compared to models trained on manually labeled datasets, with significant reductions in annotation effort and cost. The proposed method not only advances the field of 3D detection but also provides a scalable, cost-effective solution for training perception systems. This work highlights the potential of simulation-driven approaches in reducing reliance on manual annotations, paving the way for more efficient and accessible 3D perception technologies.  

**Keywords**: 3D object detection, simulation, domain randomization, synthetic data, autonomous systems, sim-to-real transfer.
