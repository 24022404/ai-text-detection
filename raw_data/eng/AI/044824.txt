Electroencephalography (EEG) analysis plays a vital role in brain-computer interfaces and neurological research, yet real-world EEG data is frequently affected by noise and artifacts, which can significantly hinder learning and classification accuracy. This paper presents a novel framework for robust learning from corrupted EEG signals by leveraging dynamic spatial filtering techniques. Our method dynamically adapts spatial filters during model training to emphasize informative neural signals while attenuating noise and interference. Extensive experiments were conducted on multiple benchmark EEG datasets under varying levels and types of corruption, including simulated and real-world artifacts. Results demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms traditional static filtering and baseline learning methods, achieving higher classification accuracy and improved robustness to signal degradation. The dynamic spatial filtering framework also integrates seamlessly with deep learning architectures, enabling end-to-end learning and adaptability to diverse EEG tasks. These findings highlight the effectiveness of combining adaptive spatial filtering with robust machine learning for EEG analysis, offering significant advantages for clinical diagnosis, cognitive monitoring, and brain-computer interface applications. Keywords: EEG, robust learning, spatial filtering, noise reduction, brain-computer interfaces, deep learning, signal processing.
