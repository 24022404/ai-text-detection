This paper investigates the impact of increased dropout rates in the Pool5 feature maps of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on object detection performance. The objective is to improve the robustness and accuracy of object detection models by reducing overfitting in the feature extraction process. Our approach involves applying dropout techniques to the Pool5 layer, which is a critical component in CNN-based object detection architectures. The results show that higher dropout rates in Pool5 feature maps lead to significant improvements in object detection accuracy, particularly in scenarios with limited training data. Our findings suggest that this simple yet effective modification can enhance the generalizability of object detection models, making them more suitable for real-world applications. The proposed method contributes to the development of more robust and efficient object detection systems, with potential applications in areas such as autonomous vehicles, surveillance, and robotics. Key keywords: object detection, convolutional neural networks, dropout, Pool5 feature maps, computer vision.
