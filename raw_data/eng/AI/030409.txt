The aim of this research is to advance pedestrian detection in autonomous and assisted driving systems by developing a novel method, LRPD, which harnesses the unique strengths of LiDAR and RGB data for long-range 3D pedestrian detection. Traditional approaches often rely solely on either LiDAR or RGB sensors, limiting accuracy and effectiveness at extended distances. Our method integrates the high-precision spatial data provided by LiDAR with the rich contextual and color information available from RGB images. Utilizing a fusion architecture, our model effectively enhances object detection accuracy over long ranges. The approach employs a dual-path network, where LiDAR point clouds and RGB images are processed in parallel, followed by a fusion stage that combines features using an innovative attention mechanism to ensure robust feature representation. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets indicate that LRPD achieves superior results compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, showing significant improvements in detection precision and recall at distances over 100 meters. The insights derived from this study contribute to safer autonomous navigation by improving the detection of pedestrians, reducing the risk of accidents in complex driving environments. As a robust solution leveraging both LiDAR and RGB, LRPD paves the way for future advancements in 3D detection technologies within the automotive industry. Keywords: 3D pedestrian detection, LiDAR, RGB, long-range detection, sensor fusion, autonomous driving.
