The assessment of retinal image quality is critical for accurate diagnosis and analysis in ophthalmology. This study evaluates the performance of retinal image quality assessment networks across different color-spaces to determine the most effective representation for automated quality evaluation. We propose a comparative framework that leverages convolutional neural networks (CNNs) trained on retinal images transformed into RGB, grayscale, and alternative color-spaces such as HSV and LAB. The networks are trained and validated on a publicly available dataset of retinal images, with quality labels provided by expert ophthalmologists. Our results demonstrate that networks operating in the LAB color-space achieve superior performance, with higher accuracy and robustness in detecting artifacts, blur, and illumination issues compared to RGB and grayscale representations. Additionally, we analyze the computational efficiency of each color-space transformation, highlighting trade-offs between accuracy and processing time. The findings suggest that the choice of color-space significantly impacts the effectiveness of retinal image quality assessment systems, with LAB offering a promising balance between performance and interpretability. This research contributes to the development of more reliable automated tools for retinal image analysis, with potential applications in telemedicine and large-scale screening programs.  

**Keywords**: retinal image quality assessment, color-spaces, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), LAB color-space, ophthalmology, automated diagnosis.
