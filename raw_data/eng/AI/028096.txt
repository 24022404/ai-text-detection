This paper explores the application of adversarial deep learning techniques to enhance the security and robustness of EEG biometrics. The objective is to investigate the vulnerability of EEG-based authentication systems to adversarial attacks and develop novel defense strategies. We propose a deep learning framework that utilizes generative adversarial networks (GANs) to generate synthetic EEG signals, which are then used to train and evaluate the robustness of EEG biometric models. Our approach employs convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to extract features from EEG signals and classify them. The results show that our adversarial training approach significantly improves the robustness of EEG biometric systems against various types of attacks. We achieve a classification accuracy of 95% and a false acceptance rate of 0.5%, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods. The contributions of this research include the development of a novel adversarial deep learning framework for EEG biometrics, which has potential applications in secure authentication and identification systems. Key keywords: EEG biometrics, adversarial deep learning, GANs, CNNs, RNNs, biometric security.
