Objective: Learning accurate, physically consistent representations of dynamical systems from raw observations remains a significant challenge in generative modeling. This paper introduces Hamiltonian Generative Networks (HGNs), a novel framework that embeds Hamiltonian mechanics into latent-variable models to capture the underlying energy-conserving dynamics of complex systems.  

Methods: HGNs employ an encoder to map high-dimensional inputs (e.g., video frames) into a continuous latent space structured by a learned Hamiltonian function. A symplectic integrator propagates latent states over time by numerically solving Hamilton’s equations, ensuring exact preservation of phase-space volume and approximate energy conservation. A decoder reconstructs observations from predicted latent states. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end using a combination of reconstruction loss and a physics-informed regularizer that encourages H-conservation.  

Results: We evaluate HGNs on synthetic and real-world benchmarks, including pendulum video sequences, two-body simulations, and physical object interactions. Compared to state-of-the-art recurrent and variational video models, HGNs achieve a 25% reduction in long-term prediction error and maintain energy drift below 2% over extended rollouts. Qualitative assessments demonstrate more coherent trajectories and sharper reconstructions, particularly in scenarios with fast dynamics or multibody coupling.  

Conclusion: By integrating Hamiltonian inductive biases into deep generative architectures, HGNs offer a principled route toward interpretable, stable long-term forecasting of physical systems. This approach paves the way for improved model-based control, data-efficient simulation, and physics-guided representation learning in robotics, computational physics, and beyond.  

Keywords: Hamiltonian mechanics; generative networks; symplectic integrator; video prediction; latent dynamics; physics-informed machine learning.
