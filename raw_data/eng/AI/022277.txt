Facial Expression Recognition (FER) is a critical task in computer vision with applications in human-computer interaction, emotion analysis, and behavioral studies. This paper investigates the effectiveness of leveraging pre-trained Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) features for FER, aiming to address the challenges of limited labeled datasets and computational complexity. We propose a transfer learning-based approach, utilizing pre-trained CNN models such as VGG16 and ResNet50, to extract discriminative features from facial images. These features are then fine-tuned and classified using a lightweight neural network. Our experiments are conducted on widely used FER datasets, including FER2013 and CK+, to evaluate the performance of the proposed method. Results demonstrate that pre-trained CNN features achieve state-of-the-art accuracy, outperforming traditional handcrafted feature extraction methods and reducing training time significantly. The study highlights the potential of transfer learning in FER, particularly in scenarios with constrained data and computational resources. This work contributes to advancing FER systems by providing a robust, efficient, and scalable solution, with implications for real-time emotion detection and affective computing applications.  

**Keywords**: Facial Expression Recognition, Convolutional Neural Networks, Transfer Learning, Pre-trained Models, Emotion Detection, Affective Computing.
