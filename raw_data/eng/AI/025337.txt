Graph-structured data is increasingly prevalent in various domains, such as social networks, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems. However, graphs are vulnerable to adversarial attacks, which can compromise the robustness of graph-based machine learning models. This paper introduces *Adversarial Immunization*, a novel framework designed to enhance the certifiable robustness of graph neural networks (GNNs) against adversarial perturbations. The proposed approach focuses on proactively immunizing critical nodes and edges in the graph to minimize the impact of potential attacks. We formulate the immunization problem as an optimization task and develop an efficient algorithm to identify and protect the most vulnerable components of the graph. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method significantly improves the robustness of GNNs, outperforming existing defense mechanisms in terms of both accuracy and certifiable robustness. Furthermore, the framework is computationally efficient and scalable to large-scale graphs. The results highlight the potential of adversarial immunization as a practical solution for securing graph-based systems in real-world applications. Key contributions include a novel immunization strategy, theoretical guarantees for robustness, and empirical validation across diverse datasets. This work advances the field of adversarial machine learning by providing a certifiably robust defense mechanism tailored for graph-structured data.  

**Keywords**: adversarial robustness, graph neural networks, immunization, certifiable defense, graph-based machine learning.
