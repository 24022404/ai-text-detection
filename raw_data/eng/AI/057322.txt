This study aims to develop high-quality training samples for deep learning-based local climate zone classification in Korea, addressing the challenge of accurate urban climate mapping. To achieve this, a novel approach combining satellite imagery and geographic information systems (GIS) is employed, leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for image classification. The methodology involves collecting and labeling a large dataset of Korean cities, utilizing a transfer learning strategy to adapt a pre-trained CNN model to the local climate zone classification task. Experimental results show significant improvements in classification accuracy, outperforming traditional machine learning methods. The proposed approach enables the creation of detailed urban climate maps, providing valuable insights for urban planning, climate change mitigation, and environmental sustainability. This research contributes to the development of more accurate and efficient local climate zone classification systems, with potential applications in smart city initiatives and climate-resilient urban design. Key keywords: deep learning, local climate zone classification, urban climate mapping, convolutional neural networks, transfer learning, satellite imagery, GIS.
