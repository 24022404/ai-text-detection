This study aims to improve content-based image retrieval (CBIR) on 3D brain MRI by developing a disease-oriented image embedding approach with pseudo-scanner standardization. Our method utilizes a deep learning framework to learn compact and informative image representations, while accounting for variations in scanner protocols and parameters. The proposed approach involves a two-stage process, where the first stage learns a disease-specific embedding space using a convolutional neural network (CNN), and the second stage applies pseudo-scanner standardization to reduce inter-scanner variability. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art CBIR approaches on 3D brain MRI datasets, achieving higher retrieval accuracy and robustness to scanner differences. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a disease-oriented embedding approach and the application of pseudo-scanner standardization, which collectively enhance the effectiveness of CBIR systems for neuroimaging applications. This work has important implications for clinical decision support, medical image analysis, and computer-aided diagnosis, with potential applications in neurology, radiology, and neuroscience. Key keywords: content-based image retrieval, 3D brain MRI, disease-oriented image embedding, pseudo-scanner standardization, deep learning, medical image analysis.
