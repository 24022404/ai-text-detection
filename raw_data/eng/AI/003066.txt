This paper presents a comprehensive review of visual tracking algorithms and critically analyzes their application within the domain of mobile robotics. The objective is to provide an in-depth understanding of how visual trackers contribute to the perception, navigation, and interaction capabilities of mobile robots. We systematically categorize existing visual tracking methods, including classical approaches and modern techniques based on deep learning, and evaluate their strengths and limitations with respect to accuracy, robustness, and real-time performance. The analysis includes a comparison of key algorithms such as correlation filter-based trackers, Siamese networks, and object detection-based methods, considering their adaptability to dynamic environments, computational requirements, and integration with robotic sensors. Our findings highlight that advanced trackers leveraging convolutional neural networks demonstrate significant improvements in challenging scenarios such as occlusion and illumination changes but may require optimization for deployment on resource-constrained mobile platforms. The paper also discusses case studies and recent advancements in deploying visual trackers for tasks such as autonomous navigation, obstacle avoidance, and target following. In conclusion, we identify current research gaps, propose future directions for efficient and reliable visual tracking in mobile robots, and underscore the importance of algorithm-robot hardware co-design. The review contributes to guiding researchers and practitioners towards selecting and enhancing visual tracking frameworks tailored for real-world mobile robotics applications. Keywords: visual tracking, mobile robots, deep learning, real-time performance, robot perception, computer vision, autonomous navigation.
