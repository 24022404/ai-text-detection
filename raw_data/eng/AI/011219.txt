Phase retrieval, the recovery of a signal from magnitude-only measurements, is a crucial problem in various scientific fields. This paper introduces a novel phase retrieval algorithm based on the Expectation Consistent Signal Recovery (ECSR) framework, enhanced by a hypernetwork architecture. The hypernetwork is designed to adaptively estimate the parameters of the ECSR algorithm, thereby improving its convergence speed and reconstruction accuracy. Our method leverages the hypernetwork to learn optimal regularization parameters conditioned on the observed measurements, leading to a data-driven approach to parameter tuning. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed hypernetwork-ECSR algorithm significantly outperforms traditional ECSR and other state-of-the-art phase retrieval techniques, especially in noisy and undersampled scenarios. The improvement is validated through simulations on benchmark datasets, showcasing superior reconstruction quality and robustness. This adaptive approach to parameter estimation in ECSR opens new avenues for efficient and accurate phase retrieval in diverse applications.

Keywords: Phase Retrieval, Expectation Consistent Signal Recovery, Hypernetwork, Signal Reconstruction, Inverse Problems, Machine Learning.
