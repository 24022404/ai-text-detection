This paper presents a comprehensive benchmark of word image recognition systems on publicly available datasets. The objective is to provide a standardized evaluation framework for comparing the performance of different optical character recognition (OCR) techniques applied to word images. We evaluate several state-of-the-art methods, including connectionist temporal classification (CTC)-based models and attention-based sequence-to-sequence architectures, using a consistent experimental setup across multiple datasets with varying image quality and vocabulary sizes. The results highlight the strengths and weaknesses of each approach, revealing that while attention-based models generally achieve higher accuracy on clean datasets, CTC-based models demonstrate greater robustness to noise and distortions. Furthermore, we analyze the impact of data augmentation and pre-training strategies on recognition accuracy. Our findings offer valuable insights for researchers and practitioners in the field, facilitating the development of more effective and robust word image recognition systems. Keywords: word image recognition, OCR, benchmarking, performance evaluation, deep learning, CTC, attention mechanisms.
