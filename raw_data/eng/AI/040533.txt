Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have gained significant attention for their ability to model relational data, but their vulnerability to adversarial attacks remains a critical concern. This paper introduces **TDGIA (Topology-Driven Graph Injection Attack)**, a novel and effective method for injecting adversarial perturbations into graph-structured data to compromise GNN performance. Unlike existing approaches that primarily focus on modifying existing graph structures or node features, TDGIA strategically injects malicious nodes and edges into the graph, leveraging topological properties to maximize attack impact. We propose a systematic framework that optimizes the injection process by considering both node feature distributions and graph connectivity patterns. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that TDGIA significantly outperforms state-of-the-art attack methods in terms of success rate and stealthiness. Our findings reveal that even minimal injections can drastically degrade GNN performance across various tasks, including node classification and link prediction. This research highlights the inherent vulnerabilities of GNNs to topology-driven attacks and underscores the need for robust defense mechanisms. The implications of TDGIA extend to real-world applications, such as social network analysis and recommendation systems, where adversarial robustness is crucial for maintaining system integrity.  

**Keywords**: Graph Neural Networks, Adversarial Attacks, Graph Injection, Topology-Driven Attacks, Robustness, Node Classification.
