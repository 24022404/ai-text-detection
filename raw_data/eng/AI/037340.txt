This paper investigates the phenomenon of benign overfitting in the context of ridge function estimation, focusing on finite sample analysis. While overfitting is traditionally associated with poor generalization, recent studies have shown that certain models can achieve strong performance despite fitting noisy training data. We aim to provide a theoretical understanding of this phenomenon for ridge functions, which are widely used in high-dimensional data analysis and machine learning. Using a rigorous mathematical framework, we analyze the generalization behavior of ridge regression models under finite sample settings. Our approach involves deriving bounds on the excess risk and examining the interplay between model complexity, data dimensionality, and noise levels. The results demonstrate that benign overfitting can occur under specific conditions, particularly when the data exhibits low-dimensional structure. We also identify key factors influencing the transition between benign and harmful overfitting, such as the alignment of the true signal with the ridge structure and the magnitude of noise. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of overfitting in high-dimensional learning and provide insights into the design of robust estimation methods. The implications of this work extend to applications in machine learning, signal processing, and statistical inference, where ridge functions are commonly employed.  

**Keywords**: benign overfitting, ridge function estimation, finite sample analysis, high-dimensional learning, generalization bounds, ridge regression.
