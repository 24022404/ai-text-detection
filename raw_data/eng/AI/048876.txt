**Abstract:**  
Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful approach for training deep visual representation models without the need for extensive labeled data. However, most existing methods rely on traditional similarity metrics such as cosine distance or Euclidean distance, which may limit the expressiveness and robustness of learned features. In this paper, we introduce a novel framework based on Deep Bregman Divergence (DBD) for contrastive learning of visual representations. Our approach leverages the flexibility and generality of Bregman divergences to define a more informative and adaptable measure of similarity between representations. We employ a parameterized divergence function within a deep architecture, enabling the model to learn task-specific distance metrics during training. Extensive experiments on standard image recognition benchmarks demonstrate that our method achieves significant improvements over existing contrastive learning algorithms, including higher accuracy in downstream tasks and enhanced robustness to data variations. Furthermore, ablation studies highlight the versatility of Bregman divergences for capturing complex feature relationships. The proposed Deep Bregman Divergence framework paves the way for more flexible and effective metric learning in self-supervised visual representation learning, offering a promising direction for future research in unsupervised and semi-supervised deep learning.  
**Keywords:** contrastive learning, visual representations, Bregman divergence, deep learning, self-supervised learning, metric learning
