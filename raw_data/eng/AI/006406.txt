Estimating individual treatment effects (ITE) from observational data is a fundamental challenge in causal inference, with significant applications in precision medicine, marketing, and policy evaluation. Traditional matching methods often struggle to accurately estimate treatment effects due to imbalances in covariate distributions and the presence of irrelevant or noisy features. This paper introduces a novel framework that leverages selective and balanced representation learning to improve matching-based treatment effect estimation. Our approach constructs a low-dimensional representation space optimized to minimize covariate imbalance across treatment groups while actively selecting features most relevant to potential outcomes. We propose a hybrid network architecture combining adversarial learning for balance, attention mechanisms for selectivity, and a tailored matching algorithm in the learned space. Experimental results on benchmark causal inference datasets demonstrate significant improvements in treatment effect estimation accuracy, robustness to confounding, and generalization compared to state-of-the-art methods. The proposed methodology offers a new direction for reliable causal inference, enabling more accurate, interpretable, and data-efficient treatment recommendations. Keywords: treatment effects estimation, causal inference, matching, representation learning, feature selection, covariate balance.
