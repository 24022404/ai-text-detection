**Abstract**

Addressing the dual challenges of annotation scarcity and domain shifts remains a significant obstacle in deploying robust machine learning systems, especially in fields requiring extensive labeled data and domain adaptability. This paper introduces a novel framework, termed "Extreme Consistency," which leverages self-supervised learning and consistency regularization to significantly mitigate dependence on annotated datasets while maintaining strong performance across diverse domains. Our approach employs a dual-network architecture that enforces prediction consistency under a range of augmentations and domain variations, enabling effective knowledge transfer from source to target domains with minimal supervision. Comprehensive experiments on benchmark datasets in computer vision and natural language processing demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms traditional semi-supervised and domain adaptation techniques, achieving higher accuracy with far fewer labeled examples and strong resilience to domain shifts. These findings illustrate the potential of Extreme Consistency to bridge the annotation gap and enhance model generalization, offering a scalable solution for real-world applications such as medical imaging, remote sensing, and multilingual text analysis. Keywords: annotation scarcity, domain adaptation, consistency regularization, self-supervised learning, transfer learning, generalization.
