Semantic segmentation of urban scenes is a critical task in computer vision, enabling applications such as autonomous driving and smart city planning. However, models trained on one dataset often struggle to generalize to new environments due to domain shifts. This paper introduces a novel curriculum domain adaptation (CDA) approach to improve the semantic segmentation of urban scenes across diverse datasets. Our method leverages a progressive learning strategy, where the model is first trained on simpler, more generalizable features before gradually adapting to complex, domain-specific characteristics. We employ a combination of adversarial training and self-supervised learning to align feature distributions between source and target domains effectively. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including Cityscapes and GTA5, demonstrate that our CDA approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, with significant improvements in mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) compared to existing methods. The results highlight the effectiveness of curriculum-based learning in mitigating domain gaps and enhancing model robustness. This work contributes to advancing domain adaptation techniques in semantic segmentation, offering practical implications for real-world urban scene analysis.  

**Keywords**: semantic segmentation, domain adaptation, curriculum learning, urban scenes, adversarial training, self-supervised learning.
