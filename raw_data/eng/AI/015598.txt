Meta-learning, or learning-to-learn, aims to develop models capable of rapidly adapting to new tasks with limited data. However, training effective meta-learners remains challenging, often requiring extensive computational resources and carefully designed architectures. This paper investigates the efficacy of fine-tuning pre-trained models as a powerful technique for meta-learning. We propose a novel fine-tuning strategy that leverages the representational power of pre-trained models to achieve state-of-the-art meta-learning performance. Our method involves a two-stage process: first, a model is pre-trained on a large, diverse dataset; second, the pre-trained model is fine-tuned on a meta-training set consisting of a distribution of related tasks. We demonstrate that fine-tuning allows for effective knowledge transfer, enabling rapid adaptation to new tasks with significantly fewer training examples compared to traditional meta-learning approaches. Empirical results on benchmark meta-learning datasets demonstrate the superiority of our fine-tuning approach, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and convergence speed. These findings highlight the potential of fine-tuning as a practical and effective strategy for meta-learning, paving the way for more efficient and scalable learning-to-learn systems.

Keywords: Meta-learning, Fine-tuning, Pre-trained Models, Transfer Learning, Few-Shot Learning.
