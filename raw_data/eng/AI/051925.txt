This paper explores the concept of zero-shot learning (ZSL) in the context of 3D point cloud objects, aiming to recognize and classify unseen objects without prior training data. Our approach leverages a novel fusion of geometric and semantic features, enabling the model to generalize across categories and learn from limited labeled data. We propose a ZSL framework that utilizes a transformer-based architecture to learn robust and transferable representations of 3D point clouds. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets and outperforming existing ZSL methods. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a new 3D point cloud dataset for ZSL and the development of a flexible and scalable framework that can be applied to various 3D vision tasks. Our work has significant implications for applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented reality, where recognizing and interacting with unseen objects is crucial. Key keywords: zero-shot learning, 3D point clouds, transformer, geometric features, semantic features, 3D vision, robotics, autonomous driving.
