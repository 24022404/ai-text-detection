This paper introduces Wasserstein Adversarial Imitation Learning (WAIL), a novel approach to imitation learning that leverages the Wasserstein distance to enhance the stability and performance of policy learning from expert demonstrations. Imitation learning aims to train agents to mimic expert behavior without explicit reward functions. Traditional adversarial imitation learning methods, while effective, can suffer from instability due to the nature of adversarial training. WAIL addresses this by incorporating the Wasserstein distance as a measure of similarity between the learned policy's state-action distribution and the expert's distribution, providing a more robust and stable training signal. We present a theoretical analysis demonstrating WAIL's convergence properties and its ability to learn optimal policies under certain conditions. Empirical evaluations on benchmark robotic control tasks show that WAIL outperforms existing adversarial imitation learning methods in terms of sample efficiency, policy performance, and robustness to noisy or suboptimal expert data. These results highlight the potential of Wasserstein distance as a powerful tool in imitation learning, paving the way for more reliable and efficient deployment of learned policies in real-world applications. Keywords: Imitation Learning, Adversarial Learning, Wasserstein Distance, Reinforcement Learning, Robotics, Policy Learning.
