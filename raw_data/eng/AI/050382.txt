Autonomous vehicles (AVs) rely heavily on object detection for safe navigation. However, their vulnerability to adversarial perturbations poses a significant threat. This paper investigates the resilience of AV object category detection systems to universal adversarial perturbations (UAPs). We analyze the impact of UAPs generated using various attack algorithms on the detection accuracy of state-of-the-art object detectors, including YOLOv5 and Faster R-CNN, within simulated AV environments. Our findings reveal that UAPs can substantially degrade detection performance, particularly for distant and occluded objects. Furthermore, we explore the transferability of UAPs across different object detectors and environmental conditions. We propose a novel defense mechanism based on adversarial training with a dynamically adjusted perturbation magnitude. Experimental results demonstrate that our defense strategy significantly improves the robustness of object detection against UAPs, mitigating performance degradation by up to 40% while maintaining baseline accuracy on clean data. This research highlights the critical need for robust object detection algorithms and effective defense mechanisms to ensure the safety and reliability of autonomous vehicles.

Keywords: Autonomous Vehicles, Object Detection, Adversarial Perturbations, Universal Adversarial Perturbations, UAP, Cybersecurity, Machine Learning, Robustness, Adversarial Training.
