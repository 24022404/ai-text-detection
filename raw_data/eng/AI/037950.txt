This paper introduces ST3D++, a novel approach for unsupervised domain adaptation in 3D object detection, leveraging denoised self-training to bridge the gap between synthetic and real-world data. The objective is to improve the accuracy and robustness of 3D object detectors when faced with domain shifts. Our method employs a self-training framework, where a teacher model generates pseudo-labels for unlabeled target domain data, and a student model is trained to minimize the difference between its predictions and these pseudo-labels. To address the noise in pseudo-labels, we propose a denoising mechanism that selectively filters out low-confidence predictions. Experimental results demonstrate that ST3D++ outperforms state-of-the-art methods on benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in detection accuracy and domain adaptation. The key contributions of this work include the introduction of a denoising mechanism for self-training and the application of this approach to 3D object detection, highlighting the potential for unsupervised domain adaptation in this field. Our approach has important implications for real-world applications, such as autonomous driving and robotics, where domain shifts are common. Key keywords: 3D object detection, unsupervised domain adaptation, self-training, denoising, autonomous driving, robotics.
