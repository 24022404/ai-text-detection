This paper addresses the critical issue of performative risk in algorithmic systems, where the very act of prediction or classification can alter the outcome, creating a self-reinforcing cycle. Our objective is to develop a framework that optimizes performative risk, mitigating the echo chamber effect that can lead to biased and inaccurate results. We propose a novel approach that combines causal inference with online learning, allowing for the dynamic adjustment of model parameters in response to changing environmental conditions. Our method, termed "Performative Risk Optimization" (PRO), demonstrates significant improvements in reducing performative risk compared to existing methods. Key findings indicate that PRO achieves a 25% reduction in bias and a 30% increase in predictive accuracy. The implications of this research are far-reaching, contributing to the development of more robust and fair algorithmic systems, with potential applications in areas such as recommender systems, natural language processing, and decision-making under uncertainty. By optimizing performative risk, our work paves the way for more reliable and trustworthy AI systems, highlighting the importance of keywords such as causal inference, online learning, and performative risk in the context of AI models and decision-making processes.
