Hypothesis testing is a fundamental task in statistical analysis and machine learning, with distance-based and kernel-based methods being two widely used approaches. This paper investigates the theoretical and practical equivalence between these methods, providing a unified framework for understanding their relationship. We demonstrate that, under specific conditions, distance-based methods and kernel-based methods yield identical hypothesis testing results. Through rigorous mathematical analysis, we establish the exact conditions under which this equivalence holds, including the choice of distance metrics and kernel functions. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets validate our theoretical findings, showing that both approaches produce statistically indistinguishable outcomes when the equivalence conditions are met. This work not only bridges the gap between distance and kernel methods but also offers practical insights for selecting the appropriate method based on computational efficiency and interpretability. Our findings have broad implications for applications in machine learning, bioinformatics, and data-driven decision-making, where hypothesis testing plays a critical role.  

**Keywords**: hypothesis testing, distance metrics, kernel methods, statistical equivalence, machine learning, computational efficiency.
