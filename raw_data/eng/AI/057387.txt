This paper addresses the challenge of few-shot extrapolation in reinforcement learning (RL), where an agent must adapt to new, unseen situations with limited experience. Our objective is to develop a novel approach that enables RL agents to extrapolate beyond their training data, generalizing to new environments and tasks. We propose a structured maximum entropy RL framework that incorporates a set of diverse, task-agnostic solutions, rather than relying on a single, optimal policy. Our method, Few-Shot Extrapolation via Structured MaxEnt RL, leverages the strengths of maximum entropy RL and few-shot learning to achieve state-of-the-art performance in extrapolation tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods, exhibiting improved robustness and adaptability in a range of environments. The key contributions of this work include a novel RL framework, a set of new benchmarks for few-shot extrapolation, and insights into the importance of diversity and structure in RL policies. Our research has significant implications for the development of more versatile and adaptive AI systems, with potential applications in areas such as robotics, autonomous driving, and decision-making under uncertainty. Key keywords: few-shot learning, reinforcement learning, maximum entropy RL, extrapolation, adaptability, robustness.
