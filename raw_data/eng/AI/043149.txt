The objective of this research is to address the challenges in creating robust graph embeddings that accurately capture the inherent structural properties of complex networks. We introduce RECS, a novel framework that leverages connection subgraphs to enhance graph embedding robustness against noisy data and structural perturbations. The RECS approach identifies and utilizes subgraphs that preserve critical connectivity features, ensuring that the embeddings are resistant to disruptions. Our method builds upon conventional embedding techniques by integrating these enriched subgraph structures into the embedding process. To evaluate the effectiveness of RECS, we conducted extensive experiments on a range of benchmark datasets, comparing its performance to existing graph embedding models. The results indicate that RECS outperforms traditional methods in several key areas, including accuracy, resilience to noise, and efficiency in maintaining structural integrity. Additionally, RECS demonstrates superior adaptability across various graph-based applications, such as node classification, link prediction, and graph clustering, offering promising improvements in performance. The implications of this research extend to numerous applications in network analysis, data mining, and artificial intelligence, where robust graph representations are critical. Our findings highlight the potential of connection subgraphs as a fundamental component in the next generation of graph embedding techniques. Key keywords include graph embedding, connection subgraphs, network robustness, structural perturbations, and node classification.
