This paper presents a novel approach for representation learning of pedestrian trajectories by leveraging an actor-critic sequence-to-sequence autoencoder architecture. Our primary objective is to devise a robust method for encoding and reconstructing sequential trajectory data, which is essential for applications such as pedestrian behavior analysis, anomaly detection, and intelligent surveillance systems. The proposed model integrates an actor-critic framework with a sequence-to-sequence autoencoder, enabling the system to effectively capture both spatial and temporal dependencies within trajectory sequences. The actor network encodes the input trajectories, while the critic guides the optimization process, ensuring higher fidelity in the learned latent representations. Experimental evaluation was conducted on widely-used pedestrian trajectory datasets, where the method demonstrated superior performance in terms of reconstruction error and downstream task utility, outperforming conventional autoencoders and other baseline models. The results illustrate that our approach not only preserves critical information within pedestrian movement patterns but also yields compact and discriminative embeddings suitable for various trajectory analysis tasks. These findings suggest significant potential for enhanced context-awareness in intelligent transportation and public safety applications. Keywords: trajectory representation learning, sequence-to-sequence autoencoder, actor-critic model, pedestrian movement, spatiotemporal analysis.
