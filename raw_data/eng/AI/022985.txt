Crowdsourcing has become a prevalent approach for obtaining labeled data, particularly in multi-class classification tasks. This paper addresses the inherent challenges of noisy labels in yes-no type crowdsourcing, where workers answer binary questions to determine class membership. We introduce a novel full probabilistic model that explicitly captures worker reliability and item difficulty, leveraging a Bayesian framework for robust inference. Our model incorporates a hierarchical structure to account for the heterogeneity of worker skills and biases. We develop an efficient Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm for parameter estimation and class prediction. Experimental results on several real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of classification accuracy and label quality, particularly when dealing with sparse and unreliable crowdsourced data. The proposed model provides a principled approach to managing uncertainty in crowdsourced labels, leading to improved performance in multi-class classification. Keywords: Crowdsourcing, Multi-Class Classification, Probabilistic Model, EM Algorithm, Bayesian Inference, Label Aggregation.
