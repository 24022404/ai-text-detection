Clinical time series data presents unique challenges due to its high dimensionality, irregular sampling, and complex temporal dependencies. This paper addresses the problem of leveraging multitask learning (MTL) to improve predictive performance and generalization across diverse clinical tasks. We propose a novel MTL framework that integrates shared representations and task-specific adaptations to effectively model heterogeneous clinical time series data. Our approach employs a combination of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and attention mechanisms to capture temporal patterns and prioritize relevant features across tasks. To evaluate the framework, we conduct extensive experiments on a publicly available clinical dataset, benchmarking against state-of-the-art single-task and multitask baselines. Results demonstrate that our method achieves superior performance in terms of accuracy, robustness, and computational efficiency, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. Furthermore, we provide insights into the interpretability of the learned representations, highlighting their potential for clinical decision support systems. The proposed framework not only advances the state of the art in clinical time series analysis but also offers a scalable solution for real-world healthcare applications.  

**Keywords**: multitask learning, clinical time series, recurrent neural networks, attention mechanisms, healthcare analytics, benchmarking.
