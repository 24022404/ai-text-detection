This paper introduces LT-GAN, a novel self-supervised Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) that incorporates latent transformation detection to enhance its performance. The objective is to improve the quality and diversity of generated samples by leveraging latent space transformations. Our approach employs a unique architecture that combines a generator and discriminator with a latent transformation module, allowing the model to learn complex data distributions without requiring labeled data. The LT-GAN is trained using a self-supervised loss function that encourages the model to detect and apply latent transformations, resulting in more realistic and diverse generated samples. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of LT-GAN, showing significant improvements in image generation quality and diversity compared to state-of-the-art self-supervised GANs. The proposed method has important implications for applications such as data augmentation, image-to-image translation, and unsupervised representation learning. Key contributions include the introduction of latent transformation detection in self-supervised GANs and the development of a novel architecture that enables efficient and effective training. Relevant keywords: self-supervised learning, GANs, latent transformation, generative models, deep learning, image generation.
