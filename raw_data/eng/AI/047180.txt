This paper delves into the realm of interpretability and explainability in machine learning, aiming to provide a comprehensive overview of the current landscape. Our objective is to navigate the complexities of machine learning models, shedding light on the techniques and methods that enable transparency and understanding of their decision-making processes. We employ a multi-faceted approach, exploring various algorithms and technologies, including model-agnostic interpretability methods, such as feature importance and partial dependence plots, as well as model-specific techniques, like saliency maps and layer-wise relevance propagation. Our results demonstrate the effectiveness of these methods in elucidating the inner workings of complex models, including neural networks and ensemble methods. We conclude that interpretability and explainability are essential components of trustworthy machine learning, enabling the identification of biases, errors, and areas for improvement. This research contributes to the development of more transparent and accountable AI systems, with potential applications in high-stakes domains, such as healthcare, finance, and autonomous vehicles. Key keywords: interpretability, explainability, machine learning, model transparency, AI trustworthiness.
