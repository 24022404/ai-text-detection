This paper presents a novel feature clustering method that integrates Histogram of Oriented Optical Flow (HOOF) with superpixel segmentation to enhance motion-based video analysis. The objective of this research is to address the challenges of robust motion representation and object segmentation in dynamic scenes. The proposed approach first partitions video frames into superpixels, effectively preserving local spatial consistency and reducing computational complexity. Subsequently, optical flow vectors within each superpixel are extracted, and their orientations are represented using HOOF descriptors to capture dominant motion patterns. Feature vectors from all superpixels are then clustered using an unsupervised algorithm, leading to coherent segmentation of moving objects. Experimental evaluations on benchmark video datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms traditional pixel-based and global motion segmentation techniques in terms of segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency. The results indicate improved capability for distinguishing complex motion groups and robust handling of background noise. This approach is well-suited for applications in video surveillance, automated scene understanding, and activity recognition. The key contributions include a synergistic use of optical flow histograms with superpixel-based spatial constraints, delivering enhanced feature clustering performance in challenging visual environments. Keywords: optical flow, superpixels, feature clustering, video segmentation, motion analysis, HOOF.
