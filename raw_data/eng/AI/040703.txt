Accurate prediction of crop yields is critical for optimizing agricultural planning, resource allocation, and food security. This study addresses the challenge of predicting county-level corn yields in the United States using advanced deep learning techniques. We propose a novel approach based on Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models, which are well-suited for capturing temporal dependencies in sequential agricultural data. The model integrates historical yield data, weather patterns, soil conditions, and satellite imagery to enhance prediction accuracy. Our methodology involves preprocessing multi-source datasets, training the LSTM network, and evaluating its performance against traditional machine learning models and statistical methods. Results demonstrate that the LSTM-based model outperforms existing approaches, achieving higher accuracy and robustness in yield prediction across diverse geographic regions. The model’s ability to handle complex, non-linear relationships in agricultural data highlights its potential for real-world applications. This research contributes to the field of precision agriculture by providing a scalable and data-driven solution for yield forecasting. The findings have significant implications for farmers, policymakers, and stakeholders in the agricultural sector, enabling more informed decision-making and improved crop management strategies.  

**Keywords**: Corn yield prediction, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), deep learning, precision agriculture, crop modeling, temporal data analysis.
