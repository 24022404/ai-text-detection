Distributed learning systems are increasingly adopted for their scalability and efficiency in handling large-scale machine learning tasks. However, they are vulnerable to adversarial attacks and data poisoning, which can compromise model integrity and performance. This paper introduces **Aspis**, a robust detection system designed to enhance the security and reliability of distributed learning environments. Aspis employs a novel consensus-based approach to identify and mitigate malicious updates from compromised workers during the training process. By leveraging redundancy in task assignments and cross-verification mechanisms, Aspis ensures the integrity of model updates without significantly increasing computational overhead. Experimental results demonstrate that Aspis achieves superior robustness compared to existing methods, reducing the success rate of adversarial attacks by up to 90% while maintaining high model accuracy. Furthermore, the system scales efficiently across large distributed setups, making it suitable for real-world applications. The proposed framework represents a significant advancement in securing distributed learning systems, offering a practical solution for safeguarding against emerging threats in collaborative machine learning environments.  

**Keywords**: distributed learning, adversarial attacks, data poisoning, robust detection, consensus-based systems, machine learning security.
