Continual learning (CL) aims to enable models to learn new tasks sequentially without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. A major challenge in CL is catastrophic forgetting, where the performance on old tasks degrades significantly upon learning new ones. This paper introduces a novel rectification-based approach to mitigate catastrophic forgetting by explicitly retaining knowledge from past tasks. Our method employs a rectification module that learns to correct the feature representations extracted by the model, effectively preserving information relevant to previous tasks. Specifically, we use a knowledge distillation loss to encourage the rectified features to resemble those extracted by the original model on past tasks. Furthermore, we introduce an adaptive regularization term that dynamically adjusts the strength of rectification based on task similarity. We evaluate our approach on several benchmark CL datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in knowledge retention compared to state-of-the-art methods. Results show that our method effectively balances plasticity and stability, leading to enhanced performance across a sequence of learning tasks. The proposed rectification-based technique offers a promising direction for advancing continual learning research and enabling more robust and adaptive AI systems.

**Keywords:** Continual Learning, Catastrophic Forgetting, Knowledge Distillation, Rectification, Neural Networks.
