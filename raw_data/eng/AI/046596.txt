Objective: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have achieved remarkable success in realistic image synthesis, yet their training often suffers from instability and mode collapse. This work introduces a unified GAN training framework that leverages self-labeling and self-attention to enhance learning dynamics and representation quality without requiring external labels.

Methods: We propose SL-SA-GAN, a single architecture combining a pseudo-label generator and an attention-augmented discriminator. The pseudo-label generator assigns soft, data-driven labels to unlabeled samples, guiding the discriminator with richer supervisory signals. Concurrently, self-attention layers enable the model to capture long-range dependencies and fine-grained features in both generator and discriminator. Our end-to-end training pipeline alternates between updating pseudo-labels and refining attention weights, fostering mutual improvement.

Results: Extensive experiments on CIFAR-10, STL-10, and CelebA demonstrate that SL-SA-GAN outperforms baseline GANs in both quantitative and qualitative metrics. We observe up to a 25% reduction in Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and consistent gains in Inception Score (IS). Training curves exhibit faster convergence and reduced sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning, confirming enhanced stability and mode diversity.

Conclusion: By integrating self-labeling with self-attention under a unified framework, SL-SA-GAN delivers more robust and expressive generative models. This approach generalizes across datasets and can be extended to conditional and high-resolution synthesis tasks, offering a versatile tool for future GAN research.

Keywords: generative adversarial network, self-labeling, self-attention, image synthesis, stable training, representation learning.
