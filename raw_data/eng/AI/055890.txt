This paper investigates the capability of untrained, non-convolutional neural networks to serve as efficient image decoders, offering concise and effective image representations without the need for conventional convolutional structures or pre-training. The proposed method, termed Deep Decoder, utilizes a randomly initialized, fully-connected neural architecture to map low-dimensional code vectors directly to image space. Unlike traditional autoencoder frameworks or deep convolutional models, this approach does not employ any convolutional filters or require data-driven learning, yet it successfully captures essential image structures. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that Deep Decoder is able to achieve competitive image reconstruction, denoising, and inpainting performance across a variety of benchmarks. Results indicate that even untrained, non-convolutional networks can act as strong image priors, rivaling or surpassing several classical and learned methods in specific tasks. The findings challenge prevailing assumptions about the necessity of convolutions and heavy training for image representation, highlighting the potential for lightweight, interpretable architectures in numerous image processing applications. Keywords: deep decoder, image reconstruction, non-convolutional networks, untrained neural networks, image denoising, image inpainting, image priors.
