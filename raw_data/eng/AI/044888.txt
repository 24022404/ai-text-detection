The rapid proliferation of big data has revolutionized information technology, enabling significant advancements in machine learning and artificial intelligence. However, many practical applications still face scenarios where labeled data is scarce or costly to obtain, posing challenges for traditional data-hungry algorithms. This paper explores the paradigm of Small Sample Learning (SSL), which focuses on developing robust models capable of achieving high performance with limited training data, within the context of the big data era. We systematically analyze various SSL approaches, including meta-learning, transfer learning, and data augmentation, and propose a hybrid framework that leverages both domain knowledge and synthetic data generation to enhance learning efficacy. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive or superior accuracy compared to conventional methods, particularly under data-constrained conditions. The findings highlight the feasibility of effective learning from limited samples while utilizing the advantages offered by big data for auxiliary tasks. This research underscores the importance of SSL in enabling scalable, efficient, and accessible machine learning solutions, with wide-ranging implications for fields such as medical diagnosis, autonomous systems, and natural language processing. Key keywords: small sample learning, big data, meta-learning, transfer learning, data augmentation, machine learning, artificial intelligence.
