This paper investigates the challenges of online learning in environments where the structure of feedback is both complex and uncertain, modeled as uncertain feedback graphs. In traditional online learning, algorithms receive explicit feedback about their predictions, enabling them to adjust and improve over time. However, many real-world scenarios present feedback only through partially observed or dynamically changing graphs, making it difficult for learners to reliably infer optimal actions. We introduce a novel framework that extends standard online learning models to handle uncertain, potentially adversarial, feedback graphs. Our approach designs and analyzes algorithms capable of adapting their learning strategies despite incomplete or noisy feedback link information. Through theoretical analysis, we establish regret bounds for our algorithms, demonstrating robust performance even under high uncertainty. Experimental evaluations on synthetic and benchmark datasets further validate the effectiveness of our methods, showing consistent improvement over traditional algorithms that assume complete or static feedback. The results illustrate the importance of modeling feedback uncertainty and provide actionable insights for applications such as recommendation systems, sensor networks, and adaptive control. Our contributions advance the theoretical understanding of online learning with structured uncertainty, paving the way for more resilient and adaptive learning systems in complex feedback environments. 

Keywords: online learning, feedback graphs, uncertain feedback, regret minimization, adaptive algorithms, partial information, robust learning
