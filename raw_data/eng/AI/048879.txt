Off-policy reinforcement learning (RL) offers the potential for efficient learning by leveraging experience generated from diverse sources. However, learning with delayed rewards poses a significant challenge, as the temporal credit assignment problem is exacerbated. This paper investigates the impact of delayed rewards on the stability and convergence of off-policy RL algorithms. We introduce a novel approach, the Delayed Reward Correction (DRC) method, which utilizes a learned reward model to estimate and correct for reward delays during policy evaluation. The DRC method incorporates a temporal consistency constraint to improve the accuracy of the reward model. Experimental results on a suite of benchmark tasks with varying reward delays demonstrate that DRC significantly improves the sample efficiency and stability of off-policy algorithms, particularly Q-learning and SARSA, compared to existing delay-agnostic methods. Our findings indicate that explicitly addressing reward delays is crucial for effective off-policy RL in environments with sparse or delayed feedback. This work contributes a practical and theoretically grounded method for handling delayed rewards, paving the way for broader application of off-policy RL in real-world scenarios. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Off-Policy Learning, Delayed Rewards, Temporal Credit Assignment, Reward Shaping.
