This paper introduces Distance-wise Graph Contrastive Learning, a novel approach to graph representation learning that leverages contrastive learning to capture nuanced distance relationships between nodes. The objective is to learn effective node embeddings that preserve both local and global structural information in graphs. Our method employs a distance-aware contrastive loss function that adaptively weighs the importance of node pairs based on their distance, allowing the model to focus on learning distinctive representations for nodes at varying distances. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art graph representation learning methods on several benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in node classification, graph classification, and link prediction tasks. The key contributions of this research lie in its ability to capture multi-scale structural information and adapt to diverse graph topologies, making it a valuable tool for graph analysis and applications such as recommendation systems, social network analysis, and bioinformatics. Key keywords: graph representation learning, contrastive learning, node embeddings, distance-aware loss function, graph neural networks.
