Low-light image enhancement remains a challenging task in computer vision, with poor illumination often leading to degraded visual quality and loss of crucial details. This paper presents a novel approach to low-light image enhancement that leverages both global and local context modeling to effectively restore image brightness, contrast, and structure. The proposed method employs a hybrid neural network architecture that integrates global attention mechanisms and local feature extraction modules. By modeling global context, our approach captures holistic scene information, while local context modeling focuses on preserving fine-grained textures and details. Experimental evaluations on standard low-light image datasets demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques in terms of both quantitative metrics (such as PSNR and SSIM) and subjective visual quality. Additionally, our approach exhibits robust generalization across diverse low-light scenarios and varying levels of illumination degradation. These results underscore the efficacy of combining global and local context for image enhancement and highlight the potential applications in smartphone photography, surveillance, and autonomous systems. Keywords: low-light image enhancement, global context modeling, local context modeling, neural networks, image restoration.
