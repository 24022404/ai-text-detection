Session-based recommendation systems play a crucial role in providing personalized user experiences on online platforms, particularly where user identity is ambiguous or sessions are transient. However, learning effective recommendation policies is challenging due to limited and biased historical interaction data, commonly known as the batch or offline recommendation setting. This paper proposes a novel framework, Batch-Constrained Distributional Reinforcement Learning (BCDRL), to address these challenges. The method extends classical reinforcement learning by modeling the distribution of potential returns for recommendation actions while explicitly constraining learned policies to remain close to the historical data distribution, thus reducing the risk of recommending out-of-distribution items. Our approach leverages state-of-the-art distributional reinforcement learning techniques and incorporates a batch-constrained policy optimization strategy tailored for session-based recommendation environments. Experimental evaluations on multiple public session-based recommendation benchmarks demonstrate that BCDRL surpasses existing reinforcement learning and supervised baseline methods in terms of recommendation accuracy and robustness, particularly under severe data sparsity and limited exploration scenarios. The results highlight the advantages of distributional modeling and batch constraints for enhancing policy learning and mitigating exposure bias. This research offers new insights into safe and effective session-based recommendation under realistic offline settings, with implications for e-commerce, streaming, and digital content platforms.  

Keywords: session-based recommendation, batch reinforcement learning, distributional reinforcement learning, offline recommendation, policy constraint, exposure bias
