This paper presents MnasNet, a novel framework for automated neural architecture search (NAS) tailored specifically for mobile platforms. The primary objective is to develop deep learning models that simultaneously achieve high accuracy and efficient inference on resource-constrained devices. To accomplish this, we propose a multi-objective NAS approach that directly incorporates real-time mobile latency into the search process, rather than relying solely on model size or theoretical metrics. Our method utilizes a reinforcement learning controller to explore and optimize a diverse range of layer types, kernel sizes, and connection patterns, evaluating candidate architectures based on both predictive performance and measured latency on target hardware. Experimental results demonstrate that MnasNet models significantly outperform existing state-of-the-art convolutional neural networks in terms of the trade-off between accuracy and inference speed, achieving up to 78.8% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet with real mobile latency reductions. These findings highlight the effectiveness of platform-aware NAS in designing efficient deep learning architectures for practical deployment. MnasNet offers a scalable solution for optimizing neural networks for diverse edge devices, with potential applications across mobile vision, real-time analytics, and AI-powered mobile services. Key keywords: neural architecture search, MnasNet, mobile AI, deep learning, model optimization, reinforcement learning, edge computing.
