This paper introduces Neural Predictive Belief Representations (NPBR), a novel framework for learning and representing beliefs about future states in complex environments. NPBR leverages neural networks to encode belief distributions conditioned on past observations and actions, enabling efficient prediction and planning. Our approach employs a variational autoencoder architecture trained to maximize the likelihood of future observations given the learned belief representation. This allows for the probabilistic modeling of environment dynamics and uncertainty, which is crucial for robust decision-making. We demonstrate the effectiveness of NPBR on a series of benchmark tasks, including robotic navigation and manipulation, showing superior performance compared to existing belief representation methods in terms of prediction accuracy and planning efficiency. Key findings indicate that NPBR can effectively capture complex dependencies between actions and future states, leading to improved long-term prediction and more informed decision-making. The proposed framework offers a promising direction for developing intelligent agents capable of reasoning and acting in uncertain environments.
Keywords: belief representation, neural networks, prediction, planning, variational autoencoder, robotics, reinforcement learning.
