This paper introduces Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) as a novel evaluation metric for Disturbance storm time (Dst) index forecasting using machine learning. Accurate Dst forecasting is crucial for space weather monitoring and prediction, yet traditional evaluation metrics like Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) often fail to capture the temporal alignment between predicted and observed Dst variations. We propose DTW, a sequence alignment technique, to quantify the similarity between Dst time series, emphasizing shape and temporal dynamics rather than pointwise error. We implement several machine learning models, including recurrent neural networks and support vector regression, and compare their performance using both RMSE and DTW on a benchmark Dst dataset. Results demonstrate that DTW provides a more robust and insightful assessment of forecasting accuracy, particularly in capturing the timing and duration of geomagnetic storms. Furthermore, DTW-optimized models exhibit improved performance in capturing critical storm phases. This work highlights the utility of DTW as an effective evaluation metric for time series forecasting in space weather and demonstrates its potential for enhancing the accuracy and reliability of Dst predictions. Keywords: Dst index, space weather, Dynamic Time Warping, machine learning, time series forecasting, evaluation metric.
