Anomaly detection remains a critical challenge in various domains, where the identification of rare or unusual instances is crucial for maintaining system integrity and security. This paper introduces a novel approach to anomaly detection leveraging Prototype-Guided Discriminative Latent Embeddings (PGDLE). The proposed method employs deep neural networks to map input data into a latent embedding space, where class prototypes are explicitly learned to represent normal data patterns. By incorporating a prototype-guided loss function, our model enforces both intra-class compactness and inter-class separability, enhancing the discriminative ability of the latent representations. Anomalies are then effectively identified based on their distance to the nearest class prototype in this space. Extensive experiments conducted on benchmark datasets from cybersecurity, image, and industrial IoT domains demonstrate that PGDLE achieves superior performance compared to traditional autoencoder-based and distance-based anomaly detection techniques. Results show notable improvements in detection accuracy, robustness to noise, and generalization to unseen anomaly types. The proposed framework is scalable and model-agnostic, making it adaptable to various data modalities. This research provides significant insights for advancing anomaly detection systems, with broad applications in fraud detection, network intrusion monitoring, and fault diagnosis.  
Keywords: anomaly detection, latent embeddings, prototype learning, deep learning, discriminative representations, outlier detection.
