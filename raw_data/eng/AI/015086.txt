This research addresses the challenge of zero-shot learning in visual speech recognition, aiming to recognize new classes of lip movements without prior exposure during training. This capability is crucial for advancing the adaptability and scalability of automated speech reading systems.

Methods/Approach: We propose an innovative approach utilizing Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to generate synthetic visual speech data for unseen classes. Our model leverages a generator to create realistic lip movement sequences based on text prompts, while a discriminator ensures these sequences are indistinguishable from authentic visual speech. This GAN-based framework enhances the learning of a zero-shot classifier, enabling it to generalize from visual features of known classes to new, unencountered categories.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our approach significantly improves zero-shot classification accuracy in visual speech recognition tasks compared to traditional methods. Our model demonstrates an ability to bridge the gap between source and target class domains, achieving notable performance on benchmark datasets. Moreover, it outperforms existing zero-shot learning techniques by a margin, showcasing the effectiveness of GAN-generated data in enhancing model robustness and generalization.

Conclusion/Implications: The implementation of GANs in zero-shot visual speech recognition presents a promising avenue for developing more flexible and intelligent speech reading systems. Our research contributes to the field by providing an effective platform for extending recognition capabilities to new classes without retraining. This advancement has potential applications in areas such as automated sign language interpretation, human-computer interaction, and assistive technologies for the hearing impaired.

Keywords: Zero-shot learning, Visual speech recognition, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Lip movement recognition, Synthetic data generation, Human-computer interaction.
