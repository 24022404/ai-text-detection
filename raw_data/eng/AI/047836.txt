Out-of-distribution (OOD) detection is essential for ensuring the reliability and safety of deep learning systems, particularly in real-world applications where unexpected data can lead to critical failures. This paper investigates the limitations and common failure modes of deep generative models, such as Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), in the context of OOD detection. We systematically analyze how these models assign likelihoods to both in-distribution and OOD samples, revealing scenarios where OOD inputs are incorrectly classified as likely, undermining detection effectiveness. Through comprehensive experiments on benchmark datasets, we evaluate various model architectures and scoring strategies, highlighting specific conditions and data characteristics that contribute to misclassification. Our findings show that factors such as model overfitting and the choice of likelihood estimation play significant roles in detection failures. Furthermore, we propose a set of diagnostic tools to better understand and visualize the decision boundaries of generative models. This study provides valuable insights into the intrinsic challenges of OOD detection with deep generative models and outlines practical guidelines for developing more robust and reliable AI systems. Key keywords: out-of-distribution detection, deep generative models, failure analysis, variational autoencoder, GAN, likelihood estimation, model robustness.
