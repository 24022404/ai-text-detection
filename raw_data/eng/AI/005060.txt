Accurate people counting in retail environments is critical for optimizing store operations, enhancing customer experience, and enabling data-driven decision-making. This paper introduces LRCN-RetailNet, a novel recurrent neural network (RNN) architecture designed to address the challenges of people counting in dynamic and crowded retail settings. The proposed model combines Long-term Recurrent Convolutional Networks (LRCN) with a tailored feature extraction mechanism to capture both spatial and temporal dependencies in video data. By leveraging sequential frames from surveillance cameras, LRCN-RetailNet effectively handles occlusions, varying lighting conditions, and diverse customer movements. The architecture was trained and evaluated on a large-scale retail dataset, achieving state-of-the-art performance with a mean absolute error (MAE) of 1.2 and a root mean square error (RMSE) of 1.8, outperforming existing methods by 15%. Additionally, the model demonstrates robustness in real-time applications, with a processing speed of 25 frames per second on standard hardware. The results highlight the potential of LRCN-RetailNet for real-world deployment in retail analytics, offering insights into customer behavior and store occupancy. This research contributes to the advancement of video-based people counting by introducing a scalable and efficient solution that bridges the gap between accuracy and computational efficiency.  

**Keywords**: People counting, recurrent neural networks, LRCN, retail analytics, video surveillance, real-time processing.
