The concept of intrinsic dimension is a fundamental property in manifold learning, pattern recognition, and high-dimensional data analysis, serving to identify the effective dimensionality of a dataset embedded in a higher-dimensional space. This research addresses the problem of accurately estimating the intrinsic dimension, a task crucial for reducing computational costs and enhancing the performance of machine learning algorithms on complex datasets.

Methods: We introduce a novel approach for intrinsic dimension estimation that leverages a combination of topology-based techniques and advanced statistical methods. Our algorithm efficiently captures the underlying data structure by implementing a refined version of neighborhood-preserving methods and robust statistical estimators. Additionally, it incorporates sampling strategies optimized for high-dimensional datasets, enhancing the robustness and scalability of the estimation process.

Results: Our experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing intrinsic dimension estimation techniques in both accuracy and computational efficiency. Comparative analyses show significant improvements in scenarios involving noisy and high-dimensional datasets, where traditional methods often falter. The algorithm's performance is validated across several benchmark datasets, showing superior adaptability and precision.

Conclusion: This research provides a significant contribution to the field of data analysis by offering an innovative and efficient solution for intrinsic dimension estimation. The proposed method not only accelerates the preprocessing phase in large-scale data analysis but also holds potential applications in optimizing neural network architectures, improving data clustering techniques, and enhancing dimensionality reduction methods. Further exploration will examine the applicability in dynamic environments and streaming data contexts.

Keywords: Intrinsic Dimension, Manifold Learning, High-Dimensional Data, Statistical Estimation, Machine Learning, Dimensionality Reduction.
