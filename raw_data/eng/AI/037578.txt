Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data, particularly in scenarios where acquiring labeled data is costly or time-consuming. This paper presents a comprehensive survey on SSL techniques tailored for delayed partially labeled data streams, a common challenge in real-world applications such as IoT, healthcare, and financial systems. The objective of this study is to explore and evaluate existing methods that address the unique constraints of delayed and incomplete labeling in streaming environments. We systematically review state-of-the-art approaches, including adaptive algorithms, ensemble methods, and hybrid models that integrate SSL with online learning frameworks. Key findings highlight the effectiveness of these methods in handling concept drift, reducing labeling costs, and improving prediction accuracy under delayed feedback. Furthermore, we identify critical gaps in current research, such as scalability issues and the lack of benchmarks for real-time applications. The survey concludes with insights into future directions, emphasizing the need for robust, scalable, and interpretable SSL solutions for delayed data streams. This work contributes to advancing the field by providing a structured overview of existing techniques and offering practical recommendations for researchers and practitioners.  

**Keywords**: Semi-supervised learning, data streams, delayed labeling, online learning, concept drift, adaptive algorithms.
