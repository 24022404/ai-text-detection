Abstract:

Crowdsourcing has emerged as a practical solution for annotating large datasets, particularly in multi-class classification problems. Traditional approaches often focus on categorical labeling, but yes-no type crowdsourcing—where contributors answer simple binary (yes/no) questions about class membership—has gained attention for its simplicity and scalability. This paper presents a full probabilistic model specifically designed for yes-no type crowdsourcing in the context of multi-class classification. Our model captures both worker reliability and inherent task difficulty by treating worker responses as probabilistic events conditioned on class labels and worker-specific parameters. We propose an efficient expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm for parameter estimation, enabling robust inference even with sparse and noisy responses. Through extensive experiments on simulated and real-world datasets, our approach demonstrates superior accuracy and robustness compared to traditional majority voting and existing probabilistic aggregation methods. The proposed model provides interpretable confidence scores for each classification and is adaptable to varying levels of annotation granularity. These results highlight the model’s potential for effective, large-scale data annotation in applications such as machine learning, natural language processing, and image recognition. Keywords: crowdsourcing, probabilistic modeling, yes-no labeling, multi-class classification, aggregation, expectation-maximization, worker reliability.
