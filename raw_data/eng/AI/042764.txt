Skin detection is a critical preprocessing step in various computer vision applications, including face recognition, gesture analysis, and medical imaging. This study presents a comparative analysis of statistical skin detection algorithms tailored for sub-continental human images, which exhibit unique skin tone variations due to diverse ethnic backgrounds. The objective is to evaluate the performance of widely used algorithms, including Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM), Bayesian classifiers, and histogram-based methods, in accurately segmenting skin regions under varying lighting conditions and complex backgrounds. A dataset comprising sub-continental human images was curated and preprocessed to ensure diversity in skin tones and environmental settings. Each algorithm was tested for precision, recall, and computational efficiency. Results indicate that the GMM-based approach outperforms others in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly in handling illumination variations and occlusions. However, histogram-based methods demonstrated superior computational efficiency, making them suitable for real-time applications. The findings highlight the trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency, providing valuable insights for selecting appropriate algorithms based on application requirements. This study contributes to the field by addressing the challenges specific to sub-continental skin detection and offering a benchmark for future research in this domain.  

**Keywords**: skin detection, Gaussian Mixture Models, Bayesian classifiers, histogram-based methods, sub-continental images, computer vision.
