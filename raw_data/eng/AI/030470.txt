The aim of this research is to address the challenges of moving object detection in event-based vision systems, which offer advantages such as high temporal resolution and low latency over conventional vision systems. Traditional methods often struggle with the asynchronous and sparse nature of event data, necessitating effective and efficient detection strategies.

Methods/Approach: In this study, we propose a novel methodology that leverages k-means clustering for detecting moving objects within event-based data. By clustering events based on spatial and temporal coherence, the proposed method groups event data into meaningful clusters corresponding to moving objects. This approach capitalizes on the inherent properties of event-based data to enhance detection accuracy and processing speed, making it suitable for real-time applications.

Results/Findings: The experimental evaluation demonstrates that our method significantly outperforms traditional frame-based approaches in scenarios with dynamic lighting conditions and rapid motion. Quantitative results highlight improved detection rates and lower false positives, while also maintaining computational efficiency. Comparative analysis against existing event-based detection techniques shows competitive performance, indicating robustness and adaptability of the proposed clustering-based approach. 

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a novel clustering-based framework to the field of event-based vision, offering a robust solution for moving object detection. The implications of this work extend to various applications, including autonomous vehicles, surveillance, and robotics, where rapid and accurate detection is critical. By effectively utilizing the k-means algorithm, this study lays the groundwork for future development of more sophisticated event-based vision solutions that can operate in complex real-world environments.

Keywords: event-based vision, moving object detection, k-means clustering, real-time processing, asynchronous data, robotics, autonomous vehicles.
