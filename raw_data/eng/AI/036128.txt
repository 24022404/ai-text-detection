In the realm of autonomous systems and augmented reality, Visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) plays a pivotal role in navigating and understanding environments. This paper addresses the critical issue of privacy preservation in Visual SLAM systems, which has become increasingly important as these technologies are integrated into public and private spaces. The objective of the research is to enhance the standard Visual SLAM framework with novel privacy-preserving mechanisms to protect sensitive visual information from being exposed or misused. Our approach employs a hybrid model that combines advanced encryption techniques with real-time data processing algorithms to anonymize sensitive image features without compromising the system's overall performance and accuracy. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate the method's effectiveness, showing comparable localization and mapping accuracy to conventional Visual SLAM systems while significantly enhancing data privacy. The findings reveal that our privacy-preserving Visual SLAM system can be implemented on existing hardware with minimal computational overhead, making it a viable solution for practical applications. The research contributes to the field by offering a robust framework that balances the need for efficient SLAM practices and stringent privacy requirements, paving the way for safer deployment of autonomous technologies in public domains. Keywords include Visual SLAM, privacy preservation, encryption, autonomous systems, and real-time processing.
