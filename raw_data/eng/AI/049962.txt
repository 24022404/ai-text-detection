Graph representation learning has emerged as a critical area in machine learning, enabling the analysis of complex relational data across various domains. However, scaling graph spectral learning to large datasets remains a significant challenge due to computational and memory constraints. This paper introduces GRASPEL, a novel framework for efficient and scalable graph spectral learning. GRASPEL leverages a combination of spectral graph theory and advanced optimization techniques to reduce computational complexity while preserving the structural properties of graphs. Our approach employs a distributed, parallelized architecture to handle large-scale graphs, enabling real-time processing and analysis. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that GRASPEL achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of accuracy and scalability, outperforming existing methods by up to 30% in runtime efficiency. Furthermore, GRASPEL's modular design allows seamless integration with downstream tasks such as node classification, link prediction, and community detection. The framework's ability to process graphs with millions of nodes and edges makes it a valuable tool for applications in social networks, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems. This work contributes to the advancement of graph machine learning by providing a scalable, efficient, and versatile solution for spectral graph analysis.  

**Keywords**: graph representation learning, spectral graph theory, scalability, distributed computing, machine learning, large-scale graphs.
