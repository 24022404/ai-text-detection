This paper introduces Adaptive Symmetric Reward Noising (ASRN), a novel technique to enhance the stability and efficiency of reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms. Traditional RL approaches often suffer from slow convergence and susceptibility to local optima due to deterministic reward signals or overfitting to specific reward structures. ASRN addresses this limitation by dynamically injecting symmetric noise into reward signals, effectively encouraging broader exploration and mitigating the risk of premature policy convergence. The proposed approach adapts the noise magnitude based on the agent’s learning progress, maintaining a balance between exploration and exploitation throughout training. We evaluate ASRN across a suite of continuous control benchmarks and compare its performance with standard RL baselines and other reward perturbation methods. Experimental results demonstrate that ASRN consistently improves learning speed, solution diversity, and robustness to sparse or deceptive rewards. This method is agnostic to underlying RL architectures and requires minimal additional computation. Our findings suggest that adaptive reward noising can be a practical enhancement for a range of RL tasks, paving the way for more reliable deployment in real-world applications. Keywords: reinforcement learning, reward noising, exploration, adaptive algorithms, policy optimization.
