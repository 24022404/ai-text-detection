This paper presents a novel approach for automated road curb extraction from mobile LiDAR point clouds, addressing the challenges of efficient and accurate infrastructure mapping. The proposed method integrates a voxel-based filtering technique to reduce noise and data density, followed by a refined point cloud segmentation algorithm leveraging geometric features and spatial relationships to identify potential curb points. A Random Sample Consensus (RANSAC)-based model fitting is then applied to extract precise curb lines. The algorithm was tested on mobile LiDAR data collected from diverse urban and suburban environments. Experimental results demonstrate high accuracy in curb detection, achieving an average F1-score of 92%, and significant reduction in processing time compared to existing methods. The extracted curb information can be valuable for various applications, including high-definition map generation, autonomous driving, and urban planning. Key words: Mobile LiDAR, point cloud processing, road curb extraction, RANSAC, infrastructure mapping, segmentation.
