In the realm of 3D point cloud processing, adversarial attacks pose significant challenges by perturbing data to deceive AI models, leading to compromised analysis and interpretation. This paper introduces DUP-Net, a novel defense mechanism aimed at safeguarding 3D point cloud representations from adversarial attacks through efficient denoising and upsampling techniques.

Methods/Approach: DUP-Net comprises two main components: a denoiser that mitigates the adversarial noise from 3D point clouds and an upsampler that restores the point cloud's original structure by increasing its resolution. Leveraging a deep learning architecture, the network integrates spatial and structural features to accurately discern and rectify adversarial discrepancies. The network's efficacy was validated using a comprehensive set of adversarial point cloud datasets under various attack scenarios.

Results/Findings: Our experiments indicate that DUP-Net significantly enhances the robustness of 3D point cloud classifiers against adversarial threats, achieving notable improvements in accuracy and resistance to attacks compared to existing defense methods. Specifically, DUP-Net demonstrated an average accuracy improvement of over 15% across multiple benchmark datasets, showcasing its effectiveness and reliability in adversarial defenses.

Conclusion/Implications: DUP-Net represents a substantial advancement in securing 3D point cloud models against adversarial manipulation, offering a practical solution with a broad range of applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality. This research contributes a critical tool for enhancing the resilience of systems relying on 3D data, setting the foundation for future exploration into advanced defense strategies for 3D adversarial point clouds.

Keywords: DUP-Net, adversarial point clouds, denoising, upsampling, 3D data defense, deep learning.
