The growing complexity of machine learning (ML) models has heightened the need for interpretability and explainability in decision-making processes. This paper provides a comprehensive mini-tour of interpretability and explainability techniques across a diverse range of ML models, referred to as the "Machine Learning Zoo." The objective is to systematically categorize and evaluate methods that enhance the transparency of ML systems, enabling users to understand and trust their outputs. We explore approaches such as feature importance analysis, rule-based explanations, and visualization techniques, tailored to specific model types including decision trees, neural networks, and ensemble methods. Our findings highlight the trade-offs between model complexity and interpretability, demonstrating that simpler models often provide more intuitive explanations, while advanced techniques like SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) and LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) offer robust solutions for complex models. The study concludes with practical insights into selecting appropriate interpretability tools based on application requirements, emphasizing the importance of balancing accuracy and transparency. This work contributes to the ongoing discourse on responsible AI by providing a structured overview of interpretability methods, aiding researchers and practitioners in building more trustworthy ML systems.  

**Keywords**: interpretability, explainability, machine learning, SHAP, LIME, transparency, responsible AI.
