This paper addresses the challenge of achieving photorealistic style transfer in image processing, a task that involves blending the artistic style of one image with the content of another while maintaining a high degree of realism. Traditional methods often struggle to preserve fine details and textures, leading to artifacts or unnatural results. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel approach leveraging wavelet transforms, which decompose images into multi-scale frequency components. Our method separates content and style features at different scales, enabling precise control over the transfer process while preserving critical structural details. We introduce a wavelet-based neural network architecture that optimizes style transfer by combining low-frequency style elements with high-frequency content details. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of visual quality, detail preservation, and computational efficiency. Comparisons with state-of-the-art techniques highlight significant improvements in photorealism and user satisfaction. The proposed method has potential applications in digital art, augmented reality, and image editing, offering a robust solution for realistic style transfer. Key contributions include the integration of wavelet transforms into style transfer frameworks and the development of a scalable, detail-preserving algorithm.  

**Keywords**: photorealistic style transfer, wavelet transforms, image processing, neural networks, multi-scale decomposition, digital art.
