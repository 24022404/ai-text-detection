A Printed Circuit Board (PCB), as illustrated in figure 1.1, is a sophisticated, multi-layered, non-conductive substrate designed to interconnect electronic components internally, eliminating the need for external wires and thus simplifying the overall circuitry. This internal connectivity not only reduces the complexity typically associated with traditional wiring methods but also enhances the reliability and performance of electronic systems. Due to these distinct advantages, PCBs have become an essential foundation in a vast range of applications, appearing in everyday devices such as televisions, mobile phones, and computers, as well as playing a pivotal role in specialized fields including medical equipment, industrial machinery, and the automotive industry. Moreover, the design of PCBs can be precisely customized to meet specific user requirements, enabling tailored solutions for diverse technological challenges and continual advancements in electronic device functionality.
