The rapidly evolving field of reinforcement learning (RL) often confronts the challenge of defining suitable reward functions to guide the learning process. Intrinsic motivation, a concept borrowed from cognitive sciences, has emerged as a promising approach to enhance RL by fostering agents that can learn efficiently without explicit external rewards. This paper aims to provide a comprehensive survey of intrinsic motivation mechanisms in reinforcement learning, examining their principles, methodologies, and applications.

Methods/Approach: We systematically review the existing literature on intrinsic motivation in RL, categorizing the various approaches based on their underlying mechanisms such as curiosity-driven models, empowerment, novelty-seeking, and uncertainty-driven exploration. Key theoretical frameworks and algorithms are analyzed to identify the core components that contribute to the effectiveness of intrinsically motivated RL systems.

Results/Findings: The survey reveals several advancements in the use of intrinsic motivation, highlighting its role in improving exploration efficiency, faster convergence, and generalization in complex environments. Moreover, comparisons with traditional RL methods demonstrate that intrinsic motivation can significantly reduce the reliance on hand-crafted reward signals, making RL more adaptive and scalable.

Conclusion/Implications: This survey underscores the importance of intrinsic motivation as a transformative element in reinforcement learning. It provides insights into its potential to revolutionize autonomous systems by enabling them to learn more human-like strategies. The paper also identifies current challenges and proposes future research directions to further leverage intrinsic motivation in enhancing the capabilities of RL agents. Keywords such as intrinsic motivation, reinforcement learning, exploration strategies, and curiosity-driven learning are emphasized to facilitate discovery in scholarly communications.
