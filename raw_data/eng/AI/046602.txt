This paper addresses the challenge of few-shot learning, where the goal is to recognize new classes with only a few labeled examples. Traditional few-shot learning approaches often struggle to capture relationships among examples, limiting generalization capabilities. We introduce Relational Generalized Few-Shot Learning (RGFSL), which aims to enhance model understanding through relational representations, enabling better performance on a broader set of tasks with minimal data.

Methods/Approach: The proposed RGFSL framework employs a relational module that constructs dynamic graphs to capture dependencies among input examples. Utilizing a graph neural network (GNN) architecture, this module effectively propagates information, allowing the model to make more informed predictions. This approach goes beyond static feature embedding methods by iteratively refining relationships during training and inference. To validate our approach, we conduct experiments using multiple benchmark datasets, comparing its performance against existing few-shot learning techniques.

Results/Findings: Our findings demonstrate that RGFSL significantly outperforms state-of-the-art models in few-shot tasks, achieving superior accuracy and robustness across different datasets. The model excels particularly in generalized settings, where class distributions and instance-level relationships are diverse. Notably, RGFSL shows marked improvements in both 5-way 1-shot and 5-way 5-shot tasks, confirming its capability to generalize from limited data effectively.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of relational processing in few-shot learning offers a promising direction for enhancing adaptability and generalization in AI models. By incorporating relational knowledge, RGFSL contributes to more versatile applications, including autonomous systems, personalized services, and other environments needing rapid adaptation with minimal input. This research outlines a novel pathway for advancing few-shot learning through relational understanding, setting the stage for future innovations in machine learning model design.

Key Keywords: few-shot learning, relational learning, graph neural networks, few-shot classification, generalized few-shot tasks.
