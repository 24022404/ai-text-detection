This paper introduces Deep Distributed Random Samplings (DDRS), a novel supervised learning algorithm designed as a potential alternative to Random Forests. DDRS leverages a distributed framework to generate multiple random sub-samples of the training data, which are then processed by independent deep neural networks. This approach aims to capture diverse data perspectives and improve generalization performance. The method uses a unique aggregation strategy to combine predictions from individual deep networks. We evaluate DDRS on several benchmark datasets, comparing its performance against Random Forests and other state-of-the-art supervised learning techniques. Results demonstrate that DDRS achieves comparable or superior accuracy, particularly on high-dimensional datasets, while offering enhanced scalability due to its distributed nature. Furthermore, we analyze the impact of different sampling strategies and network architectures on the overall performance of DDRS. The findings suggest that DDRS represents a promising direction for scalable and accurate supervised learning. Keywords: Deep Learning, Distributed Learning, Random Sampling, Supervised Learning, Random Forests, Ensemble Methods.
