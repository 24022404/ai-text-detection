This paper addresses the pervasive issue of sparsity bias in training neural networks, where missing values are often imputed with zero. Our objective is to investigate the limitations of zero imputation and propose alternative methods to correct sparsity bias. We employ a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical experiments to demonstrate the shortcomings of zero imputation, which can lead to biased models and poor generalization performance. Our approach involves developing novel imputation strategies that account for the underlying data distribution, leveraging techniques from robust statistics and machine learning. Our results show that these alternative methods significantly outperform zero imputation in various benchmark datasets, resulting in improved model accuracy and reduced sparsity bias. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of careful handling of missing values in neural network training, with implications for applications in image and speech recognition, natural language processing, and recommender systems. By correcting sparsity bias, our work contributes to the development of more robust and reliable neural network models, with potential applications in areas such as deep learning, AI, and data science, using keywords such as neural networks, sparsity bias, zero imputation, robust statistics, and machine learning.
