Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have revolutionized the field of generative modeling by enabling the synthesis of high-quality, realistic data. However, challenges such as mode collapse and unstable training dynamics persist, limiting their effectiveness in certain applications. This paper introduces a novel approach, *Reparameterized Sampling for GANs*, which addresses these limitations by redefining the sampling process during training. Our method leverages a reparameterization technique to enhance the diversity and stability of generated samples, ensuring better coverage of the data distribution. We propose a modified training framework that integrates this sampling strategy with existing GAN architectures, enabling seamless adoption without significant computational overhead. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves superior performance in terms of sample quality, diversity, and training stability compared to state-of-the-art GAN variants. Furthermore, the proposed method shows significant improvements in applications such as image synthesis and data augmentation. This work contributes to the advancement of generative modeling by providing a robust and efficient solution to key challenges in GAN training, with potential implications for fields such as computer vision, natural language processing, and beyond.  

**Keywords**: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), reparameterized sampling, generative modeling, mode collapse, training stability, image synthesis.
