This paper addresses the challenge of color constancy in digital imaging, a fundamental issue that affects visual consistency across varying illumination conditions. The aim is to develop a novel approach that enhances color constancy performance leveraging the concept of "Bag of Features," specifically tailored towards color information.

Methods/Approach: We propose a method called Bag of Color Features (BoCF), which involves representing images as a collection of quantized color features. This approach extracts significant color attributes from images and aggregates them to perform a robust color constancy correction. The algorithm utilizes statistical color distributions and machine learning techniques to adaptively adjust colors in images, effectively neutralizing the impact of different lighting conditions.

Results/Findings: The proposed BoCF method exhibits substantial improvements over traditional color constancy approaches. Experimental results demonstrate enhanced performance in achieving color stability and neutrality across diverse lighting scenarios. The evaluation involved extensive testing on established benchmarks, where BoCF consistently outperformed existing methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency.

Conclusion/Implications: This research presents a significant advancement in the field of color constancy by introducing a feature-based framework that is both effective and computationally manageable. The implications of this work extend to various applications, including digital photography, autonomous systems, and any visual computing tasks requiring consistent color rendering. The BoCF approach sets a new benchmark for future research aiming to refine color constancy capabilities.

Keywords: Color Constancy, Bag of Features, Image Processing, Color Correction, Machine Learning, Visual Computing.
