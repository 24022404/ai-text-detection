This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised domain adaptation for stereo matching, a crucial task in computer vision. The objective is to develop a geometry-aware approach that enables accurate depth estimation across different domains without requiring labeled target domain data. Our method leverages a novel combination of geometric constraints and adversarial training to align the source and target domains. The approach utilizes a transformer-based architecture to model the spatial relationships between pixels and incorporates a geometry-aware loss function to preserve the structural information. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, outperforming state-of-the-art methods on benchmark datasets. The key findings show improved depth estimation accuracy and robustness to domain shifts. This research contributes to the field of computer vision by introducing a innovative unsupervised domain adaptation technique, with potential applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and 3D reconstruction. Key keywords: stereo matching, unsupervised domain adaptation, geometry-aware, transformer, depth estimation, computer vision.
