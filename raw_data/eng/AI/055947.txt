Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) aims to transfer knowledge from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain, addressing the challenge of domain shift. This paper proposes a novel framework, Domain-Invariant Adversarial Learning (DIAL), to enhance the generalization of models across domains. The objective is to minimize the discrepancy between source and target distributions while preserving task-specific discriminative features. DIAL employs an adversarial learning approach, where a domain discriminator is trained to distinguish between source and target domains, while the feature extractor is optimized to produce domain-invariant representations. Additionally, a consistency regularization mechanism is introduced to ensure robustness against domain-specific perturbations. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including Office-31 and VisDA, demonstrate that DIAL outperforms existing UDA methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance in cross-domain classification tasks. The results highlight the effectiveness of adversarial learning in reducing domain shift and improving model adaptability. This work contributes to advancing UDA by providing a scalable and efficient solution for real-world applications where labeled target data is scarce. Key innovations include the integration of adversarial training with consistency regularization, offering a robust framework for domain adaptation. Keywords: unsupervised domain adaptation, adversarial learning, domain-invariant features, consistency regularization, cross-domain classification.
