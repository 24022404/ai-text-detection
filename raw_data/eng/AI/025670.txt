Graph-based machine learning models have shown significant promise in various applications, yet their performance heavily relies on the quality and structure of the input graph data. This paper introduces **GABO (Graph Augmentations with Bi-level Optimization)**, a novel framework designed to enhance graph representation learning through automated and adaptive graph augmentation. The primary objective of GABO is to address the challenge of suboptimal graph structures by dynamically optimizing augmentations that improve downstream task performance.  

The proposed approach employs a **bi-level optimization strategy**, where the outer loop optimizes the augmentation parameters to maximize task-specific performance, while the inner loop trains the graph neural network (GNN) on the augmented graph. This dual-level optimization ensures that the augmentations are tailored to both the graph structure and the learning task. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that GABO significantly outperforms existing graph augmentation methods, achieving state-of-the-art results in node classification and link prediction tasks.  

The key contributions of this work include: (1) a systematic framework for adaptive graph augmentation, (2) a bi-level optimization approach that bridges graph structure and task performance, and (3) empirical validation of GABO's effectiveness across diverse datasets. The findings highlight the potential of GABO to advance graph-based learning in domains such as social network analysis, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems.  

**Keywords**: Graph Neural Networks, Graph Augmentation, Bi-level Optimization, Representation Learning, Adaptive Learning.
