This paper addresses the challenge of lesion segmentation on CT scans using a weakly-supervised co-segmentation approach. The objective is to develop an accurate and efficient method for segmenting lesions from CT scans with minimal manual annotation. Our approach utilizes a novel co-segmentation framework that leverages both image-level and patch-level information to guide the segmentation process. The method employs a deep neural network architecture that combines the strengths of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to model the complex relationships between lesions and their surrounding tissues. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance on a benchmark dataset with significant reductions in annotation requirements. The key findings of this study highlight the potential of weakly-supervised co-segmentation for lesion segmentation on CT scans, enabling faster and more accurate diagnosis and treatment of diseases. The proposed method contributes to the field of medical image analysis, particularly in the context of computer-aided diagnosis and therapy planning, with implications for improved patient outcomes and reduced healthcare costs. Key keywords: weakly-supervised learning, co-segmentation, lesion segmentation, CT scans, deep learning, medical image analysis.
