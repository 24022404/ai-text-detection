Accurate segmentation and anatomical landmarking are critical tasks in medical image analysis, enabling precise diagnosis and treatment planning. This paper introduces a novel deep learning framework, Deep Geodesic Learning (DGL), designed to address these challenges by leveraging geodesic distance metrics within a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture. The proposed approach integrates geodesic information to enhance the spatial understanding of anatomical structures, improving segmentation accuracy and landmark localization. DGL employs a multi-task learning strategy, combining pixel-wise segmentation with geodesic regression to simultaneously predict anatomical boundaries and key landmarks. Experiments were conducted on publicly available medical imaging datasets, including brain MRI and chest X-rays, demonstrating that DGL outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both segmentation and landmarking tasks. Quantitative results show significant improvements in Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) and landmark localization error (LLE) metrics. The framework’s ability to generalize across diverse anatomical structures highlights its potential for clinical applications, such as automated diagnosis and surgical planning. This work contributes a robust, interpretable, and efficient solution for medical image analysis, advancing the integration of geometric priors into deep learning models.  

**Keywords**: Deep learning, geodesic distance, medical image segmentation, anatomical landmarking, convolutional neural networks, multi-task learning.
