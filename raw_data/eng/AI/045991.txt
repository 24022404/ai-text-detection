Video summarization is a critical task in multimedia analysis, aiming to condense lengthy videos into concise, informative summaries. Traditional methods often rely on recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or convolutional architectures, which struggle with capturing long-range dependencies and temporal dynamics efficiently. This paper introduces a novel self-attention-based approach for video summarization, leveraging Universal Transformers to address these limitations. The proposed model employs a self-attention mechanism to process video frames, enabling the capture of both local and global temporal relationships without the need for recurrent structures. By integrating positional encodings and adaptive computation time, the model dynamically adjusts its focus on salient frames, ensuring robust and context-aware summarization. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art techniques in terms of summarization accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight the effectiveness of self-attention mechanisms in handling complex video data, offering a scalable and generalizable solution for video summarization tasks. This research contributes to advancing video analysis by providing a transformer-based framework that is both efficient and adaptable, with potential applications in video indexing, surveillance, and content recommendation systems.  

**Keywords**: Video summarization, self-attention, Universal Transformers, temporal modeling, multimedia analysis.
