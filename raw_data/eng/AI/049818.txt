This paper presents a novel approach to reinforcement learning (RL) by integrating masked contrastive representation learning. The objective is to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of RL agents in complex environments by learning robust and generalizable state representations. Our method, termed Masked Contrastive Representation Learning (MCRL), leverages contrastive learning to learn representations that are invariant to irrelevant factors and focused on essential state information. By applying a masking mechanism to the input state, MCRL encourages the agent to learn from partial observations, enhancing its ability to generalize across different environments and scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate that MCRL outperforms existing RL methods in various benchmark tasks, achieving significant improvements in sample efficiency and asymptotic performance. The key findings highlight the importance of representation learning in RL and demonstrate the potential of MCRL as a powerful tool for improving the performance of RL agents. This research contributes to the development of more efficient and effective RL algorithms, with potential applications in robotics, game playing, and other areas where RL is applied. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, contrastive learning, representation learning, masked learning, deep learning.
