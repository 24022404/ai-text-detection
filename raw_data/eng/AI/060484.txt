This paper introduces a novel approach to Time-Travel Rephotography, a technique that enables the seamless recreation of historical photographs from the perspective of present-day scenes. Our objective is to bridge temporal gaps by leveraging advanced computer vision and image synthesis methods, allowing users to visualize how a particular location has evolved over time. The proposed method integrates deep learning-based image alignment, semantic segmentation, and 3D scene reconstruction to accurately match historical images with contemporary viewpoints. By employing feature correspondence and robust geometric transformations, our system adapts the visual characteristics of past photographic records and aligns them spatially with modern images captured from different devices and angles. Experimental results on diverse datasets demonstrate significant improvements in alignment accuracy and visual fidelity compared to conventional rephotography techniques. The resulting rephotographs enable compelling side-by-side, overlay, and blended visualizations, facilitating a deeper understanding of urban change, cultural heritage, and temporal dynamics. Our research provides an effective tool for historians, urban planners, and digital archivists, fostering new opportunities for interactive exploration of time-varying imagery. Key contributions include the introduction of a comprehensive pipeline for automatic time-travel rephotography, empirical evidence of its superiority over baselines, and its applicability to a wide range of photographic archives. Keywords: time-travel rephotography, image alignment, 3D scene reconstruction, computer vision, historical imagery, visual localization.
