Super-resolution networks have gained significant attention for their ability to enhance image quality by reconstructing high-resolution images from low-resolution inputs. However, interpreting the decision-making processes of these networks remains a challenge, limiting their transparency and trustworthiness. This paper introduces a novel approach to interpret super-resolution networks using Local Attribution Maps (LAMs), which highlight the regions of an input image that most influence the network's output. Our method leverages gradient-based techniques to generate attribution maps, providing insights into how super-resolution networks prioritize and process spatial information. We evaluate the proposed approach on benchmark datasets and demonstrate its effectiveness in identifying critical features that drive the network's performance. The results reveal that LAMs not only enhance interpretability but also uncover potential biases and limitations in existing super-resolution models. This work contributes to the growing field of explainable AI by offering a practical tool for analyzing and improving super-resolution networks, with implications for applications in medical imaging, satellite imagery, and multimedia processing. Keywords: super-resolution, interpretability, local attribution maps, explainable AI, image enhancement.
