Lighting estimation is a critical challenge in computer vision and graphics, particularly for applications such as augmented reality and 3D scene reconstruction. This paper introduces *GMLight*, a novel framework for accurate lighting estimation through geometric distribution approximation. Unlike traditional methods that rely on complex physical models or extensive datasets, *GMLight* leverages a lightweight geometric approach to approximate light distribution in 3D scenes. The proposed method employs a multi-stage pipeline that first extracts geometric features from the scene, then models light sources using a probabilistic distribution framework, and finally refines the estimation through an optimization process. Experimental results demonstrate that *GMLight* achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, outperforming existing methods on benchmark datasets. The framework is also shown to generalize well across diverse lighting conditions and scene geometries. The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to provide robust lighting estimation with minimal computational overhead, making it suitable for real-time applications. This research opens new avenues for integrating geometric insights into lighting estimation, offering significant potential for advancements in augmented reality, virtual environments, and 3D rendering technologies.  

**Keywords**: lighting estimation, geometric distribution, 3D reconstruction, augmented reality, computer vision, real-time systems.
