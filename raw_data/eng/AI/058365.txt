Scale-free networks, characterized by a power-law degree distribution, are prevalent in diverse real-world systems. This paper introduces a novel approach to learning scale-free networks by dynamically adjusting node-specific degree priors during network construction. Unlike existing methods that rely on global parameters or static preferential attachment, our method adaptively tunes each node’s probability of attachment based on its evolving degree and local network structure. This dynamic adjustment facilitates the emergence of a power-law degree distribution without explicitly enforcing it. We evaluate our method on synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its superior performance in generating networks with tunable scale-free properties compared to traditional models like the Barabási-Albert model and its variants. The resulting networks exhibit improved structural properties and robustness. Our findings contribute to a deeper understanding of network formation mechanisms and offer a valuable tool for generating realistic network models for simulations and analysis. Keywords: Scale-free network, network modeling, preferential attachment, degree distribution, dynamic networks, complex networks.
