Objective: Trajectory optimization is critical for autonomous systems but traditional iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) methods often struggle with model inaccuracies, limited adaptability, and high computational cost. This paper introduces Neural Network iLQR, a novel reinforcement learning (RL) architecture that integrates differentiable iLQR into an end-to-end neural policy framework to overcome these limitations.

Methods/Approach: Our approach embeds a learnable system dynamics model and cost-to-go approximator within an RL loop. At each timestep, the neural network predicts local dynamics and cost gradients, which are fed into a differentiable iLQR module to generate optimal control increments. The combined architecture is trained via policy gradient methods, enabling simultaneous refinement of the dynamics model, cost approximation, and control policy. We evaluate stability and convergence through Lyapunov-inspired constraints and employ batch rollouts for efficient gradient estimation.

Results/Findings: Experiments on benchmark tasks—including cart-pole swing-up, planar quadrotor navigation, and a 7-DOF manipulator—demonstrate that Neural Network iLQR achieves up to 35% lower cumulative cost and 40% faster convergence compared to baseline iLQR and model-free RL methods. The learned policies exhibit robustness to modeling errors and external disturbances, showing improved sample efficiency and generalization to unseen operating conditions.

Conclusion/Implications: Neural Network iLQR bridges model-based and model-free control by leveraging neural approximators for dynamics and cost within a principled optimal control solver. This hybrid architecture offers a scalable, data-efficient solution for real-time trajectory optimization in robotics and autonomous vehicles.  
Keywords: trajectory optimization, reinforcement learning, iLQR, neural network, model-based control.
