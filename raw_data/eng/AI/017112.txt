Reinforcement learning agents often face the challenge of reward perturbations and inconsistencies, which can lead to suboptimal policy performance. This research aims to address this problem by investigating the robustness of regularized policies against such reward instabilities.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel approach that incorporates regularization techniques into the policy optimization process to enhance robustness. This method integrates L1 and L2 regularization frameworks within policy gradient algorithms, balancing exploration and exploitation while minimizing sensitivity to varying reward signals. Additionally, a comparative analysis with traditional policy gradient methods without regularization is conducted to assess performance differences.

Results/Findings: Our experiments, conducted across multiple simulated environments, demonstrate that regularized policies exhibit superior robustness to reward perturbations compared to their non-regularized counterparts. The proposed approach maintains stability and consistent performance, even with significant alterations in reward structures. Quantitative results highlight an improvement in policy resilience, evidenced by higher accumulated rewards and reduced variance across trials.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings underscore the significance of incorporating regularization techniques into reinforcement learning agents to achieve robust behavior in dynamic environments. These insights have potent implications for real-world applications where stable and reliable policy performance is crucial, such as autonomous control systems and robotic operations. The study paves the way for future exploration into more advanced regularization strategies, further enhancing the adaptability and effectiveness of reinforcement learning frameworks.

Keywords: regularized policies, reward robustness, reinforcement learning, policy gradient, L1 regularization, L2 regularization, policy optimization.
