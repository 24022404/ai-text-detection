This paper investigates the phenomenon of minority collapse in deep neural networks trained on imbalanced datasets. Our objective is to understand the underlying mechanisms that lead to the degradation of minority class performance. To achieve this, we propose a novel layer-peeled model that allows for the systematic analysis of neural network behavior at each layer. Our approach involves peeling away layers of the network to examine the feature representations and classification boundaries. The results show that minority collapse is a consequence of the interplay between feature learning and classification, with earlier layers exhibiting significant bias towards the majority class. Our findings indicate that this bias is exacerbated by the imbalance ratio and network depth. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our layer-peeled model in identifying and mitigating minority collapse, leading to improved performance on imbalanced datasets. The contributions of this research include a deeper understanding of the causes of minority collapse and a novel framework for analyzing and addressing this issue in deep neural networks, with potential applications in areas such as image classification, natural language processing, and recommender systems, leveraging key technologies like convolutional neural networks, recurrent neural networks, and transfer learning.
