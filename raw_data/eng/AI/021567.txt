This study addresses the challenge of skin detection in digital images specifically tailored to sub-continental human populations. The objective is to evaluate and compare the effectiveness of various statistical skin detection algorithms in accurately identifying skin pixels, considering the diverse skin tones and lighting conditions prevalent in this region.

Methods/Approach: We conduct an extensive comparative analysis of several statistical skin detection algorithms, including Gaussian Mixture Models, Naïve Bayes Classifier, and Histogram-based approaches. These methods are selected for their prevalence and reputed performance in skin detection tasks. Each algorithm is tested on a newly curated dataset that includes a wide range of images representing the diverse skin tones and complexions found across the sub-continent.

Results/Findings: The results demonstrate significant variations in the performance of the analyzed algorithms. The Gaussian Mixture Model showed superior adaptability to the variable lighting conditions, while the Histogram-based approach excelled in computational efficiency. The Naïve Bayes Classifier provided a balanced performance, offering robust accuracy across different scenarios but with increased computational cost.

Conclusion/Implications: The study highlights the importance of choosing the appropriate skin detection algorithm based on specific application requirements such as accuracy, computational efficiency, and adaptability to varying lighting conditions and skin tones. The presented findings can inform the development of more robust and efficient computer vision systems, particularly in applications like biometric authentication and augmented reality tailored for sub-continental demographics.

Keywords: Skin Detection, Statistical Algorithms, Sub-Continental Images, Gaussian Mixture Model, Naïve Bayes Classifier, Histogram-based Approach, Computer Vision, Biometric Authentication.
