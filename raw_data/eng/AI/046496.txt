Microwave imaging has emerged as a promising non-invasive technique for detecting brain anomalies, offering advantages such as portability and cost-effectiveness. However, traditional methods often struggle with reconstructing high-resolution images due to the complex nature of brain tissues and the ill-posed nature of the inverse problem. This paper proposes a novel approach leveraging Graph Attention Networks (GATs) to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of microwave imaging for brain anomaly detection. The proposed method models the brain's electromagnetic properties as a graph, where nodes represent tissue regions and edges capture spatial relationships. By incorporating attention mechanisms, the GAT dynamically prioritizes relevant features, enabling robust feature extraction and improved image reconstruction. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms conventional methods in terms of reconstruction accuracy and computational efficiency. The system achieves a 15% improvement in image resolution and a 20% reduction in processing time compared to state-of-the-art techniques. This research contributes a scalable and interpretable framework for microwave imaging, with potential applications in early diagnosis of brain disorders such as tumors and strokes. Key innovations include the integration of graph-based learning with attention mechanisms, offering a new direction for solving complex inverse problems in medical imaging. Keywords: Graph Attention Networks, Microwave Imaging, Brain Anomaly Detection, Inverse Problems, Non-Invasive Diagnostics.
