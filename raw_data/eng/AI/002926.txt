This paper presents a novel approach to virtual character animation control through data-driven reinforcement learning. The objective is to enhance the realism and adaptability of animated characters in interactive environments by using a learning-based framework that can efficiently generate responsive and lifelike animations.

Methods/Approach: We propose a reinforcement learning model that leverages large datasets of motion capture data to train virtual characters. By integrating advanced neural network architectures, specifically designed to handle high-dimensional input spaces, our approach optimizes policy learning for character control. This model is further refined through a reward function tailored to maintain natural and physically plausible movements.

Results/Findings: The proposed method demonstrates significant improvements in animation quality and control flexibility as compared to traditional keyframe-based and procedural approaches. Our system can generate seamless transitions between diverse actions and adapt to different scenarios with minimal preprogramming. Experimental results show that characters trained using our data-driven reinforcement learning method exhibit more fluid and realistic motion patterns.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field of virtual animation by introducing a robust and scalable solution for character control using reinforcement learning. The implications of this work extend to various applications, including gaming, virtual reality, and simulation environments, where dynamic and believable character interaction is crucial. Our findings open avenues for further exploration in real-time character animation driven by AI technologies, promising advancements in how virtual worlds are populated and experienced.

Keywords: reinforcement learning, virtual character animation, data-driven methods, motion capture, neural networks, AI, animation control, realistic animation.
