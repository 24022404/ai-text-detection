**Abstract**

View extrapolation of human bodies from a single image is a crucial challenge in computer vision with significant applications in virtual reality, animation, and human-computer interaction. This paper introduces a novel approach for synthesizing unseen viewpoints of human subjects from a single RGB image. Our method leverages a combination of deep neural networks and 3D geometric priors to infer human body structure, pose, and appearance, enabling realistic rendering of novel views without the need for multi-view inputs. The proposed system employs a learning-based pipeline that integrates a parametric human body model with image-based feature extraction, enhancing both the accuracy of pose estimation and the fidelity of texture reconstruction. Extensive experiments on public benchmarks demonstrate that our approach achieves superior performance compared to state-of-the-art single-view reconstruction and novel view synthesis methods, particularly in handling occlusions and preserving fine details of clothing and body shape. Qualitative and quantitative results confirm our model's ability to generate high-quality, photorealistic images from challenging inputs. These findings highlight potential advancements in virtual avatars, digital entertainment, and interactive applications. Keywords: view extrapolation, single image, human body modeling, 3D reconstruction, deep learning, pose estimation, novel view synthesis.
