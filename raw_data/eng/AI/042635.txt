Semantic segmentation has been a critical task in computer vision, aiming to assign a class label to each pixel in an image. This paper introduces Boundary Neural Fields (BNF), a novel approach designed to enhance semantic segmentation by integrating boundary information directly into the neural network model. The primary objective of our research is to address the common issue of boundary blurring in segmentation maps, which often leads to imprecise classification outputs. 

The approach involves a new architecture that incorporates boundary features as part of a neural field, which is trained alongside traditional segmentation networks. Our method refines pixel-wise classification by utilizing boundary awareness to enhance the distinction between adjacent regions. We evaluate the performance of BNF on multiple benchmark datasets, demonstrating that our model significantly improves segmentation quality compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly at object edges.

Key results show improved accuracy and precision near boundaries, with quantitative metrics marking a clear enhancement in mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) scores. This marks a significant contribution to the field, providing a more granular level of segmentation and giving rise to potential applications in various domains like autonomous driving, medical imaging, and satellite imagery analysis.

In conclusion, Boundary Neural Fields present a robust framework for semantic segmentation, offering precise boundary delineation and better overall performance, making it a valuable addition to existing segmentation techniques. This work paves the way for further research into the integration of boundary-aware components in other computer vision tasks. Keywords include semantic segmentation, boundary detection, neural fields, computer vision, and image processing.
