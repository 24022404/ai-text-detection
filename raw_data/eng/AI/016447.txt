Visual Question Answering (VQA) is a challenging task at the intersection of computer vision and natural language processing, traditionally relying on large datasets of image-question-answer pairs for supervised training. This paper presents a novel VQA approach that eliminates the need for question-answer training data. We introduce a methodology that leverages pre-trained vision-language models and unsupervised learning to associate visual content with textual queries. Our system utilizes a combination of image embedding techniques and large language models to interpret questions and generate relevant answers, relying solely on independently sourced image captions and textual corpora for representation learning. Experimental results demonstrate competitive performance on standard VQA benchmarks, with our model achieving comparable accuracy to supervised counterparts, particularly in zero-shot and few-shot settings. The findings suggest that effective VQA systems can be developed without annotated question-answer pairs, significantly reducing data collection efforts and enabling broader scalability. This research opens new avenues for cost-effective, flexible VQA deployment, and highlights the potential of unsupervised and weakly supervised learning in complex multimodal tasks. Key keywords: Visual Question Answering, unsupervised learning, vision-language models, zero-shot, natural language processing, image understanding.
