Monocular depth estimation, a challenging task in computer vision, aims to infer depth information from a single RGB image. This paper investigates the use of adversarial training to improve the accuracy and robustness of monocular depth estimation models. We propose a novel adversarial framework incorporating a discriminator network that distinguishes between predicted depth maps and ground truth depth maps, guiding the generator network (depth estimation model) to produce more realistic and accurate results. Our approach utilizes a carefully designed loss function that combines adversarial loss, depth reconstruction loss, and gradient matching loss to ensure both global consistency and local detail preservation. Experimental results on standard benchmark datasets, including KITTI and NYU Depth V2, demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing monocular depth estimation techniques. Specifically, our approach exhibits improved performance in challenging scenarios with texture-less regions and depth discontinuities. The adversarial training framework effectively mitigates common issues such as over-smoothing and artifacts, leading to more visually plausible and accurate depth predictions. Keywords: Monocular Depth Estimation, Adversarial Training, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, Depth Prediction.
