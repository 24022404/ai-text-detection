Causal discovery from incomplete datasets is a critical challenge in data-driven decision-making, as missing values can obscure underlying causal relationships. This paper addresses this problem by proposing a novel framework that combines an encoder-based data imputation model with reinforcement learning (RL) for causal structure learning. The objective is to accurately infer causal relationships from datasets with missing values while minimizing bias introduced by traditional imputation methods. Our approach leverages an encoder to learn latent representations of the incomplete data, enabling robust imputation, and employs RL to iteratively explore and optimize the causal graph structure. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing techniques in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with high missingness rates. The framework’s ability to handle incomplete data without relying on restrictive assumptions makes it a valuable tool for applications in healthcare, finance, and social sciences. This research contributes to advancing causal discovery by integrating data imputation and structure learning into a unified, adaptive framework, offering significant improvements over conventional methods.  

**Keywords**: Causal discovery, incomplete data, encoder, reinforcement learning, data imputation, causal structure learning.
