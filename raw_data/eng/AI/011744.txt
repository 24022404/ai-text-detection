Graph representation learning has become increasingly important for various applications in network analysis, recommender systems, and social network mining. However, existing approaches often struggle to capture complex data distributions and generalize to unseen graph structures. This paper introduces GraphGAN, a novel framework that integrates generative adversarial networks (GANs) with graph representation learning to address these challenges. The proposed method consists of a generator that learns to propose realistic vertex neighborhoods in the embedding space, and a discriminator that distinguishes between actual and generated node connections. By training the generator and discriminator in an adversarial manner, GraphGAN effectively captures both global and local structural information within graphs. Experimental evaluations on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that GraphGAN significantly outperforms state-of-the-art graph embedding techniques in link prediction, node classification, and network visualization tasks. The results indicate enhanced performance in representation quality, robustness to noise, and scalability to large graphs. GraphGAN’s adversarial learning paradigm opens new avenues for advancing graph-based machine learning, offering a powerful tool for knowledge discovery in complex networks.  
Keywords: Graph representation learning, Generative adversarial networks, Graph embeddings, Network analysis, Link prediction
