Cervical cancer remains a significant global health challenge, with early and accurate diagnosis being critical for effective treatment. This paper presents a novel Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based approach for the automated classification of cervical cancer in whole-slide histopathology images. The proposed method addresses the challenges of high-resolution image analysis by leveraging a multi-scale feature extraction framework, enabling the model to capture both local and global patterns indicative of cancerous regions. The CNN architecture is optimized for computational efficiency and robustness, incorporating transfer learning and data augmentation techniques to enhance generalization across diverse datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves superior classification accuracy compared to traditional methods, with a significant reduction in false positives. The system also exhibits strong performance in distinguishing between different stages of cervical cancer, providing valuable insights for clinical decision-making. This research contributes to the growing field of AI-driven medical diagnostics, offering a scalable and reliable solution for cervical cancer detection. The findings highlight the potential of deep learning in improving diagnostic workflows and reducing the burden on pathologists, paving the way for broader applications in digital pathology.  

**Keywords**: Cervical cancer, histopathology, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), whole-slide imaging, deep learning, medical diagnostics.
