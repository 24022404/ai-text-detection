**Abstract**  
The increasing deployment of image retrieval systems in various real-world applications has raised concerns regarding their vulnerability to adversarial attacks. This paper proposes a novel universal perturbation attack designed specifically to compromise the robustness and reliability of deep learning-based image retrieval models. Unlike traditional instance-specific attacks, the approach introduces a single, input-agnostic perturbation that, when applied to any image, significantly degrades retrieval performance across diverse datasets and architectures. The methodology involves an iterative optimization framework that maximizes the disruption of feature representations in the retrieval model, thereby increasing retrieval errors without introducing perceptible artifacts to the human eye. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed universal perturbation consistently reduces retrieval accuracy and mean average precision (mAP) on several benchmark datasets, outperforming baseline adversarial techniques in both effectiveness and transferability. Furthermore, analyses show the attack's adaptability to various backbone networks and its minimal impact on visual quality, highlighting potential risks in security-sensitive scenarios. The findings underscore the necessity for developing resilient image retrieval systems and introduce new benchmarks for evaluating adversarial robustness. Keywords: adversarial attack, universal perturbation, image retrieval, deep learning, security, robustness.
