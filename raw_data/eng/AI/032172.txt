This paper addresses the challenge of learning under adversarial and interventional shifts, where the distribution of the data changes due to external interventions or adversarial attacks. Our objective is to develop a robust learning framework that can adapt to such shifts and maintain its performance. We propose a novel approach that combines causal inference and adversarial training to learn invariant representations of the data. Our method utilizes a graph-based intervention model to identify the causal relationships between variables and a minimax optimization algorithm to learn robust features. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and robustness under various types of shifts. Our findings have significant implications for real-world applications, such as healthcare and finance, where data distributions can change over time due to interventions or attacks. The key contributions of this research include the development of a robust learning framework, the introduction of a graph-based intervention model, and the demonstration of improved performance under adversarial and interventional shifts. Relevant keywords: causal inference, adversarial training, robust learning, intervention shifts, graph-based models.
