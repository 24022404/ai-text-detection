Probabilistic programming has emerged as a powerful paradigm for modeling complex systems and performing Bayesian inference. However, designing efficient inference algorithms for such programs remains a significant challenge, often requiring domain expertise and manual tuning. This paper introduces a novel meta-learning framework to automate the design of inference algorithms for probabilistic programs. Our approach leverages neural networks to learn a mapping from program structures to optimized inference strategies, enabling adaptive and efficient inference across diverse probabilistic models. We evaluate the proposed framework on a range of benchmark programs, demonstrating its ability to outperform traditional, handcrafted inference methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight the potential of meta-learning to revolutionize probabilistic programming by reducing the need for manual intervention and improving scalability. This work contributes to the broader field of automated machine learning and offers practical implications for real-world applications in areas such as decision-making under uncertainty and probabilistic reasoning.  

**Keywords**: probabilistic programming, meta-learning, Bayesian inference, neural networks, automated machine learning.
