This paper introduces a novel unsupervised learning framework for multi-object tracking (MOT) by leveraging animation-inspired techniques to enhance object tracking accuracy and robustness. The proposed approach, termed *Tracking by Animation*, employs a self-supervised learning paradigm to train attentive trackers without requiring labeled data. By simulating object motion and interactions through synthetic animations, the model learns to predict object trajectories and maintain consistent identities across frames. The framework integrates a multi-object attention mechanism to handle occlusions and complex scenes, enabling precise tracking in dynamic environments. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art supervised approaches, with significant improvements in handling occlusions and long-term tracking scenarios. The study highlights the potential of unsupervised learning in MOT and provides a scalable solution for real-world applications such as surveillance, autonomous driving, and video analysis. Key contributions include the innovative use of animation-based training, the integration of attention mechanisms for multi-object tracking, and the demonstration of unsupervised learning’s effectiveness in reducing dependency on labeled datasets. Keywords: multi-object tracking, unsupervised learning, attention mechanisms, animation-based training, self-supervised learning.
