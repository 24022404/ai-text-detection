Causal inference is critical for informed decision-making in numerous fields, yet expressing and validating causal assumptions remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces enhancements to the DoWhy Python library, a leading framework for causal inference, to address these challenges. We present novel methods for formally specifying causal assumptions using a domain-specific language that integrates seamlessly with DoWhy's existing API. Furthermore, we introduce a suite of diagnostic tools and validation techniques designed to rigorously evaluate the plausibility of these assumptions based on observed data. These tools include tests for unconfoundedness, instrumental variable validity, and sensitivity analyses to assess the robustness of causal estimates to violations of assumptions. Through empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets, we demonstrate the effectiveness of our extensions in detecting assumption violations and improving the reliability of causal inferences. Our contributions enable practitioners to more confidently express, validate, and ultimately, rely on causal conclusions drawn from observational data. Keywords: Causal Inference, Causal Assumptions, DoWhy, Observational Data, Validation, Sensitivity Analysis.
