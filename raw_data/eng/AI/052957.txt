Adversarial examples pose significant challenges to the robustness and security of modern machine learning models, especially in critical applications such as image recognition and autonomous systems. This paper presents a novel methodology for generating adversarial examples that are both fast to compute and exhibit minimal perceptual distortion. Our approach combines a gradient-based optimization technique with a distortion-aware objective function, enabling the rapid creation of imperceptible adversarial perturbations. We evaluate our method on standard benchmark datasets and compare its performance against existing state-of-the-art adversarial attack algorithms in terms of speed, distortion metrics, and attack success rate. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves competitive or superior success rates while significantly reducing both computational overhead and the visual detectability of the perturbations. These findings suggest our approach offers a practical pathway for adversarial testing in real-time and resource-constrained environments. The proposed framework not only advances the understanding of adversarial vulnerability in neural networks but also provides valuable tools for stress-testing models to improve their robustness against subtle yet effective adversarial threats.

Keywords: adversarial examples, neural networks, robustness, attack algorithms, low distortion, fast computation, image classification, security
