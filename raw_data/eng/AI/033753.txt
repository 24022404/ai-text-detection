This paper presents a novel machine learning pipeline designed to aid in the identification of schools from images related to child trafficking cases. The objective is to develop an automated system that can assist law enforcement and investigators in tracing the origin of such images, potentially leading to the rescue of victims and prosecution of perpetrators. Our approach utilizes a combination of computer vision and deep learning techniques, including image processing, feature extraction, and classification using convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The pipeline is trained on a large dataset of images with annotated school information, allowing it to learn distinctive features of schools and their surroundings. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our pipeline in accurately identifying schools from unseen images, with a significant improvement over existing methods. The key contributions of this research include the development of a specialized dataset for school identification and the proposal of a robust machine learning pipeline that can be integrated into existing investigative workflows. Our work has important implications for combating child trafficking and exploiting online content for forensic analysis, highlighting the potential of AI-powered tools in supporting law enforcement efforts. Key keywords: machine learning, child trafficking, image analysis, school identification, convolutional neural networks, computer vision, forensic investigation.
