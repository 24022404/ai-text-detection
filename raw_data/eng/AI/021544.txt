Pedestrian attribute recognition and localization are critical tasks in computer vision, with applications in surveillance, security, and autonomous systems. However, existing methods often rely on fully annotated datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to obtain. This paper proposes a novel weakly-supervised learning framework to address these challenges by leveraging mid-level feature representations. Our approach eliminates the need for extensive pixel-level annotations by utilizing image-level labels, significantly reducing annotation effort while maintaining robust performance. We introduce a multi-task learning model that jointly optimizes attribute recognition and localization, enabling the network to learn discriminative mid-level features that capture both global and local contextual information. The proposed method incorporates a self-attention mechanism to enhance feature aggregation and improve attribute localization accuracy. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our framework achieves competitive performance compared to fully supervised methods, with significant improvements in attribute recognition precision and localization accuracy. The approach also exhibits strong generalization capabilities across diverse datasets and scenarios. This work contributes to advancing weakly-supervised learning in computer vision, offering a scalable and efficient solution for pedestrian attribute analysis. Key innovations include the integration of mid-level features, self-attention mechanisms, and a unified multi-task learning framework. The proposed method has practical implications for real-world applications, particularly in scenarios where labeled data is limited.  

**Keywords**: weakly-supervised learning, pedestrian attribute recognition, mid-level features, attribute localization, self-attention mechanism, multi-task learning.
