Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have emerged as powerful tools for processing graph-structured data, yet the internal mechanisms by which information is propagated and aggregated—known as message passing—remain incompletely understood. This paper aims to shed light on the message passing process within GNNs by introducing an analytical framework based on Power Iteration Clustering (PIC), a scalable and efficient clustering technique. We leverage PIC to systematically identify and visualize the evolution of node embeddings and their community structures throughout the layers of various GNN architectures. Through comprehensive experiments on multiple benchmark datasets, we demonstrate how PIC uncovers patterns of information flow, highlights bottlenecks, and reveals the convergence behavior of embeddings. Our findings indicate that message passing exhibits distinct community formation dynamics dependent on both the architecture and the dataset properties. By correlating these dynamics with model performance, we provide new insights into over-smoothing, expressive power, and the influence of graph topology on learning outcomes. This work contributes a novel perspective and practical tools for interpreting, diagnosing, and potentially improving GNN models, paving the way for more interpretable and effective graph-based learning. Key keywords: Graph Neural Networks, message passing, Power Iteration Clustering, graph representation, node embeddings, interpretability.
