Object recognition remains a critical challenge in computer vision, with applications ranging from autonomous systems to medical imaging. Traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and graph-based approaches often struggle with capturing hierarchical relationships and spatial dependencies in complex visual data. This paper introduces **Interpretable Graph Capsule Networks (IGCN)**, a novel framework designed to enhance object recognition by leveraging the strengths of graph neural networks (GNNs) and capsule networks. The proposed model integrates graph-based representations to model object parts and their relationships, while capsule networks ensure robust feature learning and pose invariance. We evaluate IGCN on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, demonstrating superior performance in terms of accuracy and interpretability compared to state-of-the-art methods. Key contributions include a dynamic routing mechanism tailored for graph-structured data and a visualization tool that provides insights into the decision-making process. The results highlight IGCN's ability to generalize across diverse object recognition tasks, offering a promising direction for interpretable and scalable computer vision systems.  

**Keywords**: Graph Capsule Networks, Object Recognition, Interpretability, Graph Neural Networks, Computer Vision, Dynamic Routing.
