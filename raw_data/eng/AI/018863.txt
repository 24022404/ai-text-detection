This paper addresses the challenge of interpreting and comparing dimensionality reduction results, a crucial task in data analysis and visualization. Our objective is to develop a framework that supports the analysis of dimensionality reduction outcomes using contrastive learning techniques. We propose a novel approach that leverages contrastive learning to identify and highlight the most informative features and patterns in the reduced data representation. Our method utilizes a combination of autoencoders and contrastive loss functions to learn a compact and meaningful representation of the data. The results show that our approach outperforms existing methods in preserving the local and global structure of the data, and provides a more accurate and interpretable representation of the dimensionality reduction results. Our framework has significant implications for data visualization, clustering, and anomaly detection tasks, and contributes to the development of more effective and efficient dimensionality reduction techniques. Key contributions include the integration of contrastive learning with dimensionality reduction, and the introduction of a new evaluation metric for assessing the quality of reduced representations. Relevant keywords: dimensionality reduction, contrastive learning, autoencoders, data visualization, feature learning.
