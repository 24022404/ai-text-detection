This paper presents a novel approach to disentangle controllable and uncontrollable factors of variation in complex environments by leveraging interaction with the world. The objective is to develop a framework that enables agents to distinguish between factors that can be influenced by their actions and those that are beyond their control. Our method employs a combination of reinforcement learning and generative modeling to learn disentangled representations of the environment. By interacting with the environment and observing the consequences of their actions, agents can identify controllable factors and develop strategies to manipulate them. Our results show that this approach leads to improved performance in a variety of tasks, including robotic control and decision-making under uncertainty. The key findings highlight the importance of considering both controllable and uncontrollable factors in decision-making processes. This research contributes to the development of more robust and adaptive artificial intelligence systems, with potential applications in areas such as robotics, autonomous driving, and healthcare. Key keywords: disentangled representation learning, reinforcement learning, generative modeling, controllable and uncontrollable factors, decision-making under uncertainty.
