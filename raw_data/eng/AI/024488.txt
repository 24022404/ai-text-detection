This paper presents an end-to-end Optical Character Recognition (OCR) approach specifically designed for ultra-low-resolution printed text images, addressing a critical challenge in document digitization and text extraction. Traditional OCR systems often struggle with degraded image quality, particularly when text resolution is significantly reduced, leading to poor recognition accuracy. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel deep learning-based framework that integrates a super-resolution module with a transformer-based text recognition model. The super-resolution module enhances the quality of low-resolution images, while the transformer architecture captures contextual relationships within the text, improving recognition performance. Our approach is evaluated on a custom dataset of ultra-low-resolution printed text images, demonstrating superior accuracy compared to state-of-the-art OCR systems. Experimental results show a significant improvement in character recognition rates, particularly for text with resolutions below 20 DPI. The proposed method also exhibits robustness to noise and varying font styles, making it suitable for real-world applications such as historical document preservation and automated text extraction from low-quality scans. This research contributes to advancing OCR technology by addressing a critical gap in handling ultra-low-resolution text, offering a scalable and efficient solution for challenging digitization tasks.  

**Keywords**: Optical Character Recognition (OCR), ultra-low-resolution, super-resolution, transformer models, deep learning, text recognition.
