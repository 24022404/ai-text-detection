Image editing guided by textual descriptions has gained increasing attention, offering an intuitive way to manipulate images. However, existing methods often struggle with complex edits requiring global scene understanding and struggle to maintain consistency across the entire image. This paper introduces a novel approach, the Global Editing Network (GEN), designed to learn global image manipulations from textual descriptions. GEN employs a transformer-based architecture to fuse textual and visual features, enabling a holistic understanding of the desired edit. A key innovation is the introduction of a spatial attention mechanism that focuses on relevant regions while ensuring consistent changes across the image. We evaluate GEN on a diverse set of image editing tasks, including object replacement, style transfer, and scene modification. Experimental results demonstrate that GEN significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of both visual quality and semantic accuracy, as measured by quantitative metrics and human evaluation. The results showcase GEN's ability to perform complex, globally consistent edits guided solely by textual descriptions, offering a promising direction for future research in text-based image manipulation.
Keywords: Image editing, text-guided manipulation, transformers, spatial attention, global consistency, generative models.
