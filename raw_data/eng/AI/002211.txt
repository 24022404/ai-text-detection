This paper addresses the problem of deep video completion, a complex task that involves filling missing or corrupted regions in a video sequence while maintaining temporal consistency and high-quality visual results. Current methods often struggle with effectively integrating spatial and temporal information, leading to suboptimal outcomes. 

Methods: We introduce an innovative approach called Onion-Peel Networks, which leverages a layered representation structure to progressively refine video frames. Our network is designed to efficiently capture and utilize hierarchical temporal features, employing a multi-stage refinement process that mimics the way layers are peeled from an onion to reveal core information. The network architecture combines convolutional layers with temporal correlation modules, enabling seamless integration of spatial and temporal data.

Results: Our Onion-Peel Networks demonstrate superior performance in terms of visual quality and processing accuracy when evaluated against state-of-the-art video completion methods. Extensive experiments show significant improvements in maintaining temporal coherency and reducing artifacts. Quantitative metrics reveal enhanced performance with notable increases in PSNR and SSIM scores, indicating the robustness of our approach.

Conclusion: This research provides a novel contribution to the realm of video completion by introducing a structured network architecture that effectively harnesses both spatial and temporal dynamics. The implications of this study extend to various applications such as video editing, post-production, and restoration of archival footage. The Onion-Peel Networks set a new benchmark for video completion tasks, showcasing the potential for future advancements in visual processing techniques.

Keywords: Video Completion, Deep Learning, Onion-Peel Networks, Temporal Consistency, Spatial-Temporal Integration, Video Processing.
