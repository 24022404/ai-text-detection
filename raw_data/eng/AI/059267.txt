Abstract:

The evaluation of image quality without reference images—known as No-Reference Image Quality Assessment (NR-IQA)—is a crucial challenge in the fields of computer vision and multimedia processing, especially for real-time and streaming applications. This paper proposes a novel attention-driven approach to NR-IQA that leverages deep learning and attention mechanisms to more effectively model human visual perception. Our method utilizes a convolutional neural network (CNN) coupled with an adaptive attention module that dynamically focuses on visually significant regions within the input image. This enables the system to identify quality-relevant features that standard models often overlook. The proposed architecture is trained and tested on multiple public benchmark datasets, and its performance is compared against leading NR-IQA models in terms of prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves higher correlation with human subjective scores and improved robustness to various image distortions, including blur, noise, and compression artifacts. These findings highlight the effectiveness of attention mechanisms in enhancing NR-IQA tasks. The proposed model holds promise for deployment in image enhancement, content delivery networks, and real-time quality monitoring scenarios. Keywords: no-reference image quality assessment, attention mechanism, convolutional neural network, visual perception, image distortion.
