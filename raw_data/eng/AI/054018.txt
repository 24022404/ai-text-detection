Monocular 3D object detection is a critical task in computer vision, with applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality. However, existing methods often rely on extrinsic camera parameters, which can be difficult to obtain or may vary across environments, limiting their practicality. This paper proposes a novel extrinsic parameter-free approach for monocular 3D object detection, eliminating the dependency on external calibration data. Our method leverages a self-supervised learning framework combined with a geometric reasoning module to infer 3D object properties directly from 2D images. By integrating depth estimation and object localization, the system achieves accurate 3D bounding box predictions without requiring prior knowledge of camera parameters. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with varying camera setups. The proposed framework not only simplifies the deployment of 3D object detection systems but also enhances their adaptability to real-world conditions. This work contributes to advancing monocular 3D vision by providing a scalable and efficient solution for applications where extrinsic parameters are unavailable or unreliable.  

**Keywords**: Monocular 3D object detection, extrinsic parameter-free, self-supervised learning, geometric reasoning, depth estimation, autonomous systems.
