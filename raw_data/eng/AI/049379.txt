Graph convolutional networks (GCNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for semi-supervised classification by leveraging graph structures to propagate label information. However, existing GCN-based methods often suffer from poor linear separability in the feature space and limited generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) data. To address these challenges, we propose an improved Graph Convolutional Framework (GC-SepOOD) that enhances feature separability and OOD robustness through novel architectural modifications.

Our approach incorporates a feature disentanglement module that explicitly separates node representations into discriminative and task-agnostic components, improving linear separability in the learned embeddings. Additionally, we introduce a regularization technique based on contrastive learning, which encourages robustness to distribution shifts and prevents over-smoothing in deep GCNs. To further enhance OOD generalization, we employ an adaptive graph augmentation strategy that improves representation learning under varying graph structures.

Experiments on standard node classification benchmarks and OOD datasets demonstrate that GC-SepOOD significantly outperforms state-of-the-art GCN models in both in-distribution accuracy and OOD robustness. These findings highlight the importance of structured feature learning and adaptive graph processing for improving generalization in semi-supervised graph classification.

Keywords: Graph Convolution, Semi-Supervised Learning, Linear Separability, Out-of-Distribution Generalization, Contrastive Learning, Graph Neural Networks.
