Seismic inverse modeling is a crucial but challenging task in geophysics, aiming to reconstruct subsurface geological structures from seismic data. This paper introduces a novel seismic inverse modeling method leveraging Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to improve the accuracy and efficiency of traditional techniques. Our approach utilizes a GAN architecture, with a generator network designed to produce high-resolution subsurface models from seismic data and a discriminator network trained to distinguish between generated and real subsurface models. The adversarial training process enables the generator to learn complex mappings between seismic data and subsurface structures, capturing intricate geological features more effectively than conventional methods. Experimental results on synthetic and field seismic datasets demonstrate that our GAN-based method achieves significantly improved reconstruction accuracy compared to traditional inversion algorithms, particularly in areas with complex geological formations. The proposed method provides a powerful tool for seismic exploration and reservoir characterization, potentially reducing uncertainty in subsurface imaging and enhancing resource exploration efforts. Keywords: Seismic Inversion, Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, Deep Learning, Subsurface Imaging, Reservoir Characterization.
