Trajectory prediction is crucial for autonomous driving and social robotics. Existing methods often struggle to capture complex social interactions among pedestrians, particularly long-range dependencies and hierarchical relationships. This paper introduces a novel Recursive Social Behavior Graph (RSBG) to explicitly model these interactions. The RSBG recursively aggregates information from neighboring agents, capturing both individual behaviors and group dynamics at different levels of granularity. Specifically, we employ graph neural networks to encode social interactions and introduce a recursive message-passing mechanism to propagate information across the graph. Furthermore, a hierarchical attention mechanism is incorporated to adaptively weight the importance of different neighbors and interaction levels. Experimental results on benchmark datasets (e.g., ETH, UCY) demonstrate that the proposed RSBG outperforms state-of-the-art methods in trajectory prediction accuracy and robustness. The RSBG effectively captures complex social behaviors, leading to more accurate and reliable predictions. Keywords: Trajectory Prediction, Social Interaction, Graph Neural Networks, Recursive Message Passing, Autonomous Driving.
