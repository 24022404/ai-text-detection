This paper addresses the challenge of forecasting complex time series systems where multiple heterogeneous tasks are present, and the underlying clustering of these tasks is unknown. Traditional time series forecasting approaches often assume either independence among tasks or pre-defined task groups, potentially overlooking inter-task dependencies and informative shared patterns. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel framework that simultaneously learns predictive leading indicators and uncovers latent clusters of related forecast tasks. Our approach leverages a multi-task learning architecture integrated with automatic clustering, enabling the dynamic identification of task clusters based on shared temporal predictors. Key innovation lies in the joint optimization of both leading indicator selection and cluster assignment, promoting both interpretability and improved forecast accuracy. Empirical evaluations on real-world multivariate time series datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms baseline models in terms of both predictive performance and the meaningful discovery of task clusters. The findings suggest practical implications for applications such as energy demand forecasting, financial analysis, and sensor network monitoring, where exploiting hidden task structure and relevant indicators can substantially enhance forecasting systems. Keywords: time series forecasting, multi-task learning, unsupervised clustering, leading indicators, temporal prediction, cluster discovery.
