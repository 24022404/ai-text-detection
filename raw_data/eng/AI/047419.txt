This paper investigates the sample complexity associated with episodic fixed-horizon reinforcement learning (RL), a critical factor in the practical deployment of RL algorithms where efficiency and data sufficiency are paramount. Our goal is to elucidate the relationship between sample size and learning performance in such settings, providing insights into how quickly an agent can learn an effective policy.

Methods: We propose a novel framework to analyze the sample complexity, leveraging theoretical models that account for episodic tasks with fixed time horizons. By incorporating advanced statistical techniques and analysis of variance within policy evaluation, the framework captures the intricacies of discounted and finite-horizon reinforcement learning scenarios. We focus on improving the bounds on sample complexity by examining different exploration-exploitation strategies.

Results: Our findings reveal that the proposed framework significantly reduces the upper bounds on the sample complexity compared to existing methods. We achieve a tighter integration of planning and learning processes, diminishing the necessity for large-scale data in training efficient RL models. Comparative analyses with baseline approaches demonstrate enhanced convergence rates and reduced computational overheads.

Conclusion: The research provides vital contributions to the understanding of sample complexity in episodic fixed-horizon RL. Our analytical approach affords a pathway to more efficient policy learning, promising substantial implications for applications requiring rapid deployment of RL systems. By refining the balance between exploration and data collection, our results offer practical guidelines for optimizing RL performance in diverse domains. Key keywords include reinforcement learning, sample complexity, fixed-horizon, episodic tasks, exploration-exploitation balance.
