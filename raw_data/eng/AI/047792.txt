This study aims to investigate the recognition of human emotions in real-world settings, leveraging multi-modal affect analysis. The objective is to develop a robust framework for affective computing, utilizing standardized data collected from subjects in natural environments. Our approach employs a fusion of audio, visual, and physiological signals to enhance emotion recognition accuracy. We propose a novel deep learning architecture that integrates these modalities, allowing for more nuanced understanding of human emotions. Results show significant improvements in affect recognition, outperforming existing methods. The findings have important implications for human-computer interaction, mental health monitoring, and social robotics. This research contributes to the advancement of affective computing, highlighting the potential of multi-modal analysis in real-world applications. Key contributions include the development of a standardized data collection protocol and a robust multi-modal fusion framework, enabling more accurate and reliable affect recognition in everyday settings. Relevant keywords: affective computing, multi-modal analysis, emotion recognition, deep learning, human-computer interaction.
