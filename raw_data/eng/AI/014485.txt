This paper addresses the vulnerability of Multiple Object Tracking (MOT) systems to adversarial attacks, highlighting the limitations of solely focusing on detection evasion. Our objective is to develop a novel attack strategy that compromises the entire tracking pipeline, including both detection and tracking components. We propose a tailored adversarial approach that generates perturbations to deceive state-of-the-art MOT systems, leveraging a combination of gradient-based optimization and reinforcement learning. Our results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed attack, achieving significant degradation in tracking performance while evading detection. Notably, our attack outperforms existing methods that only target detection, emphasizing the importance of considering the entire tracking process. The findings of this research contribute to the development of more robust MOT systems, underscoring the need for comprehensive security evaluations. Key contributions include the introduction of a novel adversarial attack framework and a thorough analysis of its implications for MOT security, with relevant applications in fields such as surveillance, autonomous vehicles, and robotics, and related keywords: Multiple Object Tracking, Adversarial Attacks, Detection Evasion, Tracking Security, Computer Vision, Deep Learning.
