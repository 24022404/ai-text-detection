**Abstract:**

Resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) provides critical insights into brain functional connectivity by capturing spontaneous neural activity patterns. However, effective analysis of rs-fMRI data remains challenging due to the complex, graph-structured nature of brain networks. This paper explores the application and interpretability of Graph Isomorphism Networks (GINs) for rs-fMRI functional connectivity analysis. We design a framework that models brain regions as graph nodes and their connectivity as weighted edges, leveraging GINs to learn discriminative network representations. Our approach systematically evaluates GINs’ ability to capture key functional connectivity patterns relevant to neurological states. Experimental results on benchmark rs-fMRI datasets demonstrate that GINs outperform conventional graph neural network models in both accuracy and robustness for tasks such as subject classification and disease prediction. In addition, we perform interpretability studies to uncover how GINs encode topological information from brain networks, offering new insights into the network features critical for classification. The findings highlight the potential of GIN-based models for advancing automated, interpretable analysis of brain functional connectivity. This work establishes a solid foundation for future applications in neuroinformatics, brain disease diagnosis, and connectomics research.
