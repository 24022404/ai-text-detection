**Abstract:**  
Semantic segmentation is a critical task in computer vision, underpinning applications such as autonomous driving, medical imaging, and augmented reality. Despite remarkable accuracy achieved by modern deep learning models, their robustness—particularly under challenging conditions such as noisy data, occlusions, and adversarial perturbations—remains a key concern. This paper presents a comprehensive benchmarking study to systematically evaluate the robustness of state-of-the-art semantic segmentation models. We assess a diverse set of architectures, including fully convolutional networks and transformer-based designs, across multiple standard datasets with systematically introduced corruptions such as Gaussian noise, blur, and class imbalance. Quantitative metrics, including mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) degradation rates and recovery capabilities, are used to compare model performance under both clean and perturbed data scenarios. Our results reveal significant disparities in robustness across architectures and highlight the trade-offs between model complexity, accuracy, and resilience. The findings offer valuable insights into the limitations of current techniques and propose recommendations for improving robustness in future semantic segmentation models. This work establishes baseline benchmarks and encourages the development of more reliable semantic segmentation solutions, with implications for safety-critical applications.  
**Keywords:** semantic segmentation, robustness, benchmarking, deep learning, computer vision, adversarial perturbations, model evaluation, transformer architectures
