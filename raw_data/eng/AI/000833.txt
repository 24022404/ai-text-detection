**Abstract**

Point set registration is a fundamental problem in computer vision and 3D data analysis, involving the alignment of two or more point clouds representing geometric structures. Traditional methods often rely on handcrafted features or iterative algorithms that can be sensitive to noise, outliers, and partial data. This paper presents a novel adversarial framework for point set registration, leveraging generative adversarial networks (GANs) to enhance robustness and accuracy. Our approach formulates the registration process as a game between two neural networks—a generator that predicts the optimal transformation and a discriminator that evaluates the alignment quality. By iteratively refining the transformation, the system effectively handles challenging scenarios such as non-rigid deformations, missing points, and varying densities. Experimental results on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our adversarial method outperforms state-of-the-art techniques in terms of registration precision and computational efficiency. The proposed framework is fully unsupervised, reducing the need for labeled data and manual intervention. This research contributes a scalable and generalized solution that is applicable to tasks in robotics, medical imaging, and autonomous navigation. Keywords: point cloud, registration, adversarial learning, GANs, 3D reconstruction, unsupervised learning, geometric alignment.
