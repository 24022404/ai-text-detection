This paper introduces Deep Adaptive Multi-Intention Inverse Reinforcement Learning (DAMIRL), a novel framework designed to address the challenge of inferring multiple, potentially conflicting intentions from observed behaviors in complex environments. Traditional Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) methods often struggle to model diverse intentions simultaneously, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios such as autonomous driving, robotics, and human-computer interaction. The proposed approach leverages deep neural networks to adaptively learn a set of reward functions, each corresponding to a distinct intention, while dynamically adjusting to the underlying structure of the observed data. By incorporating a multi-intention mechanism and an adaptive weighting scheme, DAMIRL effectively disentangles and models overlapping intentions, even in high-dimensional and noisy environments. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that DAMIRL outperforms existing IRL methods in terms of intention recovery accuracy and reward function generalization. The framework’s ability to handle diverse and conflicting intentions opens new possibilities for applications in multi-agent systems, personalized AI, and decision-making under uncertainty. This work contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in IRL by providing a robust and scalable solution for multi-intention learning.  

**Keywords**: Inverse Reinforcement Learning, Multi-Intention Learning, Deep Learning, Adaptive Modeling, Reward Inference, Decision-Making.
