This paper explores the scaling laws that govern the transfer of pre-trained models to downstream tasks, a crucial aspect of modern machine learning. The objective is to understand how the performance of transferred models relates to the size of the pre-training dataset, model complexity, and task similarity. We employ a systematic approach, analyzing a wide range of models and tasks to identify the underlying patterns and relationships. Our results reveal a set of scaling laws that describe how transfer performance improves with increased pre-training data, model size, and task relevance. Notably, we find that these laws are surprisingly consistent across different model architectures and tasks, suggesting a general framework for predicting transfer performance. Our findings have significant implications for the development of more efficient and effective transfer learning methods, enabling the creation of more accurate and robust models with reduced training requirements. Key contributions include the identification of a universal scaling law for transfer, the development of a task-agnostic transfer metric, and the demonstration of significant performance gains through informed model selection and pre-training strategies.
