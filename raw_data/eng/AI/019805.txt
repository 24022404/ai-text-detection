Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have emerged as powerful tools for synthesizing highly realistic images across various domains, raising questions about the ability to distinguish between authentic and generated content. This paper investigates whether GAN-generated images exhibit unique, identifiable artifacts—referred to as "artificial fingerprints"—that can serve as reliable indicators of their synthetic origin. Employing a comprehensive experimental framework, we analyze images produced by several state-of-the-art GAN architectures and develop detection models leveraging statistical, frequency, and deep learning-based feature extraction methods. Our results demonstrate that GAN-generated images consistently imprint subtle, model-specific patterns that are distinguishable from natural images, achieving high classification accuracy in controlled tests. Furthermore, we assess the robustness of these fingerprints against common post-processing operations, such as compression and scaling, illustrating their persistence under realistic conditions. The findings highlight the potential for artificial fingerprint analysis as a critical tool in digital image forensics, copyright protection, and content authenticity verification. This research contributes novel insights into the forensic analysis of AI-generated media and underscores the ongoing arms race between synthetic image generation and detection technologies. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, digital forensics, image synthesis, artificial fingerprints, deep learning, media authenticity.
