Neural Architecture Search (NAS) has emerged as a pivotal approach for automating the design of deep learning models, yet its computational cost and scalability remain significant challenges. This paper introduces a novel graph-based NAS framework enhanced with operation embeddings to address these limitations. The proposed method leverages graph neural networks (GNNs) to represent neural architectures as directed acyclic graphs, where nodes correspond to operations and edges denote data flow. By embedding operations into a continuous vector space, the framework captures the semantic relationships between different operations, enabling more efficient exploration of the architecture search space. The approach employs a reinforcement learning-based controller to iteratively refine the search process, guided by the learned embeddings. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance while significantly reducing search time compared to existing NAS techniques. The framework’s ability to generalize across diverse tasks and datasets highlights its potential for practical applications in automated machine learning. This work contributes to advancing NAS by introducing a scalable, interpretable, and computationally efficient solution, paving the way for broader adoption in real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Neural Architecture Search, Graph Neural Networks, Operation Embeddings, Reinforcement Learning, Automated Machine Learning.
