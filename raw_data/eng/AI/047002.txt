Probabilistic programs offer a powerful and flexible framework for modeling complex systems with uncertainty. However, performing inference in these programs remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces a novel meta-learning approach to automatically learn an efficient inference algorithm tailored to a specific class of probabilistic programs. Our method uses a recurrent neural network to parameterize an inference procedure, and trains this network across a distribution of program structures and datasets. This enables the learned inference algorithm to generalize to unseen programs within the same distribution. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach on a variety of probabilistic programming tasks, including Bayesian regression, hierarchical modeling, and time series analysis. Empirical results show that the meta-learned inference algorithm achieves significant improvements in inference speed and accuracy compared to traditional methods like Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) and variational inference. This work provides a promising direction for automating and accelerating probabilistic inference, making it more accessible and scalable for real-world applications.

**Keywords:** Meta-learning, probabilistic programming, inference algorithm, neural networks, Bayesian methods, MCMC, variational inference.
