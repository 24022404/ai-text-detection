This paper addresses the challenge of learning robust models under both adversarial and interventional distribution shifts, a common scenario in real-world applications. Adversarial shifts refer to input manipulations designed to fool the model, while interventional shifts arise from changes in the underlying causal mechanisms. We propose a novel framework that combines adversarial training with causal inference techniques to learn models that are invariant to these shifts. Specifically, we integrate an intervention variable into the training process, allowing the model to learn causal relationships while simultaneously defending against adversarial attacks. We evaluate our approach on benchmark datasets subject to both types of distribution shifts, demonstrating significant improvements in robustness and generalization compared to standard adversarial training and existing domain adaptation methods. Our findings suggest that explicitly modeling causal relationships during adversarial training is crucial for achieving robust performance under complex distribution shifts. Key keywords: Adversarial Training, Causal Inference, Distribution Shift, Robust Learning, Intervention, Generalization.
