This study investigates human visual perception of synthetic faces, exploring the extent to which artificially generated faces are perceived as realistic compared to real human faces. The objective is to understand the factors influencing the perceived realism of synthetic faces and to identify potential gaps in current generative models. Using a combination of deep learning-based generative adversarial networks (GANs) and perceptual experiments, we generated a diverse dataset of synthetic faces and conducted a series of human-subject evaluations. Participants were asked to distinguish between real and synthetic faces and rate their perceived realism. Our findings reveal that while state-of-the-art GANs can produce highly realistic synthetic faces, subtle artifacts and inconsistencies in texture, lighting, and facial features remain detectable by human observers. Furthermore, we demonstrate that specific facial attributes, such as symmetry and skin texture, significantly influence perceived realism. The results highlight the challenges in achieving perfect realism and provide insights into improving generative models for applications in computer graphics, virtual reality, and digital media. This research contributes to the growing field of synthetic media by offering a deeper understanding of human perception and guiding future advancements in realistic face generation.  

**Keywords**: synthetic faces, human perception, GANs, realism, deep learning, computer graphics.
