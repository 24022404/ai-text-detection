Accurate image segmentation is critical for medical imaging analysis, particularly in large-scale studies such as the UK Biobank Cardiac MR Imaging Study. Manual quality control (QC) of segmentation results is time-consuming and prone to human error, necessitating automated solutions. This paper presents a novel automated QC framework designed to evaluate and ensure the reliability of cardiac MR image segmentation. The proposed approach leverages a combination of deep learning-based anomaly detection and statistical outlier analysis to identify segmentation errors. A convolutional neural network (CNN) is trained to detect inconsistencies in segmented images, while a robust statistical model flags outliers based on anatomical plausibility. The framework was tested on a dataset of 10,000 cardiac MR images from the UK Biobank, achieving a 95% accuracy in detecting segmentation errors compared to manual QC. Results demonstrate that the proposed method significantly reduces the time required for QC while maintaining high precision and recall. This work contributes to the field by providing a scalable, efficient, and reliable solution for automated QC in large-scale medical imaging studies, with potential applications in other domains requiring high-accuracy segmentation.  

**Keywords**: image segmentation, quality control, deep learning, cardiac MR imaging, UK Biobank, anomaly detection, convolutional neural networks.
