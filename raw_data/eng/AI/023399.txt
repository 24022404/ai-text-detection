This paper presents a comparative study of supervised and unsupervised deep learning methods for anomaly detection in images. The objective is to identify and evaluate the effectiveness of different approaches in detecting anomalies, which is crucial in various applications such as quality control, surveillance, and medical imaging. We employ a range of deep learning architectures, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and autoencoders, to investigate their performance on benchmark datasets. Our results show that unsupervised methods, particularly autoencoders, achieve competitive performance with supervised methods, while requiring significantly less labeled data. The key findings highlight the importance of feature learning and reconstruction error in anomaly detection. This research contributes to the development of efficient and accurate anomaly detection systems, with potential applications in real-world scenarios where labeled data is scarce or expensive to obtain. The study's outcomes have significant implications for the field of computer vision and image processing, and demonstrate the viability of deep learning-based approaches for anomaly detection in images, with relevant keywords including deep learning, anomaly detection, image processing, and autoencoders.
