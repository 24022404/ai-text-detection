This paper addresses the challenge of efficient exploration in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) environments, where traditional methods often suffer from sparse rewards and inefficient state space coverage. We propose a novel self-supervised exploration framework leveraging variational dynamics to guide agents toward informative and diverse experiences. Our approach models the environment’s dynamics using a variational inference technique, enabling the agent to quantify epistemic uncertainty and prioritize exploration in under-explored regions. By integrating this variational model with an intrinsic reward signal, our method promotes robust policy learning without reliance on external supervision or hand-crafted reward shaping. Experimental evaluations across several continuous control and complex benchmark tasks demonstrate that our approach achieves superior exploration efficiency and faster convergence compared to state-of-the-art exploration strategies in DRL. The results highlight significant improvements in cumulative rewards and policy robustness, particularly in environments with high-dimensional observations and sparse feedback signals. This work contributes a scalable and effective solution for self-supervised exploration, paving the way for more autonomous and sample-efficient learning in reinforcement learning systems. Key keywords: deep reinforcement learning, self-supervised exploration, variational inference, intrinsic motivation, exploration efficiency, dynamic modeling.
