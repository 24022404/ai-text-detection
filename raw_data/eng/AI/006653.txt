This paper introduces a general framework for hypercomplex-valued Extreme Learning Machines (ELMs), addressing the growing need for efficient and scalable machine learning models capable of handling high-dimensional and structured data. The proposed framework extends traditional real-valued ELMs to hypercomplex domains, such as quaternions and octonions, enabling the modeling of complex relationships in multidimensional data spaces. The approach leverages the algebraic properties of hypercomplex numbers to enhance feature representation and learning efficiency. A systematic methodology is presented, detailing the architecture, training process, and optimization techniques tailored for hypercomplex-valued ELMs. Experimental results demonstrate the framework's superior performance in tasks such as image classification, signal processing, and 3D data analysis, outperforming conventional real-valued ELMs and other state-of-the-art methods. The study highlights the framework's ability to reduce computational complexity while maintaining high accuracy, making it suitable for real-time applications. This work contributes to the advancement of hypercomplex-valued neural networks, offering a versatile and scalable solution for modern machine learning challenges. Key innovations include the integration of hypercomplex algebra into ELMs and the development of efficient training algorithms. The proposed framework has broad implications for applications in computer vision, robotics, and natural language processing, where multidimensional data is prevalent.  

**Keywords**: Extreme Learning Machines, hypercomplex numbers, quaternions, octonions, multidimensional data, machine learning, neural networks.
