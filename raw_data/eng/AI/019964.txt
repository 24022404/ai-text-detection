We present an end-to-end trainable framework for video super-resolution (VSR) that explicitly incorporates motion compensation to enhance temporal consistency and reconstruction quality. Existing VSR methods often separate motion estimation and frame upsampling or rely on simple alignment schemes, leading to artifacts and blurred details. Our approach jointly learns optical flow estimation, feature alignment, and high-resolution frame synthesis in a single convolutional neural network. First, a lightweight flow subnetwork predicts inter-frame motion fields; these flows guide feature warping in a feature‐alignment module. Warped features from multiple low-resolution frames are then fused and passed through a residual reconstruction network to produce the final high-resolution output. We train the entire pipeline end to end using a combination of reconstruction and flow-smoothness losses. Experiments on standard video benchmarks demonstrate that our model outperforms state-of-the-art methods by up to 1.5 dB in PSNR and yields notable gains in SSIM, while maintaining real-time inference speed on modern GPUs. Qualitative results show sharper edges, fewer ghosting artifacts, and more stable textures across frames. Our contributions include (1) the seamless integration of motion compensation into an end-to-end VSR network, (2) a novel joint loss formulation that balances frame fidelity and flow accuracy, and (3) a versatile architecture that can be extended to other video restoration tasks. Potential applications span high-definition video streaming, surveillance, and medical imaging.  
Keywords: video super-resolution, motion compensation, end-to-end learning, optical flow, convolutional neural network.
