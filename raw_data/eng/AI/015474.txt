The objective of this research is to enhance the understanding of geologic storage of carbon dioxide (CO2) through the application of advanced Bayesian inversion techniques to generative models. The challenge addressed is the accurate prediction of CO2 behavior in geologic formations, which is crucial for effective carbon capture and storage (CCS) strategies. Our approach integrates Bayesian inversion with generative modeling, employing these methods to interpret complex subsurface data and simulate CO2 movements under varying conditions. These models leverage probabilistic frameworks to account for uncertainties and variabilities in geologic parameters, providing more reliable predictions compared to traditional deterministic models.

The results demonstrate significant improvements in the prediction accuracy of CO2 plume migration and pressure distribution, as verified by comparisons with empirical data and established benchmarks. The findings indicate a reduced uncertainty in model output, highlighting the effectiveness of Bayesian inversion in refining geologic models for CO2 storage.

In conclusion, this research contributes a novel framework that combines Bayesian statistics with generative modeling to improve the safety and efficiency of CO2 storage in geologic formations. These insights have significant implications for the advancement of CCS technology, potentially accelerating its deployment as a mitigation strategy against climate change. Keywords: Bayesian inversion, generative models, carbon dioxide storage, geologic formations, probabilistic modeling, carbon capture and storage (CCS).
