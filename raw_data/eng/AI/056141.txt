Face recognition systems powered by deep learning models have become increasingly prevalent in security, authentication, and surveillance applications. However, their real-world robustness against adversarial threats remains a critical concern. This paper investigates the development and evaluation of robust physical-world attacks targeting state-of-the-art deep learning face recognition systems. We propose a novel adversarial attack methodology that generates physically realizable perturbations—such as wearable accessories or printed patterns—to deceive recognition models in uncontrolled conditions. The approach leverages a combination of optimization techniques and robustness constraints to ensure attack efficacy under variable lighting, pose, and distance. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our attacks significantly reduce recognition accuracy across diverse model architectures and real-life scenarios, outperforming previously documented physical adversarial attacks. The findings highlight key vulnerabilities in current face recognition solutions and underline the necessity for enhanced defense mechanisms, particularly for security-critical applications. Our work offers new insights into adversarial machine learning, face spoofing, physical-world attacks, and the development of more resilient biometric recognition systems.
