This paper presents a novel probabilistic robustness analysis framework for Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) grounded in the Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) learning theory. The objective is to quantify the robustness of DNNs against adversarial attacks, which is crucial for their reliable deployment in safety-critical applications. Our approach leverages PAC learning to derive probabilistic bounds on the robustness of DNNs, providing a rigorous and quantitative assessment of their vulnerability to adversarial perturbations. The methodology involves formulating the robustness analysis as a PAC learning problem, where the goal is to learn a robust classifier that minimizes the probability of misclassification under adversarial attacks. Our results demonstrate that the proposed framework can effectively analyze the robustness of DNNs and provide insights into their vulnerability to different types of attacks. The key findings include the development of a probabilistic robustness metric and the demonstration of its efficacy in evaluating the robustness of DNNs. The conclusions highlight the significance of the proposed framework in enhancing the reliability and trustworthiness of DNNs, with potential applications in areas such as computer vision, natural language processing, and autonomous systems. Key keywords: DNNs, PAC learning, probabilistic robustness, adversarial attacks, deep learning, neural networks.
