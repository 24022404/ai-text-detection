**Abstract**

Understanding whether different neural networks learn similar internal representations is critical for advancing the interpretability and reliability of deep learning systems. This paper investigates the concept of convergent learning, asking whether independently trained neural networks with identical architectures but different initializations and training orders develop comparable feature representations. We introduce a quantitative framework for analyzing and comparing internal activations across layers of various neural networks. By employing statistical similarity metrics and activation alignment techniques, we assess networks trained on standard datasets using convolutional and multilayer perceptron architectures. Our results reveal that, while certain high-level features and representations are consistently learned regardless of initialization, substantial variability persists, particularly in deeper layers. Notably, we find shared representational subspaces in early layers, suggesting the existence of universal features, but observe network-specific feature development at higher abstraction levels. These findings suggest that, despite architectural similarities, neural networks do not always converge to identical internal representations. This insight holds important implications for model interpretability, ensemble methods, and transfer learning. Our analysis provides foundational knowledge for further exploration into the reproducibility and robustness of neural network learning dynamics.  
**Keywords:** convergent learning, neural networks, representation learning, feature alignment, model interpretability, deep learning
