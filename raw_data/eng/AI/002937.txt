This paper addresses the challenge of interactive video object segmentation in real-world scenarios, where the goal is to accurately segment objects of interest from videos with minimal user input. Our objective is to develop a framework that can learn to recommend the most informative frames for user annotation, thereby reducing the annotation burden and improving segmentation accuracy. We propose a novel approach that leverages deep learning techniques to identify key frames that capture the most relevant information about the object of interest. Our method utilizes a reinforcement learning-based strategy to select frames that maximize the segmentation performance while minimizing the number of annotated frames. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets and reducing the annotation time by a significant margin. The key contributions of our research include the development of a frame recommendation system that can adapt to diverse video content and the introduction of a new evaluation metric that assesses the efficiency of interactive video object segmentation methods. Our work has important implications for applications such as video editing, object tracking, and autonomous driving, where accurate and efficient object segmentation is crucial. Key keywords: interactive video object segmentation, frame recommendation, deep learning, reinforcement learning, computer vision.
