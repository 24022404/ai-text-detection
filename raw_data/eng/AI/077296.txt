This paper presents a novel self-supervised approach for estimating optical flow from facial movements, leveraging the inherent structure of human faces to improve flow estimation accuracy. Our method utilizes a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture to learn spatial and temporal features from video sequences, eliminating the need for manual labeling or supervision. The proposed approach employs a multi-scale strategy to capture both large and small facial movements, resulting in more accurate and detailed flow estimates. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our self-supervised method, outperforming state-of-the-art supervised techniques in terms of flow estimation accuracy and robustness to varying lighting conditions. The key contributions of this research include the development of a self-supervised framework for facial movement-based optical flow estimation, the introduction of a multi-scale feature extraction strategy, and the demonstration of improved performance on benchmark datasets. This work has significant implications for applications in facial analysis, human-computer interaction, and computer vision, with potential uses in fields such as affective computing, facial recognition, and animation. Key keywords: self-supervised learning, optical flow, facial movement analysis, convolutional neural networks, computer vision.
