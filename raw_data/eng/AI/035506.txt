Objective: Reliable uncertainty estimation is critical for clinical decision support systems that perform classification and risk prediction on medical tabular data. This study introduces a unified framework that quantifies both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties in supervised learning tasks, enhancing the trustworthiness of predictive models in high-stakes healthcare applications.

Methods: We propose a hybrid Bayesian deep learning architecture combining Monte Carlo dropout and deep ensembles to capture model uncertainty, alongside a heteroscedastic layer to model data-dependent noise. The approach is applied to two clinical tasks—disease classification and patient risk scoring—using publicly available cardiology and critical‐care datasets. Uncertainty estimates are derived from predictive distributions and calibrated via temperature scaling. Performance is evaluated using accuracy, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), expected calibration error (ECE), and negative log‐likelihood (NLL). We compare our method against standard neural networks, single-model dropout, and conformal prediction baselines.

Results: Our hybrid framework achieves superior calibration (ECE reduced by up to 45%) and lower NLL (20% improvement) while maintaining competitive classification accuracy (mean AUROC 0.87) and risk‐prediction concordance (C‐index 0.81). Uncertainty‐aware decisions demonstrate improved reliability: samples with high predictive uncertainty correspond to higher error rates, enabling more cautious clinical interventions. In out‐of‐distribution and corrupted‐feature scenarios, the proposed model more effectively flags unreliable predictions compared to existing methods.

Conclusion: The integration of ensemble‐based epistemic uncertainty and heteroscedastic aleatoric modeling yields a robust tool for medical classification and risk prediction on tabular data. By providing well‐calibrated confidence estimates, this framework supports safer and more transparent AI‐driven clinical workflows. Keywords: uncertainty estimation, medical tabular data, Bayesian neural networks, deep ensembles, risk prediction, calibration.
