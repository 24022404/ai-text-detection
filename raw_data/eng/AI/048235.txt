Pedestrian trajectory prediction is crucial for autonomous driving and social robotics. Existing methods often rely on dense connections to model interactions between pedestrians, leading to high computational costs and potential overfitting. This paper introduces Sparse Graph Convolution Network (SGCN), a novel approach for predicting pedestrian trajectories by adaptively learning sparse interaction graphs. SGCN employs a learnable gating mechanism to selectively filter out irrelevant connections, focusing on the most informative interactions. This sparsity promotes efficiency and reduces noise in the interaction modeling process. We evaluate SGCN on several benchmark datasets, including ETH, UCY, and Stanford Drone Dataset. Experimental results demonstrate that SGCN achieves state-of-the-art performance with fewer parameters and faster inference time compared to existing dense graph-based methods. The learned sparse graphs provide insights into the key social interactions influencing pedestrian movement. SGCN offers a promising direction for efficient and accurate pedestrian trajectory prediction.

Keywords: Trajectory Prediction, Graph Convolutional Networks, Sparse Graphs, Social Interactions, Autonomous Driving.
