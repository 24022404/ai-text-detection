Few-shot learning has emerged as a critical challenge in machine learning, particularly in scenarios where labeled data is scarce. This paper introduces *CrossTransformers*, a novel spatially-aware architecture designed to address the limitations of existing few-shot transfer learning methods. The proposed approach leverages spatial relationships within data to enhance feature extraction and alignment across tasks, enabling more robust generalization with minimal labeled examples. By incorporating attention mechanisms and spatial transformers, CrossTransformers dynamically adapt to the geometric structure of input data, improving performance in tasks such as image classification and object detection. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that CrossTransformers achieve state-of-the-art results, outperforming traditional few-shot learning models by significant margins. The findings highlight the importance of spatial awareness in few-shot transfer and provide a foundation for future research in adaptive learning systems. This work contributes to advancing the field of few-shot learning by offering a scalable and efficient solution for real-world applications where data availability is limited.  

**Keywords**: Few-shot learning, transfer learning, spatial transformers, attention mechanisms, adaptive learning, image classification.
