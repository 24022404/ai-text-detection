Objective: Deploying deep 3D segmentation models in clinical settings is often constrained by the lack of GPU resources and the high computational demands of full-precision convolutions. This work introduces TernaryNet, a novel architecture that accelerates inference on CPU-only platforms for medical volumetric segmentation without compromising accuracy.  

Methods: TernaryNet replaces conventional 3D convolutional layers with a combination of sparse and binary operations based on weight ternarization (values in {–1, 0, +1}). During training, weights are projected onto a ternary space via learnable thresholds, encouraging sparsity and enabling efficient bitwise arithmetic at inference. We further optimize memory layout and leverage CPU vector instructions to maximize throughput for 3D volumes.  

Results: On two public medical imaging benchmarks, TernaryNet achieves up to 3× faster inference on a standard multi-core CPU compared to a baseline full-precision model, while reducing memory footprint by 60%. Segmentation accuracy, measured by Dice similarity coefficient, remains within 1.5% of the baseline on brain tumor and liver lesion tasks. Power consumption during inference drops by over 40%, enabling prolonged use on low-power edge devices.  

Conclusion: By combining ternary weight quantization with sparse and binary 3D convolutions, TernaryNet offers a practical solution for real-time, GPU-free medical image segmentation. This approach democratizes advanced volumetric analysis in resource-limited environments and paves the way for on-premise, privacy-preserving clinical applications.  

Keywords: TernaryNet, 3D segmentation, sparse convolution, binary convolution, weight ternarization, CPU inference, medical imaging.
