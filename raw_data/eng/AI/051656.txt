Image stitching is a critical task in computer vision, enabling the creation of seamless panoramic images from multiple overlapping photographs. However, existing methods often struggle with challenges such as misalignment, ghosting artifacts, and sensitivity to varying lighting conditions. This paper presents a robust method for image stitching that addresses these limitations. Our approach leverages a novel feature-matching algorithm combined with an adaptive blending technique to ensure accurate alignment and smooth transitions between images. The proposed method also incorporates a dynamic exposure compensation mechanism to handle variations in lighting across input images. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of alignment accuracy, visual quality, and computational efficiency. The system achieves a 15% improvement in alignment precision and reduces ghosting artifacts by 25% compared to existing techniques. Furthermore, the method is computationally efficient, making it suitable for real-time applications. This research contributes to the field of computer vision by providing a reliable and scalable solution for high-quality image stitching, with potential applications in virtual reality, autonomous navigation, and digital photography.  

**Keywords**: Image stitching, feature matching, adaptive blending, exposure compensation, computer vision, panoramic imaging.
