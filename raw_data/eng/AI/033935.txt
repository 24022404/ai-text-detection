This paper introduces a novel end-to-end Optical Character Recognition (OCR) approach specifically designed for ultra-low-resolution printed text images, a domain where traditional OCR methods often fail. Our approach leverages a deep convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, trained directly on low-resolution images without requiring pre-processing steps such as image enhancement or binarization. The model integrates feature extraction and sequence modeling into a single trainable framework, enabling it to learn robust representations directly from the degraded input data. Experimental results on a newly created dataset of ultra-low-resolution text demonstrate a significant improvement in character recognition accuracy compared to state-of-the-art OCR engines and conventional image processing techniques. The proposed method achieves a character accuracy rate of X%, representing a Y% improvement over the next best approach. This end-to-end learning paradigm offers a promising solution for reading text in challenging scenarios where image quality is severely limited, with potential applications in document analysis, historical text digitization, and resource-constrained devices. Keywords: Optical Character Recognition, OCR, Low-Resolution Images, Deep Learning, Convolutional Neural Network, End-to-End Learning, Text Recognition.
