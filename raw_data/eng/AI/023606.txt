This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised text recognition, aiming to develop models capable of interpreting textual content without relying on labeled datasets. Traditional text recognition systems often require extensive annotated data, which is costly and time-consuming to obtain. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel approach that leverages the relationship between reading and spelling, enabling the model to learn textual representations in a self-supervised manner. Our method employs a dual-task framework where the model simultaneously learns to reconstruct (spell) and recognize (read) text from unlabeled images. By integrating a transformer-based architecture with contrastive learning, the system effectively captures contextual and visual features, improving its ability to generalize across diverse text styles and fonts. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive performance compared to supervised baselines, with significant improvements in low-resource scenarios. The findings highlight the potential of unsupervised learning for text recognition, offering a scalable and cost-effective solution for real-world applications such as document digitization and optical character recognition. This work contributes to advancing unsupervised learning techniques in computer vision and natural language processing, paving the way for more robust and adaptable text recognition systems.  

**Keywords**: unsupervised learning, text recognition, self-supervised learning, transformer models, optical character recognition, contrastive learning.
