This paper explores the realm of kernel-based reinforcement learning, aiming to establish regret bounds for this emerging field. The objective is to develop a theoretical framework that enables the analysis of kernel-based reinforcement learning algorithms, providing insights into their performance and limitations. We employ a model-based approach, leveraging kernel methods to approximate the underlying system dynamics and reward functions. Our methodology utilizes a combination of kernelized least squares and exploration strategies to balance the trade-off between exploitation and exploration. The results show that our approach achieves a regret bound of O(sqrt(T)), where T is the number of time steps, outperforming existing methods in various benchmark environments. The key findings of this research contribute to the understanding of kernel-based reinforcement learning, highlighting its potential for efficient exploration and exploitation in complex domains. This work has significant implications for the development of autonomous systems and decision-making algorithms, particularly in situations where model uncertainty and exploration are crucial. Key keywords: kernel-based reinforcement learning, regret bounds, model-based reinforcement learning, exploration-exploitation trade-off, autonomous systems.
