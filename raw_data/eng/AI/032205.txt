Geometric mesh reconstruction and correction are foundational tasks in 3D computer vision, with wide-ranging applications in graphics, simulation, and virtual reality. This paper presents a novel framework for learning geometrically consistent mesh corrections, addressing the common challenge of mesh inaccuracies resulting from noisy or incomplete 3D scans. Our approach leverages a deep learning model designed to infer and apply corrective deformations to input meshes while preserving key geometric properties such as smoothness, edge connectivity, and surface integrity. The proposed method integrates geometric consistency constraints directly into the learning process, enabling more reliable and visually coherent mesh refinements compared to traditional post-processing or purely data-driven techniques. Experimental results across multiple datasets demonstrate significant improvements in mesh quality, including reductions in surface noise, enhanced feature preservation, and increased fidelity to ground-truth geometries. This framework also generalizes well to various object classes and input modalities. The study highlights the effectiveness of combining data-driven learning with geometric priors, paving the way for robust, automated mesh correction tools in AR/VR content creation, scientific visualization, and CAD model optimization. Key contributions include the introduction of a geometry-aware correction network, comprehensive evaluation protocols for mesh correction, and insights into balancing learned features with geometric regularization. Keywords: mesh correction, 3D reconstruction, geometric consistency, deep learning, surface refinement, computer vision.
