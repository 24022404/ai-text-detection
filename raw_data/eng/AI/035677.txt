Dropout, a widely used regularization technique in deep learning, is often perceived as a method to prevent overfitting by randomly dropping neurons during training. This paper presents an alternative perspective, framing dropout primarily as an optimization trick that facilitates faster convergence and exploration of the loss landscape. We analyze the training dynamics of neural networks with dropout and demonstrate its effect on smoothing the loss landscape, enabling the optimizer to escape local minima more effectively. Through a series of experiments on various benchmark datasets and network architectures, we compare the training trajectories of networks with and without dropout, observing that dropout leads to wider, flatter minima. Furthermore, we investigate the connection between dropout rate and the sharpness of the minima, revealing an optimal range for balancing generalization and optimization. Our findings suggest that the optimization benefits of dropout are crucial to its success, often outweighing its regularization effects, particularly in complex models. Keywords: Dropout, Optimization, Regularization, Deep Learning, Generalization, Loss Landscape.
