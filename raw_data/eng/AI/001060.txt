This paper addresses the challenges of annotation scarcity and domain shifts in machine learning, proposing a novel approach termed Extreme Consistency. The objective is to develop a robust framework that can learn from limited labeled data and generalize effectively across diverse domains. Our method leverages a combination of self-supervised learning and consistency regularization to enforce invariant feature representations. We evaluate the performance of Extreme Consistency on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating its ability to outperform state-of-the-art methods in scenarios with scarce annotations and significant domain shifts. The results show improved model consistency and adaptability, leading to enhanced accuracy and reduced overfitting. Our approach has significant implications for real-world applications where data annotation is costly or impractical, and domain shifts are common. Key contributions include the introduction of a new consistency loss function and a comprehensive evaluation framework for assessing model robustness. Relevant keywords: annotation scarcity, domain shifts, self-supervised learning, consistency regularization, invariant feature representations, machine learning, robustness.
