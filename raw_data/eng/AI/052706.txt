This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised sketch-based image retrieval (SBIR), a critical task in computer vision where the goal is to retrieve relevant images from a database using freehand sketches without relying on labeled data. Traditional SBIR methods often depend on supervised learning, requiring extensive annotated datasets, which limits their scalability and applicability in real-world scenarios. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel unsupervised framework that leverages self-supervised learning and contrastive representation learning to align sketch and image embeddings in a shared latent space. Our approach utilizes a dual-branch neural network architecture, trained on unlabeled sketch-image pairs, to learn discriminative features that capture the semantic similarities between sketches and images. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art supervised approaches, while significantly reducing the dependency on labeled data. The proposed framework not only advances the field of SBIR but also opens new avenues for applications in areas such as e-commerce, digital art, and forensic analysis, where labeled data is scarce or unavailable.  

**Keywords**: sketch-based image retrieval, unsupervised learning, self-supervised learning, contrastive learning, computer vision, neural networks.
