This paper presents "Alchemy," a novel framework for structured task distribution in the domain of meta-reinforcement learning (meta-RL). The aim is to address challenges in task generalization and adaptability, enabling models to efficiently learn and adapt to new tasks with minimal data.

Methods/Approach: The Alchemy framework introduces a structured approach to task distribution by leveraging hierarchical learning architectures, which allow the meta-RL model to recognize and prioritize different task components. The model utilizes a combination of neural network-based encoding and sequential decision processes, integrating these with advanced optimization techniques to enhance learning efficiency and adaptability.

Results/Findings: Experiments demonstrate that the Alchemy framework significantly improves task adaptability and performance when compared to existing meta-RL models. Key findings show that Alchemy reduces the number of iterations required to achieve optimal performance on new tasks by up to 30%. Additionally, the structured distribution of tasks leads to better generalization across a wide variety of complex and dynamic environments.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings suggest that Alchemy can contribute to more robust and efficient meta-reinforcement learning models, offering potential applications in autonomous systems and robotics where rapid adaptation and learning are crucial. This framework represents a significant advancement by enhancing task generalization and fostering efficient distribution strategies in meta-RL. With its structured task distribution approach, Alchemy could redefine benchmarks for model adaptability in rapidly evolving domains.

Keywords: meta-reinforcement learning, task distribution, Alchemy, hierarchical learning, task generalization, neural network, optimization, autonomous systems.
