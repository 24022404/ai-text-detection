Skeleton-based human action recognition has gained significant attention due to its robustness and efficiency in analyzing human movements. However, the vulnerability of these systems to adversarial attacks remains a critical concern. This paper investigates the susceptibility of skeleton-based action recognition models to adversarial perturbations, aiming to identify potential weaknesses and propose mitigation strategies. We introduce a novel adversarial attack framework that leverages gradient-based optimization to generate imperceptible perturbations on skeleton data, effectively misleading state-of-the-art recognition models. Our approach is evaluated on widely used datasets, including NTU RGB+D and Kinetics, demonstrating significant degradation in model accuracy under attack. Experimental results reveal that even minimal perturbations can cause misclassification, highlighting the fragility of current systems. Furthermore, we analyze the transferability of adversarial examples across different models and datasets, providing insights into the generalization of such attacks. The findings underscore the need for robust defense mechanisms in skeleton-based action recognition systems, particularly in security-sensitive applications. This research contributes to the growing body of knowledge on adversarial machine learning, offering a foundation for developing more resilient models in the future.  

**Keywords**: adversarial attack, skeleton-based action recognition, human action analysis, machine learning robustness, gradient-based optimization, transferability.
