In the evolving fields of computer vision and natural language processing, integrating visual and textual data remains a challenging task. This research addresses the problem of joint reasoning across visual scenes and embedded textual information through the development of a Multi-Modal Graph Neural Network (MM-GNN).

Methods/Approach: We present a novel MM-GNN framework that leverages both image features and embedded text segments to construct a comprehensive graph representation of the scene. This model employs graph convolutional layers to facilitate information exchange between visual and textual nodes, enhancing contextual understanding. Key innovations include a cross-modal attention mechanism that dynamically adjusts the importance of each modality, coupled with a relation learning module to capture spatial and semantic relationships.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the MM-GNN model significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art models in tasks requiring joint visual-text reasoning, such as scene understanding and question answering. The proposed model exhibits robust performance improvements in datasets involving complex scene-text interactions, achieving notable accuracy gains compared to traditional convolutional neural networks and single-modal approaches.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of MM-GNN represents a significant advancement in multi-modal learning, offering a cohesive framework for integrating visual and text data. This research not only contributes to the field of graph neural networks but also opens new avenues for applications in domains such as augmented reality, automated content description, and dynamic scene analysis. Keywords: Multi-Modal, Graph Neural Network, Vision, Scene Text, Joint Reasoning, Convolutional Layers, Cross-Modal Attention.
