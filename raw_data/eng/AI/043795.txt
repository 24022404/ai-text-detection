This paper addresses the challenge of high sample complexity in traditional reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks by introducing a data-efficient hierarchical reinforcement learning (HRL) model. The goal is to enhance learning efficiency while maintaining robust performance across various complex tasks.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel HRL architecture that decomposes decision-making into a hierarchy of policies, each responsible for different subtasks or abstraction levels. The model integrates a data-efficient learning algorithm that optimizes both high-level and low-level policies simultaneously, exploiting intrinsic rewards to accelerate learning. This approach leverages transfer learning techniques to adapt policies across different environments with minimal data requirements.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our proposed HRL model significantly reduces sample efficiency, achieving faster convergence compared to existing RL and HRL methods. The framework exhibits superior performance on benchmark environments, including simulated robotic control and game-playing scenarios, where it outperforms standard models in terms of learning speed and accumulated rewards.

Conclusion/Implications: Our study introduces a groundbreaking paradigm in data-efficient RL, showcasing the potential of hierarchical structures in improving learning efficacy. By significantly reducing the training data needed, our approach makes RL more viable for real-world applications, paving the way for practical implementations in robotics, autonomous systems, and complex decision-making tasks. Key contributions include the novel integration of hierarchy with data-efficient learning algorithms and demonstrated improvements in adaptability and performance across diverse domains.

Keywords: hierarchical reinforcement learning, data efficiency, sample complexity, decision-making, transfer learning, intrinsic rewards, RL model, task decomposition.
