Graph kernels are widely used for structured data analysis, offering a powerful framework for comparing graphs by mapping them into feature spaces. However, the distinction between explicit and implicit feature maps in graph kernels remains underexplored, limiting their interpretability and scalability. This paper presents a unifying view of explicit and implicit feature maps, addressing the gap in understanding their computational and theoretical properties. We propose a novel framework that bridges these two approaches, enabling efficient computation and enhanced interpretability of graph kernels. Our method leverages advanced matrix factorization techniques and spectral graph theory to derive explicit representations from implicit feature maps, while preserving the kernel's discriminative power. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in computational efficiency and classification accuracy compared to state-of-the-art graph kernel methods. The proposed framework not only advances the theoretical understanding of graph kernels but also provides practical insights for applications in bioinformatics, social network analysis, and recommendation systems. This work contributes to the broader field of graph-based machine learning by offering a scalable and interpretable solution for graph comparison tasks.  

**Keywords**: graph kernels, explicit feature maps, implicit feature maps, matrix factorization, spectral graph theory, structured data analysis.
