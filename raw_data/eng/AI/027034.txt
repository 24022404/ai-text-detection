Transfer learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for leveraging knowledge from pre-trained models to improve performance on new, related tasks, particularly when labeled data is scarce. However, the reliability of transfer learning remains a significant concern, as negative transfer can occur, leading to decreased performance compared to training from scratch. This paper investigates the factors contributing to unreliable transfer learning and proposes a novel adaptive regularization technique to mitigate these issues. Our approach dynamically adjusts the regularization strength based on the similarity between the source and target tasks, promoting knowledge transfer while preventing overfitting to the source domain. We evaluate our method on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in transfer learning performance and robustness compared to existing regularization strategies. Specifically, our results show a consistent reduction in negative transfer instances and a higher average performance gain across various transfer scenarios. Our findings offer practical insights for designing more reliable transfer learning systems, enabling broader applicability of pre-trained models in real-world applications. Keywords: Transfer Learning, Regularization, Negative Transfer, Domain Adaptation, Machine Learning.
