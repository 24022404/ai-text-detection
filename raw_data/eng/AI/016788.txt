Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are notoriously difficult to train due to instability issues. Spectral normalization (SN) has emerged as a popular technique to stabilize GAN training, but a comprehensive understanding of its efficacy remains elusive. This paper presents a rigorous analysis of why SN stabilizes GANs, demonstrating its effect on bounding the Lipschitz constant of the discriminator. We show that while SN effectively controls the Lipschitz constant, it can also limit the discriminator's capacity, hindering its ability to accurately distinguish between real and generated samples. To address this limitation, we propose an improved spectral normalization technique, Adaptive Spectral Normalization (AdaSN), which dynamically adjusts the normalization strength based on the training progress. Empirical results on various benchmark datasets demonstrate that AdaSN not only stabilizes GAN training but also enhances the generator's performance, leading to improved image quality and faster convergence compared to standard SN and other regularization methods. Our findings provide valuable insights into the inner workings of SN and offer a practical solution for training more robust and effective GANs. Keywords: GAN, spectral normalization, Lipschitz constant, adversarial training, generative models, deep learning.
