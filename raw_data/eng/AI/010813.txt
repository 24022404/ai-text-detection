This paper presents a novel framework integrating Bayesian inference and machine learning techniques to estimate parameters for Influence Models within the IM-RO (Influence Maximization under Realistic Optimization) paradigm. The primary objective is to enhance the accuracy and robustness of parameter estimation processes that underpin influence propagation analysis in complex networks. We employ Bayesian methods to incorporate prior knowledge and quantify uncertainty, while machine learning algorithms are leveraged to identify patterns and optimize parameter selection from large-scale data. The proposed approach is evaluated on multiple benchmark social network datasets, comparing its performance with traditional estimation techniques in terms of prediction accuracy, computational efficiency, and adaptability to varying network structures. Results demonstrate that our hybrid framework consistently outperforms baseline methods, offering significant improvements in both parameter estimation precision and the predictive capability of influence spreads. These findings underscore the potential of blending probabilistic and data-driven models for influence parameter estimation, facilitating improved decision-making in applications such as viral marketing, information dissemination, and network optimization. The study contributes an effective methodology for Influence Maximization tasks, providing a scalable and interpretable solution for handling uncertainty and complexity in real-world networks. Keywords: Bayesian inference, machine learning, influence maximization, parameter estimation, IM-RO, social networks.
