**Abstract**

Efficient spectrum sharing between radar and communication systems is a growing challenge due to increased demand for limited radio frequency resources. This paper presents an experimental analysis of various reinforcement learning (RL) techniques for enhancing spectrum sharing in radar applications. The objective is to evaluate the adaptability and performance of RL-based approaches in dynamically allocating spectrum while minimizing interference with coexisting wireless networks. We implemented and compared several established RL algorithms, including Q-Learning, Deep Q-Networks (DQN), and Policy Gradient methods, within a simulated environment that models real-world spectrum usage scenarios. Metrics such as spectrum utilization efficiency, interference mitigation, and convergence speed were used to assess each technique's effectiveness. Our findings indicate that deep reinforcement learning methods, particularly DQN, significantly outperform traditional approaches in terms of adaptability and interference avoidance. Moreover, we analyze the trade-offs between learning speed and computational complexity, providing insights on the practical deployment of RL-driven spectrum management in radar systems. These results underscore the potential of RL for enabling intelligent, autonomous spectrum sharing, and highlight directions for future research on scalable and robust spectrum access strategies. **Keywords:** reinforcement learning, spectrum sharing, radar, spectrum management, Q-Learning, DQN, interference mitigation, adaptive systems.
