This paper presents AdaBins, a novel approach to depth estimation using adaptive bins. The objective is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of depth estimation, a crucial task in computer vision. Our method employs a unique adaptive binning strategy, where the bins are dynamically adjusted based on the input image, allowing for more accurate and detailed depth maps. We utilize a transformer-based architecture to model the complex relationships between pixels and their corresponding depths. The results show that AdaBins outperforms state-of-the-art methods on several benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in terms of accuracy and speed. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of adaptive binning, which enables more efficient and effective depth estimation, and the demonstration of the potential of transformer-based models in this task. Our approach has important implications for various applications, including autonomous driving, robotics, and 3D reconstruction, and highlights the potential of adaptive binning and transformer-based architectures for depth estimation and other computer vision tasks. Key keywords: depth estimation, adaptive binning, transformer, computer vision, 3D reconstruction.
