Few-shot learning, a critical challenge in machine learning, aims to enable models to generalize effectively from limited data. This paper addresses the problem of designing neural architectures that can adapt to new tasks with minimal training examples. We propose a meta-learning framework that automates the discovery of optimal neural architectures tailored for few-shot learning scenarios. Our approach leverages a meta-model trained on a diverse set of tasks to predict architectures that maximize performance on unseen tasks with limited data. The framework employs a gradient-based optimization strategy to iteratively refine the architecture search process, ensuring scalability and efficiency. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing few-shot learning techniques, achieving higher accuracy and faster convergence. The proposed approach not only reduces the dependency on large labeled datasets but also provides a flexible and generalizable solution for real-world applications where data scarcity is a common issue. This work contributes to advancing the field of meta-learning by introducing a novel architecture search mechanism that bridges the gap between automated design and few-shot learning performance.  

**Keywords**: Meta-learning, few-shot learning, neural architecture search, gradient-based optimization, automated machine learning.
