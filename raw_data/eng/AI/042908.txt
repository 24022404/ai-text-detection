Video classification is a critical task in computer vision, with applications ranging from surveillance to content recommendation. However, existing methods often struggle with irrelevant or redundant visual information, leading to suboptimal performance. This paper introduces a novel saliency-guided video classification framework that leverages adaptively weighted learning to enhance model accuracy and efficiency. The proposed approach employs a saliency detection mechanism to identify and prioritize salient regions within video frames, ensuring that the model focuses on the most informative content. An adaptive weighting scheme is then applied to dynamically adjust the importance of different temporal segments, enabling the model to better capture temporal dependencies and contextual information. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art approaches in terms of classification accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight the effectiveness of saliency-guided attention and adaptive weighting in improving video understanding. This research contributes to the field by offering a robust and scalable solution for video classification, with potential applications in real-time video analysis and multimedia retrieval.  

**Keywords**: Video classification, saliency detection, adaptive weighting, temporal modeling, computer vision.
