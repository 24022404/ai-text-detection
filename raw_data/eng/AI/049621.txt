This paper addresses the challenge of cross-sensor portability in LiDAR-based semantic labeling, a crucial aspect for enhancing the versatility and applicability of neural network architectures in dynamic environments. The study aims to refine these architectures to maintain high performance across different LiDAR sensor configurations.

Methods/Approach: We developed and assessed a series of neural network models tailored for semantic labeling, emphasizing their adaptability to various LiDAR sensors. Our approach includes a novel adaptation technique that leverages transfer learning and domain adaptation methodologies, enabling models to generalize effectively across sensor-specific discrepancies.

Results/Findings: The experimental results demonstrate that our proposed neural network architectures exhibit significant improvements in maintaining semantic labeling accuracy across diverse LiDAR configurations. Comparative analyses with existing methods reveal that our model achieves superior performance in scenarios involving sensor variability, confirming the effectiveness of our cross-sensor adaptation strategy.

Conclusion/Implications: This research provides valuable insights into the development of more robust neural network frameworks for LiDAR-based applications, enhancing their deployment potential in multi-sensor environments common in autonomous navigation and robotic systems. The innovative adaptation mechanisms introduced here contribute to the field by offering scalable solutions that can be integrated with existing technology, paving the way for more consistent and reliable semantic labeling in heterogeneous sensor landscapes. Key keywords: LiDAR, semantic labeling, cross-sensor portability, neural network architectures, transfer learning, domain adaptation.
