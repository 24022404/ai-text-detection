Variational inference is a widely-used technique for approximating complex probability distributions in fields such as machine learning and statistics. Traditional methods often rely on minimizing divergences like the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, which may lead to biased approximations, especially for multi-modal or heavy-tailed distributions. This paper investigates the use of Fisher divergence as an alternative objective for variational approximations. We propose a novel framework that leverages the properties of Fisher divergence to construct tighter and more representative variational distributions. The approach utilizes the score function and gradient-based optimization to efficiently approximate intractable posterior distributions. Experimental results demonstrate that variational approximations using Fisher divergence outperform conventional KL-based methods on benchmark tasks, leading to improved coverage of target distributions and enhanced estimation accuracy. Our findings suggest that integrating Fisher divergence into the variational inference pipeline offers significant benefits for probabilistic modeling, with applications in Bayesian neural networks, unsupervised learning, and complex generative models. Keywords: variational inference, Fisher divergence, probabilistic modeling, Bayesian learning, approximate inference.
