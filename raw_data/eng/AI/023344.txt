This paper introduces Deep Geodesic Learning (DGL), a novel framework for simultaneous segmentation and anatomical landmarking in medical images. DGL leverages deep neural networks to estimate geodesic distances from a sparse set of user-defined points, effectively encoding shape and anatomical context. By integrating geodesic information into the learning process, our method overcomes limitations of purely Euclidean approaches, particularly in cases with complex anatomical structures and topological variations. The network is trained to predict signed geodesic distance maps, which are subsequently used for both segmentation and landmark localization through simple post-processing steps. We evaluate DGL on multiple anatomical structures, demonstrating improved segmentation accuracy and landmarking precision compared to state-of-the-art methods. Specifically, DGL exhibits robustness to noise and variations in image quality, achieving superior performance in challenging anatomical regions. The proposed framework offers a flexible and efficient solution for medical image analysis, with potential applications in computer-assisted surgery, diagnostic imaging, and personalized medicine. Keywords: deep learning, geodesic distance, segmentation, anatomical landmarking, medical image analysis.
