**Abstract**

Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) have become foundational models in unsupervised learning and generative modeling, but their training is often challenged by instability issues such as poor convergence, mode collapse, and sensitivity to hyperparameters. This paper addresses these limitations by introducing novel re-parameterization techniques designed to enhance the stability of VAEs during training. Our approach systematically restructures the latent variable sampling and transformation processes to mitigate variance and gradient-related instabilities. We evaluate the proposed methods across multiple benchmark datasets and compare system performance against standard VAEs and several state-of-the-art stabilization strategies. Experimental results demonstrate that our re-parameterization framework consistently improves training robustness, accelerates convergence, and achieves superior generative quality, as measured by reconstruction error and sample fidelity. The findings suggest that re-parameterizing VAEs in the manner described not only fortifies their training process but also broadens their applicability in complex generative tasks. This work provides practical insights for deploying stable VAEs in real-world applications involving deep generative models, probabilistic inference, and latent space learning.

**Keywords:** Variational Autoencoder, re-parameterization, model stability, generative modeling, unsupervised learning, latent representation, deep learning.
