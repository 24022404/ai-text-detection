This paper aims to investigate the performance of reinforcement learning (RL) benchmarks using a novel approach called random weight guessing. The objective is to analyze the effectiveness of RL algorithms in solving complex tasks by initializing neural network weights randomly and evaluating their impact on learning outcomes. Our method involves employing a range of RL benchmarks, including Atari games and robotic control tasks, and comparing the results of random weight guessing with traditional RL methods. The results show that random weight guessing can achieve surprisingly competitive performance in certain tasks, highlighting the importance of weight initialization in RL. Our findings have significant implications for the development of more efficient and robust RL algorithms, and demonstrate the potential of random weight guessing as a simple yet effective technique for improving RL performance. Key contributions of this research include the introduction of random weight guessing as a benchmarking tool for RL, and the identification of task-specific characteristics that influence the effectiveness of this approach. Relevant keywords: reinforcement learning, random weight guessing, neural networks, Atari games, robotic control.
