Causal discovery aims to infer causal relationships from observational data, a challenging task particularly when dealing with non-Gaussian data distributions. This paper presents a novel approach for causal discovery in linear acyclic models (LAMs) that accommodates arbitrary distributions, moving beyond the limitations of traditional Gaussian assumptions. Our method leverages a combination of independence-based constraint satisfaction and score-based optimization techniques to identify the underlying causal structure. Specifically, we introduce a new score function, based on information-theoretic principles, that is robust to deviations from normality. We demonstrate the efficacy of our approach through extensive simulations on synthetic datasets with various non-Gaussian distributions and compare its performance against existing state-of-the-art causal discovery algorithms. The results show that our method achieves superior accuracy in recovering the true causal graph, especially when the data exhibits significant non-Gaussianity. This work contributes a robust and versatile tool for causal inference, applicable in diverse domains where data distributions are unknown or non-Gaussian.

Keywords: Causal discovery, linear acyclic models, non-Gaussian data, causal inference, constraint-based methods, score-based methods, information theory.
