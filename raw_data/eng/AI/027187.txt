This study aims to develop an automated quality control framework for image segmentation in cardiac magnetic resonance (MR) imaging, with a focus on the UK Biobank imaging study. Our approach utilizes a combination of machine learning algorithms and image processing techniques to detect and correct errors in image segmentation, ensuring high-quality outputs for downstream analysis. The proposed framework is evaluated on a large-scale dataset of cardiac MR images from the UK Biobank, demonstrating its effectiveness in identifying and correcting segmentation errors. Results show significant improvements in segmentation accuracy and robustness, with potential applications in large-scale imaging studies and clinical decision support systems. The novelty of this research lies in its ability to automate quality control in image segmentation, reducing manual annotation efforts and enabling faster processing of large imaging datasets. Key contributions include the development of a robust and generalizable framework for automated quality control, with implications for improved accuracy and efficiency in medical image analysis. Relevant keywords: image segmentation, quality control, cardiac MR imaging, machine learning, UK Biobank, automated annotation.
