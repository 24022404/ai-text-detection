Graph representation learning has emerged as a critical area in machine learning, enabling the extraction of meaningful features from graph-structured data. However, many existing methods rely heavily on labeled data, which can be scarce or costly to obtain. This paper introduces a novel self-supervised learning framework for graph representation learning, leveraging global context prediction to capture rich structural and semantic information without requiring labeled data. Our approach employs a graph neural network (GNN) backbone enhanced with a global context prediction task, where the model learns to predict the global properties of a graph from its local substructures. This task encourages the model to encode both local and global graph characteristics, leading to more robust and generalizable representations. We evaluate our method on multiple benchmark datasets for node classification and graph classification tasks, demonstrating superior performance compared to state-of-the-art self-supervised and semi-supervised baselines. The results highlight the effectiveness of global context prediction in improving representation quality and downstream task performance. Our work contributes a scalable and label-efficient framework for graph representation learning, with potential applications in social network analysis, molecular property prediction, and recommendation systems.  

**Keywords**: graph representation learning, self-supervised learning, graph neural networks, global context prediction, node classification, graph classification.
