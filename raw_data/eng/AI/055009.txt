This paper introduces privGAN, a novel approach designed to protect Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) from membership inference attacks. The objective is to develop a method that safeguards the privacy of training data without significantly compromising the performance of GANs. Our approach involves modifying the GAN training process to include a privacy-preserving mechanism, which adds noise to the training data in a way that prevents attackers from determining whether a given data point was used in training. We evaluate privGAN using several benchmark datasets and demonstrate its effectiveness in defending against membership inference attacks while maintaining the quality of generated samples. Our results show that privGAN achieves a significant reduction in the success rate of membership inference attacks at a low computational cost, making it a practical solution for real-world applications. The key contributions of this work include the development of a privacy-preserving GAN framework, the demonstration of its efficacy in protecting sensitive information, and the exploration of the trade-off between privacy and model performance. Relevant keywords: GANs, membership inference attacks, privacy preservation, differential privacy, deep learning.
