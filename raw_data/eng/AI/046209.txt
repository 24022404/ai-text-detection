This paper explores the innovative approach of framing branch prediction as a reinforcement learning (RL) problem, aiming to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of prediction models in computer architecture. By leveraging RL, we seek to address the inherent challenges of traditional methods and provide a robust framework for adaptive prediction.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel adaptation of reinforcement learning algorithms specifically designed for branch prediction tasks. The methodology involves the integration of policy learning techniques that enable dynamic decision-making processes in predicting branching behaviors. Additionally, we developed several case studies to illustrate the practical implementation of our approach, using a combination of simulated environments and real-world datasets.

Results/Findings: Our empirical evaluations demonstrate that the RL-based branch prediction model significantly outperforms conventional models in terms of prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. The findings indicate a remarkable improvement in handling complex branching scenarios, with the proposed model achieving higher success rates in diverse computational settings.

Conclusion/Implications: The research presents a substantive advancement in branch prediction technologies by introducing a reinforcement learning framework that adapts to varied computational demands. This approach not only advances the theoretical understanding of branch prediction but also offers practical applications in optimizing processing pipelines for modern CPUs. The success of our model highlights the potential of RL in addressing complex predictive tasks in computer architecture, setting the stage for future research and development in this domain.

Keywords: branch prediction, reinforcement learning, computer architecture, policy learning, adaptive prediction, computational efficiency.
