This paper addresses the challenge of detecting anomalies in data, a critical task in various domains such as cybersecurity, finance, and healthcare. Traditional anomaly detection models often struggle with consistently high performance across diverse datasets. This study aims to enhance detection accuracy by developing ensembles of anomaly detectors trained on synthetic data, which provide a versatile and scalable solution.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel method to create ensemble models by aggregating multiple anomaly detection techniques, each previously proven effective in its own right. Synthetic data is generated to encompass a wide range of anomaly characteristics, providing a robust foundation for evaluation and training. The ensemble approach leverages the strengths of individual models and offsets their weaknesses, using techniques like majority voting and weighted averaging to combine outputs.

Results/Findings: Our experiments, conducted on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrate improved performance in anomaly detection compared to standalone models. The ensemble method shows superior adaptability, accurately identifying anomalies with varying distributions and densities. Furthermore, comparative analysis highlights the improved precision and recall rates achieved by the ensemble model over traditional methods.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a novel ensemble-based framework for anomaly detection, emphasizing the value of synthetic data in model training. The findings suggest significant potential for application in environments requiring robust anomaly detection capabilities. This approach not only enhances detection accuracy but also reduces the likelihood of false positives, thus providing a reliable tool for practitioners across multiple domains. Key keywords include anomaly detection, ensemble learning, synthetic data, and model aggregation.
