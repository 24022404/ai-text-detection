Metric learning plays a pivotal role in numerous machine learning applications, enabling the effective measurement of similarity between data points. However, existing generalization bounds for non-linear metric learning often depend on the dimensionality of the input space, limiting their applicability in high-dimensional settings. This paper addresses this challenge by deriving dimension-free generalization bounds for non-linear metric learning algorithms. We propose a novel theoretical framework that leverages the intrinsic geometry of the data and the properties of non-linear transformations to establish bounds independent of the input dimension. Our approach employs advanced techniques from statistical learning theory and functional analysis, ensuring robustness across diverse datasets. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our bounds provide tighter guarantees compared to traditional methods, particularly in high-dimensional scenarios. The findings highlight the potential of dimension-free generalization bounds to enhance the scalability and reliability of metric learning in real-world applications, such as image retrieval, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics. This work contributes to the broader understanding of generalization in non-linear learning systems and paves the way for more efficient and scalable metric learning algorithms.  

**Keywords**: Metric learning, generalization bounds, non-linear transformations, high-dimensional data, statistical learning theory, scalability.
