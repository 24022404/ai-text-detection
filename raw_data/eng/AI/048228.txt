This paper introduces a variational inference framework for learning Markov processes from time series data. The objective is to efficiently estimate the transition probabilities of a Markov chain given observed sequences, addressing the challenge of computational complexity in traditional methods. Our approach utilizes a variational distribution to approximate the posterior distribution over transition probabilities, enabling scalable inference even with long time series. We derive a tractable evidence lower bound (ELBO) that is optimized using stochastic gradient descent. Empirical results on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our variational method achieves comparable or superior accuracy in estimating transition probabilities compared to existing techniques, while significantly reducing computational time, especially for high-dimensional state spaces. The learned Markov models can be effectively used for prediction and anomaly detection in sequential data. Keywords: Markov process, variational inference, time series, Bayesian learning, transition probabilities.
