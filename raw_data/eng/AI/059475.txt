Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have emerged as a powerful framework in machine learning for generating realistic data across various domains. Despite their success, training GANs remains a complex task due to inherent instability and convergence issues during optimization, particularly when using gradient descent methods. This paper investigates the local stability characteristics of GAN optimization under gradient descent, aiming to provide theoretical insight and practical guidance for GAN training. We develop a rigorous mathematical analysis of the GAN optimization landscape, examining the interaction between the generator and discriminator dynamics near equilibrium points. Through both analytical and empirical approaches, we demonstrate that under certain conditions related to the architecture and learning rates, gradient descent GAN optimization exhibits local stability. Our results further elucidate how specific hyperparameter choices and network structures affect stability and convergence properties, providing key recommendations for practitioners. The findings from this study advance understanding of GAN training dynamics, enabling more reliable deployment of GANs in applications such as image synthesis, data augmentation, and unsupervised representation learning. Keywords: GAN, gradient descent, optimization, stability, machine learning, convergence, generative models.
