Identifying individual trees outside forests (ITOFs) is crucial for biodiversity assessments, carbon stock estimation, and agroforestry monitoring, particularly in regions with sparse tree cover. This paper introduces a novel global method for ITOF detection using medium-resolution satellite imagery. Our approach combines spectral unmixing techniques with a newly developed spatial clustering algorithm to differentiate ITOF signatures from surrounding land cover types. We leverage Landsat imagery and Google Earth Engine to process large-scale datasets efficiently. The method was validated across diverse landscapes, including savanna, agricultural, and semi-arid regions, demonstrating high accuracy in ITOF identification compared to manual ground truth data. Results show an overall F1-score of 0.85, significantly outperforming existing pixel-based classification methods. This research provides a robust and scalable tool for mapping and monitoring ITOFs globally, enabling improved environmental management and conservation efforts. Keywords: Trees outside forests, remote sensing, satellite imagery, object detection, spectral unmixing, spatial clustering, Landsat, Google Earth Engine.
