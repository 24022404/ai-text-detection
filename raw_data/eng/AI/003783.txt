Object detection and instance segmentation models often encounter significant challenges when dealing with long-tailed data distributions, where a few classes are heavily represented while most are rare. This imbalance leads to biased predictions and poor generalization, particularly for underrepresented classes. This paper investigates model calibration—ensuring model confidence aligns with prediction accuracy—in the context of long-tailed object detection and instance segmentation. We systematically analyze the limitations of existing calibration techniques under long-tailed scenarios and propose a novel calibration framework tailored for such distributions. Our approach integrates distribution-aware loss functions with dynamic temperature scaling to balance model confidence across both frequent and rare classes, without sacrificing overall detection performance. Extensive experiments on benchmarks such as LVIS and COCO demonstrate that our method not only improves calibration metrics, such as Expected Calibration Error (ECE), but also enhances average precision and recall for rare categories. The findings highlight the importance of calibration-aware training for equitable model performance in real-world, imbalanced datasets, and suggest practical applications in AI systems where accurate uncertainty estimation is critical. Keywords: long-tailed distribution, model calibration, object detection, instance segmentation, confidence estimation, class imbalance.
