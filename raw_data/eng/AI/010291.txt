This paper addresses the challenge of efficient image restoration by introducing a novel spatial activation function, termed as xUnit. Traditional image restoration techniques often use fixed activation functions, which may not fully exploit spatial information, leading to suboptimal performance. The research targets enhancing image quality while maintaining computational efficiency.

Methods/Approach: The proposed xUnit incorporates a dynamic, learnable spatial activation mechanism within convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to adaptively modulate feature responses at each spatial location. This method optimizes the restoration process by tailoring activations to the distinct spatial characteristics of each image. The approach is validated through integration with standard image restoration architectures and experimental comparison on benchmark datasets.

Results/Findings: The implementation of xUnit demonstrated significant improvements in image restoration tasks, including denoising, deblurring, and super-resolution. Compared to existing methods with static activation functions, xUnit achieved superior quantitative metrics such as PSNR and SSIM across diverse dataset scenarios. Furthermore, the computational overhead introduced by xUnit is minimal, ensuring its applicability in real-time image processing applications.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a novel adaptive approach to spatial activation in CNNs, addressing the limitations of static functions. The results suggest that xUnit effectively enhances image restoration performance, providing a new avenue for developing efficient image processing models. Future work may explore its integration with other advanced architectures and real-world applications, such as medical imaging and autonomous driving. Keywords: Spatial Activation Function, Image Restoration, xUnit, CNN, Denoising, Deblurring, Super-resolution.
