Edge computing offers a promising paradigm for deploying Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) by performing inference closer to data sources. However, resource constraints on edge devices necessitate efficient model compression techniques. This paper introduces Foothill, a novel quasiconvex regularization method designed to optimize DNN model compression specifically for edge computing environments. Unlike traditional convex regularization approaches, Foothill leverages a quasiconvex penalty term to induce sparsity in the DNN parameters while preserving crucial performance characteristics. Our method promotes structured sparsity, facilitating hardware-aware pruning and quantization. We evaluate Foothill on several benchmark datasets and DNN architectures, demonstrating significant reductions in model size and computational complexity compared to existing L1 and L2 regularization techniques, with minimal impact on accuracy. The experimental results show that Foothill-compressed models achieve up to a 5x reduction in model size and a 3x speedup in inference time on edge devices. These findings highlight the potential of Foothill to enable efficient and accurate DNN inference in resource-constrained edge computing environments. Keywords: Edge Computing, Deep Neural Networks, Model Compression, Regularization, Quasiconvex Optimization, Sparsity.
