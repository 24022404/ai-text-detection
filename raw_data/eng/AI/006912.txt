This paper investigates the problem of regret minimization in reinforcement learning (RL) using kernel-based methods. We focus on episodic RL with continuous state and action spaces, where the transition dynamics are unknown. We introduce a novel kernelized least-squares value iteration algorithm that leverages the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) to represent value functions. Our main contribution is deriving a finite-sample regret bound for the proposed algorithm. Specifically, we show that the cumulative regret scales sublinearly with the number of episodes, achieving a rate of $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T})$, where $T$ is the total number of episodes. The analysis relies on carefully controlling the approximation error and the estimation error introduced by the kernel method. Furthermore, we provide theoretical insights into the impact of kernel choice on the regret performance. Empirical evaluations on benchmark RL environments demonstrate the effectiveness of our algorithm compared to existing kernel-based and non-kernel-based approaches. This work provides a theoretical foundation for the use of kernel methods in RL and offers a practical algorithm with provable regret guarantees.
Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Kernel Methods, Regret Bound, Value Iteration, RKHS.
