Learning invariant representations is a critical challenge in machine learning, particularly for tasks requiring robustness to domain shifts or sensitive attribute variations. Traditional approaches often rely on adversarial training, which introduces computational complexity and instability during optimization. This paper proposes a novel framework for learning invariant representations without adversarial training, offering a more efficient and stable alternative. Our method leverages a combination of contrastive learning and regularization techniques to enforce invariance while preserving task-relevant information. We evaluate the proposed approach on benchmark datasets across multiple domains, including image classification and natural language processing. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves competitive or superior performance compared to adversarial-based approaches, with significantly reduced training time and improved stability. Furthermore, the framework exhibits strong generalization capabilities under domain shifts and adversarial attacks. The findings highlight the potential of non-adversarial methods for invariant representation learning, paving the way for more scalable and reliable solutions in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: invariant representations, contrastive learning, regularization, domain adaptation, machine learning, robustness.
