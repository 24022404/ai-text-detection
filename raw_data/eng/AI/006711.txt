This paper introduces Wasserstein Adversarial Imitation Learning (WAIL), a novel framework for imitation learning that leverages the Wasserstein distance metric to enhance the stability and performance of adversarial training. Traditional adversarial imitation learning methods often suffer from training instability and mode collapse due to the use of Jensen-Shannon divergence. WAIL addresses these limitations by incorporating the Wasserstein distance, which provides smoother gradients and improved convergence properties. The proposed approach employs a generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture, where the discriminator is trained to minimize the Wasserstein distance between the expert and agent policies, while the generator learns to mimic the expert behavior. Experiments conducted on benchmark reinforcement learning environments demonstrate that WAIL achieves superior imitation performance compared to state-of-the-art methods, with faster convergence and greater robustness to hyperparameter variations. The results highlight the potential of WAIL for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and other domains requiring precise imitation of expert behavior. This work contributes a theoretically grounded and empirically validated framework that advances the field of imitation learning by addressing key challenges in adversarial training.  

**Keywords**: Imitation learning, Wasserstein distance, adversarial training, reinforcement learning, generative adversarial networks (GANs).
