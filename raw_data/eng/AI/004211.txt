Glass-like object segmentation presents unique challenges due to the transparent and reflective properties of such objects, which often blend into their surroundings. This paper introduces an enhanced boundary learning framework designed to improve the accuracy and robustness of glass-like object segmentation in complex scenes. The proposed approach leverages a dual-stream network architecture that combines global contextual information with fine-grained boundary details. A novel boundary-aware loss function is introduced to explicitly guide the model in learning precise object boundaries, addressing the limitations of traditional segmentation methods. Additionally, a synthetic dataset augmentation strategy is employed to enhance the model's generalization capabilities across diverse real-world scenarios. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing approaches in terms of both boundary precision and segmentation accuracy. The framework's ability to handle challenging cases, such as overlapping objects and varying lighting conditions, highlights its practical applicability in fields like robotics, augmented reality, and autonomous driving. This research contributes a significant advancement in glass-like object segmentation, offering a scalable and effective solution for real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: glass-like object segmentation, boundary learning, dual-stream network, boundary-aware loss, synthetic dataset augmentation, computer vision.
