Bayesian networks, represented as directed acyclic graphs (DAGs), are fundamental tools for modeling complex dependencies in probabilistic systems across various domains such as healthcare, finance, and artificial intelligence. However, the problem of learning the optimal DAG structure from observational data is both computationally challenging and susceptible to local optima, limiting the scalability and robustness of existing methods. In this paper, we systematically investigate continuous optimization strategies for learning Bayesian networks, focusing on novel formulations that relax the discrete search space while preserving essential acyclicity constraints. We propose an improved continuous optimization framework that employs smooth, differentiable penalty functions to enforce acyclicity and leverages advanced gradient-based solvers. Our experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate superior performance in terms of structure accuracy and computational efficiency compared to popular score-based and constraint-based approaches. Additionally, we dissect the impact of optimization landscape properties on convergence behavior, shedding light on the practical limitations and opportunities of continuous methods. The findings highlight the potential of continuous optimization for scalable and reliable Bayesian network learning, providing new directions for structure discovery in complex probabilistic models. Key keywords: Bayesian networks, DAG learning, continuous optimization, acyclicity constraints, probabilistic graphical models, structure learning.
