This paper presents a novel method for realistic and efficient rendering of atmospheric clouds using radiance-predicting neural networks. Traditional cloud rendering techniques often rely on Monte Carlo-based volumetric path tracing, which, while physically accurate, can be computationally intensive and unsuitable for interactive applications. Our approach leverages deep learning to directly predict radiance values for participating media, significantly accelerating the rendering process without compromising visual fidelity. We design and train a deep neural network to model complex light scattering phenomena in heterogeneous cloud volumes, incorporating multi-scale spatial and angular features to improve prediction accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves high-quality visual results that closely match ground-truth path-traced images, while reducing rendering times by an order of magnitude. The proposed framework outperforms conventional rendering techniques in both speed and image quality, enabling real-time exploration and adjustment of atmospheric cloud scenes. This work introduces an effective integration of neural networks into the physically based rendering pipeline for participating media, advancing the state-of-the-art in data-driven graphics and atmospheric simulation. Keywords: physically based rendering, neural networks, volumetric scattering, atmospheric clouds, real-time graphics, deep learning.
