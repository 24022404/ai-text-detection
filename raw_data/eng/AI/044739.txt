The rapid evolution of DeepFakes poses significant challenges for media authenticity, privacy, and information security. This paper investigates the progression of DeepFake generation techniques, focusing on the analysis of manipulated facial regions and the corresponding impact on DeepFake detection performance. We present a comparative study of state-of-the-art DeepFake generation models, systematically deconstructing how different facial areas—such as eyes, mouth, and jawline—are targeted and altered in synthetic media. Utilizing a suite of deep learning-based detectors, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformer-based architectures, we evaluate detection accuracy across various publicly available DeepFake datasets. Our findings reveal that certain facial regions are consistently more vulnerable to manipulation, and detection effectiveness varies depending on the region analyzed. Furthermore, we demonstrate that region-aware detection strategies significantly improve robustness against new generation DeepFakes. The insights derived from this work contribute to the development of more resilient DeepFake detection systems, providing a pathway towards safeguarding digital media integrity. Keywords: DeepFakes, facial region analysis, fake detection, deep learning, media forensics, CNN, transformer, synthetic media.
