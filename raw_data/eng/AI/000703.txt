Matrix Neural Networks (MNNs) represent a novel approach to neural network architectures by directly processing matrix-structured data, rather than relying on conventional vectorization techniques. This paper aims to address the limitations inherent in traditional neural networks when dealing with multi-dimensional data commonly encountered in fields such as image analysis, signal processing, and spatiotemporal modeling. We propose an enhanced matrix-based neural network framework that preserves the intrinsic structure of input data throughout the learning process, introducing specialized layers and operations designed for efficient matrix-to-matrix transformations.

Our methodology involves the development of matrix neuron units and custom activation, pooling, and normalization functions tailored to matrix data. By leveraging these innovations, MNNs reduce redundant parameters and enhance learning efficiency, demonstrating improved performance on benchmark tasks including image classification, video sequence prediction, and correlation analysis. Experimental evaluations show that MNNs outperform conventional neural networks and tensor-based approaches in both accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly in scenarios with high-dimensional structured inputs.

These findings highlight the significant potential of matrix neural networks for a wide range of machine learning and data analysis applications, paving the way for further research into matrix-centric architectures. The proposed MNN framework offers a promising direction for developing more compact, interpretable, and scalable neural networks that better exploit the latent structure of complex data. Keywords: matrix neural networks, matrix-structured data, deep learning, dimensionality reduction, neural architectures, pattern recognition.
