This paper introduces MAGNet, a novel Multi-agent Graph Network designed to enhance the coordination and learning capacity of deep multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) systems. The research addresses the challenges of scalability and cooperation among multiple agents in complex environments, aiming to improve collective decision-making efficiency.

Methods/Approach: MAGNet employs a graph-based architecture that represents agents and their interactions in a dynamic environment. The network leverages graph neural networks (GNNs) to model the inter-agent communication, enabling efficient representation and processing of both the individual and collective state-action spaces. The architecture integrates a learning mechanism that optimizes policy gradients tailored for cooperative tasks, facilitating effective learning in multi-agent settings.

Results/Findings: The experimentations demonstrate that MAGNet significantly outperforms traditional MARL frameworks in terms of learning speed and adaptability to new tasks. It showcases enhanced scalability by efficiently managing increased agent numbers without a corresponding decline in system performance. Comparative analysis across various benchmark environments reveals that MAGNet achieves higher success rates and more robust coordination, particularly in tasks requiring precise collaborative strategies.

Conclusion/Implications: MAGNet offers a substantial contribution to the field of MARL by introducing a scalable and efficient architecture capable of fostering improved agent collaboration. The insights gained from this research provide a foundation for deploying intelligent multi-agent systems across diverse applications, such as autonomous vehicles, resource management, and robotics. Future work will explore further enhancements to the MAGNet architecture to support more complex dynamic interactions and extend its applicability to real-world multi-agent scenarios. Key keywords include multi-agent reinforcement learning, deep learning, graph networks, scalability, and collaborative strategies.
