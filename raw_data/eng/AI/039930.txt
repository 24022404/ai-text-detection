This paper addresses the challenge of monocular videovisual odometry and depth estimation, crucial for understanding and navigating 3D environments using only a single camera. We explore both supervised and unsupervised learning paradigms to enhance the precision and robustness of visual odometry and depth estimation tasks.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) architecture tailored specifically for sequential video data, allowing for the effective capture of temporal dependencies between frames. Our approach leverages LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) units to retain crucial information over time and incorporates both supervised and unsupervised methods. In the supervised setting, the model is trained on ground truth data of visual odometry and depth, while the unsupervised model utilizes photometric consistency across frames to shape its understanding of the geometry.

Results/Findings: Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, our model demonstrates superior performance in visual odometry and depth estimation tasks compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. The RNN architecture significantly improves the estimation accuracy under dynamic and complex scenes, particularly in unsupervised scenarios where data labeling is costly or unavailable.

Conclusion/Implications: This research provides a compelling framework for enhancing monocular visual odometry and depth estimation using RNNs, applicable in domains such as autonomous driving, augmented reality, and robotics. Our dual approach bridges the gap between supervision levels, offering flexibility and improved accuracy, marking a substantial contribution to the field of computer vision.

Keywords: Recurrent Neural Network, RNN, monocular video, videovisual odometry, depth estimation, supervised learning, unsupervised learning, LSTM, computer vision.
