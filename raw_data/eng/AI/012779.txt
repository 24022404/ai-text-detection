Expectation Propagation (EP) is a powerful approximate Bayesian inference technique, but its computational cost and convergence speed can be limiting factors, particularly for complex models. This paper introduces two novel algorithms designed to accelerate the convergence of EP while maintaining accuracy. First, we propose a damped EP algorithm that incorporates a damping factor to stabilize the iterative updates of the approximate posterior, preventing oscillations and divergence. Second, we develop an accelerated EP algorithm based on Anderson acceleration, which extrapolates from previous iterations to predict a more optimal solution. We demonstrate the efficacy of our algorithms on several benchmark problems, including Bayesian logistic regression and Gaussian mixture models. Empirical results show that our proposed algorithms significantly reduce the number of iterations required for convergence compared to standard EP, leading to substantial computational savings. Furthermore, our algorithms exhibit improved stability and are less prone to divergence. These fast convergent EP algorithms offer a practical alternative for approximate Bayesian inference in computationally demanding scenarios. Keywords: Expectation Propagation, Bayesian Inference, Approximate Inference, Convergence Acceleration, Damping, Anderson Acceleration.
