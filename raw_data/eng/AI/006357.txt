**Abstract:**

Few-shot learning, which enables models to generalize from limited labeled data, remains a fundamental challenge in computer vision and pattern recognition. Traditional approaches often neglect the spatial relationships inherent in visual data, resulting in suboptimal feature transfer between tasks. This paper introduces CrossTransformers, a novel architecture designed to enhance few-shot transfer by incorporating spatial awareness into the transfer process. By leveraging attention mechanisms that explicitly match spatial features between support and query images, CrossTransformers learn robust mappings that preserve spatial correspondences. Our approach dynamically selects and aligns relevant regions across examples, enabling precise knowledge transfer even in highly variable or cluttered visual scenes. Experimental evaluations on multiple few-shot classification benchmarks, including miniImageNet and tieredImageNet, demonstrate that CrossTransformers consistently outperform state-of-the-art baselines in accuracy and robustness. The proposed method sets a new standard for spatially-aware transfer in few-shot learning, opening opportunities for its application in fine-grained recognition, medical imaging, and other domains where spatial context is crucial. Key innovations include a cross-attention framework for spatial feature alignment and an efficient inference mechanism for rapid adaptation. Keywords: few-shot learning, spatial attention, transfer learning, cross-attention, deep learning, pattern recognition.
