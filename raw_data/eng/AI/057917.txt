The increasing demand for efficient and accurate threat detection in X-ray security imaging has driven significant advancements in deep learning techniques. This paper provides a comprehensive survey of recent developments in the application of deep learning for automatic threat detection within X-ray security systems. The objective is to explore how state-of-the-art models, such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformer-based architectures, have been adapted to address challenges like object occlusion, varying threat types, and real-time processing constraints. We review key methodologies, including supervised, semi-supervised, and unsupervised learning approaches, as well as the integration of multi-modal data fusion techniques to enhance detection accuracy. The survey highlights notable improvements in threat classification, localization, and false-positive reduction, with comparisons to traditional machine learning and rule-based systems. Our findings demonstrate that deep learning-based methods consistently outperform conventional approaches, achieving higher precision and recall rates across diverse datasets. Furthermore, we discuss the implications of these advancements for real-world security applications, such as airport baggage screening and cargo inspection, emphasizing the potential for scalability and adaptability to emerging threats. This work underscores the transformative role of deep learning in X-ray security imaging and identifies future research directions, including the need for robust generalization and explainability in automated threat detection systems.  

**Keywords**: Deep learning, X-ray security imaging, threat detection, convolutional neural networks, object localization, multi-modal fusion.
