This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently learning latent graphical models, which are pivotal for understanding complex dependencies in high-dimensional data. Traditional global learning methods often face scalability issues, motivating the need for a more localized and sequential approach.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel method called Sequential Local Learning (SLL) that iteratively updates the structure and parameters of latent graphical models by focusing on local subgraphs. This method leverages a divide-and-conquer strategy, allowing for localized learning while ensuring consistent global integration. Our algorithm intelligently identifies relevant subgraphs and applies localized updates, enhancing overall learning efficiency without compromising accuracy.

Results/Findings: Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the SLL approach significantly outperforms traditional global learning techniques in terms of both scalability and accuracy. Results show notable improvements in learning speed while maintaining or improving the quality of inferred graphical structures. Comparative analyses with established methods highlight the competitive edge of our approach in various scenarios, particularly in large-scale applications.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed Sequential Local Learning framework offers a robust and scalable solution for latent graphical model learning, paving the way for more efficient data analysis in high-dimensional spaces. Its ability to handle complex dependencies with minimized computational overhead provides significant advantages for applications in fields such as bioinformatics, social network analysis, and machine learning. This research contributes to the ongoing advancement of local learning paradigms, offering potential pathways for future innovation in graphical model inference.

Keywords: Sequential Local Learning, Latent Graphical Models, Scalability, High-Dimensional Data, Localized Updates, Divide-and-Conquer Strategy.
