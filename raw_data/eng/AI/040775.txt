Image captioning, the task of automatically generating textual descriptions of images, remains a challenging problem in computer vision and natural language processing. This paper introduces a novel text-guided attention model designed to improve the relevance and coherence of generated captions. Our approach incorporates a text encoder that captures semantic information from previously generated words, which is then used to guide the attention mechanism in the image encoder. This allows the model to focus on image regions that are most relevant to the current textual context, leading to more accurate and contextually appropriate captions. We evaluate our model on the COCO dataset, demonstrating significant improvements over existing attention-based captioning models, particularly in generating captions that are semantically aligned with the image content and human preferences. Experimental results show that our model achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of BLEU, METEOR, and CIDEr scores. The proposed method offers a promising direction for enhancing the quality of automatically generated image descriptions by explicitly leveraging textual context to guide visual attention. Keywords: image captioning, attention mechanism, text encoder, computer vision, natural language processing.
