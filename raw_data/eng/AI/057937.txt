Learning curves, which plot performance against experience, are fundamental to understanding and improving machine learning systems. This review synthesizes a broad range of research on the shapes of learning curves, aiming to provide a comprehensive overview of their characteristics, underlying causes, and practical implications. We examine theoretical models that predict various learning curve shapes, including power laws, exponentials, and more complex functions, discussing the assumptions and limitations of each. Furthermore, we analyze empirical studies across diverse learning tasks and algorithms, highlighting how factors such as dataset properties, model architecture, and optimization techniques influence learning curve behavior. Our analysis identifies common patterns and deviations, providing insights into the generalization capabilities and sample complexity of learning algorithms. Finally, we explore applications of learning curve analysis for model selection, hyperparameter optimization, and early stopping, demonstrating how understanding learning curve shapes can improve the efficiency and effectiveness of machine learning workflows. This review offers a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners seeking to leverage learning curves for better algorithm design and performance prediction.

Keywords: learning curves, machine learning, generalization, model selection, performance prediction, sample complexity.
