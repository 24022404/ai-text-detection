Video summarization aims to generate concise and informative summaries from lengthy videos, a challenging task due to the diverse content and varying importance of different segments. This paper introduces a novel meta-learning framework for task-driven video summarization. Our approach learns to adapt quickly to new summarization tasks, defined by different user preferences or specific video content types, by leveraging prior experience from related tasks. We employ a meta-learner to initialize and guide the training of task-specific summarization models, enabling faster convergence and improved generalization. The framework incorporates a hierarchical attention mechanism to capture both local and global context within videos, crucial for identifying salient segments relevant to the given task. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our meta-learning approach outperforms state-of-the-art video summarization methods, particularly in scenarios with limited task-specific training data. The proposed method offers a flexible and efficient solution for generating personalized and context-aware video summaries. Keywords: Video Summarization, Meta-Learning, Task-Driven, Attention Mechanism, Deep Learning.
