Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have emerged as a powerful tool for generating synthetic data, with significant applications in time series analysis. This paper presents a comprehensive survey and taxonomy of GAN-based approaches for time series data, addressing the challenges and opportunities in this domain. The objective is to provide a structured overview of existing methods, highlighting their strengths and limitations. We systematically categorize GAN architectures and training strategies tailored for time series, including recurrent GANs, convolutional GANs, and hybrid models. Additionally, we evaluate their performance in tasks such as anomaly detection, forecasting, and data augmentation. Our findings reveal that GANs offer promising results in generating realistic time series data, but challenges such as mode collapse and training instability persist. The taxonomy proposed in this study serves as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners, enabling informed decisions when selecting and designing GAN-based solutions for time series applications. This work contributes to advancing the field by identifying key research directions and potential improvements in GAN architectures for time series analysis.  

**Keywords**: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), time series, anomaly detection, forecasting, data augmentation, taxonomy.
