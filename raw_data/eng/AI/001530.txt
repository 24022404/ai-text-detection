This paper presents DeeSIL, a novel approach for incremental learning that efficiently integrates deep and shallow learning paradigms. The research addresses the challenge of continuously learning new tasks without forgetting previously acquired knowledge, a common problem known as catastrophic forgetting in the field of artificial intelligence and machine learning.

Methods/Approach: DeeSIL combines the strengths of deep neural networks and shallow learning methods to construct a robust model capable of incremental learning. It leverages the representational power of deep networks for feature extraction while employing shallow classifiers to manage and update tasks incrementally. This hybrid technique ensures adaptability and scalability, with particular attention to maintaining performance on prior tasks.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations of DeeSIL demonstrate its superior capability in managing incremental learning scenarios compared to existing methodologies. The model exhibits enhanced scalability and reduced forgetting, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. Specifically, DeeSIL significantly outperforms traditional incremental learning models by retaining higher accuracy on both previously learned and new tasks.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed DeeSIL framework offers substantial contributions to the field of incremental learning. Its innovative deep-shallow integration addresses the limitations of current models, providing a practical solution for real-world applications where continual learning is essential. This approach opens new avenues for research and development in adaptive AI systems, with potential applications across various domains, such as intelligent robotics, adaptive recommendation systems, and dynamic environment modeling.

Keywords: Incremental Learning, Deep Neural Networks, Shallow Learning, Catastrophic Forgetting, Hybrid Model, Adaptive AI Systems.
