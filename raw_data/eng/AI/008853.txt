This paper presents FACIAL, a novel framework for synthesizing dynamic talking faces by leveraging implicit attribute learning. The objective of this research is to address the challenges of generating realistic and synchronized talking head videos that adapt to diverse facial attributes, such as expression, head pose, and identity. The proposed approach utilizes a deep learning architecture that implicitly learns the relationships between audio signals and facial dynamics, enabling fine-grained control over facial movements and attribute preservation. Unlike prior methods that rely on explicit attribute annotations or suffer from limited generalization, FACIAL employs a self-supervised learning strategy that infers facial attributes directly from training data, enhancing both realism and flexibility. Experiments conducted on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that FACIAL achieves superior lip-sync accuracy, expression fidelity, and visual continuity compared to existing state-of-the-art models. Qualitative and quantitative evaluations further validate the model’s ability to generalize across diverse speakers, languages, and acoustic conditions. The findings highlight the potential of implicit attribute learning for robust multimodal synthesis and open new avenues for applications in virtual avatars, video conferencing, and digital entertainment. Keywords: talking face generation, implicit attribute learning, deep learning, audio-visual synthesis, facial animation, virtual avatars.
