Emotion perception from human gait is an emerging field with significant applications in human-computer interaction, surveillance, and healthcare. This paper introduces STEP, a novel Spatial Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN) framework designed to accurately infer emotional states from gait sequences. The proposed method represents human skeletal motion as a dynamic graph, where nodes correspond to joints and edges encode both spatial (body structure) and temporal (motion dynamics) relationships. By leveraging graph convolutional operations, STEP captures salient motion patterns and subtle joint coordination reflective of various emotional expressions. Extensive experiments were conducted on benchmark gait-emotion datasets, demonstrating that STEP outperforms existing solutions in terms of recognition accuracy, robustness to noise, and generalizability across diverse subjects. Comparative analyses reveal the advantages of integrating spatial-temporal features over conventional deep learning and purely spatial models. The findings highlight the potential of graph-based deep learning for affective computing tasks and lay the groundwork for future integration of emotion-aware systems in real-world applications such as behavioral monitoring and adaptive user interfaces.  
**Keywords:** emotion recognition, gait analysis, spatial temporal graph convolutional network, ST-GCN, affective computing, human activity recognition, deep learning.
