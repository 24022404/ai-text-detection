Cycling safety remains a critical concern in urban environments, with existing risk assessment methods often lacking real-time context awareness and adaptability. This paper introduces a novel context-aware and video-based risk descriptor designed to enhance cyclist safety by dynamically evaluating potential hazards. The proposed system leverages computer vision techniques and deep learning models to analyze real-time video feeds from cyclist-mounted cameras, identifying road conditions, traffic patterns, and environmental factors. A multi-layered risk assessment framework is employed, integrating object detection, motion tracking, and contextual analysis to quantify risk levels. Experimental evaluations were conducted using diverse urban cycling scenarios, demonstrating the system's ability to accurately detect and classify risks with an average precision of 92%. Comparative analysis with traditional risk assessment methods highlights significant improvements in both accuracy and adaptability. The findings suggest that this approach can serve as a foundational tool for developing advanced safety systems for cyclists, with potential applications in smart city infrastructure and autonomous vehicle integration. This research contributes to the field by introducing a real-time, context-aware solution that addresses the limitations of static risk models, paving the way for safer urban mobility.  

**Keywords**: cyclist safety, computer vision, risk assessment, deep learning, context-aware systems, urban mobility.
