Abstract:  
Node representation learning is fundamental to various graph-based tasks such as node classification, link prediction, and community detection. While recent advances have largely focused on deep learning techniques like graph neural networks (GNNs), classical kernel methods remain underexplored despite their strong theoretical properties. This paper re-examines the role of kernel methods for node representation learning on graphs, addressing common limitations such as scalability and flexibility in capturing complex node relationships. We propose a novel kernel framework tailored to graph data, which integrates graph topology with attribute information through adaptive kernel construction. Our approach introduces efficient algorithms for large-scale kernel computation, leveraging spectral graph techniques and node sampling to reduce computational overhead. Experimental results on benchmark citation and social network datasets demonstrate that our kernel-based node embeddings achieve competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art GNNs, particularly in scenarios with limited training data. Additionally, our analysis reveals interpretability advantages and robustness to overfitting. The findings suggest that revisiting kernel methods offers a valuable alternative or complement to neural approaches in graph representation learning. Keywords: node representation learning, kernel methods, graph learning, node embeddings, scalability, graph algorithms.
