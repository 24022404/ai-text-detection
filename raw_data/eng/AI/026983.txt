This paper introduces a novel weighted nonlocal total variation (WNTV) model for image processing, addressing the limitations of traditional total variation (TV) methods in preserving fine details and textures. The proposed WNTV framework leverages nonlocal self-similarity within images to construct adaptive weights, effectively differentiating between noise and important image features. This approach enhances the regularization process, leading to improved image denoising, deblurring, and inpainting results. We demonstrate the effectiveness of the WNTV model through rigorous experimental evaluations on various image datasets. The results show that the WNTV model outperforms state-of-the-art TV-based methods and other nonlocal algorithms in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), structural similarity index (SSIM), and visual quality. The WNTV model achieves a better balance between noise removal and detail preservation, offering significant potential for applications in medical imaging, remote sensing, and computer vision. Key words: Image processing, total variation, nonlocal, denoising, inpainting, regularization.
