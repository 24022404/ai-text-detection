Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have demonstrated remarkable success in modeling complex data distributions, particularly those with light-tailed characteristics. However, their ability to accurately represent heavy-tailed distributions, which are prevalent in real-world datasets such as financial data, natural phenomena, and network traffic, remains limited. This paper introduces Pareto GAN, a novel framework designed to extend the representational power of GANs to heavy-tailed distributions. The proposed approach leverages the Pareto distribution, a well-known model for heavy-tailed data, to enhance the generator's ability to capture extreme values and long-tail behavior. We present a modified loss function and training strategy that integrates Pareto principles into the adversarial learning process, ensuring stable convergence and improved performance. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that Pareto GAN outperforms traditional GANs in modeling heavy-tailed distributions, achieving higher fidelity in tail regions and better overall data representation. The framework's contributions include a new theoretical foundation for GANs in heavy-tailed settings and practical insights for applications in finance, risk analysis, and anomaly detection. This work advances the state-of-the-art in generative modeling by addressing a critical limitation of existing methods and opening new avenues for research in heavy-tailed data generation.  

**Keywords**: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), heavy-tailed distributions, Pareto distribution, extreme value modeling, generative modeling, anomaly detection.
