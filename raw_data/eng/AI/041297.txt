In the era of algorithmic decision-making, performative risk—the risk associated with deploying predictive models that influence the environment they operate in—has emerged as a critical challenge. This paper addresses the problem of optimizing performative risk in dynamic settings, where models not only predict outcomes but also shape the underlying data distribution. We propose a novel framework that integrates iterative optimization techniques with adaptive learning strategies to mitigate the feedback loops and unintended consequences often observed in performative systems. Our approach leverages gradient-based methods to dynamically adjust model parameters in response to shifts in the data distribution, ensuring robustness and stability over time. Through extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets, we demonstrate that our framework significantly reduces performative risk compared to traditional static models, achieving up to a 30% improvement in predictive accuracy under dynamic conditions. Furthermore, we provide theoretical guarantees on convergence and stability, offering insights into the conditions under which performative risk can be effectively minimized. This work contributes to the growing body of research on responsible AI by providing a practical and theoretically grounded solution for deploying models in performative environments. The implications of this research extend to applications in finance, healthcare, and social systems, where adaptive decision-making is crucial for long-term success.  

**Keywords**: performative risk, dynamic optimization, adaptive learning, algorithmic decision-making, feedback loops, responsible AI.
