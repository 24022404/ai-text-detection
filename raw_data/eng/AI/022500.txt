Graph-structured data are prevalent across various domains, necessitating effective representation learning techniques that can capture both local and global patterns within graphs. This paper addresses the challenge of aggregating multi-hop neighborhood information in graph neural networks (GNNs), where conventional layer-wise propagation may suffer from oversmoothing or inadequate feature representation. We introduce Jumping Knowledge Networks (JK-Nets), a novel architecture that adaptively leverages intermediate node representations from multiple GNN layers. By enabling flexible neighborhood ranges through layer-wise "jumping" connections, JK-Nets can dynamically select the most informative features for each node, mitigating the limitations of fixed-depth aggregation schemes. Comprehensive experiments on benchmark datasets for node classification and graph classification demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms traditional GNN models, particularly on graphs with diverse structural properties. The results highlight the effectiveness of JK-Nets in enhancing both accuracy and robustness of graph representation learning. Our findings suggest significant implications for applications in social network analysis, molecular property prediction, and knowledge graph reasoning. Key contributions include a detailed analysis of the impact of multi-scale feature aggregation, an efficient implementation of the JK-Net framework, and extensive empirical evaluations. Keywords: graph neural networks, representation learning, jumping knowledge, node classification, multi-hop aggregation, deep learning on graphs.
