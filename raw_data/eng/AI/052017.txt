This paper presents a novel approach to enhancing the performance and efficiency of deep generative models through the integration of curriculum learning with clustering techniques. The primary objective is to address the challenge of training instability and suboptimal generation quality commonly observed in deep generative models such as variational autoencoders (VAEs) and generative adversarial networks (GANs). Our method begins by employing unsupervised clustering algorithms to partition the training dataset into subsets of increasing complexity. These ordered clusters are then introduced sequentially during model training, forming a curriculum that enables the generative model to gradually learn from simple to more complex data distributions. Experimental evaluations on benchmark image datasets demonstrate that our curriculum-based training strategy leads to faster convergence, improved sample quality, and greater model robustness compared to standard training regimes. In particular, we observe superior performance in generation fidelity and diversity, as measured by metrics such as Frechet Inception Distance (FID). The proposed framework is generalizable and can be easily integrated with a variety of deep generative architectures. These results highlight the potential of curriculum learning with clustering as a powerful paradigm for training deep generative models, offering significant benefits for applications in image synthesis, data augmentation, and representation learning. Key keywords: curriculum learning, deep generative models, clustering, variational autoencoder, generative adversarial network, image synthesis, training stability.
