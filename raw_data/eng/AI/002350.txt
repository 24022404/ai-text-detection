This paper explores the potential of deeper graph convolutional neural networks (GCNNs) by investigating the impact of training methods on their performance. The objective is to overcome the limitations of current GCNNs, which often suffer from vanishing gradients and over-smoothing issues when increasing depth. We propose a novel training approach that combines careful initialization, adaptive learning rates, and regularization techniques to unlock the full potential of deeper GCNNs. Our method enables the training of significantly deeper models than previously possible, leading to improved performance on benchmark graph classification tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving superior accuracy and robustness. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of training procedures in graph neural networks and contribute to the development of more powerful and efficient GCNN architectures, with potential applications in areas such as molecular property prediction, social network analysis, and recommendation systems. Keywords: graph convolutional neural networks, deep learning, training methods, graph classification, neural architecture.
