Medical image segmentation is a crucial yet challenging task, especially with limited labeled data. This paper presents a novel approach to enhance segmentation accuracy using a Transformation Consistent Self-ensembling Model specifically designed for semi-supervised medical image segmentation.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a self-ensembling model leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data to improve segmentation performance. The model incorporates transformation consistency where data augmentation techniques, such as rotation and scaling, are employed to generate transformed versions of input images. A consistency loss is computed between the original and transformed data predictions, encouraging model robustness against variations. The self-ensembling technique utilizes an ensemble of model predictions over iterations, refining learning and ensuring high confidence in segmentation tasks.

Results/Findings: The proposed model was evaluated on benchmark medical image datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing semi-supervised approaches. Quantitative assessments indicate significant improvements in segmentation accuracy and boundary delineation, confirmed through metrics such as Dice Coefficient and Jaccard Index.

Conclusion/Implications: Our Transformation Consistent Self-ensembling Model sets a new benchmark in semi-supervised medical image segmentation by effectively utilizing unlabeled data and enhancing model robustness against data variability. This research contributes to the broader field by providing an effective strategy for segmentation where labeled training data is scarce. Potential applications include improved diagnosis and treatment planning in medical image analysis, serving as a foundation for deploying AI-driven solutions in clinical settings. Key keywords include medical image segmentation, self-ensembling, transformation consistency, semi-supervised learning, and robustness.
