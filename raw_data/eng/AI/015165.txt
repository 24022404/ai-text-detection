Few-shot learning (FSL) has emerged as a critical area in machine learning, enabling models to generalize from limited labeled data. However, existing FSL approaches often struggle with relational reasoning, a key capability for understanding complex dependencies between data points. This paper introduces Relational Generalized Few-Shot Learning (RGFSL), a novel framework designed to address this challenge by integrating relational reasoning into the few-shot learning paradigm. The proposed approach leverages graph neural networks (GNNs) to model relationships between instances in both the support and query sets, enabling the model to capture intricate dependencies and improve generalization. We evaluate RGFSL on multiple benchmark datasets, including miniImageNet and tieredImageNet, and demonstrate its superior performance compared to state-of-the-art FSL methods. Experimental results show that RGFSL achieves significant improvements in accuracy, particularly in scenarios with high relational complexity. The framework’s ability to generalize across diverse tasks and datasets highlights its potential for real-world applications, such as medical diagnosis and autonomous systems, where labeled data is scarce and relational reasoning is essential. This work contributes to advancing few-shot learning by bridging the gap between relational reasoning and generalization, offering a robust and scalable solution for data-efficient machine learning.  

**Keywords**: Few-shot learning, relational reasoning, graph neural networks, generalization, machine learning, data efficiency.
