This paper introduces Compressive Transformers, a novel approach to long-range sequence modelling that addresses the limitations of traditional transformer architectures. The objective is to enable efficient and effective modelling of long-range dependencies in sequences, which is crucial for various applications such as natural language processing and time-series forecasting. Our approach combines the strengths of transformer models with compression techniques to reduce the computational complexity and memory requirements of traditional transformers. We propose a new compression mechanism that selectively retains the most informative elements of the input sequence, allowing the model to focus on the most relevant information. Our experiments demonstrate that Compressive Transformers outperform existing methods on several benchmark datasets, achieving state-of-the-art results in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The key contributions of this research include the development of a novel compression technique, the design of a scalable and efficient transformer architecture, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in modelling long-range sequences. Our work has significant implications for the development of more efficient and accurate sequence modelling systems, with potential applications in areas such as language translation, text summarization, and speech recognition. Key keywords: Compressive Transformers, long-range sequence modelling, transformer architectures, compression techniques, natural language processing.
