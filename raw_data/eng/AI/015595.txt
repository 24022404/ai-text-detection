This paper addresses the challenge of inducing non-monotonic rules from statistical learning models, leveraging high-utility itemset mining techniques. The objective is to develop a novel approach that integrates statistical learning with high-utility itemset mining to discover informative and useful rules. Our method employs a hybrid approach, combining the strengths of statistical learning models with the efficiency of high-utility itemset mining algorithms. The results show that our proposed approach outperforms existing methods in terms of rule accuracy and usefulness, particularly in datasets with complex relationships. Key findings include the ability to induce non-monotonic rules that capture subtle patterns and relationships, leading to improved predictive performance and insight into the underlying data. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to bridge the gap between statistical learning and high-utility itemset mining, enabling the discovery of novel and actionable rules. This work has significant implications for applications in data mining, machine learning, and decision support systems, with potential applications in fields such as recommendation systems, fraud detection, and healthcare analytics. Key keywords: non-monotonic rules, statistical learning, high-utility itemset mining, data mining, machine learning.
