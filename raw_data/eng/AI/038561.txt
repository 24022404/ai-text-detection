This paper explores the concept of branch prediction as a reinforcement learning problem, addressing the challenges of accurately predicting branch outcomes in modern computer architectures. Our objective is to develop a novel approach that leverages reinforcement learning to improve branch prediction accuracy, reducing misprediction rates and enhancing overall system performance. We employ a reinforcement learning framework, utilizing techniques such as Q-learning and deep reinforcement learning, to train agents that learn to predict branch outcomes based on program execution patterns. Our results show significant improvements in branch prediction accuracy, outperforming traditional static and dynamic prediction methods. Case studies demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in various scenarios, including speculative execution and parallel processing. The proposed method contributes to the field of computer architecture and reinforcement learning, offering a new perspective on branch prediction and its potential applications in optimizing system performance. Key contributions include the formulation of branch prediction as a reinforcement learning problem, the development of a novel reinforcement learning-based prediction framework, and the demonstration of its efficacy through comprehensive case studies. Relevant keywords: branch prediction, reinforcement learning, Q-learning, deep learning, computer architecture, speculative execution.
