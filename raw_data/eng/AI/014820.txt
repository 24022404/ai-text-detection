Generalized zero-shot learning (GZSL) presents a challenging problem in the field of machine learning, where the goal is to accurately classify instances from classes that were not present in the training data. This study introduces a novel approach to enhance GZSL by employing a two-level adversarial visual-semantic coupling mechanism to improve the correlation between visual features and semantic descriptions.

Methods/Approach: The proposed approach leverages a two-level adversarial architecture, consisting of visual and semantic encoders that engage in a collaborative adversarial process. The first level focuses on optimizing a visual feature generator to produce realistic and diverse samples, while the second level simultaneously aligns these features with semantic embeddings using a semantic coupling module. This integration allows for improved feature representation and robust generalization, thus facilitating more effective zero-shot classification.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method significantly outperforms existing GZSL techniques. The two-level adversarial coupling not only achieves superior accuracy in unseen class prediction but also maintains competitive performance in seen class recognition, striking a commendable balance between the two domains. Comparative analysis reveals enhanced performance in terms of harmonic mean and overall classification accuracy.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a novel adversarial framework to the GZSL domain, highlighting the efficacy of visual-semantic coupling. By bridging the gap in feature representation between seen and unseen classes, the proposed method offers promising potential for real-world applications where data scarcity is a challenging factor. Future work could explore further enhancements in semantic embedding spaces and integration with other learning paradigms to broaden the adaptability of the model.

Keywords: generalized zero-shot learning, adversarial learning, visual-semantic coupling, feature representation, machine learning.
