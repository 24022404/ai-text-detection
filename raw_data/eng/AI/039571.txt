Pedestrian detection in thermal imagery presents unique challenges due to low contrast, varying environmental conditions, and limited publicly available training data. This paper introduces a novel approach to enhance the robustness of pedestrian detectors in thermal infrared images by leveraging synthesized thermal images for training. Our method employs a generative adversarial network (GAN) to synthesize realistic thermal images of pedestrians, conditioned on corresponding visible light images and semantic segmentation maps. These synthesized images are then used to augment the training dataset for a convolutional neural network-based pedestrian detector. Experimental results demonstrate that training with the augmented dataset significantly improves the detector's performance in terms of detection accuracy and robustness, particularly in challenging scenarios with occlusions and varying thermal conditions. The proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art detectors trained solely on real thermal images, achieving a substantial improvement in recall rate while maintaining comparable precision. This research provides a practical solution to address the scarcity of labeled thermal data and enhances the reliability of pedestrian detection systems in critical applications such as autonomous driving and surveillance.

**Keywords:** Pedestrian Detection, Thermal Imagery, Image Synthesis, Generative Adversarial Networks, Deep Learning, Robustness.
