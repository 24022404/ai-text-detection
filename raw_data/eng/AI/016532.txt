Change-point detection aims to identify abrupt shifts in the underlying distribution of time series data. This paper introduces a novel change-point detection method based on direct density ratio estimation, addressing limitations of existing approaches that often struggle with complex, high-dimensional data or require specific distributional assumptions. Our approach utilizes kernel density estimation to directly estimate the density ratio between consecutive time windows, circumventing the need for explicit density estimation. A change-point is identified when the estimated density ratio deviates significantly from unity. We demonstrate the generalization ability of our method across various time series datasets, including synthetic data with known change-points and real-world sensor data. Experimental results show that our method achieves comparable or superior performance in terms of detection accuracy and robustness compared to state-of-the-art techniques, particularly in scenarios with non-stationary noise and complex dependencies. The proposed method offers a flexible and effective framework for change-point detection, with potential applications in anomaly detection, predictive maintenance, and environmental monitoring.

Keywords: Change-point detection, Time series analysis, Density ratio estimation, Kernel methods, Non-stationary data, Anomaly detection.
