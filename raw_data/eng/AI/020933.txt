Data augmentation (DA) is a crucial technique for improving the performance of object detection models, especially when training data is limited. However, the selection of appropriate DA policies remains a challenge, often relying on heuristics or computationally expensive search methods. This paper introduces ADA, a novel game-theoretic framework for analyzing and optimizing DA strategies. We formulate the interaction between the object detector and DA policies as a non-cooperative game, where the detector aims to minimize its loss, and each DA policy aims to maximize the detector's loss by generating challenging augmented samples. We propose an efficient algorithm to find the Nash Equilibrium of this game, representing an optimal DA policy tailored to the specific detector and dataset. Experimental results on benchmark datasets such as COCO and Pascal VOC demonstrate that ADA significantly improves the detection accuracy compared to state-of-the-art DA methods. Furthermore, ADA offers insights into the robustness of object detectors and the effectiveness of different DA transformations. Our work provides a theoretical foundation for understanding DA and a practical approach for automatically discovering effective DA policies, enhancing the performance and generalization capabilities of object detection models. Keywords: Data Augmentation, Object Detection, Game Theory, Nash Equilibrium, Robustness.
