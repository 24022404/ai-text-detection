This paper addresses the challenge of ultra high-resolution image inpainting, a critical task in computer vision with applications in photo editing, medical imaging, and digital restoration. Traditional methods often struggle with maintaining fine-grained details and contextual coherence when reconstructing missing regions in high-resolution images. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel framework called Contextual Residual Aggregation (CRA), which integrates multi-scale feature extraction with adaptive residual learning. The CRA framework leverages hierarchical contextual information to ensure global consistency while preserving local details. Our approach employs a dual-path architecture, combining a coarse-to-fine inpainting strategy with a residual aggregation mechanism to refine the reconstructed regions iteratively. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that CRA outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both visual quality and quantitative metrics, achieving superior performance in handling complex textures and large missing regions. The proposed method also exhibits robust generalization across diverse image domains. This work contributes to advancing high-resolution image inpainting by introducing a scalable and efficient solution that bridges the gap between global context and local detail preservation. Potential applications include high-quality image restoration, real-time video editing, and medical image analysis.  

**Keywords**: Image inpainting, high-resolution reconstruction, contextual aggregation, residual learning, computer vision.
