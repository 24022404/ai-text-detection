This study aims to investigate the effectiveness of pre-trained convolutional neural network (CNN) features for facial expression recognition. Our approach utilizes transfer learning, where features learned from large-scale image classification tasks are fine-tuned for the specific task of recognizing facial expressions. We employ a range of pre-trained CNN models, including VGGFace and FaceNet, and evaluate their performance on benchmark facial expression recognition datasets. Our results show that pre-trained CNN features can achieve state-of-the-art performance, outperforming traditional hand-crafted feature extraction methods. The use of pre-trained features also reduces the need for large amounts of labeled training data, making the approach more efficient and scalable. Our findings highlight the potential of leveraging pre-trained CNN features for facial expression recognition, with implications for applications in human-computer interaction, affective computing, and social robotics. Key contributions of this work include the demonstration of the effectiveness of transfer learning for facial expression recognition and the identification of the most suitable pre-trained CNN models for this task. Relevant keywords: facial expression recognition, convolutional neural networks, transfer learning, deep learning, affective computing.
