Latent tree models are widely used for probabilistic reasoning and knowledge representation, but learning them efficiently and with theoretical guarantees remains a challenge, especially with large datasets. This paper introduces a novel algorithm for learning latent tree models that achieves guaranteed scalability in terms of both computational complexity and sample complexity. Our approach leverages a spectral method combined with a novel tree structure learning technique that avoids exhaustive search. We prove that our algorithm consistently learns the true tree structure under mild conditions, and we provide explicit bounds on the sample complexity required for accurate structure recovery. Empirical evaluations on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our algorithm significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and runtime, particularly for large-scale problems. Our contributions provide a practical and theoretically sound approach for learning latent tree models, enabling their effective application in domains such as Bayesian networks, hierarchical clustering, and information retrieval.

Keywords: Latent Tree Models, Structure Learning, Scalable Algorithms, Spectral Methods, Graphical Models, Bayesian Networks.
