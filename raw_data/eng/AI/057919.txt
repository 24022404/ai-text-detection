This paper addresses the challenge of semi-supervised transfer learning, where a model is adapted to a new task with limited labeled data. Our objective is to develop a novel regularization technique that enhances the consistency of the model's predictions across different domains. We propose Adaptive Consistency Regularization (ACR), a method that dynamically adjusts the regularization strength based on the model's uncertainty. ACR is applied to a transfer learning framework, where a pre-trained model is fine-tuned on the target task with a combination of labeled and unlabeled data. Our approach leverages the strengths of both consistency regularization and transfer learning to improve the model's performance on the target task. Experimental results demonstrate that ACR outperforms state-of-the-art semi-supervised transfer learning methods, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and robustness. The key contributions of this research include the development of a novel adaptive regularization technique, the application of consistency regularization to transfer learning, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in semi-supervised settings. Our work has important implications for real-world applications, such as domain adaptation, few-shot learning, and AI model deployment, and highlights the potential of adaptive regularization techniques in improving the performance and generalizability of deep learning models. Key keywords: semi-supervised learning, transfer learning, consistency regularization, adaptive regularization, domain adaptation, few-shot learning.
