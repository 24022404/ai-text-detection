This paper presents SliderGAN, a novel generative framework for synthesizing expressive face images by bridging 3D blendshape parameter manipulation with photorealistic image generation. Our objective is to enable intuitive and continuous control over facial expressions in generated images, addressing the limitations of existing methods that often lack fine-grained or semantically meaningful expression editing capabilities. SliderGAN employs a generative adversarial network (GAN) trained to map adjustable 3D blendshape parameters—commonly used in computer graphics to define facial movements—directly to high-quality 2D face images. By “sliding” these parameters, users can synthesize a wide spectrum of expressions with precise control over intensity and blend. We constructed a large-scale dataset of paired 3D blendshape coefficients and real images to supervise the learning process. Experimental results demonstrate that SliderGAN achieves state-of-the-art performance in both the realism of generated faces and the accuracy of rendered expressions, outperforming prior models in quantitative metrics and human perceptual studies. The proposed method enables interactive facial animation, visual effects production, and data augmentation for emotion recognition. Our approach highlights the benefits of combining 3D parametric modeling with deep generative techniques, offering a versatile tool for expressive face image synthesis. Key keywords: GAN, 3D blendshape, facial expression synthesis, controllable generation, image manipulation, deep learning.
