Membership Inference Attacks (MIAs) on deep learning models have demonstrated the vulnerability of these models to revealing whether a specific data point was used in their training. This paper introduces a novel Membership Model Inversion Attack (MMIA) that goes beyond simple membership detection, aiming to reconstruct representative samples of the training data for target classes. Our approach leverages adversarial techniques and generative models, guided by membership probabilities obtained from the target model, to iteratively refine and reconstruct data samples resembling the training set. We evaluate our MMIA against state-of-the-art defense mechanisms, demonstrating its effectiveness in inverting membership information even with robust defenses in place. Experimental results on multiple benchmark datasets show that the proposed attack can generate realistic and representative samples, raising serious concerns about data privacy. This work highlights the need for more sophisticated privacy-preserving techniques in deep learning. Keywords: Membership Inference Attack, Model Inversion Attack, Deep Learning, Privacy, Adversarial Attack, Data Reconstruction, Generative Models.
