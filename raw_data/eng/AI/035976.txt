This paper addresses the challenge of improving data efficiency in hierarchical reinforcement learning (HRL) frameworks, which are often hampered by high sample complexity and slow convergence rates. We propose a novel architecture that integrates efficient state abstraction and sub-policy reuse within a hierarchical structure, enabling more effective exploration and faster learning in complex environments. The proposed method employs a meta-controller to dynamically select from a library of learned sub-policies, each specialized for specific subtasks, and leverages transfer learning techniques to share knowledge across related tasks. Through extensive experiments on benchmark continuous control and navigation tasks, our approach demonstrates significant improvements in learning speed and performance compared to standard HRL and flat reinforcement learning baselines. Results indicate a substantial reduction in the number of interactions required with the environment to achieve optimal or near-optimal policy performance. The findings highlight the potential of modular abstractions and policy sharing to make hierarchical reinforcement learning more practical for real-world applications where data collection is costly or limited. Our contributions include a scalable framework for data-efficient HRL, comprehensive empirical validation, and insights into the trade-offs between hierarchy depth and learning efficiency. This work advances the state of the art in reinforcement learning by promoting data efficiency, scalability, and adaptability in complex sequential decision-making problems. Key keywords: hierarchical reinforcement learning, data efficiency, sub-policy reuse, state abstraction, transfer learning, reinforcement learning.
