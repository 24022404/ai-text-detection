This paper addresses the challenge of demultiplexing color image sensor measurements, which is crucial for accurate color image reconstruction. The objective is to develop a numerical method that can effectively separate the mixed color signals captured by image sensors. A non-linear random forest modeling approach is proposed, leveraging its ability to handle complex interactions between variables. The method is trained on a dataset of color image sensor measurements and learns to predict the individual color channels. The results show that the proposed approach outperforms traditional linear demultiplexing methods, achieving higher accuracy and better color fidelity. The key findings indicate that non-linear random forest modeling can effectively capture the non-linear relationships between color channels, leading to improved demultiplexing performance. This research contributes to the field of color image processing and has potential applications in digital photography, computer vision, and image sensing technologies. The novelty of this work lies in the application of non-linear random forest modeling to color image sensor demultiplexing, offering a new perspective on this challenging problem. Key keywords: color image sensor, demultiplexing, non-linear random forest modeling, image processing, computer vision.
