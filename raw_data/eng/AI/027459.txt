This research addresses the critical challenge of time series forecasting, focusing on improving prediction accuracy through a novel symbolic approach utilizing Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. The study explores the integration of symbolic representations within LSTM architectures to enhance their ability to model complex, temporal patterns in diverse datasets.

Methods/Approach: We developed a symbolic-based LSTM network that leverages symbolic computations alongside traditional numeric processing. The approach involves encoding time series data into symbolic sequences before training the LSTM model. This symbolic encoding aims to capture intrinsic patterns and relationships that may not be readily apparent through conventional numerical inputs. 

Results/Findings: Our experiments applied the symbolic LSTM model to various benchmark time series datasets, demonstrating superior predictive performance compared to standard LSTM networks. The results indicate that the symbolic approach effectively enhances the network's sensitivity to temporal dependencies and improves the robustness of forecasts under varied conditions. Performance metrics such as mean squared error (MSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) show significant improvements in forecast accuracy.

Conclusion/Implications: This study introduces a pioneering method for time series forecasting by incorporating symbolic processing within LSTM networks, offering a promising direction for future research. The findings suggest that symbolic approaches can serve as a complementary mechanism to numerical models, providing richer data representations and insights. Potential applications span financial forecasting, climate modeling, and any domain reliant on accurate temporal predictions. This work sets a foundation for further exploration of symbolic methods in machine learning, particularly in enhancing deep learning architectures for time-dependent data analysis.

Key Keywords: Time Series Forecasting, LSTM Networks, Symbolic Approach, Deep Learning, Temporal Patterns, Machine Learning Models, Predictive Performance.
