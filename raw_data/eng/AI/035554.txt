Domain generalization (DG) aims to equip machine learning models with the ability to perform robustly on unseen target domains, a critical requirement for ensuring real-world applicability. This paper introduces a novel framework, "Probably Approximately Correct Domain Generalization" (PACDG), that leverages the principles of the probably approximately correct (PAC) learning paradigm to anticipate and adapt to domain shifts not encountered during training. The proposed approach formulates domain generalization as a min-max optimization problem, where the model is encouraged to learn representations that minimize expected risk across a diverse set of source domains and, crucially, can be theoretically guaranteed to generalize to novel environments within certain probabilistic bounds.

Our methodology integrates robust feature alignment techniques and an uncertainty-aware regularization mechanism that explicitly models the space of possible unseen domains. Empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets in computer vision and natural language processing demonstrate that PACDG outperforms state-of-the-art domain generalization methods, achieving superior accuracy and resilience under significant domain shifts. Theoretical analysis confirms the framework's ability to provide performance guarantees in the PAC sense, ensuring reliability when encountering unexpected scenarios.

The contributions of this work advance the theoretical understanding and practical effectiveness of DG by providing a principled approach to expectation management in unpredictable real-world settings. Our findings suggest that embedding PAC principles into domain generalization frameworks is a promising direction for developing adaptable and trustworthy AI systems.

Keywords: domain generalization, probably approximately correct learning, domain shift, robustness, uncertainty modeling, representation learning, generalization guarantees
