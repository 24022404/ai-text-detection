Accurate prediction of cancer survival is critical for personalized treatment planning and improving patient outcomes. However, existing models often lack robust uncertainty estimation, which is essential for assessing the reliability of predictions in clinical decision-making. This paper addresses the challenge of quantifying uncertainty in cancer survival prediction by proposing a novel probabilistic deep learning framework. Our approach integrates survival analysis with Bayesian neural networks to model both the predicted survival probabilities and their associated uncertainties. The framework is evaluated on a large-scale dataset comprising diverse cancer types, patient demographics, and treatment histories. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms traditional survival prediction models in terms of both accuracy and uncertainty calibration. Key findings reveal that incorporating uncertainty estimation significantly enhances the interpretability and trustworthiness of predictions, particularly in high-risk scenarios. The implications of this research extend to clinical applications, where reliable uncertainty-aware predictions can guide more informed treatment decisions and improve patient care. Keywords: cancer survival prediction, uncertainty estimation, Bayesian neural networks, probabilistic modeling, clinical decision support.
