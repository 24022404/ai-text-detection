This paper addresses the challenge of regression tasks in high-dimensional, non-Euclidean spaces, specifically focusing on hyperbolic manifolds. Traditional regression methods often fail to capture the intrinsic geometric properties of data embedded in hyperbolic spaces, which are increasingly relevant in applications such as network analysis, natural language processing, and recommendation systems. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel hyperbolic manifold regression framework that leverages the unique properties of hyperbolic geometry, such as exponential growth and hierarchical structure. Our approach employs a combination of Riemannian optimization techniques and deep learning models to perform regression directly on hyperbolic manifolds, ensuring that the geometric constraints are preserved throughout the learning process. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing Euclidean and Riemannian regression techniques in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly for data with hierarchical or tree-like structures. The proposed framework not only advances the state-of-the-art in manifold regression but also opens new avenues for applying hyperbolic geometry to machine learning tasks. Key contributions include a scalable optimization algorithm, a theoretical analysis of convergence properties, and practical insights into the benefits of hyperbolic embeddings. This work has significant implications for domains where data naturally resides in hyperbolic spaces, offering a powerful tool for modeling complex relationships.  

**Keywords**: hyperbolic geometry, manifold regression, Riemannian optimization, deep learning, hierarchical data, non-Euclidean spaces.
