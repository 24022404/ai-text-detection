Optical flow estimation, a fundamental problem in computer vision, aims to determine the apparent motion of objects between consecutive frames. This paper introduces a novel approach, Motion Feature Recovery (MFR), to enhance the accuracy and robustness of optical flow estimation. MFR operates by first extracting salient motion features, which represent significant changes in pixel intensity and spatial gradients across frames. These features are then used to recover underlying motion patterns, formulating optical flow estimation as an optimization problem that minimizes the difference between predicted and observed motion features. A coarse-to-fine strategy is employed to progressively refine the estimated flow field, enhancing computational efficiency. Experimental results on standard benchmark datasets, including Middlebury and KITTI, demonstrate that MFR achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods particularly in regions with large displacements and occlusions. The proposed method exhibits improved robustness to noise and illumination variations. MFR offers a promising alternative for accurate and reliable optical flow estimation, benefiting applications such as video analysis, autonomous driving, and robotics. Keywords: Optical Flow, Motion Estimation, Feature Extraction, Computer Vision, Motion Recovery, Image Sequence.
