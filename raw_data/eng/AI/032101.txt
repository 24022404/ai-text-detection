Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) are powerful generative models, but their interpretability remains a challenge. This paper investigates instance-based interpretability methods for VAEs, aiming to understand which data instances most influence a VAE's latent representation and subsequent reconstructions. We propose a novel approach that leverages influence functions to quantify the impact of individual training samples on specific latent vectors and reconstructed outputs. This method identifies influential instances without requiring retraining or perturbation of the model. Our experiments on image and tabular datasets demonstrate that influential instances often correspond to boundary cases or outliers within the data distribution. Further analysis reveals a correlation between instance influence and reconstruction error, suggesting that focusing on highly influential instances can improve model robustness. This research provides insights into the behavior of VAEs and offers a practical tool for identifying critical training data, potentially leading to improved model generalization and trustworthiness. Keywords: Variational Autoencoder, Interpretability, Influence Functions, Instance-based Explanation, Generative Models, Latent Space.
