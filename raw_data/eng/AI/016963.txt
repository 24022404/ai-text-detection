This paper addresses the significant challenge of accurately monitoring and identifying human activities, as well as detecting falls, utilizing computational models. The research aims to enhance reliability and efficiency in environments such as healthcare by developing an innovative approach that leverages Fourier domain features.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel methodology that extracts and analyzes features in the Fourier domain for human activity recognition and fall detection. By transforming temporal signals into the frequency domain, the model captures essential characteristics that are otherwise less discernible in the time domain. This method emphasizes the use of signal processing techniques to improve classification accuracy. 

Results/Findings: Our approach was subjected to extensive testing using public datasets consisting of various human activities and fall scenarios. The model demonstrated superior performance, achieving higher accuracy rates compared to existing time-domain feature-based methods. Notably, the system was particularly effective in distinguishing complex activity patterns and detecting falls with minimal false positives.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings suggest that the Fourier domain feature approach significantly contributes to the field of human activity recognition and fall detection, offering a robust and efficient solution for real-time applications. The implications of this research extend to enhancing safety monitoring systems in elderly care facilities and enabling proactive intervention in critical scenarios. Key keywords associated with our research include human activity recognition, fall detection, Fourier domain features, signal processing, and classification accuracy.
