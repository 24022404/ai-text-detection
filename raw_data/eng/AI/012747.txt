Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have gained widespread adoption in various applications due to their powerful ability to process graph-structured data. However, their susceptibility to adversarial attacks poses significant security risks. This paper introduces TDGIA, a novel approach for crafting effective targeted injection attacks on GNNs. The primary objective is to investigate the vulnerabilities of GNNs by systematically developing an attack method that manipulates the graph structure to deceive the model's predictions. Our approach leverages a sophisticated injection technique that strategically alters a small subset of the graph's nodes and edges, optimizing the perturbations to minimize detection while maximizing attack impact.

In our method, we utilize state-of-the-art gradient-based optimization to fine-tune the adversarial modifications, ensuring both attack effectiveness and subtlety. We evaluate TDGIA across several benchmark datasets, demonstrating its ability to significantly degrade GNN performance with minimal modifications to the graph. Comparative analyses with existing attack strategies reveal that TDGIA achieves superior success rates in misleading the network while maintaining lower visibility of the introduced changes.

The findings highlight the urgent need for developing robust defenses to protect GNNs against such vulnerabilities. In conclusion, TDGIA not only contributes to the understanding of GNNs' weaknesses but also serves as a crucial tool for testing and enhancing the resilience of these models. Future work will focus on extending these techniques to various graph-based applications, ensuring the secure deployment of GNNs in real-world scenarios. Key keywords include Graph Neural Networks, adversarial attacks, injection attacks, network security, and model vulnerability.
