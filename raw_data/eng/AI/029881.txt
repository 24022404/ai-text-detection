Metric learning aims to learn a distance function that effectively captures semantic similarity between data points. However, learning such a function in a high-dimensional embedding space poses significant challenges. This paper introduces a novel "Divide and Conquer the Embedding Space" (DACES) approach to address this issue. Our method strategically partitions the embedding space into multiple subspaces, each dedicated to learning specific aspects of the similarity metric. We employ a multi-branch neural network architecture, where each branch operates on a different subspace and learns a specialized distance metric. The final distance is computed by aggregating the distances from all subspaces. We hypothesize that this division enables more efficient and effective learning by reducing the complexity of the overall optimization problem. Experimental results on benchmark datasets for image retrieval and face recognition demonstrate that DACES achieves significant improvements in accuracy and convergence speed compared to state-of-the-art metric learning algorithms. The proposed approach offers a promising direction for scaling metric learning to high-dimensional data and complex similarity relationships.
Keywords: Metric Learning, Embedding Space, Divide and Conquer, Multi-Branch Network, Similarity Learning.
