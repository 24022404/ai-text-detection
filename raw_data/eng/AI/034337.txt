Objective: The proliferation of automatic image colorization techniques poses new challenges for digital forensics, as distinguishing between naturally colored and artificially colorized images becomes increasingly difficult. This paper presents a novel convolutional neural network (CNN) framework designed to detect colorized images with both high accuracy and strong generalization across diverse colorization methods.  
Methods: We introduce a dual-branch CNN architecture that separately analyzes chrominance artifacts and spatial consistency cues. The chrominance branch focuses on statistical discrepancies in color distributions, while the spatial branch captures structural distortions introduced by colorization algorithms. A joint feature fusion module integrates these complementary representations. To enhance robustness, we employ extensive data augmentation and a domain-­adaptive training strategy that simulates unseen colorization styles.  
Results: Experiments on benchmark datasets—including images colorized by state-of-the-art deep learning and traditional methods—demonstrate detection accuracies exceeding 98% on in-domain tests and over 90% in cross-domain evaluations. Compared to existing forensic detectors, our approach yields a 5–8% absolute improvement in accuracy and a marked reduction in false positives. Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of the dual-branch design and domain-adaptive training in boosting generalization.  
Conclusion: The proposed CNN-based detector offers a reliable solution for identifying artificially colorized images, making it well suited for applications in journalism verification, legal evidence analysis, and social media content moderation. Its strong generalization capability ensures adaptability to future colorization techniques.  
Keywords: Convolutional neural network, colorization detection, image forensics, deep learning, generalization.
