This paper addresses the challenge of determining optimal architectures for deep neural networks by leveraging reinforcement learning techniques. We propose a novel method that integrates Q-learning, a model-free reinforcement learning algorithm, to automate the process of structure learning in deep neural networks.

Methods/Approach: The approach employs Q-learning to iteratively adjust and optimize the configurations of neural network architectures. The Q-learning algorithm assesses various design choices, such as layer types, numbers, and connections, through exploration and exploitation strategies to maximize network performance. A reward function, tailored to the task-specific performance of neural networks, guides the Q-learning process.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed Q-learning-based framework effectively discovers high-performing neural network architectures across multiple benchmark datasets. Our approach consistently outperforms traditional human-designed architectures and other automated architecture search methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The results validate the capacity of Q-learning to efficiently navigate the search space of network structures.

Conclusion/Implications: This research marks a significant advancement in the field of automated machine learning (AutoML) by introducing Q-learning to structure learning for deep neural networks. The study highlights the potential of reinforcement learning methodologies in enhancing model design processes. The findings suggest promising applications for real-world scenarios where designing optimal network architectures is crucial, paving the way for more intelligent and autonomous AI systems.

Keywords: deep neural networks, Q-learning, structure learning, reinforcement learning, automated architecture search, AutoML.
