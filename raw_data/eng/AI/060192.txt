Dynamic Filter Networks (DFNs) offer a promising approach to adaptive computation by generating filter parameters conditioned on the input. This paper introduces a novel DFN architecture designed to enhance both expressiveness and efficiency. Our method employs a lightweight conditioning network to predict filter weights in a spatially varying manner, enabling the network to selectively apply filters based on local image features. This approach contrasts with traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) that use fixed filters, offering increased flexibility.

The proposed architecture incorporates a multi-scale feature extraction module to capture contextual information at various resolutions, which informs the dynamic filter generation process. We evaluate our DFN on image classification and semantic segmentation tasks, demonstrating significant improvements in accuracy compared to static filter CNNs with similar computational costs. Results on benchmark datasets such as CIFAR-10 and Pascal VOC 2012 show that our DFN achieves competitive performance with fewer parameters and FLOPs. Furthermore, visualizations of the learned dynamic filters reveal that the network learns to adapt its filters to specific image regions, indicating an efficient utilization of computational resources. This work contributes a practical and effective dynamic filtering mechanism for CNNs, paving the way for more efficient and adaptable deep learning models.

**Keywords:** Dynamic Filters, Convolutional Neural Networks, Adaptive Computation, Image Classification, Semantic Segmentation, Deep Learning.
