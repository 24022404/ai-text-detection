This paper introduces MEx, a novel multi-modal dataset designed for human activity recognition. The objective is to provide a comprehensive platform for developing and evaluating machine learning models that can accurately identify various human activities from diverse sensor data. Our approach involves collecting and integrating data from multiple sources, including inertial measurement units, depth sensors, and RGB cameras, to create a rich and diverse dataset. The MEx dataset comprises over 10,000 exercises performed by 100 participants, covering a wide range of activities such as fitness exercises, daily living activities, and sports. Our results show that machine learning models trained on MEx achieve state-of-the-art performance in human activity recognition, outperforming existing datasets in terms of accuracy and robustness. The MEx dataset has significant implications for various applications, including fitness tracking, healthcare monitoring, and smart home automation. By providing a large-scale, multi-modal dataset, MEx contributes to the advancement of human activity recognition research, enabling the development of more accurate and reliable models. Key keywords: human activity recognition, multi-modal dataset, machine learning, sensor fusion, exercise recognition.
