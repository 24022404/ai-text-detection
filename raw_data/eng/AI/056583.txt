This study investigates the robustness and performance of keypoint detectors and binary descriptors under varying degrees of photometric and geometric transformations, a critical challenge in computer vision applications such as object recognition, image stitching, and 3D reconstruction. The objective is to evaluate and compare the effectiveness of state-of-the-art keypoint detection algorithms (e.g., SIFT, ORB, and AKAZE) and binary descriptors (e.g., BRIEF, FREAK, and ORB) in handling transformations such as scaling, rotation, illumination changes, and noise. A comprehensive experimental framework is designed, utilizing benchmark datasets with controlled transformations to systematically assess detection accuracy, descriptor matching performance, and computational efficiency. Results indicate that while certain detectors excel under geometric transformations, others demonstrate superior resilience to photometric variations. Binary descriptors, known for their efficiency, exhibit varying trade-offs between matching accuracy and computational speed. The findings provide valuable insights into selecting optimal combinations of detectors and descriptors for specific application scenarios. This research contributes to advancing the understanding of feature extraction techniques and offers practical guidelines for improving the robustness of vision-based systems under real-world conditions.  

**Keywords**: Keypoint detection, binary descriptors, photometric transformations, geometric transformations, computer vision, feature matching.
