The increasing reliance on machine learning (ML) models for arrhythmia classification using electrocardiogram (ECG) data has raised concerns about their vulnerability to adversarial attacks. This paper introduces **ECGadv**, a novel framework for generating adversarial ECG signals capable of misleading state-of-the-art arrhythmia classification systems. The primary objective is to investigate the robustness of ML-based ECG classifiers against adversarial perturbations and to propose a method for generating realistic yet malicious ECG signals.  

We employ a gradient-based adversarial attack strategy, leveraging the Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) and Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), to craft subtle perturbations that are imperceptible to human experts but significantly degrade classifier performance. The adversarial ECG signals are generated by optimizing perturbations that maximize classification errors while maintaining the physiological plausibility of the signals.  

Experimental results demonstrate that ECGadv successfully reduces the accuracy of arrhythmia classification models by up to 40%, highlighting the susceptibility of these systems to adversarial attacks. Furthermore, we evaluate the transferability of adversarial examples across different ML architectures, revealing that attacks on one model can often generalize to others.  

This study underscores the critical need for robust and secure ML models in healthcare applications. ECGadv not only exposes vulnerabilities in existing systems but also provides a foundation for developing defense mechanisms to enhance the reliability of ECG-based diagnostic tools. The implications of this research extend to improving the security of AI-driven medical technologies, ensuring patient safety, and fostering trust in automated healthcare systems.  

**Keywords**: adversarial attacks, ECG classification, arrhythmia detection, machine learning, healthcare security, gradient-based methods.
