**Abstract**

Accurate and timely recognition of pain in neonates is critical for effective clinical care, as infants lack the ability to communicate their discomfort verbally. This paper presents a novel approach for automated neonatal pain expression recognition leveraging transfer learning techniques. The proposed methodology utilizes a pre-trained convolutional neural network (CNN) model, fine-tuned on a curated dataset of neonatal facial images, to classify pain and no-pain states with high accuracy. Data augmentation and preprocessing steps were employed to enhance image quality and address dataset limitations. Experimental results demonstrate that the transfer learning model significantly outperforms traditional machine learning classifiers and achieves a notable increase in sensitivity and overall classification accuracy. These findings suggest the approach’s robustness in recognizing subtle facial cues specific to neonatal pain expressions, an area previously challenged by limited annotated data and high intra-class variability. The automated framework can support clinical decision-making, reduce subjectivity in pain assessment, and potentially facilitate continuous pain monitoring in neonatal intensive care units. Key contributions include the successful adaptation of deep learning models through transfer learning to a low-resource biomedical task and the provision of an efficient, scalable solution for neonatal pain assessment. Keywords: neonatal pain, facial expression recognition, transfer learning, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, clinical decision support.
