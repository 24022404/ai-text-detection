Accurate segmentation of volumetric medical images is a critical task in computer-aided diagnosis, yet it remains challenging due to the scarcity of annotated data. This paper introduces a novel semi-supervised framework, Momentum Contrastive Voxel-wise Representation Learning (MC-VRL), designed to enhance volumetric medical image segmentation. The proposed approach leverages momentum contrastive learning to exploit both labeled and unlabeled data, learning robust voxel-wise representations tailored for 3D medical images. Specifically, we integrate a momentum encoder into a contrastive learning pipeline, enabling the model to generate discriminative features while mitigating the need for extensive manual annotations. Experiments are conducted on benchmark volumetric datasets, including 3D MRI and CT scans, to assess segmentation performance. Results demonstrate that MC-VRL significantly outperforms conventional semi-supervised and fully supervised baselines, particularly in scenarios with limited annotated samples. The framework achieves improved segmentation accuracy and boundary delineation, as well as enhanced representation generalization across various medical imaging modalities. Our findings suggest that momentum-based contrastive representation learning offers a promising path toward more efficient, scalable, and high-quality volumetric medical image segmentation with minimal reliance on labeled data. Keywords: semi-supervised learning, contrastive learning, voxel-wise representation, volumetric segmentation, medical image analysis, momentum encoder, MRI, CT.
