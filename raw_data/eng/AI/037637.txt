This research addresses the challenge of general image recognition in scenarios where only limited labeled data is available for multiple categories, known as the few-shot learning problem. The study aims to enhance the performance of multi-label few-shot learning by incorporating domain knowledge into the learning process.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel knowledge-guided approach that integrates external semantic information with advanced machine learning techniques to improve few-shot learning capabilities. The method employs a combination of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for feature extraction and a multi-label classification framework. Additionally, the model leverages a knowledge graph to inject contextual information, thereby enriching the feature space and enabling more accurate predictions with fewer samples.

Results/Findings: The evaluation of our method demonstrates significant improvements over traditional few-shot learning models. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including complex multi-label image recognition tasks, show that our approach achieves higher accuracy and robustness, especially in scenarios with severely limited data. Comparisons indicate a noticeable enhancement in identifying and correctly labeling multiple categories within images.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed knowledge-guided multi-label few-shot learning approach presents a significant step forward in addressing the limitations of previous models by leveraging domain knowledge to enhance learning efficiency and effectiveness. This methodology not only contributes to the theoretical understanding of few-shot learning but also offers practical implications for applications in areas such as real-time image recognition and automated systems where annotated data is scarce. Our findings suggest potential extensions to other domains where integrating structured knowledge could similarly benefit learning algorithms.

Keywords: few-shot learning, multi-label classification, knowledge graph, convolutional neural networks, image recognition.
