This research addresses the limitations of farthest point sampling (FPS) in point-wise analysis, a critical process in 3D data processing and computer graphics. The study aims to develop a more efficient and effective sampling technique that enhances the accuracy of 3D model representations.

Methods/Approach: We proposed an innovative sampling method that leverages advanced geometric insights and computational efficiency to improve upon traditional FPS approaches. The new method incorporates both deterministic and probabilistic elements to optimize point distribution across diverse 3D surfaces, thereby reducing computational overhead and improving data quality.

Results/Findings: Our empirical evaluations demonstrate that the proposed method surpasses FPS in preserving geometric details and representing point clouds with greater fidelity. Benchmark tests on standard 3D datasets reveal significant improvements in surface reconstruction accuracy and processing speed. Comparative analysis indicates our approach achieves up to a 30% reduction in computation time while maintaining or enhancing data integrity compared to existing techniques.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings highlight the viability of this new sampling technique as a superior alternative to FPS for point-wise analysis in 3D data processing. This advancement not only contributes to the field by offering a novel approach for more accurate 3D reconstructions but also holds potential applications in various domains such as virtual reality, autonomous driving, and robotics, where efficient and precise 3D data representation is crucial.

Keywords: point-wise analysis, farthest point sampling, 3D data processing, geometric optimization, surface reconstruction, computational efficiency.
