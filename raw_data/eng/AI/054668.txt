Crowd counting in densely populated scenes poses significant challenges due to variations in crowd density, occlusions, and perspectives. This paper introduces HA-CCN, a novel Hierarchical Attention-based Crowd Counting Network designed to address these challenges effectively. The objective is to enhance the accuracy of crowd counting by exploiting hierarchical attention mechanisms that focus on varying levels of detail across complex scenes. The proposed model leverages an innovative architecture that combines both spatial and channel-wise attention mechanisms, facilitating the network to selectively concentrate on essential features pertinent to accurate count estimation. Comprehensive experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets to evaluate the performance of HA-CCN. The results demonstrate a marked improvement in counting accuracy compared to existing methods, achieving a substantial reduction in Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Mean Squared Error (MSE) across diverse scenes. The system's ability to adaptively adjust to different crowd densities and occlusions is a significant advancement, emphasizing its robustness and scalability. The implications of this research extend to real-world applications such as public safety monitoring, event management, and traffic analysis. The HA-CCN sets a new paradigm in crowd counting by integrating hierarchical attention at various levels, offering a significant contribution to the field and paving the way for future developments in intelligent crowd analysis systems. Keywords include crowd counting, hierarchical attention, MAE, MSE, and deep learning.
