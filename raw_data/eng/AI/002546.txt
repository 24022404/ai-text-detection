This paper addresses the challenge of achieving realistic and adaptive virtual character animation control through data-driven reinforcement learning (RL). The objective is to develop a robust framework that enables virtual characters to perform complex, lifelike movements in dynamic environments without extensive manual tuning. We propose a novel RL-based approach that leverages motion capture data to train a policy network, enabling the character to learn and generalize diverse motion patterns. The method integrates a hierarchical control architecture, combining high-level task planning with low-level motion synthesis, to ensure smooth and context-aware animations. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms traditional animation techniques in terms of adaptability, realism, and computational efficiency. The system achieves significant improvements in motion quality and responsiveness, particularly in scenarios requiring rapid transitions between actions. Furthermore, the framework is scalable and can be applied to various animation tasks, including gaming, virtual reality, and simulation. This research contributes to the advancement of data-driven animation control by introducing a flexible and efficient RL-based solution, paving the way for more immersive and interactive virtual environments.  

**Keywords**: reinforcement learning, virtual character animation, motion control, data-driven methods, hierarchical control, motion synthesis.
