The interpretability of machine learning models, particularly in the context of Variational Auto-Encoders (VAEs), remains a critical challenge in understanding their decision-making processes. This paper investigates instance-based interpretability of VAEs, focusing on how individual data points influence the model's latent space and reconstruction outputs. We propose a novel framework that leverages gradient-based attribution methods to analyze the contributions of input features to the latent representations and generated outputs. Our approach provides a granular understanding of how VAEs encode and decode data, enabling better insights into their internal mechanisms. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method effectively identifies key features driving the model's behavior, outperforming existing interpretability techniques in terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency. The findings highlight the potential of instance-based interpretability for improving the transparency and trustworthiness of VAEs in real-world applications, such as anomaly detection and data generation. This work contributes to the broader field of explainable AI by offering a practical and scalable solution for interpreting complex generative models.  

**Keywords**: Variational Auto-Encoders, interpretability, instance-based analysis, gradient-based attribution, explainable AI, latent space, generative models.
