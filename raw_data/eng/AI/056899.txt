This study aims to investigate the application of Graph Isomorphism Networks (GINs) in analyzing functional connectivity from resting-state functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (rs-fMRI) data. To address the complexity of brain network analysis, we employ a novel approach using GINs, which learn to identify and classify graph-structured data. Our method involves constructing brain networks from rs-fMRI data and applying GINs to analyze functional connectivity patterns. The results show that GINs can effectively capture subtle differences in brain network topology, outperforming traditional methods in identifying distinct functional connectivity patterns. Our findings have significant implications for the diagnosis and treatment of neurological disorders, highlighting the potential of GINs in rs-fMRI analysis. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of GINs to rs-fMRI analysis, demonstrating improved performance over existing methods, and providing new insights into the application of graph neural networks in neuroimaging. Relevant keywords: Graph Isomorphism Networks, rs-fMRI, functional connectivity, brain network analysis, graph neural networks, neuroimaging.
