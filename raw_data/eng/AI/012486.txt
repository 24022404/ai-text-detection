Unsupervised image-to-image translation aims to learn mappings between different image domains without paired examples. While existing methods have shown promise, they often struggle with complex scenarios involving many-to-many mappings. This paper introduces XGAN, a novel framework for unsupervised image-to-image translation designed to handle many-to-many relationships effectively. XGAN employs a shared latent space and multiple generators, each specialized in capturing a distinct mode of the target domain. A novel cross-reconstruction loss enforces consistency and prevents mode collapse. Furthermore, we introduce a diversity-promoting loss that encourages the generators to produce diverse outputs. Experimental results on several benchmark datasets demonstrate that XGAN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of visual quality, diversity, and fidelity. Specifically, XGAN exhibits superior performance in translating images across multiple styles or attributes. The proposed method offers a significant advancement in unsupervised image translation, enabling more flexible and realistic image manipulation. Keywords: Image-to-Image Translation, Unsupervised Learning, Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, Many-to-Many Mapping, Latent Space.
