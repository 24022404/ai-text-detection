This paper addresses the challenge of developing a generic and flexible representation of random variables to enhance the effectiveness and scalability of machine learning algorithms. Traditional approaches often rely on specific parametrizations or assumptions about variable distributions, which can limit adaptability across diverse learning tasks. To overcome these limitations, we propose a unified framework that abstracts random variables as modular data structures, capable of encoding a wide spectrum of probability distributions and transformations. Our method leverages probabilistic programming concepts and employs efficient sampling techniques to ensure compatibility with modern machine learning pipelines. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the proposed generic representation facilitates seamless integration with various models, such as neural networks and probabilistic graphical models, while maintaining computational efficiency and accuracy. Comparisons with standard parametrization methods reveal improved flexibility and reduced implementation overhead, particularly in scenarios involving heterogeneous or complex data. The results suggest that adopting a generic representation of random variables can significantly streamline the development of machine learning systems and accommodate future advances in probabilistic modeling. This work has broad implications for fields such as Bayesian inference, uncertainty quantification, and automated machine learning, serving as a foundation for more robust and reusable ML components.

Keywords: random variables, generic representation, machine learning, probabilistic modeling, Bayesian inference, uncertainty quantification, probabilistic programming.
