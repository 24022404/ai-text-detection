Neural network training is often characterized by nondeterministic behavior and instability, leading to variations in performance even with identical initial conditions and hyperparameters. This paper investigates the sources of this nondeterminism and instability in neural network optimization, focusing on the interplay between stochastic gradient descent (SGD), minibatch sampling, and inherent properties of the loss landscape. We employ a combination of empirical analysis and theoretical modeling to quantify the impact of these factors on training convergence and generalization. Our approach involves tracking the trajectories of multiple training runs, analyzing the variance in weight updates, and examining the sensitivity of the loss function to small perturbations. Results demonstrate that minibatch sampling significantly contributes to nondeterminism, especially in highly non-convex loss landscapes, while architectural choices also play a role in exacerbating instability. Furthermore, we propose a novel metric for characterizing the "flatness" of minima reached by different training runs and show that flatter minima tend to correlate with improved generalization and robustness. Our findings offer practical guidelines for mitigating nondeterminism and improving the stability of neural network training, ultimately leading to more reliable and reproducible results. Keywords: neural networks, optimization, nondeterminism, instability, stochastic gradient descent, generalization.
