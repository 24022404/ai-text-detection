This paper introduces Subject2Vec, a novel generative-discriminative approach that transforms a set of image patches into a compact, informative vector representation. The primary objective is to bridge the gap between fine-grained local image information and robust global feature encoding, facilitating efficient downstream analysis such as classification, retrieval, or anomaly detection. The proposed framework employs a dual-module architecture: a generative model learns the intrinsic structure within sets of image patches, while a discriminative network ensures that the vector embedding preserves class-relevant and subject-specific information. Extensive experiments are conducted on benchmark datasets to assess the effectiveness of Subject2Vec in capturing image semantics and providing superior performance compared to conventional aggregation and set-based learning methods. Results demonstrate significant improvements in both quantitative accuracy and computational efficiency, highlighting the model's ability to generalize across diverse image types and resolutions. The implications of this work extend to medical imaging, remote sensing, and any domain requiring interpretable and scalable image set processing. Keywords: image representation, generative models, discriminative learning, set-to-vector, deep learning, feature embedding, image patches, Subject2Vec.
