Human action recognition (HAR) using inertial sensors is challenged by the difficulty of extracting discriminative features robust to variations in execution speed and style. This paper introduces a novel approach to HAR by encoding inertial sensor data into image representations, leveraging the strengths of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for visual pattern recognition. Specifically, we transform tri-axial accelerometer and gyroscope data into a multi-channel image, where each channel corresponds to a specific sensor axis and temporal window. These images are then fed into a custom-designed CNN architecture optimized for HAR tasks. The proposed method is evaluated on benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to traditional feature engineering techniques and other deep learning approaches for inertial sensor-based HAR. Our results show that encoding inertial data as images effectively captures temporal dependencies and enhances recognition accuracy. The image encoding strategy facilitates the utilization of advanced CNN architectures, opening new avenues for robust and efficient HAR systems, applicable in areas like healthcare monitoring and activity tracking. Keywords: Human Action Recognition, Inertial Sensors, Image Encoding, Convolutional Neural Networks, Deep Learning, Activity Tracking.
