This paper presents an automatic face recognition system leveraging local Fourier-Bessel features to enhance identification accuracy and robustness. The primary objective is to address the challenges posed by variations in pose, illumination, and facial expression, which commonly affect traditional face recognition methods. Our approach involves extracting discriminative facial features by applying the Fourier-Bessel transform locally across predefined facial regions, capturing essential geometric and textural information. The extracted features are then concatenated to form a compact representation, which is fed into a supervised classification algorithm for recognition. Experimental evaluations conducted on standard face image datasets demonstrate that our system outperforms several existing methods in terms of recognition rate, particularly under challenging conditions of varying lighting, expressions, and partial occlusions. The results indicate that local Fourier-Bessel features provide superior robustness and discriminative power compared to conventional techniques that rely on global or purely spectral features. In conclusion, the proposed system offers a reliable and efficient solution for automatic face recognition, with significant potential for real-world applications such as security access, user authentication, and surveillance. Keywords: face recognition, Fourier-Bessel features, feature extraction, pattern recognition, image analysis, biometrics.
