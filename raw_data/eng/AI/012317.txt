This paper introduces X-volution, a novel unified framework that bridges convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and self-attention mechanisms, two of the most influential architectures in deep learning for computer vision and natural language processing. The objective is to address the limitations of each approach—CNNs’ localized feature extraction and self-attention’s computational overhead—by proposing a hybrid architecture that dynamically adapts to input characteristics. X-volution incorporates a mechanism that combines spatial locality inherent to convolution with global context modeling characteristic of self-attention, allowing for flexible and efficient feature learning. We detail the model design, including the adaptive fusion module, and provide comprehensive experiments on benchmark datasets for image classification and sequence modeling. Results demonstrate that X-volution consistently outperforms pure CNNs and transformer-based models in terms of accuracy and efficiency, with reduced computational requirements and improved scalability. The proposed approach exhibits strong generalization across diverse tasks and datasets, highlighting its potential as a unifying backbone for future deep learning models. This work paves the way for more efficient, scalable, and robust architectures applicable in fields such as computer vision, natural language processing, and multimodal learning.

Keywords: X-volution, convolutional neural networks, self-attention, hybrid architectures, deep learning, model unification, computer vision, sequence modeling.
