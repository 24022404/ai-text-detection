Product embedding techniques have emerged as fundamental components in modern e-commerce machine learning systems, enabling personalized recommendations, search relevance, and targeted advertising. This paper aims to provide a comprehensive theoretical analysis of product embeddings, examining how they capture and represent meaningful attributes and relationships among products. We begin by formalizing the problem of product representation and reviewing prevalent embedding approaches, such as collaborative filtering, content-based models, and deep learning-based encodings. Our methodological contribution lies in establishing a unified framework to assess the expressiveness, generalizability, and robustness of various embedding strategies within e-commerce applications. Through extensive theoretical analysis and empirical validation on benchmark datasets, we identify critical factors influencing embedding quality, including data sparsity, product heterogeneity, and feature interaction complexities. Our results demonstrate the circumstances under which certain embedding methods outperform others and highlight the trade-offs between interpretability and predictive accuracy. The insights from this study advance the understanding of embedding design and provide actionable guidelines for practitioners aiming to enhance recommendation, ranking, and product-match systems. These findings pave the way for developing more effective, scalable, and interpretable product embeddings in dynamic e-commerce environments. Key keywords include product embedding, e-commerce, machine learning, representation learning, recommendation systems, and deep learning.
