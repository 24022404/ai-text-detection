Person re-identification (Re-ID) is a critical task in computer vision, aiming to match individuals across non-overlapping camera views. Despite significant progress, challenges such as occlusions, pose variations, and lighting conditions persist, limiting the performance of existing methods. This paper introduces a novel loss function, termed *Support Neighbor Loss (SNL)*, designed to enhance feature discrimination and robustness in person Re-ID. SNL leverages the relationships between neighboring samples in the feature space, encouraging the model to focus on both intra-class compactness and inter-class separability. We evaluate the proposed approach on benchmark datasets, including Market-1501, DukeMTMC-reID, and MSMT17, demonstrating consistent improvements over state-of-the-art methods. Experimental results show that SNL achieves superior performance in terms of rank-1 accuracy and mean average precision (mAP), highlighting its effectiveness in handling complex real-world scenarios. The proposed loss function is model-agnostic, making it easily integrable into existing Re-ID frameworks. This work contributes to advancing person Re-ID by addressing key limitations in feature learning, with potential applications in surveillance, security, and human-computer interaction.  

**Keywords**: Person re-identification, loss function, feature learning, support neighbor loss, computer vision, surveillance.
