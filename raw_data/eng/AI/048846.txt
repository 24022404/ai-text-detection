This paper investigates the hidden technical debts that hinder the implementation of fair machine learning models in financial services. The objective is to identify and address the underlying issues that lead to biased outcomes in lending, credit scoring, and other financial applications. We employ a multi-faceted approach, combining data analysis, model auditing, and fairness metrics to detect and mitigate technical debts. Our results show that common practices, such as data preprocessing and feature engineering, can inadvertently introduce biases, and that ignoring these debts can lead to significant disparities in model performance across different demographic groups. We propose a framework for fair machine learning that incorporates debt-aware model development, regular auditing, and continuous monitoring. Our findings have important implications for the development of fair and transparent AI systems in financial services, highlighting the need for careful consideration of technical debts to ensure equitable outcomes. Key contributions include the identification of hidden technical debts, the development of a debt-aware framework, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in promoting fair machine learning practices. Relevant keywords: fair machine learning, technical debt, financial services, bias mitigation, AI fairness, model auditing.
