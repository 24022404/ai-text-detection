This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised ensemble classification, focusing on sequential and networked data. The objective is to develop a novel approach that can effectively integrate multiple classifiers to improve the accuracy and robustness of classification models. Our method employs a hybrid framework that combines the strengths of sequential and network-based techniques to learn complex patterns in data. The approach utilizes a graph-based representation to model relationships between data instances and a sequential clustering algorithm to identify dense regions in the data. Experimental results demonstrate that our ensemble classification method outperforms state-of-the-art techniques in terms of accuracy, F1-score, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve. The key findings highlight the importance of incorporating both sequential and networked information to capture nuanced relationships in the data. This research contributes to the development of more accurate and reliable unsupervised classification models, with potential applications in areas such as social network analysis, time-series forecasting, and anomaly detection. Key keywords: unsupervised ensemble classification, sequential data, networked data, graph-based representation, clustering algorithm.
