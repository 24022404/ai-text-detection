This paper addresses the challenge of language-guided navigation, where an agent must navigate a physical environment based on natural language instructions. The objective is to improve the agent's ability to ground linguistic commands into actionable spatial and visual cues, enabling more accurate and efficient navigation. To achieve this, we propose a novel framework that integrates cross-modal grounding with alternate adversarial learning. The cross-modal grounding module aligns linguistic inputs with visual and spatial representations, while the adversarial learning component enhances the robustness of the model by simulating challenging navigation scenarios. Our approach leverages a dual-network architecture, where one network generates navigation trajectories, and the other adversarially evaluates and refines these trajectories. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in navigation accuracy and generalization compared to existing methods. The results highlight the effectiveness of our framework in handling complex instructions and dynamic environments. This research contributes to advancing language-guided navigation systems, with potential applications in robotics, assistive technologies, and autonomous systems. Key innovations include the integration of adversarial learning for trajectory refinement and the development of a robust cross-modal grounding mechanism. Keywords: language-guided navigation, cross-modal grounding, adversarial learning, natural language processing, robotics, autonomous systems.
