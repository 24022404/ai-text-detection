Abstract:

Dense multi-view 3D reconstruction is a critical task in computer vision, enabling the creation of detailed three-dimensional models from multiple images. Traditional methods often rely on dense correspondences—precise pixel-to-pixel matches across views—which are computationally expensive and prone to errors in textureless or repetitive regions. This paper presents a novel framework for dense 3D reconstruction that eliminates the need for explicit dense correspondences. Our approach leverages geometric priors, advanced photometric consistency measures, and robust aggregation techniques to infer depth information directly from raw multi-view image data. We introduce an optimization pipeline that integrates sparse feature alignment with dense depth inference, thereby significantly reducing computational overhead and enhancing reconstruction accuracy in challenging scenarios. Experimental results on public benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of both reconstruction fidelity and processing efficiency, outperforming conventional correspondence-based techniques, especially in cases with poor texture or wide baselines. The proposed solution opens new possibilities for real-time 3D modeling in applications such as robotics, augmented reality, and cultural heritage preservation. Keywords: 3D reconstruction, multi-view, dense depth estimation, correspondence-free, computer vision, photometric consistency, geometric priors.
