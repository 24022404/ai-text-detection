This paper addresses the critical task of medical image segmentation and localization, aiming to enhance diagnostic accuracy and precision in clinical settings. The primary objective is to develop a robust framework that effectively identifies and delineates anatomical structures in medical images. Our approach leverages deformable templates, a sophisticated technique that adapts to the shape and size variations inherent in medical imaging data. We utilize a deformable model-based algorithm that iteratively refines the template to fit the target structures within medical images, ensuring accurate segmentation and localization. Evaluation of our methodology on various imaging datasets, including MRI and CT scans, demonstrates superior performance compared to traditional segmentation approaches. Notably, our results indicate improved boundary delineation and localization precision, with significant reductions in computational time. The research highlights the greater adaptability of deformable templates in handling complex anatomical variability, leading to substantial improvements in medical image analysis. Our findings underscore the potential applications of this technique in automated diagnostic tools, facilitating enhanced patient care through precise medical imaging technologies. Keywords: medical image segmentation, localization, deformable templates, anatomical structures, MRI, CT scans, automated diagnostics.
