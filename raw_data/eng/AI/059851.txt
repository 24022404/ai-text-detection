Self-supervised learning has shown remarkable success in learning representations from unlabeled data. However, these representations often inadvertently encode undesirable information, hindering performance in downstream tasks requiring invariance to such factors. This paper introduces Conditional Contrastive Learning (CCL), a novel framework designed to remove specific undesirable information from self-supervised representations. CCL leverages conditional information during contrastive learning to encourage the model to be invariant to the specified attributes. Specifically, we modify the contrastive loss function to condition on the undesirable information, allowing the model to learn representations that are robust to its variations. We evaluate CCL on several benchmark datasets and demonstrate that it effectively removes undesirable information while preserving task-relevant features. Results show significant improvements in downstream task performance compared to standard contrastive learning and other invariance learning techniques. Our findings highlight the potential of CCL for learning more robust and controllable self-supervised representations, with implications for fairness, privacy, and generalization. Keywords: self-supervised learning, contrastive learning, invariance, representation learning, conditional learning, debiasing.
