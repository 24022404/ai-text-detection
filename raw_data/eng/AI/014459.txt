Distributed deep learning has become essential for training large models on massive datasets. This paper investigates the critical parameters affecting the scalability and efficiency of distributed training, specifically focusing on large batch sizes and asynchronous stochastic gradient descent (SGD). We analyze the trade-offs between data parallelism, model convergence, and communication overhead when employing large mini-batches. Our approach involves a series of experiments on image classification tasks using ResNet architectures, varying batch sizes, learning rates, and staleness levels in asynchronous updates. The results demonstrate that careful tuning of the learning rate and gradient aggregation frequency is crucial for mitigating the negative impact of delayed gradients associated with asynchronous SGD and large batch sizes. We identify an optimal range of parameters that maintains convergence while maximizing throughput. Furthermore, we propose an adaptive learning rate adjustment strategy that dynamically compensates for gradient staleness, leading to improved convergence speed and overall system efficiency. These findings provide valuable insights for practitioners seeking to optimize distributed training pipelines for large-scale machine learning applications.
Keywords: distributed learning, asynchronous SGD, large batch training, deep learning, scalability, convergence.
