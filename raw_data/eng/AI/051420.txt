This paper investigates the training dynamics of deep neural networks (DNNs) under class-imbalanced conditions, revealing a novel phenomenon termed "minority collapse." We introduce the Layer-Peeled Model (LPM), a diagnostic tool that systematically removes layers from a trained DNN to assess the retained performance on both majority and minority classes. Through extensive experiments on diverse datasets, including image classification and natural language processing tasks, we observe that DNNs trained on imbalanced data often exhibit a disproportionate decline in minority class performance as layers are peeled away, indicating that crucial minority-specific information is concentrated in the deeper layers. This contrasts with majority class performance, which remains relatively robust to layer removal. The LPM analysis further suggests that minority collapse is exacerbated by increasing class imbalance ratios and network depth. Our findings provide valuable insights into the challenges of training DNNs with imbalanced data and highlight the need for targeted regularization or architectural modifications to mitigate minority collapse and improve overall model generalization. Keywords: deep neural networks, class imbalance, minority collapse, layer-peeled model, imbalanced learning, neural network training.
