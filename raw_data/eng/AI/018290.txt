**Abstract:**

This paper presents a novel visibility constrained generative model for depth-based 3D facial pose tracking, addressing key challenges in robust and accurate head pose estimation under partial occlusions. The primary objective is to enhance the reliability of 3D facial tracking systems by explicitly modeling visibility and occlusion within the generative framework, leveraging depth imaging data. Our approach integrates a visibility-aware generative model with a depth-based facial representation, enabling dynamic adaptation to varying degrees of facial occlusion and separation of visible from obscured regions in real time. To validate our method, we conducted comprehensive experiments using benchmark depth datasets with annotated facial poses under diverse occlusion scenarios. The proposed system outperformed state-of-the-art techniques in terms of pose estimation accuracy, occlusion robustness, and computational efficiency. Notably, our method demonstrated a significant reduction in tracking errors and maintained stable performance even when large portions of the face were occluded. These findings underscore the effectiveness of incorporating visibility constraints in generative modeling for depth-based facial analysis. The research offers valuable contributions to real-time 3D face tracking applications, such as human-computer interaction, virtual/augmented reality, and biometric authentication, where reliable performance under occlusion is essential. Keywords: 3D facial pose tracking, depth imaging, generative model, visibility constraints, occlusion handling, computer vision, real-time tracking.
