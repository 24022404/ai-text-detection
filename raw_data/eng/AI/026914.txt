This paper addresses the challenge of accurately estimating 3D human poses from video sequences, a critical task in applications such as motion analysis, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction. Traditional methods often struggle with occlusions, complex motions, and varying viewpoints, limiting their effectiveness. To overcome these limitations, we propose an enhanced approach leveraging an attention-based neural network integrated with dilated convolutions. The attention mechanism focuses on relevant spatial and temporal features, while dilated convolutions expand the receptive field without increasing computational complexity, enabling the model to capture long-range dependencies in the data. Our method is trained and evaluated on widely used benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to state-of-the-art techniques. Experimental results show significant improvements in accuracy, robustness, and generalization across diverse scenarios. The proposed framework not only achieves higher precision in 3D pose estimation but also reduces computational overhead, making it suitable for real-time applications. This research contributes to advancing the field of 3D human pose estimation by introducing a novel architecture that effectively combines attention mechanisms and dilated convolutions, offering a scalable and efficient solution for complex motion analysis tasks.  

**Keywords**: 3D human pose estimation, attention mechanism, dilated convolutions, neural networks, video analysis, motion capture.
