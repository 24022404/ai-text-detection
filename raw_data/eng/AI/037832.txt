This paper proposes a stochastic alternating minimizing method for sparse phase retrieval, addressing the challenging problem of recovering sparse signals from quadratic measurements with unknown phases. Our objective is to develop an efficient and robust algorithm that can handle large-scale datasets and provide accurate recovery of sparse signals. We employ a stochastic alternating minimization approach, combining techniques from sparse recovery and phase retrieval to minimize a non-convex loss function. The method iteratively updates the signal estimate and phase parameters, utilizing a stochastic gradient descent scheme to reduce computational complexity. Our results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in recovering sparse signals from noisy measurements, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of recovery accuracy and computational efficiency. The key contributions of this research include the development of a novel stochastic algorithm for sparse phase retrieval, which has important implications for various applications such as imaging, optics, and signal processing. The proposed method has the potential to be applied to a wide range of problems involving sparse signal recovery and phase retrieval, and our findings highlight the benefits of combining stochastic optimization techniques with sparse recovery methods. Key keywords: sparse phase retrieval, stochastic alternating minimization, sparse recovery, phase retrieval, stochastic gradient descent.
