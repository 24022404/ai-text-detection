The increasing reliance on algorithmic decision-making in critical domains necessitates robust mechanisms for ensuring accuracy, accountability, and auditability. This paper introduces *Dirichlet Uncertainty Wrappers* (DUWs), a novel framework designed to enhance the transparency and reliability of machine learning models by quantifying and managing predictive uncertainty. The proposed approach leverages the Dirichlet distribution to model uncertainty in classification tasks, enabling actionable insights into model confidence and potential errors. Unlike traditional methods, DUWs provide a probabilistic interpretation of predictions, facilitating real-time monitoring and post-hoc analysis. We evaluate the framework on benchmark datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving model interpretability and reducing misclassification risks. Results show that DUWs outperform existing uncertainty quantification techniques in terms of calibration and reliability, particularly in high-stakes applications such as healthcare and finance. The framework also supports auditability by generating traceable uncertainty metrics, enabling stakeholders to assess model performance and compliance with regulatory standards. By integrating uncertainty quantification with actionable accountability measures, this research contributes to the development of trustworthy and auditable AI systems, addressing a critical gap in the deployment of machine learning in sensitive domains.  

**Keywords**: uncertainty quantification, Dirichlet distribution, algorithmic accountability, auditability, machine learning, predictive confidence.
