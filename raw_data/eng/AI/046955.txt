Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown remarkable success in various domains, but its generalization capabilities often remain limited, especially in complex, real-world environments. This paper introduces a novel approach to enhance RL generalization by leveraging human instructions as supplementary guidance during training. The proposed method, termed *Human-Guided Reinforcement Learning (HGRL)*, integrates natural language instructions provided by humans into the RL framework to improve decision-making and adaptability in unseen scenarios. We employ a hybrid architecture that combines deep reinforcement learning with a language understanding module, enabling the agent to interpret and utilize human instructions effectively. Experiments conducted across diverse environments demonstrate that HGRL significantly outperforms traditional RL methods in terms of generalization, achieving higher success rates and faster convergence in unfamiliar settings. The results highlight the potential of incorporating human knowledge to bridge the gap between simulation and real-world applications. This work contributes to the growing field of human-in-the-loop RL, offering a scalable and practical solution for improving agent performance in dynamic and uncertain environments.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Human-in-the-Loop, Generalization, Natural Language Processing, Human Instructions, Deep Learning.
