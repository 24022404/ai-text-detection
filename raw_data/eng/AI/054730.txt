Human Activity Recognition (HAR) is crucial for applications in healthcare, fitness tracking, and assisted living. Existing HAR datasets often lack the diversity and complexity required to train robust, real-world models. This paper introduces MEx, a novel multi-modal exercise dataset designed to address these limitations. MEx comprises synchronized video, inertial measurement unit (IMU), and depth sensor data captured during the performance of a wide range of exercises, including both common and specialized movements. We detail the data acquisition process, annotation methodology, and dataset characteristics, highlighting its comprehensive coverage of diverse exercise types and variations in execution. We evaluate the dataset's utility by training and testing several state-of-the-art HAR models, demonstrating its potential for improving recognition accuracy and generalization. Experimental results show that models trained on MEx outperform those trained on existing datasets, particularly in recognizing complex activities. The MEx dataset facilitates the development of more accurate and reliable HAR systems, advancing the field of activity recognition.

**Keywords:** Human Activity Recognition, Multi-modal Dataset, Exercise Recognition, Deep Learning, IMU data, Video Data, Depth Sensing.
