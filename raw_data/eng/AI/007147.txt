Semi-supervised learning offers a promising avenue to leverage unlabeled data for medical image segmentation, addressing the challenge of scarce annotations. This paper introduces a transformation-consistent self-ensembling model designed to improve the accuracy and robustness of medical image segmentation in semi-supervised settings. Our approach leverages a teacher-student framework where the student model learns from the predictions of the teacher. We enforce consistency between the student's predictions on transformed and untransformed versions of the same input image. This transformation consistency regularization encourages the model to learn features invariant to common image variations, such as rotations and scaling. The proposed model incorporates a novel loss function that explicitly minimizes the discrepancy between the student's predictions under different transformations. We evaluated our method on two publicly available medical image segmentation datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in segmentation accuracy compared to state-of-the-art semi-supervised techniques. The results highlight the effectiveness of transformation consistency regularization in enhancing the performance of self-ensembling models for medical image segmentation, paving the way for more efficient use of unlabeled data in clinical applications. Keywords: Semi-supervised learning, medical image segmentation, self-ensembling, transformation consistency, deep learning.
