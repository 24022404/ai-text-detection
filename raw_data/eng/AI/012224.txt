This paper presents a novel method for Optical Braille Recognition (OBR) aimed at enhancing the accessibility of printed Braille materials for visually impaired individuals. The proposed approach specifically addresses the limitations of existing recognition technologies in terms of accuracy and processing speed.

Methods/Approach: We utilize the Circular Hough Transform, a technique commonly employed in detecting circular shapes in images, to accurately identify and interpret Braille dots on scanned documents. The process involves pre-processing the scanned images, applying the Circular Hough Transform to detect circles corresponding to Braille dots, and subsequently translating these configurations into readable text. This method is designed to be robust against variations in dot size and spacing, as well as changes in lighting and image quality.

Results/Findings: The proposed Optical Braille Recognition system demonstrates high recognition accuracy, outperforming traditional template-matching techniques. In our experiments, the system achieved a recognition accuracy of over 95% on a diverse dataset of Braille documents. Additionally, the processing time per page was significantly reduced compared to conventional methods, indicating enhanced efficiency.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of the Circular Hough Transform into Optical Braille Recognition enhances the accuracy and efficiency of converting Braille documents into digital text, thus improving accessibility for users. This advancement holds significant potential for facilitating the inclusion of visually impaired individuals in educational and professional environments by enabling faster and more reliable access to Braille materials. Future work will focus on further optimization and exploring real-time applications to extend the practical utility of this technology.

Keywords: Optical Braille Recognition, Circular Hough Transform, Braille Dots Detection, Accessibility, Image Processing, Assistive Technology.
