Multiphase flow phenomena are central to diverse fields such as oil and gas production, chemical engineering, and environmental science, yet their accurate prediction remains challenging due to complex interactions between phases and non-linear system dynamics. This paper presents a novel deep neural network (DNN) framework for predicting key parameters of multiphase flows, including phase distribution, velocity profiles, and pressure gradients. The proposed model is trained on a comprehensive dataset obtained from high-fidelity computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations, capturing a wide range of operating conditions and fluid properties. Advanced network architectures, such as convolutional and recurrent layers, are employed to effectively extract spatial and temporal features from the input data. Experimental results demonstrate that the DNN-based approach significantly outperforms traditional physics-based models and standard machine learning algorithms in terms of prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. The findings highlight the capability of deep learning methods to generalize across varying multiphase regimes, reducing the need for extensive empirical calibration. This research offers valuable insights for the real-time monitoring and control of industrial multiphase flow systems, paving the way for smarter process management and automation. Keywords: multiphase flow, deep neural networks, predictive modeling, computational fluid dynamics, machine learning, process automation.
