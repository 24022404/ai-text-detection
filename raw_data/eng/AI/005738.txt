Objective: This paper introduces PointContrast, an unsupervised pre-training framework designed to improve 3D point cloud understanding across tasks such as semantic segmentation, object classification, and detection. By leveraging large collections of unlabeled scans, PointContrast aims to reduce reliance on expensive manual annotation and to provide robust feature representations for downstream applications.

Methods: We adopt a self-supervised contrastive learning approach that enforces consistency between corresponding 3D points under different views and augmentations. Specifically, local regions are sampled from raw scans and processed through a shared backbone network to produce point-level embeddings. A contrastive loss encourages matching of true correspondences while repelling non-matching pairs. This architecture-agnostic scheme can be applied to existing point-based networks (e.g., PointNet++, DGCNN).

Results: When fine-tuned on standard benchmarks, PointContrast pre-training yields significant gains over models trained from scratch. On ScanNet semantic segmentation, it improves mIoU by up to 4.2%. On ModelNet40 object classification, it boosts accuracy by 2.5%. On KITTI 3D object detection, it raises average precision by 3.1%. Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of point-level contrast and varying augmentation strategies.

Conclusion: PointContrast establishes a new paradigm for self-supervised learning in 3D vision, demonstrating that contrastive pre-training can produce transferable, high-quality representations that accelerate convergence and enhance performance across diverse point cloud tasks.

Keywords: 3D point cloud, self-supervised learning, contrastive learning, pre-training, feature representation.
