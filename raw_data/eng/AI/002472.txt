This paper introduces a novel masked contrastive representation learning (MCRL) framework for reinforcement learning (RL). Addressing the challenge of learning effective state representations in high-dimensional environments, MCRL leverages masked autoencoding within a contrastive learning paradigm. Specifically, we randomly mask portions of the input state and train an encoder to reconstruct the masked regions while simultaneously maximizing the agreement between representations of different masked views of the same state. This encourages the learning of robust and informative representations that are invariant to irrelevant variations. We evaluate MCRL on a suite of challenging continuous control tasks, demonstrating significant improvements in sample efficiency and asymptotic performance compared to existing RL methods and contrastive learning baselines. Our results indicate that MCRL effectively captures essential state features, leading to more efficient exploration and policy optimization. The learned representations also show strong generalization capabilities. This work offers a promising approach to representation learning in RL, potentially accelerating the development of intelligent agents in complex environments.
Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Representation Learning, Contrastive Learning, Masked Autoencoding, Continuous Control.
