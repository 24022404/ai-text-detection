This paper investigates the impact of agent behavior—prosocial versus selfish—in automated contract negotiation scenarios using reinforcement learning (RL). The objective is to understand how different behavioral strategies influence negotiation outcomes, efficiency, and overall system performance in multi-agent environments. We design a negotiation framework where agents are modeled with either prosocial tendencies, prioritizing collective benefit, or selfish tendencies, maximizing individual gains. Deep reinforcement learning algorithms are employed to enable agents to adapt and refine their negotiation tactics over repeated interactions. We conduct extensive simulations comparing both types of agents across various negotiation settings and task complexities. Our results demonstrate that prosocial agents tend to achieve higher collective utility and faster agreement rates, while selfish agents secure better individual payoffs but at the cost of increased negotiation time and occasional impasses. The findings highlight the trade-offs inherent in agent design and the potential for prosocial strategies to enhance cooperation and system-level outcomes. This research contributes to the fields of automated negotiation, multi-agent systems, and reinforcement learning by providing insights into how agent behavioral models can shape negotiation dynamics and outcomes. Key keywords: contract negotiation, reinforcement learning, prosocial behavior, selfish behavior, multi-agent systems, automated agents.
