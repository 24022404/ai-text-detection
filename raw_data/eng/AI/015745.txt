Graph-structured data is fundamental in various domains, from social networks to molecular modeling. However, effectively encoding graph structures into transformer-based models remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces **GraphiT**, a novel framework designed to integrate graph structural information into transformers, enabling enhanced representation learning for graph-based tasks. The proposed approach leverages positional encoding and attention mechanisms tailored to capture both local and global graph properties, such as node connectivity and hierarchical relationships. GraphiT employs a multi-head attention mechanism augmented with graph-specific features, ensuring that the model retains structural context while benefiting from the scalability and parallelism of transformers.  

Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets for node classification, link prediction, and graph classification tasks demonstrate that GraphiT outperforms existing graph neural networks (GNNs) and transformer-based models. Specifically, GraphiT achieves state-of-the-art performance on molecular property prediction and social network analysis tasks, with improvements in accuracy and computational efficiency. The framework's ability to handle large-scale graphs while preserving structural integrity highlights its practical applicability.  

This research contributes a scalable and versatile solution for graph representation learning, bridging the gap between graph-based methods and transformer architectures. GraphiT's innovations open new avenues for applying transformers to graph-structured data, with potential applications in drug discovery, recommendation systems, and network analysis.  

**Keywords**: Graph neural networks, transformers, graph representation learning, attention mechanisms, positional encoding, node classification, link prediction.
