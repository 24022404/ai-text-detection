Land cover mapping is a critical task in environmental monitoring and urban planning, but it often faces challenges due to domain shifts between labeled and unlabeled datasets. This paper proposes a novel Bayesian-inspired, deep learning-based, semi-supervised domain adaptation technique to address these challenges. The objective is to improve the accuracy of land cover classification in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or originates from a different domain. Our approach integrates Bayesian principles to model uncertainty in predictions, enabling robust adaptation to target domains with limited labeled data. A deep neural network architecture is employed to extract domain-invariant features, while semi-supervised learning leverages both labeled and unlabeled data to enhance generalization. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing domain adaptation techniques, achieving significant improvements in classification accuracy and robustness to domain shifts. The findings highlight the potential of combining Bayesian uncertainty modeling with deep learning for effective semi-supervised domain adaptation. This research contributes to advancing land cover mapping by providing a scalable and accurate solution applicable to diverse geographical regions and environmental conditions.  

**Keywords**: domain adaptation, deep learning, Bayesian modeling, semi-supervised learning, land cover mapping, uncertainty estimation.
