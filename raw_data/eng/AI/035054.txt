This paper introduces DOTA, a large-scale dataset designed to facilitate object detection in aerial images. The objective is to address the challenges of detecting objects in overhead imagery, where traditional detection methods often fall short due to the unique characteristics of aerial scenes. To construct DOTA, a comprehensive approach was employed, involving the collection and annotation of a vast number of aerial images captured from various sources. The dataset comprises 2,806 images, annotated with 188,282 object instances representing 15 common object categories. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of DOTA in training and evaluating state-of-the-art object detection models, including those based on deep learning architectures. The introduction of DOTA contributes significantly to the field of aerial image analysis, enabling the development of more accurate and robust object detection systems. Key findings highlight the importance of large-scale, high-quality datasets in advancing object detection capabilities. The DOTA dataset is poised to become a benchmark for object detection in aerial images, with potential applications in fields such as urban planning, environmental monitoring, and disaster response. Relevant keywords include object detection, aerial images, large-scale dataset, deep learning, computer vision.
