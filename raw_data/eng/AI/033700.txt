Objective: Hyperspectral images contain rich spectral signatures but suffer from high dimensionality and limited labeled samples, which challenge downstream analysis. This paper introduces Joint and Progressive Subspace Analysis (JPSA) with Spatial-Spectral Manifold Alignment to address semi-supervised dimensionality reduction in hyperspectral imaging.  

Methods: JPSA constructs a progressive learning pipeline that jointly optimizes subspace projection and manifold alignment. First, a coarse spectral subspace is learned from both labeled and unlabeled pixels to capture global variance. Next, spatial continuity is enforced by aligning local manifolds in the reduced space, leveraging neighborhood graphs to preserve spatial structures. A semi-supervised loss function balances reconstruction fidelity, class separability, and manifold smoothness. Optimization proceeds iteratively, refining projections and alignment constraints until convergence.  

Results: Extensive experiments on three benchmark datasets (Indian Pines, Pavia University, Salinas) demonstrate that JPSA outperforms state-of-the-art techniques (e.g., PCA, LDA, Laplacian Eigenmaps) in classification accuracy, especially under scarce labeling (5–10% labeled data). JPSA achieves average improvements of 3–6% overall accuracy and exhibits greater robustness to noise and varying spatial contexts. Computational analysis shows that progressive optimization converges within a few iterations, making it practical for real-time preprocessing.  

Conclusion: By jointly integrating spectral subspace learning with spatial-spectral manifold alignment, JPSA provides an effective semi-supervised framework for hyperspectral dimensionality reduction. Its ability to harness unlabeled data and preserve intrinsic structures offers significant advantages for remote sensing, environmental monitoring, and precision agriculture.  

Keywords: Hyperspectral imaging, dimensionality reduction, subspace learning, manifold alignment, semi-supervised learning, spatial-spectral analysis.
