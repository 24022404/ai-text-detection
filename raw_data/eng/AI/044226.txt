Abstract:

The sim-to-real transfer challenge remains a significant bottleneck in deploying robot learning systems trained in simulation to real-world environments, especially when input data varies in length, such as sensor streams or temporal sequences. This paper addresses the problem of enabling robust robot policy transfer from simulation to reality by introducing a novel framework capable of processing variable length inputs. We propose a hybrid neural network architecture that leverages recurrent layers and attention mechanisms to dynamically adapt to differing input lengths without loss of temporal context or information. Extensive experiments are conducted on robotic manipulation and navigation tasks, where the input sequences from simulation and real-world sensors differ in both length and structure. Results demonstrate that our approach achieves superior generalization and transfer performance compared to fixed-length input baselines, exhibiting higher success rates and reduced adaptation time in real-world deployments. Our findings suggest that accommodating variable input lengths is critical for practical and resilient sim-to-real robot learning systems. The proposed method significantly advances the applicability of deep reinforcement learning, domain adaptation, and robotics in dynamic and unstructured environments.

Keywords: sim-to-real transfer, robot learning, variable length inputs, recurrent neural networks, attention mechanism, deep reinforcement learning, domain adaptation, robotic manipulation, sensor fusion.
