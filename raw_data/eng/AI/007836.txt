This paper investigates the phenomenon of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) developing blind spots, where certain regions of the input data are consistently misclassified. Our objective is to understand the causes of this issue and propose solutions to mitigate it. We employ a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical experiments to demonstrate that blind spots can arise from the interplay between convolutional layers, padding strategies, and training protocols. Our approach involves designing a series of controlled experiments to identify the key factors contributing to blind spot formation. The results show that CNNs can indeed develop significant blind spots, leading to substantial performance degradation. We propose a novel padding-aware training method that adaptively adjusts the padding strategy during training to minimize blind spot formation. Our findings have important implications for the development of reliable and robust CNN-based systems, particularly in applications where spatial awareness is critical, such as image segmentation, object detection, and autonomous driving. Key contributions include the identification of blind spots as a previously underappreciated issue in CNNs and the introduction of a practical solution to address this problem, with potential applications in computer vision, machine learning, and deep learning.
