The thesis is systematically structured to explore significant advancements in coding theory, with a particular emphasis on combinatorial objects and their practical implications. Chapter 2 provides an essential foundation by introducing coding theory alongside de Bruijn sequences, highlighting their wide-ranging applications and summarizing key related results from existing research. Building on these concepts, Chapter 3 presents the novel idea of a run length limited de Bruijn sequence, elaborating on its construction and visual representation through graphs. In Chapter 4, the thesis delves deeper into analyzing the properties of the longest run length limited de Bruijn sequence, investigating critical questions related to its achievable length, coding rate, and the distribution of its substrings. Throughout the work, there is a special focus on leveraging de Bruijn sequences within emerging fields such as synchronization processes in quantum communication, showcasing their potential for new and impactful applications. This comprehensive approach underlines both theoretical development and innovative practical uses in modern coding theory.
