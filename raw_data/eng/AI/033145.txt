This paper presents a novel framework for online initialization and extrinsic spatial-temporal calibration in monocular visual-inertial odometry (VIO) systems. Monocular VIO is widely adopted in robotics and augmented reality applications due to its low cost and flexibility. However, achieving robust and accurate state estimation is challenging because the initial alignment between the camera and inertial measurement unit (IMU), as well as the time synchronization, are often unknown or subject to drift. To address these challenges, we propose an online joint optimization method that estimates both the extrinsic spatial parameters (rotation and translation between camera and IMU) and the temporal offset during system initialization, and continuously refines them during operation. Our approach leverages feature-based visual tracking and tightly couples inertial measurements in a sliding window optimization framework, ensuring fast convergence and resilience to sensor noise. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing offline and static calibration techniques, especially in scenarios where initial calibration data is unavailable or conditions change dynamically. The framework enhances the accuracy and reliability of monocular VIO in real-world environments, reducing the need for manual intervention and pre-calibration. Keywords: visual-inertial odometry, monocular camera, online calibration, spatial-temporal alignment, sensor fusion, SLAM.
