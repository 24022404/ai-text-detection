Transfer learning, leveraging pre-trained models on large datasets like ImageNet, has become a cornerstone of modern computer vision. This work investigates the underlying properties of ImageNet that contribute to its effectiveness as a source dataset for transfer learning. We analyze the dataset's composition, diversity, and statistical characteristics, examining their influence on downstream task performance. Our approach involves training a series of models with varying architectures on modified versions of ImageNet, controlling for factors such as class balance, image resolution, and object complexity. We evaluate the transferability of these models across a diverse set of target datasets, measuring accuracy and fine-tuning efficiency. Findings indicate that both the breadth of object categories and the inter-class variance within ImageNet are crucial for achieving robust transfer learning performance. Specifically, a high degree of semantic diversity and a balanced representation of visual features are identified as key determinants. The study reveals that while high resolution contributes, its impact is less pronounced compared to diversity. These insights offer valuable guidance for curating more effective pre-training datasets and optimizing transfer learning strategies. Keywords: Transfer Learning, ImageNet, Computer Vision, Pre-training, Dataset Bias, Deep Learning.
