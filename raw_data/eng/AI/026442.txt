This paper explores the application of curriculum learning to enhance the performance of deep generative models, particularly in the context of clustering tasks. The objective is to address the challenges of training generative models, such as variational autoencoders (VAEs) and generative adversarial networks (GANs), which often struggle with convergence and quality of generated outputs when dealing with complex, high-dimensional data. We propose a novel curriculum learning framework that systematically introduces data samples to the model based on their clustering complexity, enabling the model to learn progressively from simpler to more challenging patterns. Our approach integrates clustering algorithms, such as k-means and spectral clustering, to guide the curriculum design. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method significantly improves model convergence, clustering accuracy, and the quality of generated samples compared to traditional training approaches. The findings highlight the potential of curriculum learning as a robust strategy for optimizing deep generative models, with implications for applications in unsupervised learning, data augmentation, and synthetic data generation. Keywords: curriculum learning, deep generative models, clustering, variational autoencoders, generative adversarial networks, unsupervised learning.
