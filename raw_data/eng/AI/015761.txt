Objective/Purpose: Bipartite matching is a fundamental problem in computer science with applications in resource allocation, recommendation systems, and task assignment. Traditional algorithms—while exact—lack adaptability to data-specific patterns and struggle in large-scale or noisy settings. This paper introduces Neural Bipartite Matching, an end-to-end learnable framework that integrates graph structure and assignment constraints into a unified model.

Methods/Approach: We propose a Differentiable Matching Network (DMN) that combines graph neural networks (GNNs) with an attention-based scoring module and a soft Hungarian layer. First, node embeddings are learned via message passing to capture local and global graph features. Second, an attention mechanism computes pairwise affinities between nodes on opposite partitions. Finally, a continuous relaxation of the Hungarian algorithm enforces matching constraints while allowing gradient flow. The model is trained using a loss that balances matching accuracy and assignment regularity.

Results/Findings: Experiments on synthetic benchmarks and real-world datasets—including job-market allocation and collaborative filtering tasks—show that Neural Bipartite Matching achieves up to 12% higher F1-score and reduces inference time by 30% compared to classical solvers and recent ML-based baselines. It generalizes well to unseen graph sizes and demonstrates robustness to input noise.

Conclusion/Implications: Our work provides the first scalable, data-driven solution for bipartite matching that adapts to problem-specific structures. The model’s efficiency and flexibility open new opportunities for real-time matching in dynamic environments.

Keywords: neural bipartite matching, graph neural network, differentiable Hungarian algorithm, assignment problem, optimal transport, deep learning.
