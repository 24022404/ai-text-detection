This paper addresses the active contours problem, a fundamental challenge in image processing and computer vision, by introducing a novel approach based on Eikonal equations. The objective is to develop more efficient and accurate methods for image segmentation and object detection. Our approach involves formulating the active contours problem as a minimal geodesic path problem, which can be solved using Eikonal equations. We propose a new algorithm that leverages the properties of Eikonal equations to compute minimal geodesic paths and active contours in a more efficient and robust manner. The results show that our method outperforms traditional active contours models in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The key findings include improved handling of complex image topologies and reduced sensitivity to initial conditions. Our research contributes to the development of more effective image segmentation and object detection techniques, with potential applications in medical imaging, autonomous vehicles, and surveillance systems. The proposed method combines concepts from geometry, partial differential equations, and computer vision, making it a significant advancement in the field of image processing. Key keywords: active contours, Eikonal equations, minimal geodesic paths, image segmentation, object detection, computer vision.
