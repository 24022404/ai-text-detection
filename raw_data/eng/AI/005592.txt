Auto-pruning, a crucial technique for reducing the computational cost and memory footprint of deep neural networks, faces significant challenges in convergence speed when integrated with reinforcement learning (RL). This paper introduces a novel approach, Historical Data-Enhanced Reinforcement Learning (HD-RL), to accelerate the convergence of RL-based auto-pruning. HD-RL leverages historical pruning decisions and their corresponding performance metrics to pre-train and guide the RL agent, effectively narrowing the search space and promoting more informed exploration. We propose a data replay mechanism that samples and re-evaluates past pruning configurations, creating a refined dataset for policy optimization. Empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, demonstrate that HD-RL significantly improves the convergence rate compared to traditional RL-based auto-pruning methods, achieving comparable or superior compression ratios with reduced training epochs. The findings suggest that incorporating historical data provides a valuable inductive bias, leading to more efficient and effective neural network pruning. Keywords: Auto-pruning, Reinforcement Learning, Neural Network Compression, Historical Data, Convergence, Deep Learning.
