Skeleton-based action recognition has gained significant attention due to its applications in human-computer interaction, surveillance, and healthcare. However, existing methods often struggle with noise in skeleton data, which degrades recognition accuracy. This paper introduces a novel Predictively Encoded Graph Convolutional Network (PEGCN) designed to enhance noise robustness in skeleton-based action recognition. The proposed approach integrates predictive encoding mechanisms with graph convolutional networks to model spatiotemporal dependencies in skeleton sequences effectively. By leveraging predictive encoding, PEGCN learns to anticipate and mitigate the impact of noise, improving the model's resilience to data imperfections. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including NTU RGB+D and Kinetics-Skeleton, demonstrate that PEGCN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in noisy environments, achieving superior recognition accuracy. The results highlight the model's ability to generalize across varying noise levels while maintaining computational efficiency. This research contributes a robust framework for action recognition in real-world scenarios, where noisy data is prevalent, and offers potential applications in robotics, video analysis, and human activity monitoring.  

**Keywords**: Skeleton-based action recognition, graph convolutional networks, predictive encoding, noise robustness, spatiotemporal modeling.
