Partial dependence plots (PDPs) are widely used tools for interpreting machine learning models by illustrating the marginal relationship between selected features and model predictions. However, the reliability of PDPs hinges on the integrity of the underlying training data. This paper investigates the vulnerability of PDPs to targeted data poisoning attacks, where an adversary injects carefully crafted data points to manipulate the resulting interpretations without significantly affecting overall model accuracy. We propose a novel poisoning framework that optimizes perturbations to training data, inducing arbitrary or misleading patterns in the generated PDPs. Through comprehensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets with popular machine learning models such as decision trees and gradient boosting machines, we demonstrate that it is possible to substantially distort PDP outputs with minimal modification to the training set. Our results highlight critical security and trustworthiness concerns for model interpretation tools dependent on data-driven insights. In conclusion, this work underscores the need for robust defenses against interpretation attacks and encourages the development of more resilient model explanation techniques. Key keywords: Partial Dependence, Data Poisoning, Model Interpretation, Machine Learning Security, Adversarial Attacks.
