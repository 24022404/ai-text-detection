Skeleton-based action recognition has gained significant attention due to its robustness in representing human motion. However, existing methods often struggle to capture complex spatiotemporal dependencies and higher-order interactions between skeletal joints. This paper proposes a novel approach to enhance skeleton-based action recognition by fusing higher-order features in Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). The proposed framework leverages advanced graph convolution techniques to model both local and global dependencies within skeletal data, enabling the extraction of richer hierarchical features. Specifically, we introduce a multi-scale feature fusion mechanism that integrates higher-order relationships, such as joint correlations and motion dynamics, into the GNN architecture. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including NTU RGB+D and Kinetics-Skeleton, demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art approaches in terms of accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the effectiveness of incorporating higher-order features for capturing intricate motion patterns. This work contributes to advancing skeleton-based action recognition by providing a scalable and efficient solution with potential applications in human-computer interaction, surveillance, and healthcare.  

**Keywords**: Graph Neural Networks, Skeleton-Based Action Recognition, Higher-Order Features, Spatiotemporal Modeling, Human Motion Analysis.
