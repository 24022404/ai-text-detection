This paper addresses the challenge of extreme relative pose estimation for RGB-D scans, a crucial task in 3D reconstruction and scene understanding. Our objective is to develop a robust method for estimating the relative pose between two RGB-D scans with large baselines and significant appearance changes. We propose a novel approach that leverages scene completion techniques to improve the accuracy and robustness of pose estimation. Our method uses a deep learning-based scene completion network to fill in missing regions and generate a complete 3D scene, which is then used to establish correspondences between the two scans. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and robustness, especially in cases with large baselines and significant occlusions. The key contributions of our research include a novel scene completion-based approach for extreme relative pose estimation and a comprehensive evaluation of the method on various RGB-D datasets. Our work has important implications for applications such as 3D reconstruction, robotic mapping, and augmented reality, where accurate and robust pose estimation is critical. Key keywords: RGB-D scans, relative pose estimation, scene completion, 3D reconstruction, deep learning.
