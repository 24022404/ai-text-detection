Pedestrian trajectory prediction is a critical challenge in autonomous systems, particularly for applications such as self-driving cars and robotics, where understanding human movement is essential for safe navigation. This paper addresses the problem by proposing a novel Context-Augmented Transformer Network (CATN) that integrates spatial, temporal, and contextual information to improve prediction accuracy. The proposed model leverages the self-attention mechanism of transformers to capture long-range dependencies in pedestrian trajectories while incorporating contextual features such as scene semantics and social interactions. A multi-modal fusion approach is employed to combine these features, enabling the model to adapt to dynamic environments. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including ETH and UCY, demonstrate that CATN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of prediction accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the model's ability to handle complex scenarios, such as crowded spaces and abrupt changes in pedestrian behavior. This research contributes to the field by introducing a scalable and efficient framework for trajectory prediction, with potential applications in autonomous navigation, crowd analysis, and urban planning.  

**Keywords**: pedestrian trajectory prediction, transformer networks, context-aware models, autonomous systems, multi-modal fusion, self-attention.
