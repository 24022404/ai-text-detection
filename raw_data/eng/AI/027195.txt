This paper introduces Probability Functional Descent (PFD), a novel framework that unifies Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Variational Inference (VI), and Reinforcement Learning (RL) under a single theoretical umbrella. The objective is to provide a comprehensive understanding of the underlying mechanisms that govern these disparate fields. By leveraging tools from probability theory and functional analysis, PFD reveals a common descent-based structure that underlies GANs, VI, and RL. The approach employs a probabilistic functional formulation, which enables the derivation of new algorithms and insights into the existing ones. Key findings demonstrate the efficacy of PFD in improving the stability and efficiency of GAN training, enhancing the accuracy of VI-based approximations, and facilitating the development of more robust RL policies. The conclusions highlight the potential of PFD to foster new research directions and applications at the intersection of machine learning, artificial intelligence, and probability theory, with implications for areas such as deep learning, unsupervised learning, and decision-making under uncertainty. Ultimately, PFD contributes to a deeper understanding of the fundamental principles governing these fields, paving the way for innovative solutions and advancements in AI research.
