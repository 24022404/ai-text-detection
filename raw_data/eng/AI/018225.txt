This paper addresses the challenge of synthetic-real domain shift in depth estimation tasks, where models trained on synthetic images often struggle to generalize to real-world scenarios. Our objective is to develop a novel approach, termed Domain Decluttering, which simplifies images to mitigate this shift and improve depth estimation accuracy. We propose a method that removes clutter and irrelevant objects from images, thereby reducing the domain gap between synthetic and real data. Our approach utilizes a combination of image segmentation and filtering techniques to identify and remove distracting elements, resulting in a simplified representation of the scene. Experimental results demonstrate that our Domain Decluttering method significantly improves the performance of depth estimation models, particularly in scenarios where synthetic data is used for training. The key findings of our research show that by simplifying images, we can reduce the synthetic-real domain shift and achieve state-of-the-art results in depth estimation tasks. Our contributions have important implications for applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and computer vision, where accurate depth estimation is crucial. Key keywords: Domain Decluttering, Depth Estimation, Synthetic-Real Domain Shift, Image Simplification, Computer Vision.
