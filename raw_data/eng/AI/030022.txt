This paper addresses the problem of discrete-time mean field control (MFC) in environments with dynamic states, a critical challenge in large-scale multi-agent systems. The objective is to develop a scalable framework for optimizing the collective behavior of agents interacting in a shared environment, where the environment itself evolves over time. We propose a novel approach that integrates mean field theory with environment state dynamics, enabling efficient modeling and control of large populations of agents. Our method leverages a discrete-time formulation to capture the interactions between agents and the environment, ensuring computational tractability while maintaining accuracy. Through extensive simulations, we demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in achieving optimal control policies, outperforming traditional methods in terms of scalability and convergence speed. The results highlight the framework's ability to handle complex, state-dependent environments, making it suitable for applications such as swarm robotics, traffic management, and distributed resource allocation. This work contributes to the field by introducing a unified MFC framework that accounts for environment dynamics, offering a significant advancement in the control of large-scale systems.  

**Keywords**: mean field control, discrete-time systems, multi-agent systems, environment dynamics, optimal control, scalability.
