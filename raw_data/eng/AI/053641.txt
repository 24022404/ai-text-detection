Low-light imaging poses significant challenges for computer vision systems and everyday photography, often resulting in noisy, low-contrast images that obscure critical details. This paper presents "Kindling the Darkness," a practical and efficient low-light image enhancement system designed to improve image quality while maintaining real-time performance. Our approach leverages a hybrid deep learning model that combines attention mechanisms with adaptive image processing techniques to selectively enhance brightness, suppress noise, and restore color fidelity. The system requires minimal computational resources and is suitable for deployment on consumer devices and embedded platforms. Experimental evaluations on multiple public low-light image datasets demonstrate substantial improvements in visual clarity, peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), and structural similarity index (SSIM) over existing state-of-the-art methods. User studies further confirm the system’s effectiveness in visual enhancement and usability. The proposed method offers a robust solution for applications in mobile photography, surveillance, and autonomous navigation under challenging lighting conditions. Key innovations include dynamic parameter adjustment based on scene characteristics and an end-to-end trainable framework. Our findings highlight the practical value of integrating advanced deep learning techniques into real-world low-light image enhancement systems. 

Keywords: low-light image enhancement, deep learning, attention mechanism, image restoration, real-time processing, image quality, computer vision.
