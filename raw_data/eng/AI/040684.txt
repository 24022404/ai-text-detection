**Abstract:**

Self-supervised learning has demonstrated remarkable progress in producing transferable representations for various downstream tasks; however, these representations often lack robust spatial awareness essential for tasks involving object localization. This paper introduces CAST (Context-Aware Supervised Training), a novel self-supervised learning framework designed to explicitly incorporate localization signals during representation learning. Our approach integrates a localization objective into the training process, encouraging the model to not only recognize semantic content but also infer spatial distributions and context within images. We employ a dual-branch architecture that jointly optimizes contrastive objectives and a weak localization task, enabling the learning of spatially rich features without the need for explicit bounding box annotations. Experimental results on benchmark image datasets demonstrate that CAST consistently improves model performance on localization and recognition-oriented tasks compared to conventional self-supervised methods. Notably, CAST enhances transferability to downstream tasks such as object detection and segmentation, underscoring the benefits of spatially-aware representations. Our findings suggest that learning to localize is a powerful tool for enriching self-supervised features, paving the way for more effective and generalizable visual models. 

**Keywords:** self-supervised learning, representation learning, localization, computer vision, image understanding, CAST, transfer learning
