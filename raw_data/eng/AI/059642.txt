This paper proposes a novel approach to sequential decision making by leveraging meta-learning to develop surrogate models. The objective is to improve the efficiency and accuracy of decision-making processes in complex, dynamic environments. Our method employs a meta-learning framework to train surrogate models that can adapt to new tasks and scenarios, reducing the need for extensive retraining. The approach utilizes a combination of model-agnostic meta-learning and neural networks to learn effective representations of the decision-making process. Results show that our meta-learning surrogate models outperform traditional methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly in situations with limited data and high uncertainty. The key findings of this research highlight the potential of meta-learning to enhance sequential decision making in a wide range of applications, including robotics, finance, and healthcare. By enabling faster and more accurate decision making, our approach has significant implications for fields that require real-time adaptation and optimization. Key keywords: meta-learning, surrogate models, sequential decision making, model-agnostic meta-learning, neural networks.
