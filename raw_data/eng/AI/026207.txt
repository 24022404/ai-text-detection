The semantic segmentation of urban scenes plays a crucial role in numerous applications such as autonomous driving and urban planning. This study addresses the challenges of domain adaptation in semantic segmentation, specifically targeting the discrepancies that occur when models trained on one domain are applied to another. 

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel curriculum domain adaptation strategy designed to progressively adapt the semantic segmentation models to a target urban domain. The approach leverages a curriculum learning framework that systematically introduces domain complexity, utilizing a sequential learning path from simple to complex tasks. This strategy employs a combination of synthetic data pre-training, intermediate domain adaptation stages, and final adaptation with real-world data to enhance segmentation accuracy.

Results/Findings: Experiments demonstrate that our curriculum domain adaptation approach significantly improves semantic segmentation performance compared to traditional domain adaptation techniques. Our method achieves superior segmentation accuracy, particularly in complex urban environments, outperforming baseline models and reducing domain gap effects. 

Conclusion/Implications: The findings highlight the potential of curriculum learning frameworks for domain adaptation tasks in semantic segmentation. The proposed approach not only enhances model robustness across varying urban scenes but also offers a scalable solution for real-time applications in autonomous systems and smart city development. The research introduces a new trajectory in adapting AI models to heterogeneous environments, with implications for future advancements in computer vision systems.

Key Keywords: semantic segmentation, domain adaptation, urban scenes, curriculum learning, computer vision, autonomous driving, AI models.
