Forecasting complex systems accurately remains a significant challenge, particularly when dealing with high-dimensional data. This paper introduces a novel approach to time series forecasting that leverages suboptimal embeddings to capture underlying dynamics in high-dimensional systems. We demonstrate that by strategically selecting embedding parameters, even those considered suboptimal under traditional reconstruction metrics, we can achieve superior forecasting performance. Our method uses a genetic algorithm to optimize embedding parameters based on forecasting error rather than reconstruction fidelity. The approach is tested on several benchmark datasets, including chaotic systems and real-world sensor data. Results show a significant improvement in forecast accuracy compared to methods relying on traditional embedding optimization techniques. Furthermore, the method demonstrates robustness to noise and reduced computational cost in some scenarios. These findings suggest that focusing on forecasting performance during embedding selection can unlock previously unexploited predictive power. This work contributes a practical and effective forecasting strategy applicable to a wide range of high-dimensional dynamical systems.
Keywords: Time series forecasting, embedding, dynamical systems, chaos, genetic algorithm, high-dimensional data.
