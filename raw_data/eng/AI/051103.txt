This paper introduces R4Dyn, a novel approach for self-supervised monocular depth estimation in dynamic scenes using radar data. Traditional monocular depth estimation methods often struggle in dynamic environments due to moving objects and occlusions, leading to inaccurate depth predictions. To address this challenge, R4Dyn leverages radar as an additional modality to provide robust depth cues, particularly in scenarios with dynamic elements. The proposed method integrates radar measurements with a self-supervised learning framework, enabling the model to learn depth estimation without requiring ground-truth depth labels. A key innovation is the use of radar to guide the network in distinguishing between static and dynamic regions, improving depth accuracy in complex scenes. Experimental results demonstrate that R4Dyn outperforms existing self-supervised monocular depth estimation methods, particularly in dynamic environments, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. The findings highlight the potential of radar as a complementary sensor for enhancing depth estimation in real-world applications, such as autonomous driving and robotics. This work contributes to advancing self-supervised learning techniques and opens new avenues for integrating multi-modal sensing in dynamic scene understanding.  

**Keywords**: Radar, monocular depth estimation, self-supervised learning, dynamic scenes, autonomous systems, multi-modal sensing.
