This paper addresses the challenge of continual learning in variational autoencoders, where the goal is to learn a sequence of tasks without forgetting previous knowledge. Our objective is to improve the performance and understanding of variational continual learning methods, which have shown promise in mitigating catastrophic forgetting. We propose a novel approach that combines a modified variational inference objective with a regularization technique to balance the trade-off between retaining previous knowledge and adapting to new tasks. Our method utilizes a hierarchical Bayesian framework to model the distribution of tasks and a replay-based strategy to selectively retain important information from previous tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing variational continual learning methods on several benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in terms of accuracy and forgetting rate. Our findings provide new insights into the importance of balancing retention and adaptation in continual learning, and our approach has potential applications in real-world scenarios where data is streaming or tasks are constantly evolving. Key contributions include a novel variational inference objective, a hierarchical Bayesian framework for task modeling, and a replay-based strategy for selective retention, with relevant keywords including variational autoencoders, continual learning, catastrophic forgetting, and hierarchical Bayesian models.
