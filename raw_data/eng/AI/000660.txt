Few-shot segmentation of volumetric images presents a significant challenge in medical imaging and computer vision, particularly due to limited annotated data and the complexity of 3D structures. This paper introduces a novel approach leveraging the 'Squeeze & Excite' (SE) mechanism to enhance feature representation and improve segmentation accuracy in few-shot learning scenarios. The proposed method integrates SE blocks into a 3D convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, enabling adaptive channel-wise feature recalibration to emphasize informative spatial regions within volumetric data. By focusing on critical features, the model achieves robust performance even with minimal training samples. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the SE-guided framework outperforms existing few-shot segmentation methods, achieving higher Dice scores and improved generalization across diverse imaging modalities. The results highlight the effectiveness of SE mechanisms in addressing the challenges of volumetric image segmentation with limited data. This work contributes to advancing few-shot learning in medical imaging, offering a scalable solution for applications such as tumor detection, organ segmentation, and other 3D image analysis tasks.  

**Keywords**: Few-shot learning, volumetric image segmentation, Squeeze & Excite, 3D CNN, medical imaging, adaptive feature recalibration.
