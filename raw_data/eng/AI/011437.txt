This paper explores the underlying relationship between self-attention mechanisms and convolutional layers, two foundational components in modern deep learning architectures for tasks such as natural language processing and computer vision. The primary objective is to analyze and compare the representational capabilities, computational characteristics, and operational principles of self-attention and convolutional networks. We first present a theoretical framework that formally connects the operations of self-attention modules to convolutional filters, highlighting the conditions under which self-attention can mimic or generalize traditional convolutional behavior. Through systematic experiments on benchmark datasets, we evaluate model performance, parameter efficiency, and generalization ability across various tasks. Our findings reveal that, while convolutional layers excel at capturing local patterns with fewer computations, self-attention provides greater flexibility in modeling long-range dependencies and adaptive receptive fields. Furthermore, we demonstrate how integrating these mechanisms can yield synergistic benefits, leading to improved accuracy and efficiency. The insights presented in this study offer a unified view of self-attention and convolution, informing the design of next-generation neural network architectures. Keywords: self-attention, convolutional layers, deep learning, neural networks, representation learning, long-range dependencies, model efficiency.
