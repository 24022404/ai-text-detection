Objective: Deep learning on 3D point clouds has advanced shape understanding, but irregular sampling and lack of connectivity information limit network generalization. This paper introduces GAMesh, a Guided and Augmented Meshing framework that enriches point-based representations with mesh-derived structure during training.  
Methods: GAMesh constructs lightweight meshes over raw point clouds using a guided triangulation algorithm that preserves local geometry. These meshes are then used to generate augmentations—edge-based perturbations, face-aware sampling, and synthetic connectivity cues—that complement standard point‐wise transformations. The augmented data are fed into existing deep point networks without architectural changes.  
Results: Extensive experiments on ModelNet40, ShapeNetPart, and S3DIS demonstrate that integrating GAMesh boosts classification accuracy by up to 2.8% and segmentation mean IoU by up to 3.5% over strong baselines such as PointNet++ and DGCNN. Ablation studies confirm the individual contributions of guided triangulation, mesh‐based sampling, and edge perturbations. Further analysis shows improved robustness against sparse sampling and noise.  
Conclusion: By injecting mesh-structured information into point‐based pipelines, GAMesh enhances feature learning and generalization without increasing inference cost. This approach opens new pathways for combining mesh and point modalities in 3D perception tasks, with potential applications in robotics, augmented reality, and digital content creation.  
Keywords: guided meshing, mesh augmentation, point cloud networks, 3D deep learning, triangulation, point cloud processing.
