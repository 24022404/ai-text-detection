The expanding use of machine learning for arrhythmia classification presents significant opportunities and challenges, particularly in relation to system security. This paper introduces ECGadv, a novel system designed to generate adversarial electrocardiogram (ECG) signals aimed at misguiding arrhythmia classification systems, highlighting potential vulnerabilities in these intelligent healthcare tools.

Methods/Approach: ECGadv employs advanced adversarial attack techniques to synthesize ECG signal perturbations that remain undetectable to human inspection yet cause erroneous classifications in state-of-the-art arrhythmia detection frameworks. Our approach integrates a tailored adversarial training mechanism to iteratively refine these deceptive signals. The method was rigorously tested against multiple machine learning models commonly employed in ECG analysis, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks.

Results/Findings: Experiments demonstrate the efficacy of ECGadv in significantly reducing the accuracy of prominent arrhythmia classification systems by over __% while maintaining imperceptible changes to the original signals. Comparative analysis with existing adversarial strategies confirmed ECGadv's superior ability to evade detection and disrupt model performance, evidencing a critical demand for enhanced security measures.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings elucidate pivotal security risks in current ECG-based arrhythmia classification models, underscoring the need for improved defense mechanisms against adversarial interference. ECGadv illustrates an essential step in understanding and fostering resilience among healthcare AI systems. Future applications of this research can bolster the reliability of machine learning frameworks in medical diagnostics, ultimately contributing to the safer implementation of AI in healthcare.

Keywords: adversarial attacks, electrocardiogram, ECGadv, arrhythmia classification, machine learning security, CNN, LSTM.
