This paper presents a novel approach to unsupervised ensemble classification specifically designed for sequential and networked data. Traditional ensemble methods typically assume independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) samples, which limits their effectiveness in real-world applications where data often exhibit sequential dependencies or complex network structures. To address this challenge, we introduce an adaptive ensemble framework that leverages both sequential information and network relations among data points, without requiring labeled data. Our method integrates multiple base classifiers through unsupervised model aggregation, while capturing temporal and structural dependencies via graph-based and temporal modeling techniques. Experimental evaluation on benchmark datasets demonstrates that our approach significantly improves classification accuracy compared to conventional unsupervised ensembles, especially in scenarios involving dynamic data streams and interconnected entities. The proposed framework also exhibits strong stability and scalability across varying data sizes and network topologies. These findings underscore the importance of considering sequential and networked dependencies in unsupervised ensemble learning, paving the way for more robust and context-aware classification in areas such as social network analysis, biological sequence interpretation, and sensor data processing. Keywords: unsupervised learning, ensemble classification, sequential data, networked data, graph-based models, temporal dependencies.
