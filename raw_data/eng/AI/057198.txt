This paper addresses the challenge of obtaining calibrated probabilities from boosting algorithms, which are widely used in machine learning for improving model accuracy. While boosting effectively combines multiple weak classifiers to form a strong classifier, it often produces biased probability estimates that can affect decision-making processes in various applications.

Methods: We propose a novel approach to integrate calibration techniques with the boosting framework. Our method involves adapting common calibration algorithms, such as Platt scaling and isotonic regression, specifically to work in conjunction with boosting models. By implementing these techniques in an iterative manner, we aim to adjust the probability outputs to ensure they reflect true probabilities more accurately.

Results: Through extensive experimentation on benchmark datasets, our calibrated boosting approach demonstrates superior probability estimates compared to traditional boosting methods. The results show significant improvements in Brier scores, logarithmic loss, and discrimination ability. Furthermore, the calibrated probabilities enhance performance in tasks requiring reliable confidence estimates, such as classification under uncertainty and risk-sensitive decision making.

Conclusion: Our research contributes a novel methodology for deriving calibrated probabilities from boosting algorithms, providing a better alignment between predicted and observed probabilities. This advancement has significant implications for applications that depend on accurate probability assessments, such as medical diagnosis, financial forecasting, and autonomous systems. By enabling more reliable decision-making frameworks, our approach opens avenues for robust AI and machine learning applications.

Keywords: calibrated probabilities, boosting algorithms, machine learning, Platt scaling, isotonic regression, probability calibration, confidence estimates.
