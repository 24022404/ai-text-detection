Graph representation learning has become a cornerstone for analyzing relational data, with kernel methods traditionally playing a significant role. However, the scalability and adaptability of these methods in modern graph-based applications remain limited. This paper rethinks the application of kernel methods for node representation learning on graphs, proposing a novel framework that integrates kernel-based techniques with contemporary graph neural network (GNN) architectures. Our approach leverages the strengths of kernel methods in capturing structural patterns while addressing their computational inefficiencies through a hybrid model. We introduce a scalable kernel approximation method that reduces the complexity of traditional kernel computations, enabling efficient learning on large-scale graphs. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our framework achieves competitive performance in node classification and link prediction tasks, outperforming traditional kernel methods and rivaling state-of-the-art GNNs. The proposed method also exhibits robustness to sparse and noisy graph structures, making it suitable for real-world applications. This work contributes to the growing body of research on graph representation learning by bridging the gap between classical kernel methods and modern deep learning techniques, offering a scalable and effective solution for node representation learning.  

**Keywords**: graph representation learning, kernel methods, graph neural networks, node classification, link prediction, scalable learning.
