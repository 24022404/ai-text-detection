This paper investigates the robustness of ensemble models, specifically focusing on \(\ell p\)-norm robustness in decision stumps and trees, which are foundational building blocks for complex machine learning models. The study aims to address and enhance the understanding of how these small and computationally efficient models can be fortified against adversarial attacks.

Methods/Approach: We developed a comprehensive framework that evaluates the \(\ell p\)-norm robustness of ensemble stumps and trees by examining their structural properties and decision-making processes. The approach includes constructing theoretical bounds and simulations to assess performance under various attack scenarios. Additionally, we propose a novel technique for increasing robustness through adaptive boosting strategies, which selectively strengthens weak points in the models.

Results/Findings: Our findings demonstrate that ensemble stumps and trees, while typically susceptible to adversarial perturbations, can be significantly enhanced in terms of robustness using our proposed methods. The theoretical analysis is supported by experiments illustrating that our approach yields a notable improvement in robustness compared to standard ensemble methods. These improvements are quantified across different norm metrics, showcasing the effectiveness of the adaptive boosting technique.

Conclusion/Implications: The study provides new insights into the defensive capabilities of simple ensemble models like stumps and trees, highlighting their potential in robust machine learning applications. The advancements proposed have implications for developing more secure and resilient AI systems, particularly in environments where computational resources are limited, and robustness is critical. This work sets the foundation for further exploration into robust ensemble learning, aligning with the growing need for dependable AI solutions.

Keywords: \(\ell p\)-norm robustness, ensemble stumps, trees, adversarial attacks, machine learning, adaptive boosting, computational efficiency, theoretical bounds.
