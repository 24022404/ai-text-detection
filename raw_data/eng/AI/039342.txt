Causal discovery aims to infer causal relationships from observational data, often struggling with limited data and identifiability issues. This paper introduces a novel collaborative causal discovery framework that leverages atomic interventions across multiple related datasets to enhance causal structure learning. Our approach combines observational and interventional data by formulating a unified optimization problem that encourages consistency in causal relationships across different environments. We propose an efficient algorithm that alternates between learning causal graphs and identifying intervention targets, incorporating constraints derived from atomic interventions to refine the search space. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our method significantly improves the accuracy and robustness of causal structure learning compared to existing methods, especially in scenarios with limited data or complex causal dependencies. The proposed framework offers a practical solution for discovering causal relationships in complex systems by effectively integrating information from multiple interventional studies.
Keywords: Causal Discovery, Interventions, Causal Inference, Structure Learning, Collaboration.
