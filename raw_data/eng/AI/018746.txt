This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing geodesic active contours, a technique widely employed in image segmentation, by introducing a novel characteristic function-based algorithm. The goal is to improve the accuracy and computational efficiency of contour detection in images.

Methods/Approach: We propose an innovative algorithm that integrates a characteristic function-based approach with traditional geodesic active contours. The algorithm leverages characteristic functions to dynamically adapt contour evolution, thereby improving segmentation precision. This method utilizes curvature-based optimization and incorporates an energy minimization framework, allowing contours to effectively lock onto boundaries in complex images.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the proposed algorithm consistently outperforms existing methods in terms of segmentation accuracy and computational speed. Comparisons with state-of-the-art techniques reveal significant improvements in delineating object boundaries, particularly in images with challenging topology or weak edges. Quantitative assessments show a reduction in iteration time and enhanced robustness against noise.

Conclusion/Implications: The characteristic function-based algorithm presents a substantial advancement in the field of image analysis, offering increased precision and efficiency for geodesic active contour models. Its application extends to numerous domains including medical imaging, object tracking, and pattern recognition, providing robust solutions for complex segmentation tasks. The contributions of this research pave the way for further exploration and enhancement in computer vision applications.

Keywords: geodesic active contours, image segmentation, characteristic function, computational efficiency, noise robustness, curvature-based optimization, energy minimization.
