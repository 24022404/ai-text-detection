Ranking structured objects, such as graphs, is a fundamental challenge in various domains, including social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics. Traditional ranking methods often struggle to capture the complex relationships and dependencies inherent in graph-structured data. This paper introduces a novel approach leveraging Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to rank structured objects effectively. The proposed method employs a GNN-based architecture to learn node and graph-level representations, enabling the model to capture both local and global structural information. We evaluate the approach on multiple benchmark datasets, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art ranking algorithms. Experimental results demonstrate that the GNN-based model consistently outperforms existing methods in terms of ranking accuracy and scalability. The findings highlight the potential of GNNs in addressing ranking challenges for structured data, offering significant improvements in applications such as personalized recommendations and network analysis. This work contributes to advancing the understanding of graph-based ranking and provides a robust framework for future research in this domain.  

**Keywords**: Graph Neural Networks, structured data, ranking algorithms, graph representation learning, recommendation systems.
