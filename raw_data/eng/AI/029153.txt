This survey investigates the challenges and opportunities presented by semi-supervised learning (SSL) in the context of delayed partially labelled data streams. These streams are characterized by instances arriving sequentially, with labels that are both delayed and incomplete, posing significant obstacles for traditional SSL algorithms. We comprehensively review existing SSL techniques, categorizing them based on their adaptability to data streams, handling of delayed labels, and strategies for leveraging unlabeled data. Furthermore, we analyze the performance metrics and evaluation protocols commonly employed in this domain. Our survey identifies key research gaps, including the need for robust algorithms that can effectively manage concept drift, adapt to varying degrees of label scarcity, and minimize the impact of label delay. Finally, we discuss promising future research directions, such as exploring novel ensemble methods, reinforcement learning approaches for label acquisition, and self-training techniques tailored to dynamic data stream environments. This work provides a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners seeking to develop and apply SSL methods to real-world applications involving complex and evolving data streams.

**Keywords:** Semi-Supervised Learning, Data Streams, Delayed Labels, Partially Labelled Data, Concept Drift, Machine Learning.
