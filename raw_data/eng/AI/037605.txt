Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have shown promise in medical image analysis, particularly for diffuse lung disease (DLD) classification. However, understanding the feature representations learned by these networks remains a challenge. This paper introduces a novel two-stage feature transfer approach to analyze and enhance feature representation in CNNs for DLD classification. In the first stage, features from a pre-trained CNN model are transferred to a target CNN model to initialize the weights and accelerate training. The second stage involves a refined feature transfer strategy that selectively transfers high-level features based on their relevance to DLD classification, assessed using a feature importance metric. Experiments on a publicly available DLD dataset demonstrate that the proposed two-stage transfer approach improves classification accuracy compared to training from scratch and single-stage transfer learning methods. Specifically, the proposed method achieves a 5% improvement in average F1-score. Furthermore, visualization of the learned feature maps reveals that the two-stage approach enables the network to focus on disease-specific regions within the lung images. This research provides a valuable framework for understanding and improving feature representation in CNNs for medical image analysis, with potential implications for computer-aided diagnosis of DLDs.

Keywords: Convolutional Neural Network, Feature Transfer, Medical Image Analysis, Diffuse Lung Disease, Classification, Deep Learning.
