This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of classification tasks in high-dimensional data spaces. Traditional methods often struggle with the curse of dimensionality and noise in data, limiting their performance. The purpose of this research is to propose a novel hybrid model that robustly tackles these issues through a combination of deep learning and ensemble techniques.

Methods: We introduce the Hybrid Embedded Deep Stacked Sparse Autoencoder (HEDSSA) as a foundational model for dimensionality reduction and feature extraction. The extracted features are further processed through a weighted Local Pairwise Probability Density Support Vector Machine (w LPPD SVM) ensemble to improve classification outcomes. The deep stacked sparse autoencoder is employed for its ability to learn hierarchical representations, while the SVM ensemble adds robustness to variations in data distributions and enhances decision boundary accuracy.

Results: Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed HEDSSA with w LPPD SVM ensemble outperforms existing benchmark models in terms of classification accuracy and computational efficiency. Comparative analysis indicates a significant improvement in handling complex data structures, with the hybrid model achieving superior results on numerous datasets, including high-dimensional and noise-infused datasets.

Conclusion: This research presents a novel approach to classification in high-dimensional spaces by leveraging the strengths of deep learning and ensemble methods. The integration of a stacked sparse autoencoder with an SVM ensemble provides a potent tool for high-dimensional data analysis, offering significant improvements in accuracy and robustness. The hybrid model's practical implications include its potential applications in various fields such as image recognition, bioinformatics, and other domains requiring precise classification capabilities.

Keywords: hybrid model, deep learning, stacked sparse autoencoder, SVM ensemble, high-dimensional data, classification, dimensionality reduction, feature extraction.
