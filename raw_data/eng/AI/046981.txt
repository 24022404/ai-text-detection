This paper presents a comparative study of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for the joint task of object detection and pose estimation. The objective is to evaluate and compare the performance of several state-of-the-art CNN architectures in simultaneously identifying objects within an image and estimating their 3D pose. We examine established models, including Faster R-CNN and Mask R-CNN, alongside more recent architectures designed for this dual task. Our methodology involves training and testing these models on benchmark datasets, evaluating their accuracy in object detection (mAP) and pose estimation (Mean Per-Joint Position Error). The results highlight the trade-offs between accuracy, computational cost, and complexity for each model. We identify key factors influencing performance, such as network depth, feature representation, and loss function design. Ultimately, this work provides valuable insights into the effectiveness of different CNN approaches for joint object detection and pose estimation, offering guidance for selecting appropriate architectures for specific applications. Keywords: Convolutional Neural Networks, Object Detection, Pose Estimation, Deep Learning, Computer Vision.
