This paper addresses the challenge of spatial mapping in environments characterized by non-uniform variability. Traditional spatial mapping methods often struggle with capturing local variations and adapting to nonstationary conditions. Our research aims to improve spatial representation and prediction accuracy by utilizing Gaussian Processes (GPs) enhanced with nonstationary Fourier features.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel approach integrating Gaussian Processes with nonstationary Fourier features, which allows for flexible modeling of spatial data characterized by varying conditions. This method employs a tailored kernel adaptation that effectively captures local nonstationarity and enhances the expressiveness of the model. Our approach is computationally efficient, leveraging the inherent structure of Fourier features to approximate the GP kernel, thus enabling scalable spatial mapping in high-dimensional spaces.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms traditional Gaussian Processes and state-of-the-art adaptive kernel methods in both synthetic and real-world datasets. The proposed approach exhibits superior accuracy in capturing spatial variability, particularly in scenarios with abrupt changes or irregular patterns. Quantitative analysis shows an improvement in predictive performance, with reduced mean squared error and enhanced spatial fidelity.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of nonstationary Fourier features into Gaussian Processes represents a significant advancement in spatial mapping techniques. Our approach provides a robust framework for capturing complex spatial phenomena, with potential applications ranging from environmental monitoring to autonomous navigation. The ability to model nonstationary environments with greater precision offers a valuable tool for various fields requiring detailed spatial analysis and prediction.

Keywords: Spatial Mapping, Gaussian Processes, Nonstationary Fourier Features, Kernel Adaptation, Predictive Performance, Environmental Monitoring, Autonomous Navigation.
