Abstract:

Understanding the complex relationships among various brain connectivity graphs is crucial for advancing research in neuroscience and neuroinformatics. This paper introduces a novel topology-aware generative adversarial network (TA-GAN) designed for the joint prediction of multiple brain graphs from a single observed brain graph. The proposed method leverages both the topological characteristics of brain networks and the adversarial learning paradigm to model intricate inter-graph dependencies, which traditional graph-generation approaches often overlook. Our approach integrates graph convolutional networks for topology-preserving feature extraction and a multi-head generator architecture to simultaneously predict diverse target brain graphs. Extensive experiments on publicly available brain connectivity datasets demonstrate that TA-GAN significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of graph similarity metrics, structural fidelity, and prediction accuracy. The results highlight the model’s ability to capture cross-graph topological patterns while maintaining individual graph nuances. This research provides a powerful framework for multi-graph brain analysis, with potential applications in brain disease diagnosis, neuroscience research, and computational connectomics.

Keywords: generative adversarial network, topology-aware, brain networks, graph prediction, multi-graph learning, brain connectivity, graph neural networks.
