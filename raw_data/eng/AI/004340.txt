The paper addresses the challenge of effectively learning hierarchical representations within Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) to improve decision-making efficiency and scalability. Traditional reinforcement learning approaches often struggle with complex environments due to the flat representation of states and actions, leading to inefficient learning.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel hierarchical representation learning framework tailored for MDPs, which builds on the integration of hierarchical reinforcement learning and representation learning techniques. The proposed model utilizes a hierarchical structure to decompose the state-action space into more manageable sub-spaces, facilitating the learning process by incorporating both macro-actions and lower-level details. Our approach leverages deep learning architectures to automatically extract and learn meaningful hierarchical features from the data, enhancing the scalability of the MDPs.

Results/Findings: The experimental evaluation demonstrates that our hierarchical representation learning framework significantly outperforms conventional methods in terms of convergence speed and policy performance across a suite of simulated environments with varying levels of complexity. The results indicate that the model effectively captures hierarchical state-action structures, leading to superior generalization and robustness in policy learning tasks.

Conclusion/Implications: This research presents a substantial advancement in hierarchical representation for MDPs, offering a scalable and efficient pathway to tackle complex decision-making problems. The proposed framework can be applied to various domains such as robotics, autonomous systems, and strategic game playing, where efficient navigation through large and intricate decision spaces is crucial. Our approach highlights the importance of integrating hierarchical thinking into MDPs, setting a new precedent for future developments in the field.

Keywords: Markov Decision Processes, Hierarchical Representation Learning, Reinforcement Learning, Deep Learning, Scalability, Policy Learning.
