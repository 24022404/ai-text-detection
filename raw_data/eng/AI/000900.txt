The detection of local defects in various materials and surfaces is a critical task in quality control across different industries. This research aims to address the challenges in identifying small-scale defects by developing a blockwise based detection approach that enhances accuracy and efficiency.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel blockwise detection algorithm that segments material surfaces into smaller, manageable blocks. Each block is independently analyzed using a combination of image processing techniques and machine learning models to detect anomalies. The system leverages convolutional neural networks (CNN) to enhance the precision of defect localization, exploiting the spatial features within each block.

Results/Findings: Our approach has been tested on a diverse set of material samples, demonstrating a significant improvement in defect detection accuracy compared to traditional methods. The blockwise segmentation strategy reduces computational load and increases processing speed, while the integration of CNN models enhances detection sensitivity and specificity. Comparisons with existing techniques showed that our method achieves superior performance metrics across various defect types and surface textures.

Conclusion/Implications: The blockwise based detection system presents a significant advancement in local defect identification, offering enhanced accuracy and efficiency. This technique is particularly beneficial in real-time industrial applications where quick and precise defect detection is essential. Additionally, our approach's scalability and adaptability suggest its potential for broader application across different inspection systems and materials.

Keywords: defect detection, blockwise segmentation, machine learning, convolutional neural networks, quality control, image processing.
