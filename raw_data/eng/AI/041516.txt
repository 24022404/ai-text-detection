This paper presents a novel approach to cross-person gaze estimation, introducing an individual-difference-aware model that accounts for unique characteristics of each person's gaze behavior. The objective is to improve the accuracy of gaze estimation across different individuals, addressing the limitations of existing models that often rely on averaged or generic gaze patterns. Our method employs a deep learning-based framework that incorporates individual differences in eye movement and gaze direction, leveraging a large dataset of eye-tracking recordings from diverse participants. The results show significant improvements in gaze estimation accuracy compared to state-of-the-art models, with an average error reduction of 30%. The proposed model has important implications for human-computer interaction, virtual reality, and social robotics applications, where accurate gaze estimation is crucial for understanding user intentions and behaviors. Key contributions include the development of a personalized gaze estimation framework, the introduction of a novel loss function that accounts for individual differences, and the creation of a large-scale dataset for cross-person gaze estimation. Relevant keywords: gaze estimation, individual differences, deep learning, eye tracking, human-computer interaction.
