This paper addresses the challenge of reinforcement learning in environments with parameterized action spaces, where the number of possible actions is vast and depends on the current state. Our objective is to develop hierarchical approaches that can efficiently explore and learn in such complex domains. We propose a novel framework that combines hierarchical reinforcement learning with action embedding techniques, allowing the agent to learn a compact representation of the action space and make informed decisions. Our method utilizes a hierarchical architecture consisting of a high-level policy that selects a subset of actions and a low-level policy that refines the selected actions. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in several benchmark environments, achieving significant improvements in terms of sample efficiency and cumulative rewards. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a hierarchical action embedding scheme and a novel exploration strategy that adapts to the parameterized action space. Our work has important implications for applications in robotics, game playing, and other domains where reinforcement learning can be applied, and highlights the potential of hierarchical approaches for tackling complex decision-making problems in AI. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, hierarchical reinforcement learning, parameterized action space, action embedding, exploration strategies.
