Transfer learning has revolutionized Natural Language Processing (NLP) by enabling models to leverage pre-trained knowledge for downstream tasks. However, existing approaches often require substantial computational resources, large datasets, and extensive fine-tuning, limiting their scalability and efficiency. This paper introduces Conditionally Adaptive Multi-Task Learning (CAMTL), a novel framework designed to improve transfer learning in NLP while reducing the need for excessive parameters and data. CAMTL dynamically adapts shared representations across tasks based on conditional task-specific parameters, enabling efficient knowledge transfer with minimal resource overhead. Our approach leverages lightweight adapters and task-conditioned transformations, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters compared to traditional fine-tuning methods. Experiments on benchmark NLP datasets demonstrate that CAMTL achieves competitive performance with state-of-the-art models while using up to 50% fewer parameters and requiring less training data. The results highlight the framework's ability to generalize across diverse tasks, making it particularly suitable for low-resource settings. CAMTL's innovations lie in its parameter efficiency, adaptability, and scalability, offering a practical solution for deploying NLP models in resource-constrained environments. This research contributes to advancing transfer learning methodologies, paving the way for more sustainable and accessible NLP applications.  

**Keywords**: Transfer Learning, Multi-Task Learning, NLP, Parameter Efficiency, Few-Shot Learning, Adaptive Models.
