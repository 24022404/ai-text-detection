Iris recognition is a widely adopted biometric technology, but challenges arise when matching iris images captured under different spectral bands (e.g., visible vs. near-infrared) or resolutions. This paper addresses the problem of cross-spectral and cross-resolution iris recognition by proposing a novel deep learning framework based on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). The objective is to bridge the domain gap between heterogeneous iris images while preserving discriminative features for accurate recognition. Our approach leverages a dual-path GAN architecture to synthesize high-quality, resolution-invariant iris images across spectral domains. The model is trained on a diverse dataset of paired and unpaired iris images, incorporating adversarial and perceptual loss functions to ensure realistic and identity-preserving transformations. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in recognition accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods, achieving a 15% increase in cross-spectral matching performance and a 20% enhancement in cross-resolution scenarios. The proposed framework also exhibits robustness to variations in image quality and environmental conditions. This research contributes to advancing iris recognition systems by enabling seamless interoperability across different imaging modalities and resolutions, with potential applications in security, surveillance, and identity management. Key innovations include the integration of spectral and resolution adaptation within a unified GAN-based framework and the introduction of a novel loss function tailored for biometric feature preservation. Keywords: iris recognition, GAN, cross-spectral, cross-resolution, deep learning, biometrics.
