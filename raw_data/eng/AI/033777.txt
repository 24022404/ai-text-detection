This survey aims to provide a comprehensive overview of self-supervised visual feature learning methods using deep neural networks. The objective is to understand the current state-of-the-art techniques that enable learning effective visual representations without relying on annotated data. We describe various approaches, including autoencoders, generative adversarial networks, and contrastive learning methods, highlighting their strengths and weaknesses. Our analysis reveals that self-supervised learning can achieve competitive performance with supervised methods on several benchmarks, such as image classification and object detection. The key findings suggest that self-supervised learning can reduce the need for labeled data, improve model robustness, and enhance transferability across different tasks. This research contributes to the development of more efficient and scalable visual learning systems, with potential applications in computer vision, robotics, and autonomous driving. The survey emphasizes the novelty of self-supervised learning methods, including their ability to learn from raw data, discover hidden patterns, and adapt to new environments. Key keywords: self-supervised learning, deep neural networks, visual feature learning, autoencoders, contrastive learning, computer vision, robotics.
