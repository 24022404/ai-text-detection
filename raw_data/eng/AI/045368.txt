This paper proposes a novel self-supervised approach for time series representation learning, leveraging inter-intra relational reasoning to capture complex temporal dependencies. The objective is to learn robust and informative representations of time series data without relying on labeled information. Our method employs a relational reasoning module to model both inter-series and intra-series relationships, enabling the discovery of underlying patterns and structures. The approach utilizes a combination of contrastive learning and graph-based neural networks to learn effective representations. Experimental results demonstrate the superiority of our method over existing self-supervised and supervised approaches, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark time series datasets. The proposed framework has significant implications for real-world applications, such as anomaly detection, forecasting, and clustering. Key contributions include the introduction of inter-intra relational reasoning for time series representation learning and the development of a self-supervised framework that outperforms supervised methods. Relevant keywords: self-supervised learning, time series representation, relational reasoning, contrastive learning, graph neural networks.
