Symmetric and hash-based cryptographic methods remain robust against quantum attacks, whereas common asymmetric cryptosystems such as RSA and Elliptic Curve Cryptography are vulnerable to powerful quantum computers capable of running Shor’s algorithm, which can efficiently break these schemes. In contemporary security protocols, public key cryptosystems play a crucial role in establishing shared secret keys between parties. However, the emergence of Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) marks a significant advancement, as it enables two parties to securely generate and share a random secret key that is intrinsically protected against eavesdropping, even by adversaries equipped with quantum computational capabilities.
