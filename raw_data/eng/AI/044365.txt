This paper presents Fractal Gaussian Networks, a novel sparse random graph model inspired by the principles of Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos. The objective of this research is to explore the intricate structures and properties of random graphs that exhibit fractal characteristics, amplifying our understanding of complex networks. We develop a robust mathematical framework that integrates concepts from probability theory and fractal geometry to construct these networks. By employing advanced simulation techniques and analytical methods, we systematically analyze the connectivity, clustering coefficients, and other critical metrics of our proposed model.

Our findings reveal that Fractal Gaussian Networks demonstrate unique scaling properties and enhanced resilience compared to traditional random graph models. Notably, the introduction of Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos leads to richer topology, characterized by a diverse range of connected components and fractal dimensions, effectively capturing the inherent complexity of real-world networks. The implications of this research extend to various fields, including computer science, physics, and biological systems, where the understanding of connectivity patterns can inform the design of more efficient networks.

In conclusion, our study not only provides a fresh perspective on random graph generation but also highlights the potential applications of Fractal Gaussian Networks in modeling real-life phenomena. This work paves the way for future explorations into the intersection of graph theory and chaotic systems, presenting opportunities for innovative applications across multiple domains.

Keywords: Fractal Gaussian Networks, sparse random graphs, Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos, complex networks, network resilience.
