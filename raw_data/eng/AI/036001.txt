This paper introduces ALBA, a novel approach to video object segmentation leveraging reinforcement learning. The objective is to accurately segment objects of interest across consecutive video frames, addressing the challenges of object deformation, occlusion, and complex backgrounds. ALBA employs a deep reinforcement learning framework, utilizing a policy network to guide the segmentation process and a value network to evaluate the quality of the segmented masks. The method is trained end-to-end, allowing the model to learn an effective policy for segmenting objects in videos. Experimental results demonstrate that ALBA outperforms state-of-the-art video object segmentation methods, achieving significant improvements in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a reinforcement learning-based approach to video object segmentation, the design of a novel policy-value network architecture, and the demonstration of the effectiveness of ALBA in handling complex video sequences. This work has important implications for applications such as video editing, surveillance, and autonomous driving, where accurate object segmentation is crucial. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, video object segmentation, deep learning, policy network, value network.
