This paper investigates the concept of using programs as black-box explanations, exploring how software can be interpreted and leveraged to provide insights into complex systems without requiring direct access to their internal mechanisms. The primary objective is to address the challenge of explaining the behavior of opaque systems commonly encountered in software engineering and artificial intelligence.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel framework that treats entire programs as black-box entities, utilizing a combination of symbolic execution, automated testing, and machine learning to derive meaningful explanations of program behavior. The approach emphasizes extracting patterns, causal relationships, and logical rules that account for the observable outputs of these programs under various inputs.

Results/Findings: Our experimental evaluation demonstrates that the proposed framework can generate accurate and comprehensible explanations for a diverse range of software systems. Comparisons with existing methods reveal significant improvements in explanation quality, interpretability, and performance. The framework empowers users to understand program behavior with minimal manual intervention, bridging the gap between abstraction and comprehensibility.

Conclusion/Implications: The study presents a significant advancement in the field of explainable software systems, offering a practical methodology for understanding black-box programs. The contributions include enhanced transparency of complex systems, aiding developers in debugging and enhancing software reliability. Potential applications extend to areas such as software verification, AI model interpretability, and education, where understanding inherently non-transparent systems is crucial.

Keywords: black-box explanations, software interpretability, symbolic execution, automated testing, machine learning, program behavior, explainable AI.
