**Abstract**

Deep neural networks (DNNs) have achieved remarkable success in various computer vision and machine learning tasks, but their deployment in resource-constrained environments remains challenging due to their high computational complexity and memory demands. This paper introduces a novel framework that combines the Fast Walsh-Hadamard Transform (FWHT) with smooth-thresholding techniques to enhance binary layers in deep neural networks. Our approach leverages the computational efficiency of FWHT for rapid transformation and projection of feature maps within binary neural architectures, significantly reducing the arithmetic operations compared to traditional convolutions. Moreover, a smooth-thresholding function is integrated to address the gradient mismatch inherent in hard binarization, enabling better gradient flow and improved model learning during training. Experimental results on benchmark image classification datasets demonstrate that our proposed FWHT and smooth-thresholding based binary layers achieve competitive accuracy with substantially lower computational overhead, outperforming existing binary network baselines in both speed and efficiency. The proposed methodology offers a compelling solution for deploying high-performance DNNs on edge devices and real-time applications. Keywords: Fast Walsh-Hadamard Transform, smooth-thresholding, binary neural networks, model compression, efficient deep learning, edge computing.
