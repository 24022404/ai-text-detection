Toxicity prediction is a critical task in drug discovery and environmental safety, yet traditional methods often rely on complex molecular descriptors and computationally expensive models. This paper proposes a novel approach for efficient toxicity prediction using simple molecular features combined with shallow neural networks and decision trees. The objective is to reduce computational overhead while maintaining high prediction accuracy. We employ a feature selection process to identify a minimal set of interpretable molecular properties, such as atom counts, bond types, and basic topological indices, which are then fed into a hybrid model comprising shallow neural networks and decision trees. The model is trained and validated on widely used toxicity datasets, demonstrating competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art methods. Results show that our approach achieves comparable accuracy while significantly reducing training and inference times. The simplicity of the selected features also enhances interpretability, making the model more accessible for practical applications. This work contributes a lightweight, efficient, and interpretable solution for toxicity prediction, offering potential applications in high-throughput screening and real-time toxicity assessment.  

**Keywords**: Toxicity prediction, shallow neural networks, decision trees, molecular features, interpretability, computational efficiency.
