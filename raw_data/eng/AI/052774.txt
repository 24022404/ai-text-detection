This research addresses the challenge of uncovering vulnerabilities in black-box machine learning models, which are often opaque and difficult to interpret. The objective is to develop a method that efficiently identifies these vulnerabilities while maintaining interpretability, crucial for both model evaluation and regulatory compliance.

Methods/Approach: We introduce "Patch Shortcuts," a novel approach utilizing interpretable proxy models to emulate black-box models' behavior. This method leverages perturbation techniques to identify and analyze vulnerability patterns in the model's decision space without requiring direct access to its internals. The proxy models are trained to approximate the black-box's outputs, facilitating the discovery of specific input conditions that lead to incorrect or unexpected behavior.

Results/Findings: The proposed Patch Shortcuts approach was applied to various commonly used machine learning models across different datasets. It demonstrated a high efficiency in identifying and explaining model vulnerabilities with greater accuracy compared to traditional black-box testing methods. Notably, it reduced the computational overhead typically associated with exhaustive testing, while providing clear visual representations of vulnerability paths.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes significant advancements in the field of model interpretability and vulnerability assessment. The Patch Shortcuts method not only enhances our ability to uncover hidden flaws in black-box models but also aids in understanding their decision-making processes. Its applications are wide-ranging, from enhancing AI safety and robustness to providing tools for regulatory compliance in fields where model transparency is critical. This work bridges the gap between black-box model performance and stakeholder interpretability, paving the way for more secure and reliable AI systems.

Keywords: Patch Shortcuts, black-box vulnerabilities, interpretable proxy models, machine learning, AI safety, model interpretability, perturbation techniques.
