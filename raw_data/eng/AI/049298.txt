Gesture recognition is a critical area of research in human-computer interaction, with applications ranging from virtual reality to assistive technologies. This paper addresses the challenge of identifying and prioritizing important actions within a sequence of gestures, which is essential for efficient and accurate recognition systems. We propose a novel approach based on a path searching method in a weighted graph, where gestures are modeled as nodes and transitions between gestures as edges with associated weights. The weights are determined by the significance and contextual relevance of each gesture, enabling the system to focus on key actions while filtering out less important ones. Our method leverages graph traversal algorithms to identify optimal paths, ensuring robust recognition even in complex gesture sequences. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves superior accuracy and computational efficiency compared to traditional methods, particularly in scenarios with high variability and noise. The findings highlight the potential of this method for real-time applications, offering a scalable and adaptable solution for gesture recognition systems. This research contributes to the field by introducing a graph-based framework that prioritizes important actions, paving the way for more intuitive and context-aware human-computer interfaces.  

**Keywords**: gesture recognition, weighted graph, path searching, human-computer interaction, action prioritization, real-time systems.
