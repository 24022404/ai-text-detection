Transfer learning, where knowledge gained from one task is applied to another, has become a cornerstone of modern machine learning. This paper investigates scaling laws governing transfer learning performance, examining how performance improves with increased model size, dataset size, and compute during both pre-training and fine-tuning stages. We present empirical evidence across a range of transfer learning benchmarks, demonstrating that transfer performance follows predictable power-law relationships with these key scaling factors. Our results indicate that the optimal strategy for transfer involves balancing resources between pre-training and fine-tuning, with a diminishing returns effect observed beyond certain scale thresholds. Furthermore, we analyze the impact of dataset similarity on transfer scaling laws, finding that more similar source and target datasets lead to more efficient transfer. These findings provide practical guidelines for resource allocation in transfer learning pipelines and contribute to a deeper understanding of generalization in large-scale models. Keywords: Transfer Learning, Scaling Laws, Pre-training, Fine-tuning, Generalization, Model Size, Dataset Size, Compute.
