This paper addresses the challenge of achieving explainable disease classification in medical imaging by leveraging weakly-supervised segmentation techniques. Traditional deep learning models for disease classification often lack interpretability, making it difficult for clinicians to trust and understand their predictions. To bridge this gap, we propose a novel framework that integrates weakly-supervised segmentation with explainable classification, enabling the model to localize disease-related regions while simultaneously providing accurate diagnostic predictions. Our approach utilizes a multi-task learning architecture, where a segmentation branch is trained using only image-level labels, eliminating the need for costly pixel-level annotations. The classification branch is then guided by the segmentation outputs to enhance interpretability. Experiments conducted on benchmark medical imaging datasets demonstrate that our method achieves competitive classification accuracy while providing meaningful visual explanations of the decision-making process. The results show significant improvements in both localization precision and diagnostic performance compared to existing weakly-supervised methods. This work contributes to the growing field of explainable AI in healthcare by offering a practical solution for disease classification that is both accurate and interpretable, paving the way for greater adoption of AI-driven diagnostic tools in clinical settings.  

**Keywords**: explainable AI, weakly-supervised segmentation, disease classification, medical imaging, multi-task learning, interpretability.
