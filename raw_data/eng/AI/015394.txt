Object detection remains a critical challenge in computer vision, with applications ranging from autonomous driving to surveillance systems. This paper explores the potential of transformer-based architectures for object detection, aiming to address limitations in traditional convolutional neural network (CNN)-based approaches. We propose a novel framework that leverages the self-attention mechanism of transformers to capture global context and improve detection accuracy, particularly in complex scenes with occlusions and varying object scales. Our approach integrates a transformer encoder-decoder structure with a lightweight feature extraction backbone, enabling efficient processing of high-resolution images. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, such as COCO and Pascal VOC, demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance, outperforming state-of-the-art CNN-based detectors in terms of mean average precision (mAP) while maintaining computational efficiency. The results highlight the effectiveness of transformers in modeling long-range dependencies and handling diverse object distributions. This work contributes to advancing object detection by introducing a scalable and robust transformer-based solution, with potential applications in real-time systems and edge computing environments.  

**Keywords**: Object detection, transformers, self-attention, computer vision, deep learning, feature extraction.
