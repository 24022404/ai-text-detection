This paper presents a comprehensive benchmarking study on recognition results achieved with various word image datasets, a critical task in the fields of document analysis and computer vision. The primary objective is to evaluate and compare the performance of leading recognition algorithms on diverse and publicly available word image datasets, which vary in font styles, noise levels, backgrounds, and real-world complexities. We systematically analyze several state-of-the-art word recognition methods, including both traditional feature-based approaches and modern deep learning models. The benchmarking covers essential metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and computational efficiency, providing a thorough assessment of system strengths and weaknesses in different scenarios. Key findings highlight notable differences in algorithm performance, with deep learning techniques demonstrating superior accuracy on complex datasets, while traditional models offer advantages in processing speed and resource efficiency. Our analysis also identifies inherent challenges, such as occlusion, distortion, and variable lighting conditions, that significantly impact recognition outcomes. The study’s results furnish valuable guidelines for researchers and practitioners in selecting appropriate recognition systems for specific word image applications, such as scene text recognition, document digitization, and assistive technologies. Finally, we discuss the implications for future research, including the necessity for more robust and diverse benchmarking datasets and the ongoing development of adaptive recognition models. Keywords: word image recognition, benchmarking, datasets, deep learning, scene text, document analysis, image processing.
