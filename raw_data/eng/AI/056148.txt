This paper addresses the challenge of slow convergence in reinforcement learning-based auto-pruning methods by leveraging historical data to inform the pruning process. Our objective is to develop an efficient and effective approach that accelerates the convergence of auto-pruning models. We propose a novel framework that integrates historical data into the reinforcement learning loop, enabling the model to learn from past experiences and adapt to new scenarios. Our method utilizes a combination of offline and online learning strategies, where historical data is used to pre-train the model and initialize the pruning process, while online learning fine-tunes the model and updates the pruning policy. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly improves the convergence speed and pruning accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods. The key findings of this study highlight the importance of incorporating historical data in reinforcement learning-based auto-pruning, leading to improved model efficiency and reduced computational costs. Our research contributes to the development of more efficient and scalable auto-pruning methods, with potential applications in deep learning, computer vision, and natural language processing. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, auto-pruning, historical data, convergence acceleration, deep learning, model efficiency.
