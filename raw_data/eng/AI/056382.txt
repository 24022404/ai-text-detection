This research addresses the challenge of enhancing image classification and regression tasks by leveraging knowledge distillation and generative adversarial networks (GANs). We focus on the potential of conditional GANs (cGANs) to generate synthetic samples that effectively transfer learned representations from teacher models to student models.

Methods/Approach: Our approach utilizes cGANs to create augmented datasets that encapsulate essential features derived from pre-trained teacher models. The cGAN-generated samples are then employed to train student models, allowing for the distillation of knowledge in both classification and regression contexts. We explore various configurations of cGAN architectures to optimize the quality and diversity of generated samples, thereby improving model transferability.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that using cGAN-generated samples significantly enhances the performance of student models in both image classification and regression tasks. Our method shows improvements in accuracy and regression metrics when compared to traditional knowledge distillation techniques that do not incorporate GAN-generated data. This advancement is consistent across multiple benchmark datasets, illustrating the robustness and effectiveness of our approach.

Conclusion/Implications: The study underscores the potential of cGANs in augmenting and transferring knowledge within deep learning frameworks for image classification and regression. By synthesizing informative samples, our method provides a novel pathway for improving model efficiency and effectiveness, with implications for various real-world applications such as medical imaging and autonomous systems. This research contributes to the field by introducing an innovative strategy for leveraging GAN capabilities in knowledge distillation contexts.

Keywords: knowledge distillation, conditional GANs (cGANs), image classification, regression, synthetic samples, deep learning, model transferability.
