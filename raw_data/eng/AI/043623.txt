Understanding the decision-making processes of deep reinforcement learning (RL) agents remains a significant challenge, particularly in complex environments such as Atari video games. This paper presents a comprehensive benchmarking study of perturbation-based saliency map methods for explaining the behavior of RL agents trained on the Atari suite. Our objective is to systematically evaluate the reliability, fidelity, and interpretability of various perturbation-based explanation techniques applied to Atari game-playing agents. We implement and analyze multiple saliency mapping methods, including occlusion and noise injection, across a set of representative Atari environments. The experimental methodology assesses the capacity of each method to highlight key input features influencing agent actions, employing both qualitative visualizations and quantitative metrics such as faithfulness and localization accuracy. Our results reveal important differences in the explanatory power and computational efficiency of the studied techniques, with some methods better capturing the salient pixels responsible for decision-making under diverse game scenarios. The study also uncovers practical limitations and potential sources of misleading explanations in existing approaches. We conclude by discussing best practices for employing perturbation-based saliency maps in RL agent interpretability and propose recommendations for future research. This benchmark serves as a foundational resource for the community, aiding the development of more transparent and trustworthy AI systems in interactive environments. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, explainable AI, saliency maps, perturbation methods, Atari agents, model interpretability.
