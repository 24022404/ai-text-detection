This paper addresses the challenge of leveraging unlabeled data to improve regression tasks in semi-supervised learning (SSL) settings. Traditional regression models often rely heavily on labeled data, which can be expensive or time-consuming to obtain. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel approach called Semi-supervised Deep Kernel Learning (SSDKL), which integrates deep learning with kernel methods to minimize predictive variance using both labeled and unlabeled data. The proposed method combines the expressive power of deep neural networks with the robustness of kernel-based regression, enabling effective learning from limited labeled data while exploiting the structure of unlabeled data.  

Our approach introduces a predictive variance minimization objective that regularizes the model to produce consistent predictions across the unlabeled dataset, thereby improving generalization. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that SSDKL outperforms existing semi-supervised regression methods, achieving significant reductions in prediction error. The results highlight the effectiveness of leveraging unlabeled data to enhance model performance, particularly in scenarios with scarce labeled data.  

This work contributes a scalable and efficient framework for semi-supervised regression, with potential applications in domains such as healthcare, finance, and environmental modeling, where labeled data is often limited but unlabeled data is abundant. The proposed method advances the state-of-the-art in SSL by bridging deep learning and kernel methods, offering a robust solution for regression tasks in data-scarce environments.  

**Keywords**: Semi-supervised learning, deep kernel learning, regression, predictive variance, unlabeled data, deep learning, kernel methods.
