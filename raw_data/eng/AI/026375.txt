Optical flow estimation is a fundamental problem in computer vision, with applications ranging from motion analysis to video processing. Traditional methods often rely on fixed norms, which may not adapt well to varying motion patterns and noise levels in real-world scenarios. This paper introduces an adaptive mixed norm optical flow estimation framework designed to address these limitations. The proposed approach dynamically combines L1 and L2 norms to balance robustness against noise and sensitivity to fine motion details. A novel optimization algorithm is developed to efficiently solve the mixed norm problem, leveraging iterative reweighting techniques and gradient-based methods. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the adaptive mixed norm approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly in challenging conditions such as occlusions and illumination changes. The framework's flexibility and computational efficiency make it suitable for real-time applications, including autonomous navigation and video surveillance. This research contributes a significant advancement in optical flow estimation by introducing adaptability into the norm selection process, offering a practical solution for diverse real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: optical flow, mixed norm, adaptive estimation, motion analysis, computer vision, optimization.
