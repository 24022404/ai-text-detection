This paper presents a novel approach to hue modification localization by leveraging pair matching techniques. The objective is to accurately identify and localize regions of an image where the hue has been modified, which is crucial for various applications such as image forensics and authentication. Our method employs a deep learning-based framework that utilizes pair matching to compare the original and modified images, enabling the detection of subtle hue changes. The approach involves training a neural network to learn the patterns and relationships between the paired images, allowing for effective localization of hue modifications. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our method, outperforming existing state-of-the-art techniques in terms of accuracy and robustness. The proposed technique has significant implications for image forensics, authentication, and security, and contributes to the development of more sophisticated image analysis tools. Key contributions include the introduction of pair matching for hue modification localization and the demonstration of its potential in real-world applications, highlighting the importance of this research in the fields of computer vision, image processing, and machine learning.
