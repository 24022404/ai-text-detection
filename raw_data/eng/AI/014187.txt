Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved remarkable success in various domains, yet its generalization capabilities remain limited compared to biological learning systems. Inspired by principles of neuroscience, this paper proposes a novel framework that integrates neural mechanisms into RL agents to enhance their adaptability and generalization. The objective is to bridge the gap between artificial and biological learning by leveraging insights from synaptic plasticity, memory consolidation, and reward processing in the brain. We introduce a biologically inspired RL model that incorporates dynamic reward modulation, hierarchical memory structures, and meta-learning mechanisms to improve learning efficiency and task transferability. Experiments conducted on benchmark environments demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms traditional RL methods in terms of sample efficiency, generalization to unseen tasks, and robustness to environmental changes. Key findings reveal that the integration of neuroscience principles enables agents to learn more effectively in complex, dynamic scenarios. This research contributes to the development of more general and adaptive RL agents, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and decision-making under uncertainty. The proposed framework highlights the importance of interdisciplinary approaches in advancing artificial intelligence.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Neuroscience, Generalization, Synaptic Plasticity, Meta-Learning, Adaptive Systems.
