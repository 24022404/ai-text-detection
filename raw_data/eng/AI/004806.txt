Understanding and separating the factors that influence observations is a fundamental challenge in artificial intelligence and machine learning, particularly for applications in reinforcement learning and autonomous systems. This paper presents a novel framework for disentangling controllable and uncontrollable factors of variation in complex environments by enabling agents to actively interact with the world. Our approach combines unsupervised representation learning with interactive exploration, allowing the agent to distinguish between aspects of the environment it can directly manipulate and those it cannot. The proposed method leverages deep neural networks to encode observational data while incorporating action-conditioned modules that trace the impact of agent behavior on the latent representations. Experimental results in simulated scenarios demonstrate that our model effectively learns interpretable and disentangled representations, improving both generalization and downstream task performance, such as transfer learning and robust policy learning. Our findings suggest that facilitating interaction-based disentanglement leads to more adaptive and resilient agents, with significant implications for robotics, autonomous navigation, and intelligent decision-support systems. Key contributions include a principled framework for interaction-driven disentanglement, empirical validation across diverse benchmarks, and insights into the role of active exploration in representation learning. Keywords: disentanglement, controllable factors, uncontrollable factors, representation learning, reinforcement learning, interactive agents, deep learning.
