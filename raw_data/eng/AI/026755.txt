With the increasing demand for efficient video analysis, our research addresses the challenge of whether image classifiers can be effectively applied to video understanding tasks. Traditional approaches to video understanding often rely on complex and computationally intensive models. This study explores the feasibility of using pre-trained image classifiers to simplify and enhance video content analysis.

Methods/Approach: We developed a framework that repurposes conventional image classification models for video understanding tasks. This approach involves extracting frames from video sequences and applying image classifiers to each frame. We introduce novel techniques to aggregate frame-level predictions into coherent video-level insights, leveraging temporal consistency and motion cues for improved accuracy.

Results/Findings: Our findings indicate that image classifiers can achieve competitive performance in video understanding when adapted appropriately. The proposed method demonstrated superior or comparable accuracy to state-of-the-art video processing techniques across multiple datasets, while significantly reducing computational overhead. The experiments reveal that leveraging image-based models can streamline video analysis, offering efficient processing without a substantial loss in accuracy.

Conclusion/Implications: This research highlights a paradigm shift in video understanding, showcasing that existing image classifiers can be repurposed to handle video content effectively. The proposed approach not only simplifies the process but also has the potential to drastically reduce the computational cost associated with video processing. These insights may inspire further research into optimizing image-based techniques for broader video applications, including real-time surveillance, multimedia indexing, and automated content moderation.

Keywords: video understanding, image classifiers, video analysis, computational efficiency, temporal aggregation, pre-trained models.
