This paper proposes a novel approach to unsupervised skeleton-based action recognition, leveraging prototypical contrast and reverse prediction to enhance model performance. The objective is to develop an effective method for recognizing human actions from skeletal data without relying on labeled training data. Our approach employs a contrastive learning framework, where prototypical representations are learned to capture the underlying structure of actions. Additionally, a reverse prediction module is introduced to further refine the model's ability to distinguish between similar actions. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our method, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. The proposed approach contributes to the field of action recognition by providing a robust and unsupervised solution, with potential applications in human-computer interaction, surveillance, and healthcare. Key contributions include the introduction of prototypical contrast and reverse prediction for unsupervised action recognition, and the achievement of competitive results without requiring labeled training data. Relevant keywords: unsupervised learning, skeleton-based action recognition, prototypical contrast, reverse prediction, human-computer interaction.
