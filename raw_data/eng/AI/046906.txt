This paper investigates the composition properties of inferential privacy in the context of time-series data, addressing the growing need for robust privacy guarantees in dynamic data environments. The objective is to analyze how inferential privacy, a framework designed to limit the leakage of sensitive information, behaves under sequential data releases and complex temporal dependencies. We propose a novel mathematical model to quantify privacy loss in time-series settings, leveraging differential privacy principles and statistical inference techniques. Our approach incorporates adaptive mechanisms to account for temporal correlations and ensures privacy guarantees even under repeated queries. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our model in maintaining strong privacy bounds while preserving data utility, outperforming traditional methods in scenarios with high temporal dependencies. The findings highlight the importance of tailored privacy mechanisms for time-series data and provide insights into designing scalable privacy-preserving systems for real-world applications such as healthcare monitoring, financial forecasting, and IoT analytics. This research contributes to the advancement of privacy-preserving technologies by addressing the unique challenges posed by time-series data composition, offering a foundation for future work in dynamic data privacy.  

**Keywords**: inferential privacy, time-series data, differential privacy, temporal correlations, privacy-preserving mechanisms, data utility.
