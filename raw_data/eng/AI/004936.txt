This paper addresses the challenge of extracting meaningful patterns and reducing dimensionality in multi-way data through Regularized Tensor Factorizations and Higher-Order Principal Components Analysis (HOPCA). As data from various domains increasingly take the form of tensors rather than matrices, efficient techniques for tensor decomposition and analysis become crucial for uncovering implicit structures.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework integrating regularization techniques with tensor factorization methods, enhancing their robustness and interpretability. Our approach extends traditional Principal Components Analysis to higher-order data contexts, leveraging advancements in multilinear algebra. The model incorporates constraints specific to domain knowledge, ensuring the extracted components are not only statistically significant but also contextually relevant. Extensive simulations and empirical analyses are conducted to validate the proposed methods.

Results/Findings: The proposed regularized tensor factorization techniques demonstrate superior performance in terms of accuracy and sparsity when applied to synthetic and real-world datasets. Through quantitative evaluations, our methods show significant improvements in data reconstruction errors and component stability compared to existing approaches. Moreover, HOPCA effectively handles noise and identifies latent structures across various applications, such as signal processing, bioinformatics, and social network analysis.

Conclusion/Implications: Our research contributes a robust and versatile toolset for higher-order data analysis, offering a significant advancement over traditional factorization techniques. The integration of regularization within the tensor decomposition process not only enhances the interpretability of results but also extends the applicability of HOPCA to diverse fields dealing with complex, multidimensional datasets. Potential applications include improved pattern recognition, anomaly detection, and enhanced data-driven decision-making processes. 

Keywords: Regularized Tensor Factorization, Higher-Order Principal Components Analysis, Multilinear Algebra, Dimensionality Reduction, Data Analysis, Signal Processing, Bioinformatics.
