This paper proposes a novel approach to video summarization, leveraging self-attention mechanisms and universal transformers to efficiently capture key frames and events in videos. The objective is to develop a model that can automatically summarize videos by identifying the most informative and representative segments. Our method employs a self-attention based architecture, allowing the model to weigh the importance of different frames and selectively focus on the most relevant information. We utilize universal transformers to enable the model to handle variable-length input sequences and capture long-range dependencies. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, outperforming state-of-the-art video summarization methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The key findings highlight the importance of self-attention in video summarization, enabling the model to adapt to diverse video content and styles. Our research contributes to the development of more efficient and accurate video analysis techniques, with potential applications in video editing, surveillance, and content retrieval. Key keywords: video summarization, self-attention, universal transformers, deep learning, computer vision.
