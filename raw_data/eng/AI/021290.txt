This paper addresses the challenge of autonomous driving on highways by introducing an innovative approach that combines the strengths of model-based and model-free reinforcement learning. The objective is to develop an efficient and adaptive control strategy for autonomous vehicles to navigate complex highway scenarios. Our method, termed Amortized Q-learning with Model-based Action Proposals, leverages a model-based approach to generate action proposals, which are then evaluated and refined using Q-learning. This synergy enables the agent to learn effective policies while reducing the complexity of the action space. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of safety, efficiency, and adaptability. Key findings include improved handling of merging and lane-changing maneuvers, as well as enhanced robustness to sensor noise and uncertainties. The proposed framework contributes to the development of more autonomous and reliable driving systems, with potential applications in real-world highway driving scenarios. Our research highlights the benefits of integrating model-based and model-free techniques, paving the way for future advancements in autonomous driving and reinforcement learning. Keywords: autonomous driving, Q-learning, model-based reinforcement learning, action proposals, highway driving.
