Objective: Neural network auto-pruning via reinforcement learning (RL) offers a promising path to compress models without manual tuning, but suffers from slow convergence due to a vast action space and sparse rewards. This paper introduces a novel framework that leverages historical pruning data to accelerate policy learning and improve final model quality.

Methods: We augment a standard RL-based auto-pruning agent with a Historical Experience Module (HEM) that stores and reuses past state–action–reward trajectories collected across pruning tasks. At training start, HEM provides prioritized warm-start experiences to the policy network. During exploration, the agent dynamically balances fresh samples with replayed historical transitions, guided by a reward-shaping mechanism that emphasizes both sparsity targets and validation accuracy preservation.

Results: Experiments on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet with ResNet-50 and MobileNetV2 show that our approach achieves target sparsity levels up to 80% with 2× faster convergence compared to baseline RL pruning. Model accuracy drops by less than 1.5% while reducing compute cost by 45% on average. Ablation studies confirm that both prioritized replay and reward shaping are critical to performance gains.

Conclusion: Integrating historical pruning experiences into RL-based auto-pruning significantly boosts convergence speed and model quality. This strategy reduces training overhead and simplifies the deployment of compressed networks on resource-constrained devices. Keywords: reinforcement learning, auto-pruning, model compression, historical data, convergence acceleration.
