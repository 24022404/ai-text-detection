Objective: Offline reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms often struggle with distributional shifts and limited coverage of expert data, leading to suboptimal policies. This paper introduces a novel framework that leverages residual generative modeling to enrich offline datasets and improve policy learning.

Methods: We propose Residual Generative Modeling for Offline RL (ResGen-RL), which trains a generative model to capture the residual between the empirical offline data distribution and the true environment dynamics. By sampling corrective transitions from this residual model and integrating them with the original dataset, ResGen-RL produces an augmented dataset that better reflects underrepresented or missing state–action pairs. We then apply a standard offline RL algorithm (e.g., conservative Q-learning) on the augmented data, with a consistency regularizer to control modeling errors.

Results: Experiments on standard benchmark suites (including MuJoCo locomotion and D4RL tasks) demonstrate that ResGen-RL consistently outperforms state-of-the-art offline RL methods. On average, our approach yields a 15–25% improvement in normalized return, particularly in environments with sparse or biased expert trajectories. Ablation studies confirm that residual augmentation and the consistency regularizer are both critical to performance gains.

Conclusion: By explicitly modeling and compensating for gaps in offline datasets, ResGen-RL offers a scalable means to mitigate distributional shift and enhance policy quality without additional environment interactions. This approach opens new avenues for robust, data-efficient offline policy learning in real-world applications.

Keywords: Offline Reinforcement Learning, Residual Generative Modeling, Data Augmentation, Distribution Shift, Policy Optimization.
