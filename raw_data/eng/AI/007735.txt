Pedestrian trajectory prediction is a critical component in the development of autonomous vehicles, intelligent surveillance systems, and crowd analysis applications. However, accurately forecasting human movement in dynamic and complex environments remains challenging due to the influence of contextual factors such as surrounding pedestrians, static obstacles, and environmental semantics. This paper presents a novel approach to pedestrian trajectory prediction by leveraging Context-Augmented Transformer Networks (CATN). The proposed model integrates transformer-based self-attention mechanisms with augmented contextual encodings, effectively capturing both spatial and temporal dependencies alongside rich environmental cues. Extensive experiments were conducted on benchmark trajectory datasets, demonstrating that our method outperforms state-of-the-art models in terms of prediction accuracy and robustness, particularly in highly interactive scenarios. The results show substantial improvements in trajectory consistency and adaptability to varying scene contexts. This research highlights the effectiveness of incorporating contextual information within transformer architectures, offering substantial advancements for real-world applications in autonomous navigation, crowd safety, and urban planning. Keywords: pedestrian trajectory prediction, transformer networks, context augmentation, autonomous vehicles, trajectory forecasting, intelligent surveillance.
