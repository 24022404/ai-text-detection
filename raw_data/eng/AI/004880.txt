This paper presents a comprehensive survey on the intersection of object detection and pose estimation using monocular vision, a rapidly growing area of research with significant implications for advancements in computer vision and robotics. The objective of this survey is to consolidate existing methodologies and algorithms that simultaneously address object detection and pose estimation from a single camera perspective. We explore various approaches, including deep learning models, hybrid frameworks, and innovative use of neural networks, assessing their effectiveness in detecting objects while accurately predicting their 3D orientations and positions. Key findings highlight the advantages of integrated systems over traditional, isolated approaches, demonstrating improvements in computational efficiency and accuracy. We also draw comparisons across a range of datasets and application scenarios, providing insights into their performance and potential limitations. The review emphasizes the shift towards real-time processing capabilities and the increasing role of unsupervised and semi-supervised learning techniques in handling diverse and unstructured environments. In conclusion, our survey identifies current challenges and outlines potential future directions, including the need for robust models capable of operating under occlusions and varying lighting conditions. This survey serves as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners looking to enhance systems reliant on monocular vision for tasks such as autonomous driving, augmented reality, and intelligent surveillance. Key keywords: object detection, pose estimation, monocular vision, deep learning, computer vision, neural networks.
