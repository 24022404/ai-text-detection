This study aims to investigate the differences between Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Independent Component Analysis (ICA) from a data distribution perspective. PCA and ICA are widely used dimensionality reduction techniques in data analysis, but they have distinct approaches to extracting meaningful features. Our approach involves applying both PCA and ICA to various datasets and comparing the resulting component distributions. The results show that ICA is more effective in capturing non-Gaussian and independent components, whereas PCA is better suited for Gaussian data distributions. Our findings indicate that the choice of technique depends on the underlying data structure, and selecting the appropriate method can significantly impact the accuracy of subsequent analyses. This research contributes to the understanding of PCA and ICA by highlighting their strengths and limitations, providing valuable insights for data analysts and researchers. Key keywords: PCA, ICA, dimensionality reduction, data distribution, feature extraction, data analysis.
