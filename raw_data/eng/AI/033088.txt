This paper addresses the critical issue of learning fair and transferable representations in machine learning, a crucial aspect of artificial intelligence (AI) and deep learning. The objective is to develop a novel approach that mitigates bias in representations while maintaining their transferability across different domains and tasks. To achieve this, we propose a new adversarial learning framework that leverages domain-adversarial training to learn domain-invariant representations. Our method incorporates a fairness regularizer to ensure that the learned representations are fair and unbiased. The results show that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of fairness and transferability, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and fairness metrics on benchmark datasets. The key findings highlight the importance of adversarial training in learning fair and transferable representations, with implications for a wide range of applications, including computer vision, natural language processing, and recommender systems. Our research contributes to the development of more equitable and generalizable AI models, with potential applications in areas such as fairness-aware AI, transfer learning, and domain adaptation, and is relevant to keywords such as adversarial learning, fair representations, transfer learning, and domain adaptation.
