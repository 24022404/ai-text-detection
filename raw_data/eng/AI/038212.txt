Accurate traffic speed prediction across large-scale road networks is vital for intelligent transportation systems and effective urban planning. This paper investigates the efficacy of deep recurrent neural network architectures—specifically, bidirectional and unidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks—for network-wide traffic speed prediction. We develop and evaluate deep LSTM models that capture both forward and backward temporal dependencies in traffic data, leveraging historical speed records from extensive sensor networks. Our approach incorporates spatial-temporal feature fusion to address road network topology and dynamic traffic patterns. Empirical results on real-world datasets demonstrate that the bidirectional LSTM outperforms the unidirectional variant in forecasting accuracy, particularly during peak traffic fluctuations and incident events. Comparative evaluation against traditional statistical methods and baseline neural networks further highlights the proposed models’ superior performance in terms of mean absolute error and root mean square error. The findings substantiate the advantages of incorporating bidirectional temporal context and deep learning in large-scale traffic prediction tasks. This research offers novel insights into scalable deep learning frameworks for urban traffic management and provides a foundation for enhanced traffic control, navigation, and congestion mitigation systems.

Keywords: LSTM, bidirectional LSTM, unidirectional LSTM, traffic speed prediction, deep learning, recurrent neural network, time series forecasting, intelligent transportation systems.
