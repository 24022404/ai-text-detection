This paper addresses the challenge of aligning natural language with noisy, real-world video data, a critical task for applications such as video understanding, retrieval, and human-computer interaction. Traditional methods often rely on large-scale annotated datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to obtain. To overcome this limitation, we propose a self-supervised learning framework that leverages unlabeled video-text pairs to learn meaningful representations. Our approach employs a novel contrastive learning mechanism that maps video frames and corresponding textual descriptions into a shared embedding space, while robustly handling noise and variability inherent in real-world video data. We introduce a multi-modal transformer architecture that dynamically captures temporal and contextual relationships between visual and textual modalities. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in video-text alignment tasks, outperforming existing supervised and unsupervised approaches. Notably, the framework shows strong generalization capabilities across diverse domains, including sports, cooking, and daily activities. The results highlight the potential of self-supervised learning for scalable and efficient video-language modeling, paving the way for applications in automated video captioning, content-based search, and interactive AI systems.  

**Keywords**: self-supervised learning, video-text alignment, contrastive learning, multi-modal transformers, real-world video understanding.
