This paper provides a comprehensive review of learning curves, a fundamental concept in machine learning and artificial intelligence that characterizes the relationship between model performance and the amount of training data or computational resources. The objective is to analyze the theoretical foundations, empirical observations, and practical implications of learning curves across various domains. We explore different methodologies for modeling and interpreting learning curves, including parametric and non-parametric approaches, as well as their applications in hyperparameter tuning, dataset size estimation, and model selection. Key findings highlight the diversity of learning curve shapes, which are influenced by factors such as model complexity, data quality, and task difficulty. We also discuss the implications of learning curves for resource allocation, scalability, and the design of efficient training pipelines. This review emphasizes the importance of understanding learning curves to optimize machine learning workflows and improve generalization performance. The insights presented contribute to advancing research in model evaluation and deployment, particularly in resource-constrained environments. Keywords: learning curves, machine learning, model performance, scalability, hyperparameter tuning, generalization.
