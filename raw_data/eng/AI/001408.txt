Multivariate time series forecasting is a fundamental challenge in information technology, vital for applications such as financial analysis, energy demand prediction, and healthcare monitoring. Traditional models often struggle to capture complex temporal patterns and dependencies present in multivariate data. This paper introduces R2N2, a novel Residual Recurrent Neural Network framework designed to enhance the accuracy and robustness of multivariate time series forecasting. The proposed approach integrates recurrent neural networks (RNNs), specifically long short-term memory (LSTM) units, with residual learning principles to effectively model both short-term fluctuations and long-term trends. By combining the strengths of RNNs in handling sequential data with residual connections that enable efficient training of deeper architectures, R2N2 addresses issues of vanishing gradients and overfitting commonly associated with deep sequence models. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that R2N2 consistently outperforms conventional methods, such as autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) models, standalone RNNs, and recent deep learning baselines, in terms of forecasting accuracy and computational efficiency. The findings highlight the model’s ability to capture intricate inter-variable relationships and dynamic patterns across diverse domains. R2N2’s architecture presents a scalable and adaptable solution, offering practical implications for real-world time series analysis, anomaly detection, and predictive maintenance. Keywords: multivariate time series, forecasting, recurrent neural networks, residual learning, deep learning, LSTM.
