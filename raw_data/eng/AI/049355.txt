Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for leveraging unlabeled data to learn meaningful representations, particularly in computer vision tasks. However, existing SSL methods often overlook the importance of local feature interactions, which are critical for capturing fine-grained details in images. This paper introduces a novel self-supervised learning framework that incorporates **local attention-aware features** to enhance representation learning. Our approach employs a **hierarchical attention mechanism** to dynamically focus on salient local regions while maintaining global context, enabling the model to learn more discriminative features. We evaluate the proposed method on multiple benchmark datasets for image classification and object detection tasks, demonstrating significant improvements over state-of-the-art SSL techniques. Experimental results show that our model achieves superior performance in downstream tasks, with a **10-15% increase in accuracy** compared to baseline methods. The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to integrate local attention into SSL, offering a more robust and interpretable framework for unsupervised representation learning. This research has broad implications for applications in medical imaging, autonomous systems, and other domains where fine-grained feature understanding is crucial.  

**Keywords**: Self-supervised learning, local attention, feature representation, hierarchical attention, computer vision, unsupervised learning.
