Objective: This paper develops novel exponential concentration inequalities for time-inhomogeneous (nonstationary) Markov chains, addressing a gap in probabilistic tail bounds when transition dynamics evolve over time. Such results are crucial for performance guarantees in sequential Monte Carlo methods, online learning, and stochastic optimization where stationarity cannot be assumed.

Methods/Approach: Building on coupling arguments and time-varying mixing coefficients, we introduce a framework that tracks the chain’s contraction behavior across successive transitions. We define a sequence of ergodicity parameters that measure instantaneous dependence and employ martingale-difference decompositions to handle the nonstationarity. Our approach yields explicit Bennett- and Bernstein-type inequalities under minimal regularity conditions, without requiring uniform ergodicity or reversibility.

Results/Findings: We prove that for any bounded function of the chain’s path, the deviation probability decays exponentially with rates determined by aggregated mixing parameters. In particular, we obtain  
  – A Hoeffding-style bound: P(|Sn−E[Sn]|≥t) ≤ 2 exp(−t²/(2V + ct)),  
  – A Bernstein-style bound with sharper constants when variance proxies are small.  
Numerical illustrations demonstrate substantially tighter tail estimates compared to applying stationary-chain bounds naively.

Conclusion/Implications: Our inequalities extend the toolkit for analyzing nonstationary stochastic processes and provide rigorous error controls in algorithms with evolving dynamics. Potential applications include adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo, reinforcement learning in changing environments, and performance analysis of randomized network protocols.

Keywords: nonstationary Markov chains; exponential inequalities; concentration bounds; mixing coefficients; tail estimates.
