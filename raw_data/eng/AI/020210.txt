Domain generalization (DG) is a critical challenge in machine learning, where models trained on one set of domains must perform well on unseen, potentially diverse domains. This paper introduces a novel framework, *Probably Approximately Correct Domain Generalization* (PAC-DG), which formalizes the problem of domain generalization under the lens of computational learning theory. The objective of this work is to provide theoretical guarantees for generalization across unseen domains, addressing the limitations of existing empirical approaches that lack robustness guarantees. We propose a probabilistic framework that leverages distributional robustness and meta-learning principles to ensure models generalize with high probability across a wide range of domains. Our approach combines domain-invariant feature learning with a PAC-based optimization strategy, enabling the model to adapt to unexpected domain shifts. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that PAC-DG outperforms state-of-the-art domain generalization methods, achieving significant improvements in cross-domain accuracy and robustness. The key contribution of this work lies in its theoretical foundation, which bridges the gap between empirical domain generalization techniques and rigorous learning guarantees. This research has broad implications for applications requiring reliable performance in dynamic environments, such as autonomous systems, healthcare, and natural language processing.  

**Keywords**: Domain generalization, PAC learning, distributional robustness, meta-learning, domain adaptation, machine learning.
