Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have become a cornerstone in computer vision tasks, yet their robustness to input variations remains a critical area of study. This paper investigates a previously underexplored phenomenon: the development of blind spots in CNNs due to padding mechanisms during convolution operations. We demonstrate that standard padding techniques, such as zero-padding, can inadvertently create regions in the input space where the network fails to detect or respond to features, leading to degraded performance in tasks like object detection and segmentation. To address this, we propose a novel padding-aware training framework that explicitly accounts for these blind spots by incorporating spatial awareness into the learning process. Our experiments on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, reveal that the proposed approach significantly improves model robustness and accuracy, particularly in edge cases where traditional padding methods falter. The findings highlight the importance of rethinking padding strategies in CNN architectures and offer a practical solution to mitigate blind spots, advancing the reliability of CNNs in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Convolutional Neural Networks, padding, blind spots, robustness, computer vision, spatial awareness.
