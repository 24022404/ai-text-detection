Automated species recognition from camera trap images is a promising tool for ecological monitoring, yet poses significant challenges due to the variability of natural habitats and the diversity of animal appearances. This study presents a novel approach for classifying mammal species in a European temperate forest using artificial intelligence (AI), with a focus on deep learning-based image analysis. The objective is to streamline the labor-intensive process of manual image review and enable large-scale biodiversity assessments. We constructed a dataset comprising thousands of camera trap images collected over a full year and developed a convolutional neural network (CNN) model trained to identify key mammal species native to the region. The model’s performance was evaluated using precision, recall, and overall accuracy metrics, and compared against baseline manual classification results. Our findings demonstrate that the AI-driven approach achieves high recognition accuracy, effectively differentiating between similar species and coping with common issues such as partial visibility, varied lighting, and background clutter. The results highlight the potential of automated species recognition to accelerate ecological studies and support wildlife conservation efforts. This work lays the foundation for future development of fully automated monitoring systems that can be adapted to other forest ecosystems and taxonomic groups. Key keywords: species recognition, camera trap, artificial intelligence, deep learning, convolutional neural network, wildlife monitoring, European temperate forest.
