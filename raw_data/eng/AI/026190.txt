This paper addresses the challenge of sample-efficient feature selection in Factored Markov Decision Processes (FMDPs), a critical problem in reinforcement learning and decision-making under uncertainty. The objective is to identify the most relevant features from high-dimensional state spaces while minimizing the number of samples required, thereby improving computational efficiency and scalability. We propose a novel algorithm that leverages structured sparsity and Bayesian optimization techniques to systematically reduce the feature space without compromising the accuracy of the learned policy. Our approach integrates a dynamic weighting mechanism to prioritize features based on their contribution to the decision-making process, enabling efficient exploration of the state-action space. Experimental results on benchmark FMDP environments demonstrate that our method significantly reduces sample complexity compared to existing techniques, achieving comparable or superior performance with fewer training iterations. The proposed algorithm also exhibits robustness to noise and scalability to larger state spaces, making it suitable for real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous systems, and resource management. This work contributes to the advancement of sample-efficient reinforcement learning by providing a practical and theoretically grounded solution for feature selection in FMDPs, with implications for improving the interpretability and efficiency of decision-making systems.  

**Keywords**: Factored MDPs, feature selection, sample efficiency, reinforcement learning, Bayesian optimization, structured sparsity.
