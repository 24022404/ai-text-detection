This paper presents the design and evaluation of a one-dimensional (1D) convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture tailored for classification tasks involving leaf datasets and general time series data. The objective of this research is to address the challenge of accurate and efficient pattern recognition in non-image sequential data, which is prevalent in domains such as plant species identification and temporal data analysis. The proposed 1D CNN model leverages convolutional layers to automatically extract hierarchical features from raw input sequences, eliminating the need for extensive manual feature engineering. To evaluate the effectiveness of the approach, experiments were conducted on publicly available leaf classification benchmarks as well as various standard time series classification datasets. Results indicate that the 1D CNN achieves competitive or superior accuracy compared to traditional machine learning methods and other deep learning models, demonstrating robust performance across diverse sequences. Furthermore, the model maintains a lightweight footprint, facilitating faster training and inference times. This study highlights the versatility and applicability of 1D convolutional architectures for sequential data analysis and suggests possible extension to other domains where structured temporal or ordered inputs are prevalent. Key contributions include the introduction of a unified neural network framework for both leaf and time series classification, comprehensive experimental validation, and insights into feature extraction capabilities of 1D CNNs. Keywords: 1D convolutional neural network, time series classification, leaf classification, deep learning, sequential data, pattern recognition.
