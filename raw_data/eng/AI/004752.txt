Conservation decisions, such as resource allocation and intervention strategies, are often complex and require balancing ecological, economic, and social factors. This paper explores the application of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) to optimize these decisions. We develop a novel DRL framework that models conservation management as a Markov Decision Process, learning policies directly from simulated environmental dynamics and management actions. The framework incorporates diverse data sources, including ecological models, economic forecasts, and spatial information, to inform the reward function and state representation. Results demonstrate that the DRL agent outperforms traditional rule-based and optimization methods in achieving long-term conservation goals, specifically biodiversity preservation and ecosystem service provision, under various environmental change scenarios. The DRL approach exhibits adaptability to unforeseen disturbances and provides insights into effective conservation strategies. This research contributes a flexible and data-driven approach to conservation decision-making, potentially enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of environmental management. Keywords: Deep Reinforcement Learning, Conservation, Environmental Management, Markov Decision Process, Biodiversity, Ecosystem Services.
