Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have emerged as a powerful framework for generative modeling across various domains. This paper presents a comparative study of several prominent GAN architectures, focusing on their performance in image generation tasks. We evaluate and compare the original GAN formulation with improved variations, including Deep Convolutional GANs (DCGANs), Conditional GANs (cGANs), and Wasserstein GANs (WGANs), assessing their stability, training efficiency, and the quality of generated images. The evaluation employs quantitative metrics such as Inception Score (IS) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), alongside qualitative visual assessments. Our findings reveal significant differences in the convergence behavior and generative capabilities of these models. Specifically, WGANs demonstrate enhanced training stability compared to traditional GANs, while cGANs offer superior control over the generated output. The study provides insights into the strengths and weaknesses of each GAN architecture, offering guidance for selecting the most appropriate model for specific generative tasks. This comparative analysis contributes to a deeper understanding of GANs and facilitates their more effective application in diverse fields.

**Keywords:** Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, DCGAN, cGAN, WGAN, Image Generation, Deep Learning, Comparative Analysis.
