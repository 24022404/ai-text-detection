Adversarial attacks have emerged as a significant threat to the reliability and security of machine learning models in time-series applications, including finance, healthcare, and energy systems. This paper presents a comprehensive benchmarking study of adversarial attack and defense techniques specifically designed for time-series data. We systematically evaluate the effectiveness of state-of-the-art adversarial attack methods—including Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM), Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), and time-series-specific perturbations—across multiple time-series classification tasks and datasets. Additionally, we implement and benchmark a range of defense mechanisms, such as adversarial training, input preprocessing, and robust model architectures, to assess their capabilities in mitigating attack impacts. Our experiments reveal distinct vulnerabilities unique to time-series data, such as sensitivity to temporal distortions and sequential dependencies, and highlight the varying strengths and weaknesses of existing defenses. Based on extensive empirical results, we provide actionable recommendations for selecting and combining attack and defense methods in practical time-series scenarios. This work contributes a standardized evaluation framework, curated datasets, and performance metrics to the research community, advancing the development of robust machine learning systems for time-series analysis. Key keywords: adversarial attacks, time-series data, defenses, robustness, benchmarking, machine learning security.
