This paper proposes DeepV2D, a novel deep learning architecture that estimates depth from video sequences through an end-to-end differentiable structure from motion (SfM) framework. The objective of DeepV2D is to bridge the gap between classical SfM techniques and modern deep neural networks, enabling robust, accurate, and efficient depth reconstruction from monocular or multi-view video inputs. The method integrates traditional geometric constraints into a learned architecture by jointly optimizing camera motion and scene depth using convolutional neural networks and differentiable pose estimation modules. Extensive experiments are conducted on standard benchmarks, demonstrating that DeepV2D outperforms existing state-of-the-art approaches in both accuracy and generalizability across diverse and challenging scenes. Key findings indicate that the differentiable formulation of both depth refinement and pose estimation results in improved convergence and robustness to noisy inputs. The main contributions include a unified framework for video-based depth prediction, the integration of geometric reasoning with deep learning, and significant improvements over previous monocular and multi-view depth estimation methods. DeepV2D has potential applications in robotics, augmented reality, autonomous driving, and 3D scene reconstruction, where accurate scene understanding from video is critical.

Keywords: depth estimation, video segmentation, structure from motion, deep learning, 3D reconstruction, differentiable optimization.
