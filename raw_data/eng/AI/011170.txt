Abstract:  
Universal Machine Translation (UMT) seeks to enable accurate translation across diverse language pairs, including low-resource and unseen languages. A significant challenge in UMT is learning representations that are robust and invariant to the structural and lexical differences among languages. This paper investigates novel methods for learning language-invariant representations within neural translation models. We propose a multi-task framework combining cross-lingual masked language modeling, adversarial training, and contrastive alignment to explicitly encourage encoder representations that are agnostic to input language. Experimental results on benchmark multilingual translation datasets demonstrate substantial improvements over conventional systems, particularly for zero-shot and low-resource language pairs. Detailed analysis reveals that the proposed approach reduces language-specific biases and fosters better transfer learning across typologically diverse languages. Our findings indicate that learning language-invariant representations is key to improving the generalizability and scalability of UMT systems. This work offers insights for advancing multilingual Natural Language Processing (NLP) and highlights the potential for more inclusive, accessible global communication technologies.  

Keywords: Universal Machine Translation, language-invariant representations, multilingual neural networks, zero-shot translation, cross-lingual learning, representation learning, NLP.
