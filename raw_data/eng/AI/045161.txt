This paper introduces GATSBI, a novel generative framework for modeling agent-centric spatio-temporal object interactions. The objective is to develop a system that can learn and predict complex interactions between agents and objects in dynamic environments. GATSBI employs a multi-modal approach, combining graph neural networks and transformer architectures to capture spatial and temporal relationships. The method is evaluated on various benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements in interaction prediction and scene understanding compared to existing methods. The results show that GATSBI can effectively generate realistic and diverse interaction scenarios, outperforming state-of-the-art models in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to model complex spatio-temporal interactions, enabling applications in areas such as autonomous systems, human-robot interaction, and smart environments. Key aspects of GATSBI include its agent-centric perspective, spatio-temporal reasoning, and generative capabilities, making it a unique and valuable tool for understanding and predicting dynamic interactions. Relevant keywords: generative models, spatio-temporal reasoning, graph neural networks, transformer architectures, agent-centric interaction, human-robot interaction.
