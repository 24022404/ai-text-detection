The accurate prediction of extubation readiness in extremely preterm infants is critical for improving neonatal care outcomes. This study addresses the challenge of analyzing cardiorespiratory behavior data, which is often imbalanced and complex, by proposing a novel approach combining undersampling and bagging techniques with decision trees. The objective is to enhance the predictive performance of machine learning models in identifying infants ready for extubation. A dataset comprising cardiorespiratory signals from preterm infants was preprocessed and analyzed using a decision tree-based model. To address class imbalance, an undersampling strategy was applied, followed by bagging to improve model robustness and generalization. The proposed method was evaluated using metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, and compared against traditional machine learning approaches. Results demonstrate that the undersampling and bagging framework significantly improves prediction accuracy and reduces false positives, outperforming conventional methods. The findings highlight the potential of this approach to support clinical decision-making by providing reliable predictions of extubation readiness. This research contributes to the field of neonatal care by introducing a scalable and interpretable machine learning solution, with implications for improving patient outcomes and reducing healthcare costs.  

**Keywords**: extubation readiness, preterm infants, cardiorespiratory behavior, decision trees, undersampling, bagging, machine learning, neonatal care.
