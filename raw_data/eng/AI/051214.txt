This paper delves into the multiview differential geometry of curves to address the lack of comprehensive frameworks for understanding and analyzing the geometry of curves across multiple views. This research aims to bridge the gap between traditional single-view geometric analysis and the complexities introduced by multiview systems.

Methods/Approach: Our approach involves extending classical differential geometry techniques to a multiview context, enabling the investigation of curves observed from various perspectives. We develop mathematical models that integrate concepts from projective geometry and differential calculus and formulate algorithms that efficiently compute geometric invariants of curves across multiple views.

Results/Findings: The study demonstrates that the proposed multiview differential geometry framework can accurately capture and describe the geometric properties of curves when viewed from different angles. Our experimental results show improved accuracy in curve reconstruction and recognition tasks when compared to traditional single-view methods. Additionally, the computational efficiency of our algorithms allows for scalability in processing complex scenes.

Conclusion/Implications: The insights generated from this research provide a significant extension to the field of differential geometry, offering a novel perspective on curve analysis that is highly applicable to computer vision and graphics. This multiview framework enhances our ability to interpret and reconstruct three-dimensional properties from two-dimensional images effectively. Potential applications include advancements in areas such as 3D modeling, automated surveillance, and robotics. Key keywords include multiview geometry, differential geometry, curve analysis, 3D reconstruction, and computer vision.
