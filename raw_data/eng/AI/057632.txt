This paper introduces a novel approach to unsupervised representation learning that efficiently preserves similarity relationships in high-dimensional data using modular sparse distributed codes (SDCs) combined with a new novelty-contingent noise mechanism. The proposed method addresses the challenge of achieving efficient, scalable, and robust unsupervised learning without labeled data, while maintaining similarity structure critical for downstream tasks such as clustering, retrieval, and anomaly detection. Our framework leverages a modular architecture to generate sparse, distributed codes, facilitating efficient storage and fast retrieval while reducing interference between representations. Central to our approach is the integration of novelty-contingent noise, where controlled stochastic perturbations are introduced selectively in the presence of novel or unrecognized inputs, enhancing the system’s capacity to differentiate and encode new patterns while mitigating catastrophic forgetting. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing unsupervised learning models in preserving both local and global similarity, while also achieving superior compactness and computational efficiency. Furthermore, ablation studies confirm the substantial contribution of both modularity and the novelty-contingent noise component to the observed performance gains. These findings highlight the potential of our framework for scalable, biologically inspired learning architectures and open new avenues for efficient unsupervised learning in real-world applications. Keywords: unsupervised learning, similarity preservation, sparse distributed codes, modular networks, novelty detection, noise-driven learning, representation learning, high-dimensional data.
