In reinforcement learning (RL), the balance between exploration and exploitation is critical for achieving optimal performance. This paper introduces a novel approach, the Masked Model-based Actor-Critic (MMAC), which leverages model confidence to guide decision-making. The objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency and stability of RL algorithms by dynamically trusting the model's predictions only when it exhibits high confidence. The proposed method integrates a masked mechanism into a model-based actor-critic framework, enabling the agent to selectively rely on the learned dynamics model during policy optimization. Experimental results demonstrate that MMAC outperforms traditional model-free and model-based RL algorithms across a range of benchmark environments, achieving higher sample efficiency and more robust performance. The key innovation lies in the confidence-based masking strategy, which reduces the impact of inaccurate model predictions and improves exploration in uncertain states. This work contributes to advancing model-based RL by providing a principled approach to balancing model trust and exploration, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and other domains requiring efficient and reliable decision-making.  

**Keywords**: reinforcement learning, model-based RL, actor-critic, confidence masking, exploration-exploitation, sample efficiency.
