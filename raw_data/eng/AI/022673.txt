This paper investigates the evaluation metrics of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), with the intention of identifying reliable and robust methods for assessing their performance. Despite GANs' success in generating realistic data across various domains, consistent evaluation remains a challenge due to the subjective nature of human perception and the diversity of applications. 

Methods/Approach: The study conducts an empirical analysis of a range of existing evaluation metrics, including Inception Score (IS), Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), and others. We employ a variety of dataset types and GAN architectures to comprehensively analyze their applicability and efficacy. Advanced statistical and computational techniques are leveraged to benchmark these metrics against human judgment and real-world applicability.

Results/Findings: Our findings reveal significant discrepancies among existing metrics in terms of sensitivity, bias, and correlation with human perceptual judgments. The study highlights the limitations of current metrics in capturing nuanced improvements in GAN-generated data quality and proposes a novel composite metric that integrates several evaluated aspects for a more holistic assessment.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field by providing deeper insights into the strengths and weaknesses of prominent evaluation metrics used in GAN research. Our proposed composite metric offers a more robust means of evaluation, facilitating better model development and comparison. These insights have the potential to enhance the deployment and refinement of GANs across various applications, from image synthesis to data augmentation. Keywords: GANs, evaluation metrics, Inception Score, Fréchet Inception Distance, empirical analysis, composite metric.
