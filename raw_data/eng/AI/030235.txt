Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have demonstrated remarkable success in various domains, yet they remain notoriously challenging to stabilize during training due to issues such as mode collapse and gradient explosion. This paper investigates the underlying mechanisms of spectral normalization, a key regularization technique widely used to enhance GAN stability. We provide a comprehensive theoretical analysis elucidating how spectral normalization constrains the Lipschitz constant of the discriminator, promoting more balanced gradients and robust convergence. Building upon these insights, we propose several novel improvements to the spectral normalization process, including dynamic adjustment strategies and enhanced normalization metrics tailored for deep network architectures. Through extensive experiments on standard image generation benchmarks, our enhanced spectral normalization methods consistently yield superior training stability, improved sample quality, and more reliable convergence compared to baseline approaches. Our findings not only deepen the understanding of why spectral normalization plays a pivotal role in stabilizing GANs, but also introduce practical techniques that advance the field of adversarial learning. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, spectral normalization, stability, Lipschitz continuity, image generation, deep learning, regularization.
