The objective of this research is to address the challenge of open-world node classification in dynamic and evolving networks through lifelong learning of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). Traditional GNN approaches typically assume that the network is static and fully observable, which limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where new classes and nodes continually appear over time. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel framework that enables GNNs to adapt to new data and evolving structures without retraining from scratch. Our approach employs a combination of transfer learning techniques and continual adaptation mechanisms to retain previously learned knowledge while efficiently assimilating new information. Through extensive experiments on dynamic datasets, our method demonstrates superior performance in both predictive accuracy and adaptability compared to existing GNN models. The findings highlight our model's capability to effectively handle non-stationary node distributions and identify novel classes as they emerge in the graph. This research contributes to the field by providing a robust solution for lifelong learning in GNNs, with potential applications in areas such as social network analysis, fraud detection, and recommendation systems. Key innovations include the seamless integration of lifelong learning principles into the GNN architecture and significant advancements in the adaptability of models to open-world scenarios.
