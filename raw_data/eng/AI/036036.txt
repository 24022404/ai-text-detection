This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the use of autoencoders in representation learning, aiming to explore their fundamental principles, evaluate their efficacy in diverse tasks, enhance understanding through explainability, and identify existing challenges related to their application. Autoencoders, a type of artificial neural network, are instrumental in transforming input data into efficient, reduced-dimensional representations while retaining essential information. Our approach begins with an in-depth exploration of autoencoder architectures and their foundational role in representation learning. Subsequently, we conduct case studies across various learning tasks to assess the performance and adaptability of autoencoders, providing insights into their application in areas such as image processing, natural language understanding, and anomaly detection. Emphasizing interpretability, this study employs explainable AI techniques to address the often opaque nature of autoencoder-generated features, thereby aiding in comprehension and trust in their outputs. The findings demonstrate that autoencoders can effectively capture latent features, contribute to improved downstream task performance, and offer intuitive insights when augmented with explainability methods. However, challenges remain, including optimization difficulties, potential overfitting in specific scenarios, and their computation-intensive nature. These issues are critically assessed, proposing potential directions for future research. This paper contributes to the ongoing discourse on representation learning by offering a holistic overview of autoencoder applications, emphasizing their potential while being transparent about their limitations. Keywords: autoencoders, representation learning, explainability, neural networks, machine learning challenges.
