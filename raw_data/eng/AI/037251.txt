Approximate inference in probabilistic graphical models often suffers from inaccuracies due to the limitations of tractable computation. This paper introduces Bucket Renormalization (BR), a novel approach for improving the accuracy of approximate inference algorithms, particularly in loopy belief propagation. BR iteratively refines the messages passed between variables by incorporating local evidence and renormalizing the resulting distributions within each bucket. This process encourages consistency and mitigates the effects of double-counting, a common source of error in loopy graphs. We demonstrate the effectiveness of BR on several benchmark problems, including image denoising and network anomaly detection. Empirical results show that BR significantly improves the accuracy of approximate marginals compared to standard belief propagation and other state-of-the-art methods, while maintaining computational efficiency. The proposed method offers a practical and scalable solution for enhancing the reliability of approximate inference in complex probabilistic models. Keywords: Approximate Inference, Belief Propagation, Graphical Models, Renormalization, Loopy Belief Propagation.
