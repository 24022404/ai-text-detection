This paper introduces **ASM2TV**, an **Adaptive Semi-Supervised Multi-Task Multi-View Learning Framework**, designed to address the challenges of learning from complex, multi-view datasets with limited labeled data. The objective of this research is to enhance the performance of multi-task learning by leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data across multiple views, while dynamically adapting to the inherent relationships between tasks and views. The proposed framework integrates a novel adaptive weighting mechanism that balances the contributions of different tasks and views, ensuring robust and efficient learning. Additionally, a semi-supervised approach is employed to maximize the utilization of available data, reducing dependency on extensive labeled datasets.  

The framework is evaluated on benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in accuracy and generalization compared to existing multi-task and multi-view learning methods. Experimental results highlight the effectiveness of ASM2TV in scenarios with sparse labeled data, showcasing its ability to adaptively learn task-specific and view-specific representations. Key findings reveal that the adaptive weighting mechanism plays a critical role in optimizing performance, particularly in heterogeneous data environments.  

The contributions of this work include a scalable and flexible framework that advances the state-of-the-art in multi-task and multi-view learning, with potential applications in areas such as computer vision, natural language processing, and bioinformatics. By addressing the limitations of traditional methods, ASM2TV provides a foundation for future research in semi-supervised learning and adaptive model design.  

**Keywords**: Multi-task learning, multi-view learning, semi-supervised learning, adaptive weighting, machine learning, representation learning.
