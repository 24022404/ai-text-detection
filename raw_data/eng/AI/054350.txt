Ensemble methods, such as decision stumps and trees, are widely used in machine learning for their interpretability and robustness. However, their performance under adversarial perturbations, particularly in the context of \(\ell_p\)-norm bounded attacks, remains underexplored. This paper investigates the \(\ell_p\)-norm robustness of ensemble stumps and trees, aiming to provide theoretical and empirical insights into their resilience against adversarial examples. We propose a novel framework to analyze the robustness of these models under \(\ell_p\)-norm constraints, leveraging optimization techniques to derive bounds on their adversarial vulnerability. Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, we demonstrate that ensemble stumps and trees exhibit varying degrees of robustness across different \(\ell_p\)-norms, with notable trade-offs between accuracy and robustness. Our findings reveal that ensemble methods can achieve competitive robustness compared to more complex models, particularly in low-dimensional settings. Furthermore, we introduce a regularization-based approach to enhance the \(\ell_p\)-norm robustness of ensemble trees without sacrificing interpretability. The results highlight the potential of ensemble methods as robust and interpretable alternatives in adversarial machine learning, offering practical implications for real-world applications where robustness and transparency are critical.  

**Keywords**: Ensemble methods, decision trees, adversarial robustness, \(\ell_p\)-norm, machine learning, interpretability.
