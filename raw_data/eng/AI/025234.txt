The primary objective of this research is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of breast cancer image classification by developing an innovative attention model enhanced network. Breast cancer remains one of the leading causes of mortality among women worldwide, and early detection through imaging plays a crucial role in reducing its impact.

Methods/Approach: This study introduces a novel deep learning architecture that integrates attention mechanisms within a convolutional neural network (CNN) to enhance the discriminative power of the model. By focusing on relevant areas of mammographic images, the attention model dynamically allocates computational resources to important features, ultimately improving classification performance. The proposed architecture was evaluated using a comprehensive dataset of breast cancer images, and its performance was compared with several state-of-the-art CNN approaches.

Results/Findings: The attention model enhanced network demonstrated superior performance in breast cancer image classification, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy, precision, and recall. The results showed a significant improvement in the detection of malignant cases, highlighting the model's efficacy in distinguishing between benign and malignant tissues. The inclusion of attention mechanisms effectively improved feature extraction, resulting in more focused and relevant areas being analyzed.

Conclusion/Implications: This research presents a significant advancement in the field of medical image classification by leveraging attention mechanisms to bolster the performance of CNNs. The proposed approach offers substantial potential for clinical applications, providing a more accurate and reliable tool for early breast cancer detection. Future work may explore the integration of additional data modalities and further refinement of the attention model architecture to extend its applicability and accuracy in other domains.

Keywords: breast cancer classification, attention model, convolutional neural network, medical imaging, deep learning.
