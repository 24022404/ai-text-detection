Object detection remains a critical challenge in computer vision, with significant advancements driven by deep learning techniques. This paper investigates the impact of increasing dropout rates in the Pool5 feature maps of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on object detection performance. While dropout is traditionally applied in fully connected layers to prevent overfitting, its application in intermediate convolutional layers, particularly Pool5, has been underexplored. We propose a novel approach that systematically increases dropout rates in Pool5 to evaluate its effect on model generalization and detection accuracy. Using the Faster R-CNN framework, we conduct extensive experiments on benchmark datasets such as PASCAL VOC and COCO. Our results demonstrate that higher dropout rates in Pool5 lead to improved robustness and generalization, particularly in scenarios with limited training data. The proposed method achieves a notable reduction in overfitting and enhances detection performance, outperforming baseline models without dropout modifications. These findings suggest that strategic dropout application in convolutional layers can significantly enhance object detection systems, offering a simple yet effective technique for improving model performance. This work contributes to the broader understanding of regularization techniques in deep learning and provides practical insights for designing more robust object detection architectures.  

**Keywords**: object detection, dropout, Pool5, convolutional neural networks, Faster R-CNN, regularization, deep learning.
