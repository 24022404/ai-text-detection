This paper introduces Wavelet Networks (WaveNets), a novel deep learning architecture designed for scale-equivariant representation learning directly from raw waveform data. WaveNets leverage learnable wavelet bases within a convolutional neural network framework, enabling the network to decompose input signals into multiple scales and learn features that are inherently equivariant to scale transformations. This approach contrasts with traditional methods that rely on fixed signal processing techniques or data augmentation to achieve scale invariance. We demonstrate that WaveNets achieve state-of-the-art performance on several audio and seismic signal classification tasks, exhibiting superior generalization capabilities compared to conventional convolutional networks and recurrent neural networks. Furthermore, we analyze the learned wavelet bases, revealing interpretable patterns related to the underlying signal characteristics. The findings suggest that WaveNets provide a powerful and efficient method for analyzing raw waveform data, offering significant advantages in domains where scale variations are prevalent. Keywords: Wavelet Networks, Scale Equivariance, Waveforms, Deep Learning, Signal Processing, Convolutional Neural Networks.
