Semi-supervised metric learning (SSML) aims to learn a distance function from both labeled and unlabeled data, addressing limitations of purely supervised approaches. This paper introduces a novel deep learning framework, termed "Deep Resurrection," for SSML. The approach leverages a carefully designed loss function that combines contrastive loss on labeled pairs with a novel self-supervision loss derived from the intrinsic data structure revealed by unlabeled data. Specifically, we resurrect information from the latent space representations learned by an autoencoder trained on the unlabeled data, using this information to guide metric learning. This avoids common pitfalls of noisy pseudo-labels. We evaluate Deep Resurrection on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to state-of-the-art SSML methods. The results show significant improvements in classification accuracy and nearest neighbor retrieval precision, particularly when labeled data is scarce. The proposed framework offers a robust and effective approach for leveraging unlabeled data to enhance metric learning in various applications. Keywords: Semi-Supervised Learning, Metric Learning, Deep Learning, Contrastive Loss, Autoencoders, Self-Supervision.
