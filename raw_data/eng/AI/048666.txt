This paper proposes TDAN, a Temporally Deformable Alignment Network designed to enhance video super-resolution capabilities. The objective is to address the challenge of aligning and fusing features from multiple frames in a video sequence to produce high-quality, super-resolved frames. TDAN employs a novel approach by incorporating temporally deformable convolutional layers, which enable the network to adaptively align features across different frames. The method leverages a combination of spatial and temporal attention mechanisms to selectively focus on relevant features and suppress unnecessary information. Experimental results demonstrate that TDAN outperforms state-of-the-art video super-resolution methods, achieving significant improvements in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM). The proposed network offers a robust and efficient solution for video super-resolution, with potential applications in various fields, including surveillance, entertainment, and virtual reality. Key contributions of this work include the introduction of temporally deformable convolutions and a novel attention-based feature fusion strategy, making TDAN a valuable addition to the field of video super-resolution and related areas such as computer vision, image processing, and deep learning.
