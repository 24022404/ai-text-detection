This paper introduces Hyperbolic Manifold Regression, a novel framework for learning continuous mappings from Euclidean input spaces to target variables that inherently reside on a hyperbolic manifold. Unlike traditional regression models that assume flat geometry, our method leverages Riemannian geometry in the Poincaré ball and Lorentz models to capture hierarchical or tree-like structures common in complex networks, taxonomy embeddings, and phylogenetic data. We formulate a geodesic loss function based on hyperbolic distance and derive closed-form expressions for the exponential and logarithmic maps, enabling efficient optimization via Riemannian gradient descent. Extensive experiments on synthetic hierarchical datasets and real-world network embedding tasks demonstrate that our approach reduces mean squared geodesic error by up to 30% compared to Euclidean and spherical regression baselines, while better preserving latent hierarchical relationships. Moreover, an ablation study highlights the importance of curvature-adaptive learning rates and manifold-aware regularization in combating overfitting. Our results confirm that modeling the target space’s intrinsic curvature leads to more faithful predictions and interpretable parameter trajectories. The proposed Hyperbolic Manifold Regression advances regression analysis on non-Euclidean domains and opens new avenues for applications in knowledge graph completion, social network analysis, and biological taxonomy inference.  
Keywords: hyperbolic manifold, regression, Riemannian optimization, geodesic loss, Poincaré model, Lorentz model.
