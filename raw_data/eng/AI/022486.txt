The organization of biological tissues plays a critical role in understanding disease progression and developing targeted therapies. However, modeling the complex spatial and structural patterns of tissues remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces a novel approach to learning generative models of tissue organization using supervised Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). The proposed method leverages labeled tissue datasets to guide the generation of realistic tissue structures, ensuring both fidelity to biological principles and adaptability to diverse tissue types. By incorporating supervised learning into the GAN framework, the model achieves improved control over the generated outputs, enabling the synthesis of tissue patterns with specific morphological features. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms existing unsupervised methods in terms of structural accuracy and biological relevance. The generated tissue models are validated through quantitative metrics and expert evaluations, highlighting their potential for applications in computational pathology, drug discovery, and personalized medicine. This work contributes a scalable and interpretable framework for tissue modeling, advancing the integration of machine learning into biomedical research.  

**Keywords**: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), tissue organization, supervised learning, computational pathology, biomedical modeling.
