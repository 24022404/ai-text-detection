This paper addresses the challenging problem of optical flow estimation, which is crucial for various computer vision applications such as motion analysis, scene reconstruction, and autonomous navigation. Traditional methods often face difficulties in handling complex motion patterns and preserving fine details.

Methods/Approach: We propose an adaptive mixed norm approach for optical flow estimation, which leverages a combination of multiple norm models to better capture varying local motion characteristics. Our method dynamically adjusts the norm selection in response to local image content, thereby maintaining a balance between regularization and data fidelity. Advanced optimization techniques are employed to efficiently solve the underlying variational problem.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our adaptive mixed norm framework significantly outperforms traditional single-norm approaches in terms of accuracy and robustness. We conducted extensive evaluations on standard benchmark datasets, where our method achieved superior performance metrics, particularly in scenarios involving sharp motion discontinuities and large displacements.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed adaptive mixed norm method introduces a novel perspective on optical flow estimation, enhancing both detail preservation and error reduction in complex scenes. This approach has potential applications in enhancing the accuracy and reliability of computer vision systems in dynamic environments. Future work will explore the integration of our method into real-time systems and its application to related problems such as depth estimation and scene understanding.

Keywords: Optical flow, mixed norm, adaptive estimation, variational methods, computer vision, motion analysis, scene reconstruction.
