Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) is a widely used algorithm for measuring similarities between temporal sequences, with applications spanning speech recognition, bioinformatics, and time series analysis. However, the standard DTW algorithm can be computationally expensive due to the large alignment path search space, especially when dealing with long or complex sequences. This paper proposes an innovative sparsification approach to reduce the alignment path search space in DTW without significantly compromising accuracy. The proposed method introduces a selective path constraint mechanism, leveraging statistical properties and adaptive pruning strategies to eliminate low-probability alignment paths early in the computation. Experimental analysis on multiple real-world and synthetic datasets demonstrates that the sparsified DTW significantly lowers computational time and memory usage compared to conventional DTW and other constrained variants, with minimal loss in alignment quality. Furthermore, we analyze the impact of different sparsification parameters on both performance and accuracy, providing insights into their optimal selection for various applications. The results underline the effectiveness of our approach in accelerating DTW-based sequence analysis, making it more viable for large-scale or real-time scenarios. This work contributes a practical and efficient enhancement to temporal data analysis and has broad potential applicability in domains requiring fast and reliable time series alignment. Keywords: Dynamic Time Warping, path sparsification, alignment path search space, time series analysis, sequence matching, computational efficiency.
