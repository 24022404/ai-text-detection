Off-policy deep reinforcement learning (DRL) holds significant promise for training agents from previously collected data, circumventing the need for online exploration. However, existing methods often suffer from instability and suboptimal performance due to distributional shift and extrapolation errors. This paper introduces a novel off-policy DRL algorithm that mitigates these issues without relying on explicit exploration. Our approach leverages a conservative policy optimization framework coupled with a learned behavior regularizer to constrain policy updates. This regularizer encourages the learned policy to stay close to the behavior policy, thereby reducing extrapolation errors and improving stability. We evaluate our algorithm on a range of benchmark tasks, demonstrating significant performance improvements over state-of-the-art off-policy DRL methods. The results show that our approach effectively learns robust policies from offline datasets, even in the absence of exploratory data. Key contributions include a novel behavior regularizer, a stable off-policy learning framework, and empirical validation of its effectiveness. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Deep Learning, Off-Policy Learning, Policy Optimization, Behavior Regularization.
