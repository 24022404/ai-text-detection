This paper presents an advanced method for the super-resolution of brain Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) images by integrating overcomplete dictionary learning with nonlocal similarity constraints. The primary objective is to enhance the spatial resolution of low-quality brain MRI scans, which is critical for accurate medical diagnosis and analysis. The proposed approach constructs an overcomplete dictionary from high-resolution image patches, enabling sparse coding of image structures and preserving fine details during the reconstruction process. Additionally, nonlocal similarity is leveraged to identify and utilize repeated patterns within the images, further improving the reconstruction accuracy and reducing artifacts. Experimental results on benchmark brain MRI datasets demonstrate that our method achieves superior performance in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM) compared to conventional interpolation and example-based super-resolution techniques. The findings suggest that combining overcomplete dictionaries with nonlocal similarity not only enhances image resolution but also maintains anatomical fidelity. This work offers significant potential for clinical applications such as improved neuroimaging analysis and computer-aided diagnosis, and highlights the value of advanced signal processing and machine learning techniques in medical image enhancement. Key keywords: super-resolution, brain MRI, overcomplete dictionary, nonlocal similarity, sparse representation, medical image processing.
