The objective of this research is to address the challenge of shot boundary detection (SBD) in video processing by introducing a novel approach leveraging a new extensive dataset alongside a combination of mixed features. Current SBD techniques face limitations in accuracy and adaptability when applied to diverse video genres and formats. To overcome these challenges, our method utilizes a newly compiled dataset that encompasses a wide range of video types, ensuring a comprehensive foundation for training and validation. The approach integrates mixed features, combining color histograms, motion vectors, and deep learning-derived features within a unified detection framework. Our experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in detection accuracy and robustness across various video types compared to existing methods. The findings reveal that the integration of mixed features allows the model to better distinguish between abrupt and gradual transitions, thereby reducing false positives and missed boundaries. Our research contributes to the advancement of video analysis technology by offering an adaptable and high-performing SBD method that can be incorporated into multimedia indexing, archiving, and retrieval systems. Key implications include enhanced video content analysis tools and applications in digital media industries. Keywords: shot boundary detection, video processing, extensive dataset, mixed features, video analysis.
