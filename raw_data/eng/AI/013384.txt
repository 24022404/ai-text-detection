Hyperspectral dimensionality reduction (HDR) plays a crucial role in hyperspectral image (HSI) analysis by mitigating the Hughes phenomenon. This paper introduces a novel semi-supervised HDR method, Joint and Progressive Subspace Analysis (JPSA) with Spatial-Spectral Manifold Alignment, designed to effectively leverage both labeled and unlabeled data. JPSA simultaneously optimizes multiple low-dimensional subspaces progressively, capturing the intrinsic structure of the data while enhancing discrimination. The spatial-spectral manifold alignment integrates local spatial neighborhood information with spectral features to preserve the inherent geometric structure of HSI data. This alignment is achieved by constructing a graph Laplacian regularization term that encourages neighboring pixels to share similar low-dimensional representations. Experiments on several benchmark HSI datasets demonstrate that JPSA outperforms state-of-the-art semi-supervised and unsupervised dimensionality reduction techniques in terms of classification accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight JPSA's ability to extract more discriminative and compact feature representations, making it well-suited for various HSI applications. Keywords: Hyperspectral dimensionality reduction, semi-supervised learning, manifold alignment, subspace analysis, hyperspectral imaging.
