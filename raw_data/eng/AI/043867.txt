This paper addresses the challenging problem of dense 3D object reconstruction from a single depth view, a crucial task in robotics, augmented reality, and computer vision. We propose a novel approach combining deep learning with geometric priors to infer complete 3D shapes from sparse and noisy depth data. Our method utilizes a convolutional neural network (CNN) to predict a dense volumetric representation of the object, guided by a shape completion network trained on a large dataset of 3D models. A key innovation is the integration of a surface normal consistency loss, encouraging geometrically plausible reconstructions even in areas with limited depth information. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of reconstruction accuracy and completeness, particularly for objects with complex geometries and significant occlusions. The generated 3D models exhibit finer details and improved structural integrity. This work contributes a robust and efficient solution for single-view 3D reconstruction, enabling wider applications in real-world scenarios.

**Keywords:** 3D reconstruction, depth completion, single-view reconstruction, deep learning, shape completion, computer vision.
