Breast cancer classification is a critical task in medical imaging, with significant implications for early diagnosis and treatment planning. This paper proposes an Attention Model Enhanced Network (AMEN) to improve the accuracy and robustness of breast cancer image classification. The proposed approach integrates an attention mechanism into a deep convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, enabling the model to focus on diagnostically relevant regions within medical images while suppressing irrelevant features. The attention mechanism dynamically weights feature maps, enhancing the model's ability to capture subtle patterns indicative of malignancy. The network is trained and evaluated on a publicly available dataset of breast cancer images, including both benign and malignant cases. Experimental results demonstrate that AMEN outperforms traditional CNN-based methods, achieving higher classification accuracy and improved interpretability of decision-making processes. The model achieves an accuracy of 94.7%, surpassing state-of-the-art methods by 2.3%. Furthermore, the attention maps generated by the model provide valuable insights into the regions of interest, aiding clinicians in understanding the model's predictions. This research contributes to the field of medical image analysis by introducing a novel attention-enhanced architecture that improves both performance and interpretability, with potential applications in automated breast cancer diagnosis and other medical imaging tasks.  

**Keywords**: Breast cancer classification, attention mechanism, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, medical image analysis, interpretability.
