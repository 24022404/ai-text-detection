Domain-invariant object detection remains a critical challenge in computer vision, particularly when models trained on one domain fail to generalize to others due to domain shifts. This paper introduces a novel approach, Vector-Decomposed Disentanglement (VDD), to address this issue by explicitly separating domain-specific and domain-invariant features in the latent space. The proposed method leverages a vector decomposition framework to disentangle feature representations, ensuring that object detection models focus on domain-agnostic attributes while minimizing the influence of domain-specific variations. We evaluate VDD on benchmark datasets, including cross-domain scenarios such as synthetic-to-real and weather-adaptive object detection. Experimental results demonstrate that VDD outperforms existing domain adaptation techniques, achieving significant improvements in mean average precision (mAP) across diverse domains. The framework’s ability to generalize across unseen environments highlights its potential for real-world applications, such as autonomous driving and surveillance systems. This work contributes a scalable and interpretable solution to domain adaptation, paving the way for more robust and reliable object detection systems in dynamic environments.  

**Keywords**: Domain adaptation, object detection, feature disentanglement, vector decomposition, domain-invariant learning, computer vision.
