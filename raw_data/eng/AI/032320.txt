This paper presents a novel approach to image editing using language-based inputs, leveraging recurrent attentive models to bridge the gap between natural language processing and computer vision. The objective is to enable users to edit images using textual descriptions, allowing for more intuitive and user-friendly image manipulation. Our method employs a recurrent neural network architecture with attention mechanisms to parse linguistic inputs and generate corresponding image edits. The model is trained on a large dataset of image-text pairs, enabling it to learn the relationships between language and visual features. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving significant improvements in image editing tasks such as object removal, colorization, and texture transfer. The proposed model outperforms existing methods, showcasing its potential for real-world applications in image editing, graphic design, and visual content creation. Key contributions include the introduction of a recurrent attentive framework for language-based image editing and the demonstration of its capabilities in handling complex textual inputs. Relevant keywords: language-based image editing, recurrent attentive models, natural language processing, computer vision, image manipulation, deep learning, attention mechanisms.
