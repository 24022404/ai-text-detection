This paper addresses the challenge of generating realistic images from scene graphs, focusing on improving the contextualized layout of objects to enhance visual coherence and semantic accuracy. The objective is to bridge the gap between structured scene descriptions and high-quality image synthesis, ensuring that generated images accurately reflect the relationships and attributes defined in the scene graph. To achieve this, we propose a novel approach that integrates a contextualized object layout refinement module into the image generation pipeline. This module leverages graph neural networks (GNNs) to model object relationships and refine their spatial arrangements, ensuring better alignment with the scene graph's semantic structure. Additionally, we employ a conditional generative adversarial network (cGAN) to synthesize high-resolution images from the refined layouts. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in both visual quality and semantic fidelity compared to existing methods, as validated by quantitative metrics and user studies. The proposed approach not only advances the state-of-the-art in scene graph-to-image generation but also offers practical applications in areas such as virtual reality, content creation, and human-computer interaction. Key contributions include the introduction of a context-aware layout refinement mechanism and a robust framework for generating semantically consistent images from complex scene descriptions.  

**Keywords**: Scene graph, image generation, graph neural networks, generative adversarial networks, object layout refinement, semantic fidelity.
