Discriminative visual tracking performance is highly dependent on the quality of the training data used to update the tracker. This paper introduces a novel framework, Adaptive Decontamination of the Training Set (ADTS), designed to mitigate the impact of noisy or contaminated samples during online learning. The core idea is to dynamically assess the reliability of newly acquired training samples and selectively incorporate only those that demonstrably improve the tracker's discriminative power. ADTS is formulated as a unified optimization problem that jointly learns the tracker parameters and the contamination level of each training sample. An efficient alternating minimization algorithm is presented to solve the resulting non-convex optimization. Extensive experiments on challenging benchmark datasets demonstrate that ADTS significantly outperforms state-of-the-art tracking algorithms, particularly in scenarios with occlusions, illumination variations, and background clutter. The proposed method exhibits superior robustness and accuracy by adaptively refining the training set, leading to more reliable and long-term tracking performance. Key words: visual tracking, online learning, training set contamination, discriminative tracking, adaptive learning.
