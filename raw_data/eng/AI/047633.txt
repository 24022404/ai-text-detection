This paper introduces DDSL (Deep Differentiable Simplex Layer), a novel neural network component designed for efficient learning and processing of geometric signals. Traditional approaches to geometric learning often struggle with end-to-end differentiability when handling complex geometric representations such as meshes, point clouds, and simplicial complexes. In response, DDSL provides a fully differentiable framework that enables seamless integration of geometric signal processing into deep learning architectures. The proposed layer leverages simplex-based parameterizations to represent geometric data, supporting both forward and backward computations for neural network training. 

We incorporate DDSL into various deep learning models and evaluate its performance across multiple geometric learning tasks, including 3D shape classification, surface segmentation, and reconstruction. Results demonstrate that DDSL significantly improves accuracy and training stability compared to conventional methods, while maintaining computational efficiency. Notably, DDSL enables efficient differentiation with respect to both geometric structure and associated features, facilitating advanced operations such as differentiable rendering and end-to-end shape optimization. 

Our findings highlight the flexibility and generality of DDSL for a wide range of geometric deep learning problems. The layer’s differentiable design paves the way for new applications in computer vision, computer graphics, and scientific computing. Overall, DDSL contributes a substantial advancement in geometric signal processing by combining the expressiveness of simplex representations with deep learning paradigms.

Keywords: deep learning, differentiable layer, geometric signals, simplex, neural networks, 3D reconstruction, shape analysis.
