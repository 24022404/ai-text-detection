This paper addresses the critical issue of distribution inference risks, which pose significant threats to data privacy. Our objective is to formalize and estimate these risks, providing a comprehensive framework for understanding and mitigating potential vulnerabilities. We employ a novel approach, leveraging statistical models and machine learning techniques to quantify the likelihood of sensitive information disclosure. Our methodology involves developing a theoretical foundation for distribution inference risks, followed by the design of algorithms for risk estimation and mitigation. The results of our experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in identifying and reducing distribution inference risks, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency. Our research contributes to the development of more secure and private data analysis systems, with significant implications for applications in areas such as healthcare, finance, and social media. Key contributions include the introduction of a formal framework for distribution inference risks, the development of efficient risk estimation algorithms, and the demonstration of their efficacy in real-world scenarios. Relevant keywords: distribution inference, data privacy, statistical models, machine learning, risk estimation, privacy preservation.
