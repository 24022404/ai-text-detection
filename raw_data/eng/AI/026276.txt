Object detection has traditionally relied on specialized architectures and task-specific pipelines, limiting flexibility and generalization. This paper introduces **Pix2seq**, a novel language modeling framework that reformulates object detection as a sequence generation task. By treating bounding boxes and class labels as discrete tokens, Pix2seq leverages a unified sequence-to-sequence model to predict objects in an image. The approach eliminates the need for handcrafted components such as anchor boxes or non-maximum suppression, simplifying the detection pipeline while maintaining competitive performance.  

The proposed framework employs a transformer-based architecture trained end-to-end, enabling it to learn spatial and semantic relationships directly from pixel inputs. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including COCO, demonstrate that Pix2seq achieves state-of-the-art results, outperforming traditional detection methods in terms of both accuracy and scalability. Furthermore, the framework exhibits strong generalization capabilities, making it adaptable to diverse detection tasks beyond standard benchmarks.  

Pix2seq represents a significant shift in object detection methodology, offering a more flexible and unified approach that aligns with advancements in language modeling. Its simplicity, combined with high performance, opens new avenues for research in vision-language tasks and multimodal learning.  

**Keywords**: Object detection, sequence modeling, transformer, language modeling, Pix2seq, computer vision.
