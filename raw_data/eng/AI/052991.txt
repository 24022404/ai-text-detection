The processing and analysis of 3D point clouds have become increasingly important in applications such as autonomous driving, augmented reality, and robotics. However, traditional methods often struggle to efficiently capture the complex geometric structures and local variations inherent in 3D data. This paper introduces a novel **Region Adaptive Graph Fourier Transform (RAGFT)**, a framework designed to enhance the representation and analysis of 3D point clouds by leveraging adaptive graph-based signal processing. The proposed approach dynamically constructs region-specific graphs that adapt to the local geometry of the point cloud, enabling more effective spectral decomposition. By incorporating spatial and spectral information, RAGFT improves the accuracy of feature extraction and compression compared to conventional methods. Experimental results demonstrate that RAGFT achieves superior performance in tasks such as denoising, classification, and compression, outperforming state-of-the-art techniques. The framework’s adaptability and efficiency make it a promising tool for real-time applications and large-scale 3D data processing. This work contributes to advancing the field of 3D point cloud analysis by providing a robust, region-aware spectral transformation method with broad applicability in computer vision and beyond.  

**Keywords**: 3D point clouds, graph Fourier transform, adaptive graph construction, spectral analysis, feature extraction, signal processing.
