This paper addresses the challenge of image inpainting by proposing a novel approach that leverages a hierarchical VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder) to generate diverse structural content for missing regions. The objective is to develop a method that can effectively restore damaged or missing parts of an image while maintaining its original coherence and realism. Our approach utilizes a hierarchical representation of images, allowing the model to capture both local and global structures. The VQ-VAE is trained to learn a discrete and compact representation of images, which is then used to generate new content for the missing regions. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing image inpainting techniques, producing more realistic and diverse results. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a hierarchical VQ-VAE architecture for image inpainting and the demonstration of its ability to generate high-quality, structurally coherent images. This work has significant implications for applications such as image restoration, editing, and generation, and highlights the potential of VQ-VAE models for image processing tasks. Key keywords: image inpainting, VQ-VAE, hierarchical representation, image generation, deep learning.
