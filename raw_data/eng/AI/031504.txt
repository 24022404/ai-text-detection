This paper investigates the innovative integration of physical modeling techniques within active contour frameworks to enhance image segmentation processes. The study addresses the challenge of accurately and efficiently delineating object boundaries in digital images, which is crucial in various applications including medical imaging, computer vision, and remote sensing.

Methods/Approach: Our approach involves the incorporation of physical modeling principles into the active contour model, traditionally used for non-rigid shape detection. We develop a novel methodology that applies mechanical properties of elasticity and surface tension to enhance the contour's adaptability and robustness in varying image contexts. Our algorithm leverages numerical simulation methods to adjust contour dynamics, targeting improved alignment with object boundaries while reducing computational complexity.

Results/Findings: The experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in segmentation accuracy over conventional active contour models, particularly in images with varying contrast and noise levels. The proposed technique consistently outperformed classical models and recent algorithms in both quantitative metrics and visual assessment. The application of physical modeling yielded a higher degree of precision in edge localization, providing more reliable segmentation outputs.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field by extending traditional active contour models with physical modeling concepts, offering a more versatile and effective tool for image segmentation. The integration of these techniques not only enhances segmentation quality but also expands the applicability of active contour models in complex image environments. Future work may explore further refinement of mechanical parameters and the extension of this framework to 3D image data.

Keywords: active contours, image segmentation, physical modeling, elasticity, numerical simulation, edge detection.
