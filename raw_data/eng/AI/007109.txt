This paper investigates the efficacy of deep learning approaches on tabular data, challenging the assumption that these models are universally superior across all data types. Despite the widespread success of deep learning in fields such as image and video processing, its advantages for tabular datasets remain controversial.

Methods/Approach: We conduct a comprehensive evaluation using various state-of-the-art deep learning models, examining their performance against traditional machine learning techniques specifically on tabular datasets. We consider factors such as model complexity, training time, and generalization ability. Additionally, we introduce a novel benchmarking framework designed to accurately assess and compare the performance of deep learning and traditional methods on tabular data.

Results/Findings: Our experiments reveal that traditional machine learning methods often outperform deep learning models on tabular datasets, particularly in scenarios with limited data or when interpretability and computational efficiency are priorities. We provide detailed insights into when deep learning may still be beneficial and propose scenarios where hybrid approaches combining traditional and deep learning techniques could be advantageous.

Conclusion/Implications: We conclude that the choice of model depends heavily on the nature of the tabular data and the specific requirements of the task at hand. This study contributes a critical perspective to the ongoing debate on the applicability of deep learning to tabular data, suggesting that practitioners should carefully evaluate the suitability of deep learning before application. Our findings highlight the need for more research in developing methodologies that can effectively bridge the gap between traditional and deep learning models for tabular data use cases.

Keywords: Tabular data, Deep learning, Machine learning, Model performance, Benchmarking, Hybrid approaches, Data interpretability.
