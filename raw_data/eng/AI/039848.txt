This paper investigates the phenomenon of representation mitosis in wide neural networks, a critical aspect that influences the decision boundaries and generalization capabilities of deep learning models. The study aims to understand how diverse feature representations arise as neural network width increases and their impact on model performance.

Methods/Approach: We employ a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical experiments on various benchmark datasets to explore the dynamics of representation mitosis. The study utilizes advanced visualization techniques and statistical metrics to evaluate the structural diversity and functionality of emergent representations. Our approach includes modifying established neural network architectures to systematically vary their widths and analyzing the implications on feature space partitioning.

Results/Findings: The findings reveal that as neural networks expand in width, a distinct process akin to biological mitosis occurs, where initial representations proliferate and diversify. This representation mitosis contributes to more finely-tuned decision boundaries, enhancing the network's ability to generalize across complex data distributions. Our results demonstrate that wide neural networks can achieve superior performance over their narrower counterparts by leveraging increased representational diversity.

Conclusion/Implications: The research provides significant insights into the role of network width in shaping feature representations, offering a novel perspective on neural network design. The concept of representation mitosis opens avenues for developing more robust architectures with improved generalization and performance. These findings contribute to the advancement of deep learning by suggesting new strategies for model optimization and architecture selection. Key implications include the potential for enhancing model training efficiency and application in areas requiring high precision and adaptability.

Keywords: representation mitosis, wide neural networks, deep learning, neural network width, feature representations, generalization, model architecture.
