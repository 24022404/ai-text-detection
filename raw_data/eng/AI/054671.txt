This paper addresses the persistent challenge of semantic segmentation in video data, particularly focusing on the degradation of features due to distortions inherent in video sequences. Identifying and correcting these distortions is essential for enhancing the accuracy and reliability of segmentation outcomes.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel method titled "Distortion-Aware Feature Correction" (DAFC) to improve semantic segmentation in videos. Our approach integrates a correction mechanism within the segmentation pipeline, which utilizes a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) model enhanced by a distortion-detection module. This module identifies distorted features across consecutive frames and applies a correction layer that refines feature maps to align more closely with true semantic content.

Results/Findings: Evaluation of our method on standard video segmentation datasets demonstrates a substantial improvement in segmentation accuracy compared to existing techniques. Our approach not only reduces the error rate in detecting semantic boundaries but also enhances overall system robustness against common video distortions such as motion blur and compression artifacts. We observe a remarkable increase in Intersection over Union (IoU) scores, showcasing the efficacy of the DAFC mechanism.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed distortion-aware approach marks a significant advancement in video semantic segmentation, offering better handling of video distortions and improving segmentation precision. This research contributes valuable insights into video data preprocessing and has potential applications in fields such as autonomous driving, surveillance, and augmented reality, where video content analysis is crucial. Future exploration may extend this model to real-time processing scenarios while maintaining high accuracy. Key Keywords: video semantic segmentation, distortion-aware, feature correction, CNN, video analysis, segmentation accuracy.
