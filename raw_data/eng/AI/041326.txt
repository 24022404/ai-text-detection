This paper introduces ICON, a novel framework for learning regular maps through inverse consistency, addressing the challenge of ensuring bijective and smooth mappings in complex data transformations. The objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient method for generating invertible mappings that maintain consistency across forward and inverse transformations, which is critical for applications such as image registration, 3D reconstruction, and generative modeling. ICON leverages a dual-network architecture trained with an inverse consistency loss, ensuring that the composition of forward and inverse mappings approximates the identity function. The proposed approach is evaluated on multiple datasets, including medical imaging and synthetic data, demonstrating superior performance in terms of mapping accuracy, invertibility, and computational efficiency compared to existing methods. Results show that ICON achieves state-of-the-art performance, with significant improvements in preserving structural integrity and reducing distortion. The framework’s ability to generalize across diverse domains highlights its potential for real-world applications. This work contributes a scalable and versatile solution for learning regular maps, offering insights into the design of inverse-consistent models and their broader implications for data-driven transformation tasks.  

**Keywords**: Inverse consistency, regular maps, bijective mappings, image registration, 3D reconstruction, dual-network architecture.
