Active contour models, also known as snakes, have become a foundational tool in image segmentation, enabling accurate extraction of object boundaries in diverse imaging contexts. This paper investigates the integration of physical modeling techniques into active contours to address challenges such as boundary leakage, initialization sensitivity, and computational efficiency. We present a comprehensive approach that models active contours as dynamic physical systems governed by internal elasticity and external image-derived forces. By incorporating principles from elasticity theory, energy minimization, and damping mechanisms, the proposed model achieves improved adaptability to complex shape variations and robust performance in noisy or low-contrast images. Experimental evaluations on standard medical and natural image datasets demonstrate significant improvements in segmentation accuracy and stability compared to classical and region-based active contour methods. The results highlight reduced sensitivity to contour initialization and enhanced capability to segment overlapping or closely situated objects. This work contributes a novel framework for physical modeling in image segmentation, paving the way for more reliable and efficient contour-based techniques in computer vision, medical diagnostics, and object recognition. Key keywords: active contours, image segmentation, physical modeling, elasticity, energy minimization, computer vision.
