Link prediction in graphs is a fundamental problem in network analysis, with applications ranging from social networks to biological systems. However, traditional methods often struggle in few-shot scenarios, where limited labeled data is available for training. This paper introduces a novel subgraph-aware meta-learning framework for few-shot inductive link prediction, addressing the challenge of generalizing to unseen nodes and edges in dynamic graphs. The proposed approach leverages meta-learning to adaptively learn transferable knowledge from a set of training tasks, enabling effective prediction on new tasks with minimal labeled data. Specifically, we design a subgraph extraction mechanism that captures local structural patterns and relational features, enhancing the model's ability to generalize across diverse graph structures. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art approaches in terms of prediction accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with sparse or evolving graphs. The results highlight the framework's potential for real-world applications, such as recommendation systems and knowledge graph completion, where inductive learning and few-shot adaptability are critical. This work contributes a scalable and efficient solution to the problem of few-shot link prediction, offering insights into the interplay between subgraph representation and meta-learning in graph-based tasks.  

**Keywords**: link prediction, few-shot learning, meta-learning, inductive learning, subgraph extraction, graph neural networks.
