Graph-structured data is ubiquitous across various domains, yet effectively learning from it remains a challenge. This paper introduces Convolutional Kernel Networks (CKNs), a novel approach that combines the strengths of convolutional neural networks and kernel methods for graph representation learning. CKNs leverage localized convolutional filters to extract features from graph nodes and edges, which are then implicitly mapped into a high-dimensional feature space via a kernel function. This allows for capturing complex relationships and non-linear dependencies within the graph. We demonstrate the effectiveness of CKNs on several benchmark graph classification and node classification tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance compared to existing graph neural networks and kernel-based methods. Furthermore, we analyze the computational complexity and generalization properties of CKNs. The results show that CKNs provide a powerful and scalable framework for learning from graph-structured data, offering improved accuracy and robustness. Keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Kernel Methods, Convolutional Networks, Graph Classification, Node Classification, Graph Representation Learning.
