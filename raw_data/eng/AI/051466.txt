This paper addresses the challenge of facial attribution classification by proposing a novel approach that leverages gradient-weighted class activation mapping to direct the attention of deep neural networks (DNNs). The objective is to improve the accuracy and interpretability of facial attribute classification, such as predicting age, gender, and emotions from facial images. Our method utilizes a gradient-weighted class activation mapping technique to visualize and understand the contributions of different facial regions to the classification decision. The approach is based on a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, which is fine-tuned using a large-scale facial attribute dataset. Experimental results demonstrate that our proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art approaches, achieving a significant improvement in classification accuracy and providing insightful visualizations of the decision-making process. The key contributions of this research include the development of a novel attention-directed DNN architecture and the demonstration of its effectiveness in facial attribute classification. This work has important implications for various applications, including facial recognition, sentiment analysis, and human-computer interaction, and highlights the potential of gradient-weighted class activation mapping as a powerful tool for interpreting and improving DNN performance. Key keywords: facial attribution classification, deep neural networks, gradient-weighted class activation mapping, attention mechanisms, convolutional neural networks, facial recognition.
