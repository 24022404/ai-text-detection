Distance-based methods and kernel methods are fundamental tools in nonparametric hypothesis testing. While each has been extensively studied, their precise relationship remains unclear. This paper establishes the exact equivalence between a broad class of distance-based tests and kernel-based tests for a wide range of null hypotheses, including but not limited to two-sample, independence, and goodness-of-fit tests. We demonstrate that for any distance-based test statistic, a corresponding kernel test statistic exists that yields identical test results, and vice versa. This equivalence is proven analytically and validated empirically through simulations on synthetic and real-world datasets. These findings provide theoretical insights into the nature of nonparametric hypothesis testing and offer practical implications for selecting appropriate testing methodologies. Specifically, the established equivalence allows practitioners to leverage the computational advantages of one approach while benefiting from the statistical properties of the other. Keywords: Hypothesis testing, kernel methods, distance methods, nonparametric statistics, equivalence.
