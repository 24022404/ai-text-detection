Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition often characterized by atypical gaze patterns, which can serve as an early diagnostic marker. This paper presents a novel Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based approach for detecting gaze preference in children, aiming to provide a potential tool for ASD diagnostics. The proposed model leverages eye-tracking data to analyze gaze patterns, focusing on identifying deviations from typical developmental trajectories. The CNN architecture is designed to process high-dimensional visual data, extracting spatial and temporal features that correlate with gaze behavior. Training and validation were conducted on a dataset comprising gaze recordings from both neurotypical children and those diagnosed with ASD. Results demonstrate that the model achieves high accuracy in distinguishing between typical and atypical gaze patterns, outperforming traditional machine learning methods. The system’s performance is further validated through comparisons with clinical assessments, showing strong alignment with expert evaluations. This research contributes a scalable, non-invasive tool for early ASD detection, offering potential applications in clinical settings and reducing reliance on subjective diagnostic methods. The study highlights the effectiveness of deep learning in addressing complex behavioral diagnostics and underscores the potential of AI-driven solutions in healthcare.  

**Keywords**: Autism Spectrum Disorder, Convolutional Neural Network, gaze preference, eye-tracking, diagnostics, deep learning, healthcare AI.
