This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently learning graph representations without the need for labeled data. The research focuses on developing a self-supervised approach that leverages the global context of graph structures to improve the quality and applicability of representations for various downstream tasks.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel self-supervised methodology designed to enhance graph representation learning by predicting the global context of nodes within a graph. Our approach employs a unique architecture that integrates node-level and graph-level information to form a coherent representation. By utilizing context prediction as a supervisory signal, our method can autonomously learn expressive representations that capture essential structural and relational information.

Results/Findings: The proposed method was evaluated on multiple benchmark graph datasets and demonstrated superior performance in comparison to existing self-supervised graph learning techniques. Our approach showed significant improvements in tasks such as node classification, link prediction, and community detection. In particular, the global context prediction mechanism enabled our model to generalize better and achieve higher accuracy rates, proving its effectiveness and robustness across diverse graph structures.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a novel perspective to self-supervised graph representation learning by emphasizing the role of global context in forming meaningful node embeddings. The findings offer valuable insights into enhancing unsupervised learning techniques, paving the way for advancements in applications like social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics. The introduction of global context prediction charts a new course for future explorations in graph-based learning models.

Keywords: Self-supervised learning, graph representation, global context prediction, node classification, link prediction, unsupervised learning.
