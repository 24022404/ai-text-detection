This study investigates the intricacies of human visual perception when confronted with synthetic faces, aiming to understand the conditions under which synthetic faces appear more realistic than real ones. As digital face generation becomes increasingly sophisticated, discerning its impact on human perception is crucial for applications in virtual reality, social media, and security.

Methods/Approach: Leveraging advanced machine learning techniques and neural networks, we conducted experiments using a dataset comprising both real and synthetic facial images. Participants were asked to evaluate the realism of these faces under controlled conditions. We employed a combination of quantitative and qualitative analyses to assess perception accuracy and preference.

Results/Findings: Our findings reveal that synthetic faces generated by state-of-the-art models, under certain conditions, are perceived as more authentic than actual human faces. The study identified key characteristics that contribute to this perception, including symmetry and subtle nuances in facial features that advanced generation models can enhance. Comparative analyses demonstrated that synthetic faces surpassed human faces in perceived realism in 65% of cases.

Conclusion/Implications: The research highlights significant insights into human visual perception and the influence of synthetic face generation. This work contributes to the field by offering a deeper understanding of how synthetic images can manipulate perceptions, with implications for technology development in digital media and security. As artificial face generation continues to evolve, these findings could inform ethical guidelines and practices in digital content creation and dissemination.

Keywords: synthetic faces, visual perception, machine learning, neural networks, digital media, realism analysis, human-computer interaction.
