This paper introduces MEVA, a large-scale multiview, multimodal video dataset designed to advance research in activity detection. The objective of this work is to address the limitations of existing datasets, which often lack diversity in viewpoints, modalities, and real-world complexity. MEVA comprises over 10,000 video clips captured from multiple camera angles, synchronized with audio, depth, and metadata streams, enabling comprehensive analysis of human activities in dynamic environments. To construct the dataset, we employed a distributed data collection framework across diverse indoor and outdoor settings, ensuring a wide range of activities, lighting conditions, and interactions. A novel annotation pipeline was developed to label activities at fine-grained levels, incorporating temporal and spatial context.  

Our experiments demonstrate the utility of MEVA for training and evaluating state-of-the-art activity detection models. Results show that leveraging multiview and multimodal data significantly improves detection accuracy compared to single-view approaches, with a 15% increase in mean average precision (mAP). Additionally, we provide baseline benchmarks using advanced deep learning architectures, including transformer-based models and multimodal fusion techniques.  

The MEVA dataset represents a significant contribution to the field, offering a robust resource for developing and testing activity detection systems in real-world scenarios. Its unique combination of multiview and multimodal data opens new avenues for research in video understanding, human-computer interaction, and surveillance applications. Key innovations include the integration of synchronized modalities and the scalability of the dataset, which supports both supervised and self-supervised learning paradigms. Keywords: activity detection, multiview video, multimodal data, video understanding, deep learning, transformer models.
