This paper presents Neural Bellman-Ford Networks (NBFN), a novel and general graph neural network framework designed for the link prediction task. The objective of our research is to address the limitations of existing graph neural networks (GNNs) in effectively modeling complex relational patterns and long-range dependencies within large-scale graphs. Drawing inspiration from the Bellman-Ford algorithm, our approach integrates dynamic programming principles into the neural message passing process, enabling efficient aggregation of multi-hop relational information. We propose a flexible neural architecture that generalizes several existing GNN models, such as Graph Convolutional Networks and Graph Attention Networks, by incorporating learned distance-aware propagation mechanisms tailored for link prediction. Extensive experiments are conducted on both synthetic and real-world datasets, including social, citation, and knowledge graphs. Results demonstrate that Neural Bellman-Ford Networks consistently outperform state-of-the-art link prediction methods in terms of accuracy and scalability, particularly in scenarios with sparse or noisy graph structure. Furthermore, ablation studies highlight the effectiveness of our distance-aware propagation scheme in capturing essential structural features relevant to the prediction task. In conclusion, NBFN offers a unified and extensible framework for general graph representation learning, opening new possibilities for downstream applications such as recommendation systems, biological network analysis, and knowledge graph completion.

Keywords: neural Bellman-Ford, graph neural networks, link prediction, message passing, graph representation, dynamic programming, multi-hop reasoning.
