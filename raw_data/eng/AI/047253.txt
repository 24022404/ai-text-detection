Unsupervised domain adaptation between synthetic and real images remains a key challenge in deploying robust object detection models in real-world scenarios. This paper addresses the problem of transferring anchorless object detectors, which predict object locations without predefined anchor boxes, from synthetic domains—where labeled data is abundant—to real-world domains, where annotation is expensive or unavailable. We propose a novel unsupervised domain adaptation framework that leverages a feature alignment strategy combined with domain-invariant representation learning. Our approach utilizes adversarial learning and consistency regularization to minimize domain discrepancies at both the image and feature levels, enabling the anchorless detector to generalize effectively to unseen real-world data. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in detection accuracy compared to existing domain adaptation methods, highlighting the effectiveness of our method in handling diverse and complex real-world variations. The findings suggest that our framework substantially bridges the synthetic-to-real domain gap for anchorless detection, reducing reliance on labeled real data and paving the way for more scalable applications in fields such as autonomous driving, robotics, and video surveillance. Keywords: unsupervised domain adaptation, synthetic-to-real, anchorless object detection, feature alignment, adversarial learning, deep learning.
