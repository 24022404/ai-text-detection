This paper introduces FlowNet3D, a novel approach for learning scene flow in 3D point clouds. The objective is to estimate the 3D motion of objects and scenes from consecutive point cloud scans, which is crucial for various applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and 3D reconstruction. Our method utilizes a deep learning architecture that takes two consecutive point cloud frames as input and predicts the scene flow, which represents the 3D motion of each point. The approach employs a combination of point-wise features, convolutional neural networks, and flow estimation layers to accurately capture the complex motion patterns in 3D scenes. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of FlowNet3D, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The key contributions of this work include the design of a robust and efficient architecture for scene flow estimation, as well as the creation of a large-scale dataset for training and evaluating scene flow models. This research has significant implications for various fields, including computer vision, robotics, and autonomous systems, and paves the way for future work on 3D scene understanding and motion analysis. Key keywords: scene flow, 3D point clouds, deep learning, computer vision, 3D reconstruction.
