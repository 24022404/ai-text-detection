Gaze estimation, a critical task in human-computer interaction and computer vision, often relies on large-scale labeled datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to acquire. This paper introduces *GazeGAN*, a novel unpaired adversarial image generation framework designed to address the challenge of limited labeled data for gaze estimation. Unlike traditional methods that require paired training data, GazeGAN leverages unsupervised learning to generate synthetic gaze images that preserve the structural and semantic features of real-world gaze data. The proposed framework integrates a generative adversarial network (GAN) with a gaze-specific feature extractor, enabling the generation of high-quality, domain-consistent images without the need for explicit supervision. Experimental results demonstrate that GazeGAN significantly improves gaze estimation accuracy when used for data augmentation, outperforming existing methods on benchmark datasets. Furthermore, the framework exhibits robustness across diverse environments and lighting conditions, showcasing its potential for real-world applications. This work contributes a scalable and efficient solution to the gaze estimation problem, reducing dependency on labeled datasets and advancing the state-of-the-art in unpaired image generation for computer vision tasks.  

**Keywords**: Gaze estimation, GAN, unpaired image generation, adversarial learning, data augmentation, computer vision.
