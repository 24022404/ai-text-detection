This paper introduces TRACE, a novel framework designed to enhance image search capabilities by incorporating text feedback through the transformation, aggregation, and composition of visiolinguistic representations. The objective is to improve the accuracy and relevance of image search results by leveraging the synergy between visual and linguistic information. TRACE employs a multimodal approach, utilizing a combination of computer vision and natural language processing techniques to learn compact and informative representations of images and text. The method involves transforming visual features into a linguistic space, aggregating text feedback to refine the search query, and composing the refined query with visual features to generate a visiolinguistic representation. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of TRACE in improving image search performance, particularly in scenarios where text feedback is provided. The proposed framework contributes to the development of more intuitive and user-friendly image search systems, with potential applications in areas such as multimedia retrieval, visual question answering, and human-computer interaction. Key contributions include the introduction of a novel visiolinguistic representation learning paradigm and the demonstration of the benefits of incorporating text feedback in image search. Relevant keywords: visiolinguistic representations, image search, text feedback, multimodal learning, computer vision, natural language processing.
