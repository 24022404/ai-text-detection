Face recognition has become a cornerstone of modern biometric systems, yet achieving robust and discriminative feature representations remains a challenge. This paper revisits and revitalizes the SphereFace framework, a pioneering approach in hyperspherical face recognition, by unifying its principles with contemporary advancements. The primary objective is to enhance the discriminative power of face embeddings by optimizing angular margin-based loss functions on a hyperspherical manifold. We propose a refined methodology that integrates SphereFace with state-of-the-art deep learning architectures, enabling more efficient optimization and improved generalization across diverse datasets. Experimental results demonstrate significant performance gains, with our approach outperforming existing methods on benchmark datasets such as LFW, CFP-FP, and AgeDB-30. Notably, the unified framework achieves superior accuracy in handling large-scale face recognition tasks while maintaining computational efficiency. The findings underscore the enduring relevance of hyperspherical embeddings in face recognition and provide a foundation for future research in this domain. This work contributes a novel perspective on unifying classical and modern techniques, offering practical implications for real-world applications in security, surveillance, and identity verification.  

**Keywords**: Face recognition, hyperspherical embeddings, SphereFace, angular margin, deep learning, biometric systems.
