Temporal decision-making is a critical challenge in reinforcement learning (RL), where agents must learn not only *what* actions to take but also *when* to act. This paper introduces **TempoRL**, a novel framework designed to optimize action timing in dynamic environments. The objective of this research is to address the inefficiencies caused by suboptimal timing in traditional RL approaches, which often lead to increased computational costs and reduced performance. TempoRL integrates a temporal gating mechanism with a deep Q-network (DQN) architecture, enabling the agent to dynamically decide when to execute actions based on environmental states and learned temporal patterns. Experiments were conducted across multiple benchmark environments, including Atari games and robotic control tasks, to evaluate the framework's effectiveness. Results demonstrate that TempoRL significantly reduces the number of actions required while maintaining or improving task performance compared to standard RL methods. Furthermore, the framework exhibits robustness in environments with varying temporal dynamics. The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to enhance RL efficiency by learning optimal action timing, offering potential applications in real-time systems, robotics, and resource-constrained environments. This research advances the field of RL by introducing a scalable and adaptive approach to temporal decision-making, paving the way for more efficient and intelligent autonomous systems.  

**Keywords**: reinforcement learning, temporal decision-making, action timing, deep Q-network, autonomous systems, efficiency optimization.
