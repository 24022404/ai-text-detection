This paper introduces a novel exploration strategy, Temporally-Extended ε-Greedy (TEεG), designed to enhance reinforcement learning in environments requiring temporally consistent actions. Traditional ε-Greedy exploration suffers from frequent action switches, which can be detrimental in domains where maintaining actions over time is crucial for efficient learning. TEεG addresses this by extending the duration of exploratory actions. Specifically, instead of selecting a random action at each time step with probability ε, TEεG samples a random action and maintains it for a duration drawn from a predefined distribution. This encourages the agent to commit to exploratory actions for longer periods, allowing it to better assess their long-term consequences. We evaluate TEεG across a suite of continuous control tasks, comparing its performance against standard ε-Greedy and other exploration methods. Results demonstrate that TEεG significantly improves sample efficiency and asymptotic performance in tasks demanding temporal consistency. Furthermore, we analyze the impact of different duration distributions on learning outcomes. The proposed method offers a simple yet effective way to improve exploration in reinforcement learning, particularly in robotics and other control applications where temporally coherent actions are vital. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Exploration, ε-Greedy, Temporal Consistency, Continuous Control.
