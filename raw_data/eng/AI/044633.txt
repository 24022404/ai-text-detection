This paper addresses the challenge of inductive link prediction in graph-structured data, where the goal is to predict new links between nodes in a previously unseen graph. Our objective is to develop a subgraph-aware few-shot learning approach that can effectively adapt to new graphs with limited labeled data. We propose a meta-learning framework that learns to recognize and leverage subgraph patterns across different graphs, enabling accurate link prediction with only a few examples. Our approach utilizes a novel graph neural network architecture that incorporates subgraph-aware attention mechanisms, allowing the model to focus on relevant subgraph structures when making predictions. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines in few-shot inductive link prediction tasks. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of subgraph-aware meta-learning for inductive link prediction and the development of a robust graph neural network architecture that can generalize well to new, unseen graphs. This work has significant implications for applications such as recommender systems, social network analysis, and knowledge graph completion, where few-shot learning and adaptability to new data are crucial. Key keywords: meta-learning, few-shot learning, inductive link prediction, graph neural networks, subgraph-aware attention.
