This paper introduces Evolving-Graph Gaussian Processes, a novel framework for modeling dynamic graph-structured data. The objective is to develop a probabilistic approach that captures the evolving relationships between nodes in a graph over time. Our method leverages Gaussian processes to model the temporal dynamics of graph signals, incorporating a graph kernel that adapts to the changing graph topology. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach through experiments on several real-world datasets, showcasing improved performance in graph signal forecasting and imputation tasks compared to existing methods. The key findings highlight the importance of accounting for temporal and structural changes in graph data. Our research contributes to the development of more accurate and robust models for dynamic graph analysis, with potential applications in social network analysis, traffic forecasting, and recommendation systems. The proposed framework provides a flexible and scalable approach to modeling evolving graphs, making it a valuable tool for researchers and practitioners in the field of graph learning and artificial intelligence, particularly in areas such as graph neural networks, time-series analysis, and spatial-temporal modeling.
