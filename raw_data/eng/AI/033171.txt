Accurate forecasting of aggregated renewable energy generation is critical for the effective integration of renewable resources into power grids. However, the widespread adoption of neural network-based forecasting models is often limited by their lack of interpretability, making it challenging for operators and stakeholders to trust and act upon their predictions. This paper addresses this problem by proposing a novel framework that enhances the interpretability of neural network models specifically tailored for forecasting aggregated renewable generation, such as wind and solar. Our approach combines state-of-the-art neural network architectures with advanced interpretability techniques, including feature attribution methods and model-agnostic visualization tools. We evaluate the framework on multiple real-world datasets, demonstrating competitive forecasting accuracy alongside improved transparency in model decision-making. Key findings highlight the ability to identify influential input factors, track temporal feature importance, and generate actionable insights for grid management. The research contributes a practical solution for increasing trust and usability of deep learning models in renewable energy forecasting, and lays the groundwork for transparent AI-driven systems in power sector operations. Keywords: neural network interpretability, renewable energy forecasting, aggregated generation, deep learning, feature attribution, transparency, power systems.
