Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as powerful alternatives to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) like ResNets, particularly in image recognition tasks. However, their success often relies heavily on large-scale pretraining or extensive data augmentations, raising questions about their inherent capabilities. This paper investigates whether ViTs can outperform ResNets without these dependencies, focusing on scenarios with limited computational resources and minimal data augmentation. We conduct a systematic comparison of ViTs and ResNets under controlled conditions, evaluating their performance on standard image classification benchmarks. Our experiments reveal that ViTs, when carefully optimized, can surpass ResNets in accuracy and efficiency, even without pretraining or strong data augmentations. Key findings highlight the role of architectural design choices, such as patch embedding and self-attention mechanisms, in enabling ViTs to achieve superior performance. These results challenge the conventional wisdom that ViTs require extensive pretraining or augmentation to excel, offering new insights into their potential for resource-constrained applications. This work contributes to the growing understanding of transformer-based models in computer vision and provides practical guidance for deploying ViTs in scenarios where pretraining or data augmentation is impractical.  

**Keywords**: Vision Transformers, ResNets, image classification, self-attention, pretraining, data augmentation.
