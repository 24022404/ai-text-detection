Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in various applications, raising concerns about privacy, particularly regarding membership inference attacks. These attacks aim to determine if a specific data point was used in the GAN's training set. This paper introduces privGAN, a novel approach to mitigate membership inference risks in GANs while maintaining high utility and low computational overhead. Our method incorporates a differential privacy mechanism tailored to the GAN training process, specifically targeting the discriminator's updates. By carefully calibrating the noise injection, privGAN effectively limits the information leakage about individual training samples. We evaluate privGAN on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating a significant reduction in membership inference attack success rates compared to standard GAN training. The experimental results show that privGAN achieves strong privacy guarantees with minimal impact on the GAN's generative performance, outperforming existing privacy-preserving GAN techniques in terms of both privacy and utility trade-offs. Our findings suggest that privGAN offers a practical and efficient solution for deploying privacy-sensitive GAN models in real-world applications. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, Differential Privacy, Membership Inference Attacks, Privacy-Preserving Machine Learning, Data Privacy.
