Expectation Propagation (EP) is a widely used approximate Bayesian inference method, but its convergence can be slow, limiting its applicability in large-scale or time-sensitive scenarios. This paper addresses the challenge of accelerating EP convergence while maintaining its accuracy and robustness. We propose a novel family of fast convergent algorithms that leverage adaptive step-size optimization and dynamic message scheduling to improve the efficiency of EP. Our approach integrates advanced optimization techniques with probabilistic inference, ensuring faster convergence without compromising the quality of the approximation. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed algorithms achieve up to 40% faster convergence compared to traditional EP methods, while maintaining comparable or superior accuracy. Furthermore, we provide theoretical insights into the conditions under which our algorithms guarantee convergence. The proposed methods are particularly beneficial for applications requiring real-time inference, such as online learning and large-scale Bayesian networks. This work contributes to the broader field of probabilistic machine learning by offering a scalable and efficient solution for approximate Bayesian inference, with potential applications in areas such as computer vision, natural language processing, and decision-making systems.  

**Keywords**: Expectation Propagation, Bayesian Inference, Fast Convergence, Adaptive Optimization, Probabilistic Machine Learning, Real-Time Inference.
