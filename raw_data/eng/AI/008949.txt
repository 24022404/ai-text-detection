Understanding the behavior of multiscale systems is a critical challenge in various scientific and engineering domains. This paper addresses the need for explainable deep learning models capable of capturing and interpreting complex interactions across multiple scales. We propose a novel framework that leverages mutual information as a key metric to enhance the interpretability and performance of deep learning models in multiscale system analysis. Our approach integrates mutual information estimation into the training process, enabling the model to identify and prioritize the most informative features across different scales. We evaluate the proposed framework on benchmark datasets, demonstrating its ability to outperform traditional methods in terms of both accuracy and interpretability. The results highlight the effectiveness of mutual information in uncovering hidden relationships within multiscale systems, providing valuable insights for domain experts. This work contributes to the growing field of explainable AI by offering a scalable and interpretable solution for analyzing complex systems, with potential applications in fields such as climate modeling, materials science, and biomedical engineering.  

**Keywords**: mutual information, explainable AI, deep learning, multiscale systems, interpretability, feature selection.
