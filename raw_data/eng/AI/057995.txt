This paper addresses the challenge of Bayesian inference in high-dimensional time-series data, where traditional methods often struggle with scalability and computational complexity. We propose the Orthogonal Stochastic Linear Mixing Model (OSLMM), a novel framework designed to efficiently handle high-dimensional time-series by leveraging orthogonal transformations and stochastic linear mixing. The OSLMM introduces a probabilistic approach that reduces dimensionality while preserving critical temporal dependencies, enabling robust inference in complex datasets. Our method employs a combination of variational inference and Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) techniques to approximate posterior distributions, ensuring computational efficiency without sacrificing accuracy. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the OSLMM outperforms existing methods in terms of inference speed, scalability, and predictive performance. The model achieves significant improvements in capturing latent structures and temporal dynamics, particularly in applications such as financial forecasting and climate modeling. This research contributes a scalable and interpretable solution for high-dimensional time-series analysis, offering practical implications for domains requiring real-time decision-making and large-scale data processing. Key innovations include the integration of orthogonal transformations to reduce computational overhead and the use of stochastic mixing to enhance model flexibility. Keywords: Bayesian inference, high-dimensional time-series, orthogonal transformations, stochastic linear mixing, variational inference, MCMC.
