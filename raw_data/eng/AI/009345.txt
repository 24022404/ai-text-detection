This paper investigates the theoretical properties of neural networks through the lens of reproducing kernel Banach spaces (RKBS). Our objective is to provide a novel framework for understanding and analyzing the behavior of deep learning models, moving beyond traditional reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) approaches. We leverage the unique characteristics of RKBS to characterize the function space learned by neural networks, focusing on their capacity to represent complex, non-smooth functions. We develop a theoretical analysis demonstrating that neural networks, when viewed as RKBS elements, exhibit enhanced approximation capabilities for certain function classes compared to their RKHS counterparts. Furthermore, we provide bounds on the generalization error and analyze the impact of network architecture on the RKBS norm. Our findings suggest that the RKBS framework offers valuable insights into the generalization and expressivity of neural networks, paving the way for improved model design and theoretical understanding. Key words: Neural Networks, Reproducing Kernel Banach Space, RKBS, Deep Learning, Generalization Error, Approximation Theory.
