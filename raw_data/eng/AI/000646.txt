This paper provides a comprehensive survey of recent advancements in self-supervised visual feature learning using deep neural networks. It addresses the growing need for efficient learning techniques that rely less on labeled data, thereby enhancing the versatility and scalability of visual tasks in artificial intelligence.

Methods/Approach: We systematically categorize existing self-supervised learning methods based on their task configurations, model architectures, and the types of pretext tasks utilized. By examining the structure and operation of various deep neural network models, including convolutional and transformer-based architectures, we explore how these systems learn to extract and represent visual features without explicit supervision.

Results/Findings: Our survey highlights significant findings regarding the performance of self-supervised methods across different visual benchmarks. We provide comparative analyses indicating that self-supervised techniques are closing the gap with traditionally supervised methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency. Notable improvements in domains such as image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation are documented, showcasing their potential in reducing dependency on labeled data.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings underscore the potential of self-supervised learning as a transformative force in the realm of visual data processing. Our survey identifies key challenges and future directions for research, emphasizing the importance of innovative pretext tasks and model designs. The insights gained contribute to a deeper understanding of self-supervised mechanisms, setting the stage for their application in real-world scenarios where data labeling is a constraint.

Key Keywords: self-supervised learning, visual feature learning, deep neural networks, pretext tasks, convolutional networks, transformer architectures, image classification, object detection.
