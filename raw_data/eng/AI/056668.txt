Class imbalance poses a significant challenge in deep learning, hindering the development of robust and generalizable models. This paper introduces ReMix, a novel resampling technique designed to mitigate the adverse effects of class imbalance by calibrating the sampling probabilities. Unlike traditional resampling methods that often lead to overfitting or underutilization of data, ReMix dynamically adjusts the sampling rate of each class based on its representation in the training data and the model's current performance. We evaluate ReMix across several benchmark datasets with varying degrees of class imbalance, demonstrating significant improvements in classification accuracy and F1-score compared to existing resampling strategies and cost-sensitive learning approaches. Our experiments show that ReMix effectively balances the trade-off between exploration and exploitation, enabling the model to learn more effectively from both majority and minority classes. The results highlight ReMix as a promising solution for addressing class imbalance in deep learning, with potential applications in diverse domains such as medical diagnosis, fraud detection, and object recognition. Keywords: Class Imbalance, Resampling, Deep Learning, Calibration, Machine Learning, Classification.
