In the rapidly evolving field of healthcare informatics, effective representation learning from Electronic Health Records (EHR) is crucial for enhancing patient care and medical research. This study aims to explore and evaluate the efficacy of autoencoder-based models for representation learning in EHR data, addressing the challenges posed by the high dimensionality and complexity inherent in medical datasets.

Methods/Approach: We systematically compared several state-of-the-art autoencoder architectures, including vanilla autoencoders, variational autoencoders, and denoising autoencoders, to determine their effectiveness in learning meaningful representations from EHR data. Our study utilized large-scale, anonymized EHR datasets and implemented robust pre-processing techniques to prepare the data for training and evaluation.

Results/Findings: The experimental results indicate that variational autoencoders outperformed other models in capturing the underlying structure and semantics of the EHR data, demonstrating superior dimensionality reduction and feature extraction capabilities. In addition, the representations learned by variational autoencoders facilitated improved predictive performance in downstream tasks such as patient outcome prediction and disease classification.

Conclusion/Implications: This comparative study highlights the potential of autoencoder-based models, particularly variational autoencoders, as powerful tools for representation learning in the healthcare domain. Our findings suggest significant implications for the deployment of these models in real-world clinical settings, enabling improved decision support systems and personalized medicine. Future research will focus on enhancing the scalability and interpretability of these models to address broader applications in health informatics.

Keywords: autoencoders, electronic health records, representation learning, variational autoencoders, healthcare informatics, dimensionality reduction, feature extraction.
