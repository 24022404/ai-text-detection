This paper introduces SOM-VAE, a novel approach to learning discrete representations of time series data. The objective is to develop an interpretable and efficient method for extracting meaningful patterns and features from complex temporal datasets. SOM-VAE combines the strengths of Self-Organizing Maps (SOM) and Variational Autoencoders (VAE) to learn a compact and structured representation of time series. The approach utilizes a SOM-based clustering mechanism to identify distinct patterns in the data, which are then encoded into a discrete latent space using a VAE. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of SOM-VAE in learning interpretable and meaningful representations, outperforming existing methods in terms of clustering quality and feature extraction. The proposed method has significant implications for time series analysis, anomaly detection, and predictive modeling, particularly in applications where interpretability and explainability are crucial. Key contributions include the integration of SOM and VAE for time series representation learning, and the demonstration of improved performance and interpretability compared to state-of-the-art methods. Relevant keywords: time series analysis, discrete representation learning, Self-Organizing Maps, Variational Autoencoders, interpretable machine learning.
