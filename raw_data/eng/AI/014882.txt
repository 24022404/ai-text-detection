This paper explores the concept of representation mitosis in wide neural networks, aiming to understand how these networks can efficiently represent and process complex data. Our objective is to investigate the mechanisms by which wide neural networks can learn multiple, diverse representations of the input data, and how this property can be leveraged to improve their performance. We employ a novel approach based on a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical experiments, utilizing techniques from machine learning and neural network architecture design. Our results show that wide neural networks can indeed exhibit representation mitosis, leading to improved performance on a range of tasks, including image classification and natural language processing. The key findings of our research include the identification of the factors that influence representation mitosis, such as network width and depth, and the development of new methods for promoting this property in neural networks. Our conclusions highlight the importance of representation mitosis in wide neural networks, and demonstrate its potential to improve the performance and efficiency of these models, with implications for applications in areas such as computer vision, AI, and deep learning. Key keywords: wide neural networks, representation mitosis, machine learning, neural network architecture, deep learning.
