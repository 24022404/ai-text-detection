Object detection in autonomous driving systems faces significant challenges due to domain shifts caused by variations in lighting, weather, and geographical conditions. This paper addresses the problem of domain adaptation for object detection by proposing a novel dual-level approach that operates at both the pixel and feature levels. At the pixel level, we introduce an image-to-image translation technique to align source and target domain images, reducing visual discrepancies. At the feature level, we employ a domain-adversarial neural network to minimize feature distribution mismatches, ensuring robust detection across domains. Our method integrates these levels into a unified framework, leveraging both low-level visual consistency and high-level semantic alignment. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms existing methods, achieving significant improvements in mean average precision (mAP) under cross-domain scenarios. The results highlight the effectiveness of combining pixel and feature-level adaptations for enhancing object detection in autonomous driving applications. This work contributes to the advancement of domain adaptation techniques, offering a scalable solution for real-world deployment in diverse environmental conditions.  

**Keywords**: Domain adaptation, object detection, autonomous driving, pixel-level alignment, feature-level alignment, adversarial learning.
