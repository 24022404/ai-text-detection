This paper presents a novel approach to image caption generation, enabling fine-grained control over the output captions through the utilization of abstract scene graphs. The objective is to address the limitation of existing image captioning models, which often lack the flexibility to incorporate user preferences or specific requirements. Our method employs a graph-based representation of the scene, allowing for the explicit specification of desired caption content and structure. We propose a multimodal framework that integrates visual features, scene graphs, and natural language processing to generate captions that align with user-provided abstract scene graphs. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in producing high-quality, user-controlled captions, outperforming state-of-the-art models in terms of accuracy, fluency, and relevance. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of abstract scene graphs as a means of controlling image caption generation and the development of a novel graph-based captioning model. This work has significant implications for applications such as image description generation, visual question answering, and human-computer interaction, particularly in areas where fine-grained control over output is crucial. Key keywords: image caption generation, abstract scene graphs, fine-grained control, multimodal learning, natural language processing.
