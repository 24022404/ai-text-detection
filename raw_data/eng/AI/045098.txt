Modern neural networks are known to be poorly calibrated, often exhibiting overconfidence in their predictions. This work revisits the calibration problem in contemporary architectures, investigating the factors that contribute to miscalibration. We analyze the impact of network depth, width, regularization techniques, and training procedures on calibration error using a suite of benchmark datasets and architectures, including ResNets, Transformers, and DenseNets. Our experiments reveal that while techniques like batch normalization and dropout can improve accuracy, they can also negatively impact calibration. Furthermore, we demonstrate that common calibration methods, such as temperature scaling, are less effective on modern architectures compared to their performance on older models. Finally, we propose a novel regularization strategy, "Calibration Regularization," that explicitly encourages well-calibrated predictions during training. Evaluation shows that this approach significantly improves calibration without sacrificing accuracy, offering a practical solution for deploying reliable neural networks. Keywords: Neural Networks, Calibration, Deep Learning, Uncertainty, Temperature Scaling, Regularization.
