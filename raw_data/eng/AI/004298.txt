This paper presents a comprehensive survey on semi-supervised learning techniques for handling delayed partially labelled data streams. The objective is to address the challenge of processing continuous data flows where labels are partially available and often delayed, which hinders the performance of traditional supervised learning methods. We employ a systematic approach to review existing semi-supervised learning models, including self-training, co-training, and graph-based methods, and examine their suitability for delayed partially labelled data streams. Our analysis reveals that combining active learning strategies with semi-supervised techniques can effectively leverage limited labelled data and improve model performance. The results show that the proposed framework outperforms traditional supervised learning methods in terms of accuracy and robustness. This research contributes to the development of more efficient and adaptive machine learning systems for real-world applications, such as sensor networks, financial transactions, and social media analytics, where data streams are often partially labelled and delayed. Key contributions include the identification of novel semi-supervised learning techniques, the evaluation of their performance on delayed partially labelled data streams, and the discussion of future research directions. Relevant keywords: semi-supervised learning, delayed partially labelled data streams, active learning, machine learning, data streams, adaptive systems.
