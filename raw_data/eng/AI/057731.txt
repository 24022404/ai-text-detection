Orphan screening, the task of identifying whether a given query 3D structure is similar to any structure within a large database, presents a significant computational challenge. This paper introduces a novel approach, Corresponding Projections, to accelerate orphan screening by efficiently indexing and comparing 3D structural data. Our method employs a multi-stage projection technique to generate compact, discriminative signatures representing the shape and spatial relationships of 3D structures. Specifically, we project 3D models onto a set of 2D planes and extract salient features from the resulting images. These features are then organized into a hierarchical index for rapid similarity search. Experimental results on a large dataset of protein structures demonstrate that our Corresponding Projections method achieves significantly improved search speeds compared to existing techniques, while maintaining comparable accuracy in identifying true positives. The proposed approach reduces the computational cost of orphan screening, enabling faster and more comprehensive analysis of structural data. This has implications for drug discovery, protein function prediction, and structural genomics.

Keywords: Orphan Screening, 3D Structure, Similarity Search, Structural Biology, Indexing, Projection.
