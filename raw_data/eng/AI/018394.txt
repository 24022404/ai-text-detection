Accurately estimating the intrinsic dimension of high-dimensional data is a fundamental challenge in machine learning and data analysis, with significant implications for dimensionality reduction, feature extraction, and model optimization. This paper addresses the problem of intrinsic dimension estimation by proposing a novel, computationally efficient algorithm that leverages local geometric properties of data manifolds. Our approach combines neighborhood-based density estimation with spectral analysis to robustly infer the intrinsic dimension, even in the presence of noise and nonlinear structures. We evaluate the proposed method on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its superior performance compared to state-of-the-art techniques in terms of accuracy and scalability. The results highlight the algorithm's ability to handle complex, high-dimensional data while maintaining low computational overhead. This work contributes to the field by providing a practical and theoretically grounded solution for intrinsic dimension estimation, with potential applications in unsupervised learning, data compression, and exploratory data analysis. Key innovations include the integration of local and global geometric insights, as well as a noise-resilient framework that adapts to varying data densities. Keywords: intrinsic dimension, dimensionality reduction, manifold learning, spectral analysis, unsupervised learning.
