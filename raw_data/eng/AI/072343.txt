The rapid evolution of neural architecture search (NAS) has highlighted the need for efficient and accurate neural predictors to evaluate candidate architectures. This paper introduces a novel approach, *Homogeneous Architecture Augmentation* (HAA), designed to enhance the performance and generalization capabilities of neural predictors. The proposed method leverages a unified framework to augment training data by generating synthetic architectures with homogeneous structural properties, ensuring diversity and representativeness in the training set. We employ a graph-based representation to model neural architectures and utilize a generative model to produce augmented samples that maintain consistency with real-world design patterns. Experimental results demonstrate that HAA significantly improves the predictive accuracy of neural predictors across multiple benchmark datasets, outperforming existing augmentation techniques by up to 15% in correlation metrics. Furthermore, the method exhibits robust generalization to unseen architectures, making it particularly valuable for large-scale NAS applications. The findings suggest that HAA not only enhances the efficiency of NAS but also provides a scalable solution for improving neural predictors in resource-constrained environments. This work contributes to the growing body of research on NAS by introducing a data-centric approach that bridges the gap between architecture diversity and predictor performance.  

**Keywords**: Neural Architecture Search, Neural Predictor, Homogeneous Augmentation, Graph-Based Representation, Data Augmentation, NAS Efficiency.
