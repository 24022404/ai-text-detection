This paper addresses the challenge of dataset bias in the field of visual emotion recognition, where the accuracy and fairness of predictive models are often compromised due to inherent biases in training data. The research seeks to understand these biases and proposes methods to mitigate their effects, thereby enhancing model reliability and performance.

Methods/Approach: We conducted an in-depth analysis of existing visual emotion datasets to identify prevalent bias patterns, such as overrepresentation of specific demographics or emotional states. To address these biases, we developed a novel bias-mitigation framework that employs data augmentation and re-weighting techniques. Additionally, we introduce a fairness-aware model architecture that adjusts learning based on detected biases during training.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework significantly reduces bias in visual emotion recognition models, leading to more equitable predictions across diverse demographic groups. Compared to baseline models, our approach achieved an improvement in accuracy and fairness metrics across multiple benchmark datasets. This highlights the effectiveness of our methods in overcoming dataset biases.

Conclusion/Implications: Our research contributes to the field by providing a clearer understanding of the biases inherent in visual emotion datasets and offering practical solutions to address them. The innovations presented in this paper have the potential to be applied in various applications requiring emotion recognition, such as human-computer interaction, surveillance, and media analysis, ensuring that systems deliver unbiased and fair outcomes. Keywords: visual emotion recognition, dataset bias, fairness, bias mitigation, emotion datasets, machine learning.
