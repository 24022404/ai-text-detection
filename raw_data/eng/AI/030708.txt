Complex networks, particularly those rich in triangles, play a critical role in modeling real-world systems such as social networks, biological interactions, and communication systems. While low-rank representations have been widely adopted for network analysis due to their computational efficiency and interpretability, their applicability to triangle-rich networks remains underexplored. This paper investigates the theoretical and practical limitations of low-rank representations in capturing the intricate triangle structures inherent in such networks. We demonstrate that low-rank approximations inherently fail to preserve the high-order connectivity patterns, such as triangles, which are essential for accurately modeling these networks. Through rigorous mathematical analysis and empirical evaluations on synthetic and real-world datasets, we show that low-rank methods consistently underperform in triangle-rich scenarios, leading to significant information loss and reduced predictive accuracy. Our findings highlight the fundamental trade-offs between dimensionality reduction and the preservation of higher-order network properties. This work contributes to the understanding of the limitations of low-rank representations and underscores the need for alternative approaches, such as higher-order network models, to effectively analyze triangle-rich complex networks.  

**Keywords**: complex networks, triangle-rich networks, low-rank representations, higher-order connectivity, network analysis, dimensionality reduction.
