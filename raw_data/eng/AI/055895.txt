Human pose estimation is a critical challenge in computer vision, involving the precise localization of body joints from visual data. This paper proposes a novel framework that jointly trains a convolutional neural network (CNN) and a graphical model to improve the accuracy and consistency of human pose estimation. The CNN is employed to extract robust, high-level image features and generate initial predictions of joint locations, while the graphical model encodes spatial relationships and anatomical constraints among body parts. By integrating these two components into a unified end-to-end learning process, the framework leverages both strong appearance-based evidence and structural priors, overcoming the limitations of treating them in isolation. Experiments conducted on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate that our joint training approach achieves superior performance compared to methods that train CNNs and graphical models separately, particularly in scenarios with severe occlusion and complex body articulations. The results highlight significant gains in localization accuracy and pose consistency. Our findings suggest that the joint optimization of feature extraction and structural reasoning is essential for advancing human pose estimation. This work has implications for applications in action recognition, human-computer interaction, and augmented reality. Key keywords: human pose estimation, convolutional neural network, graphical model, joint training, deep learning, structured prediction.
