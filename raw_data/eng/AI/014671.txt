The paper addresses the challenge of enhancing image resolution in resource-constrained environments by proposing a novel and efficient method for image super-resolution. High computational cost and memory requirements hinder many existing models, making them unsuitable for deployment in real-world applications with limited resources.

Methods/Approach: We introduce the Adaptive Weighted Learning Network (AWLN), a lightweight model designed to perform high-quality image super-resolution with reduced computational burden. The model leverages an adaptive weighting mechanism to dynamically adjust the influence of different features during the learning process. This approach enhances the capability of the network to focus on critical image features while maintaining computational efficiency.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the AWLN achieves superior results compared to existing lightweight super-resolution models. Our network consistently outperforms in both qualitative and quantitative assessments across multiple benchmark datasets, achieving competitive peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM) values. Moreover, the model requires significantly fewer parameters and computational resources, confirming its suitability for deployment on edge devices.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed AWLN provides a significant step forward in the field of image super-resolution by combining effectiveness and efficiency. Its adaptive learning strategy not only ensures high-quality image enhancement but also enables practical application in environments with limited computing capabilities. The research contributes to broader applications in mobile technology, remote sensing, and real-time video processing, where lightweight and efficient models are essential.

Keywords: Image Super-Resolution, Lightweight Model, Adaptive Weighted Learning, Computational Efficiency, Edge Devices.
