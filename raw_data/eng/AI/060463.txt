Distributed learning has become a cornerstone for training large-scale machine learning models, yet communication efficiency remains a critical bottleneck. This paper addresses the challenge of reducing communication overhead in distributed learning systems without compromising model accuracy. We propose a novel approach that eliminates the need for error feedback, a common technique used to mitigate the impact of gradient compression in distributed settings. Our method leverages a combination of adaptive quantization and dynamic gradient aggregation to achieve communication efficiency while maintaining convergence guarantees. Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, we demonstrate that our approach significantly reduces communication costs by up to 40% compared to state-of-the-art error feedback methods, while achieving comparable or superior model performance. Furthermore, our solution is scalable and compatible with various distributed learning frameworks. This work provides a practical and theoretically grounded alternative to error feedback, paving the way for more efficient distributed learning systems in resource-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: distributed learning, communication efficiency, gradient compression, adaptive quantization, error feedback, machine learning.
