The objective of this research is to address the challenges in automated gastric histopathology image classification, which is critical for accurate cancer diagnosis and treatment planning. Traditional methods often struggle with the complexity and variability of histopathological features in gastric tissues.

Methods/Approach: We propose GasHis-Transformer, a novel multi-scale visual transformer model designed to enhance the classification of gastric histopathology images. Our approach leverages the power of transformer architectures, specifically tailored for capturing both local and global features across different scales in histopathological imagery. The model incorporates a multi-scale attention mechanism that enables it to effectively account for varying levels of detail and context, thereby improving overall classification accuracy.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that GasHis-Transformer outperforms existing models in several key metrics, including precision, recall, and F1-score, on benchmark datasets. The model exhibits superior capability in distinguishing between different classes of gastric tissue anomalies, showcasing its effectiveness and reliability. Furthermore, comparative analysis indicates that our approach significantly reduces misclassification rates, setting a new benchmark in gastric histopathology image analysis.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of GasHis-Transformer offers substantial advancements in the field of medical image classification, providing a tool that could revolutionize the way gastric histopathology is approached. By facilitating more precise and efficient diagnostic processes, our model holds potential applications in clinical settings, contributing to improved patient outcomes and streamlined pathological workflows. The multi-scale visual transformer framework presents a promising direction for future research in medical imaging and beyond.

Keywords: gastric histopathology, image classification, visual transformer, multi-scale attention, medical imaging, cancer diagnosis.
