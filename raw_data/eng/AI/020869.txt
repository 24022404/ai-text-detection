This paper proposes a novel approach to learning visual relations using graph networks in a weakly-supervised setting. The objective is to develop a model that can effectively capture complex relationships between objects in images without requiring large amounts of labeled training data. Our method leverages explanation-based techniques to identify relevant visual features and relations, which are then represented as a graph network. This approach enables the model to learn from weak supervision signals, such as image-level labels, and to generalize to unseen relations. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods on benchmark datasets. The key findings include improved relation detection accuracy and enhanced model interpretability. Our research contributes to the development of more efficient and effective visual relation learning methods, with potential applications in image understanding, scene graph generation, and visual question answering. Key keywords: weakly-supervised learning, visual relations, graph networks, explanation-based learning, computer vision.
