Visual object tracking remains a critical challenge in computer vision, particularly in scenarios with dynamic environments and occlusions. This paper introduces a novel approach, *Adaptive Decontamination of the Training Set (ADTS)*, which addresses the limitations of discriminative visual tracking methods caused by contaminated training data. The proposed framework provides a unified formulation to dynamically refine the training set by identifying and mitigating the impact of corrupted samples, such as those resulting from occlusions or misalignments. ADTS leverages an adaptive weighting mechanism to prioritize reliable samples while suppressing noisy or misleading data, ensuring robust model updates during tracking. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that ADTS significantly improves tracking accuracy and robustness compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly in challenging scenarios. The results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed approach in maintaining tracking performance under adverse conditions. This work contributes a scalable and generalizable solution for enhancing discriminative visual tracking, with potential applications in surveillance, autonomous systems, and human-computer interaction.  

**Keywords**: Visual tracking, discriminative models, training set decontamination, adaptive weighting, object tracking, computer vision.
