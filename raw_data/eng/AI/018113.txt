The presence of snow in images can severely affect image processing and computer vision tasks, leading to degradation in performance for applications such as autonomous driving, surveillance, and outdoor scene analysis. This paper introduces "DesnowNet," a novel context-aware deep network designed specifically for the task of automatic snow removal from images.

Methods/Approach: DesnowNet leverages a deep learning architecture that integrates context-aware mechanisms to intelligently distinguish between snow and the underlying scene. The network architecture incorporates convolutional layers alongside attention modules that enable the model to focus dynamically on snow-affected regions while preserving important details of the image. Our approach involves training the model on a large dataset comprising diverse snowy scenes to ensure robustness across various environments and snow densities.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that DesnowNet outperforms existing snow removal methods by achieving higher quality image restoration with improved visual clarity and scene recognition accuracy. Quantitative metrics indicate a significant performance increase in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSI), showcasing the network's capability in effectively eliminating snow artifacts while maintaining the integrity of the scene.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of DesnowNet represents a significant advancement in the field of image processing for challenging weather conditions. By providing a reliable tool for snow removal, this research facilitates improved performance in computer vision tasks under snowy conditions. The effectiveness and adaptability of DesnowNet suggest potential applications in real-time image processing systems employed in autonomous vehicles, surveillance technology, and augmented reality systems. Key Keywords: snow removal, deep learning, image processing, autonomous driving, context-aware network.
