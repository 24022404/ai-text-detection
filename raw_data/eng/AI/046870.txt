Object detection systems powered by deep learning have become integral to applications such as autonomous vehicles, surveillance, and robotics. However, their dependence on contextual and visual cues makes them susceptible to novel adversarial threats. This paper introduces Contextual Camouflage Attack (CCA), a new paradigm of attack that subtly manipulates the surrounding context of target objects to impair object detection performance without altering the objects themselves. We systematically explore the CCA methodology, leveraging generative techniques and contextual perturbations to design imperceptible modifications in various background environments. Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets and state-of-the-art object detection frameworks, including YOLO and Faster R-CNN, we demonstrate that CCA significantly reduces detection accuracy, outperforming traditional pixel-wise and adversarial patch attacks in stealth and effectiveness. The results reveal that contextual integrity is a critical yet overlooked vulnerability in current models. Our findings highlight the necessity for object detectors to better understand contextual semantics and inspire future research on robustifying detection systems against advanced environmental manipulations. Keywords: object detection, adversarial attacks, contextual camouflage, deep learning, security, computer vision.
