This paper addresses the critical issue of anomaly detection in Internet of Things (IoT) environments, particularly in hierarchical edge computing architectures. The objective is to develop an adaptive anomaly detection system that can efficiently identify and respond to anomalies in real-time, while minimizing false positives and negatives. To achieve this, we propose a novel contextual-bandit approach that leverages the concept of edge computing to enable distributed and autonomous decision-making. Our method utilizes a hierarchical framework, where edge devices and servers collaborate to detect anomalies, and a contextual-bandit algorithm is employed to optimize the detection strategy. The results show that our approach outperforms traditional anomaly detection methods, achieving higher accuracy and lower latency. The key contributions of this research include the development of a scalable and adaptive anomaly detection system, the application of contextual-bandit theory to IoT anomaly detection, and the demonstration of the effectiveness of hierarchical edge computing in improving detection performance. This study has significant implications for the security and reliability of IoT systems, and its findings can be applied to various IoT applications, including smart cities, industrial automation, and healthcare monitoring. Key keywords: anomaly detection, Internet of Things, edge computing, contextual-bandit, adaptive learning, IoT security.
