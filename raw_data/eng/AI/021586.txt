This paper introduces a novel unsupervised approach for decomposing complex 3D scenes into individual objects via volume segmentation. The method addresses the challenge of identifying and separating distinct objects in 3D data without relying on manual annotations or predefined object categories. We propose a volumetric deep learning architecture that learns to segment 3D scenes based on geometric and structural features, employing a contrastive learning framework to encourage feature separation between different objects. The approach utilizes a 3D convolutional neural network to extract volumetric features, followed by a clustering algorithm to group voxels belonging to the same object. Evaluation on benchmark datasets demonstrates that our unsupervised method achieves competitive segmentation performance compared to existing supervised and weakly-supervised techniques. Furthermore, we show that the learned object segmentations can be used to improve downstream tasks such as object recognition and scene understanding. The proposed method offers a promising avenue for automated 3D scene analysis and reconstruction, with potential applications in robotics, augmented reality, and computer-aided design.

**Keywords:** 3D scene understanding, unsupervised learning, volume segmentation, deep learning, contrastive learning, object decomposition.
