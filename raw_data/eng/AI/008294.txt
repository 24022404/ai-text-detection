This paper investigates the sample complexity of episodic fixed-horizon reinforcement learning (RL), focusing on the theoretical guarantees required for efficient learning in finite-horizon Markov decision processes (MDPs). We address the fundamental question of how many samples are necessary to learn a near-optimal policy in this setting. To this end, we propose a novel algorithm that leverages value iteration with optimistic exploration to balance exploration and exploitation effectively. Our approach is analyzed under the assumption of a generative model, where the agent can sample transitions from any state-action pair. We derive upper bounds on the sample complexity, demonstrating that our algorithm achieves a near-optimal regret bound that scales polynomially with the horizon and state-action space size. Furthermore, we provide a matching lower bound, establishing the minimax optimality of our results. The findings highlight the interplay between horizon length, state-action space complexity, and exploration strategies in episodic RL. This work contributes to the theoretical understanding of RL by providing a rigorous framework for analyzing sample efficiency in fixed-horizon settings, with implications for practical applications in robotics, gaming, and autonomous systems.  

**Keywords**: reinforcement learning, sample complexity, episodic RL, Markov decision processes, exploration-exploitation, regret bounds.
