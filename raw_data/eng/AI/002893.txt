This study investigates graph-based methodologies for semi-supervised time series classification, addressing the challenge of efficiently classifying time series data when labeled instances are scarce. The research seeks to evaluate the effectiveness of graph-based approaches in capturing temporal dependencies and enhancing classification accuracy.

Methods/Approach: We employed various graph-based models, including graph convolutional networks (GCNs) and graph attention networks (GATs), to leverage the underlying temporal relationships inherent in time series data. These models were integrated into a semi-supervised learning framework, allowing for the utilization of both labeled and unlabeled data. The graph structures were constructed to represent similarity and adjacency between time series instances, facilitating the propagation of label information across the dataset.

Results/Findings: Our empirical evaluation demonstrates that graph-based approaches significantly improve classification performance over traditional methods in semi-supervised time series contexts. Compared to baseline techniques such as k-nearest neighbors and support vector machines, graph-based models exhibited superior accuracy, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. The results highlight the capability of these techniques to harness temporal patterns and inter-instance relationships for more informed decision-making.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the growing field of time series analysis by showcasing the potential of graph-based models in semi-supervised settings. The findings underscore the effectiveness of graph structures in enhancing classification outcomes and offer insights into their broader applicability across various domains such as finance, healthcare, and environmental monitoring. Future work could extend this framework to other types of data and explore the integration of advanced graph neural network architectures. 

Keywords: graph-based approaches, semi-supervised learning, time series classification, GCN, GAT, temporal dependencies.
