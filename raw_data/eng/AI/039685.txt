Video-based person re-identification (Re-ID) is a critical task in surveillance and security systems, aiming to match individuals across non-overlapping camera views. Despite significant progress, existing methods often struggle with spatial and temporal inefficiencies, limiting their scalability and real-world applicability. This paper introduces a Spatially and Temporally Efficient Non-local Attention Network (STENAN) to address these challenges. The proposed framework leverages a novel non-local attention mechanism that efficiently captures long-range dependencies in both spatial and temporal dimensions, enabling robust feature extraction from video sequences. By integrating lightweight attention modules and hierarchical temporal modeling, STENAN significantly reduces computational overhead while maintaining high accuracy. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including MARS and DukeMTMC-VideoReID, demonstrate that STENAN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both performance and efficiency. Key results show a 5% improvement in mean average precision (mAP) and a 30% reduction in inference time compared to existing approaches. The proposed method not only advances the field of video-based Re-ID but also offers practical insights for deploying efficient deep learning models in resource-constrained environments. Keywords: person re-identification, non-local attention, video analysis, deep learning, spatiotemporal modeling.
