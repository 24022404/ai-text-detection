This paper addresses the challenge of goal-oriented visual dialog, where an agent must infer the goal of a questioner through a sequence of visual and textual interactions. We propose an information-theoretic framework, *Answerer in Questioner's Mind* (AQM), which models the questioner's intent by leveraging mutual information between visual observations and dialog history. The AQM approach enables the answering agent to predict the questioner's goal efficiently by minimizing uncertainty in the dialog process. Our method integrates a deep learning-based encoder-decoder architecture with a probabilistic reasoning module to dynamically update belief states about the questioner's intent. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that AQM outperforms existing methods in goal inference accuracy and dialog efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art performance. The results highlight the effectiveness of information-theoretic principles in reducing ambiguity and improving communication in visual dialog systems. This work contributes to advancing goal-oriented AI systems, with potential applications in human-computer interaction, assistive technologies, and interactive visual search engines.  

**Keywords**: goal-oriented dialog, visual dialog, information theory, mutual information, deep learning, human-computer interaction.
