**Abstract:**

Video-based person re-identification (re-ID) plays a vital role in intelligent surveillance and security systems, yet it remains challenging due to varying illumination, occlusions, and complex motions across frames. This paper proposes a novel Multi-Granularity Reference-Aided Attentive Feature Aggregation (MG-RAAFA) framework to address these challenges. The objective of our research is to enhance the robustness and accuracy of person re-ID by effectively aggregating discriminative features across multiple temporal and spatial scales. Our method employs a multi-granularity feature extraction backbone, which captures both coarse and fine-grained visual cues from video sequences. An attentive aggregation module, guided by dynamically selected reference frames, adaptively weights frame-level features by considering both local frame importance and holistic sequence context. Extensive experiments conducted on benchmark video-based person re-ID datasets demonstrate that our MG-RAAFA framework outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving significant improvements in Rank-1 accuracy and mean Average Precision (mAP). The results reveal our model’s capability to maintain discriminative power even in challenging surveillance scenarios with occlusions and background clutter. In conclusion, the proposed approach offers a robust and scalable solution for video-based person re-identification, with potential applications in real-time surveillance, smart city systems, and public safety monitoring. Key innovations include the introduction of dynamic reference-aided attention and multi-granularity feature modeling, which together substantially advance video-based person re-ID performance. 

**Keywords:** person re-identification, video-based re-ID, multi-granularity features, attentive aggregation, reference frames, deep learning, surveillance.
