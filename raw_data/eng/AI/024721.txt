The aim of this research is to address the challenge of forecasting in multivariate time series data that are irregularly sampled and contain missing values. Such data scenarios are common in real-world applications like finance and healthcare, where regular sampling is often disrupted, and complete data acquisition is difficult.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel forecasting methodology designed specifically for handling multivariate time series characterized by irregular intervals and missing data points. Our approach leverages advanced imputation techniques integrated with state-of-the-art machine learning models capable of adaptive learning and temporal context awareness. The proposed model utilizes recurrent neural networks (RNNs) with specialized architectures to handle irregular sampling patterns effectively.

Results/Findings: The experimental evaluation demonstrates that our method significantly improves forecasting accuracy in comparison to traditional methods and existing models not tailored for irregular sampling. Through comprehensive testing on publicly available datasets from various domains, including finance and healthcare, our approach consistently outperforms benchmarking strategies, achieving superior handling of missing values and improved temporal predictions.

Conclusion/Implications: This research presents an innovative solution for forecasting in challenging multivariate time series data environments, contributing to the fields of time series analysis and machine learning. The proposed model not only enhances prediction accuracy but also offers potential applications in sectors that routinely deal with incomplete and irregularly sampled data. Our findings underscore the importance of accommodating irregularities and missing data in forecasting models, paving the way for more reliable analytics in applicable domains.

Keywords: multivariate time series, irregular sampling, missing values, forecasting, machine learning, recurrent neural networks, imputation techniques.
