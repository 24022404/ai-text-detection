This paper explores the multiview differential geometry of curves, addressing the challenge of analyzing and understanding complex curves from multiple viewpoints. Our objective is to develop a comprehensive framework for describing the geometric properties of curves in a multiview setting, enabling the extraction of meaningful features and patterns. We employ a novel approach that combines differential geometry and multiview geometry, utilizing tools such as curvature, torsion, and Frenet-Serret frames to characterize the intrinsic and extrinsic properties of curves. Our method allows for the computation of multiview curve descriptors, which capture the geometric relationships between different views of a curve. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in various applications, including curve matching, registration, and reconstruction. The key findings of this research contribute to the development of more robust and accurate curve analysis techniques, with potential applications in computer vision, graphics, and engineering. Our work highlights the importance of considering multiple viewpoints in curve analysis and provides a foundation for future research in multiview geometry and differential geometry, with relevant keywords including differential geometry, multiview geometry, curve analysis, and computer vision.
