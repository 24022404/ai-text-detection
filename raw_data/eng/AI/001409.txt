This paper reexamines the sampling process in 3D point cloud generative adversarial networks (GANs), addressing the long-standing issue of inefficient and biased point cloud generation. Our objective is to develop a novel sampling strategy that enhances the quality and diversity of generated 3D point clouds. We propose a hierarchical sampling approach that leverages a combination of deterministic and stochastic methods to capture complex geometric structures and spatial relationships. Our method utilizes a multi-resolution framework, allowing for efficient exploration of the point cloud distribution. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in generated point cloud quality, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy, completeness, and visual fidelity. The proposed approach enables more effective generation of diverse and realistic 3D point clouds, with potential applications in fields such as computer-aided design, robotics, and virtual reality. Key contributions include a novel sampling strategy, improved point cloud generation quality, and enhanced diversity, making this work relevant to researchers and practitioners in the areas of 3D reconstruction, generative models, and geometric deep learning.
