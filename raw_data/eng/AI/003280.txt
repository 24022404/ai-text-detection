This paper introduces a novel approach to panoptic image segmentation by presenting the Generator Evaluator-Selector Net (GES Net), designed to effectively segment entire images and deconstruct unfamiliar objects into constituent parts. The study addresses the challenge of accurately separating known and unknown object classes in diverse visual environments.

Methods/Approach: The proposed GES Net integrates three primary components: a generator that produces initial segmentation maps, an evaluator that assesses the quality and coherence of these segmentations, and a selector that refines the output by splitting novel objects into identifiable segments. The model leverages advanced deep learning architectures, employing convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for detailed feature extraction, alongside attention mechanisms to enhance focus on complex image regions.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the GES Net outperforms existing panoptic segmentation models on multiple benchmark datasets, achieving superior accuracy in both segmentation quality and the ability to handle previously unseen objects. The system demonstrates a marked improvement in distinguishing between object parts, showing a significant leap in generalization capabilities over state-of-the-art methods.

Conclusion/Implications: The GES Net represents a significant advancement in panoptic image segmentation, offering improved segmentation accuracy and adaptability to unknown objects. Its ability to deconstruct unfamiliar shapes into meaningful parts holds potential for applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and image analysis systems where understanding complex environments is crucial. This research contributes a novel methodology for enhancing segmentation systems' versatility and robustness in real-world scenarios.

Keywords: panoptic segmentation, image segmentation, object recognition, deep learning, neural networks, attention mechanisms, GES Net.
