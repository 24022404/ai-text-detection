This paper introduces a novel generative model for robust 3D facial pose tracking from depth images, explicitly addressing the challenges posed by self-occlusion. The proposed Visibility Constrained Generative Model (VCGM) incorporates a visibility estimation module within a generative adversarial network (GAN) framework. This allows the model to learn a latent representation of 3D facial poses while simultaneously predicting visible facial landmarks in the depth image. By conditioning the pose estimation on the estimated visibility, the VCGM demonstrates improved accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with significant occlusions. We evaluate the VCGM on both synthetic and real-world depth datasets, showing that it outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of pose estimation error and tracking stability. The VCGM's ability to handle visibility variations contributes to more reliable and accurate 3D facial pose tracking for applications such as human-computer interaction and facial animation. Keywords: 3D Facial Pose Tracking, Depth Imaging, Generative Adversarial Networks, Visibility Estimation, Self-Occlusion.
