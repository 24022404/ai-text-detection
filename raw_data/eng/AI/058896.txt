Smart contracts, fundamentally, are self-executing agreements digitally encoded to autonomously enforce and fulfill the terms set by their parties, eliminating the need for intermediaries and promoting a secure, decentralized transaction environment. Pioneered in concept by Nick Szabo during the 1990s, the widespread growth of smart contracts began with Ethereum’s introduction in 2015, which uniquely facilitated the development of intricate smart contracts and a broad range of decentralized applications (DApps). What truly sets smart contracts apart is their immutability—once deployed, their code cannot be altered, ensuring the integrity of the agreement—and their transparency, as all interactions and conditions are recorded and visible on the blockchain. These characteristics, combined with the ability to automate complex business processes, create new possibilities for improving operational efficiency and significantly reducing reliance on costly intermediaries. As a result, industries such as finance, supply chain, real estate, insurance, and more are actively exploring and implementing smart contracts to enhance transparency, accelerate settlement times, boost security, and ultimately, reduce administrative overheads while fostering trustless transactions in an increasingly digital world.
