This paper presents a novel approach to generating procedural videos for training deep action recognition networks. The objective is to address the scarcity of annotated video data, a major bottleneck in the development of accurate action recognition models. Our method utilizes a combination of computer-generated imagery and machine learning algorithms to create a large dataset of synthetic videos, which can be used to augment existing real-world datasets. The procedural generation approach allows for the creation of diverse and realistic video sequences, enabling the training of more robust and generalizable action recognition models. Experimental results demonstrate that our generated videos can significantly improve the performance of deep action recognition networks, particularly in scenarios where real-world data is limited. The key contributions of this research include the development of a scalable and efficient video generation pipeline, as well as the demonstration of the effectiveness of synthetic data in improving action recognition accuracy. Our approach has important implications for applications such as surveillance, human-computer interaction, and autonomous systems, where accurate action recognition is crucial. Key keywords: procedural generation, video synthesis, deep learning, action recognition, computer vision.
