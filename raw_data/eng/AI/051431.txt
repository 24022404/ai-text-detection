Self-attention mechanisms, critical for processing long sequences in transformers, suffer from quadratic computational complexity. The Nyströmformer offers a linear approximation but can struggle with complex data distributions. This paper introduces a novel approach, Spectral Shifting, to approximate self-attention. Instead of relying on landmark selection as in Nyström methods, Spectral Shifting leverages spectral properties of the attention matrix to identify and emphasize its dominant components through a frequency shift operation. This allows for a more accurate low-rank approximation, capturing finer-grained dependencies within the sequence. We demonstrate, both theoretically and empirically, that Spectral Shifting provides a tighter approximation error bound compared to the Nyströmformer. Experiments on benchmark datasets for long-range sequence modeling, including text and time series, show that our method achieves comparable or superior performance with reduced computational cost. The findings suggest Spectral Shifting offers a promising alternative for efficient self-attention approximation, enabling transformers to process even longer sequences effectively. Keywords: Self-Attention, Transformer, Nyström Method, Spectral Analysis, Low-Rank Approximation, Long Sequence Modeling.
