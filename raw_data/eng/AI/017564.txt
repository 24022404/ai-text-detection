Video Scene Graph Generation (Video SGG) aims to represent video content as structured graphs, facilitating high-level video understanding. Current approaches often struggle to adapt context aggregation strategies effectively to different targets (subjects, objects, predicates) within the scene graph. This paper introduces a Target Adaptive Context Aggregation (TACA) framework designed to dynamically adjust the context aggregation process based on the specific target being considered. TACA employs a target-aware attention mechanism to selectively aggregate relevant contextual information from both spatial and temporal domains. Furthermore, we introduce a novel graph propagation module that refines node and edge representations by iteratively exchanging information within the graph structure. Experiments on the Action Genome dataset demonstrate that TACA significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in video SGG, achieving improvements in both predicate and relation prediction accuracy. The proposed framework enables more accurate and contextually informed video understanding, offering potential benefits for applications such as video retrieval, activity recognition, and video captioning.
Keywords: Video Scene Graph Generation, Context Aggregation, Attention Mechanism, Graph Neural Networks, Video Understanding.
