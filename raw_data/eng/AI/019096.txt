This paper introduces the Natural Wake-Sleep Algorithm, a novel approach to unsupervised learning that leverages the strengths of both wake-sleep and contrastive divergence methods. The objective is to develop an efficient and scalable algorithm for training deep generative models, particularly in scenarios where labeled data is scarce. Our approach employs a hybrid wake-sleep framework, combining the benefits of wake-sleep and contrastive divergence to improve model convergence and stability. The algorithm is based on a natural gradient descent method, which adaptively adjusts the learning rate to optimize the model's evidence lower bound. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the Natural Wake-Sleep Algorithm in training deep generative models, outperforming existing wake-sleep and contrastive divergence methods in terms of model accuracy and training efficiency. The key contributions of this research include the development of a novel wake-sleep algorithm, the introduction of a natural gradient descent method, and the demonstration of improved model performance in unsupervised learning tasks. The Natural Wake-Sleep Algorithm has significant implications for applications in artificial intelligence, machine learning, and deep learning, particularly in areas such as image and speech recognition, natural language processing, and generative modeling. Key keywords: wake-sleep algorithm, contrastive divergence, deep generative models, unsupervised learning, natural gradient descent.
