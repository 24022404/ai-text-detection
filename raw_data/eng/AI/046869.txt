Iris recognition systems are vulnerable to presentation attacks (PAs) using printed iris images, contact lenses, or textured surfaces, creating a need for robust presentation attack detection (PAD). This paper introduces D-NetPAD, a novel deep learning-based iris PAD framework designed for enhanced explainability and interpretability. D-NetPAD employs a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture coupled with Grad-CAM visualization to identify and highlight regions of interest indicative of presentation attacks. The network is trained on a diverse dataset of real and spoofed iris images, enabling it to learn subtle differences between genuine and attack presentations. Experimental results demonstrate that D-NetPAD achieves state-of-the-art performance in detecting various types of iris PAs, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and generalization ability. Furthermore, the integrated Grad-CAM visualization provides interpretable insights into the network's decision-making process, enhancing trust and facilitating the diagnosis of potential vulnerabilities. D-NetPAD offers a practical and explainable solution for securing iris recognition systems against presentation attacks, contributing to more reliable and trustworthy biometric authentication.

Keywords: Iris Recognition, Presentation Attack Detection, Deep Learning, Explainable AI, Grad-CAM, Biometrics.
