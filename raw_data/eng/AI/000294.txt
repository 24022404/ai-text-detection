Graph neural networks (GNNs) have emerged as a key technology for processing structured data, yet their expressive power in capturing complex graph structures remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces a novel class of graph networks with provably powerful representational capabilities, bridging the gap between theoretical limits and practical graph learning tasks. We formalize new architectures that extend the standard message-passing framework by incorporating higher-order interactions and carefully designed aggregation functions, enabling the proposed models to distinguish between intricate graph topologies that previous GNNs often conflate. Through rigorous theoretical analysis, we establish conditions under which our networks match or surpass the discriminative power of established graph isomorphism tests. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets for node classification, graph classification, and chemical property prediction demonstrate superior accuracy and robustness over state-of-the-art graph neural models. Our results provide critical insights into the design of expressive graph architectures and affirm the potential for these provably powerful graph networks in broad applications across machine learning, data mining, and computational biology. Keywords: graph neural networks, expressive power, graph isomorphism, deep learning, message passing, graph classification.
