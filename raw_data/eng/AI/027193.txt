This paper presents the Monash University, UEA, UCR Time Series Extrinsic Regression Archive, a comprehensive resource aimed at advancing research in the burgeoning field of time series regression. The archive addresses the need for standardized datasets that facilitate the development and benchmarking of new algorithms in time series analysis, specifically focusing on extrinsic regression tasks.

Methods/Approach: The archive compiles a diverse collection of datasets sourced from various real-world domains, each with distinct characteristics and complexities. These datasets are meticulously curated to cover a wide range of applications, ensuring their utility in developing robust regression models. The paper outlines the methodology for dataset selection, preprocessing techniques employed, and the integration of metadata that aids researchers in selecting appropriate data for their experimental needs.

Results/Findings: Key findings from the initial analysis of the archive demonstrate its potential to challenge existing models and encourage innovation in time series regression. Benchmark experiments conducted using several state-of-the-art regression algorithms reveal insights into model performance across different types of data, highlighting areas where current methods excel and where further improvement is needed.

Conclusion/Implications: The Monash University, UEA, UCR Time Series Extrinsic Regression Archive represents a critical contribution to the time series analysis community, providing a standardized platform for evaluating and comparing regression models. By facilitating more transparent and reproducible research, the archive encourages the development of more effective algorithms and methodologies. Its broad applicability offers researchers a foundation to explore novel approaches in domains from finance to healthcare, emphasizing the archive’s potential to impact both academic and industrial advancements. Keywords include time series regression, dataset standardization, model benchmarking, and extrinsic regression tasks.
