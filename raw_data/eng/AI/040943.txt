Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have demonstrated remarkable success in generating high-quality images. However, controlling the generation process to achieve specific desired attributes remains a challenge. This paper investigates the "steerability" of GANs, defined as the ability to manipulate the generated output by directly modifying latent space vectors to control image attributes. We propose a novel approach that combines unsupervised discovery of semantic directions in the GAN's latent space with a regularized optimization technique to enhance the precision and consistency of attribute manipulation.  Our method identifies meaningful directions corresponding to interpretable image features without requiring explicit labels. We evaluate our approach on several GAN architectures and datasets, demonstrating its superior performance in controlled attribute editing compared to existing methods. Quantitative results show improved attribute manipulation accuracy and reduced unintended artifacts. This work contributes a practical framework for enhancing the steerability of GANs, enabling finer-grained control over image generation and facilitating applications in image editing and content creation. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, steerability, latent space, image generation, attribute manipulation.
