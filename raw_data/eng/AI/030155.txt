Image segmentation in the presence of intensity inhomogeneity presents significant challenges in diverse applications, ranging from medical imaging to computer vision. This paper introduces a novel local active contour model designed to effectively address these challenges by accurately segmenting images with non-uniform intensity distributions.

Methods/Approach: The proposed model integrates a local region-based active contour approach that adapts to intensity variations within the image. Our methodology employs an energy minimization framework that leverages local image statistics, allowing for the precise adaptation of contour evolution to local intensity inhomogeneity. This approach contrasts with traditional models that often assume uniform intensity within image regions, thus reducing accuracy in inhomogeneous conditions.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed local active contour model significantly improves segmentation accuracy in cases with pronounced intensity inhomogeneity compared to existing methods. The evaluation was conducted on a diverse dataset, including synthetic and real-world images, showcasing the model's robustness and precision. Key findings highlight substantial performance gains in segmentation quality, with enhanced delineation of object boundaries and reduced segmentation errors.

Conclusion/Implications: The research provides a significant contribution to the field of image segmentation by introducing a local active contour model that effectively handles intensity inhomogeneity. This advancement has potential applications in areas requiring precise image analysis, such as medical diagnostics and automated surveillance systems. The model's adaptability to local intensity variations suggests its suitability for integration into advanced image processing pipelines, offering improved results where accurate segmentation is crucial.

Keywords: Image segmentation, intensity inhomogeneity, local active contour model, energy minimization, image analysis.
