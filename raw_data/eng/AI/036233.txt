This paper addresses the challenge of ranking items when each item is described by a set of features, a problem common in information retrieval, recommendation systems, and machine learning. We propose a novel algorithmic framework that integrates item features into the ranking process, moving beyond traditional methods that consider only item-to-item comparisons or pairwise preferences. Our approach constructs a feature-induced weighted graph, representing the relationships among items through their feature similarities and associated scores, and formulates the ranking problem as a graph-theoretic optimization task. Through comprehensive analysis, we examine the structural properties of the resulting graph, including connectivity and spectral characteristics, to provide theoretical insights into the stability and robustness of the generated rankings. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our algorithm outperforms standard ranking methods in terms of accuracy, scalability, and adaptability to noisy or incomplete feature information. Our findings highlight the effectiveness of leveraging feature representations for improved ranking quality and open avenues for applications in domains such as search engines, recommender systems, and social network analysis. Keywords: ranking algorithms, feature-based ranking, graph theory, information retrieval, machine learning, spectral analysis.
