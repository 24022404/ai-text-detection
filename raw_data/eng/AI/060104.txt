This paper investigates the spectral underpinnings of the word2vec model, a widely used approach in natural language processing for learning word embeddings. The objective is to uncover the mathematical and structural foundations that enable word2vec to capture semantic relationships between words effectively. We analyze the model through the lens of spectral graph theory, demonstrating that word2vec implicitly performs a low-rank factorization of a shifted positive pointwise mutual information (PPMI) matrix. This perspective provides a deeper understanding of how word2vec leverages co-occurrence statistics to generate meaningful vector representations. Our findings reveal that the model’s success in capturing semantic and syntactic relationships can be attributed to its ability to approximate the spectral properties of word-context matrices. Furthermore, we propose a simplified framework based on spectral decomposition that achieves comparable performance to traditional word2vec implementations while offering greater interpretability. The results highlight the importance of spectral methods in understanding and improving word embedding techniques, with potential applications in enhancing language models and downstream NLP tasks. This work contributes to the theoretical foundation of word embeddings and opens new avenues for optimizing embedding algorithms using spectral analysis.  

**Keywords**: word2vec, spectral graph theory, word embeddings, natural language processing, PPMI, low-rank factorization.
