Driving style analysis is crucial for enhancing road safety and developing personalized Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS). This paper introduces a novel approach to driving style analysis by decomposing driving behavior into primitive driving patterns. We employ Bayesian nonparametric methods, specifically the Hierarchical Dirichlet Process Hidden Markov Model (HDP-HMM), to automatically discover and model these patterns from multivariate time-series driving data without predefining the number of states. The HDP-HMM captures the temporal dependencies and variability inherent in driving maneuvers. We evaluate our approach on a real-world driving dataset, demonstrating its ability to identify distinct driving styles (e.g., aggressive, normal, and cautious) based on the learned primitive driving patterns. Compared to traditional methods, our approach offers improved accuracy and adaptability by automatically learning the underlying structure of driving behavior. The identified driving styles can be used to personalize ADAS, improve driver feedback systems, and enhance insurance risk assessment.
Keywords: Driving Style Analysis, Bayesian Nonparametrics, Hidden Markov Model, Hierarchical Dirichlet Process, Primitive Driving Patterns, ADAS.
