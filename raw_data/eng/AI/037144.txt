This paper presents a novel approach to human action generation using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). The objective is to synthesize realistic human actions, such as walking, running, and jumping, by leveraging the capabilities of GANs in generating coherent and context-specific sequences. Our method employs a two-stage framework, consisting of a generator network that produces action sequences and a discriminator network that evaluates the realism of the generated actions. The generator is trained using a combination of adversarial loss and reconstruction loss, enabling it to learn the underlying patterns and structures of human actions. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in generating diverse and realistic human actions, outperforming existing methods in terms of action quality and diversity. The proposed framework has significant implications for applications such as human-computer interaction, robotics, and computer vision, and contributes to the development of more sophisticated and human-like action generation systems. Key contributions include the introduction of a novel GAN-based architecture for human action generation and the demonstration of its potential in generating realistic and diverse actions. Relevant keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, Human Action Generation, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, Robotics.
