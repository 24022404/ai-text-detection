Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful technique for learning representations from unlabeled data. This paper introduces a novel self-supervised learning framework leveraging local attention mechanisms to enhance feature extraction. Our approach, Local Attention-Aware Feature Learning (LAAFL), focuses on capturing fine-grained contextual information within local regions of the input data. Specifically, LAAFL employs a multi-head attention module to identify and emphasize salient local features, followed by a contrastive learning objective to promote feature invariance. We evaluate LAAFL on benchmark datasets for image classification and object detection. Experimental results demonstrate that LAAFL significantly outperforms existing SSL methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance with improvements of up to 5% in downstream tasks. The learned representations exhibit improved robustness and generalization capabilities, suggesting the effectiveness of local attention in capturing meaningful and discriminative features. This work provides a promising direction for self-supervised learning by explicitly modeling local dependencies and attention mechanisms.

Keywords: Self-Supervised Learning, Attention Mechanism, Feature Representation, Contrastive Learning, Local Attention.
