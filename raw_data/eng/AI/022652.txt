This paper introduces LSM, a novel Learning Subspace Minimization framework tailored for low-level vision tasks such as image denoising, super-resolution, and inpainting. Traditional approaches in low-level vision rely heavily on handcrafted priors or deep networks that operate in high-dimensional spaces, often resulting in increased computational complexity and less interpretable features. LSM addresses these challenges by leveraging subspace learning techniques to model essential image structures in a lower-dimensional, task-specific subspace. Through an end-to-end trainable architecture, LSM jointly optimizes for subspace representation and task-specific restoration, enabling efficient minimization of image degradation artifacts. Empirical evaluations on standard benchmarks demonstrate that LSM outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods with notable improvements in peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity (SSIM), while significantly reducing model size and inference time. The results highlight LSM’s effectiveness in capturing underlying image manifolds and generalizing across various low-level vision problems. In conclusion, LSM offers a robust, interpretable, and computationally efficient alternative for low-level vision enhancement, paving the way for its integration into real-world image restoration and enhancement applications. Key keywords: subspace learning, minimization, low-level vision, image restoration, deep learning.
