This paper introduces D-NetPAD, a novel and explainable iris presentation attack detection (PAD) system designed to enhance security in biometric authentication. The increasing sophistication of presentation attacks, such as printed or synthetic iris images, necessitates robust and interpretable detection mechanisms. D-NetPAD leverages a deep learning-based framework combined with explainability techniques to identify and classify presentation attacks with high accuracy while providing insights into the decision-making process. The proposed approach integrates a dual-stream convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, which processes both texture and contextual features of iris images, enabling the system to distinguish between genuine and spoofed samples effectively. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that D-NetPAD achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of detection accuracy and robustness. Furthermore, the incorporation of explainability tools, such as gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM), allows for transparent and interpretable predictions, addressing the "black-box" nature of traditional deep learning models. This work contributes to the field of biometric security by offering a reliable, interpretable, and scalable solution for iris PAD, with potential applications in access control, identity verification, and fraud prevention systems.  

**Keywords**: Iris presentation attack detection, deep learning, explainable AI, biometric security, convolutional neural networks, Grad-CAM.
