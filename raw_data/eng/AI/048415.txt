This paper addresses the challenge of evaluating the consistency and effectiveness of semi-supervised learning algorithms on graph-based data structures. Specifically, we investigate the probit and one-hot encoding methods within the context of graph-based semi-supervised learning, aiming to enhance classification accuracy in scenarios with limited labeled data.

Methods/Approach: We employ two distinct approaches: the probit model and the one-hot encoding method, integrated into a semi-supervised learning framework on graph datasets. The probit model utilizes a probabilistic approach to infer labels on graph nodes, while the one-hot method employs categorical encoding for discrete label representation. Our methodology involves theoretical analysis and experimental validation over several benchmark graph datasets to assess the performance and consistency of these approaches.

Results/Findings: Our findings demonstrate that both the probit and one-hot methods exhibit high consistency and effectiveness in graph-based semi-supervised learning tasks. The probit model provides smoother transition characteristics and probabilistic interpretations of node labels, whereas the one-hot method improves classification performance by leveraging categorical encoding. Comparative analysis indicates that these methods outperform traditional fully supervised models in terms of accuracy, especially in sparse labeling conditions.

Conclusion/Implications: The study highlights the potential of probit and one-hot encoding methods to significantly improve graph-based semi-supervised learning applications. These approaches offer robust solutions for domains where label scarcity is a critical challenge, such as social network analysis and bioinformatics. The proposed methods advance the current state of semi-supervised learning by integrating probabilistic and categorical techniques, providing valuable contributions to the field of graph-based machine learning.

Keywords: Semi-supervised learning, graph algorithms, probit model, one-hot encoding, label inference, classification consistency.
