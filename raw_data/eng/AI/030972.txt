This paper delves into the fundamental mathematical principles underlying the word2vec algorithm, a widely used method for learning vector representations of words. The objective is to uncover the spectral properties that drive the performance of word2vec, shedding light on its ability to capture semantic relationships between words. Our approach involves analyzing the matrix factorization inherent in word2vec, revealing a profound connection to spectral decomposition techniques. The results show that the quality of the learned embeddings is intimately tied to the spectral characteristics of the input data, allowing for a more informed selection of hyperparameters and optimization strategies. Our findings contribute to a deeper understanding of word2vec's strengths and limitations, with implications for the development of more efficient and effective word representation models. Key contributions include the identification of spectral bottlenecks in word2vec and the proposal of novel, spectrally-aware training methods. This research has significant potential applications in natural language processing, information retrieval, and machine learning, particularly in the context of neural networks, language models, and text analysis. Relevant keywords: word2vec, spectral analysis, matrix factorization, natural language processing, language models.
