This paper addresses the challenge of accurately reconstructing and preserving cultural heritage artifacts by leveraging data fusion techniques. Given the diverse characteristics and limitations of individual imaging methods, the research aims to integrate data from laser scanning, structured light, and photogrammetry to enhance the fidelity and utility of digital reconstructions.

Methods/Approach: Our approach involves the simultaneous deployment of laser scanning, structured light, and photogrammetry to acquire detailed 3D data of cultural heritage objects. We developed a novel fusion algorithm that synthesizes these data sources, capitalizing on the strengths of each technique—high precision of laser scanning, detailed surface capture of structured light, and the photographic detail of photogrammetry. The integration process prioritizes data consistency, minimizing discrepancies between datasets through advanced registration and optimization techniques.

Results/Findings: The experimental results demonstrate that our data fusion method significantly improves the accuracy and visual quality of 3D reconstructions of cultural artifacts when compared to individual methods. Quantitative assessments show enhancements in spatial resolution and texture mapping, while qualitative evaluations confirm improved realism and artifact detail retention. Our approach outperforms traditional single-method captures in various controlled and real-world scenarios.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a powerful tool for digital preservation and analysis of cultural heritage, enabling more comprehensive documentation and virtual restoration processes. The implications extend to museum curation, academic study, and public education, with potential applications in virtual reality experiences and digital archiving. Our fusion methodology illustrates a scalable model for integrating diverse imaging technologies, paving the way for future innovations in heritage conservation.

Keywords: Data Fusion, Laser Scanning, Structured Light, Photogrammetry, Cultural Heritage, 3D Reconstruction, Digital Preservation.
