This paper addresses the challenge of generating relevant counter-examples from a positive unlabeled dataset to improve the robustness of image classification models. Our objective is to develop an approach that can effectively identify and generate counter-examples that are likely to be misclassified by a given model, thereby enhancing its performance and reliability. We propose a novel method that leverages a combination of generative models and adversarial attack techniques to generate counter-examples from a positive unlabeled dataset. Our approach utilizes a conditional generative adversarial network (CGAN) to generate new images that are similar to the positive examples but have different class labels. We then use these generated counter-examples to fine-tune the image classification model, resulting in improved robustness and accuracy. Our experiments demonstrate that the proposed method can significantly improve the performance of image classification models on various datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. The key contributions of this research include the development of a novel counter-example generation method and the demonstration of its effectiveness in improving the robustness of image classification models. Our approach has important implications for applications where robust image classification is critical, such as autonomous vehicles and medical diagnosis. Key keywords: image classification, counter-examples, generative models, adversarial attacks, robustness, CGAN, fine-tuning.
