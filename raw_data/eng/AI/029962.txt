Graph kernels offer a powerful approach to analyzing graph-structured data, but their computational complexity can be a limiting factor. This paper presents a unifying framework that elucidates the relationship between explicit and implicit feature map constructions for graph kernels. We demonstrate that several commonly used graph kernels, previously considered distinct, can be interpreted as instantiations of a single generalized feature map. This framework allows for the derivation of novel, efficient implementations of these kernels. Through theoretical analysis and empirical evaluation on benchmark graph classification datasets, we show that explicitly constructing feature maps based on our unified view can lead to significant speedups in computation time without sacrificing classification accuracy. Our findings provide a deeper understanding of graph kernel behavior and offer practical tools for scaling graph kernel methods to larger datasets. Keywords: Graph Kernels, Feature Maps, Graph Classification, Kernel Methods, Computational Efficiency.
