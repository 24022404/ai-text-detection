Human Activity Recognition (HAR) from RGB video data is a critical challenge in computer vision, with applications ranging from surveillance to healthcare. This paper addresses the problem of recognizing human activities by focusing on skeletal representations derived from RGB video frames, which provide a compact and robust representation of human motion. We propose a novel framework that leverages skeleton-based features to improve recognition accuracy while reducing computational complexity. Our approach integrates a lightweight pose estimation model to extract skeletal keypoints and employs a graph convolutional network (GCN) to model spatiotemporal relationships between joints. The proposed method is evaluated on benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to traditional RGB-based and hybrid approaches. Experimental results show that our skeleton-focused model achieves state-of-the-art accuracy while being computationally efficient, making it suitable for real-time applications. The findings highlight the effectiveness of skeleton-based representations in HAR and provide insights into the design of lightweight, interpretable models for activity recognition. This work contributes to advancing the field by offering a scalable and efficient solution for real-world deployment.  

**Keywords**: Human Activity Recognition, Skeleton-based Features, Pose Estimation, Graph Convolutional Networks, RGB Video, Spatiotemporal Modeling.
