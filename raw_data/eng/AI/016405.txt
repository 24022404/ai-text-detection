The increasing reliance on autonomous vehicles (AVs) for safe navigation necessitates robust object detection systems capable of withstanding adversarial attacks. This study investigates the resilience of AV object category detection systems to universal adversarial perturbations (UAPs), which are small, input-agnostic perturbations designed to deceive machine learning models. We evaluate the performance of state-of-the-art object detection models, including YOLOv5 and Faster R-CNN, under UAP attacks across diverse driving scenarios. A novel framework is proposed to generate UAPs tailored to AV-specific datasets, such as KITTI and BDD100K, and assess their impact on detection accuracy. Our experiments reveal that UAPs significantly degrade model performance, with misclassification rates increasing by up to 40% in critical object categories like pedestrians and vehicles. To address this vulnerability, we introduce a lightweight adversarial training method that enhances model robustness without compromising inference speed. Results demonstrate that our approach reduces UAP-induced errors by 60% while maintaining real-time detection capabilities. This research highlights the critical need for adversarial robustness in AV systems and provides a practical solution to mitigate the risks posed by UAPs, contributing to safer and more reliable autonomous driving technologies.  

**Keywords**: autonomous vehicles, object detection, universal adversarial perturbations, adversarial robustness, YOLOv5, Faster R-CNN, adversarial training.
