Deep learning has shown immense promise in medical image analysis, but its reliance on large, labeled datasets poses a significant challenge due to the scarcity and cost of expert annotations. This paper introduces a novel surrogate supervision framework to address this limitation, enabling effective training of deep learning models with limited labeled medical images. Our approach leverages readily available, inexpensive surrogate labels derived from alternative imaging modalities or automated pre-processing techniques to guide the learning process. We introduce a multi-stage training strategy that first pre-trains a model using surrogate labels, followed by fine-tuning with a small set of expert annotations. The proposed method is evaluated on multiple medical imaging tasks, including lesion segmentation and disease classification, using different imaging modalities such as CT and MRI. Experimental results demonstrate that our surrogate supervision framework significantly improves the performance of deep learning models compared to training solely on limited expert annotations. Specifically, we observe an average increase of 15% in Dice score for segmentation tasks and a 10% improvement in AUC for classification tasks. This research provides a practical and efficient solution for training accurate deep learning models for medical image analysis, even when only limited labeled data is available, thereby broadening the applicability of AI in healthcare. Keywords: Deep Learning, Medical Image Analysis, Surrogate Supervision, Limited Labeled Data, Segmentation, Classification.
