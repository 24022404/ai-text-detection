This paper investigates the application of metareasoning to dynamically optimize the configuration of modular software systems. We address the challenge of selecting the most appropriate module configurations at runtime, given varying environmental conditions and performance goals. Our approach utilizes reinforcement learning (RL) with a novel representation of contextual information, encompassing both system-level metrics and environmental factors. This rich contextual representation enables the RL agent to effectively learn optimal configuration policies tailored to specific situations. We implemented our approach and evaluated it on a simulated modular system, demonstrating significant improvements in performance compared to static configurations and baseline RL methods with simpler contextual representations. The results show that our metareasoning framework allows software systems to adapt autonomously to changing conditions, leading to enhanced efficiency and robustness. This work contributes a practical and data-driven method for on-the-fly configuration in modular software, opening new avenues for self-adaptive systems. Keywords: Metareasoning, Reinforcement Learning, Modular Software, Dynamic Configuration, Self-Adaptive Systems, Contextual Representation.
