Breast cancer subtype classification is critical for personalized treatment and improved patient outcomes. This paper proposes a novel hybrid approach combining Relation Networks (RN) and Localized Graph Convolutional Filtering (LGCF) to enhance the accuracy and interpretability of subtype classification. The proposed method leverages the relational reasoning capabilities of RN to model complex interactions between genomic and clinical features, while LGCF captures localized structural patterns within the data, enabling more robust feature representation. The model is trained and evaluated on a comprehensive breast cancer dataset, incorporating multi-omics data and clinical attributes. Experimental results demonstrate that the hybrid approach outperforms existing methods, achieving superior classification accuracy and robustness across diverse subtypes. Key findings reveal that the integration of relational reasoning and localized graph-based learning significantly improves the model's ability to discern subtle differences between subtypes. This research contributes a scalable and interpretable framework for breast cancer subtype classification, with potential applications in precision oncology and biomarker discovery. The proposed method also highlights the effectiveness of combining relational and graph-based techniques in addressing complex biomedical classification tasks.  

**Keywords**: Breast cancer, subtype classification, Relation Networks, Graph Convolutional Filtering, multi-omics data, precision oncology.
