This paper introduces a novel approach to morphology evolution in robotics, moving away from task-specific designs to task-agnostic solutions. We present a framework that utilizes a combination of evolutionary algorithms and physics-based simulation to generate robot morphologies adaptable to a wide range of tasks. Our method focuses on evolving structural parameters such as limb length, joint placement, and material distribution, guided by metrics of locomotion efficiency, stability, and robustness, independent of a predefined task. We evaluate the performance of our evolved morphologies on a diverse set of simulated tasks, including navigation over uneven terrain, object manipulation, and obstacle avoidance. Results demonstrate that task-agnostically evolved robots exhibit comparable or superior performance to robots designed for specific tasks, highlighting the potential for creating versatile and adaptable robotic systems. The framework contributes a methodology for automated robot design applicable to unstructured and dynamically changing environments.

**Keywords:** Morphology Evolution, Robotics, Evolutionary Algorithms, Task-Agnostic Design, Automated Robot Design, Simulation.
