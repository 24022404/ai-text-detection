This paper explores a novel approach to reinforcement learning (RL) by integrating principles from neuroscience to develop more general and adaptable agents. Current RL algorithms often struggle with generalization across diverse environments and tasks. To address this, we introduce a learning framework inspired by the brain's reward circuitry and hierarchical processing. Our method incorporates a biologically plausible reward modulation mechanism coupled with a modular neural network architecture that facilitates the learning of reusable skills. We evaluate our approach on a suite of challenging continuous control tasks and demonstrate that the resulting agent exhibits superior generalization performance compared to standard RL algorithms and other biologically inspired architectures. Specifically, our agent achieves higher average returns and faster adaptation rates when transferred to unseen environments with varying dynamics. These results suggest that incorporating neuroscience principles can lead to more robust and versatile RL agents, offering a promising direction for developing artificial intelligence systems that can learn and adapt in complex, real-world scenarios. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Neuroscience, Generalization, Reward Modulation, Modular Networks, Continuous Control.
