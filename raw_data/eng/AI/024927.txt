This paper presents a novel voting-based multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) framework designed to enhance decision-making in intelligent Internet of Things (IoT) environments. The objective of this research is to address the challenges of scalability, adaptability, and coordination among heterogeneous IoT devices operating in dynamic and distributed settings. Our approach integrates a voting mechanism into the MARL process, enabling agents to collectively evaluate and select optimal actions while considering network constraints and individual device preferences. Extensive simulations are conducted using realistic IoT scenarios, such as smart energy management and adaptive traffic control. The results demonstrate that the proposed method improves system-wide performance metrics, including resource utilization, response time, and fault tolerance, compared to conventional centralized and non-voting-based MARL strategies. Furthermore, the framework exhibits robust adaptation to changes in environment topologies and agent populations. These findings highlight the effectiveness of incorporating collective decision-making through voting in distributed IoT systems. The proposed methodology contributes to advancing smart IoT applications by providing a scalable, flexible, and resilient solution for multi-agent coordination. Keywords: multi-agent reinforcement learning, voting mechanism, Internet of Things, distributed decision-making, intelligent systems.
