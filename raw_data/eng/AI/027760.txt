This paper investigates the application of graph-based semi-supervised learning techniques for time series classification. We address the challenge of limited labeled data in time series analysis by leveraging unlabeled data to improve classification accuracy. Our approach constructs graphs where nodes represent time series instances, and edges represent similarity relationships. We then employ label propagation algorithms to infer labels for unlabeled instances based on the graph structure. We empirically evaluate several graph construction methods, including k-nearest neighbors and epsilon-neighborhood graphs, coupled with various label propagation strategies. The performance of these methods is assessed on a diverse set of benchmark time series datasets, considering different label ratios. Results demonstrate that graph-based semi-supervised learning significantly improves classification accuracy compared to purely supervised methods, particularly when labeled data is scarce. Furthermore, our findings highlight the impact of graph construction parameters on the effectiveness of label propagation. This research contributes to the development of robust and efficient time series classification models in data-limited scenarios. Keywords: Time series classification, semi-supervised learning, graph-based learning, label propagation.
