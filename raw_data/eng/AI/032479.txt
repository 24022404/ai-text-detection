Recommender systems have become essential in filtering vast amounts of information and providing personalized user experiences. Traditionally, these systems rely on static input features or manual engineering to optimize recommendations, often limiting their adaptability and performance. This paper presents a novel approach called Differentiable Neural Input Search (DNIS), which enables the automated discovery and optimization of input representations for recommender systems through a differentiable search framework. By integrating input selection directly into the neural network training process, DNIS simultaneously learns both the recommendation model and the most relevant input features in an end-to-end manner. We evaluate DNIS on multiple benchmark datasets, comparing its performance with state-of-the-art collaborative filtering and neural recommendation techniques. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in recommendation accuracy, model interpretability, and computational efficiency. Our method also exhibits enhanced adaptability to varying user behaviors and item characteristics. The findings suggest that differentiable input search provides a powerful paradigm for advancing the effectiveness and robustness of recommender systems. Key implications include streamlined feature engineering, better resource utilization, and the potential for broader application in diverse recommendation scenarios. Keywords: recommender systems, differentiable search, neural networks, input optimization, feature selection, personalization.
