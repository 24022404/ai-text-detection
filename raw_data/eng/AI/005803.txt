Deep face recognition has achieved remarkable success, yet challenges remain in disentangling appearance and geometric features to improve robustness and generalization. This paper addresses the problem of enhancing face recognition performance by explicitly separating appearance-based attributes (e.g., texture, lighting) from geometric structures (e.g., pose, shape). We propose a novel framework that leverages disentangled representation learning to isolate these two critical components. Our approach employs a dual-branch neural network architecture, where one branch focuses on encoding appearance features, while the other captures geometric information. A disentanglement loss function is introduced to ensure minimal overlap between the two feature spaces, enabling more discriminative and interpretable representations. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art face recognition models, particularly in scenarios with significant pose variations and lighting changes. The results highlight the importance of disentangling appearance and geometry for robust face recognition. This work contributes a scalable and effective solution for improving deep face recognition systems, with potential applications in security, surveillance, and human-computer interaction.  

**Keywords**: Deep face recognition, disentangled representation, appearance features, geometric features, neural networks, pose invariance.
