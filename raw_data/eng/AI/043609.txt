The identification of lane-change maneuvers, particularly during on-ramp merges, is critical for understanding driver behavior and improving traffic safety. This study addresses the challenge of unsupervised lane-change identification in naturalistic driving data, where labeled datasets are often unavailable or costly to obtain. We propose a novel unsupervised approach leveraging clustering techniques and vehicle trajectory analysis to detect lane-change events without prior annotations. Our method integrates spatiotemporal features, such as relative velocity, acceleration, and lateral displacement, to distinguish lane changes from other driving behaviors. The algorithm is evaluated on a large-scale naturalistic driving dataset, demonstrating high accuracy in identifying lane-change maneuvers during on-ramp merges. Comparative analysis with existing supervised methods reveals that our approach achieves competitive performance while eliminating the need for labeled training data. The results highlight the potential of unsupervised techniques for scalable and cost-effective analysis of driving behaviors. This research contributes to the development of advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS) and autonomous driving technologies by providing a robust framework for lane-change detection in real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: lane-change identification, unsupervised learning, naturalistic driving data, on-ramp merge, trajectory analysis, driver behavior, ADAS.
