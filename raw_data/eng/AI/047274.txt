The reliability and robustness of deep neural networks (DNNs) are critical when deployed in video-based applications such as surveillance, autonomous driving, and video analytics. This study addresses the challenge of ensuring consistent performance and robustness of DNNs when processing dynamic video data subject to perturbations and adversarial attacks.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework that integrates temporal consistency analysis and adversarial training strategies to enhance the robustness of DNNs on video data. The framework leverages a combination of spatiotemporal feature extraction, resilience evaluation metrics, and perturbation mitigation techniques to assess and improve the robustness of DNNs. Advanced methodologies such as robust optimization and uncertainty quantification are employed to derive robustness guarantees against common adversarial techniques.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the proposed approach significantly improves the robustness of DNNs on video datasets. When applied to benchmark video datasets, our model outperforms existing methods by reducing adversarial vulnerability and maintaining accuracy under various perturbation scenarios. The findings indicate a noticeable enhancement in ensuring the integrity of video-based DNN applications, as evidenced by superior performance in both controlled and real-world settings.

Conclusion/Implications: The research provides a pioneering contribution to the domain of video-based deep learning by offering robust frameworks that can be applied across diverse video analysis tasks. The implications of this work suggest that integrating robustness guarantees into DNN architectures can lead to more reliable and trustworthy video-based systems. Future applications may extend to critical fields such as autonomous navigation and security systems where robustness is paramount.

Key Keywords: deep neural networks, video analysis, robustness guarantees, adversarial attacks, temporal consistency, spatiotemporal features, adversarial training, video datasets.
