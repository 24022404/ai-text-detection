This paper introduces a novel approach to spectral total variation (TV) decomposition using deep learning. Traditional spectral TV decomposition methods often suffer from computational complexity and limitations in handling complex signals. To address these challenges, we propose a deep neural network architecture that learns to efficiently decompose signals into distinct spectral components based on their TV characteristics. Our network, trained on a large dataset of diverse signals, approximates the spectral TV decomposition process, offering significant speed improvements over iterative optimization techniques. Experimental results demonstrate that our deeply learned spectral TV decomposition achieves comparable or superior performance in terms of decomposition accuracy and noise reduction, while significantly reducing computational time. We evaluate the performance of our method on both synthetic and real-world data, including image and audio signals, highlighting its potential for applications in signal processing, image analysis, and data compression. Key words: Spectral Total Variation, Deep Learning, Signal Decomposition, Image Processing, Neural Networks.
