This research explores the phenomenon of minority collapse in imbalanced training datasets using deep neural networks, specifically employing a novel approach referred to as the Layer-Peeled Model. The study aims to address the challenges faced when training neural networks on datasets with an unequal distribution of classes, which can lead to poor generalization for minority classes.

Methods/Approach: We introduce the Layer-Peeled Model, a framework that allows analysis of deep neural network layers to understand the discriminative capabilities and structural changes when exposed to imbalanced data. This approach dissects layers individually to identify how they contribute to the minority class collapse during the training process. We employ various neural network architectures to validate our approach and demonstrate its utility in identifying critical layers influencing class imbalance effects.

Results/Findings: Our findings reveal distinct patterns of representation in the layers of neural networks trained on imbalanced datasets, particularly highlighting the layers where minority collapse is most pronounced. The Layer-Peeled Model successfully identifies these collapse points, offering insights into their formulation. Moreover, our analysis indicates that utilizing strategies such as layer-specific re-weighting based on our model can effectively mitigate collapse and enhance performance for minority classes.

Conclusion/Implications: This research provides a significant contribution to understanding class imbalance in deep learning through a novel layer-focused analysis model. The Layer-Peeled Model offers a robust tool for diagnosing and addressing minority collapse, paving the way for improved training protocols that enhance minority class performance. These insights can lead to more equitable and effective neural network applications across various domains suffering from class imbalance issues.

Keywords: minority collapse, imbalanced training, deep neural networks, Layer-Peeled Model, class imbalance, layer analysis.
