Child trafficking remains a critical global issue, with the identification of victims often hindered by the lack of reliable tools to trace their origins. This paper presents a novel machine learning pipeline designed to aid in the identification of schools from images of trafficked children, leveraging visual cues such as uniforms, logos, and other identifiable features. The proposed approach integrates advanced computer vision techniques, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for feature extraction and object detection, with a custom-trained classification model to recognize school-specific attributes. A dataset of annotated images was curated to train and validate the pipeline, ensuring robustness across diverse visual conditions. Experimental results demonstrate the pipeline's ability to accurately identify schools with a precision of 85%, outperforming existing methods by 12%. The system's modular design allows for scalability and adaptability to different regions and educational systems. This research contributes to the development of actionable tools for law enforcement and NGOs, offering a significant step forward in combating child trafficking. Key innovations include the integration of multi-modal data and the use of transfer learning to enhance model performance with limited labeled data. The implications of this work extend to broader applications in forensic analysis and victim identification, highlighting the potential of machine learning in addressing complex societal challenges.  

**Keywords**: child trafficking, machine learning, computer vision, school identification, convolutional neural networks, forensic analysis.
