Correlation clustering is a fundamental problem in data mining and machine learning, with applications ranging from social network analysis to bioinformatics. This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing large-scale correlation clustering, where traditional methods often struggle with scalability and efficiency. We propose a novel distributed optimization framework that leverages parallel computing and advanced graph partitioning techniques to handle massive datasets. Our approach combines a scalable initialization strategy with an iterative refinement process, ensuring both computational efficiency and high-quality clustering results. Experimental evaluations on real-world datasets demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art algorithms in terms of runtime and clustering accuracy, achieving up to a 40% improvement in scalability for datasets with millions of nodes. Furthermore, we provide theoretical insights into the convergence properties of our algorithm, ensuring robustness across diverse datasets. The proposed framework not only advances the state of correlation clustering but also offers practical implications for large-scale data analysis in domains such as recommendation systems, community detection, and anomaly detection. Key contributions include a scalable optimization model, a distributed implementation, and empirical validation of its effectiveness. Keywords: correlation clustering, distributed optimization, scalability, graph partitioning, large-scale data analysis.
