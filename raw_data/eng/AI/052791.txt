This paper explores the enhancement of hierarchical reinforcement learning through the development of goal embeddings via self-play. The primary objective is to tackle the limitations in existing hierarchical reinforcement learning frameworks by effectively utilizing goal embeddings to improve decision-making and learning efficiency.

Methods/Approach: We introduce an innovative approach to hierarchical reinforcement learning where agents learn to generate goal embeddings through self-play mechanisms. By employing a multi-level hierarchical architecture, our model leverages self-play to simulate diverse scenarios, enabling agents to discover optimal goal embeddings that succinctly represent desired outcomes. This framework enhances the agent's ability to decompose complex tasks into manageable sub-goals and learn policies that generalize well across varying tasks.

Results/Findings: Experiments conducted demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms traditional hierarchical reinforcement learning models. Our model consistently exhibits improved learning speed and task performance across a range of complex environments. We present quantitative analyses showing substantial gains in efficiency and robustness, attributed to the effective abstraction and representation of task goals through self-play-generated embeddings.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed method highlights the potential of integrating self-play in goal embedding learning to facilitate more efficient and adaptable hierarchical reinforcement learning systems. This research not only contributes novel insights into goal representation but also paves the way for practical applications in complex real-world scenarios, such as robotics and automated systems, where goal-oriented decision-making is crucial.

Key Keywords: Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning, Goal Embeddings, Self-Play, Decision-Making, Multi-Level Architecture, Task Performance.
