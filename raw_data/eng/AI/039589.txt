Synthetic images, generated through computer graphics techniques, often suffer from visual artifacts and lack realism, limiting their utility in training machine learning models. This paper introduces a novel image-based generator architecture, termed "SynRefine," designed to refine synthetic images, enhancing their photorealism and reducing the domain gap between synthetic and real data. SynRefine employs a multi-scale generative adversarial network (GAN) conditioned on the input synthetic image. A key innovation is the integration of a perceptual loss function that leverages pre-trained convolutional neural networks to preserve semantic content and improve image quality. We evaluated SynRefine on several datasets, including generated faces and rendered objects, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art image translation techniques. Results demonstrate that SynRefine significantly improves the realism of synthetic images, achieving higher scores on perceptual similarity metrics and improving the performance of downstream machine learning tasks trained on the refined synthetic data. Our findings suggest that SynRefine offers a promising approach for generating high-quality synthetic data suitable for a wide range of applications, particularly in scenarios where real data is scarce or difficult to acquire.

Keywords: Synthetic Images, Image Refinement, Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, Photorealism, Domain Adaptation, Image Quality.
