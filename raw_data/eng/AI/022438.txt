This paper investigates a fundamental question in neural network research: whether different neural network architectures, when trained on the same task, converge towards learning similar representations. Understanding this could provide insights into network generalization capabilities and the intrinsic nature of learned features across architectures.

Methods/Approach: We analyze the representation learning of various neural network architectures, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformer models, using widely recognized datasets for classification tasks. Employing techniques such as representational similarity analysis and activation alignment, we evaluate and quantify the similarities and differences in learned representations across networks.

Results/Findings: Our findings reveal that despite differences in architecture, different neural networks exhibit convergence in certain levels of their learned feature spaces. Specifically, lower-layer representations show greater alignment across architectures, while higher-layer representations vary, reflecting task-specific adaptation. Additionally, the results highlight the role of hyperparameters and training strategies in influencing representational convergence.

Conclusion/Implications: The study brings to light critical insights into the convergent nature of neural network learning and its impact on feature representation. These results could inform future neural network design, suggesting that leveraging cross-architecture similarities might enhance model ensemble strategies and transfer learning applications. Furthermore, understanding representational convergence can aid in developing robust and interpretable AI systems. Key keywords: neural networks, representation learning, convergence, CNN, transformers, feature alignment.
