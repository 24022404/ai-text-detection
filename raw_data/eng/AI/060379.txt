This paper introduces a novel diagnostic evaluation benchmark called DEVIL designed to address the growing need for precise and objective assessment of video inpainting algorithms. Video inpainting is crucial for restoring and editing video content, yet evaluating the effectiveness of these algorithms remains a challenge.

Methods/Approach: The DEVIL benchmark provides a comprehensive framework for assessing video inpainting methods by encompassing diverse scenarios and metrics that mimic real-world challenges. It uses both qualitative insights and quantitative measures to evaluate critical aspects such as temporal coherence, spatial accuracy, and visual consistency. This is achieved by employing a suite of targeted tests tailored to various inpainting tasks and conditions.

Results/Findings: Through extensive evaluation, the DEVIL benchmark reveals significant insights into the performance of existing video inpainting techniques. Our results indicate that while many algorithms perform well in controlled environments, they often falter under complex conditions captured in our benchmark, highlighting areas for improvement. This evaluation sets a detailed standard, unveiling the intricacies of algorithm performance that are often overlooked in traditional evaluation methods.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of the DEVIL benchmark represents a substantial advancement in the field of video inpainting by providing a robust and scalable platform for comparison and development. This work contributes to the community by offering a clearer understanding of algorithmic strengths and weaknesses, guiding future research towards more resilient and adaptable methods. The findings are expected to catalyze further innovations in video editing technologies, enhancing applications in film production, digital restoration, and multimedia communications.

Keywords: video inpainting, benchmark, evaluation, temporal coherence, spatial accuracy, algorithm performance.
