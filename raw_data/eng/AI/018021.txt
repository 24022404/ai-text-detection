The objective of this paper is to survey the burgeoning field of applying Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to time series data, providing a comprehensive taxonomy of existing methodologies. GANs, originally developed for image generation, have shown remarkable potential in modeling temporal dependencies and generating synthetic time series data, which are crucial for applications ranging from finance to healthcare. This survey systematically categorizes these approaches based on the underlying architecture, training techniques, and specific time series applications. To compile this taxonomy, we conducted an extensive review of current literature, focusing on the innovative adaptations of GANs for temporal contexts and the challenges faced when applying these models to sequential data. Our findings reveal a trend towards hybrid models that combine GANs with recurrent architectures to better capture dynamic sequences, as well as improvements in stabilization techniques for adversarial training. Furthermore, we identify key performance metrics used to evaluate synthetic time series generation and discuss common datasets employed across studies. The paper concludes with a discussion on the implications of these advancements, emphasizing the transformative potential of GANs in predictive modeling and simulation tasks within time series analysis. The insights provided by this survey aim to guide future research and application developments, encouraging further exploration into this dynamic intersection of deep learning and time series data. Key keywords include generative adversarial networks, GAN, time series, taxonomy, synthetic data generation, and machine learning.
