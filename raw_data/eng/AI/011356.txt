This paper introduces DeepBlindness, a novel approach for fast blindness map estimation and blindness type classification in outdoor scenes from a single color image. The objective is to develop an efficient and accurate method to detect and categorize blindness in images, which is crucial for various applications such as autonomous vehicles, robotics, and image processing. Our approach utilizes a deep learning-based framework, leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to estimate blindness maps and classify blindness types. The method is trained on a large dataset of images with annotated blindness regions and types, enabling the model to learn robust features and patterns. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of DeepBlindness, achieving high accuracy and efficiency in estimating blindness maps and classifying blindness types. The proposed approach outperforms existing methods, making it a valuable contribution to the field of computer vision and image processing. Key contributions include the development of a fast and accurate blindness map estimation algorithm, a robust blindness type classification framework, and the creation of a large annotated dataset for blindness detection and classification. Relevant keywords: blindness map estimation, blindness type classification, deep learning, CNNs, computer vision, image processing, outdoor scene understanding.
