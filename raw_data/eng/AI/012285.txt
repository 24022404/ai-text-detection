Abstract:

This paper introduces a novel approach to language inference by leveraging multi-head automata trained via reinforcement learning. Traditional language inference methods often rely on fixed-rule systems or neural models that may struggle with complex language constructs and long-range dependencies. To address these limitations, we propose a framework that models formal language inference as a sequential decision-making problem, where multi-head finite automata serve as adaptive learners capable of simultaneously tracking multiple information streams within text. Our method utilizes reinforcement learning algorithms to optimize the automata's transition policies, improving their ability to generalize across diverse language structures. Experimental evaluations were conducted on standard language inference benchmarks, demonstrating that our approach achieves superior accuracy and efficiency compared to conventional automata and neural network-based models. The results highlight notable improvements in processing ambiguous or interleaved patterns, which are challenging for single-head or untrained automata. This research contributes a new perspective on integrating formal language theory with advanced machine learning techniques, offering practical implications for natural language understanding, symbolic reasoning, and the development of interpretable AI models. Key keywords include language inference, multi-head automata, reinforcement learning, formal languages, and sequential decision-making.
