Optical flow estimation, a critical task in computer vision, has seen significant advancements with the introduction of deep learning techniques. This paper presents FlowNet 2.0, an evolution of the original FlowNet architecture designed to address its limitations in accuracy and robustness. Our approach involves stacking multiple FlowNet architectures, incorporating novel data augmentation strategies, and introducing a sub-network specifically trained on small displacement estimation. The resulting model significantly improves upon FlowNet, achieving state-of-the-art performance on standard optical flow benchmarks, including the Sintel and KITTI datasets. Quantitative evaluations demonstrate a substantial reduction in endpoint error and an increased ability to handle large displacements and occlusions. The improved performance of FlowNet 2.0 enables more reliable motion analysis in various applications such as video processing, autonomous driving, and robotics. Keywords: Optical Flow, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, Motion Estimation, Convolutional Neural Networks.
