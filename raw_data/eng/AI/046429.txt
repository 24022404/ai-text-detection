Neural networks are vulnerable to Trojan attacks, where malicious functionalities are embedded into the model. This paper introduces a novel Trojan attack strategy, termed Deep Feature Space Trojan Attack by Controlled Detoxification (DFS-Tox), which operates within the deep feature space of the network. Unlike existing attacks that focus on manipulating the output layer or specific neurons, DFS-Tox strategically perturbs feature representations to subtly activate the Trojan trigger while simultaneously employing a detoxification mechanism to mitigate the impact on clean data accuracy. Our method carefully controls the magnitude and direction of feature perturbations, ensuring stealthiness and effectiveness. We evaluate DFS-Tox on several benchmark datasets and network architectures, demonstrating its superior performance in terms of attack success rate and stealthiness compared to state-of-the-art Trojan attacks. Furthermore, our analysis reveals that the detoxification process significantly reduces the impact on clean accuracy, maintaining model utility. The proposed DFS-Tox presents a significant advancement in Trojan attack methodologies, highlighting the need for robust defenses against such threats. Keywords: Trojan attack, neural networks, deep learning, feature space, adversarial attacks, model security, detoxification.
