**Abstract:**

Efficient data collection from distributed wireless sensor networks is critical for a wide range of applications, including environmental monitoring, precision agriculture, and smart city management. Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) have emerged as flexible mobile data harvesters capable of improving collection efficiency. This paper addresses the problem of UAV path planning for wireless data harvesting with the objective of maximizing data collection while minimizing travel distance and energy consumption. We propose a novel deep reinforcement learning (DRL) framework that utilizes a deep Q-network (DQN) to enable UAVs to autonomously learn optimal trajectories across dynamic and partially known environments. The system model incorporates realistic constraints such as limited flight time, communication distance, and obstacles. Extensive simulation experiments demonstrate that our DRL-based approach significantly outperforms traditional heuristic and rule-based methods in terms of data harvesting rate, coverage efficiency, and adaptability to environmental changes. The results indicate improved scalability and robustness, particularly in dense sensor deployments and environments with unpredictable network conditions. This research contributes an intelligent and adaptive solution to UAV path planning, facilitating reliable and energy-efficient wireless data collection. Key implications include potential enhancements in wireless sensor network operations, resource allocation, and real-time data acquisition in diverse application scenarios. **Keywords:** UAV, path planning, wireless data harvesting, deep reinforcement learning, DQN, sensor networks, autonomous systems.
