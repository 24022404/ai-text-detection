**Abstract**

Semantic forecasting, which involves predicting future scene semantics, is vital for advanced applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and video surveillance. This paper presents a novel recurrent flow-guided approach for semantic forecasting that leverages temporal sequences to enhance predictive accuracy. Our method integrates recurrent neural networks with optical flow estimation to jointly model semantic evolution and pixel-wise motion dynamics over time. By using flow-guided feature warping and recurrent temporal aggregation, the model captures both spatial context and temporal dependencies, enabling robust long-term predictions of semantic segmentation maps in video streams. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in terms of accuracy and temporal consistency, particularly in complex dynamic scenes with significant object motion. The proposed framework also exhibits computational efficiency, making it suitable for real-time deployments. Our findings highlight the effectiveness of combining recurrent architectures with motion cues for semantic forecasting and offer promising directions for future research involving predictive perception in intelligent systems. Key keywords: semantic forecasting, recurrent neural networks, optical flow, temporal modeling, video scene understanding, autonomous systems.
