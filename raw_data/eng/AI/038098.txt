This paper addresses the problem of uncovering relationships between sets of multidimensional data by introducing a novel method termed Canonical Autocorrelation Analysis (CAA). Traditional approaches to correlation analysis often focus on examining relationships within pairs of datasets, but our research aims to extend these capabilities to more complex data interactions.

Methods/Approach: We propose the Canonical Autocorrelation Analysis as an advanced technique that integrates elements of canonical correlation analysis (CCA) and autocorrelation principles to effectively identify linear relationships between multiple datasets. The proposed method uses a dual-step approach, firstly optimizing the linear projections that maximize autocorrelation within each dataset, followed by optimizing inter-dataset projections to enhance canonical relationships.

Results/Findings: The findings demonstrate that CAA outperforms traditional CCA and other correlation techniques in scenarios involving complex datasets with significant autocorrelation. Through extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets, our model achieved consistently higher accuracy in capturing meaningful data correlations, as evidenced by improved metric performance such as mutual information scores and correlation coefficients.

Conclusion/Implications: The Canonical Autocorrelation Analysis represents a significant advancement in the field of data analysis, offering a more robust and comprehensive tool for multidimensional relationship discovery. Its potential applications are widespread, including improved data fusion in machine learning, enhanced insight generation in exploratory data analysis, and sophisticated dimensionality reduction strategies. By providing a deeper understanding of complex data interactions, CAA paves the way for more accurate and insightful data-driven decision-making.

Keywords: Canonical Autocorrelation Analysis, correlation analysis, multidimensional data, data relationships, canonical correlation analysis, data fusion.
