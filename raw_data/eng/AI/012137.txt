This paper introduces a novel unsupervised approach to text recognition, termed "Learning to Read by Spelling" (LRBS), addressing the challenge of training accurate text recognition systems without labeled data. LRBS leverages the inherent structure of language, training a model to reconstruct text by predicting character sequences from visual inputs. The method utilizes a convolutional neural network (CNN) for feature extraction, followed by a recurrent neural network (RNN) with attention mechanism for character prediction. A key innovation is the incorporation of a spelling correction module, trained adversarially, to refine the predicted sequences and enforce linguistic plausibility. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that LRBS achieves competitive accuracy compared to existing unsupervised and weakly supervised methods, showcasing its ability to learn meaningful text representations from unlabeled images. Furthermore, we show that LRBS can be effectively fine-tuned with limited labeled data, significantly boosting performance. This research contributes a practical and scalable solution for text recognition in scenarios where labeled data is scarce, opening avenues for broader applications in document analysis and image understanding. Keywords: Text Recognition, Unsupervised Learning, Character Prediction, Spelling Correction, Adversarial Training, OCR.
