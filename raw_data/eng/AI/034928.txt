This paper presents a novel Bayesian-inspired, deep learning-based, semi-supervised domain adaptation technique for accurate land cover mapping. The objective is to address the challenge of adapting pre-trained deep learning models to new, unseen environments with limited labeled data. Our approach combines the strengths of Bayesian neural networks and semi-supervised learning to develop a robust domain adaptation framework. The method utilizes a Bayesian-inspired uncertainty estimation module to guide the adaptation process, enabling the model to effectively transfer knowledge from the source domain to the target domain. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our technique, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark land cover mapping datasets. Key findings include improved classification accuracy, reduced domain shift, and enhanced robustness to labeling noise. The proposed technique has significant implications for real-world land cover mapping applications, enabling accurate and efficient mapping with limited labeled data. Our contributions include the development of a novel domain adaptation framework, demonstration of its effectiveness in land cover mapping, and exploration of the potential of Bayesian-inspired deep learning techniques in remote sensing applications. Keywords: domain adaptation, semi-supervised learning, Bayesian neural networks, deep learning, land cover mapping, remote sensing.
