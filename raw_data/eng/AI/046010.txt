Multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL) has shown promise in solving complex cooperative tasks. Parameter sharing, a common technique in MADRL, aims to improve learning efficiency and coordination by allowing agents to share knowledge. This paper revisits the effectiveness of parameter sharing in MADRL, identifying scenarios where it can be detrimental to performance. We propose a novel adaptive parameter sharing (APS) method that dynamically adjusts the degree of sharing based on agent similarity and task requirements. APS leverages a similarity metric derived from agent state-action distributions to guide the sharing process. Empirical evaluations across various cooperative environments demonstrate that APS outperforms both full parameter sharing and independent learning strategies. Specifically, APS achieves faster convergence, higher asymptotic performance, and improved robustness to agent heterogeneity. Our findings highlight the importance of carefully considering parameter sharing strategies in MADRL and offer a practical approach to enhance learning in complex multi-agent systems.
Keywords: Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning, Parameter Sharing, Deep Learning, Cooperative Learning, Adaptive Algorithms.
