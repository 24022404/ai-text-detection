The paper addresses the challenging problem of video-based person re-identification, which requires identifying individuals across different camera views with temporal and spatial variations. The main focus is developing a deep learning network that efficiently captures crucial features for improving identification accuracy.

Methods: We propose a novel Spatially and Temporally Efficient Non-local Attention Network (STENLAN), designed to enhance feature learning in video sequences. Our approach integrates non-local attention mechanisms to dynamically assess spatial and temporal dependencies across video frames. This enables the network to focus on relevant information, reducing computational overhead without compromising accuracy. The method combines convolutional and recurrent neural network architectures to optimize feature extraction and representation.

Results: STENLAN achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmark datasets for video-based person re-identification, such as MARS and DukeMTMC-VideoReID. Quantitative analysis shows significant improvements in retrieval accuracy and computational efficiency compared to existing methods. Our approach balances precision and resource usage, providing a scalable solution suitable for real-world applications.

Conclusion: The proposed STENLAN framework offers a substantial advance in the video-based person re-identification domain by effectively managing spatial and temporal feature integration. Its ability to enhance accuracy while being computationally efficient makes it a viable option for practical deployment in surveillance systems and smart city applications. Future work will explore integrating this method with other AI technologies to further improve performance and adaptability.

Keywords: video-based person re-identification, non-local attention, deep learning, spatial-temporal efficiency, feature learning, STENLAN.
