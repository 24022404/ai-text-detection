Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning meaningful representations from unlabeled data. However, SSL models often encode undesirable or irrelevant information, such as biases or domain-specific artifacts, which can hinder their generalization and downstream task performance. This paper introduces **Conditional Contrastive Learning (CCL)**, a novel framework designed to remove undesirable information from self-supervised representations while preserving task-relevant features. CCL leverages conditional adversarial training and contrastive learning to disentangle and suppress unwanted features during representation learning. We evaluate CCL on multiple benchmarks, including image classification and domain adaptation tasks, demonstrating its ability to improve model robustness and generalization compared to existing SSL methods. Our results show that CCL achieves state-of-the-art performance by effectively filtering out undesirable information without requiring additional labeled data. The proposed framework has broad implications for applications in fairness-aware machine learning, domain generalization, and transfer learning, where clean and unbiased representations are critical.  

**Keywords**: Self-supervised learning, contrastive learning, representation learning, adversarial training, domain adaptation, fairness-aware AI.
