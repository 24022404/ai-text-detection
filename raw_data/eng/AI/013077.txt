Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have emerged as a powerful approach for generating synthetic data, yet their evaluation remains a challenging problem. This paper presents an empirical study on the performance of various evaluation metrics for GANs, including Inception Score (IS), Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Kernel Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD), and Coverage. We assess these metrics across a diverse set of GAN architectures and datasets, focusing on their sensitivity to different types of generated image artifacts and their correlation with human perception. Our findings reveal inconsistencies in the rankings produced by different metrics, highlighting the limitations of relying on any single metric for GAN evaluation. Specifically, we observe that IS often overestimates the quality of GANs with mode collapse, while FID is sensitive to both image quality and diversity. The study also explores the impact of dataset characteristics on metric performance. The results provide practical guidance for researchers and practitioners on the appropriate selection and interpretation of GAN evaluation metrics, emphasizing the need for a multifaceted evaluation approach. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, Evaluation Metrics, Inception Score, Fréchet Inception Distance, Mode Collapse, Image Quality.
