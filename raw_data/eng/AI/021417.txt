This study presents MEVA, a comprehensive large-scale video dataset tailored for advancing research in activity detection. With the increasing demand for robust activity recognition systems, there is a critical need for datasets that provide diverse scenarios and perspectives. 

Methods/Approach: MEVA incorporates multiview and multimodal data, offering synchronized video streams from multiple camera angles along with accompanying audio and metadata. This setup reflects real-world environments more accurately than existing datasets. The dataset is meticulously organized to facilitate the training and evaluation of novel activity detection algorithms, supporting both supervised and unsupervised learning approaches.

Results/Findings: Initial experiments using MEVA demonstrate enhanced activity detection performance when leveraging its multimodal and multiview characteristics. Comparisons with existing datasets show MEVA's considerable advantage in capturing complex activities that span different viewpoints. Our analysis indicates significant improvements in accuracy and robustness of detection algorithms when trained on MEVA, particularly in occlusion-rich scenarios.

Conclusion/Implications: MEVA's introduction marks a significant advancement in the resources available for activity detection research, providing a unique platform for exploring more sophisticated models capable of understanding complex human activities. Its diverse and comprehensive nature makes it an invaluable asset not only for enhancing current methodologies but also for fostering innovation in fields such as surveillance, human-computer interaction, and autonomous systems. Future work will focus on expanding the dataset's richness and its usability for cutting-edge machine learning models. 

Keywords: MEVA, activity detection, multiview, multimodal, video dataset, machine learning, supervised learning, unsupervised learning.
