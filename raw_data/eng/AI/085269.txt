This paper investigates the vulnerability of partial dependence plots, a widely used interpretability tool in machine learning, to data poisoning attacks. The objective is to demonstrate how an adversary can manipulate the training data to fool partial dependence plots, leading to misleading interpretations of model behavior. We propose a novel data poisoning approach that leverages gradient-based optimization to craft malicious data points, which are then added to the training dataset. Our results show that even a small fraction of poisoned data can significantly alter the partial dependence plots, making them unreliable for model interpretation. We evaluate our method on several benchmark datasets and compare it to existing attacks, demonstrating its effectiveness and efficiency. The findings of this research highlight the importance of data quality and integrity in machine learning, particularly when using interpretability tools like partial dependence plots. Our contributions include a new attack methodology and a deeper understanding of the vulnerabilities of partial dependence plots, with implications for the development of more robust and reliable interpretability techniques. Key keywords: data poisoning, partial dependence plots, machine learning interpretability, adversarial attacks, model reliability.
