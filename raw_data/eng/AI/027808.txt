This paper explores the potential role of probabilistic integration in statistical computation, addressing the challenges of accurately estimating integrals in high-dimensional spaces, which are fundamental to many statistical and machine learning applications. Traditional numerical integration methods often struggle with scalability and efficiency in such settings. To address this, we propose a probabilistic approach that leverages Monte Carlo techniques and Bayesian inference to approximate integrals with improved accuracy and computational efficiency. Our methodology involves constructing probabilistic models that adaptively sample from the integrand, reducing variance and computational cost compared to conventional methods. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms existing techniques in terms of both accuracy and runtime, particularly in high-dimensional scenarios. The findings suggest that probabilistic integration can significantly enhance statistical computation, offering a robust alternative for applications such as Bayesian inference, uncertainty quantification, and probabilistic machine learning. This work contributes to the growing body of research on probabilistic methods, highlighting their potential to address complex computational challenges in statistics and beyond.  

**Keywords**: probabilistic integration, statistical computation, Monte Carlo methods, Bayesian inference, high-dimensional integration, uncertainty quantification.
