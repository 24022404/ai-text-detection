The rapid advancement of autonomous vehicles has accentuated the importance of robust object detection systems for safe navigation. This study investigates the resilience of object category detection in autonomous vehicles against universal adversarial perturbations, which pose a significant threat by potentially altering the vehicle's perception. The objective is to assess vulnerability and enhance the robustness of these systems. Our approach involves employing deep neural networks as the core of the vehicle's object detection capability and systematically applying universal adversarial perturbations to test their impact. The results indicate a varied susceptibility across different object categories, with some being more prone to misclassification or incorrect detection due to these perturbations. By analyzing these vulnerabilities, we propose a novel defense mechanism that integrates adversarial training with adaptive feature-space adjustments, demonstrating a substantial increase in detection accuracy under attack conditions. This study contributes to our understanding of the defensive capabilities of autonomous systems, offering insights that could be crucial for enhancing safety and reliability. The findings have significant implications for the development and deployment of more resilient autonomous vehicle technologies. Key keywords: autonomous vehicles, object category detection, adversarial perturbations, robustness, deep neural networks.
