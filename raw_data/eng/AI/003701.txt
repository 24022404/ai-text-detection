**Abstract**

One-shot medical image segmentation remains a significant challenge due to the limited availability of annotated data and substantial variability in anatomical structures. This paper presents a novel data augmentation framework that leverages learned image transformations to enhance one-shot segmentation accuracy. Unlike traditional augmentation methods, which rely on handcrafted operations such as rotations or flips, our approach utilizes a neural transformation model trained to generate realistic and anatomically plausible variations of medical images. The proposed method integrates these learned transformations into existing one-shot segmentation pipelines, promoting better model generalization and robustness. Extensive experiments conducted on benchmark medical image datasets demonstrate that our augmentation technique outperforms conventional strategies, achieving notable improvements in segmentation accuracy, especially under extremely limited annotation settings. The results indicate that the synergy of data augmentation and learned transformation models can bridge the gap between data scarcity and model performance in clinical applications. This research paves the way for more effective and efficient use of deep learning in medical image analysis, with potential implications for automated diagnosis and treatment planning. **Keywords:** data augmentation, medical image segmentation, one-shot learning, neural transformations, deep learning.
