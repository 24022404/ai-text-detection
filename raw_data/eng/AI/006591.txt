This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of automatic face recognition systems by introducing a novel approach based on Local Fourier-Bessel Features (LFBF). The primary objective is to improve the performance of face recognition in diverse environments with varying lighting conditions and facial expressions. Our methodology involves the extraction of local features using the Fourier-Bessel transform, which inherently captures both frequency and spatial domain information, enabling a robust representation of facial characteristics. Comparative analysis is conducted using standard face recognition databases to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed LFBF-based system against existing methods such as Eigenfaces and Fisherfaces. The results demonstrate superior recognition accuracy and speed, particularly in scenarios with noise and occlusion, validating the potential of LFBF in demanding real-world applications. The findings suggest significant improvements, underscoring the versatility of Local Fourier-Bessel Features in addressing common limitations observed in traditional face recognition frameworks. This research contributes a novel feature extraction technique with implications for enhanced biometric security systems, access control, and surveillance technologies. Key keywords include face recognition, Fourier-Bessel transform, local features, biometric authentication, and pattern recognition.
