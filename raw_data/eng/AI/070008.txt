This study aims to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of writer-independent offline handwritten signature verification systems. To achieve this, we investigate the impact of feature selection and transfer learning on system performance. Our approach involves evaluating various feature extraction techniques and selecting the most relevant features to represent handwritten signatures. We also explore the application of transfer learning to leverage pre-trained models and fine-tune them for the specific task of signature verification. The results show that the proposed method achieves significant improvements in verification accuracy, outperforming traditional methods. Our findings highlight the importance of careful feature selection and the potential of transfer learning in adapting to new datasets and writing styles. This research contributes to the development of more reliable and robust writer-independent offline handwritten signature verification systems, with potential applications in document authentication, identity verification, and forensic analysis. Key keywords: writer-independent signature verification, feature selection, transfer learning, offline handwritten signatures, deep learning, biometric authentication.
