This paper presents an empirical study on graph-based approaches for semi-supervised time series classification, addressing the challenge of limited labeled data in real-world applications. Our objective is to investigate the effectiveness of graph convolutional networks (GCNs) and graph attention networks (GATs) in leveraging unlabeled data to improve classification accuracy. We propose a novel framework that integrates graph-based models with time series features, and evaluate its performance on several benchmark datasets. Our results show that the proposed approach outperforms traditional semi-supervised methods, achieving significant improvements in classification accuracy and F1-score. The study highlights the importance of graph structure in time series data and demonstrates the potential of graph-based approaches in semi-supervised learning. Our contributions include the development of a graph-based framework for time series classification, and the empirical evaluation of its effectiveness in real-world datasets. The findings of this study have implications for applications such as anomaly detection, predictive maintenance, and time series forecasting, and can be extended to other domains with limited labeled data. Key keywords: semi-supervised learning, time series classification, graph convolutional networks, graph attention networks, anomaly detection.
