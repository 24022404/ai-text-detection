Weakly supervised learning (WSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training machine learning models with limited labeled data, leveraging noisy or incomplete annotations. This paper introduces *Knodle*, a modular and flexible framework for weakly supervised learning implemented in PyTorch. The primary objective of Knodle is to provide researchers and practitioners with a unified platform to design, experiment, and deploy WSL models across diverse domains. The framework supports a wide range of weak supervision techniques, including rule-based labeling, distant supervision, and multi-instance learning, enabling users to integrate domain-specific knowledge effectively.  

Knodle's architecture is designed for scalability and extensibility, offering modular components for data preprocessing, label generation, model training, and evaluation. We demonstrate the framework's effectiveness through experiments on benchmark datasets, showcasing its ability to achieve competitive performance compared to traditional supervised learning approaches while significantly reducing annotation costs. Key results highlight Knodle's robustness in handling noisy labels and its adaptability to various tasks, such as text classification and image recognition.  

The implications of this work are substantial, as Knodle lowers the barrier to entry for weakly supervised learning research and applications. By providing an open-source, user-friendly toolkit, this framework empowers the community to explore innovative WSL strategies and accelerate advancements in domains where labeled data is scarce. Keywords: weakly supervised learning, PyTorch, machine learning, rule-based labeling, distant supervision, modular framework.
