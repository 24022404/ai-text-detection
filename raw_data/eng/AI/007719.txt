Deep learning classifiers have achieved remarkable success in various domains, but their reliability in real-world applications remains a significant concern. This paper addresses the challenge of assessing the robustness and reliability of deep learning classifiers by combining robustness evaluation techniques with the construction of operational profiles. We propose a novel methodology that first evaluates the classifier's performance under various types of input perturbations and adversarial attacks. The resulting robustness metrics are then integrated with operational profiles, which capture the expected distribution of inputs in the target deployment environment. This allows for a more realistic assessment of the classifier's reliability compared to traditional benchmark datasets. Our experiments on image classification tasks demonstrate that this approach provides a more accurate and nuanced understanding of classifier performance, revealing potential failure modes that are not apparent from standard accuracy metrics. The findings highlight the importance of considering both robustness and operational context when deploying deep learning classifiers in safety-critical applications. This work contributes a practical framework for evaluating and improving the reliability of deep learning systems. Keywords: Deep Learning, Reliability, Robustness Evaluation, Operational Profiles, Adversarial Attacks, Machine Learning.
