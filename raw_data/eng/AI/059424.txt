The accurate detection and segmentation of defects in solar cell images are critical for improving manufacturing efficiency and ensuring product quality. However, existing methods often struggle with limited annotated datasets and imbalanced defect distributions. This paper introduces SIGAN, a novel image generation method designed specifically for solar cell defect segmentation and data augmentation. SIGAN leverages a generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture enhanced with a spatial attention mechanism to synthesize high-quality, realistic defect images while preserving the structural integrity of solar cells. The proposed approach generates diverse defect patterns, including micro-cracks and hotspots, which are challenging to capture in real-world datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that SIGAN significantly improves the performance of defect segmentation models by augmenting training data, achieving a 12% increase in mean intersection-over-union (mIoU) compared to state-of-the-art methods. Furthermore, the generated images exhibit high visual fidelity and diversity, enabling robust model generalization. SIGAN’s contributions lie in its ability to address dataset limitations and enhance defect detection accuracy, offering practical implications for solar cell manufacturing and quality control.  

**Keywords**: Solar cell defect detection, image generation, GAN, data augmentation, defect segmentation, spatial attention mechanism.
