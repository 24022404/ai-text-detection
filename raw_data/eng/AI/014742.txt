Person image generation is a challenging task in computer vision, requiring the synthesis of realistic and contextually consistent images. This paper introduces MUST-GAN, a novel framework for self-driven person image generation that leverages multi-level statistics transfer to enhance realism and coherence. The proposed approach addresses the limitations of existing methods by explicitly transferring statistical features—such as pose, texture, and style—across multiple levels of abstraction. MUST-GAN integrates a hierarchical architecture that captures both global and local attributes, enabling the generation of high-quality images that preserve fine-grained details and semantic consistency.  

The framework employs a generative adversarial network (GAN) enhanced with a multi-level feature alignment mechanism, ensuring robust transfer of statistical properties from source to target domains. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that MUST-GAN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of image quality, pose accuracy, and identity preservation. Additionally, the model exhibits superior generalization capabilities across diverse scenarios, including pose transfer and style adaptation.  

The key contributions of this work include the introduction of a multi-level statistics transfer mechanism, a self-driven training strategy that reduces reliance on paired data, and a comprehensive evaluation highlighting the framework's effectiveness. MUST-GAN opens new avenues for applications in virtual try-on, animation, and augmented reality, offering a scalable and efficient solution for person image generation.  

**Keywords**: Person image generation, GAN, multi-level statistics transfer, self-driven learning, pose transfer, virtual try-on.
