**Abstract**

Image super-resolution (SR) seeks to reconstruct high-resolution images from low-resolution counterparts, an essential task in computer vision applications such as medical imaging, surveillance, and satellite imagery. While generative adversarial networks (GANs) have advanced perceptual quality in SR, current approaches often struggle to balance fidelity and visual realism. This paper proposes RankSRGAN, a novel framework that integrates a ranker module into the GAN architecture to address this challenge. The ranker, trained to assess the perceptual quality of generated images, provides an additional supervisory signal during training, guiding the generator to produce more visually plausible and structurally accurate results. The RankSRGAN model incorporates the traditional adversarial loss, content loss, and a new ranking loss derived from human-perceived image quality metrics. Experimental evaluations on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate that RankSRGAN achieves superior performance compared to state-of-the-art SR methods, delivering enhanced details and perceptual quality as measured by both objective metrics and user studies. The proposed approach promotes a more effective trade-off between numerical accuracy and perceptual realism. RankSRGAN’s architecture can be flexibly adapted to various GAN-based enhancement tasks, indicating its potential for broader applications in image processing and computer vision. **Keywords:** Image super-resolution, GAN, ranker module, perceptual quality, deep learning, image enhancement.
