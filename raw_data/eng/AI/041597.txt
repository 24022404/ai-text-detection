This paper addresses the challenge of controlling large systems with interacting agents in a discrete-time setting by investigating mean field control problems that incorporate environment states. Traditional mean field control approaches often overlook the dynamic influence of a changing environment on agent behavior, a gap this research aims to fill.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework for discrete-time mean field control problems that integrates environment state dynamics into the decision-making process. The proposed approach combines mean field game theory with stochastic control techniques, allowing for a refined estimation of the agents' collective behavior and its interaction with fluctuating environmental factors. Our method leverages a discrete-time analog of mean field equations, incorporating environment states into the equations to achieve a versatile and comprehensive solution.

Results/Findings: The developed model demonstrates improved system performance over traditional approaches by accounting for environmental variations. Simulation results show that our method achieves superior control over the system, reflected in enhanced stability and efficiency, when tested against models that do not consider environmental states. Additionally, the approach allows for scalable computations, making it applicable to complex, large-scale systems.

Conclusion/Implications: Incorporating environment states into mean field control provides significant improvements and has far-reaching implications for systems where agent-environment interactions are critical. This research contributes a key advancement in the understanding and application of mean field control in discrete-time settings, paving the way for more resilient and adaptive multi-agent systems. Potential applications span across various domains like economics, network systems, and autonomous vehicle coordination, where real-time adaptability to environmental conditions is paramount.

Keywords: mean field control, discrete-time systems, environment states, stochastic control, multi-agent systems, dynamic environments.
