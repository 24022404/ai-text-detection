This paper proposes a novel Predictively Encoded Graph Convolutional Network (PE-GCN) for skeleton-based action recognition, designed to enhance noise robustness. The objective is to develop a model that can accurately recognize human actions from skeletal data despite the presence of noise or missing values. Our approach integrates predictive encoding into a graph convolutional network, allowing the model to learn robust features from noisy skeletal sequences. The PE-GCN model utilizes a predictive encoding scheme to forecast future skeletal poses, which are then used to update the model's parameters, thereby improving its ability to handle noisy data. Experimental results demonstrate that our PE-GCN model outperforms state-of-the-art methods on benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in recognition accuracy under noisy conditions. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of predictive encoding into graph convolutional networks for action recognition and the demonstration of its effectiveness in enhancing noise robustness. This work has important implications for applications such as human-computer interaction, surveillance, and healthcare, where robust action recognition is crucial. Key keywords: skeleton-based action recognition, graph convolutional networks, predictive encoding, noise robustness, human-computer interaction.
