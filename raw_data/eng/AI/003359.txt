This paper introduces Synthesize-It-Classifier (SIC), a novel generative classification framework that leverages recurrent self-analysis for improved performance. SIC learns to generate synthetic feature representations conditioned on class labels, enabling a classifier to distinguish between real and generated data. This approach facilitates a deeper understanding of class-specific feature distributions compared to traditional discriminative classifiers. Our method employs a recurrent architecture to iteratively refine generated features, fostering a more realistic synthesis process. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that SIC achieves competitive classification accuracy, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. Furthermore, we analyze the learned generative process to gain insights into feature importance and class separability. SIC offers a new perspective on classification by integrating generative and discriminative learning, with potential applications in areas such as anomaly detection and data augmentation. Keywords: Generative Classifier, Recurrent Neural Networks, Self-Analysis, Data Synthesis, Classification, Machine Learning.
