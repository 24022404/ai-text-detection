This paper introduces a novel encoder learning approach for image captioning specifically designed to address the challenges posed by heavy rain. The primary objective is to improve the accuracy and relevance of captions generated for images significantly affected by rain, where visibility and feature extraction are severely compromised. Our method, Semantic Visual Feature Matching (SVFM), leverages a pre-trained object detection model to extract semantic features from both the rainy image and a corresponding clear image (simulated or real). These features are then matched using an attention mechanism within the encoder, guiding the caption generation process to focus on salient objects despite the rain distortion. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world rainy image datasets demonstrate that SVFM significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art captioning models in terms of caption quality, BLEU scores, and human evaluation metrics. The proposed approach effectively mitigates the adverse effects of rain on visual feature extraction, leading to more descriptive and accurate captions. This research offers a valuable contribution to image understanding in adverse weather conditions, with potential applications in autonomous driving, surveillance, and environmental monitoring.

Keywords: Image Captioning, Heavy Rain, Semantic Visual Feature Matching, Attention Mechanism, Encoder Learning, Computer Vision.
