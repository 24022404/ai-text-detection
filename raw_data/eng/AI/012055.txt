Visual Place Recognition (VPR) is a critical component for autonomous navigation systems, enabling robust spatial awareness in environments that undergo significant, often unpredictable, changes over time. This paper addresses the challenge of achieving accurate VPR in both discretely and continuously changing environments without relying on labor-intensive labeled datasets. We propose a novel framework leveraging unsupervised learning techniques, combining feature extraction through convolutional neural networks with self-supervised domain adaptation and clustering methods to learn discriminative visual embeddings directly from unannotated image sequences. Evaluation is conducted using publicly available VPR datasets exhibiting both abrupt (discrete) and gradual (continuous) environmental variations, such as lighting, weather, and seasonal changes. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive recognition accuracy compared to state-of-the-art supervised methods, while offering superior adaptability to novel conditions and unseen environments. Additionally, our framework reduces dependency on manual annotation and offers scalable training for real-world deployment. The findings suggest that unsupervised learning represents a promising direction for resilient and efficient visual place recognition in dynamic settings. Keywords: visual place recognition, unsupervised learning, domain adaptation, neural networks, environmental changes, autonomous navigation.
