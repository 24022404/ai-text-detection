This study addresses the challenge of improving gesture recognition by exploring the integration of multi-rate and multi-modal temporal information into neural network architectures. The primary objective is to enhance the accuracy and robustness of gesture classification systems, which are pivotal in various applications, including human-computer interaction and virtual reality.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel architecture, the Temporal Enhanced Network (TEN), which leverages differential temporal rates and multiple modalities for comprehensive feature extraction. The architecture incorporates sophisticated search algorithms to dynamically determine optimal configurations for temporal data integration. Multi-rate analysis is coupled with modalities such as RGB, depth, and infrared data to allow a fusion that captures intricate temporal patterns.

Results/Findings: The implementation of our proposed network results in superior performance on standard gesture recognition benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements in accuracy compared to existing models. Comparative experiments illustrate the advantage of our approach over single-rate and single-modal networks, showing enhanced robustness in diverse lighting and background conditions. The search mechanism efficiently identifies the ideal network configurations that are adaptive to various contexts and user-specific gesture patterns.

Conclusion/Implications: Our research significantly contributes to the field of gesture recognition by introducing a flexible and adaptive network architecture capable of capturing complex temporal dynamics across multiple modalities. This innovation holds potential for widespread applications, particularly in improving interactive systems where accurate gesture recognition is paramount. The methodology outlined in this study opens avenues for further research on adaptive networks in dynamic and real-time applications.

Keywords: Gesture Recognition, Temporal Networks, Multi-Rate, Multi-Modal, Neural Architecture Search, Human-Computer Interaction.
