This paper presents an unsupervised approach to video decomposition, leveraging spatio-temporal iterative inference to separate videos into their constituent components. The objective is to decompose complex video scenes into foreground and background elements without prior knowledge or manual annotation. Our method employs a novel iterative framework that integrates spatial and temporal cues to refine the decomposition process. By utilizing a combination of convolutional neural networks and probabilistic graphical models, our approach can effectively capture the dynamic and static aspects of video content. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our method, outperforming existing state-of-the-art techniques in terms of decomposition accuracy and visual quality. The proposed approach has significant implications for various applications, including video editing, object tracking, and surveillance. Key contributions of this work include the introduction of a spatio-temporal iterative inference mechanism and the demonstration of its effectiveness in unsupervised video decomposition, highlighting the potential of this technique for automated video analysis and understanding. Relevant keywords: unsupervised video decomposition, spatio-temporal analysis, iterative inference, convolutional neural networks, probabilistic graphical models.
