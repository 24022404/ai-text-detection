This paper presents a novel blockwise approach for the detection of local defects in images. The primary objective is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of defect detection by analyzing images in smaller, more manageable blocks. Our method employs a statistical analysis of pixel intensity variations within each block to identify anomalous regions indicative of defects. Specifically, we calculate the mean and standard deviation of each block and compare these values against a global threshold determined dynamically from the entire image. Blocks exceeding this threshold are further analyzed using a texture-based feature extraction technique based on Gabor filters to differentiate between actual defects and noise. Experimental results on a diverse dataset of industrial images demonstrate that our blockwise method achieves a significant improvement in defect detection accuracy, with an F1-score of 0.92, compared to traditional whole-image analysis techniques. Furthermore, the blockwise approach reduces computational complexity, allowing for faster processing times, making it suitable for real-time applications. The proposed method offers a robust and efficient solution for automated defect detection, with potential applications in manufacturing quality control and other industrial inspection tasks.

**Keywords:** Defect Detection, Blockwise Processing, Image Analysis, Statistical Analysis, Texture Analysis, Gabor Filters, Quality Control.
