This paper presents a novel approach to molecular optimization through multimodal graph-to-graph translation. The objective is to develop an efficient method for generating optimized molecular structures with desired properties. Our approach utilizes a deep learning model that learns to translate input molecular graphs into output graphs with improved properties, such as solubility or bioactivity. The model is trained on a large dataset of molecular structures and their corresponding properties, and leverages techniques from graph neural networks and multimodal learning to capture complex relationships between molecular structures and properties. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in generating high-quality optimized molecular structures, outperforming existing methods in terms of efficiency and accuracy. The proposed method has significant implications for drug discovery, materials science, and other fields where molecular optimization is crucial. Key contributions include the introduction of a multimodal graph-to-graph translation framework, which enables the generation of diverse optimized molecular structures, and the development of a novel loss function that balances property optimization and molecular diversity. Relevant keywords: graph neural networks, multimodal learning, molecular optimization, drug discovery, materials science.
