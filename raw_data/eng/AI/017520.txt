Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for decentralized model training across distributed devices while preserving data privacy. However, challenges such as data heterogeneity and communication inefficiencies often hinder its performance. This paper introduces a novel approach, Federated Transfer Learning with Dynamic Gradient Aggregation (FTL-DGA), which addresses these limitations by integrating transfer learning principles with adaptive gradient aggregation mechanisms. The proposed method leverages pre-trained models to mitigate data heterogeneity and employs a dynamic aggregation strategy that optimizes communication overhead by selectively updating model parameters based on their contribution to global convergence. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that FTL-DGA achieves superior accuracy and faster convergence compared to traditional FL methods, particularly in scenarios with non-IID (non-independent and identically distributed) data distributions. Furthermore, the approach reduces communication costs by up to 40%, making it highly scalable for real-world applications. The results highlight the potential of FTL-DGA to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of federated learning systems, paving the way for broader adoption in privacy-sensitive domains such as healthcare and finance.  

**Keywords**: Federated Learning, Transfer Learning, Gradient Aggregation, Non-IID Data, Communication Efficiency, Privacy-Preserving Machine Learning.
