**Abstract**

Fluid simulation plays a crucial role in a wide range of applications, from visual effects in entertainment to engineering analysis in scientific domains. Traditional physics-based fluid simulation methods are computationally intensive and often require significant manual tuning to achieve desired results, particularly when adjusting simulation parameters. In this paper, we present Deep Fluids, a generative neural network architecture designed for the fast and flexible generation of parameterized fluid simulation data. Our approach leverages convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to learn the mapping between simulation parameters, such as initial conditions, boundary settings, and physical constants, and the resulting flow fields. The proposed model is trained on a diverse dataset of precomputed fluid simulations, enabling it to synthesize high-quality fluid flow outputs at a fraction of the computational cost of conventional solvers. Experimental results demonstrate that Deep Fluids generates visually realistic and physically plausible fluid behaviors, achieving significant speed-up during inference without sacrificing accuracy as compared to traditional methods. The network also exhibits strong generalization capabilities, allowing for interpolation and extrapolation across a wide range of parameter settings. Our findings highlight the potential of deep generative models to revolutionize fluid simulation workflows, offering efficiency and adaptability for both interactive applications and large-scale simulations. Key contributions include a novel neural architecture for parameterized fluid modeling, comprehensive evaluation of simulation quality, and insights into the integration of deep learning into physics-based graphics systems. Keywords: fluid simulation, generative networks, deep learning, parameterized modeling, convolutional neural networks, physical simulation.
