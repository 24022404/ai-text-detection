This paper addresses the challenge of distribution matching, where the goal is to find the best representation among multiple samples. The objective is to develop an efficient algorithm that can effectively match distributions, even when the samples are diverse and numerous. To achieve this, we propose a novel approach that leverages a combination of statistical analysis and machine learning techniques. Our method, termed "Best-of-Many-Samples" distribution matching, utilizes a hierarchical clustering framework to identify the most representative samples, followed by a fine-tuning process to refine the matching. The results show that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency, particularly in scenarios with high-dimensional data and large sample sizes. The key findings include improved distribution matching accuracy, reduced computational complexity, and enhanced robustness to noise and outliers. The implications of this research are significant, enabling applications in fields such as data integration, anomaly detection, and generative modeling. Our contributions include a novel algorithmic framework, a comprehensive evaluation methodology, and insights into the importance of representative sampling in distribution matching. Key keywords: distribution matching, statistical analysis, machine learning, hierarchical clustering, fine-tuning, data integration, anomaly detection, generative modeling.
