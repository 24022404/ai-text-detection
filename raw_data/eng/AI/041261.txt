Image inpainting, the task of reconstructing missing or damaged regions in images, remains a challenging problem in computer vision. This paper introduces the **Learning Pyramid-Context Encoder Network (LPCEN)**, a novel deep learning framework designed to achieve high-quality image inpainting by leveraging multi-scale contextual information. The proposed model integrates a pyramid-based encoder to capture hierarchical features at different resolutions, combined with a context-aware attention mechanism to refine the reconstruction process. By explicitly modeling both global and local structures, LPCEN ensures coherent and visually plausible results, even for large missing regions.  

We evaluate the performance of LPCEN on benchmark datasets, including Places2 and CelebA, and compare it against state-of-the-art methods. Experimental results demonstrate that LPCEN achieves superior performance in terms of both quantitative metrics (e.g., PSNR and SSIM) and qualitative visual assessments. The model's ability to preserve fine details and maintain structural consistency highlights its effectiveness in handling complex inpainting scenarios.  

The key contributions of this work include: (1) a pyramid-context encoder architecture that enhances feature representation across multiple scales, (2) an attention mechanism tailored for image inpainting to improve context-aware reconstruction, and (3) a comprehensive evaluation showcasing the model's robustness and generalization capabilities. The proposed approach has potential applications in photo editing, restoration of historical images, and autonomous systems requiring reliable image completion.  

**Keywords**: Image inpainting, deep learning, pyramid-context encoder, attention mechanism, multi-scale feature extraction, computer vision.
