Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown significant promise in simulated environments, but its application to real-world robotics remains challenging due to hardware constraints, environmental variability, and safety concerns. This study aims to benchmark state-of-the-art RL algorithms on real-world robotic platforms to evaluate their performance, robustness, and adaptability in practical scenarios. We employ a diverse set of RL algorithms, including Deep Q-Networks (DQN), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), and test them on a range of robotic tasks such as object manipulation, navigation, and dynamic control. Our experimental setup involves multiple robotic systems, including manipulator arms and mobile robots, operating in unstructured environments. Results indicate that SAC consistently outperforms other algorithms in terms of task completion rates and adaptability to environmental changes, while PPO demonstrates superior stability during training. However, all algorithms exhibit limitations in handling real-world noise and hardware constraints, highlighting the need for further research into robust RL frameworks. This work provides valuable insights into the practical deployment of RL in robotics, offering a foundation for future advancements in real-world applications. Key contributions include a comprehensive benchmarking framework and actionable recommendations for improving RL algorithms in robotics.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Robotics, Benchmarking, Deep Q-Networks, Proximal Policy Optimization, Soft Actor-Critic, Real-World Applications.
