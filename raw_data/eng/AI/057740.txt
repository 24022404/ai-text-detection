This paper explores the capabilities and limitations of time-lagged autoencoders in identifying slow modes within dynamical systems. These slow modes are crucial for understanding long-term system behaviors and can significantly aid in simplifying and predicting complex dynamics.

Methods/Approach: We utilize time-lagged autoencoders, a form of neural network designed to process sequential data, to detect and isolate slow modes in various dynamical systems. The autoencoders are trained on time-delayed data to uncover latent variables that correspond to slow dynamical features. A series of simulations are conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of the approach across multiple types of dynamical systems, ranging from simple to highly intricate models.

Results/Findings: Our findings indicate that time-lagged autoencoders are effective in capturing slow dynamics within certain parameter ranges, showing significant promise in reducing system complexity while preserving essential predictive features. However, the study also reveals limitations in the approach, particularly in systems with overlapping time scales or high noise levels, where the model's performance diminishes. Through comparisons with traditional methods, such as Proper Orthogonal Decomposition and Dynamic Mode Decomposition, we highlight the relative advantages and drawbacks of the autoencoders.

Conclusion/Implications: The research underscores the potential of time-lagged autoencoders as a tool for slow mode discovery in dynamical systems, offering a novel method for dimensionality reduction and long-term prediction. Notwithstanding their current limitations, with further refinement and hybrid strategies involving complementary techniques, these models offer a promising avenue for advancing our understanding and manipulation of complex dynamical systems. Keywords include slow mode discovery, time-lagged autoencoders, dynamical systems, dimensionality reduction, neural networks.
