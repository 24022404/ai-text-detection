Modern neural networks have achieved remarkable performance across various tasks, but their reliability is often compromised by poor calibration, where confidence scores do not align with actual predictive accuracy. This paper revisits the calibration of modern neural networks, aiming to address the gap between model confidence and true performance. We investigate the impact of architectural choices, training procedures, and regularization techniques on calibration, focusing on deep learning models such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformers. Through extensive experiments, we evaluate the effectiveness of existing calibration methods, including temperature scaling and ensemble techniques, and propose a novel adaptive calibration framework that dynamically adjusts confidence estimates based on model uncertainty and data distribution. Our results demonstrate significant improvements in calibration across multiple benchmarks, with our method outperforming state-of-the-art approaches in terms of expected calibration error (ECE) and reliability diagrams. The findings highlight the importance of calibration in deploying trustworthy AI systems, particularly in high-stakes applications such as healthcare and autonomous driving. This work provides actionable insights for improving neural network calibration and underscores the need for continued research in this critical area.  

**Keywords**: neural networks, calibration, confidence estimation, deep learning, uncertainty quantification, reliability, AI systems.
