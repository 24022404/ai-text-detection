Abstract:

This paper addresses the challenge of acquiring large-scale, high-quality datasets for training deep action recognition networks, a critical bottleneck in advancing video understanding applications. We propose a novel procedural video generation framework that creates synthetic action sequences by programmatically composing realistic environments, agents, and motion patterns. Our method leverages physics-based simulation, customizable animation libraries, and domain-randomization techniques to produce diverse and annotated video datasets that span a wide variety of human actions and contexts. We conduct extensive experiments by training state-of-the-art deep action recognition models on both procedurally generated and real-world datasets, evaluating their performance on standard benchmarks. The results demonstrate that networks trained with our synthetic videos achieve competitive accuracy and show enhanced generalization capabilities, particularly in scenarios with limited real data availability. Our approach reduces dependence on costly manual data collection and annotation, accelerates training cycles, and allows the flexible generation of rare or dangerous action events. This research contributes a scalable toolset for augmenting video datasets and improving the robustness of action recognition systems across surveillance, robotics, and human-computer interaction domains. Key keywords: procedural generation, synthetic videos, action recognition, deep learning, data augmentation, video understanding.
