Bayesian learning often benefits from integrating diverse data sources, but the computational cost grows significantly with each additional source. This paper addresses the challenge of selecting a near-optimal subset of data sources to minimize computational overhead while preserving predictive accuracy. We introduce a novel selection algorithm based on a submodular optimization framework that balances the information gain from each data source with its associated computational cost. Our approach leverages the theoretical properties of submodular functions to provide near-optimality guarantees. Experimental results on a range of benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method can achieve comparable or superior prediction performance to using all available data sources, while significantly reducing computational time. Specifically, we show a reduction in computation time of up to 70% with minimal loss in accuracy. The proposed method offers a practical solution for scalable Bayesian learning in scenarios with abundant, heterogeneous data.
Keywords: Bayesian Learning, Data Source Selection, Submodular Optimization, Scalable Learning, Active Learning.
