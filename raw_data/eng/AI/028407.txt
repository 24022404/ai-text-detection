This paper addresses the challenge of network clustering, with a specific focus on brain-network analysis, by introducing a novel approach based on Kernel-ARMA modeling and the Grassmannian framework. The objective is to develop a robust and scalable method for identifying meaningful clusters in complex network structures, particularly in the context of brain connectivity data. Traditional clustering techniques often struggle with high-dimensional and nonlinear network data, prompting the need for advanced methodologies. Our approach leverages Kernel-ARMA modeling to capture temporal dependencies and nonlinear relationships within network data, while the Grassmannian framework provides a geometric perspective for representing and comparing subspaces of network features. Together, these components enable efficient and interpretable clustering of brain networks. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world brain-network datasets demonstrate the superiority of the proposed method over existing techniques, achieving higher accuracy and computational efficiency. The findings highlight the potential of this approach for applications in neuroscience, such as identifying biomarkers for neurological disorders and understanding functional brain connectivity. This work contributes to the field by offering a mathematically grounded and scalable solution for network clustering, with broader implications for analyzing complex networks in other domains.  

**Keywords**: Network clustering, Kernel-ARMA modeling, Grassmannian, Brain networks, Nonlinear data analysis, High-dimensional data.
