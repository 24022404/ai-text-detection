This paper proposes D-NetPAD, a novel iris presentation attack detector designed to provide explainable and interpretable results. The objective is to enhance the security of iris recognition systems by accurately detecting and preventing presentation attacks, such as printed photos or prosthetic eyes. Our approach utilizes a deep learning-based model that incorporates attention mechanisms and layer-wise relevance propagation to offer insights into the decision-making process. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of D-NetPAD, achieving a significant reduction in presentation attack detection errors compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. The key findings highlight the importance of explainability in iris presentation attack detection, enabling a deeper understanding of the model's strengths and weaknesses. The contributions of this research include the development of a transparent and trustworthy iris presentation attack detection system, with potential applications in various biometric authentication scenarios. Key keywords: iris presentation attack detection, explainable AI, interpretable machine learning, biometric security, deep learning.
