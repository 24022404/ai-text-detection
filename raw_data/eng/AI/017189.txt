Scene Graph Generation (SGG) is a critical task in computer vision, aiming to model the relationships between objects in an image as a structured graph. Despite significant progress, existing methods often struggle with generalizing across diverse domains due to the inherent variability in object and relationship distributions. This paper introduces a novel approach, Target-Tailored Source-Transformation (TTST), which addresses this challenge by dynamically adapting source domain data to align with the target domain's characteristics. TTST leverages a transformer-based architecture to learn domain-invariant features while preserving the semantic integrity of object relationships. The proposed method employs a two-stage process: first, it identifies domain-specific discrepancies through a contrastive learning framework, and second, it applies a tailored transformation to the source data to minimize these discrepancies. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that TTST outperforms state-of-the-art methods in cross-domain SGG, achieving significant improvements in relationship prediction accuracy. The results highlight the effectiveness of TTST in enhancing generalization capabilities, making it a promising solution for real-world applications where domain shifts are prevalent. This work contributes to advancing SGG by introducing a domain-adaptive framework that bridges the gap between source and target domains, paving the way for more robust and scalable scene understanding systems.  

**Keywords**: Scene Graph Generation, Domain Adaptation, Transformer Models, Contrastive Learning, Cross-Domain Generalization.
