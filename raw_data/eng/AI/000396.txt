This paper addresses the challenge of generating relevant counter-examples from a Positive Unlabeled (PU) dataset for image classification tasks. While PU learning focuses on leveraging only positive and unlabeled data, the absence of explicitly labeled negative examples often limits model robustness and generalization. To overcome this, we propose a novel framework that systematically generates high-quality counter-examples by leveraging adversarial techniques and feature-space analysis. Our approach employs a combination of generative adversarial networks (GANs) and clustering algorithms to identify and synthesize plausible negative examples that are semantically distinct from the positive class. Experimental results on benchmark image datasets demonstrate that the generated counter-examples significantly improve classification accuracy and model interpretability compared to traditional PU learning methods. The proposed method achieves a 12% improvement in F1-score and reduces false positives by 18% on average. This work contributes to the field by providing a scalable and effective solution for enhancing PU learning frameworks, with potential applications in medical imaging, anomaly detection, and other domains where labeled negative data is scarce. Keywords: Positive Unlabeled Learning, Counter-Example Generation, Image Classification, Adversarial Networks, Clustering.
