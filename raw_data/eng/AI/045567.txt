This paper emphasizes the crucial role of parameter selection in machine learning and artificial intelligence, highlighting its often-overlooked significance in model performance and overall system efficiency. The objective is to investigate the impact of parameter selection on predictive accuracy, computational complexity, and model interpretability. A comprehensive approach is employed, combining theoretical analysis with empirical evaluations of various parameter selection methods, including grid search, random search, and Bayesian optimization. The results show that careful parameter selection can lead to substantial improvements in model performance, with reductions in error rates and increases in precision. The findings also underscore the importance of considering the interplay between parameters and the potential for overfitting. This research contributes to the field by demonstrating the necessity of rigorous parameter selection in machine learning pipelines, with implications for applications in data science, natural language processing, and computer vision. Key contributions include the development of a framework for systematic parameter selection and the identification of best practices for avoiding common pitfalls. Relevant keywords: parameter selection, machine learning, model performance, hyperparameter tuning, artificial intelligence.
