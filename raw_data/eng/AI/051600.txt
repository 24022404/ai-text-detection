Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have demonstrated significant success in processing complex graph-structured data, utilizing either spectral or spatial approaches to encode node relationships. However, a clear gap remains between these two domains, with spectral methods often capturing global graph properties and spatial methods excelling at local neighborhood aggregation. This paper presents a novel framework designed to effectively bridge the spectral and spatial domains in GNNs, enabling more robust learning and representation of graph data. Our approach introduces a hybrid model that integrates frequency-domain filters with localized message passing mechanisms, allowing for adaptive balancing between global and local feature extraction. Extensive experiments on several benchmark graph datasets demonstrate that our model outperforms state-of-the-art spectral and spatial GNNs in terms of node classification and graph classification tasks, exhibiting improved accuracy and generalization. The proposed method provides important insights into the complementarity of spectral and spatial perspectives in graph representation learning and offers a flexible architecture suitable for diverse graph applications such as social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics. Keywords: graph neural networks, spectral methods, spatial methods, hybrid models, representation learning, graph data, node classification.
