This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the resolution of brain MRI images, a critical task for improving diagnostic accuracy and enabling detailed analysis in medical imaging. We propose a novel super-resolution framework that leverages overcomplete dictionaries and nonlocal similarity to reconstruct high-resolution images from low-resolution inputs. The approach combines sparse representation techniques with nonlocal self-similarity priors, enabling the capture of intricate structural details and textures inherent in brain MRI data. The overcomplete dictionary is learned from high-resolution training patches, while nonlocal similarity ensures the preservation of global structural consistency. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing super-resolution techniques in terms of both quantitative metrics (e.g., PSNR and SSIM) and visual quality. The framework achieves significant improvements in edge sharpness, texture clarity, and artifact reduction, making it highly suitable for clinical applications. This research contributes to the field of medical image processing by introducing a robust and efficient super-resolution method that enhances the utility of MRI data for diagnosis and research. Key innovations include the integration of overcomplete dictionaries with nonlocal similarity, offering a scalable and adaptable solution for various imaging modalities.  

**Keywords**: Super-resolution, MRI, overcomplete dictionaries, nonlocal similarity, sparse representation, medical imaging.
