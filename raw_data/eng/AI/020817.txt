The objective of this research is to enhance the representation learning of pedestrian trajectories through the development of a novel Actor-Critic Sequence-to-Sequence Autoencoder. This approach aims to address the complexities inherent in accurately predicting pedestrian movements, which are influenced by a multitude of factors and environmental interactions. The proposed method integrates an actor-critic framework with a sequence-to-sequence autoencoder architecture, enabling the system to learn robust representations of trajectory data. Using deep learning techniques, the model effectively captures temporal dependencies and nuances in pedestrian paths. Experimentation with real-world trajectory datasets demonstrates that our method excels in terms of accuracy and generalization compared to conventional models, such as recurrent neural networks and convolutional sequence models. The findings underscore significant improvements in trajectory prediction metrics, thereby highlighting the effectiveness of embedding reinforcement learning principles into traditional autoencoder structures. This research contributes a novel methodology that not only advances predictive accuracy but also offers insights into the underlying patterns of pedestrian behavior, with potential applications in autonomous vehicle navigation, urban planning, and crowd management systems. Key keywords include representation learning, pedestrian trajectories, actor-critic models, sequence-to-sequence autoencoder, and deep learning.
