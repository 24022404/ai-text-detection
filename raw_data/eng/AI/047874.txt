This paper investigates the challenge of evaluating the synthesizability of dynamic texture samples, a pivotal element in enhancing immersive experiences in various computer graphics applications. The ability to automatically determine which samples can be effectively synthesized is crucial for efficient and high-quality texture generation.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel computational model that leverages machine learning techniques to predict the synthesizability of dynamic textures. Our approach involves the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to extract spatiotemporal features, combined with a classification framework to assess the synthesizability likelihood of given texture samples. The model is trained on a carefully curated dataset of dynamic textures, allowing it to learn complex patterns and variations pertinent to dynamic texture synthesis.

Results/Findings: The proposed model achieved notable accuracy in predicting the synthesizability of dynamic textures, surpassing traditional heuristic-based methods. Our findings illustrate that the model can effectively differentiate between high and low synthesizability samples, providing substantial improvements in texture quality and synthesis speed in generative applications. Comparative evaluations demonstrate the model's robustness and generalization across diverse texture categories.

Conclusion/Implications: This research introduces an effective tool for dynamic texture analysis, marking a significant contribution to the fields of computer graphics and texture synthesis. By streamlining the selection process for texture samples, the model facilitates enhanced visual fidelity and performance in real-time rendering applications. Future work may explore extending the framework to incorporate user-defined criteria for custom texture applications, broadening its utility and impact.

Keywords: dynamic textures, texture synthesis, synthesizability prediction, machine learning, convolutional neural networks, computer graphics.
