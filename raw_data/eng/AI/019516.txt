In the field of causal inference, LiNGAM (Linear Non-Gaussian Acyclic Model) stands out for its ability to uncover causal relationships from observational data. This paper addresses the challenge of applying LiNGAM in contexts where the number of variables exceeds the number of observations, a situation often encountered in high-dimensional data scenarios. We propose a novel approach that adapts the traditional LiNGAM to effectively handle this imbalance. Our method leverages sparsity-promoting techniques and dimensionality reduction to maintain robustness in the causal discovery process while avoiding overfitting, a common issue in such settings. Through simulated experiments and real-world datasets, we demonstrate the enhanced performance of our extended LiNGAM method compared to existing models, particularly in its ability to accurately identify causal structures with limited data points. The results underscore the model's utility in fields like genomics and economics, where data dimensionality is often prohibitive. Our research contributes a scalable and efficient solution to extracting meaningful causal insights, thus broadening the application of LiNGAM in complex data environments. Keywords: LiNGAM, causal inference, high-dimensional data, sparsity, dimensionality reduction.
