**Abstract**

Multiview learning aims to integrate information from multiple data representations to improve learning performance across various tasks. While significant progress has been made in both supervised and self-supervised paradigms, existing methods often treat correlation modeling and supervision separately, potentially overlooking shared principles. This paper introduces a unifying framework that leverages latent correlation analysis to bridge multiview learning and self-supervised strategies. We propose a novel approach that models the underlying dependencies among views using latent variable models, enabling the discovery of cross-view shared representations without the need for explicit manual supervision. The method employs a self-supervision signal derived from optimizing latent correlations, thus enhancing feature alignment and consistency across diverse data views. Experiments are conducted on standard multiview datasets, including image, text, and sensor domains. The proposed approach achieves superior or comparable results to state-of-the-art methods in classification and retrieval tasks, demonstrating robustness in scenarios with limited or noisy labels. Furthermore, our analysis provides theoretical insights into the relationship between latent correlation maximization and mutual information objectives commonly used in self-supervised learning. This work offers a comprehensive perspective that unifies disparate strands of multiview representation learning and self-supervision, paving the way for more flexible and generalizable learning algorithms. **Keywords:** multiview learning, latent correlation, self-supervised learning, representation learning, cross-modal analysis.
