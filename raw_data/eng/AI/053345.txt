Scene graph to image generation is a challenging task in computer vision, aiming to synthesize realistic images from structured semantic representations. This paper introduces a novel framework for learning canonical representations that bridge the gap between scene graphs and image generation. The proposed approach leverages a hierarchical encoder-decoder architecture, where the encoder maps scene graphs into a canonical latent space, and the decoder generates high-quality images conditioned on these representations. To enhance the model's ability to capture complex relationships and object attributes, we incorporate a graph attention mechanism and a multi-scale feature fusion strategy. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing approaches in terms of image quality, semantic consistency, and diversity. The framework achieves significant improvements in generating visually coherent images while preserving the structural details specified in the input scene graphs. This work contributes to advancing the field of image synthesis by providing a robust and interpretable solution for scene graph-based generation, with potential applications in areas such as virtual reality, content creation, and autonomous systems.  

**Keywords**: scene graph, image generation, canonical representations, graph attention, multi-scale fusion, computer vision.
