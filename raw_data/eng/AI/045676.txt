This paper investigates the local geometry of hyperedges in hypergraphs, with a focus on its applications to modeling and analyzing social networks. Hypergraphs provide a natural framework for capturing complex, multi-way interactions that are prevalent in social systems, such as group dynamics and community structures. The objective of this study is to develop a novel approach for characterizing the geometric properties of hyperedges, enabling a deeper understanding of their roles in network topology and behavior. We propose a computational framework that leverages geometric embeddings and topological data analysis to quantify hyperedge interactions and their influence on network connectivity. Our method is evaluated on real-world social network datasets, demonstrating its ability to uncover hidden patterns and relationships that are not captured by traditional graph-based models. Results show significant improvements in community detection accuracy and robustness compared to existing techniques. The findings highlight the importance of hyperedge geometry in understanding social network dynamics and offer new insights for applications such as influence maximization, anomaly detection, and recommendation systems. This work contributes to the growing body of research on hypergraph analytics and provides a foundation for future studies on complex network structures.  

**Keywords**: hypergraphs, hyperedges, social networks, geometric embeddings, topological data analysis, community detection.
