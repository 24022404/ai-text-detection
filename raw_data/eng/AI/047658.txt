This paper introduces a light-weight method to enhance the interpretability and explainability of classification networks using Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM). While Grad-CAM has proven effective in visualizing decision-making processes in deep learning models, its reliance on complex architectures often limits its applicability in resource-constrained environments. Our approach proposes a streamlined framework that reduces computational overhead while maintaining or improving the quality of interpretability. By optimizing the feature extraction process and integrating efficient gradient computation techniques, the method achieves comparable or superior performance to traditional Grad-CAM implementations. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant reductions in inference time and memory usage, with minimal impact on interpretability accuracy. Furthermore, the proposed method is evaluated against state-of-the-art techniques, showcasing its robustness and scalability across diverse classification tasks. This work contributes to the growing demand for efficient and transparent AI systems, particularly in domains requiring real-time decision-making and resource efficiency. The implications of this research extend to applications in healthcare, autonomous systems, and edge computing, where interpretability and computational efficiency are critical.  

**Keywords**: Grad-CAM, interpretability, explainability, classification networks, light-weight methods, deep learning, AI transparency.
