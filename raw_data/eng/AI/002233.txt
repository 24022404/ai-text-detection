Video-based person re-identification (Re-ID) is a critical task in surveillance and security systems, aiming to match individuals across non-overlapping camera views. Despite recent advancements, challenges such as occlusions, pose variations, and background clutter persist, limiting the performance of existing methods. This paper proposes a novel framework, Multi-Granularity Reference-Aided Attentive Feature Aggregation (MGRAFA), to address these challenges. The approach leverages multi-granularity feature extraction, combining global and local cues to capture both holistic and fine-grained details of individuals. A reference-aided attention mechanism is introduced to dynamically focus on discriminative regions, enhancing feature representation by suppressing irrelevant background information. Additionally, an attentive aggregation module is employed to fuse temporal features across video frames, ensuring robust and consistent representations. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that MGRAFA achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and robustness. The proposed framework not only improves person Re-ID performance but also provides insights into effective feature aggregation and attention mechanisms for video-based applications. This research contributes to advancing video-based Re-ID systems, with potential applications in security, surveillance, and smart city technologies.  

**Keywords**: Video-based person re-identification, multi-granularity features, attentive feature aggregation, reference-aided attention, temporal fusion.
