The increasing complexity of real-world applications necessitates the development of robust frameworks capable of handling heterogeneous multi-task learning (MTL) scenarios. This paper addresses the challenge of automatically constructing multi-network models tailored for heterogeneous MTL, where tasks vary significantly in their data types, structures, and objectives. We propose a novel approach that leverages adaptive neural architecture search (NAS) techniques to dynamically design task-specific subnetworks while optimizing shared representations across tasks. Our method integrates a hierarchical optimization framework that balances task-specific performance and inter-task dependencies, ensuring efficient resource utilization and scalability. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms existing MTL methods in terms of accuracy, computational efficiency, and adaptability to diverse task requirements. The framework also exhibits superior generalization capabilities, making it suitable for applications in domains such as healthcare, natural language processing, and computer vision. This research contributes a scalable and automated solution for heterogeneous MTL, paving the way for more effective and versatile multi-task learning systems.  

**Keywords**: multi-task learning, neural architecture search, heterogeneous tasks, adaptive models, multi-network frameworks.
