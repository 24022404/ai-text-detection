This paper investigates hierarchical representation learning within the context of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), aiming to enhance the efficiency and scalability of sequential decision-making algorithms. We propose a novel framework that automatically discovers multi-level hierarchical structures in the state and action spaces of MDPs using deep learning techniques. Our approach integrates feature abstraction modules with hierarchical policy optimization, enabling agents to learn high-level state representations and corresponding sub-policies in a data-driven manner. We evaluate the method on a suite of benchmark environments ranging from discrete to continuous domains. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in learning speed, sample efficiency, and final performance compared to flat and non-hierarchical baselines. The hierarchical representations also facilitate better generalization and transfer to related tasks. These findings suggest that hierarchical representation learning holds considerable promise for addressing the challenges of high-dimensional state spaces and long-horizon planning in complex MDPs. Potential applications include reinforcement learning, autonomous robotics, and adaptive control systems. Key keywords: hierarchical reinforcement learning, Markov Decision Processes, representation learning, policy optimization, deep learning, abstraction, transfer learning.
