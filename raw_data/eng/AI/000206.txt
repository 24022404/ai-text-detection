Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for node classification tasks, leveraging graph-structured data to achieve state-of-the-art performance. However, the lack of standardized evaluation pipelines has led to inconsistent comparisons across studies, making it challenging to assess the true effectiveness of different GNN architectures. This paper introduces a comprehensive pipeline designed to enable fair and reproducible comparisons of GNNs in node classification tasks. The proposed approach includes standardized data preprocessing, consistent hyperparameter tuning, and rigorous evaluation metrics across multiple benchmark datasets. We evaluate several popular GNN models, including Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), Graph Attention Networks (GATs), and GraphSAGE, using this pipeline. Our results demonstrate significant variations in model performance under different evaluation conditions, highlighting the importance of a unified framework for comparison. The pipeline not only provides insights into the strengths and limitations of existing GNN architectures but also serves as a foundation for future research in graph-based machine learning. By promoting transparency and reproducibility, this work contributes to advancing the field of GNNs and supports the development of more robust and generalizable models for node classification tasks.  

**Keywords**: Graph Neural Networks, Node Classification, Fair Comparison, Evaluation Pipeline, Reproducibility, GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE.
