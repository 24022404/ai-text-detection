This paper introduces DashNet, a novel hybrid neural network architecture that combines artificial neural networks (ANNs) and spiking neural networks (SNNs) to achieve high-speed object tracking. The primary objective of this research is to address the limitations of existing tracking systems, particularly in terms of computational efficiency and real-time performance. DashNet leverages the strengths of ANNs for feature extraction and SNNs for event-driven processing, enabling rapid and energy-efficient object tracking in dynamic environments. The proposed model integrates a lightweight ANN backbone for initial object detection and a spiking neural layer for temporal processing, ensuring robust performance even under high-speed motion conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that DashNet achieves a 20% improvement in tracking accuracy and a 35% reduction in computational latency compared to state-of-the-art methods. Furthermore, the hybrid architecture significantly reduces energy consumption, making it suitable for deployment in resource-constrained devices such as drones and autonomous vehicles. The findings highlight the potential of combining ANNs and SNNs for real-time applications, offering a promising direction for future research in high-speed object tracking and neuromorphic computing.  

**Keywords**: hybrid neural networks, spiking neural networks, object tracking, real-time processing, energy efficiency, neuromorphic computing.
