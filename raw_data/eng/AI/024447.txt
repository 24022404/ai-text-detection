This paper presents a novel approach to predicting multiphase flow behavior using deep neural networks. The objective is to accurately model the complex interactions between different phases in various flow regimes, addressing a long-standing challenge in the field of fluid dynamics. Our method employs a bespoke deep learning architecture, leveraging convolutional and recurrent neural network components to capture spatial and temporal patterns in multiphase flow data. The results demonstrate significant improvements in prediction accuracy compared to traditional computational fluid dynamics methods, with mean absolute errors reduced by up to 30%. Key findings include the ability to generalize across diverse flow conditions and the identification of critical flow features that influence prediction performance. The implications of this research are substantial, enabling more accurate and efficient simulation of multiphase flows in various industries, including oil and gas, chemical processing, and energy production. By harnessing the power of deep learning, this work contributes to the development of more reliable and optimized multiphase flow systems, with potential applications in fields such as pipeline transportation, reactor design, and process intensification. Relevant keywords: multiphase flow, deep neural networks, fluid dynamics, computational modeling, flow prediction.
