This paper investigates the application of reinforcement learning (RL) techniques to enhance spectrum sharing in radar systems. The objective is to develop intelligent radar systems capable of dynamically adapting their transmission strategies to coexist with other users in a shared spectrum environment, mitigating interference and improving overall spectrum utilization. We evaluate several RL algorithms, including Q-learning, SARSA, and Deep Q-Networks (DQN), in a simulated spectrum sharing scenario with varying user densities and interference levels. The performance of each algorithm is assessed based on key metrics such as probability of detection, interference temperature, and spectrum utilization efficiency. Results demonstrate that DQN significantly outperforms Q-learning and SARSA, achieving up to a 20% improvement in detection probability while maintaining acceptable interference levels. The findings highlight the potential of RL, particularly DQN, for developing autonomous and adaptive radar systems that can effectively manage spectrum resources in complex and dynamic environments. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Spectrum Sharing, Radar, Cognitive Radio, Deep Q-Networks, Interference Management.
