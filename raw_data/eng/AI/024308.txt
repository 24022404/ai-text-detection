Neural sequence models have achieved remarkable success in various applications such as machine translation, text summarization, and speech recognition. However, there remains a critical trade-off between computational cost and output quality, particularly as models become more complex and resource-intensive. This paper investigates methods for dynamically controlling the computation versus quality trade-off in neural sequence models. We introduce a novel adaptive inference framework that adjusts decoding complexity based on user-defined quality constraints or computational budgets. Our approach leverages early exit strategies, adaptive beam sizes, and progressive layer utilization to provide flexible response times while maintaining acceptable generation quality. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our methods can reduce inference latency by up to 40% with minimal degradation in output quality when compared to conventional static decoding strategies. Furthermore, we provide an in-depth analysis of the impact of various adaptive mechanisms on both performance and quality metrics. Our findings highlight practical avenues for deploying neural sequence models in resource-constrained environments and real-time applications, offering significant improvements in efficiency without compromising user experience. Keywords: neural sequence models, computation-quality trade-off, adaptive inference, efficiency, early exit, dynamic decoding.
