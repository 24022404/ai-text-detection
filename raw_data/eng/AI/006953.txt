Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful approach for learning visual representations without explicit supervision. However, existing methods often rely on simple similarity metrics, potentially limiting their ability to capture complex data distributions. This paper introduces a novel contrastive learning framework leveraging deep Bregman divergence to enhance representation learning. We propose a neural network architecture that learns a feature space where Bregman divergence accurately reflects the semantic similarity between samples. Our method adaptively learns the Bregman divergence function, allowing for flexible and data-dependent similarity measurements. Experimental results on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance compared to existing contrastive learning methods. The learned representations exhibit improved transferability and robustness. We further analyze the properties of the learned Bregman divergence, providing insights into its ability to capture fine-grained semantic relationships. The proposed method offers a promising direction for unsupervised visual representation learning by utilizing adaptive, geometrically-motivated similarity measures.

Keywords: Contrastive Learning, Visual Representation, Bregman Divergence, Unsupervised Learning, Deep Learning.
