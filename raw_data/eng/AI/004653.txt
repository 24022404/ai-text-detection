Semantic segmentation is a critical task in computer vision, enabling precise pixel-level understanding of images. However, existing models often struggle with robustness, particularly in scenarios with noisy or incomplete data. This paper introduces a novel approach, termed *Painting-by-Numbers*, to enhance the robustness of semantic segmentation models. The proposed method leverages a structured data augmentation technique that systematically alters pixel-level annotations while preserving semantic consistency. By simulating diverse and challenging scenarios, the approach trains models to generalize better across varying conditions. We evaluate the method on benchmark datasets, including Cityscapes and Pascal VOC, and demonstrate significant improvements in model robustness, achieving a 12% increase in mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) under noisy conditions compared to baseline models. Additionally, the approach shows superior performance in cross-domain adaptation tasks, highlighting its versatility. The results suggest that *Painting-by-Numbers* not only improves segmentation accuracy but also enhances the reliability of models in real-world applications, such as autonomous driving and medical imaging. This work contributes a practical and scalable solution to address robustness challenges in semantic segmentation, paving the way for more dependable computer vision systems.  

**Keywords**: semantic segmentation, robustness, data augmentation, computer vision, deep learning, image understanding.
