Understanding human attention mechanisms in video is crucial for various applications, including video summarization and action recognition. This paper introduces a novel approach for learning to attend to relevant regions in videos by leveraging human eye fixation data. We propose a deep learning model that incorporates a spatial attention mechanism, trained to predict regions of interest based on eye-tracking data collected from human viewers. The model utilizes a convolutional neural network (CNN) to extract spatial features from video frames and a recurrent neural network (RNN) to capture temporal dependencies. The attention mechanism learns to weigh these features based on their relevance to the observed eye fixations. Experimental results on benchmark video datasets demonstrate that our model accurately predicts human attention and outperforms existing saliency prediction methods. Furthermore, we show that incorporating the learned attention maps into video classification tasks improves performance, indicating the effectiveness of the proposed approach in capturing relevant visual information. This work provides a novel framework for understanding and modeling human attention in video, with potential applications in content analysis and human-computer interaction.

Keywords: Video attention, Eye tracking, Deep learning, Spatial attention, Recurrent neural networks, Saliency prediction, Action recognition.
