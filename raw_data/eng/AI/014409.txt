This paper addresses the challenge of effective representation learning by introducing a novel framework that leverages the integration of adversarial learning and contrastive techniques with optimal transport theory. The goal is to enhance the quality and robustness of learned representations for diverse machine learning tasks.

Methods/Approach: We propose the Adversarially-Contrastive Optimal Transport (ACOT) model, which synergistically combines adversarial learning with contrastive methods to improve representation learning. The use of optimal transport theory facilitates the alignment of data distributions, allowing for more effective feature extraction and transformation processes. This framework enhances the model's capacity to learn invariant and discriminative features by optimizing the transport map through adversarial objectives.

Results/Findings: The ACOT model demonstrates superior performance in benchmark datasets, surpassing existing representation learning techniques in terms of accuracy and robustness. Our experimental evaluations reveal that ACOT not only boosts classification and clustering outcomes but also excels in handling domain adaptation tasks. The model's ability to effectively disentangle complex data structures is underscored by qualitative assessments and visualization analyses.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of adversarial and contrastive objectives with optimal transport presents a significant advancement in the field of representation learning. The ACOT framework provides a versatile tool that can be applied to a range of applications, including image recognition, natural language processing, and anomaly detection. Its innovative approach paves the way for more resilient and adaptable AI systems, underscoring the potential of this method to redefine state-of-the-art performance across various domains.

Keywords: representation learning, adversarial learning, contrastive learning, optimal transport, domain adaptation, data alignment, feature extraction.
