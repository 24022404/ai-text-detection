Graph-structured data is ubiquitous in various domains, from social networks to molecular structures, yet effectively analyzing such data remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces Convolutional Kernel Networks (CKNs) tailored for graph-structured data, aiming to bridge the gap between traditional convolutional approaches and the irregular, non-Euclidean nature of graphs. The proposed method leverages kernel-based techniques to generalize convolutional operations, enabling the extraction of meaningful features from graph data without requiring explicit graph embeddings. By combining spectral graph theory with kernel methods, the approach captures both local and global structural information, ensuring robust performance across diverse graph types. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed CKNs outperform existing graph neural networks (GNNs) in tasks such as node classification and graph classification, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency. The framework also exhibits strong generalization capabilities, making it suitable for applications in bioinformatics, recommendation systems, and network analysis. This work contributes a novel, scalable, and interpretable solution for graph-based learning, paving the way for further advancements in graph representation learning.  

**Keywords**: Convolutional Kernel Networks, Graph-Structured Data, Graph Neural Networks, Kernel Methods, Spectral Graph Theory, Node Classification, Graph Classification.
