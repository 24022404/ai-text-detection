This paper addresses the pressing need for more precise classification of medical reports, aiming to enhance the accessibility and usability of medical data through a semi-supervised natural language processing (NLP) approach. By targeting fine-grained classification, the research seeks to improve the accuracy and detail in categorizing medical documentation, which is critical for patient care, research, and resource allocation.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel semi-supervised learning framework that leverages a small amount of labeled data complemented by a large corpus of unlabeled medical texts. The approach utilizes advanced NLP techniques, incorporating transformer-based architectures renowned for their contextual understanding capabilities. The system employs a dual-phase training process, refining classification models through both supervised learning with annotated data and unsupervised learning leveraging self-training algorithms.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate a significant improvement in classification accuracy and granularity over traditional supervised methods. The proposed model achieves superior performance, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled datasets, indicating its robustness in real-world applications. Comparative analyses highlight its efficiency in distinguishing nuanced categories and its adaptability across various medical subdomains.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings underscore the potential of semi-supervised NLP in the medical field, offering a scalable solution for enhanced report categorization. This approach not only facilitates more informed clinical decision-making and research initiatives but also provides a foundational step towards automated, intelligent processing of medical literature. The research contributes to the broader field of medical informatics, showcasing the application of AI in improving healthcare systems.

Keywords: semi-supervised learning, natural language processing, NLP, fine-grained classification, medical reports, transformer models, healthcare AI.
