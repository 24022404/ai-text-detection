Neural sequence models offer state-of-the-art performance in various tasks, but their computational cost during inference remains a significant bottleneck. This paper investigates methods for dynamically controlling the trade-off between computational cost and output quality in neural sequence models. We introduce a novel approach that allows the model to adaptively adjust the number of computational steps based on input complexity and desired quality level. Our method incorporates a learned controller that predicts the optimal number of steps, balancing accuracy and latency. We evaluate our approach on machine translation and text summarization tasks, demonstrating significant reductions in computation time with minimal impact on output quality. Results show that our adaptive approach outperforms static computation budgets, achieving up to a 40% reduction in computation with less than a 1% drop in BLEU score. The findings suggest a promising direction for deploying efficient neural sequence models in resource-constrained environments. Keywords: Neural Sequence Models, Computational Efficiency, Adaptive Computation, Machine Translation, Text Summarization, Inference Optimization.
