Abstract  
Human action generation—the synthesis of realistic human motion sequences—is fundamental for applications in animation, virtual reality, and data augmentation for recognition models. This paper presents a novel Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework tailored to human action generation. Our model comprises a temporally aware generator that maps random noise and action labels into sequences of 3D joint coordinates, and a spatial–temporal discriminator that assesses both motion plausibility and label consistency. To capture long-term dependencies and preserve smooth transitions, we integrate a gated recurrent unit (GRU)–based temporal module within the generator and employ multi-scale convolutional filters in the discriminator.  

We evaluate our approach on two benchmark datasets, Human3.6M and NTU RGB+D, comparing against state-of-the-art motion synthesis methods. Quantitative metrics—including Fréchet Gesture Distance (FGD) and classification accuracy under a pre-trained action recognizer—demonstrate a 22% reduction in motion distribution divergence and a 15% improvement in label coherence. Qualitative assessments confirm that our GAN produces more diverse and lifelike action sequences, with fewer artifacts and abrupt transitions.  

Our contributions are threefold: (1) a conditional temporal GAN architecture for fine-grained action control; (2) a dual-path discriminator that enforces both geometric and temporal realism; and (3) extensive validation showing significant gains in diversity and fidelity. The proposed method paves the way for scalable human motion generation in interactive environments, game development, and training data synthesis.  

Keywords: human action generation, generative adversarial networks, motion synthesis, temporal GAN, deep learning.
