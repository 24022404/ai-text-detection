Abstract:

In the era of multimodal data, effective integration of heterogeneous information sources such as images, text, and audio represents a significant challenge in Information Technology. This paper addresses the problem of cross-modal learning by introducing a novel framework that leverages pairwise constraints to enhance the alignment and understanding between different modalities. Our approach formulates the learning task as an optimization problem, where pairwise relationships between samples across modalities are used to guide the representation learning process. We implement a deep neural architecture capable of encoding diverse data types into a common latent space, using the pairwise constraints to enforce semantic consistency and improve retrieval and classification tasks. Experimental results on several publicly available datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms traditional cross-modal matching techniques and recent deep learning baselines, achieving higher accuracy and robustness in scenarios involving image-text and audio-text associations. The findings highlight the effectiveness of pairwise constraints in mitigating modality discrepancies and improving the expressiveness of joint representations. Our research contributes a scalable and generalizable solution for cross-modal applications, with potential impacts in areas such as multimedia retrieval, multi-sensor fusion, and human-computer interaction. Key keywords: cross-modal learning, pairwise constraints, multimodal data, deep learning, representation learning, information retrieval.
