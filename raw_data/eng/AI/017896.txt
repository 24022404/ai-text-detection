This paper introduces Generative Adversarial Forests (GAF), a novel approach to adversarial learning that improves the conditioning of generative models. The objective is to address the challenges of mode collapse and unstable training in traditional Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Our method combines the strengths of decision forests with adversarial training, enabling more efficient and effective exploration of the data distribution. We propose a forest-based architecture that consists of multiple discriminators, each focusing on a specific subset of the data, and a generator that learns to produce realistic samples by navigating the forest. Our results show that GAF outperforms state-of-the-art GANs in terms of image quality, diversity, and training stability. The key findings include improved inception scores, reduced mode collapse, and more efficient training convergence. The proposed approach has significant implications for applications such as image synthesis, data augmentation, and adversarial robustness. By leveraging the strengths of both decision forests and adversarial learning, GAF offers a new direction for conditioned adversarial learning, with potential applications in computer vision, robotics, and machine learning. Key keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, Decision Forests, Adversarial Learning, Mode Collapse, Image Synthesis.
