This paper presents a rigorous and explicit mapping between the Variational Renormalization Group (VRG), a well-established concept in statistical physics, and modern deep learning architectures, particularly deep neural networks. The objective is to uncover fundamental similarities in the way both VRG and deep learning frameworks process, compress, and represent complex, high-dimensional data. We systematically construct a mathematical correspondence that demonstrates how hierarchical coarse-graining in the VRG method aligns with layer-wise feature extraction in deep neural networks. By formalizing this analogy, we develop a model where neural network training parallels the optimization of variational parameters in renormalization schemes. Our findings reveal that the algorithms underlying deep learning can be interpreted as an emergent renormalization process, facilitating improved understanding of network generalization, robustness, and interpretability. Numerical experiments with canonical datasets underscore the accuracy of this mapping and illustrate how renormalization-inspired architectures can enhance learning performance and feature hierarchy discovery. This interdisciplinary framework opens new pathways for applying physical principles to the design of neural networks and for leveraging machine learning advances in statistical mechanics. Key keywords: Variational Renormalization Group, deep learning, neural networks, hierarchical representations, statistical physics, machine learning, coarse-graining.
