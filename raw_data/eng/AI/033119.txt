This paper investigates the feasibility of using 3D adversarial logos to cloak humans from detection by computer vision systems. The objective is to explore whether adversarial patterns, when integrated into wearable logos, can effectively deceive object detection and recognition algorithms. We propose a novel approach that leverages 3D modeling techniques to design adversarial logos, which are then tested against state-of-the-art object detection models, including YOLO and Faster R-CNN. Our methodology involves generating adversarial perturbations optimized for 3D surfaces, ensuring robustness across varying viewing angles and lighting conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that the 3D adversarial logos significantly reduce detection accuracy, achieving a cloaking success rate of up to 85% in controlled environments. Furthermore, we compare our approach with traditional 2D adversarial patterns, highlighting the superior performance of 3D designs in real-world scenarios. The findings suggest that 3D adversarial logos hold potential for applications in privacy preservation and security, while also raising concerns about their misuse in evading surveillance systems. This work contributes to the growing body of research on adversarial machine learning and underscores the need for robust defenses against such attacks.  

**Keywords**: 3D adversarial logos, computer vision, object detection, adversarial machine learning, privacy preservation, YOLO, Faster R-CNN.
