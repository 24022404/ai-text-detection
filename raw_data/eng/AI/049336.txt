This paper addresses the challenge of learning navigation costs for autonomous systems through demonstration, leveraging semantic observations to enhance environmental understanding. With the growing reliance on autonomous navigation in diverse applications, accurately modeling navigation costs remains critical for system efficiency and safety.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework that integrates semantic observations into the learning process, enabling more intuitive and context-aware navigation strategies. The approach employs advanced machine learning techniques to infer navigation costs directly from demonstration data, utilizing semantic information extracted from the environment to inform decision-making. Key components include the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for visual data processing and advanced algorithms for cost prediction.

Results/Findings: Experiments conducted in simulated environments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method, achieving superior performance in navigation tasks compared to traditional cost models. Our approach shows significant improvements in path-planning efficiency and adaptability, particularly in complex environments with dynamic elements. The system was benchmarked against state-of-the-art models, showing a reduction in navigation time and increased safety margins.

Conclusion/Implications: The research provides valuable insights into the integration of semantic observations for cost learning in navigation tasks, offering a foundation for more intelligent and adaptive autonomous systems. This work not only contributes to the fields of robotics and machine learning by introducing a semantic-based navigation cost learning model but also holds potential for practical applications in autonomous vehicles and drones. Future developments may explore real-world deployment scenarios, enhancing system robustness and scalability.

Keywords: navigation costs, semantic observations, machine learning, autonomous systems, demonstration learning, convolutional neural networks.
