The study introduces "ExtremeWeather," a comprehensive large-scale climate dataset designed to enhance the detection, localization, and understanding of extreme weather events using semi-supervised machine learning techniques. This research aims to address the challenges of accurately predicting and analyzing extreme weather patterns, which have significant implications on societal infrastructure and safety.

Methods/Approach: The "ExtremeWeather" dataset is curated to include diverse meteorological data points spanning multiple decades, facilitating a robust analysis of climate variability and extreme events. The dataset supports semi-supervised learning approaches, allowing models to leverage both labeled and unlabeled data to improve prediction accuracy and generalization. The paper employs a combination of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and attention mechanisms to process and interpret complex climate data efficiently.

Results/Findings: Preliminary experiments demonstrate that the use of the "ExtremeWeather" dataset significantly enhances the performance of weather prediction models. The integration of semi-supervised learning techniques resulted in superior detection and localization capabilities, outperforming existing fully-supervised methods. The study also highlights the dataset's ability to capture nuanced weather patterns and anomalies, which are crucial for understanding the underlying dynamics of climate change.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a novel, publicly available dataset that offers a valuable resource for the scientific community working on climate modeling and extreme weather prediction. The advancements in semi-supervised learning approaches showcased in this study have the potential to improve early warning systems, aiding in risk management and disaster preparedness. Future work includes expanding the dataset and exploring the integration of additional data modalities to further refine prediction models.

Keywords: ExtremeWeather, climate dataset, semi-supervised learning, extreme weather events, CNN, attention mechanisms, weather prediction, climate change.
