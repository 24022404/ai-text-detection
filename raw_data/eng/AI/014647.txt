This paper introduces the Projection Pursuit Forest (PPF) algorithm, a novel approach for supervised classification tasks in high-dimensional data environments. The primary objective is to enhance classification performance by addressing limitations present in traditional ensemble methods, such as Random Forests, when applied to complex data structures with significant feature interactions or non-linear patterns. The PPF algorithm integrates projection pursuit techniques into the construction of individual decision trees, enabling each tree to optimize splits based on projections that uncover the most informative directions in the feature space. Extensive experiments were conducted on publicly available benchmark datasets to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method. Results demonstrate that the PPF algorithm consistently outperforms conventional ensemble classifiers in terms of accuracy, robustness, and interpretability, particularly in scenarios where relevant features are embedded within complex, high-dimensional relationships. The findings suggest that the Projection Pursuit Forest offers significant improvements for data-driven classification tasks and provides a flexible framework for further extensions in both supervised learning and feature selection domains. Key contributions include the seamless integration of projection pursuit with tree-based methods and extensive empirical validation across diverse application areas. Keywords: projection pursuit, ensemble learning, supervised classification, decision trees, high-dimensional data, machine learning.
