This paper presents a novel approach for developing an energy-efficient locomotion gait policy for snake-like robots using deep reinforcement learning (DRL). The objective is to address the challenge of optimizing gait patterns to minimize energy consumption while maintaining effective mobility across diverse terrains. We propose a DRL framework that enables the autonomous learning of locomotion strategies by modeling the robot and its environment in simulation, incorporating realistic actuator and friction constraints. The policy network is trained to maximize forward progression per unit of energy expended, resulting in gait patterns that dynamically adapt to surface variations and operational demands. Experimental results demonstrate that the learned gait outperforms traditional sinusoidal and heuristic-based methods in terms of both energy efficiency and adaptability. The study further analyzes the transferability of the learned policy to real-world hardware, confirming robust performance improvements. Our findings highlight the potential of DRL-driven gait optimization to extend the operational lifetime of snake robots in applications such as search and rescue, environmental monitoring, and industrial inspection. Key contributions include the design of an energy-centric reward function, the integration of physics-based simulation, and empirical validation in both virtual and physical setups. Keywords: snake robot, deep reinforcement learning, energy-efficient locomotion, gait optimization, robotics, adaptive control.
