Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have revolutionized the ability to synthesize realistic data across various domains. However, their reliance on large labeled datasets poses practical challenges. This paper introduces LT-GAN, a novel self-supervised GAN framework designed to enhance the data efficiency and effectiveness of GANs by detecting transformations within latent spaces.

Methods: The proposed LT-GAN employs a self-supervised learning approach to train on unlabeled data. It incorporates a transformation detection mechanism within the latent space, enabling the network to capture and utilize inherent data variations without external supervision. This is achieved by enforcing the generator's ability to transform latent vectors in meaningful ways while the discriminator validates these transformations, fostering a more robust feature representation.

Results: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that LT-GAN achieves superior performance in generating high-quality synthetic data across various datasets, compared to traditional GAN methods. Results indicate significant improvements in image quality and diversity, particularly under constrained data scenarios. Additionally, LT-GAN shows better robustness in capturing complex data distributions and transformations, delivering a comprehensive improvement over existing self-supervised GAN models.

Conclusion: LT-GAN presents a significant advancement in the realm of GANs, eliminating the need for large labeled datasets by leveraging latent transformation detection to drive its learning process. This technique not only enhances model efficiency but extends the applicability of GANs to situations where labeling is impractical or costly. The approach opens avenues for future research in self-supervision and the development of more autonomous generative models.

Keywords: LT-GAN, Self-Supervised Learning, Latent Space, Transformation Detection, Generative Adversarial Networks, Unlabeled Data, Data Efficiency.
