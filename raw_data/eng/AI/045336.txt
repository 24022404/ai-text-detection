Reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated impressive capabilities across various domains, yet its widespread adoption is often limited by substantial data requirements. This paper investigates the use of pretraining techniques to enhance data efficiency in RL by learning robust representations prior to task-specific learning. We propose a novel framework that leverages unsupervised and self-supervised pretraining on unlabeled observation data to construct foundational state representations. These representations are then transferred to downstream RL tasks, enabling agents to learn policies with significantly reduced interaction experience. Our experimental evaluation spans diverse benchmark environments, comparing our approach with standard RL methods and existing representation learning baselines. Results indicate that pretrained representations consistently accelerate convergence, improve sample efficiency, and, in some cases, enhance final policy performance. Furthermore, our analysis reveals that the benefits of pretraining are especially pronounced in settings with limited task-specific data. The proposed methodology advances the integration of representation learning and reinforcement learning, providing new avenues for building data-efficient RL agents. Keywords: reinforcement learning, pretraining, representation learning, data efficiency, transfer learning, self-supervised learning.
