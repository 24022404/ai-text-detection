Reinforcement learning (RL) has seen significant advancements through the integration of value-based and policy-based methods. This paper explores the combination of policy gradient and Q-learning, two widely used RL approaches, to leverage their complementary strengths. The objective is to develop a hybrid framework that enhances learning efficiency, stability, and performance in complex environments. We propose a novel algorithm that integrates policy gradient methods, which excel in handling continuous action spaces, with Q-learning, known for its effectiveness in discrete action spaces. The approach employs a dual-network architecture to simultaneously optimize policy and value functions, ensuring robust exploration and exploitation. Experimental results on benchmark environments demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior performance compared to standalone policy gradient or Q-learning approaches, particularly in tasks requiring fine-grained control and high-dimensional state spaces. The findings highlight the potential of combining these methods to address challenges in real-world applications, such as robotics and autonomous systems. This research contributes to the growing body of work on hybrid RL techniques, offering a scalable and efficient solution for complex decision-making problems.  

**Keywords**: reinforcement learning, policy gradient, Q-learning, hybrid methods, decision-making, autonomous systems.
