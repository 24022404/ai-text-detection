This paper investigates the consistency of semi-supervised learning algorithms on graphs, focusing on Probit and one-hot methods. The objective is to analyze the performance and reliability of these algorithms in graph-based learning tasks, where labeled data is scarce. We employ a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical evaluation to compare the consistency of Probit and one-hot methods. Our approach involves modeling graph-structured data using probabilistic frameworks and assessing the algorithms' ability to generalize from limited labeled examples. The results show that both methods exhibit consistency under certain conditions, but the Probit method demonstrates superior performance in terms of accuracy and robustness. Our findings have significant implications for the development of reliable semi-supervised learning algorithms on graphs, with potential applications in network analysis, recommendation systems, and graph neural networks. Key contributions include the introduction of a novel probabilistic framework for analyzing consistency and the identification of critical factors influencing algorithmic performance. Relevant keywords: semi-supervised learning, graph algorithms, Probit method, one-hot method, graph neural networks, network analysis.
