Abstract:

Monocular stereo matching aims to estimate depth information from a single image, addressing a challenging problem in computer vision with broad applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and 3D scene reconstruction. This paper proposes a novel unsupervised approach to monocular stereo matching that eliminates the need for ground truth disparity maps or stereo image pairs during training. Our method leverages a deep convolutional neural network that is trained using a self-supervised photometric loss, enabling the estimation of dense depth maps from individual monocular images. To enhance depth accuracy, we introduce a consistency constraint that enforces robustness against viewpoint changes and occlusions. Experimental results on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art supervised and unsupervised methods. The findings indicate that effective monocular depth estimation can be realized without requiring large labeled datasets, thus reducing annotation costs and increasing scalability. The proposed unsupervised monocular stereo matching framework paves the way for practical deployment in resource-constrained environments and presents new opportunities for advancing single-image 3D understanding. Keywords: monocular stereo matching, unsupervised learning, depth estimation, computer vision, self-supervised learning, convolutional neural networks.
