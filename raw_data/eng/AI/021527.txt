This paper addresses the challenge of imbalanced data in corporate credit rating prediction, a critical task in financial risk assessment. Traditional machine learning methods often struggle with class imbalance, leading to biased predictions and undermined reliability in identifying companies with poor creditworthiness. To overcome this, we propose a novel contrastive pre-training framework tailored to enhance the representation learning of underrepresented rating categories. Our approach leverages contrastive learning to distinguish subtle differences between similar credit profiles, ensuring improved feature discrimination even under severe imbalance. We conduct extensive experiments using real-world corporate credit rating datasets, benchmarking our method against classic and state-of-the-art techniques, including oversampling and cost-sensitive learning. The results demonstrate significant improvements in both minority class recall and overall classification metrics, highlighting the robustness and adaptability of our approach. Notably, our method requires no domain-specific data augmentation, preserving the integrity of financial information. In conclusion, the proposed contrastive pre-training method presents a scalable and effective solution for imbalanced corporate credit rating prediction, offering valuable insights for financial institutions and credit risk analysts. Keywords: contrastive learning, pre-training, class imbalance, corporate credit rating, financial risk assessment, machine learning.
