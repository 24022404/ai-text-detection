This paper introduces DeepBlindness, a novel deep learning framework for simultaneously estimating blindness maps and classifying blindness types from a single color image of an outdoor scene. Addressing the limitations of existing assistive technologies for visually impaired individuals, DeepBlindness leverages a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture trained on a large, diverse dataset of simulated blindness patterns. The proposed framework incorporates a multi-task learning approach, enabling it to predict the spatial distribution of visual impairment (blindness map) and categorize the specific type of blindness (e.g., glaucoma, macular degeneration) concurrently. Experimental results demonstrate that DeepBlindness achieves state-of-the-art performance in both blindness map estimation and blindness type classification, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and processing speed. Specifically, DeepBlindness enables real-time analysis on resource-constrained devices. The estimated blindness maps can be integrated into assistive technologies, such as scene understanding and navigation systems, to enhance the accessibility and safety of outdoor environments for individuals with visual impairments. Keywords: Blindness Map Estimation, Blindness Type Classification, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, Assistive Technology, Visual Impairment.
