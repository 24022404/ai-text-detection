Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have shown promise in representation learning for graph-structured data. However, their performance heavily relies on labeled data, which is often scarce and expensive to obtain. To address this, we propose RRLFSOR, a novel and Efficient Self-Supervised Learning strategy for GCNs. RRLFSOR leverages node feature reconstruction and structure-oriented relations to generate pseudo-labels for self-supervised training. Specifically, we introduce a feature reconstruction module that learns node embeddings by predicting masked node features. Furthermore, we incorporate structure-oriented relation learning, capturing high-order graph dependencies and propagating information between nodes with similar structural roles. Experiments on several benchmark datasets demonstrate that RRLFSOR significantly outperforms existing self-supervised GCN methods in node classification and clustering tasks, achieving comparable or superior results to supervised methods in some cases. RRLFSOR provides an efficient and effective approach to learn graph representations without relying on extensive labeled data, offering a promising direction for real-world graph learning applications. Keywords: Graph Convolutional Networks, Self-Supervised Learning, Graph Representation Learning, Node Classification, Feature Reconstruction.
