This paper addresses the limitations of traditional farthest point sampling (FPS) in point-wise analysis, a crucial task in various computer vision and geometric processing applications. Our objective is to develop a more efficient and effective sampling strategy that captures the underlying structure of point clouds. We propose a novel approach that combines FPS with a density-aware sampling method, leveraging the strengths of both techniques to achieve a more representative and informative point set. Our method utilizes a multi-scale framework to adaptively sample points based on their local density and geometric features. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art FPS methods in terms of point cloud reconstruction, registration, and classification tasks. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of considering point density and geometric context in sampling strategies, leading to improved performance and robustness in point-wise analysis. Our contributions have significant implications for applications such as 3D reconstruction, robotic perception, and autonomous driving, where accurate and efficient point cloud processing is critical. Key keywords: point-wise analysis, farthest point sampling, density-aware sampling, point cloud processing, 3D reconstruction.
