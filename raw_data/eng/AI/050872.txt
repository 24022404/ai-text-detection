This paper explores the concept of using 3D adversarial logos to cloak humans from object detection systems. The objective is to investigate whether strategically designed 3D logos can be used to deceive deep learning-based detectors, making humans undetectable. Our approach involves generating adversarial logos using a combination of computer-aided design and generative models. We then evaluate the effectiveness of these logos in evading detection by state-of-the-art object detectors. The results show that our 3D adversarial logos can significantly reduce the detection accuracy of human objects, demonstrating the potential of this technique for cloaking. The findings have important implications for the development of more robust and secure object detection systems, highlighting the need for adversarial training and testing. This research contributes to the growing field of adversarial machine learning, with applications in areas such as surveillance, security, and autonomous systems. Key keywords: 3D adversarial logos, object detection, deep learning, cloaking, adversarial machine learning.
