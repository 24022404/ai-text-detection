This paper introduces a novel approach to spatial mapping using Gaussian Processes (GPs) with nonstationary Fourier features. The objective is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of mapping in complex and non-uniform environments. Traditional GP-based spatial mapping often struggles with computational scalability and the assumption of stationarity. To address these limitations, we propose a method that leverages nonstationary Fourier features to represent the spatial covariance structure. This allows the GP to adapt to local variations in the environment, providing a more flexible and accurate representation. Our approach uses a spectral mixture kernel with parameters learned through a variational inference framework. Experimental results on benchmark datasets and real-world robotic mapping scenarios demonstrate that our method achieves comparable or superior mapping accuracy compared to state-of-the-art GP techniques, while significantly reducing computational cost. The proposed method enables efficient and accurate spatial mapping in dynamic and nonstationary environments, which can be used for autonomous navigation, environmental monitoring, and robotic exploration. Keywords: Spatial Mapping, Gaussian Processes, Nonstationary Processes, Fourier Features, Variational Inference, Robotics.
