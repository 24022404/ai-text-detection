This paper addresses the challenge of reinforcement learning in parameterized action spaces, a complex environment where actions are defined by discrete and continuous parameters. Our objective is to develop hierarchical approaches that enhance learning efficiency and performance in such intricate settings.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel hierarchical reinforcement learning framework that decomposes the parameterized action space into manageable subspaces. This involves creating a hierarchy of policies where a high-level policy selects among discrete actions while low-level policies fine-tune continuous parameters. The approach employs deep learning techniques to integrate both components effectively, optimizing policy networks through a combination of policy gradient methods and actor-critic models. 

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that the hierarchical approach significantly improves learning speed and decision-making accuracy compared to traditional non-hierarchical reinforcement learning methods. Results show superior performance across various benchmark environments, highlighting the model's capability to generalize well in multi-dimensional action spaces. The framework also provides robustness against sparse reward scenarios, often a bottleneck in parameterized environments.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of hierarchical approaches in reinforcement learning for parameterized action spaces presents a substantial advancement, offering a scalable and flexible solution for complex decision-making tasks. This research contributes to expanding the applicability of reinforcement learning in diverse areas such as robotics, automated control, and gaming. Future work could explore the integration of transfer learning techniques to further boost adaptability and efficiency in more dynamic contexts.

Keywords: hierarchical reinforcement learning, parameterized action spaces, deep learning, policy gradient, actor-critic, scalability, decision-making tasks.
