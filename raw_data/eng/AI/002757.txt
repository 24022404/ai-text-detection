This paper addresses the challenge of generating realistic human actions using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), a class of deep learning models known for their prowess in creating high-quality synthetic data. The objective is to enhance the synthesis of dynamic human movements, which are critical for applications in animation, simulation, and human-computer interaction. We employ an innovative GAN architecture tailored to capture the intricate temporal-spatial dependencies inherent in human actions. Our approach incorporates a novel generator and discriminator design that effectively models and evaluates sequences of movements, ensuring plausible and diverse action generation. The results demonstrate the superior performance of our model, producing lifelike human actions that surpass existing methods in both qualitative and quantitative assessments. Additional evaluations using objective metrics highlight marked improvements in action authenticity and variation. The implications of this research are significant for the fields of virtual reality, gaming, and robotics, where realistic human motion generation is paramount. Our contributions include not only a robust model but also a comprehensive analysis of its application potential, paving the way for future advancements in generative modeling of human activities. Key keywords include Generative Adversarial Networks, Human Action Generation, Deep Learning, Temporal-Spatial Modeling, and Synthetic Data.
