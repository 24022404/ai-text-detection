**Abstract**

Estimating feature importance is critical for interpretability and trust in machine learning models, especially when models face changing or unseen data distributions, known as distribution shifts. This paper addresses the challenge of delivering accurate and robust feature importance measures that remain reliable under such distribution changes. We propose a novel framework that integrates distributionally robust optimization with adaptive model weighting to account for both observed and potential shifts in the input data distribution. The method leverages a combination of synthetic data generation and invariant causal prediction to assess and calibrate feature contributions dynamically. Experiments are conducted across multiple benchmark datasets with induced covariate, label, and concept shifts, demonstrating that our approach consistently outperforms existing feature importance estimation techniques in terms of stability, precision, and adaptability. Notably, our framework provides interpretable attributions even when standard approaches degrade significantly. The proposed solution has significant implications for critical domains such as healthcare, finance, and risk assessment, where distribution shifts frequently occur and reliable interpretability is essential. Keywords: Feature Importance, Distribution Shift, Robust Machine Learning, Interpretability, Causal Inference, Model Reliability.
