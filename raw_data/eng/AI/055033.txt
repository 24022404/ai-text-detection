Image segmentation is a crucial step in many computer vision applications. This paper introduces a novel multi-thresholding technique for image segmentation based on histogram sampling to improve accuracy and efficiency. The method involves adaptively sampling the image histogram to identify optimal thresholds for segmenting the image into multiple regions. Specifically, a sampling strategy based on peak prominence is employed to select candidate thresholds, followed by an optimization step using Otsu's method to refine these thresholds and minimize intra-class variance. The proposed approach is evaluated on a diverse set of benchmark images and compared against existing multi-thresholding techniques, including traditional Otsu's method and k-means clustering. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed technique achieves superior segmentation accuracy, particularly in images with complex histograms and varying illumination conditions, while maintaining a competitive computational cost. The findings suggest that histogram sampling provides an effective means of automating the multi-thresholding process, offering a robust and efficient solution for image segmentation tasks. Keywords: Image Segmentation, Multi-Thresholding, Histogram Sampling, Otsu's Method, Computer Vision.
