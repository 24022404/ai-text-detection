This paper explores the concept of lifelong reinforcement learning systems, where an agent learns from a sequence of tasks over its lifetime. The objective is to develop a framework that enables agents to retain and transfer knowledge across tasks, improving overall performance and adaptability. We propose a novel approach that combines meta-learning and transfer learning techniques to facilitate efficient knowledge acquisition and retention. Our method utilizes a modular architecture, allowing the agent to adapt to new tasks while minimizing forgetting of previously learned knowledge. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, showing significant improvements in learning speed and performance compared to traditional reinforcement learning methods. The findings of this research have important implications for the development of autonomous systems that can learn and adapt in dynamic environments, with potential applications in areas such as robotics and decision-making. Key contributions include the introduction of a new modular architecture and a transfer learning mechanism that enables lifelong learning, highlighting the potential of reinforcement learning systems to learn and adapt over extended periods. Relevant keywords: lifelong learning, reinforcement learning, meta-learning, transfer learning, autonomous systems.
