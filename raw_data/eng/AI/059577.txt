This paper investigates the vulnerability of skeleton-based human action recognition systems to adversarial attacks. The objective is to develop a novel attack method that can effectively deceive these systems, highlighting potential security risks. Our approach utilizes a gradient-based optimization algorithm to craft subtle perturbations in the skeleton data, which can mislead the recognition model into incorrect classifications. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our attack, achieving a significant drop in recognition accuracy. Our findings show that even small perturbations can have a profound impact on the model's performance, emphasizing the need for robustness and security measures in human action recognition systems. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to expose vulnerabilities in current systems and inspire the development of more resilient models, with potential applications in areas such as surveillance, human-computer interaction, and healthcare. Key keywords: adversarial attack, skeleton-based action recognition, human action recognition, deep learning, computer vision, security.
