This paper presents a novel approach to driving style analysis by leveraging primitive driving patterns and Bayesian nonparametric methods. The objective is to develop a robust and adaptive framework for characterizing individual driving behaviors, which can be applied to various applications such as driver profiling, personalized feedback, and autonomous vehicle development. Our approach employs a combination of Bayesian nonparametric techniques, including Dirichlet processes and Gaussian processes, to model and cluster primitive driving patterns extracted from real-world driving data. The results show that our method can effectively identify distinct driving styles and provide insights into the underlying factors that influence driver behavior. Compared to existing methods, our approach demonstrates improved accuracy and flexibility in capturing the complexity of driving patterns. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to provide a data-driven and probabilistic framework for driving style analysis, which can be used to inform the development of more personalized and adaptive driver assistance systems. Key keywords: driving style analysis, primitive driving patterns, Bayesian nonparametrics, Dirichlet processes, Gaussian processes, driver profiling, autonomous vehicles.
