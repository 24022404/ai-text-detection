This paper investigates the problem of automatically assessing the synthesizability of dynamic texture samples. Dynamic textures, such as fire, water, and smoke, are characterized by their temporal evolution, making their synthesis a challenging task. Current methods often rely on visual inspection to determine if a sample is suitable for synthesis, which is subjective and inefficient. We propose a novel learning-based approach to predict the synthesizability of dynamic textures directly from sample data. Our method employs a convolutional neural network (CNN) trained on a large dataset of dynamic texture samples, labeled according to their synthesis quality using established texture synthesis algorithms. The CNN learns to extract features indicative of synthesizability, effectively classifying samples as either easily synthesizable or difficult to synthesize. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves high accuracy in predicting synthesizability, outperforming traditional feature-based approaches. This automated assessment can significantly streamline the texture synthesis pipeline, enabling faster iteration and improved quality control in computer graphics applications. Keywords: dynamic texture, texture synthesis, synthesizability, machine learning, convolutional neural network.
