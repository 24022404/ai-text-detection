Invertible Neural Networks (INNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for solving inverse problems by learning a direct mapping between data and latent spaces. This paper investigates the application of INNs to a range of inverse problems, focusing on improving reconstruction accuracy and computational efficiency. We propose a novel INN architecture incorporating adaptive coupling layers and regularization techniques tailored to address ill-posedness common in inverse problems. Our approach is evaluated on benchmark datasets from image deblurring, super-resolution, and seismic inversion. Results demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction quality, outperforming traditional regularization methods and existing INN-based approaches. Furthermore, we analyze the learned latent space to gain insights into the underlying data structure and uncertainty quantification. This work contributes a practical and effective INN framework for solving inverse problems with enhanced accuracy, efficiency, and interpretability, paving the way for broader applications in scientific computing and data analysis.

Keywords: Invertible Neural Networks, Inverse Problems, Deep Learning, Image Reconstruction, Seismic Inversion, Ill-Posed Problems.
