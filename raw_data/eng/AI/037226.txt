This paper addresses the challenge of accurately classifying the primary tumor origin of lung nodules in spectral CT images, which is crucial for effective patient treatment planning. Traditional imaging techniques have limitations in differentiating between primary and metastatic tumors, necessitating advanced methods for improved diagnostic accuracy.

Methods/Approach: We explore the application of transfer learning using convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for the classification of primary tumor origins in spectral CT scans. Our approach leverages a pre-trained deep learning model, which is fine-tuned on a comprehensive dataset of annotated spectral CT images. The model utilizes advanced spectral imaging features, enhancing its capability to discern subtle differences between tumor types.

Results/Findings: The proposed approach demonstrates superior performance, achieving a classification accuracy significantly higher than traditional methods. Our model exhibits robust generalization across diverse datasets, with a notable improvement in sensitivity and specificity. Comparative analysis with existing models shows a marked advancement in the automated classification of lung nodules.

Conclusion/Implications: This study highlights the potential of transfer learning in enhancing diagnostic capabilities for lung cancer screening using spectral CT. The findings suggest that integrating deep learning techniques with spectral imaging can lead to more precise and reliable tumor classification, potentially improving prognosis and treatment strategies. Future work will explore the integration of additional imaging modalities and further refinement of the model for clinical application.

Keywords: Transfer Learning, Spectral CT, Lung Nodules, Tumor Classification, Convolutional Neural Networks, Deep Learning.
