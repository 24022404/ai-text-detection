**Abstract:**

This paper presents a novel approach to radio control and signal detection using deep reinforcement learning (DRL), leveraging KeRLym, an open-source environment built for reinforcement learning agents modeled after the OpenAI Gym framework. The primary objective of this research is to address the challenges of adaptive radio frequency (RF) control and efficient signal identification in dynamic wireless environments. We develop a DRL agent that interacts with simulated RF environments using KeRLym, employing deep Q-networks (DQN) and policy gradient methods to optimize radio parameters in real time. The proposed agent learns to autonomously select transmission frequencies and modulation schemes, as well as detect the presence and type of signals amidst varying noise and interference conditions.

Experimental evaluation demonstrates that our DRL agent significantly outperforms traditional rule-based and supervised learning approaches, achieving faster adaptation to changing spectrum conditions and higher accuracy in signal detection tasks. Performance was assessed using key metrics such as spectral efficiency, detection accuracy, and response time. The flexibility of the KeRLym environment enabled rapid prototyping and benchmarking against existing RL baselines.

Our findings highlight the potential of DRL in advancing the autonomy and intelligence of cognitive radio systems, enabling more efficient and resilient wireless communication. The integration of KeRLym streamlines the development and testing of RL-based radio control systems for both academic research and practical applications. Key contributions include the design of a flexible DRL framework for RF control, empirical demonstration of improved performance, and open-source tools to facilitate broader adoption. Keywords: deep reinforcement learning, radio control, signal detection, KeRLym, cognitive radio, OpenAI Gym, wireless communication, spectrum management.
