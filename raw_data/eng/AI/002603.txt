This paper addresses the challenge of merging images in the context of low-level machine vision tasks, where lossless and reversible processing is crucial for maintaining data integrity. The research explores a novel approach to reversible image merging that ensures the original images can be perfectly reconstructed while enhancing the capabilities of various machine vision applications.

Methods/Approach: We propose a cutting-edge algorithm that leverages reversible data hiding techniques combined with advanced image transformation methods to achieve lossless image merging. The algorithm is designed to process images with minimal computational overhead, maintaining high efficiency critical for real-time machine vision systems. Key technical mechanisms include bit-plane decomposition and reversible prediction error expansion, which facilitate an exact reconstruction of the merged images.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our reversible image merging approach significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of both merge efficiency and recovery accuracy. The method yields superior results with a minimal storage overhead, proving effective across a range of image resolutions and types. Benchmark comparisons indicate improvements in accuracy and processing speed, validating the robustness of the proposed solution in practical machine vision scenarios.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a novel solution to the field of low-level machine vision by enabling reversible image merging without data loss. The implications of this study extend to various applications, including surveillance, medical imaging, and real-time image analysis, where maintaining original image quality is paramount. The findings pave the way for further research into reversible image processing methods, enhancing the performance and capabilities of automated vision systems.

Keywords: reversible image merging, low-level machine vision, lossless processing, data hiding, image transformation, computational efficiency.
