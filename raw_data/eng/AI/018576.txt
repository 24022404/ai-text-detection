Boundary-aware segmentation is crucial for many computer vision tasks, yet traditional pixel-wise loss functions often struggle to produce accurate and well-defined object boundaries. This paper introduces InverseForm, a novel loss function designed to explicitly address this limitation by penalizing deviations from ideal boundary structures. InverseForm leverages concepts from integral geometry to quantify the structural integrity of predicted boundaries, encouraging the formation of smooth, continuous, and geometrically plausible contours. We evaluate InverseForm on several benchmark datasets for image and LiDAR segmentation, demonstrating significant improvements in boundary F-score and overall segmentation accuracy compared to commonly used loss functions like cross-entropy and Dice loss. Furthermore, we show that InverseForm promotes better generalization and robustness to noisy data. The proposed loss function is easily integrated into existing segmentation frameworks and offers a powerful tool for applications requiring high-fidelity boundary delineation, such as autonomous driving and medical image analysis. Keywords: segmentation, boundary-aware, loss function, integral geometry, deep learning, computer vision.
