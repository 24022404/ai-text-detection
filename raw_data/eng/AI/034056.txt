The increasing demand for robust image classification models in dynamic environments necessitates efficient methods for learning image labels during training. This paper addresses the challenge of training classification models with on-the-fly label acquisition, where labels are generated or refined during the training process rather than being fully predefined. We propose a novel framework that integrates active learning and semi-supervised techniques to dynamically assign and update labels based on model confidence and data distribution. Our approach leverages a lightweight annotation module and a self-supervised pre-training phase to enhance label quality and reduce dependency on large labeled datasets. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves competitive accuracy compared to traditional supervised learning approaches, while significantly reducing annotation costs. Furthermore, the framework exhibits improved robustness to noisy and incomplete labels, making it suitable for real-world applications with limited labeled data. The study highlights the potential of on-the-fly label learning as a scalable and cost-effective solution for training robust classification models in resource-constrained settings.  

**Keywords**: image classification, on-the-fly labeling, active learning, semi-supervised learning, robust models, annotation efficiency.
