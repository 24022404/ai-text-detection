Adaptive Symmetric Reward Noising for Reinforcement Learning introduces a novel method to enhance the performance and robustness of reinforcement learning algorithms by modifying the reward signal with adaptive noise. This study addresses the challenges of exploration-exploitation balance and overfitting in dynamic environments.

Methods/Approach: The proposed approach involves the integration of symmetric reward noising mechanisms, which adaptively scale the noise added to the reward signals based on temporal and spatial factors within the learning environment. The method leverages Gaussian noise adjustments calibrated through reinforcement learning feedback loops, ensuring optimal exploration without substantial loss of accuracy or learning efficiency.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that adaptive symmetric reward noising significantly improves exploration strategies and stabilizes learning curves in reinforcement learning models. The approach was benchmarked against standard algorithms across various environments, showcasing superior performance in terms of convergence speed and cumulative reward. Notably, enhancement was observed in challenging scenarios with sparse reward distributions.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a robust framework for refining reward guidance in reinforcement learning, offering a practical solution to common pitfalls associated with reward manipulation. The adaptive symmetric reward noising framework holds significant potential for applications in dynamic and complex environments, such as autonomous navigation, game playing, and industrial automation, where adaptability and resilience are critical.

Keywords: reinforcement learning, reward noising, exploration-exploitation, Gaussian noise, adaptive algorithms, autonomous systems, dynamic environments.
