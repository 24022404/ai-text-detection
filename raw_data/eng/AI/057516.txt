Adversarial training has emerged as a promising approach to enhance the robustness of machine learning models against adversarial attacks, particularly in predictive tasks. This paper investigates the theoretical foundations and limitations of adversarial training in deterministic settings, where the model's predictions are uniquely determined by the input data. We present a comprehensive analysis of the optimization dynamics involved in adversarial training, focusing on its ability to improve generalization and robustness. Our approach leverages mathematical frameworks to characterize the trade-offs between model accuracy and adversarial resilience, providing insights into the conditions under which adversarial training is effective. Through extensive experiments, we demonstrate that while adversarial training can significantly improve robustness, it is inherently limited by the deterministic nature of the model, leading to potential overfitting to adversarial perturbations. Our findings highlight the importance of balancing robustness and generalization, offering practical guidelines for designing adversarial training strategies in deterministic environments. This research contributes to the broader understanding of adversarial robustness and provides a foundation for future work in developing more resilient machine learning systems.  

**Keywords**: adversarial training, robustness, deterministic models, machine learning, optimization, generalization, predictive tasks.
