This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently performing reinforcement learning in environments with large and complex state spaces. We propose an innovative integration of cover trees with Bayesian reinforcement learning (RL) to improve scalability and decision-making accuracy.

Methods/Approach: Our approach utilizes cover trees, a data structure that supports dynamic insertion and deletion of nodes while maintaining computational efficiency, to organize the large state space. The cover tree facilitates rapid nearest-neighbor searches and state transitions. We integrate this structure with a Bayesian framework that probabilistically models uncertainty over rewards and state transitions, leveraging it to guide exploration and exploitation. This combination aims to enhance the efficiency of Bayesian RL in high-dimensional environments by focusing computational resources on the most relevant parts of the state space.

Results/Findings: Experiments demonstrate that Cover Tree Bayesian Reinforcement Learning significantly outperforms traditional approaches, especially in terms of sample efficiency and computational speed. We show that our model requires fewer interactions with the environment to achieve comparable or superior policy performance when benchmarked across various complex simulated tasks. The results underscore the importance of structure-aware modeling in effectively deploying Bayesian RL methods.

Conclusion/Implications: The research introduces a novel integration of cover trees in the Bayesian RL domain, highlighting their utility in handling large-scale problems with dynamic and continuous state spaces. This method offers a scalable solution that can be applied to numerous real-world applications, from autonomous robotics to large-scale system management, where rapid decision-making and computational efficiency are critical. The contributions pave the way for future exploration of structured data representations in reinforcement learning contexts. 

Keywords: Bayesian Reinforcement Learning, Cover Trees, State Space, Scalability, Sample Efficiency, Decision-Making, Autonomous Systems.
