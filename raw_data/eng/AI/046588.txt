This paper introduces a novel approach to dictionary learning by leveraging the principles of optimal transport. Specifically, we propose Wasserstein Dictionary Learning (WDL), which addresses the challenges in unsupervised non-linear dictionary learning by utilizing the Wasserstein distance, a metric from optimal transport theory, to effectively capture underlying data relationships.

Methods/Approach: Our method employs a non-linear transformation framework that incorporates the Wasserstein distance for learning dictionary atoms. This approach enables the generation of dictionaries that can better represent complex intrinsic structures within data. We employ an unsupervised learning paradigm, allowing the model to automatically discover features without requiring labeled data. The framework is designed to be flexible and scalable, making it applicable to a range of datasets and dimensionalities.

Results/Findings: The experiments demonstrate that Wasserstein Dictionary Learning outperforms traditional dictionary learning methods across multiple benchmarks. Key findings reveal that WDL achieves superior reconstruction accuracy and better clustering performance due to its ability to capture non-linear data structures. Comparisons with other state-of-the-art methods highlight significant improvements in representational quality and computational efficiency.

Conclusion/Implications: Wasserstein Dictionary Learning represents a significant advancement in unsupervised dictionary learning by integrating optimal transport methodologies. This research contributes to the fields of machine learning and data representation by offering a robust approach for discovering non-linear structures in data. Potential applications include image processing, data compression, and feature extraction, where capturing intricate data patterns is crucial. By driving improvements in dictionary learning, our approach opens new avenues for research and application in complex data environments.

Keywords: Wasserstein distance, dictionary learning, optimal transport, unsupervised learning, non-linear transformation, data representation.
