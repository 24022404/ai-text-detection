This paper addresses the challenge of improving skeleton-based action recognition by leveraging richer feature representations through the integration of higher-order features in Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). The goal is to enhance the discriminative power of existing models and improve recognition accuracy in complex human actions.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel architecture that incorporates higher-order feature fusion within the framework of GNNs. Our approach systematically combines different levels of geometric and kinematic features extracted from skeletal data. By applying advanced graph convolutional mechanisms, the model captures intricate patterns and relationships between joints, thereby extending the expressive capability of traditional GNNs. Additionally, the architecture employs feature aggregation techniques to synthesize and refine these higher-order representations effectively.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our proposed model achieves superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods across multiple benchmark datasets for skeleton-based action recognition. The fusion of higher-order features significantly boosts the model's accuracy, exhibiting noteworthy improvements in recognizing both common and nuanced human activities. Comparative analyses highlight the robustness and adaptability of our GNN-based approach to diverse and challenging scenarios.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a significant advancement in the domain of skeleton-based action recognition by introducing a method that effectively fuses higher-order features within GNNs. The findings underline the potential of these enriched representations to enhance model performance, offering valuable insights for the development of more effective human action recognition systems. Potential applications span areas such as surveillance, human-computer interaction, and sports analytics, where precise and reliable action recognition is critical.

Keywords: skeleton-based action recognition, Graph Neural Networks, higher-order features, geometric features, kinematic features, graph convolutional mechanisms, feature fusion, human action recognition.
