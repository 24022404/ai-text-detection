In the domain of embedded automation, deep neural networks (DNNs) face significant challenges related to stability, efficiency, and resource constraints. This paper proposes a novel approach, Mean Spectral Normalization (MSN), to enhance the performance and robustness of DNNs specifically tailored for embedded systems.

Methods/Approach: Our approach involves incorporating a mean spectral normalization technique into the architecture of DNNs, which systematically normalizes the spectral values of weight matrices. This method aims to stabilize training dynamics and improve the convergence speed of models used in embedded applications. We have implemented MSN on various benchmark datasets and state-of-the-art DNN models optimized for embedded environments, ensuring minimal computational overhead and maintaining real-time performance capabilities.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that models enhanced with the MSN technique achieve improved generalization with a reduction in overfitting, leading to higher model accuracy and robustness. Compared to traditional normalization methods, our approach requires less computational power and shows superior performance metrics across several embedded automation tasks, including sensor data processing and real-time decision-making.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of Mean Spectral Normalization offers a significant contribution to the field of embedded automation by providing a scalable and efficient normalization technique that complements existing DNN models. This advancement not only enhances the DNN performance but also paves the way for more reliable deployments in resource-constrained environments. Future work may explore the extension of this approach to other machine learning models to further validate its applicability. 

Keywords: Deep Neural Networks, Mean Spectral Normalization, Embedded Automation, Model Stability, Computational Efficiency, Real-Time Systems.
