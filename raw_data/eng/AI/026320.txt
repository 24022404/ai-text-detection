This paper addresses the complexity of architecture search for Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), a crucial task in leveraging graph-structured data. Our objective is to simplify the architecture search process, reducing the computational cost and expertise required. We propose a novel approach that utilizes a reinforcement learning-based method to efficiently explore the architecture space. Our method, termed Efficient Architecture Search (EAS), leverages a graph-aware reward function to guide the search towards optimal architectures. Experimental results demonstrate that EAS achieves state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency. Key findings include a significant reduction in search time and improved generalizability of the discovered architectures. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to simplify the architecture search process, making GNNs more accessible to practitioners and researchers alike. This work has important implications for applications such as node classification, graph classification, and link prediction, with potential extensions to other graph-based tasks. Relevant keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Architecture Search, Reinforcement Learning, Efficient Architecture Search, Graph-Aware Reward Function.
