This paper investigates how incorporating invariances into random feature and kernel models can enhance learning performance, particularly in tasks where data exhibits inherent symmetries. We explore systematic methods to embed invariance properties—such as translation, rotation, and permutation invariance—into both random features and kernel-based learning algorithms. Our approach extends conventional random feature maps and kernel constructions by introducing mechanisms that enforce invariance directly within the feature generation or kernel computation process. Through theoretical analysis, we demonstrate that models augmented with such invariances can improve generalization and robustness, especially in scenarios with limited training data or noisy inputs. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets, including image and structured data tasks, confirm that our invariant models outperform standard random feature and kernel approaches by achieving higher accuracy and faster convergence. The results indicate that learning with invariances in these models not only enhances predictive performance but also reduces model complexity and sensitivity to irrelevant variations in data. Our findings suggest broad applicability for invariant random feature and kernel methods across a range of machine learning problems where data symmetries are present. Keywords: invariance, random features, kernel models, symmetry, generalization, robust learning.
