Panoptic segmentation, which unifies semantic and instance segmentation, presents a complex challenge, particularly in handling unfamiliar objects. This paper introduces a novel Generator-Evaluator-Selector Network (GES-Net) designed to improve panoptic segmentation performance and, uniquely, to decompose unfamiliar objects into meaningful parts. Our approach employs a generator to propose potential segmentation masks, an evaluator to assess the quality and consistency of these proposals, and a selector to choose the optimal mask for each object or object part. Furthermore, for unfamiliar objects, GES-Net leverages contextual information and learned shape priors to split them into constituent parts, enabling more accurate scene understanding. Experiments on the Cityscapes and COCO datasets demonstrate that GES-Net achieves state-of-the-art panoptic segmentation accuracy, especially in the challenging task of segmenting and splitting unfamiliar objects. The results show a significant improvement in Intersection-over-Union (IoU) scores for both known and unknown object categories, indicating enhanced robustness and generalizability. This work contributes a novel architecture and methodology for panoptic segmentation with part decomposition, advancing the state-of-the-art in scene understanding and object recognition. Keywords: Panoptic Segmentation, Image Segmentation, Unfamiliar Objects, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, Part Decomposition, Scene Understanding.
