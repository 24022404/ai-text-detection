Graph Contrastive Learning (GCL) has emerged as a powerful technique for unsupervised graph representation learning. However, GCL methods are often vulnerable to noisy or adversarial graph structures, leading to suboptimal performance. This paper introduces a novel framework, Robust Graph Contrastive Learning (RGCL), designed to enhance the robustness of GCL against graph perturbations. RGCL leverages a spectral-based graph smoothing technique coupled with an adaptive contrastive loss function. The spectral smoothing effectively reduces noise in the graph structure, while the adaptive loss dynamically adjusts the importance of different negative samples based on their similarity to the anchor node. We evaluate RGCL on a variety of benchmark datasets with both clean and perturbed graph structures. Empirical results demonstrate that RGCL significantly outperforms existing GCL methods, exhibiting superior performance and resilience to adversarial attacks. Our findings highlight the importance of robust graph structure encoding in contrastive learning and offer a promising direction for developing more reliable graph representation learning techniques. Keywords: Graph Contrastive Learning, Graph Representation Learning, Robustness, Spectral Smoothing, Contrastive Loss, Graph Neural Networks.
