Detecting detachable objects in complex scenes and accurately determining their depth ordering is a critical challenge in computer vision, with applications in robotics, augmented reality, and scene understanding. This paper introduces a novel framework for detachable object detection that simultaneously performs segmentation and depth ordering from short-baseline video sequences. Our approach leverages a multi-task neural network architecture that integrates spatial and temporal information to predict object boundaries and relative depth relationships. The proposed method employs a combination of optical flow estimation and depth-aware feature fusion to enhance the accuracy of object segmentation and depth ordering, even in scenarios with limited baseline information. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our framework outperforms existing methods in terms of segmentation precision and depth ordering accuracy, achieving state-of-the-art performance. The system’s ability to handle occlusions and overlapping objects is particularly noteworthy, making it suitable for real-world applications. This research contributes to advancing the field of scene understanding by providing a robust and efficient solution for detachable object detection, with potential implications for autonomous navigation and interactive systems.  

**Keywords**: detachable object detection, segmentation, depth ordering, short-baseline video, optical flow, multi-task learning, scene understanding.
