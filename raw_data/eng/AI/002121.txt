This paper introduces CycleCluster, a novel clustering regularisation method designed to enhance the performance of deep semi-supervised classification. The primary objective is to address the challenge of effectively leveraging unlabelled data to improve classification accuracy in scenarios where labelled examples are scarce. CycleCluster integrates cyclic consistency principles into the clustering regularisation process, encouraging the model to learn representations that are both discriminative and robust across labelled and unlabelled data. Our approach modifies standard semi-supervised frameworks by incorporating a cycle-consistent objective, which enforces agreement between cluster assignments over multiple data transformations. Extensive experiments on benchmark image and text classification datasets demonstrate that CycleCluster consistently improves classification accuracy compared to existing clustering-based regularisation techniques. The proposed method achieves higher robustness to noisy labels and greater generalisation on out-of-distribution samples. Our results highlight the effectiveness of modernised clustering regularisation in semi-supervised learning, paving the way for more reliable and scalable AI models. Key contributions include a generalisable cycle-consistent regularisation framework, empirical improvements on standard benchmarks, and insights into the interplay between cluster structure and deep representation learning. Keywords: semi-supervised learning, clustering regularisation, deep learning, cycle consistency, representation learning, classification.
