In the domain of machine learning and computer vision, recovering semantic attributes from data is a critical task, empowering a variety of applications, such as object recognition and scene understanding. This paper addresses the challenge of recovering such attributes utilizing weakly supervised methodologies, which require minimal labeled data compared to fully supervised approaches.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework that leverages weak supervision to infer semantic attributes efficiently. The approach integrates convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with sparse coding techniques to build a model that can learn from partial annotations. By incorporating regularization strategies and leveraging available unlabeled data, the model captures complex semantic relationships with reduced dependency on extensive labeled datasets.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our framework outperforms existing weakly supervised models in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The results indicate a substantial improvement in the ability to recover semantic attributes across multiple datasets, showcasing the robustness and generalization ability of our approach. Our method achieves competitive performance compared to fully supervised benchmarks, with significant reductions in required labeled data.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a valuable methodology for attribute recovery in scenarios where labeled data is scarce, reducing the cost and effort associated with data annotation. The proposed approach holds potential for deployment in various applications, including automated image tagging, video content analysis, and augmented reality. The innovation lies in effectively combining weak supervision with advanced neural network architectures to enhance semantic understanding.

Key Keywords: weakly supervised learning, semantic attributes, convolutional neural networks, sparse coding, minimal annotation, machine learning, computer vision.
