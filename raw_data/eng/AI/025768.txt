Gradient Temporal Difference (GTD) learning is a widely used approach for solving reinforcement learning problems with function approximation. However, traditional GTD methods often require careful tuning of hyperparameters, such as step sizes, which can be time-consuming and suboptimal. This paper introduces a novel parameter-free GTD algorithm that eliminates the need for manual hyperparameter tuning while maintaining robust performance across diverse environments. The proposed method leverages adaptive optimization techniques to automatically adjust learning rates based on gradient statistics, ensuring stable and efficient convergence. Experiments conducted on benchmark reinforcement learning tasks demonstrate that the parameter-free GTD algorithm achieves competitive or superior performance compared to traditional GTD methods with hand-tuned parameters. Furthermore, the approach significantly reduces the computational overhead associated with hyperparameter optimization, making it more practical for real-world applications. This work contributes to the advancement of reinforcement learning by providing a more accessible and efficient framework for value function approximation, with potential applications in robotics, game AI, and autonomous systems.  

**Keywords**: Gradient Temporal Difference Learning, Reinforcement Learning, Parameter-free Optimization, Adaptive Learning Rates, Function Approximation.
