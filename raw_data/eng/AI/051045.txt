This paper introduces *Subject2Vec*, a novel generative-discriminative framework designed to transform a set of image patches into a compact, informative vector representation. The primary objective is to address the challenge of efficiently encoding high-dimensional image data into a low-dimensional space while preserving critical discriminative features. The proposed approach combines generative modeling to capture the underlying distribution of image patches with a discriminative mechanism to ensure the resulting vector is optimized for downstream tasks such as classification, clustering, or retrieval. The framework leverages advanced deep learning techniques, including variational autoencoders (VAEs) and contrastive learning, to achieve robust and generalizable representations. Experimental results demonstrate that *Subject2Vec* outperforms existing methods in terms of both reconstruction accuracy and task-specific performance across multiple benchmark datasets. The model's ability to generate interpretable and task-agnostic vectors highlights its potential for applications in medical imaging, remote sensing, and computer vision. This work contributes a scalable and versatile solution for feature extraction from image sets, offering significant improvements in efficiency and performance over traditional approaches.  

**Keywords**: Image representation, generative-discriminative models, deep learning, variational autoencoders, contrastive learning, feature extraction.
