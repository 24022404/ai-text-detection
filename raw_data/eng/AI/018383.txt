This paper addresses the challenge of evaluating the correctness of unsupervised many-to-many image translation models. The objective is to develop a comprehensive framework for assessing the accuracy and fidelity of translated images in the absence of paired training data. Our approach employs a multi-metric evaluation methodology, combining traditional metrics such as peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM) with more recent perceptual metrics like Frechet inception distance (FID) and learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS). We apply this framework to several state-of-the-art unsupervised image translation models, including cycle-consistent generative adversarial networks (CycleGAN) and multimodal unsupervised image-to-image translation (MUNIT). Our results show that the proposed evaluation framework provides a more nuanced understanding of the strengths and weaknesses of each model, highlighting the importance of considering multiple metrics when evaluating image translation quality. The findings of this study have significant implications for the development of more accurate and reliable image translation models, with potential applications in fields such as computer vision, robotics, and medical imaging. Key keywords: unsupervised image translation, many-to-many translation, evaluation metrics, CycleGAN, MUNIT, computer vision.
