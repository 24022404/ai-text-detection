This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the interpretability of convolutional neural networks (ConvNets) by proposing a novel multi-shot learning approach for constructing interpretable part graphs. The primary objective is to enable ConvNets to not only achieve accurate classification but also provide transparent, part-based reasoning of their decisions. The proposed method leverages multi-shot learning techniques to identify and group semantically meaningful parts from a limited set of annotated samples. These identified parts are then organized into graph structures that capture their spatial and relational dependencies within the neural network feature maps. Experiments were conducted on standard visual recognition benchmarks, demonstrating that the generated part graphs are both highly interpretable and robust, maintaining competitive classification performance with state-of-the-art models. The findings highlight significant improvements in visual reasoning, part localization, and model transparency while requiring only a few annotated examples per category. This research offers a promising direction for the development of interpretable deep learning models, facilitating their adoption in critical applications where explainability and model trust are essential. Keywords: Convolutional Neural Networks, interpretability, multi-shot learning, part-based models, graph representations, visual reasoning.
