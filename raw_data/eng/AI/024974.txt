**Abstract**

The increasing adoption of digital histopathology has highlighted the critical challenge of domain shift, where analytic models trained on images from one source often perform poorly when applied to new datasets due to varying staining procedures, scanners, and acquisition settings. This paper addresses the problem of unsupervised domain adaptation in histopathological image analytics using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). We propose a novel GNN-based framework that models both local pixel relationships and high-level semantic similarities within and across domains. Unlike traditional deep learning approaches, our method constructs representative image graphs to capture spatial and contextual information without relying on labeled target domain data. Experimental evaluations on multiple histopathological image datasets demonstrate that the proposed model significantly improves classification accuracy and robustness compared to state-of-the-art domain adaptation techniques. Our approach enables effective feature alignment between source and target domains while preserving critical pathological details. The results indicate the potential of GNNs to facilitate reliable and scalable deployment of automated image analysis tools in real-world clinical settings. Key contributions include an innovative graph-based domain adaptation mechanism and comprehensive validation on diverse histopathological benchmarks. Keywords: graph neural networks, unsupervised domain adaptation, histopathology, medical image analysis, transfer learning.
