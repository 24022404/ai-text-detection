Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown significant promise in solving complex decision-making tasks, yet challenges remain in scenarios where rewards are delayed, making it difficult to establish effective learning policies. This paper addresses the problem of off-policy reinforcement learning in environments with delayed rewards, aiming to improve the efficiency and stability of learning algorithms under such conditions. We propose a novel approach that integrates temporal difference learning with reward redistribution mechanisms to better align delayed rewards with the actions that caused them. Our method leverages a combination of experience replay and prioritized sampling to enhance the learning process, ensuring that critical experiences are revisited and learned from effectively. Experimental results on benchmark environments demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing off-policy RL methods in terms of convergence speed and final performance, particularly in tasks with sparse and delayed rewards. The proposed framework provides a robust solution for real-world applications where reward signals are inherently delayed, such as robotics, healthcare, and financial systems. This work contributes to advancing RL methodologies by addressing a critical limitation in reward handling, offering a scalable and efficient solution for delayed reward scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Off-Policy Learning, Delayed Rewards, Temporal Difference Learning, Reward Redistribution, Experience Replay.
