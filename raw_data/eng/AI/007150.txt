Ensemble classifiers are widely used to improve prediction accuracy and robustness in machine learning. However, the inherent geometric relationships between individual classifiers within an ensemble and their impact on the ensemble's overall performance remain underexplored. This paper introduces a novel dataset-level geometric framework to analyze and optimize ensemble classifiers. We represent each classifier's predictions on a given dataset as a point in a high-dimensional space, enabling the quantification of ensemble diversity and individual classifier contributions through geometric measures such as volume and distance. Our approach facilitates the identification of redundant or conflicting classifiers, guiding informed ensemble pruning and member selection. Experimental results on various benchmark datasets demonstrate that ensembles optimized using our geometric framework achieve significantly improved classification accuracy and generalization performance compared to traditional ensemble methods and baseline approaches. This framework offers valuable insights into ensemble behavior and provides a principled approach for designing more effective and efficient ensemble classifiers. Keywords: Ensemble Learning, Geometric Analysis, Classifier Diversity, Machine Learning, Classification, Dataset Analysis.
