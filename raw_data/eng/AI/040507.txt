The inverse design of inorganic materials poses a significant challenge due to the vastness and complexity of chemical space. This paper introduces a novel approach leveraging generative adversarial networks (GANs) for efficient sampling of this space, targeting the discovery of materials with desired properties. Our method employs a GAN architecture trained on a dataset of known inorganic material compositions and their corresponding properties. The generator network learns to propose new material candidates, while the discriminator network distinguishes between generated and real materials, driving the generator to produce increasingly realistic and promising compositions. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach by targeting materials with specific band gaps and formation energies. Results show that our GAN-based sampling strategy significantly outperforms random sampling and other traditional optimization techniques in identifying materials with the desired properties within a reduced computational cost. This work offers a powerful tool for accelerating materials discovery, facilitating the design of novel inorganic compounds with tailored functionalities. Keywords: generative adversarial networks, GAN, inverse design, inorganic materials, chemical space, materials discovery.
