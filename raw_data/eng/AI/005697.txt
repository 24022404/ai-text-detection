This paper addresses the challenge of pedestrian attribute recognition and localization, a crucial task in various computer vision applications. The objective is to develop a weakly-supervised learning approach that can effectively learn mid-level features from pedestrian images without requiring extensive manual annotation. Our method leverages a novel architecture that combines convolutional neural networks with attention mechanisms to focus on relevant regions of interest. The approach enables the model to learn discriminative features for attribute recognition, such as clothing, accessories, and demographics, while simultaneously localizing these attributes within the image. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. The key findings highlight the importance of mid-level features in attribute recognition and the benefits of weakly-supervised learning in reducing annotation efforts. This research contributes to the development of more accurate and efficient pedestrian attribute recognition systems, with potential applications in surveillance, security, and autonomous driving. Key keywords: weakly-supervised learning, mid-level features, pedestrian attribute recognition, localization, computer vision, convolutional neural networks.
