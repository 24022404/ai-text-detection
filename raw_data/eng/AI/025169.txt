Tiny object detection remains a challenging task in computer vision due to the limited semantic and spatial information available in small-scale features. This paper introduces an innovative approach to enhance detection accuracy through the introduction of an Effective Fusion Factor (EFF) within the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) architecture. The proposed method adaptively weighs multi-scale feature flows, allowing for more optimal information fusion between different layers. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our EFF-FPN significantly outperforms conventional FPN by achieving higher precision and recall rates in tiny object detection scenarios, particularly in densely populated and cluttered scenes. Comparative analysis with state-of-the-art detectors showcases improvements in mean average precision (mAP) without considerable increases in computational overhead. The EFF approach provides a flexible and robust mechanism for feature fusion, making it suitable for real-time applications such as autonomous driving, surveillance, and aerial imagery analysis. Our findings highlight the critical role of adaptive fusion strategies in enhancing multi-scale detection frameworks. Keywords: tiny object detection, Feature Pyramid Network, effective fusion factor, multi-scale features, computer vision, deep learning.
