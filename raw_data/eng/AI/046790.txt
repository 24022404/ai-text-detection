Recent advancements in neural networks have increased their complexity, often leading to a lack of interpretability of their decision-making processes. This paper introduces LioNets, an innovative approach focused on enhancing the local interpretability of neural network predictions by decoding the representations in the penultimate layer.

Methods/Approach: LioNets employs a novel decoding mechanism that operates on the penultimate layer of neural networks to extract meaningful interpretations of the network's internal representations. The approach involves training auxiliary models to map decoded representations back to human-interpretable features, allowing insights into the decision pathways of the neural network.

Results/Findings: Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that LioNets provides significant improvements in local interpretability while maintaining high predictive performance. The analysis reveals that the method successfully extracts interpretable attributes that align with key features used by the neural network for its predictions. Comparative studies show LioNets outperforming existing interpretability techniques in terms of coherence and clarity of interpretations.

Conclusion/Implications: LioNets presents a substantial advancement in the field of neural network interpretability, bridging the gap between model complexity and interpretability. By focusing on local interpretations through penultimate layer decoding, this approach offers practical applications in areas where understanding the model's decision process is critical, such as healthcare, autonomous systems, and financial services. The research paves the way for the development of more transparent AI systems and encourages further exploration into the interpretability of complex models.

Keywords: LioNets, neural networks, interpretability, penultimate layer, decoding, auxiliary models, AI transparency, local interpretation.
