This paper introduces a novel information-geometric framework for feature extraction and moment reconstruction in dynamical systems. Addressing the challenge of efficiently representing complex system dynamics, we leverage concepts from information geometry to derive a coordinate-free representation of system trajectories. Our method employs the Fisher-Rao metric to define a Riemannian manifold structure on the space of probability distributions associated with system states. Feature extraction is achieved by identifying geodesics on this manifold that capture dominant modes of system evolution. For moment reconstruction, we develop an optimization procedure that utilizes the geometric properties of the manifold to estimate higher-order moments from limited observations. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach on benchmark dynamical systems, showing significant improvements in feature compactness and reconstruction accuracy compared to traditional methods like Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD). The information-geometric perspective provides new insights into the underlying structure of dynamical systems and offers a powerful tool for dimensionality reduction and data-driven modeling. Keywords: Information Geometry, Dynamical Systems, Feature Extraction, Moment Reconstruction, Riemannian Manifold, Fisher-Rao Metric.
