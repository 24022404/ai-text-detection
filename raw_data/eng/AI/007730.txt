Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved significant advancements in solving complex decision-making tasks, yet the challenge of efficiently exploring large state-action spaces remains a critical bottleneck. Intrinsic motivation, which drives agents to explore and learn without explicit external rewards, has emerged as a promising approach to address this issue. This survey provides a comprehensive overview of intrinsic motivation techniques in RL, focusing on their role in enhancing exploration, improving sample efficiency, and enabling autonomous learning in sparse-reward environments. We categorize and analyze key methods, including curiosity-driven exploration, novelty-seeking, and information-theoretic approaches, while discussing their theoretical foundations and practical implementations. Furthermore, we compare the performance of these methods across various benchmarks and highlight their strengths and limitations. The survey also identifies open challenges, such as scalability, generalization, and integration with extrinsic rewards, and outlines future research directions. By synthesizing existing literature, this work aims to serve as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners seeking to leverage intrinsic motivation to advance the capabilities of RL systems.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Intrinsic Motivation, Exploration, Curiosity, Autonomous Learning, Sparse Rewards.
