Sparsely activated networks have shown promise in reducing computational costs by activating only a subset of neurons for each input. This paper investigates novel methods for training sparsely activated networks to improve their performance and efficiency. We introduce a dynamic routing mechanism that adapts the network's active sub-structure based on input characteristics, promoting more efficient computation. Furthermore, we explore regularizations that encourage sparsity while maintaining representational power. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves comparable or superior accuracy to densely connected networks with significantly reduced computational overhead. The proposed techniques offer a practical approach to deploying deep learning models in resource-constrained environments. Key words: Sparse Activation, Neural Networks, Dynamic Routing, Efficiency, Deep Learning.
