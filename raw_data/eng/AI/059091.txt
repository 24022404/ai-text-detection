Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) aims to recover the reward function that explains an expert's behavior, a critical task in imitation learning. However, traditional IRL methods often struggle with multi-intention scenarios, where the expert’s behavior is driven by multiple, potentially conflicting goals. This paper introduces Deep Adaptive Multi-Intention Inverse Reinforcement Learning (DAMIRL), a novel approach that leverages deep neural networks to adaptively disentangle and learn the underlying reward functions corresponding to these multiple intentions. DAMIRL employs a variational inference framework to infer both the intention behind each demonstrated behavior and the reward function associated with that intention. Furthermore, it incorporates an attention mechanism to dynamically weigh the influence of different intentions based on the context of the demonstrated state. Experiments on several simulated environments demonstrate that DAMIRL significantly outperforms existing IRL algorithms in accurately recovering reward functions and predicting expert behavior, particularly in complex, multi-intention settings. The results highlight DAMIRL’s ability to learn nuanced reward structures and provide insights into the expert's decision-making process. Keywords: Inverse Reinforcement Learning, Multi-Intention Learning, Deep Learning, Imitation Learning, Reward Function Recovery, Attention Mechanism.
