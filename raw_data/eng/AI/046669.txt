This paper presents a novel approach to message passing inference by leveraging deep learning techniques to improve the accuracy and efficiency of probabilistic models. The objective is to enhance the traditional message passing algorithms by learning effective message representations that capture complex dependencies between variables. Our method employs a neural network-based framework to learn the messages, allowing for flexible and adaptive inference in complex graphical models. The results show significant improvements in inference accuracy and speed compared to traditional message passing methods, particularly in models with large numbers of variables and complex factor graphs. The key findings highlight the importance of learned message representations in capturing subtle dependencies and facilitating efficient inference. This research contributes to the development of more accurate and scalable probabilistic models, with potential applications in areas such as computer vision, natural language processing, and decision-making under uncertainty. Key keywords: message passing inference, deep learning, probabilistic models, graphical models, neural networks.
