This research addresses the challenge of parameter tuning in the Gradient Temporal Difference (GTD) learning algorithms, a critical component in reinforcement learning models, which often requires extensive manual intervention and specialized knowledge. Methods: We propose a novel parameter-free GTD learning approach that eliminates the need for manual parameter specification, leveraging adaptive learning techniques to maintain stability and performance. The proposed method utilizes an automatic adjustment mechanism based on system feedback, allowing for dynamic adaptation to changing environments without requiring preset parameters. Results: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our parameter-free GTD learning outperforms standard GTD methods, achieving comparable or superior convergence rates and predictive accuracy across a variety of benchmarks in both simulated and real-world environments. Conclusion: The findings suggest that our approach not only simplifies the application of GTD learning by removing manual tuning processes but also enhances its adaptability and robustness. This advancement provides significant implications for deploying reinforcement learning models in dynamic and complex environments, ultimately broadening the applicability of GTD methods in real-time decision-making systems. Keywords: Gradient Temporal Difference, Reinforcement Learning, Parameter-free Learning, Adaptive Learning, Dynamic Environments.
