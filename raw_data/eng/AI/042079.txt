This paper introduces Likelihood Quantile Networks (LQN), a novel approach to coordinating multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) in complex, partially observable environments. LQN addresses the challenge of decentralized execution by enabling agents to reason about the likelihood of other agents' policies, thereby improving coordination and decision-making. The method involves learning a distribution over quantiles representing the likelihood of each agent's policy, allowing agents to estimate the joint action distribution and select actions that maximize expected team reward. We evaluate LQN on a set of challenging cooperative MARL tasks, including StarCraft II micromanagement scenarios, and demonstrate significant performance improvements compared to state-of-the-art MARL algorithms. The results indicate that LQN fosters more effective coordination and robustness, particularly in environments with noisy communication and limited information. The learned likelihood quantiles provide a valuable mechanism for agents to understand and anticipate the behavior of their teammates, leading to more efficient and reliable collaboration. Keywords: multi-agent reinforcement learning, coordination, decentralized execution, likelihood estimation, quantile regression, deep learning.
