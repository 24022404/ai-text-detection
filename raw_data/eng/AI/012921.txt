This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised learning for 3D point cloud data, a critical task in computer vision and robotics. Traditional methods often rely on supervised learning, which requires extensive labeled datasets, limiting their scalability and applicability. To overcome this, we propose a novel deep unsupervised learning framework that leverages graph topology inference and adaptive filtering to extract meaningful representations from raw 3D point clouds. Our approach constructs a dynamic graph structure to model the spatial relationships between points, enabling the inference of intrinsic topological properties. A filtering mechanism is then applied to refine the graph, enhancing the robustness of the learned representations against noise and outliers. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in tasks such as shape classification, segmentation, and reconstruction, outperforming existing unsupervised techniques. The proposed framework not only eliminates the need for labeled data but also provides a scalable and efficient solution for processing large-scale 3D point clouds. This work contributes to advancing unsupervised learning in 3D vision, with potential applications in autonomous systems, augmented reality, and medical imaging.  

**Keywords**: 3D point clouds, unsupervised learning, graph topology inference, adaptive filtering, deep learning, computer vision.
