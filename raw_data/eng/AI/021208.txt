Offline reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a promising approach for training policies using pre-collected datasets, eliminating the need for costly online interactions. However, existing offline RL methods often exhibit excessive conservativeness, leading to suboptimal performance and limited generalization. This paper addresses the challenge of reducing conservativeness in offline RL while maintaining stability and safety. We propose a novel framework that leverages uncertainty quantification and adaptive policy constraints to balance exploration and exploitation in offline settings. Our approach integrates a probabilistic model to estimate the uncertainty of state-action pairs and dynamically adjusts the policy constraints based on dataset coverage and risk tolerance. Experimental results on benchmark tasks demonstrate that our method significantly reduces conservativeness, achieving higher performance compared to state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms. The proposed framework also exhibits improved generalization to unseen environments, highlighting its potential for real-world applications where data efficiency and safety are critical. This work contributes to advancing offline RL by providing a principled and practical solution to mitigate conservativeness, paving the way for more robust and scalable deployment of RL in data-driven domains.  

**Keywords**: Offline reinforcement learning, conservativeness reduction, uncertainty quantification, adaptive policy constraints, generalization, data efficiency.
