This paper proposes a novel Graph-based Visual-Semantic Entanglement Network (GVSEN) designed to tackle the challenging task of zero-shot image recognition. The objective is to recognize unseen classes without prior training data, leveraging the relationship between visual and semantic features. Our approach employs a graph-based framework to model the entanglement between visual and semantic information, enabling the network to learn a robust and generalizable representation. The GVSEN model utilizes a graph convolutional network to integrate visual features extracted from images and semantic features represented as word embeddings. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of GVSEN, outperforming state-of-the-art methods on benchmark datasets such as ImageNet and CUB. The key findings highlight the importance of visual-semantic entanglement in zero-shot learning, achieving significant improvements in recognition accuracy. This research contributes to the development of more advanced zero-shot image recognition systems, with potential applications in areas like computer vision, robotics, and autonomous systems. Key keywords: zero-shot learning, graph neural networks, visual-semantic entanglement, image recognition, deep learning.
