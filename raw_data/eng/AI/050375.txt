Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has emerged as a pivotal paradigm for training autonomous agents in complex, interactive environments. This paper presents a selective overview of the theoretical foundations and algorithmic advancements within MARL, focusing on key challenges such as non-stationarity, credit assignment, and scalability. We explore core theoretical concepts including Markov games, Nash equilibrium, and convergence properties in various MARL settings. Algorithmically, the paper surveys prominent approaches, contrasting value-based, policy-based, and actor-critic methods adapted for multi-agent scenarios. Specific attention is given to algorithms addressing partial observability and communication strategies. The overview highlights the trade-offs between centralized and decentralized execution, and the impact of different reward structures on emergent behavior. This paper contributes a consolidated perspective on the current state of MARL, providing insights into both the theoretical underpinnings and practical implementations, thereby facilitating future research directions. Keywords: Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning, Markov Games, Game Theory, Decentralized Control, Reinforcement Learning, Autonomous Agents.
