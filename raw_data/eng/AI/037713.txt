**Abstract:**

Skeleton-based action recognition has emerged as a powerful technique for understanding human movements in videos, leveraging the structured representation of body joints. However, most existing approaches rely on static or fully-connected graphs, often leading to increased computational complexity and potential redundancy in modeling joint relationships. This paper proposes an optimized skeleton-based action recognition framework through sparsified graph regression, aiming to enhance recognition accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency. The method first constructs a data-driven, sparsified skeletal graph by applying a graph regression technique that learns an optimal connection structure among joints based on training data. This adaptive sparsification effectively captures essential inter-joint dependencies without introducing unnecessary edges, reducing both model complexity and overfitting. The optimized skeleton graph is then integrated into a graph neural network (GNN) architecture to learn discriminative spatiotemporal features for action classification. Experimental evaluations on benchmark action recognition datasets demonstrate notable improvements in recognition performance and computational speed compared to traditional fully-connected and heuristic graph structures. The results highlight the proposed framework’s capacity to generalize across diverse actions and subjects. Our approach offers a practical solution for real-time action recognition in applications such as surveillance, human-computer interaction, and virtual reality. Key contributions include the introduction of a sparsified graph regression module for skeleton modeling and the demonstration of its effectiveness in improving skeleton-based action recognition. 

**Keywords:** Skeleton-based action recognition, sparsified graph regression, graph neural network, spatiotemporal features, human action classification, computational efficiency.
