Material classification is crucial for various applications, including robotics, recycling, and manufacturing. This paper investigates the efficacy of transfer learning techniques using convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for material classification tasks, particularly when training data is limited. We leverage pre-trained CNN models, specifically VGG16 and ResNet50, on the ImageNet dataset and fine-tune them on a novel dataset of material images. The dataset comprises images of diverse materials such as metal, plastic, paper, and glass, captured under varying lighting conditions and viewpoints. Experimental results demonstrate that transfer learning significantly improves classification accuracy compared to training CNNs from scratch. Fine-tuning VGG16 achieved a classification accuracy of 92% while ResNet50 achieved 94% on the material dataset, outperforming models trained without transfer learning by an average of 15%. This study highlights the potential of transfer learning to enable accurate and efficient material classification, even with limited labeled data. Keywords: Transfer Learning, Convolutional Neural Networks, Material Classification, Image Recognition, Deep Learning.
