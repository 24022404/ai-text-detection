This paper presents a novel full probabilistic model for yes-no type crowdsourcing in multi-class classification problems. The objective is to improve the accuracy of classification tasks by leveraging the wisdom of crowds, where workers provide yes-no answers to questions about the class labels of items. Our approach employs a Bayesian framework to model the uncertainty of worker responses and item classifications, allowing for the estimation of worker expertise and item difficulty. The model is trained using a variational inference algorithm, which enables efficient and scalable computation. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our model in achieving higher accuracy and robustness compared to existing methods, particularly in scenarios with noisy or sparse worker responses. The key contributions of this research include the development of a probabilistic framework for modeling yes-no type crowdsourcing, the incorporation of worker expertise and item difficulty into the model, and the demonstration of its applicability to multi-class classification problems. Our model has significant implications for real-world applications, such as data annotation, content moderation, and sentiment analysis, where crowdsourcing is increasingly used to collect and label data. Key keywords: crowdsourcing, multi-class classification, probabilistic modeling, Bayesian inference, variational inference, worker expertise, item difficulty.
