This paper addresses the challenge of weakly supervised semantic segmentation, a task that seeks to segment a given image into semantically meaningful parts, despite limited annotation data. Traditional methods often struggle with accuracy due to inadequate supervision signals. Here, we propose a novel approach that leverages classifiers fully to enhance segmentation performance by modifying the cues used during training.

Methods/Approach: Our approach introduces modified classifiers which are utilized to generate enhanced cues that serve as richer supervision signals. These cues are iteratively refined and employed in a feedback loop, improving the learning process. The proposed model integrates these modified cues into a segmentation neural network, augmenting the conventional weakly supervised frameworks. We employ a combination of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and attention mechanisms to facilitate robust feature extraction and segmentation.

Results/Findings: Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, our method demonstrates superior performance compared to existing weakly supervised semantic segmentation techniques. Quantitative results show a significant improvement in accuracy metrics, while qualitative analyses highlight the model's ability to produce cleaner and more precise segmentations. The comparisons to state-of-the-art methods reveal the efficacy of using fully leveraged classifiers in generating modified cues.

Conclusion/Implications: Our research contributes a novel perspective on enhancing weakly supervised learning models for semantic segmentation by optimizing cue generation through classifiers. This advancement not only increases segmentation accuracy but also broadens the practical applicability of such methods in real-world scenarios. The approach holds potential for deployment in various domains such as autonomous driving, medical imaging, and object recognition, paving the way for more reliable and efficient image segmentation solutions.

Keywords: Weakly Supervised Learning, Semantic Segmentation, Classifiers, Modified Cues, Convolutional Neural Networks, Attention Mechanisms.
