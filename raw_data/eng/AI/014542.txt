The paper addresses the challenge of causal inference in Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs), specifically focusing on a class known as Block MDPs. The primary goal is to enhance decision-making processes by identifying causal structures that remain invariant across diverse environments.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework for Invariant Causal Prediction (ICP) within Block MDPs. This approach leverages structural assumptions inherent in Block MDPs to separate confounding variables from causative factors that are robust across different settings. Our method combines elements of causal discovery with machine learning techniques to identify these invariant causal structures without reliance on pre-existing data partitions.

Results/Findings: The experimental evaluation conducted across a range of synthetic and real-world Block MDP scenarios demonstrates the effectiveness of our causal prediction model. Our approach consistently outperforms traditional methods and existing causal inference frameworks, showing superior performance in terms of accuracy and generalization to unseen environments. This enhancement in model robustness contributes to more reliable policy development in decision-making systems operating under uncertainty.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of invariant causal prediction within Block MDPs represents a significant advancement in bridging causal inference with reinforcement learning under partial observability. This research not only extends theoretical knowledge in the intersection of causality and POMDPs but also presents practical implications for domains such as robotics, autonomous systems, and dynamic decision-making applications where understanding latent causal factors is crucial for performance. Future directions include exploring scalability and application in high-dimensional state spaces.

Keywords: Block MDPs, Invariant Causal Prediction, Causal Inference, Reinforcement Learning, POMDP, Structural Assumptions, Decision-Making Systems.
