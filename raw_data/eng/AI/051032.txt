Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have achieved exceptional success in synthesizing high-quality data, yet their training remains challenging due to issues of instability and mode collapse. Consistency regularization has recently emerged as a promising technique to address these challenges by encouraging the generator and discriminator to produce stable outputs under input perturbations. In this paper, we propose an improved consistency regularization framework for GANs, introducing novel loss functions and augmentation strategies that enhance training stability and sample quality. Our approach systematically integrates adaptive perturbations and multi-scale feature alignment to enforce consistency, leading to more robust generator-discriminator dynamics. We evaluate our method on established image synthesis benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements in Inception Score (IS) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) compared to baseline consistency regularization techniques. The results indicate better mode coverage and reduced overfitting, with minimal computational overhead. Our findings contribute a practical and effective framework for stabilizing GAN training, with potential applications in image generation, data augmentation, and unsupervised representation learning. 

Keywords: GANs, consistency regularization, training stability, image synthesis, generative models, robust learning.
