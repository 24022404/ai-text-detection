**Abstract:**  
Assessing extubation readiness in extremely preterm infants remains a critical and challenging task in neonatal intensive care, with significant implications for patient outcomes. This study aims to improve predictive modeling of extubation readiness by leveraging machine learning techniques, specifically focusing on the application of undersampling and bagging with decision tree algorithms. Comprehensive cardiorespiratory data, including heart rate, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation, were collected from a cohort of extremely preterm infants during mechanical ventilation. To address the inherent class imbalance in successful versus failed extubation cases, an undersampling technique was employed to balance the dataset. Subsequently, bagging (bootstrap aggregating) of decision trees was implemented to enhance the robustness and accuracy of the predictive model. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed ensemble approach outperforms standard single decision tree models and other baseline classifiers in terms of sensitivity, specificity, and overall prediction accuracy. Notably, the enhanced model achieved a balanced performance, reducing the risk of false predictions that can lead to adverse clinical consequences. These findings suggest that combining undersampling with bagging provides an effective and interpretable strategy for early identification of extubation readiness in this vulnerable population, supporting more informed clinical decision-making and potentially improving patient outcomes. **Keywords:** extubation readiness, preterm infants, decision trees, bagging, undersampling, cardiorespiratory analysis, machine learning, neonatal care.
