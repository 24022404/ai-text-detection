This paper presents a novel unsupervised framework for holistic 3D motion understanding, emphasizing pixel-level geometry learning from monocular video data. The primary objective is to achieve precise 3D scene reconstruction and motion estimation without reliance on annotated ground-truth labels. Our approach leverages a unified neural architecture that jointly models depth, camera motion, and object-level dynamics, allowing the system to capture both rigid and non-rigid transformations within complex environments. The method integrates differentiable geometric constraints directly into the learning process, ensuring each pixel contributes meaningfully to the inferred 3D structure and motion fields. Experimental evaluations on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate that our model significantly outperforms existing unsupervised and self-supervised methods in depth and motion accuracy. Furthermore, qualitative results show enhanced scene consistency and object boundary delineation, even in challenging scenarios with dynamic motions and occlusions. The proposed framework offers general applicability to tasks such as scene flow estimation, video-based robotics navigation, and augmented reality. Our contributions highlight the importance of holistic, pixel-wise understanding for advancing unsupervised 3D geometry learning and provide a foundation for future research in large-scale scene perception. Keywords: unsupervised learning, 3D reconstruction, motion estimation, monocular video, pixel-wise geometry, scene flow.
