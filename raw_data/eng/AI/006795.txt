Factored Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) offer a compact representation for sequential decision-making problems with state spaces that grow exponentially with the number of features. However, identifying the relevant features for optimal policy determination remains a significant challenge, particularly in sample-constrained settings. This paper introduces a novel feature selection algorithm for factored MDPs designed to maximize sample efficiency. Our approach leverages a combination of mutual information estimation and a novel variant of the Bellman error to identify and prioritize features relevant to the optimal value function. We formulate a submodular optimization problem to select the most informative subset of features, balancing exploration and exploitation. Empirical evaluations on benchmark factored MDP domains demonstrate that our algorithm achieves comparable or superior performance to existing methods while requiring significantly fewer samples. Specifically, results show a reduction in sample complexity by up to 40% in certain domains. This work contributes a practical and efficient approach to feature selection in factored MDPs, enabling effective planning in complex, high-dimensional environments with limited data. Keywords: Factored MDPs, Feature Selection, Reinforcement Learning, Sample Efficiency, Submodular Optimization.
