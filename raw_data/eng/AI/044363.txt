Neural network optimization is a cornerstone of modern artificial intelligence, yet it is often marked by nondeterministic behavior and instability, leading to variability in model performance across training runs. This paper investigates the sources and impacts of nondeterminism and instability in the optimization processes of deep neural networks. Employing a combination of controlled experiments, statistical analysis, and visualization techniques, we systematically examine factors such as random initialization, data shuffling, hardware-induced non-reproducibility, and the influence of optimization algorithms. Our results demonstrate that even under fixed hyperparameter settings, seemingly minor sources of randomness can result in significant fluctuations in both model accuracy and convergence trajectories. Furthermore, we analyze the sensitivity of common neural architectures, including convolutional and transformer-based models, to various sources of nondeterminism. The findings reveal that instability not only affects final model performance but can also hinder model interpretability and reproducibility, posing challenges for real-world deployment. We propose practical guidelines and mitigation strategies, such as robust initialization schemes and deterministic training pipelines, to reduce performance variability. This study highlights the critical need for greater transparency and stability in neural network training, with implications for benchmarking, deployment, and ongoing research in neural optimization. Key keywords: neural networks, optimization, nondeterminism, instability, reproducibility, deep learning, model performance.
