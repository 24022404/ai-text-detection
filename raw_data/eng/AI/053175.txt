This paper aims to provide a comprehensive tutorial on recursive models for analyzing and predicting path choice behavior, a critical area of study within transportation systems and decision-making processes. Our objective is to offer a clear understanding of how recursive modeling techniques can be effectively employed to interpret the complexity of path choice decisions. Through this tutorial, we explore various recursive models, including nested logit models and dynamic choice algorithms, which offer structured approaches for capturing the decision-making process in path selection. By integrating both probabilistic frameworks and sequential decision theories, we enable readers to comprehend the nuanced aspects that influence path choice. Our findings illustrate the models' capability to accurately forecast path choice behavior with high precision, demonstrating significant improvements over traditional linear models. These recursive models also prove advantageous in scenarios involving real-time decision adjustments and evolving environmental conditions. The implications of this study underscore the potential applications of recursive models in optimizing transportation networks, enhancing navigational systems, and informing policy-making efforts in urban planning. Our contribution lies in demystifying the recursive modeling process, thus empowering practitioners and researchers to apply advanced predictive techniques in their respective fields. Key keywords include recursive models, path choice behavior, nested logit, dynamic decision-making, and transportation systems.
