This paper addresses the challenge of domain adaptation in machine learning by introducing an unsupervised meta domain translation framework. The purpose is to enable effective knowledge transfer between disparate domains without relying on labeled data, thereby expanding the applicability of machine learning models to new, unlabeled environments.

Methods/Approach: Our approach leverages a novel meta-learning algorithm that is capable of learning transferable representations across multiple domains. By employing unsupervised learning techniques, the proposed framework can learn domain-invariant features, reducing the dependency on annotated datasets. The method utilizes an innovative architecture that combines domain adversarial networks with meta-learning strategies to achieve robust translation across domains.

Results/Findings: Experiments demonstrate the framework's superior performance in comparison to existing domain adaptation techniques, showcasing improved accuracy and adaptability in various benchmark datasets. Key findings highlight the ability of the proposed method to generalize effectively, achieving consistent results even in challenging scenarios with significant domain shifts.

Conclusion/Implications: The research presents a significant advancement in the field of domain adaptation, offering a scalable and flexible solution for cross-domain learning without the need for labeled data. This unsupervised meta domain translation framework paves the way for practical applications in areas such as autonomous systems, natural language processing, and computer vision, where continuous adaptation to new data is essential. The innovations presented hold the potential to substantially impact the development of more autonomous and intelligent systems capable of seamless operation across diverse environments.

Key Keywords: domain adaptation, unsupervised learning, meta-learning, domain-invariant features, knowledge transfer, machine learning models.
