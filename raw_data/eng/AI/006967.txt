**Abstract**

This paper presents U-Time, a novel fully convolutional neural network (FCN) architecture designed for robust and efficient segmentation of time series data, with a specific application to automatic sleep staging. The objective of this study is to address the challenges of accurate and scalable sleep stage classification from polysomnographic (PSG) recordings, a crucial task in sleep medicine that often relies on labor-intensive manual annotation. Our approach adapts the U-Net framework, widely known for biomedical image segmentation, to the domain of sequential physiological signals by employing temporal convolutions coupled with a multi-scale feature extraction strategy. U-Time processes raw or minimally preprocessed time series inputs and produces precise stage-wise predictions without the need for manually engineered features. To validate our model, we conduct extensive experiments on publicly available sleep datasets, comparing U-Time's performance against conventional machine learning methods and recent deep learning techniques. Results demonstrate that U-Time achieves superior accuracy and robustness, particularly in handling variable-length recordings and noisy data. Furthermore, the network's architecture allows for real-time segmentation and efficient training, making it suitable for large-scale clinical applications. In conclusion, U-Time advances automated sleep staging by providing an end-to-end solution that generalizes well across diverse subjects, with potential extensions to other time series segmentation tasks in healthcare and beyond. Key keywords: time series segmentation, sleep staging, fully convolutional network, U-Net, deep learning, biomedical signal processing.
