**Abstract**

Gastric cancer diagnosis through histopathological image analysis is critical for effective clinical intervention, yet manual examination remains labor-intensive and prone to variability. This paper introduces GasHis-Transformer, a novel multi-scale visual transformer framework designed to address the challenges of automatic classification in gastric histopathology images. Unlike traditional convolutional neural network (CNN) approaches, our method leverages transformer architectures to capture both local and global contextual features at multiple image scales, enhancing representational power while reducing information loss. GasHis-Transformer integrates patch-based embedding with hierarchical attention mechanisms, allowing for precise feature extraction across different magnifications. Extensive experiments conducted on publicly available gastric histopathology datasets demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art models in terms of accuracy, F1-score, and robustness, particularly in identifying intricate tissue structures and cancer subtypes. The findings indicate that GasHis-Transformer not only improves classification performance but also offers interpretability through visualization of attention maps, potentially aiding pathologists in decision-making. This research highlights the transformative potential of visual transformers in digital pathology and paves the way for advanced computer-aided diagnosis systems in gastric cancer detection. Keywords: visual transformer, multi-scale analysis, gastric histopathology, image classification, digital pathology, attention mechanism.
