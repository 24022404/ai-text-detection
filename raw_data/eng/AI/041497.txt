Graph coarsening, the process of simplifying a graph by merging nodes and edges, has long been a crucial technique in scientific computing for solving problems on large-scale networks. This paper explores the applicability and adaptation of graph coarsening methods to the field of machine learning, where graph-structured data is increasingly prevalent. We investigate several existing algebraic and spectral graph coarsening algorithms, analyzing their performance when applied to tasks such as graph classification and node embedding. We introduce novel adaptations of these methods, specifically designed to preserve crucial structural information relevant to machine learning tasks, including node centrality and community structure. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our adapted coarsening algorithms achieve significant reductions in computational complexity while maintaining or improving the accuracy of machine learning models compared to using the original graphs. This work highlights the potential of graph coarsening as a powerful tool for scaling machine learning algorithms to handle large and complex graph datasets, paving the way for new applications in areas such as social network analysis and drug discovery. Keywords: Graph Coarsening, Machine Learning, Graph Neural Networks, Spectral Methods, Scientific Computing, Graph Algorithms.
