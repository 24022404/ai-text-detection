This paper presents an unsupervised learning classifier designed to achieve competitive error performance with state-of-the-art supervised approaches. The primary objective is to address the challenge of accurate classification in scenarios where labeled data is limited or unavailable. The proposed method employs a novel clustering-based framework that integrates feature extraction and adaptive similarity metrics to form robust class representations without prior labels. By leveraging iterative optimization and prototype refinement, the classifier effectively distinguishes between underlying data patterns. Experimental evaluations were conducted on several benchmark datasets, including image and text domains, demonstrating that our approach attains error rates comparable to, and in some cases surpassing, established supervised algorithms. Key findings highlight the classifier's strong generalization capability, reduced reliance on manual annotation, and resilience to noise in the input data. The results suggest that unsupervised learning can close the performance gap with traditional supervised methods, opening new possibilities for real-world deployment in fields such as anomaly detection, natural language processing, and computer vision. This work contributes a scalable and adaptive solution to unsupervised classification, paving the way for further research into self-sufficient machine learning systems. Keywords: unsupervised learning, classification, clustering, error performance, prototype refinement, machine learning.
