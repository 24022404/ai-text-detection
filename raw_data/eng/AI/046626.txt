This paper presents a novel approach to enhancing in-vehicle comfort and driving experience by mining personalized climate preferences for assistant driving systems. The objective of the research is to address the variability in individual thermal comfort needs and bridge the gap between generic climate control settings and drivers’ specific preferences. We propose a data-driven framework that leverages machine learning algorithms to analyze real-time sensor data, historical user interactions, and contextual factors, such as weather, time of day, and driving patterns. The system continuously learns and adapts to each driver’s unique climate preferences, enabling dynamic adjustment of temperature, airflow, and humidity within the vehicle. Our experimental evaluation, conducted on a representative dataset collected from instrumented vehicles, demonstrates that the proposed model accurately predicts user comfort settings, achieving a significant improvement in satisfaction rates compared to existing rule-based and manual systems. The findings highlight the potential of personalized climate control in reducing driver distraction and energy consumption while promoting sustainable vehicle operation. This work contributes to the fields of intelligent transportation, human-centered AI, and automotive user experience by introducing adaptable, context-aware assistants tailored to individual occupants. Key keywords include personalized climate control, assistant driving, thermal comfort, machine learning, adaptive systems, and in-vehicle user experience.
