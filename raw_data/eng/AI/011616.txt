Coral reef ecosystems are vital to marine biodiversity, yet their classification and monitoring remain challenging due to the sparse and complex nature of underwater imagery. This paper addresses the problem of sparse coral classification by leveraging deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to improve accuracy and efficiency in identifying coral species from limited and noisy datasets. We propose a novel CNN-based framework that integrates advanced data augmentation techniques and transfer learning to enhance model performance under sparse data conditions. Our approach is evaluated on a publicly available coral dataset, demonstrating significant improvements in classification accuracy compared to traditional methods. Experimental results show that the proposed model achieves a 15% higher accuracy rate and better generalization across diverse coral species. The findings highlight the potential of deep learning in addressing sparse data challenges in marine biology, offering a scalable solution for automated coral reef monitoring. This research contributes to the development of robust AI-driven tools for environmental conservation, with implications for biodiversity assessment and ecosystem management.  

**Keywords**: coral classification, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, sparse data, marine biodiversity, transfer learning.
