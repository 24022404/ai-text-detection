The increasing complexity of high-performance neural networks often leads to significant computational and memory overhead, limiting their deployment in resource-constrained environments. This paper introduces *RicciNets*, a novel curvature-guided pruning framework that leverages principles from differential geometry, specifically the Ricci flow, to optimize neural network architectures. The objective of this work is to reduce model complexity while preserving or even enhancing performance by identifying and removing redundant parameters based on the intrinsic curvature of the network's loss landscape. Our approach employs a dynamic pruning strategy that iteratively adjusts the network topology, guided by curvature metrics derived from the Ricci flow. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that RicciNets achieve up to a 40% reduction in model size with minimal accuracy loss, outperforming traditional pruning methods such as magnitude-based and random pruning. Furthermore, the pruned models exhibit improved generalization capabilities, particularly in low-data regimes. The results highlight the potential of integrating geometric insights into neural network optimization, offering a scalable and efficient solution for deploying high-performance models in real-world applications. This work contributes to the growing field of efficient deep learning by introducing a mathematically grounded pruning technique that bridges differential geometry and machine learning.  

**Keywords**: Neural network pruning, Ricci flow, curvature optimization, model compression, efficient deep learning.
