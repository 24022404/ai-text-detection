This paper investigates the phenomena of nondeterminism and instability in neural network optimization, a crucial aspect of deep learning that affects the reliability and performance of artificial intelligence models. Our objective is to understand the underlying causes of these issues and develop strategies to mitigate them. We employ a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical experiments to study the optimization processes of various neural network architectures. Our approach involves analyzing the impact of different optimization algorithms, hyperparameters, and initialization methods on the stability and convergence of neural networks. The results show that nondeterminism and instability can be attributed to the interplay between the optimization algorithm, the network architecture, and the training data. We propose a novel regularization technique that reduces the instability of neural networks and improves their generalization performance. Our findings have significant implications for the development of more robust and reliable neural network models, and our approach can be applied to a wide range of applications, including computer vision, natural language processing, and speech recognition. Key keywords: neural networks, optimization, nondeterminism, instability, deep learning, regularization techniques.
