This paper addresses the challenge of recovering semantic attributes from weakly labeled data, a crucial task in various computer vision applications. Our objective is to develop a framework that can effectively learn semantic attributes without requiring extensive manual annotation. We propose a novel weakly supervised approach that leverages a combination of attention mechanisms and graph-based models to identify relevant attributes. Our method utilizes a multi-task learning strategy, where the model is trained to predict both the object class and its corresponding semantic attributes. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our approach, outperforming state-of-the-art methods on benchmark datasets. The key findings of our research highlight the importance of incorporating weak supervision and attention mechanisms in attribute recovery tasks. Our contributions have significant implications for applications such as image retrieval, object recognition, and scene understanding, where semantic attributes play a vital role. The proposed framework offers a promising solution for recovering semantic attributes in a weakly supervised setting, with potential applications in computer vision, machine learning, and artificial intelligence. Key keywords: weakly supervised learning, semantic attributes, attention mechanisms, graph-based models, computer vision, machine learning.
