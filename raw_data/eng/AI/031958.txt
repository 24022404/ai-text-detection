This paper investigates the efficacy of bonus-based exploration methods within the Arcade Learning Environment (ALE). We address the challenge of sparse rewards in reinforcement learning by implementing and evaluating several intrinsic motivation techniques that provide artificial rewards for exploring novel states. Our approach focuses on count-based, prediction error-based, and pseudo-count methods to encourage exploration. We benchmark these algorithms across a diverse set of Atari 2600 games, comparing their performance against traditional exploration strategies like ε-greedy and Boltzmann exploration. Results indicate that pseudo-count based methods outperform other approaches in many ALE games, particularly those with sparse rewards, leading to significantly improved learning speeds and higher asymptotic performance. Furthermore, we analyze the impact of different bonus scaling factors on exploration efficiency. The findings highlight the potential of intrinsic motivation for accelerating reinforcement learning in complex environments and offer insights into selecting appropriate bonus mechanisms for specific game characteristics. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Exploration, Intrinsic Motivation, Arcade Learning Environment, Atari, Bonus-Based Exploration, Pseudo-count.
