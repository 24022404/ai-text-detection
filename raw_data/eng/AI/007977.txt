Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) has emerged as a promising approach to address the challenges of scalability and sample inefficiency in complex decision-making tasks. However, existing HRL methods often require extensive interaction with the environment, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios where data collection is costly or time-consuming. This paper proposes a novel data-efficient HRL framework that leverages hierarchical task decomposition and meta-learning techniques to significantly reduce the amount of training data required. Our approach introduces a multi-level hierarchy where higher-level policies guide lower-level policies through abstract subgoals, enabling efficient exploration and transfer of knowledge across tasks. We evaluate the proposed framework on a suite of benchmark environments, demonstrating superior performance in terms of sample efficiency and task completion rates compared to state-of-the-art HRL methods. The results highlight the potential of our approach for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and other domains where data efficiency is critical. This work contributes to advancing HRL by providing a scalable and practical solution for learning in data-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning, Data Efficiency, Meta-Learning, Task Decomposition, Sample Efficiency, Autonomous Systems.
