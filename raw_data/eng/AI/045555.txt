This paper addresses the challenge of effectively learning from graph-structured data, which is prevalent in domains such as social networks, molecular chemistry, and knowledge graphs. We propose a novel approach, Convolutional Kernel Networks (CKNs), specifically designed to capture both local and global structural information inherent in graphs. Our method integrates the expressive power of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with the versatility of kernel methods, enabling robust representation learning for graph data. The proposed CKN framework introduces kernelized convolutional operations that aggregate neighborhood information while preserving graph invariances such as node permutation. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including graph classification and node recognition tasks, demonstrate that CKNs consistently outperform traditional graph kernels and recent graph neural network (GNN) architectures in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. Furthermore, our approach exhibits superior scalability to larger graphs and flexibility across diverse graph types. This research highlights the potential of convolutional kernel techniques for advancing graph-based machine learning and offers promising directions for further applications in areas like cheminformatics, bioinformatics, and network analysis. Key keywords: convolutional kernel networks, graph-structured data, graph neural networks, graph kernels, representation learning, machine learning.
