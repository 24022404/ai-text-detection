This study addresses the challenges of manual-label dependency in 3D object detection systems, proposing a novel approach that leverages an open-source simulation environment for training and validation. The research aims to reduce the labor-intensive process of manual annotation in 3D datasets while enhancing detection accuracy and efficiency.

Methods: The proposed system integrates a realistic simulation platform that generates synthetic data to train 3D detection models. By employing advanced algorithms in computer vision and machine learning, the simulator replicates diverse real-world scenarios and object interactions, eliminating the need for manually labeled datasets. The utilization of domain adaptation techniques ensures the synthesized data is transferable to real-world applications.

Results: Experimental evaluation demonstrates that the simulator-trained models achieve competitive performance, comparable to those trained on traditional manually-labeled datasets. Significant improvements in training efficiency and model scalability were observed. The results also show effective generalization across various environments and object types, with reduced computational overhead.

Conclusion: This research contributes to the field of 3D detection by providing an innovative, cost-effective method for model training without manual labeling. The open-source simulator offers a scalable platform for further research and application in areas such as autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality. By eliminating the dependence on labor-intensive labeling processes, this approach has the potential to accelerate advancements in 3D detection technologies.

Keywords: 3D Detection, Open-Source Simulator, Manual-Label Free, Synthetic Data, Computer Vision, Machine Learning, Autonomous Driving, Robotics.
