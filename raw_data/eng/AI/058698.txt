This paper presents ProLab, a novel perceptually uniform projective color coordinate system designed to enhance color representation and processing in digital imaging and graphics applications. Traditional color systems often struggle to evenly model color perceptions under diverse viewing conditions, leading to discrepancies in digital reproductions. ProLab addresses these challenges by focusing on uniformity and perceptual accuracy.

Methods: We introduce a projective transformation that maps traditional color coordinates into a new space where perceptual uniformity is prioritized. The ProLab system employs advanced algorithms that integrate aspects of human vision science, ensuring that changes in digital color values correspond more accurately to perceived color differences. Comparative analyses were conducted against existing color models to evaluate performance and perceptual accuracy.

Results: Our experiments demonstrate that the ProLab system significantly improves perceptual uniformity when assessed against established metrics in color differentiation and reproduction accuracy. The system consistently outperformed traditional models such as sRGB and Lab in maintaining perceptual consistency across a range of media and display settings.

Conclusion: ProLab makes a substantial contribution to the field of digital color technology, providing a more reliable and perceptually accurate tool for color management in diverse applications, including digital photography, video production, and graphic design. Its adoption could lead to more consistent color experiences across devices and applications, ultimately enhancing visual accuracy and aesthetics. Key keywords include perceptual uniformity, projective color model, digital imaging, and color representation.
