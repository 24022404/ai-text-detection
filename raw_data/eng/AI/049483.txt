This paper introduces ResOT, a novel approach to neural signal classification using resource-efficient oblique trees. The objective is to develop a classification model that accurately identifies neural signals while minimizing computational resources and power consumption. ResOT achieves this by employing an innovative oblique tree architecture that leverages sparse representations and efficient splitting criteria. The method is evaluated on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in classification accuracy and reductions in computational cost compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. Key findings include the ability of ResOT to achieve high accuracy with reduced tree depth and node count, resulting in faster inference times and lower energy consumption. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to provide a highly efficient and accurate neural signal classification framework, making it suitable for real-time applications in brain-computer interfaces, neuroprosthetics, and neural decoding. The ResOT approach has significant implications for the development of resource-constrained neural signal processing systems, and its novelty lies in the synergistic combination of oblique trees, sparse representations, and efficient splitting criteria. Relevant keywords: neural signal classification, oblique trees, resource-efficient, brain-computer interfaces, neuroprosthetics.
