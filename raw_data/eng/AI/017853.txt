Event cameras offer significant advantages for visual perception tasks, including high temporal resolution and low latency. However, their output—streams of asynchronous events—presents substantial challenges for conventional deep learning methods, particularly in handling the variability introduced by diverse motion patterns. This paper proposes Motion Equivariant Networks (MENs) designed specifically for event cameras through the introduction of the Temporal Normalization Transform (TNT). Our approach integrates TNT to normalize temporal distributions of events, enabling the network to maintain consistent performance across a wide range of motion dynamics. The MEN architecture combines motion-aware feature extraction with temporal normalization, ensuring equivariance to motion-induced transformations inherent in event data. We evaluate our model on multiple benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in accuracy and robustness over existing state-of-the-art methods in tasks such as object recognition and optical flow estimation. Results reveal that MENs with TNT exhibit enhanced generalization and stability under varying motion conditions. This work introduces a novel framework for event-based vision, advancing the application of deep learning to event camera data and expanding possibilities for real-time robotics, autonomous vehicles, and edge computing. 

Keywords: event cameras, motion equivariance, neural networks, temporal normalization, event-based vision, deep learning, object recognition, optical flow.
