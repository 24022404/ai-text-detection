Generating fonts that accurately reflect desired aesthetic impressions remains a challenging task. This paper introduces Impressions2Font, a novel framework that enables font generation by specifying high-level, subjective impressions. Our method utilizes a generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture, conditioned on impression embeddings learned from a large corpus of text associated with different font styles. This allows users to control font characteristics through intuitive, semantic descriptions rather than low-level parameters. We train the model using a multi-task learning approach, simultaneously optimizing for font reconstruction and impression classification. Experimental results demonstrate that Impressions2Font can generate diverse and visually appealing fonts that effectively capture the intended impressions, outperforming existing font generation techniques in terms of stylistic control and aesthetic quality. The generated fonts exhibit a strong correlation between specified impressions and perceived visual attributes. This research offers a valuable tool for designers and typographers, providing a more intuitive and efficient way to create custom fonts. Keywords: Font generation, generative adversarial networks, impression learning, multi-task learning, typography, style transfer.
