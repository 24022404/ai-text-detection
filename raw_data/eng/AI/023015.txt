Gastric cancer remains a significant global health challenge, necessitating accurate and efficient diagnostic tools. This paper introduces **GasHis-Transformer**, a novel multi-scale visual transformer approach designed for the classification of gastric histopathology images. The proposed method addresses the limitations of traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by leveraging the self-attention mechanism of transformers to capture long-range dependencies and intricate patterns in histopathological images. GasHis-Transformer integrates a multi-scale feature extraction framework, enabling the model to analyze both local and global image features effectively. The approach is evaluated on a comprehensive dataset of gastric histopathology images, demonstrating superior performance in terms of classification accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods. Experimental results reveal that GasHis-Transformer achieves a significant improvement in sensitivity and specificity, particularly in distinguishing between benign and malignant cases. The model's robustness is further validated through extensive ablation studies and cross-dataset evaluations. This research contributes to the field of medical image analysis by providing a scalable and interpretable solution for gastric cancer diagnosis, with potential applications in clinical decision support systems. Key innovations include the integration of multi-scale visual transformers and the adaptation of self-attention mechanisms for histopathology image analysis. Keywords: Gastric cancer, histopathology, visual transformer, multi-scale feature extraction, image classification, self-attention mechanism.
