This study investigates the geometric information content embedded in multiclass classification tasks and its implications for data-driven intelligence. The objective is to quantify and analyze how coarse- and fine-scale geometric structures within data influence classification performance and decision boundaries. We propose a novel framework that leverages topological data analysis (TDA) and manifold learning techniques to extract and interpret these geometric features. By integrating these methods with machine learning models, we evaluate their impact on classification accuracy and robustness across diverse datasets. Our results demonstrate that fine-scale geometric features significantly enhance model performance, particularly in complex, high-dimensional datasets, while coarse-scale structures provide critical insights into global data organization. The findings reveal that incorporating geometric information into classification models improves interpretability and generalizability, offering a deeper understanding of data-driven intelligence. This research contributes to advancing the theoretical foundations of machine learning by highlighting the importance of geometric structures in classification tasks and provides practical implications for designing more efficient and interpretable AI systems.  

**Keywords**: multiclass classification, geometric information, topological data analysis, manifold learning, data-driven intelligence, machine learning.
