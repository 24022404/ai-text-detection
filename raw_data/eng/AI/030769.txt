**Abstract**

This paper explores the application of the Sugeno integral, a nonlinear aggregation operator from fuzzy measure theory, in the context of binary classification within machine learning. The objective is to address challenges in effectively capturing and modeling complex feature interactions that traditional linear models may overlook. We propose a novel classification framework that integrates the Sugeno integral into supervised learning algorithms, enabling flexible aggregation of input attributes based on their importance and collective contribution to the prediction task. The methodological approach includes the construction of fuzzy measures tailored to binary class labels and the optimization of model parameters using gradient-based and heuristic optimization techniques. Experimental evaluations were conducted on multiple standard binary classification datasets, comparing the performance of the Sugeno-based model with established methods such as support vector machines and neural networks. The results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves comparable or superior accuracy, particularly in scenarios with ambiguous or interdependent features. The findings highlight the potential of the Sugeno integral in enhancing model interpretability and robustness, offering a promising direction for advancing fuzzy logic and aggregation techniques in machine learning. Key implications include improved adaptability to diverse data types and the facilitation of decision-making processes where feature interplay is critical. Keywords: Sugeno integral, fuzzy measures, binary classification, machine learning, nonlinear aggregation, interpretability.
