This paper introduces MEVA, a novel large-scale dataset designed to advance research in activity detection within multiview, multimodal video streams. Current activity detection models often lack the capacity to effectively leverage the complementary information offered by diverse sensor modalities and multiple viewpoints. MEVA addresses this limitation by providing synchronized video data from multiple calibrated cameras, along with corresponding audio tracks and inertial measurement unit (IMU) data for a wide range of human activities. We present baseline performance evaluations using state-of-the-art activity detection models, demonstrating the challenges and opportunities presented by MEVA. Results indicate that leveraging multiview and multimodal data significantly improves activity recognition accuracy compared to unimodal, single-view approaches. MEVA provides a valuable resource for developing and evaluating robust, sensor-fusion-based activity detection algorithms, paving the way for more accurate and context-aware human activity understanding.
