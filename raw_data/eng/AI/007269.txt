Contract negotiation is a complex task, often involving agents with conflicting goals. This paper investigates the impact of agent behavior on negotiation outcomes using reinforcement learning. We develop a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework where agents, trained with different reward structures, exhibit either prosocial or selfish behaviors. Prosocial agents are incentivized to reach mutually beneficial agreements, while selfish agents prioritize their individual gains. We evaluate the performance of these agents in a series of simulated contract negotiation scenarios, comparing negotiation success rates, Pareto efficiency, and individual payoffs. Results show that prosocial agents are more likely to reach agreements and achieve higher overall welfare, although selfish agents can sometimes secure better individual outcomes at the expense of the collective. This research provides insights into the design of AI agents for negotiation and highlights the importance of aligning agent incentives with desired social outcomes. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Multi-Agent Systems, Contract Negotiation, Agent Behavior, Prosociality, Game Theory.
