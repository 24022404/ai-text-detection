Throughput statistics were measured for connection counts of 1000, 2000, and 4000, revealing significant trends as the number of connections increased. Most notably, the median and lower percentile (such as the 5th and 10th percentiles) throughput values declined sharply, in many cases dropping to extremely low or even zero levels at higher connection counts. In contrast, the upper percentiles (like the 95th and 99th) as well as maximum throughputs not only remained high but in some cases increased, indicating that while a small fraction of connections maintained strong throughput, the bulk experienced severe degradation. The standard deviation of throughput also rose across higher connection counts, reflecting the growing disparity between the best and worst performing connections. For each level of concurrency, detailed statistics were reported, including precise values for the 5th, 10th, 50th, 95th, and 99th percentiles, the averages, and the maximum observed throughputs, illustrating the highly uneven distribution of network resources as connection load grows.
