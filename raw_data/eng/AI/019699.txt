This paper addresses the challenge of efficient multi-label segmentation in images containing multiple "hedgehog" shapes—a class of objects characterized by complex, radially-oriented boundaries. We present a novel adaptation of the α-expansion algorithm, a graph-cut based optimization technique widely used for discrete energy minimization, to effectively handle the unique geometric and topological properties of hedgehog-like objects. Our approach incorporates shape priors and boundary directionality into the energy function, enabling more accurate delineation of each individual shape even in the presence of significant overlap or occlusion. Experimental evaluation on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrates that our method outperforms existing multi-label segmentation techniques in terms of boundary accuracy, computational efficiency, and robustness to noise. The proposed algorithm maintains scalability for images with a high number of objects and complex interactions. Our contributions include an enhanced formulation of α-expansion for specialized shape constraints, a practical graph construction for hedgehog modeling, and a comprehensive analysis of performance across varied datasets. These insights facilitate improved segmentation in medical imaging, biological studies, and automated object recognition where accurate boundary extraction of spiky or radiate forms is essential. Keywords: α-expansion, graph cuts, multi-label segmentation, hedgehog shapes, shape priors, computer vision.
