Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for solving complex decision-making tasks, yet its performance heavily depends on the initialization of model parameters. This paper investigates the efficacy of random weight guessing as an alternative to traditional RL training methods. The objective is to evaluate whether random initialization can serve as a competitive baseline or even outperform conventional RL approaches in benchmark environments. We conduct extensive experiments across multiple RL benchmarks, comparing random weight guessing with state-of-the-art RL algorithms such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Deep Q-Networks (DQN). Our findings reveal that, in certain scenarios, random weight guessing achieves comparable or superior performance, particularly in environments with low-dimensional state spaces or sparse rewards. These results challenge the assumption that iterative optimization is always necessary for effective RL solutions. The study highlights the potential of random weight guessing as a simple yet effective method for benchmarking and evaluating RL algorithms, offering insights into the role of initialization in model performance. This work contributes to the broader understanding of RL training dynamics and provides a novel perspective on the trade-offs between simplicity and optimization in machine learning.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Random Weight Guessing, Benchmarking, Model Initialization, Decision-Making, Optimization.
