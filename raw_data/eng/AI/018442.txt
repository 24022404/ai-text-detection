This paper addresses the challenge of dense video captioning by leveraging both audio and visual information to generate more accurate and context-aware descriptions for segmented events within videos. We propose a novel Bi-modal Transformer architecture that integrates synchronized audio-visual cues, enabling the model to effectively attend to complementary features while capturing temporal dependencies across multiple modalities. Our approach incorporates specialized fusion layers to combine audio and visual streams and employs a cross-modal attention mechanism to enhance feature representation for each event segment. Extensive experiments on benchmark dense video captioning datasets demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing uni-modal and sequential fusion techniques, delivering higher precision and richer semantic coverage in generated captions. Quantitative results show clear improvements in key evaluation metrics such as METEOR and CIDEr, while qualitative analysis highlights the model’s ability to generate contextually relevant and temporally accurate descriptions. The proposed Bi-modal Transformer approach advances the state of the art in multi-modal video understanding and opens up new possibilities for applications in automated video summarization, content indexing, and assistive technologies. Keywords: dense video captioning, bi-modal transformer, audio-visual cues, multi-modal learning, cross-modal attention.
