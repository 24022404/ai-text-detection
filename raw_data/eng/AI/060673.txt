This study aims to investigate the effectiveness of autoencoders in representation learning for electronic health records (EHRs). Our approach utilizes various autoencoder architectures to learn compact and informative representations of EHR data, which can be used for downstream tasks such as patient clustering, disease prediction, and personalized medicine. We employ a range of autoencoder models, including vanilla autoencoders, denoising autoencoders, and variational autoencoders, and evaluate their performance on a large EHR dataset. Our results show that autoencoders can learn meaningful representations of EHR data, outperforming traditional feature extraction methods in terms of clustering quality and prediction accuracy. We also compare the performance of different autoencoder architectures and identify the most effective models for EHR representation learning. Our study demonstrates the potential of autoencoders in improving the analysis and interpretation of EHR data, with implications for clinical decision support, patient outcomes, and healthcare resource allocation. Key contributions of this work include the development of novel autoencoder-based methods for EHR representation learning and a comprehensive evaluation of their performance in real-world healthcare applications, with relevant keywords including autoencoders, electronic health records, representation learning, and deep learning.
