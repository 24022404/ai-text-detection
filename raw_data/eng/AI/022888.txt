**Abstract**

Traditional belief propagation algorithms are widely used for approximate inference in graphical models, but their computational demands often pose significant challenges in large-scale or real-time applications. This paper introduces an anytime belief propagation framework that leverages sparse domain representations to address these limitations. Our approach reformulates message computations by dynamically focusing on the most influential variables and restricting updates to sparse, high-impact regions of the solution space. This method allows for early extraction of approximate results with progressively improving accuracy as computation proceeds, enabling flexible trade-offs between inference quality and runtime. Experimental results on benchmark graphical models demonstrate substantial improvements in efficiency, with comparable or superior inference accuracy relative to conventional belief propagation and state-of-the-art anytime algorithms. Key contributions include a novel sparse domain message scheduling strategy and a principled procedure for error-bounded approximations at any time step. The proposed approach is broadly applicable to probabilistic inference tasks in domains such as computer vision, sensor networks, and machine learning. Our findings suggest that exploiting sparsity and anytime properties can substantially enhance the scalability and practicality of belief propagation in complex systems. 

**Keywords:** belief propagation, anytime algorithms, sparse domains, graphical models, approximate inference, probabilistic reasoning, scalable algorithms
