This paper addresses the challenge of few-shot learning, where a model must generalize to new tasks with only a handful of examples. Our research introduces CrossTransformers, a novel spatially-aware approach that enhances few-shot transfer by leveraging spatial relationships within the data, an aspect often overlooked in traditional models.

Methods/Approach: CrossTransformers builds upon the transformer architecture by incorporating spatial awareness into the self-attention mechanism. This is achieved by integrating position-aware embeddings that capture spatial dependencies alongside feature similarities. The model is designed to dynamically adapt the attention weights, focusing on both spatial context and relevant features.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that CrossTransformers significantly improves performance on standard few-shot learning benchmarks. The model outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods not only in accuracy but also in its ability to maintain robustness across various domains. These improvements are particularly notable in datasets where spatial information is crucial.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of spatially-aware attention mechanisms in the transformer framework marks a substantial advancement in few-shot learning capabilities. CrossTransformers' ability to effectively utilize spatial context offers promising applications in areas such as computer vision and remote sensing, where spatial relationships play a critical role. This research underscores the potential of enhancing transformer models to further advance transfer learning methodologies.

Keywords: CrossTransformers, few-shot learning, spatially-aware, transformer, attention mechanism, computer vision, transfer learning.
