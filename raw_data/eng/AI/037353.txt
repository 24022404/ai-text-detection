**Abstract:**

Electronic Health Records (EHRs) present unique challenges for data analysis due to their high dimensionality, sparsity, and heterogeneity. This paper investigates the effectiveness of autoencoder-based representation learning techniques for EHR data, aiming to improve downstream predictive tasks such as disease classification and risk prediction. We systematically evaluate and compare several autoencoder architectures, including standard, variational, and denoising autoencoders, across multiple real-world EHR datasets. Our comparative analysis focuses on the quality of the learned representations, assessed through clustering performance, classification accuracy, and reconstruction error. Experimental results indicate that variational autoencoders outperform other models in capturing meaningful and disentangled features, enabling more accurate predictions with reduced dimensionality. Additionally, denoising autoencoders demonstrate enhanced robustness to missing data, a common issue in EHRs. The study highlights the importance of autoencoder selection based on the specific properties of EHR data and task requirements. Our findings provide practical guidance for health informatics researchers seeking to leverage deep learning for effective EHR data representation, with implications for improved clinical decision support and personalized medicine. 

**Keywords:** Autoencoders, Electronic Health Records, Representation Learning, Variational Autoencoder, Denoising Autoencoder, Health Informatics, Deep Learning, Predictive Modeling
