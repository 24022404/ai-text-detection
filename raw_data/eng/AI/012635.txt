Data augmentation is crucial for improving the generalization ability of deep learning models, especially when training data is limited. This paper introduces GridMask, a novel data augmentation strategy designed to address overfitting by strategically masking regions of input images. Unlike random erasing techniques, GridMask applies a structured grid of masks, ensuring that the model learns to recognize objects from partial information while preventing excessive information loss. We formulate GridMask with carefully chosen parameters to balance the trade-off between removing too much informative content and retaining discriminatory features. We evaluate GridMask on several benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, across various network architectures. Experimental results demonstrate that GridMask consistently improves the classification accuracy and robustness of these models compared to baseline models and other augmentation techniques like Cutout and Random Erasing. Notably, GridMask exhibits superior performance, particularly in scenarios with limited training data. The simplicity and effectiveness of GridMask make it a valuable tool for enhancing the performance of deep learning models in image classification tasks.
Keywords: Data Augmentation, Deep Learning, Image Classification, Overfitting, GridMask, Convolutional Neural Networks.
