Point cloud processing is a critical task in 3D vision, with applications ranging from autonomous driving to robotics. However, traditional convolution operations struggle to handle the irregular and sparse nature of point cloud data. This paper introduces KPConv, a novel convolution operator designed specifically for point clouds, offering both flexibility and deformability. KPConv leverages kernel points to define convolution weights, enabling precise and adaptable feature extraction. Unlike rigid grid-based convolutions, KPConv dynamically adjusts its kernel points to better align with the underlying point cloud structure, improving representation accuracy. We evaluate KPConv on multiple benchmark datasets for tasks such as classification and segmentation, demonstrating its superior performance compared to existing methods. Experimental results show significant improvements in accuracy and robustness, particularly in complex scenes with varying point densities. KPConv's deformable nature also reduces computational overhead, making it scalable for large-scale applications. This work advances the state-of-the-art in point cloud processing, providing a versatile and efficient solution for 3D data analysis. The proposed method has broad implications for fields requiring precise 3D understanding, including augmented reality, environmental mapping, and industrial automation.  

**Keywords**: Point clouds, 3D convolution, deformable kernels, KPConv, 3D segmentation, feature extraction.
