This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of semi-supervised learning (SSL) techniques for fine-grained classification tasks, where large amounts of labeled data are scarce. Our objective is to investigate the effectiveness of SSL methods in improving classification accuracy when only a limited number of labeled samples are available. We employ a range of SSL approaches, including self-training, co-training, and graph-based methods, and evaluate their performance on several benchmark datasets. Our results show that SSL techniques can significantly improve classification accuracy, especially when the number of labeled samples is small. We also compare the performance of different SSL methods and identify the most effective approaches for fine-grained classification. Our findings have important implications for real-world applications where labeled data is limited, and demonstrate the potential of SSL to improve the accuracy and robustness of fine-grained classification systems. Key contributions of this work include a thorough evaluation of SSL techniques for fine-grained classification, and the identification of best practices for applying SSL in this context. Relevant keywords: semi-supervised learning, fine-grained classification, self-training, co-training, graph-based methods, limited labeled data.
