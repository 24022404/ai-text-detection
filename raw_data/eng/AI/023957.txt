Dynamic textures—temporal sequences exhibiting consistent visual patterns, such as flowing water, smoke, or waving grass—present unique challenges for computer vision and graphics, especially in synthesizability assessment. This paper investigates the problem of automatically learning the synthesizability of dynamic texture samples, aiming to predict how well novel dynamic texture samples can be synthesized by computational models. We propose a learning-based approach that leverages deep neural networks to extract multi-scale spatio-temporal features from video sequences and build a regression model that estimates the synthesizability score for each sample. The method is trained and evaluated on a curated dataset of dynamic textures, annotated with ground-truth synthesizability ratings from human assessments. Experiments show that our approach outperforms existing hand-crafted and traditional feature-based methods, achieving higher correlation with human perception and improved generalization to unseen textures. The results provide deeper insights into the intrinsic properties that determine dynamic texture synthesizability and facilitate the automatic selection or filtering of suitable texture samples for synthesis tasks in computer graphics, animation, and vision-based simulation. Our findings advance the understanding of dynamic texture modeling and open new possibilities for intelligent content creation. Keywords: dynamic texture, synthesizability, deep learning, spatio-temporal features, video analysis, texture synthesis.
