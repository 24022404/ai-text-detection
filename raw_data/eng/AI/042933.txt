This paper introduces a novel unsupervised learning framework for acquiring procedural knowledge from narrated instruction videos. Our approach addresses the challenge of learning sequential tasks without explicit action labels by leveraging the inherent structure present in paired video and speech data. We propose a method that uses temporal alignment between visual frames and spoken instructions to discover latent action segments. These segments are then clustered based on both visual similarity and the semantic content of the corresponding narration, forming a basis for learning action primitives. A key contribution is a dynamic time warping-based alignment algorithm, enhanced with cross-modal attention, enabling robust alignment even with noisy or imperfect narration. We evaluate our method on a dataset of cooking videos, demonstrating its ability to identify meaningful action sequences and achieve state-of-the-art performance in unsupervised action segmentation. Furthermore, we show how the learned action primitives can be used for video summarization and instruction following. This work paves the way for automated learning of procedural tasks from readily available online video resources, reducing the need for costly manual annotation.

**Keywords:** Unsupervised Learning, Instruction Videos, Action Segmentation, Cross-Modal Alignment, Procedural Knowledge, Temporal Alignment, Video Analysis.
