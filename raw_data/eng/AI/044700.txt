This paper introduces GraphGen-Redux, a novel recurrent model designed for efficient labeled graph generation. The objective is to address the limitations of existing graph generation methods, which often suffer from high computational complexity and poor scalability. To achieve this, we propose a lightweight and fast approach that leverages recurrent neural networks to generate graphs sequentially. Our method utilizes a carefully designed encoding scheme and a conditional random field to ensure the generated graphs are valid and labeled correctly. Experimental results demonstrate that GraphGen-Redux outperforms state-of-the-art models in terms of generation speed and quality, while requiring significantly fewer parameters. The key findings of this research highlight the effectiveness of recurrent models in graph generation tasks, particularly in applications where speed and efficiency are crucial. The contributions of this work include a novel graph generation architecture, a scalable encoding scheme, and a conditional random field-based labeling approach. Overall, GraphGen-Redux has the potential to facilitate a wide range of applications, including network analysis, graph-based data augmentation, and molecule generation, making it a valuable tool for researchers and practitioners in the field of graph learning and generation, with relevant keywords including graph generation, recurrent neural networks, labeled graphs, and network analysis.
