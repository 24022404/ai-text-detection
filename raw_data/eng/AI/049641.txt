This paper addresses the challenge of effectively embedding temporal networks, which are dynamic and continuously evolving structures, into a latent space that captures both structural and temporal features. Temporal networks often vary significantly over time, making it imperative to develop robust methods that can efficiently learn their dynamic representations.

Methods: We introduce a novel approach to temporal network embedding through tensor factorization. Our method leverages higher-order tensor decomposition techniques to model the temporal evolution of networks. By treating temporal edges as a third-order tensor, the proposed framework decomposes the network structure into a set of latent factors that encode both network topology and time-related changes. This technique enables the extraction of dynamic patterns while maintaining computational efficiency.

Results: Our approach is evaluated on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance in capturing temporal dynamics compared to existing network embedding methods. The tensor factorization-based model excels in tasks such as link prediction and node classification, consistently outperforming baseline models. Quantitative analysis shows a significant improvement in both accuracy and efficiency, highlighting the effectiveness of our methodology in temporal network analysis.

Conclusion: The proposed tensor factorization framework offers a transformative approach to temporal network embedding, providing a comprehensive and scalable solution for analyzing dynamic network data. The contributions of this research advance the understanding of temporal network behaviors and offer valuable insights for applications in areas such as social network analysis, bioinformatics, and communication networks. By emphasizing the novel integration of temporal information into the embedding process, this work sets a new benchmark for future research in dynamic network analysis.

Keywords: Temporal Networks, Network Embedding, Tensor Factorization, Dynamic Representation, Link Prediction, Node Classification.
