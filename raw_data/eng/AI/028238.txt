Learned generative models often suffer from biases stemming from limitations in training data or model architecture, leading to inaccurate data synthesis. This paper introduces a novel bias correction method based on likelihood-free importance weighting (LFIW) to mitigate these biases. Our approach leverages a discriminator network trained to distinguish between real and generated data to approximate the importance weights, thereby correcting the model's output distribution without requiring explicit likelihood computation. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our method on both synthetic and real-world datasets, showing significant improvements in reducing bias and enhancing the fidelity of generated samples compared to uncorrected models and other bias mitigation techniques. Key findings indicate that LFIW effectively re-calibrates the generative model, leading to more representative and unbiased outputs. This work contributes a practical and scalable approach for improving the reliability of learned generative models, particularly in scenarios where likelihood evaluation is intractable. Keywords: generative models, bias correction, importance weighting, likelihood-free inference, machine learning, data synthesis.
