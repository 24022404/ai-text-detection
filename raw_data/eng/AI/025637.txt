Object localization remains a central challenge in computer vision, particularly under weakly supervised learning settings where only image-level labels are available. This paper introduces an innovative Attention-based Dropout Layer (ADL) designed to enhance the performance of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for weakly supervised object localization tasks. The proposed ADL dynamically suppresses dominant attention regions during training, compelling the network to discover diverse and less discriminative object parts. Our method integrates seamlessly with existing CNN architectures and employs attention maps generated from intermediate layers to tailor the dropout process, thus overcoming the limitations of traditional spatial dropout techniques. Experimental results on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in localization accuracy and comprehensiveness compared to conventional methods, including Class Activation Mapping and other attention-based approaches. The findings indicate that ADL effectively reduces overfitting to salient regions and promotes holistic object representation. This approach not only advances the state of the art in weakly supervised object localization but also presents valuable implications for related tasks such as image segmentation and fine-grained recognition. Keywords: weakly supervised learning, object localization, attention mechanism, dropout, convolutional neural networks, computer vision.
