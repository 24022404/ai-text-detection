This paper introduces GMLight, a novel approach for lighting estimation in 3D scenes by approximating the geometric distribution of light sources. The objective is to accurately estimate the lighting conditions in a scene, which is crucial for various computer vision and graphics applications. Our method employs a geometric distribution approximation technique to model the complex lighting environment, allowing for efficient and accurate estimation of light sources. We utilize a deep learning framework to learn the geometric distribution of light sources from a dataset of 3D scenes, and demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach through extensive experiments. The results show that GMLight outperforms existing lighting estimation methods, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and robustness. Our approach has important implications for applications such as 3D reconstruction, image synthesis, and augmented reality, and contributes to the development of more realistic and immersive visual experiences. Key contributions include the introduction of geometric distribution approximation for lighting estimation, and the demonstration of its potential for improving the accuracy and efficiency of lighting estimation in 3D scenes. Relevant keywords: lighting estimation, geometric distribution approximation, 3D reconstruction, deep learning, computer vision.
