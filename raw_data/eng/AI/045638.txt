This paper presents a novel approach to optical thin-film design using reinforcement learning, aiming to optimize film structures for specific optical performance metrics. The objective is to develop an efficient and adaptive method for designing thin-film coatings that meet precise requirements, such as reflectance, transmittance, or absorptance. Our approach employs a reinforcement learning algorithm that iteratively improves the design by interacting with a simulation environment, allowing for the exploration of a vast design space. The method utilizes a combination of convolutional neural networks and Monte Carlo tree search to efficiently search for optimal solutions. Results show that our approach can achieve significant improvements in design efficiency and accuracy compared to traditional methods, with potential applications in fields such as optics, photonics, and materials science. Key contributions include the introduction of a novel reward function that balances design complexity and performance, and the demonstration of the method's ability to generalize to new design tasks. This work highlights the potential of reinforcement learning in optical thin-film design, enabling the creation of high-performance coatings with reduced computational effort and increased design flexibility, with relevant keywords including reinforcement learning, optical thin-film design, convolutional neural networks, and Monte Carlo tree search.
