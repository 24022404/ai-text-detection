The paper introduces a novel Periphery-Fovea Multi-Resolution Driving Model inspired by the human visual system's attention mechanisms, aimed at improving autonomous vehicle perception and decision-making capabilities. This model seeks to address challenges related to dynamically focusing on critical driving scene elements while efficiently processing peripheral information.

Methods/Approach: The proposed approach leverages a multi-resolution structure that mimics the human eye's foveal and peripheral vision, employing deep learning techniques to selectively allocate computational resources to different regions within a scene. By incorporating attention-guided neural networks, the model prioritizes salient areas, enhancing real-time decision accuracy in complex driving scenarios. 

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the Periphery-Fovea Multi-Resolution Driving Model achieves superior performance in both accuracy and efficiency compared to traditional single-resolution models. The model exhibits improved detection rates for critical objects and events, thereby enhancing decision-making processes in autonomous driving systems. Analysis indicates substantial reductions in computational load while maintaining high-resolution focus where required.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of human-like attention mechanisms into autonomous driving systems represents a significant advancement in the field, providing a framework for more adaptive and efficient environmental perception. The contributions of this work have notable implications for real-world applications, potentially enhancing the safety and reliability of autonomous vehicles. This research underscores the value of biologically inspired models in addressing complex challenges in artificial intelligence and machine learning. 

Keywords: autonomous vehicles, human attention, multi-resolution, deep learning, neural networks, perception, decision-making.
