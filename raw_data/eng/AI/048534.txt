Moving object detection is a critical task in computer vision with applications in surveillance, autonomous driving, and video analysis. This paper addresses the challenge of accurately identifying moving objects in complex scenes, particularly in the presence of dynamic backgrounds and occlusions. We propose a novel approach based on detecting contiguous outliers in the low-rank representation of video frames. By leveraging the inherent low-rank structure of background scenes and the sparse nature of moving objects, our method effectively separates foreground objects from the background. The proposed algorithm employs robust principal component analysis (RPCA) to decompose video frames into low-rank and sparse components, followed by a contiguous outlier detection mechanism to refine the detection of moving objects. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with illumination changes and camera motion. The proposed method achieves a significant reduction in false positives while maintaining high detection rates. This work contributes to advancing the field of moving object detection by introducing a computationally efficient and highly accurate framework, with potential applications in real-time video processing and intelligent surveillance systems.  

**Keywords**: moving object detection, low-rank representation, contiguous outliers, robust principal component analysis (RPCA), video analysis, computer vision.
