This paper introduces BRP-NAS, a novel Neural Architecture Search (NAS) framework that leverages Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) for efficient and accurate architecture prediction. Traditional NAS methods often suffer from high computational costs and time-consuming evaluations, limiting their scalability. To address these challenges, BRP-NAS employs a prediction-based approach, where GCNs are utilized to model the relationships between neural network architectures and their performance metrics. The proposed method encodes architectures as graphs, enabling the GCN to capture structural dependencies and predict performance without exhaustive training. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that BRP-NAS significantly reduces search time while maintaining competitive accuracy compared to state-of-the-art NAS methods. Key contributions include a scalable framework for architecture evaluation, improved efficiency in search space exploration, and the integration of GCNs for performance prediction. The results highlight the potential of BRP-NAS to advance NAS research by enabling faster and more resource-efficient discovery of optimal neural architectures. This work has broad implications for applications in automated machine learning, particularly in scenarios where computational resources are constrained.  

**Keywords**: Neural Architecture Search (NAS), Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), performance prediction, automated machine learning, scalable AI.
