Point-based neural rendering has emerged as a promising technique for novel view synthesis, offering advantages in terms of rendering speed and memory efficiency. However, existing methods often struggle with view-dependent effects and geometric inconsistencies, leading to artifacts in synthesized images. This paper introduces a novel point-based neural rendering framework incorporating per-view optimization to address these limitations. Our approach leverages a differentiable rendering pipeline combined with a neural network to learn per-point features optimized independently for each camera viewpoint. This allows the network to effectively capture view-dependent appearance variations and refine local geometry on a per-view basis, leading to improved rendering quality. We evaluate our method on a variety of synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in image quality compared to state-of-the-art point-based and mesh-based neural rendering techniques. Quantitative results show a reduction in LPIPS and PSNR metrics, indicating enhanced perceptual realism and fidelity. The proposed framework offers a practical solution for high-quality novel view synthesis, particularly in scenarios with complex lighting and geometric details. Keywords: Neural Rendering, Point Clouds, Novel View Synthesis, Per-View Optimization, Differentiable Rendering.
