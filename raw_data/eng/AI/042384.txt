Latent tree models (LTMs) are powerful tools for representing complex dependencies in high-dimensional data, with applications in bioinformatics, natural language processing, and network analysis. However, learning LTMs from data remains challenging due to scalability issues and the lack of guarantees on model accuracy. This paper addresses these challenges by proposing a novel, scalable algorithm for learning LTMs with provable guarantees. Our approach leverages a combination of spectral methods and hierarchical clustering to efficiently recover the underlying tree structure and latent variables. The proposed method is computationally efficient, requiring only polynomial time in the number of observed variables, and is robust to noise in the data. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our algorithm outperforms existing methods in terms of both accuracy and scalability. Furthermore, we provide theoretical guarantees on the recovery of the true tree structure under mild assumptions. The implications of this work extend to various domains, enabling the analysis of large-scale datasets with complex dependencies. Key contributions include a scalable learning framework, theoretical guarantees, and empirical validation, making this approach a significant advancement in the field of probabilistic graphical models.  

**Keywords**: Latent tree models, scalable learning, spectral methods, hierarchical clustering, probabilistic graphical models.
