Breast cancer screening is a critical area of healthcare where accurate and reliable diagnostic tools are essential. This paper introduces a novel evaluation metric for breast cancer screening, leveraging Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs) to provide robust confidence measures in diagnostic predictions. The proposed approach addresses the limitations of traditional evaluation metrics by incorporating uncertainty quantification, enabling more informed decision-making in clinical settings. The methodology involves training BNNs on a comprehensive dataset of mammographic images, where the network's probabilistic outputs are used to derive confidence scores for each prediction. These scores are then integrated into a new evaluation metric that assesses both accuracy and reliability. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed metric outperforms conventional methods, achieving higher sensitivity and specificity while effectively reducing false positives. The findings highlight the potential of BNNs in enhancing the interpretability and trustworthiness of AI-driven diagnostic systems. This research contributes to the field by providing a more nuanced and reliable framework for evaluating breast cancer screening tools, with implications for improving early detection and patient outcomes.  

**Keywords**: Bayesian Neural Networks, confidence measures, breast cancer screening, evaluation metric, uncertainty quantification, diagnostic accuracy.
