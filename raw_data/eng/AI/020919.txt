Link prediction in n-ary relational data is a critical challenge in knowledge graph completion and relational learning, with applications in recommendation systems, social network analysis, and semantic web technologies. This paper addresses the problem of accurately predicting missing links in n-ary relations by introducing a novel approach based on relatedness evaluation. Unlike traditional binary link prediction methods, our approach leverages the multi-dimensional nature of n-ary relations, capturing complex dependencies among entities and their attributes. We propose a hybrid model that combines graph-based embeddings with a relatedness scoring mechanism, enabling the evaluation of entity-relation interactions across multiple dimensions. The model is trained and evaluated on benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods in terms of precision, recall, and F1-score. Key findings reveal that incorporating relatedness evaluation significantly improves prediction accuracy, particularly in sparse and heterogeneous datasets. The proposed method also exhibits robustness in handling noisy and incomplete data. This research contributes to advancing n-ary relational learning by providing a scalable and interpretable framework for link prediction, with potential applications in knowledge graph enrichment and dynamic network analysis.  

**Keywords**: Link prediction, n-ary relations, relatedness evaluation, knowledge graphs, graph embeddings, relational learning.
