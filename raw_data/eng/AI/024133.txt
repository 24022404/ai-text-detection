Product embedding plays a crucial role in enhancing the performance of machine learning models for e-commerce applications, enabling personalized recommendations, product search, and customer behavior analysis. This paper delves into the theoretical underpinnings of product embedding techniques, aiming to provide a comprehensive understanding of their effectiveness and limitations. We investigate various embedding methodologies, including matrix factorization, deep learning architectures such as autoencoders and graph neural networks, and contrastive learning approaches, analyzing their mathematical properties and expressive power in capturing product relationships. Our theoretical analysis provides insights into the conditions under which specific embedding methods excel, considering factors like data sparsity, feature representation, and scalability. Furthermore, we explore the impact of different loss functions and regularization techniques on embedding quality and generalization performance. We present a theoretical framework to evaluate the robustness and stability of learned embeddings against adversarial attacks and data noise. The results of our theoretical analysis are validated through empirical experiments on real-world e-commerce datasets, demonstrating the practical implications of our findings. This research contributes to a deeper understanding of product embedding, offering guidance for selecting appropriate embedding techniques and improving the design of machine learning models for e-commerce. Keywords: Product Embedding, E-commerce, Machine Learning, Recommender Systems, Deep Learning, Theoretical Analysis.
