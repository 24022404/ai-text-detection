In the realm of deep learning, the architecture of neural networks plays a crucial role in their performance and efficiency. Recently, there has been growing interest in designing architectures that maintain high performance while reducing computational cost and energy consumption. This paper presents a novel approach to training neural networks, entitled "Go Wide, Then Narrow," which focuses on initially using wider networks to enhance training efficiency before refining the architecture to a thinner form for deployment.

Methods/Approach: The proposed methodology involves a two-stage training process. Initially, large and wide network architectures are employed to capture a broad set of features, enhancing generalization during the training phase. Once the network is sufficiently trained, a narrowing algorithm is applied to prune and compress the network into a thinner model without significantly compromising accuracy. This is achieved through intelligent pruning strategies and hyperparameter optimization to ensure that the final network remains efficient and effective.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that the "Go Wide, Then Narrow" approach significantly reduces the model size and inference time, with minimal impact on accuracy. Compared to traditional network training and pruning methods, our approach yields a reduction in computational resources and energy usage by more than 30%, while remaining competitive in benchmark tasks. The results indicate that starting with a wide network and narrowing it provides an optimal balance between training complexity and deployment efficiency.

Conclusion/Implications: The significance of this research lies in its potential to revolutionize the training of neural networks, particularly beneficial for applications demanding resource-efficient models such as mobile and IoT devices. This work highlights the viability of alternate training methodologies that prioritize both computational efficiency and high-performance outcomes, paving the way for future advancements in scalable neural network architectures.

Keywords: deep neural networks, efficient training, network pruning, architecture optimization, resource-efficient AI.
