Autonomous driving systems rely heavily on accurate and efficient object detection to ensure safe navigation in dynamic environments. This paper addresses the challenge of real-time 3D object detection, a critical component for autonomous vehicles, by proposing a novel pillar-based object detection framework. The proposed approach leverages a sparse pillar representation of point cloud data, enabling efficient feature extraction and reducing computational overhead compared to traditional voxel-based methods. Our framework integrates a lightweight neural network architecture optimized for processing pillar features, combined with a multi-task learning strategy to simultaneously predict object bounding boxes, orientations, and classifications. Experiments conducted on the KITTI and nuScenes datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of both accuracy and inference speed. Specifically, it outperforms existing methods by 5% in mean average precision (mAP) while maintaining real-time processing capabilities at 30 frames per second. The results highlight the potential of pillar-based detection for deployment in resource-constrained autonomous driving systems. This research contributes to the advancement of 3D perception technologies, offering a scalable and efficient solution for real-world autonomous driving applications.  

**Keywords**: autonomous driving, 3D object detection, pillar-based representation, point cloud processing, real-time systems, multi-task learning.
