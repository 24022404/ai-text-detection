Objective: This paper introduces a novel hybrid framework that combines deep representation learning with a weighted Locality Preserving Principal Discriminant (w-LPPD) Support Vector Machine (SVM) ensemble for robust classification. The goal is to leverage unlabeled data to learn discriminative features while preserving local geometric structure, and to boost generalization through ensemble decision-making.

Methods: We first construct a Deep Stacked Sparse Autoencoder (DSSA) that embeds hierarchical feature extraction with sparsity constraints to capture salient data representations. The output of the topmost autoencoder layer is then fed into multiple w-LPPD SVM classifiers, each trained on a different weighted projection emphasizing local manifold information. The ensemble combines individual SVM outputs via a voting mechanism weighted by margin confidence scores, yielding a final prediction.

Results: Experiments on handwritten digit (MNIST), object recognition (CIFAR-10), and various UCI benchmarks demonstrate that our hybrid model consistently outperforms single classifiers and conventional autoencoder-SVM pipelines. On MNIST, it achieves 99.1% accuracy; on CIFAR-10, 94.3%; and on UCI tasks, up to a 5% relative improvement over state-of-the-art deep learning baselines. Computational analysis shows competitive training time and scalable inference.

Conclusion: By integrating deep sparse coding with manifold-aware SVM ensembles, the proposed framework delivers significant gains in feature discriminability and classification accuracy. This approach offers a versatile blueprint for applications in image recognition, bioinformatics, and other domains requiring robust pattern analysis.  
Keywords: deep stacked sparse autoencoder, w-LPPD SVM ensemble, feature learning, manifold preservation, classification.
