This paper summarizes key themes and emerging research directions discussed at the NIPS 2016 Workshop on Representation Learning in Artificial and Biological Neural Networks (MLINI 2016). The workshop explored the intersection of representation learning techniques across artificial intelligence and neuroscience, focusing on how biological neural networks inspire and inform the development of more robust and efficient machine learning models. Discussions centered on novel algorithms for unsupervised and supervised learning, including advancements in deep learning architectures, sparse coding, and manifold learning. A key finding was the increasing importance of incorporating biological constraints and principles, such as hierarchical organization and feedback connections, into artificial neural networks to improve their generalization capabilities and robustness to adversarial examples. Furthermore, the workshop highlighted the potential of representation learning to uncover underlying principles of biological intelligence. The workshop fostered interdisciplinary collaboration and identified promising avenues for future research, including the development of biologically plausible learning rules and the application of representation learning to understand cognitive processes. Keywords: Representation Learning, Neural Networks, Deep Learning, Machine Learning, Neuroscience, Biological Plausibility, AI.
