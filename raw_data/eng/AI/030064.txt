This paper explores the potential of deep distributed random samplings (DDRS) as an alternative to traditional random forests in supervised learning tasks. The objective is to address the limitations of random forests, such as scalability and computational efficiency, while maintaining or improving predictive accuracy. We propose a novel approach that leverages distributed computing frameworks and deep learning techniques to generate diverse and robust random samplings of data subsets. The DDRS method employs a hierarchical sampling strategy, where deep neural networks are trained on distributed subsets of the data, enabling efficient feature extraction and ensemble learning.  

Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that DDRS achieves comparable or superior performance to random forests, particularly in high-dimensional and large-scale datasets. The method shows significant improvements in training time and resource utilization, making it a scalable solution for modern machine learning applications. Furthermore, DDRS exhibits enhanced generalization capabilities, reducing overfitting risks commonly associated with traditional ensemble methods.  

The findings suggest that DDRS offers a promising alternative to random forests, combining the strengths of deep learning and distributed computing. This research contributes to the field by introducing a scalable, efficient, and accurate supervised learning framework, with potential applications in areas such as big data analytics, real-time decision-making, and resource-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: supervised learning, random forests, deep learning, distributed computing, ensemble methods, scalability, predictive accuracy.
