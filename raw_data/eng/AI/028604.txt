Abstract:

Action recognition in videos is often hindered by the low spatial resolution of input frames, which can obscure crucial motion and appearance details necessary for accurate classification. This paper introduces a novel two-stream video super-resolution framework specifically designed to enhance action recognition performance. The proposed approach integrates spatial and temporal super-resolution streams: the spatial stream reconstructs fine-grained texture details within frames, while the temporal stream focuses on refining motion continuity and consistency between frames. Both streams are jointly optimized with an action recognition-guided loss to ensure that the super-resolved videos not only attain high visual quality but also better preserve action-relevant features. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in recognition accuracy compared to conventional super-resolution methods, particularly under challenging low-resolution conditions. Furthermore, our method achieves competitive computational efficiency, making it suitable for real-time applications. The findings highlight the importance of task-oriented video enhancement and suggest that specialized super-resolution models can substantially boost the performance of downstream vision tasks like action recognition. Key keywords: action recognition, video super-resolution, two-stream networks, deep learning, spatiotemporal modeling, video classification.
