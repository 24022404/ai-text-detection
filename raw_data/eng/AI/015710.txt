Video retrieval is a critical task in multimedia systems, requiring efficient methods to analyze and index temporal information. This paper addresses the challenge of temporal aggregation in video retrieval by proposing a novel graph-based approach. The objective is to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of retrieving relevant video segments by modeling temporal relationships within video data. Our method constructs a temporal graph where nodes represent video frames or segments, and edges capture temporal dependencies and contextual relationships. To achieve this, we introduce a graph neural network (GNN) architecture that learns to aggregate temporal features dynamically, enabling robust representation learning for video content. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms traditional methods, achieving significant improvements in retrieval precision and recall. The results highlight the effectiveness of leveraging graph-based temporal aggregation for capturing complex temporal patterns in videos. This research contributes a scalable and interpretable framework for video retrieval, with potential applications in video summarization, surveillance, and multimedia search systems.  

**Keywords**: Video retrieval, temporal aggregation, graph neural networks, temporal dependencies, multimedia systems.
