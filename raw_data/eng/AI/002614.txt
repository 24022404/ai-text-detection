This paper investigates the challenge of accurately estimating human poses in ancient vase paintings, which possess unique stylistic elements and artistic distortions that can impede conventional pose estimation algorithms. The purpose of the research is to improve pose estimation accuracy by leveraging the distinctive art style inherent in these historical artifacts. 

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel approach that applies perceptually-grounded style transfer learning to adapt modern human pose estimation models to the stylistic nuances of ancient vase paintings. Our method involves training a custom machine learning model that integrates style transfer techniques to bridge the gap between contemporary pose data and the artistic styles of ancient vases. The model incorporates advanced neural networks, including elements of convolutional architectures, to execute style adaptations effectively.

Results/Findings: Empirical evaluations demonstrate a significant enhancement in pose estimation accuracy on a curated dataset of ancient vase paintings when utilizing our style transfer-based approach compared to traditional methods. Quantitative metrics reveal improvements in keypoint detection precision and overall robustness across varied stylistic expressions.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field of computer vision by presenting a novel method that blends art-inspired style transfer with pose estimation techniques. The findings underscore the potential of perceptually-aware models in adapting to stylistic variability, opening new avenues for analyzing and understanding artistic representations in historical contexts. The implications extend to applications in digital humanities, cultural preservation, and the enhancement of AI-based art interpretation technologies.

Keywords: human pose estimation, ancient vase paintings, style transfer learning, perceptual grounding, computer vision, cultural heritage.
