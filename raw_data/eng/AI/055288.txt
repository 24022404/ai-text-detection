This paper introduces a novel pedestrian detection framework based on deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The objective is to improve the accuracy and robustness of pedestrian detection in challenging real-world scenarios. The proposed approach utilizes a deep CNN architecture, incorporating customized convolutional layers and pooling strategies, optimized for feature extraction and classification of pedestrians across varying scales and occlusions. We trained and evaluated our model on publicly available datasets, including the Caltech Pedestrian Dataset and the KITTI dataset. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance, surpassing existing pedestrian detection algorithms in terms of both detection accuracy and false positive rates. The key findings highlight the effectiveness of deep CNNs in learning robust and discriminative features for pedestrian detection. The proposed framework offers significant potential for applications in autonomous driving, video surveillance, and intelligent transportation systems. Keywords: Pedestrian Detection, Convolutional Neural Networks, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, Object Detection, Autonomous Driving.
