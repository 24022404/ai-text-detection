This paper proposes a novel approach to Video Story Question Answering (VSQA) by introducing a Multimodal Dual Attention Memory (MDAM) framework. The objective is to improve the accuracy of answering questions related to video stories by effectively leveraging both visual and textual information. The MDAM model employs a dual attention mechanism, focusing on both intra-modal and inter-modal interactions, to capture complex relationships between video frames, subtitles, and questions. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed MDAM outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and efficiency. The key findings highlight the importance of multimodal fusion and attention-based memory in VSQA tasks. This research contributes to the development of more sophisticated video understanding systems, with potential applications in areas such as video analysis, question answering, and human-computer interaction. Key keywords: Video Story Question Answering, Multimodal Fusion, Dual Attention, Memory-based Models, Visual Question Answering.
