Understanding the structure and relationships within groups of points in low-dimensional representations is a critical challenge in data analysis and visualization. This paper addresses the problem of explaining the underlying patterns and groupings in such representations, which are often derived from dimensionality reduction techniques like t-SNE or UMAP. We propose a novel framework that combines interpretable clustering algorithms with explainable AI (XAI) techniques to provide meaningful insights into the formation of point groups. Our approach leverages graph-based clustering to identify cohesive groups and employs feature importance analysis to explain the contributing factors behind their formation. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our method in uncovering interpretable groupings and providing actionable insights. The proposed framework outperforms existing methods in terms of both clustering accuracy and interpretability, offering a significant advancement in the analysis of low-dimensional data. This research contributes to the broader field of data visualization and interpretability, with potential applications in domains such as bioinformatics, social network analysis, and anomaly detection.  

**Keywords**: dimensionality reduction, clustering, explainable AI, t-SNE, UMAP, interpretability, data visualization.
