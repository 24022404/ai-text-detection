In this thesis, the smart contract emerges as a pivotal cornerstone within the entire ecosystem, underpinning its security, reliability, and operational integrity through a multitude of essential roles. Foremost, the smart contract assumes responsibility for securely storing and meticulously managing all configuration updates, guaranteeing that any modification to system parameters occurs in a tamper-proof and auditable manner. This resilience is critical to preventing unauthorized changes and upholding system trustworthiness. Additionally, the smart contract maintains an up-to-date whitelist of authorized Decentralized Applications (DApps), serving as a robust gatekeeper that ensures only legitimate DApps interact with the ecosystem, thus mitigating the risk of malicious or unintended interactions. Furthermore, the smart contract orchestrates asynchronous rounds of the Perdesen Distributed Key Generation (DKG) protocol, enabling nodes to collaboratively generate cryptographic keys without requiring synchronous communication. This approach enhances scalability, efficiency, and fault tolerance, making distributed key management practical in dynamic, networked environments. The contract’s governance over the key generation process is executed with unwavering transparency, allowing all stakeholders to audit operations while maintaining strict privacy and security guarantees. In addition to this oversight, the smart contract also undertakes the crucial task of verifying node signatures, authenticating the identities and integrity of participants throughout protocol operations. This layer of rigorous verification prevents impersonation, ensures accountability, and builds collective confidence in the system’s outputs. Collectively, by integrating these multifaceted functions—secure storage, configuration management, access control, protocol facilitation, process transparency, and participant authentication—the smart contract does not merely automate routine tasks but substantially augments the protocol’s operational efficiency, transparency, security, and overarching reliability.
