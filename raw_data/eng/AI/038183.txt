The objective of this research is to address the limitations of traditional object detection frameworks by introducing Sparse R-CNN, a novel approach that integrates learnable proposals into the end-to-end object detection process. Existing methods typically rely on densely populated proposals, which can lead to inefficiencies in both computational resources and detection accuracy. Our proposed method leverages sparse proposals, allowing the system to learn from a compact and optimized set of candidate regions. The Sparse R-CNN is characterized by the use of a sparse set of learnable object proposals, each of which is dynamically adjusted during training for increased detection precision. Through extensive experimentation and comparative analysis, Sparse R-CNN has demonstrated superior performance in terms of accuracy and speed, significantly outperforming contemporary object detection methods across several benchmark datasets. Our findings reveal that Sparse R-CNN maintains a high detection quality while reducing computational demands. The introduction of learnable proposals not only enhances the adaptability of the model but also simplifies the pipeline by removing the need for heuristic-driven proposal generation components. This research contributes to the field of object detection by presenting a more efficient and streamlined framework with potential applications in real-time systems and resource-constrained environments. Key innovations include learnable proposals and an end-to-end training strategy that advances object detection capabilities. Keywords: object detection, Sparse R-CNN, learnable proposals, end-to-end, efficiency, accuracy.
