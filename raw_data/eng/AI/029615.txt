This paper introduces a novel logic-based approach to generatively defined discriminative modeling, addressing the challenge of integrating generative and discriminative paradigms in machine learning. The proposed method leverages formal logic frameworks to define generative models that inherently support discriminative tasks, enabling improved interpretability and performance. By combining probabilistic reasoning with logical constraints, the approach ensures robust feature representation and decision-making capabilities. The methodology is implemented using a hybrid architecture that integrates symbolic reasoning with neural networks, allowing for efficient training and inference. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in classification accuracy and model interpretability compared to traditional discriminative models. The findings highlight the potential of logic-based generative definitions to enhance discriminative modeling, offering a pathway for developing more transparent and effective machine learning systems. This research contributes to advancing the integration of symbolic and statistical AI, with applications in areas such as natural language processing, computer vision, and decision support systems.  

**Keywords**: generative modeling, discriminative modeling, logic-based AI, hybrid architectures, interpretability, machine learning.
