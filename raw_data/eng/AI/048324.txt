Weakly Supervised Object Localization (WSOL) aims to identify object regions in images using only image-level labels, which remains a challenging task due to the lack of precise localization information. This paper introduces an Attention-based Dropout Layer (ADL), a novel approach designed to enhance the localization capabilities of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in WSOL tasks. The proposed ADL leverages attention mechanisms to selectively drop less informative feature activations during training, thereby encouraging the network to focus on more discriminative object regions. Unlike traditional dropout methods, ADL dynamically adapts to the spatial importance of features, improving the model's ability to localize objects accurately. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including CUB-200-2011 and ILSVRC, demonstrate that ADL significantly outperforms existing WSOL methods in terms of localization accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the effectiveness of ADL in capturing fine-grained object details while maintaining computational efficiency. This work contributes a practical and scalable solution for WSOL, with potential applications in medical imaging, autonomous driving, and other domains requiring precise object localization under weak supervision.  

**Keywords**: Weakly Supervised Object Localization, Attention Mechanism, Dropout Layer, Convolutional Neural Networks, Image Classification.
