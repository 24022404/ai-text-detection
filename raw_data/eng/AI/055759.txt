The Natural Wake-Sleep Algorithm (NWSA) is introduced as a novel unsupervised learning method inspired by the alternating wake and sleep phases of the brain. This work addresses limitations in traditional unsupervised learning by proposing a biologically plausible algorithm that dynamically adjusts its learning parameters based on the inherent structure of the input data. NWSA employs a two-phase learning process: a "wake" phase where the model learns to recognize and encode observed data, and a "sleep" phase where it generates synthetic data to refine its internal representations. We demonstrate that NWSA achieves superior performance in clustering and dimensionality reduction tasks compared to standard algorithms like k-means and PCA, particularly on complex, high-dimensional datasets. Experimental results on benchmark datasets reveal a significant improvement in clustering accuracy and reconstruction error. The NWSA's ability to adaptively learn data distributions offers potential applications in areas such as anomaly detection, feature learning, and generative modeling, contributing a new perspective on unsupervised learning through biologically-inspired principles.

Keywords: Unsupervised Learning, Wake-Sleep Algorithm, Neural Networks, Clustering, Dimensionality Reduction, Biologically-Inspired Algorithm.
