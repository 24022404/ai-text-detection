This paper presents a comprehensive performance analysis of keypoint detectors and binary descriptors under a wide range of photometric and geometric transformations. The objective is to evaluate the robustness and invariance of established and recent feature extraction techniques in realistic scenarios. We analyze the performance of several keypoint detectors, including both traditional methods like Harris and SIFT, and modern approaches such as ORB and AKAZE. These detectors are paired with binary descriptors like BRIEF, BRISK, and FREAK. The evaluation is conducted using a benchmark dataset subjected to controlled variations in image brightness, contrast, blur, scale, rotation, and viewpoint. Results demonstrate significant performance variations across different detector-descriptor combinations under specific transformations. We identify the strengths and weaknesses of each method, providing insights into their suitability for different computer vision applications. Our findings contribute to the selection of appropriate feature extraction techniques for robust image matching and object recognition, particularly in challenging imaging conditions. Keywords: Keypoint Detectors, Binary Descriptors, Image Matching, Photometric Transformations, Geometric Transformations, Performance Evaluation.
