This paper aims to investigate the effectiveness of perturbation-based saliency maps in explaining the decision-making processes of Atari agents. To achieve this, we employ a range of perturbation techniques, including occlusion and noise injection, to generate saliency maps for various Atari games. Our approach involves analyzing the performance of these saliency maps in identifying key features that influence agent decisions. The results show that perturbation-based saliency maps can provide insightful explanations for agent behavior, with some techniques outperforming others in certain games. Notably, our findings highlight the importance of carefully selecting perturbation parameters to ensure accurate and informative saliency maps. This research contributes to the development of more transparent and explainable AI models, particularly in the context of deep reinforcement learning. Our work has significant implications for the design of more reliable and trustworthy AI systems, and paves the way for future research in explainable AI, Atari games, and saliency maps. Key keywords: explainable AI, saliency maps, perturbation techniques, Atari games, deep reinforcement learning.
