This paper addresses the challenge of inducing hierarchical compositional models in generative networks, a critical step toward achieving interpretable and structured representations in artificial intelligence. We propose a novel approach that leverages sparsity constraints within a generator network to naturally uncover hierarchical structures in data. By systematically sparsifying the network's connections, our method encourages the emergence of compositional patterns that align with the underlying data hierarchy. The proposed framework is evaluated on synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its ability to recover meaningful hierarchical representations without explicit supervision. Experimental results show significant improvements in interpretability and model performance compared to traditional generative approaches. Furthermore, the induced hierarchical models exhibit robustness across diverse tasks, including image generation and structured data synthesis. This work contributes a scalable and unsupervised technique for discovering compositional hierarchies, offering potential applications in explainable AI, data compression, and domain-specific knowledge extraction. Keywords: hierarchical models, sparsity, generative networks, unsupervised learning, interpretability, structured representations.
