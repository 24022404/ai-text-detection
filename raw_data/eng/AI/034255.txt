Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are widely used in image recognition tasks, but their robustness to adversarial examples remains a concern. This paper investigates a novel vulnerability: the development of “blind spots” within CNNs due to specific padding configurations. We demonstrate that certain padding schemes can inadvertently create regions in feature maps that are systematically under-stimulated during training, leading to reduced sensitivity to features in corresponding image regions. Using a combination of analytical techniques and empirical evaluations on standard datasets like CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, we identify padding-induced blind spots and quantify their impact on model accuracy and robustness. Our findings reveal that carefully selected adversarial perturbations targeting these blind spots can significantly degrade CNN performance. We propose mitigation strategies, including adaptive padding and regularization techniques, to alleviate this vulnerability and improve the overall resilience of CNNs. This work highlights a previously unaddressed architectural weakness in CNNs and contributes to a deeper understanding of their failure modes. Keywords: Convolutional Neural Networks, Padding, Adversarial Attacks, Robustness, Blind Spots, Image Recognition.
