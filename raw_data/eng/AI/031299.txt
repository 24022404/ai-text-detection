Anomaly detection in images is a critical task in various applications, including medical imaging, industrial inspection, and surveillance. This study compares the effectiveness of supervised and unsupervised deep learning methods for identifying anomalies in image datasets. The objective is to evaluate the performance, scalability, and robustness of these approaches under different conditions. For supervised learning, we employ convolutional neural networks (CNNs) trained on labeled datasets, while unsupervised methods leverage autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs) to detect deviations from normal patterns without labeled data. Experiments are conducted on benchmark datasets, including MNIST and CIFAR-10, as well as a custom dataset of industrial defect images. Results indicate that supervised methods achieve higher accuracy when sufficient labeled data is available, whereas unsupervised approaches demonstrate superior adaptability in scenarios with limited or no labeled data. Additionally, unsupervised methods show promising potential for real-time applications due to their lower computational overhead. This research highlights the trade-offs between these two paradigms and provides insights into selecting the appropriate method based on data availability and application requirements. The findings contribute to advancing anomaly detection techniques, offering practical guidance for deploying deep learning models in real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: anomaly detection, supervised learning, unsupervised learning, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, autoencoders, GANs, image analysis.
