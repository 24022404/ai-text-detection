This paper addresses the challenge of training effective deep learning models for medical image analysis when limited quantities of labeled data are available. Our objective is to develop a surrogate supervision approach that can leverage large amounts of unlabeled medical images to improve model performance. We propose a novel method that combines self-supervised learning techniques with transfer learning to generate surrogate labels for unlabeled data. Our approach utilizes a transformer-based architecture to learn robust representations from unlabeled images, which are then used to fine-tune a downstream task-specific model. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on several medical image analysis benchmarks, outperforming traditional supervised learning approaches that rely on limited labeled data. The key findings of this study highlight the potential of surrogate supervision to reduce the need for extensive labeled data, enabling more efficient and accurate medical image analysis. Our research contributes to the development of more effective deep learning models for medical imaging applications, with potential implications for clinical decision support, disease diagnosis, and personalized medicine. Key keywords: medical image analysis, deep learning, surrogate supervision, self-supervised learning, transfer learning, transformer architecture.
