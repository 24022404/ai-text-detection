Salient object detection in RGB-D images is a crucial task in computer vision, enabling enhanced scene understanding by leveraging both color and depth information. This paper presents a novel collaborative learning framework for accurate RGB-D salient object detection, aiming to more effectively exploit the complementary cues provided by RGB and depth modalities. The proposed approach introduces a dual-branch network architecture, where each branch is dedicated to extracting robust features from RGB and depth inputs, respectively. Through a carefully designed collaborative learning module, the framework enables dynamic information exchange and adaptive feature fusion between the two branches, enhancing the model's ability to localize and segment salient regions even in complex scenes. Extensive experiments conducted on several public RGB-D salient object detection benchmarks demonstrate that our method outperforms existing state-of-the-art approaches in terms of both detection accuracy and robustness. Results show notable improvements in precision, recall, and F-measure metrics, particularly in challenging scenarios with similar foreground and background appearances or noisy depth data. The collaborative learning strategy not only boosts cross-modal feature representation but also exhibits strong generalization capabilities. This study highlights the effectiveness of collaborative learning for multimodal data integration and sets a new benchmark for future research in RGB-D salient object detection. Keywords: salient object detection, RGB-D images, collaborative learning, feature fusion, deep learning, computer vision.
