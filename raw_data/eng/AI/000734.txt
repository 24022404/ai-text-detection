Gaussian Processes (GPs) are widely used for modeling complex, nonlinear systems, but their application to nonstationary real-world data remains challenging due to computational inefficiencies and limited adaptability. This paper addresses the problem of efficiently learning nonstationary GPs to improve their scalability and predictive accuracy in dynamic environments. We propose a novel approach that combines adaptive kernel design with sparse approximation techniques, enabling the model to capture time-varying patterns while maintaining computational tractability. Our method leverages a hierarchical structure to dynamically adjust kernel parameters based on local data characteristics, ensuring robust performance across diverse datasets. Experimental results on real-world benchmarks demonstrate significant improvements in prediction accuracy and computational efficiency compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. Specifically, our approach reduces training time by up to 40% while achieving a 15% improvement in predictive performance on nonstationary datasets. The proposed framework has broad implications for applications requiring real-time adaptability, such as environmental monitoring, financial forecasting, and autonomous systems. This work contributes to the field by introducing a scalable and efficient solution for learning nonstationary GPs, bridging the gap between theoretical models and practical real-world impact.  

**Keywords**: Gaussian Processes, nonstationary data, adaptive kernels, sparse approximation, real-time modeling, machine learning.
