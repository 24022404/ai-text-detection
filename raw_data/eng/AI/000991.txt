This paper presents a novel application of transfer learning techniques to enhance the automated detection of melanoma from dermoscopic images, as part of our participation in the ISIC 2017 Skin Lesion Classification Challenge. The study aims to address the critical challenge of accurately classifying malignant melanoma, thereby aiding in early detection and treatment.

Methods/Approach: We adopted a transfer learning approach, utilizing pre-trained Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) as a foundational model, specifically leveraging architectures such as VGG16 and ResNet50. These models were fine-tuned on the challenge dataset to adapt to the specific features of skin lesions. Data augmentation techniques were also employed to improve model robustness by expanding the diversity of input samples.

Results/Findings: Our approach demonstrated significant improvements in accuracy and performance over baseline models. The fine-tuned CNNs achieved a higher classification accuracy and increased sensitivity in detecting melanoma compared to traditional machine learning methods. Our model ranked competitively in the ISIC 2017 challenge leaderboard, highlighting its efficacy.

Conclusion/Implications: The study illustrates the potential of transfer learning in medical image analysis, particularly in enhancing the early detection of melanoma. The application of advanced CNN architectures for skin lesion classification presents a promising tool for improving diagnostic accuracy in dermatology. Further research could explore the integration of this approach in clinical settings, potentially contributing to automated diagnostic workflows. Key keywords include transfer learning, melanoma detection, convolutional neural networks, ISIC 2017, and skin lesion classification.
