This paper addresses the challenge of 3D object localization, a crucial task in various computer vision applications. The objective is to accurately estimate the 3D pose of objects from 2D image estimates, enhancing the performance of vision-based systems. Our approach leverages a novel fusion of 2D detection and 3D reconstruction techniques, utilizing deep learning models to refine 2D estimates and project them into 3D space. The results demonstrate significant improvements in localization accuracy, outperforming existing methods by reducing errors in translation and rotation. Key findings include the effectiveness of our method in handling occlusions and varying lighting conditions. The proposed technique has important implications for applications such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and augmented reality, where precise 3D object localization is essential. By advancing the state-of-the-art in 3D object localization, this research contributes to the development of more sophisticated computer vision systems, with potential applications in fields requiring accurate 3D scene understanding, including object recognition, tracking, and scene reconstruction, using relevant technologies such as CNNs, 3D reconstruction, and pose estimation.
