The objective of this research is to analyze and characterize driving styles by utilizing primitive driving patterns identified through Bayesian nonparametric approaches. Traditional methods in driving behavior analysis often struggle with the dynamic and complex nature of real-world driving data. Our approach leverages Bayesian nonparametrics to model these intricate driving behaviors flexibly and accurately, without being constrained by pre-defined assumptions about data distribution. We employed a method that automatically discovers latent driving patterns, subsequently clustering these patterns to depict distinctive driving styles. Key findings reveal significant variations in driving behavior, which were effectively captured and categorized using our method, outperforming traditional parametric models in both accuracy and adaptability. Furthermore, our analysis demonstrated that these distinctive patterns could predict certain outcomes related to vehicle wear, efficiency, and driver's risk level. The implications of this research are substantial for the development of advanced driver assistance systems, personalized insurance models, and predictive maintenance services. Our work contributes to the field by offering a robust, novel approach to understanding driver behavior dynamics, with potential applications in enhancing road safety and improving automotive technologies. Keywords integral to this study include Bayesian nonparametrics, driving behavior analysis, primitive driving patterns, and driving style modeling.
