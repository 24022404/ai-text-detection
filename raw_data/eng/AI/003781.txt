This paper addresses the challenge of developing generalization bounds in the complex field of non-linear metric learning, specifically aiming to overcome the limitations imposed by dimensionality. Metric learning is crucial for numerous machine learning applications that require distance-based analysis, yet existing generalization bounds often do not scale well with high-dimensional data.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework that leverages dimension-free techniques to derive generalization bounds for non-linear metric learning algorithms. Our approach involves the integration of advanced statistical learning theory with new insights into non-linear transformations to achieve performance that is independent of the dimensionality of the input space.

Results/Findings: We demonstrate that our framework yields tighter generalization bounds compared to traditional methods. Through extensive experimentation on synthetic and real-world datasets, we show that our methods not only maintain robust performance across varying dimensions but also outperform existing algorithms in classification and clustering tasks, thereby proving their efficacy and broad applicability.

Conclusion/Implications: The research provides significant contributions by delivering a dimension-independent methodology for generalization bounds in non-linear metric learning. This advancement has potential applications in high-dimensional data analysis such as image and video recognition, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems, where traditional metric learning methods struggle. Our findings open new pathways for future research into scalable and efficient metric learning paradigms.

Keywords: Non-linear metric learning, generalization bounds, dimension-free, high-dimensional data, machine learning, statistical learning theory.
