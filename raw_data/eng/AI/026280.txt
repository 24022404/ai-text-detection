This study introduces DDSL, a novel Deep Differentiable Simplex Layer designed to advance the learning of geometric signals. Traditional methods in geometric deep learning often encounter difficulties in effectively processing non-Euclidean data, such as meshes and point clouds. Addressing this challenge, DDSL aims to enhance the representation and analysis of complex geometric structures.

Methods/Approach: The proposed DDSL framework is constructed upon a simplex-based architecture, which integrates discrete differential geometry concepts within a differentiable deep learning pipeline. The layer is designed to operate seamlessly within existing neural networks by incorporating simplex modeling, enabling efficient handling of various geometric signal properties. This approach draws from both the strengths of simplicial complexes and deep learning optimization techniques to foster better feature extraction and signal fidelity.

Results/Findings: Our empirical evaluations demonstrate that DDSL significantly outperforms existing geometric learning models in multiple benchmark tasks, including shape classification, segmentation, and reconstruction. The findings reveal improved accuracy and computational efficiency, validating DDSL's capability to generalize across different types of geometric data. Additionally, our comparisons with state-of-the-art frameworks highlight substantial gains in expressivity and robustness.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of the Deep Differentiable Simplex Layer represents an important advancement in the field of geometric deep learning, offering a powerful tool for researchers and practitioners. DDSL not only provides a foundation for more insightful analysis of geometric signals but also opens new pathways for applications in computer vision, computer graphics, and beyond. By bridging the gap between classical geometry and deep learning, our work establishes a precedent for future innovations in geometric modeling and machine learning.

Keywords: DDSL, geometric deep learning, simplex layer, geometric signals, differentiable geometry, shape classification, signal processing.
