This paper addresses the challenge of accurate land cover mapping across varied geographic regions, which is critical for numerous environmental monitoring and management applications. Traditional methods often struggle with domain shifts between labeled source and unlabeled target datasets. We propose a novel Bayesian-inspired, deep learning-based, semi-supervised domain adaptation technique to improve the robustness and accuracy of land cover classification in such scenarios.

Methods: Our approach leverages a deep learning framework integrated with Bayesian principles to enhance semi-supervised domain adaptation. By combining probabilistic modeling with feature extraction through deep networks, we effectively reduce domain discrepancies between source and target datasets. The model dynamically estimates uncertainty, guiding the adaptation process and leveraging both labeled and abundant unlabeled data to improve learning.

Results: The proposed method was evaluated using publicly available land cover datasets experiencing different domain shifts. Results demonstrate superior performance in classification accuracy compared to state-of-the-art techniques, with increased adaptability across distinct environments. The Bayesian-inspired mechanism significantly improved uncertainty quantification, enabling more reliable predictions under varying conditions.

Conclusion: This research contributes a significant advancement in the realm of domain adaptation for land cover mapping, offering a robust and effective tool for environmental monitoring. The integration of deep learning with Bayesian modeling not only enhances adaptability but also provides actionable insights into uncertainty management. Potential applications include precision agriculture, urban planning, and ecological conservation, where adaptable land cover mapping is crucial.

Keywords: Bayesian-inspired, deep learning, semi-supervised learning, domain adaptation, land cover mapping, environmental monitoring, uncertainty quantification.
