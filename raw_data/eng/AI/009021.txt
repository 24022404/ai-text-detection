This paper introduces Wasserstein Dictionary Learning (WDL), a novel unsupervised framework for non-linear dictionary learning that leverages optimal transport theory. Traditional dictionary learning methods often rely on linear approximations, which can limit their effectiveness in capturing complex data structures. To address this, we propose a non-linear approach that integrates the Wasserstein distance, enabling the model to learn dictionaries that better preserve the geometric and statistical properties of the data. Our method employs an optimal transport-based optimization framework to minimize the Wasserstein distance between the data distribution and the dictionary representation, ensuring robust and interpretable learning outcomes. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that WDL outperforms conventional linear dictionary learning techniques in terms of reconstruction accuracy and feature representation. Furthermore, the proposed framework exhibits superior performance in downstream tasks such as clustering and classification. The key contribution of this work lies in the integration of optimal transport with dictionary learning, offering a new perspective for unsupervised representation learning in high-dimensional and complex data spaces. This research has potential applications in areas such as image processing, natural language processing, and bioinformatics, where capturing non-linear relationships is critical.  

**Keywords**: Wasserstein distance, optimal transport, dictionary learning, unsupervised learning, non-linear representation, feature extraction.
