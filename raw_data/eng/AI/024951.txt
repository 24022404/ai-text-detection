In the realm of medical imaging, precise segmentation of volumetric images presents significant challenges, particularly in scenarios with limited annotated data. This paper aims to address the problem of few-shot segmentation through a novel approach that integrates Squeeze & Excite mechanisms within a guided framework. We propose a hybrid model that enhances feature representation and preserves important spatial information by leveraging both global context and local details. Our methodology employs a two-step process: initially adapting the Squeeze & Excite block to prioritize informative features, followed by a guided segmentation phase that utilizes a small number of annotated examples to effectively delineate structures within the volumetric data. Experimental results demonstrate that our proposed technique outperforms existing methods in terms of segmentation accuracy and generalizability across diverse datasets. Notably, our approach yields up to a 15% improvement in Intersection over Union (IoU) scores compared to baseline algorithms. The findings indicate that our model not only enhances few-shot segmentation performance but also opens avenues for its application in resource-constrained medical environments where the availability of labeled data is limited. This work contributes to advancing the field of automated segmentation in medical imaging, promoting the adoption of deep learning techniques in real-world applications. 

Keywords: Few-shot segmentation, volumetric images, Squeeze & Excite, feature representation, medical imaging.
