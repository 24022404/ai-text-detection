Video Object Segmentation (VOS) is a critical task in computer vision, with applications ranging from autonomous driving to video editing. However, existing methods often struggle with computational inefficiency and limited scalability when processing high-resolution, long-duration videos. This paper introduces SSTVOS, a novel framework leveraging Sparse Spatiotemporal Transformers to address these challenges. The proposed approach combines sparse attention mechanisms with spatiotemporal modeling to efficiently capture long-range dependencies in video sequences while reducing computational overhead. SSTVOS employs a hierarchical transformer architecture that dynamically focuses on relevant regions and frames, enabling robust object segmentation across diverse video scenarios. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that SSTVOS achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency. Notably, the framework reduces inference time by up to 40% compared to dense transformer-based models, without compromising segmentation quality. The findings highlight the potential of sparse spatiotemporal modeling for scalable and efficient video analysis. SSTVOS opens new avenues for real-time VOS applications, particularly in resource-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: Video Object Segmentation, Sparse Transformers, Spatiotemporal Modeling, Computational Efficiency, Hierarchical Attention.
