This paper addresses the challenge of improving the efficiency and scalability of model-based deep reinforcement learning (MBRL) in complex environments. Traditional MBRL methods often struggle with high computational costs and sample inefficiency, particularly when dealing with large state-action spaces. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel approach called Variational State Tabulation (VST), which integrates variational inference techniques with state abstraction to reduce the complexity of environment modeling. VST dynamically clusters states into a compact representation, enabling faster learning and more efficient policy optimization. Our method leverages a probabilistic framework to approximate the environment dynamics, ensuring robustness while minimizing computational overhead. Experimental results on benchmark environments demonstrate that VST significantly outperforms existing MBRL methods in terms of sample efficiency and computational speed, achieving up to a 30% reduction in training time without compromising performance. Furthermore, the proposed approach exhibits superior generalization capabilities in high-dimensional and partially observable settings. The findings highlight the potential of VST to advance the practical applicability of MBRL in real-world scenarios, such as robotics and autonomous systems, where efficiency and scalability are critical. This work contributes a scalable and efficient framework for MBRL, paving the way for future research in adaptive state abstraction and probabilistic modeling.  

**Keywords**: model-based reinforcement learning, variational inference, state abstraction, sample efficiency, probabilistic modeling, scalability.
