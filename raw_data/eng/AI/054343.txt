High-dimensional data poses significant challenges for traditional machine learning model interpretability, limiting their adoption in critical applications where human understanding and transparency are vital. This paper introduces a novel framework for enhancing human-interpretable model explainability on high-dimensional datasets, such as those found in genomics, image analysis, and finance. The proposed approach combines dimensionality reduction techniques with feature attribution methods, enabling clear visualization and intuitive explanation of model behavior. Specifically, we integrate principal component analysis (PCA) and t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) with model-agnostic explainers, including SHAP and LIME, to identify and present the most influential features driving model predictions. Extensive experiments conducted on publicly available benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method improves explanatory clarity without compromising predictive performance. Comparative analyses reveal substantial gains in human interpretability, as measured by empirical user studies and quantitative interpretability metrics, over conventional black-box models. The framework supports interactive visual tools, allowing users to explore, validate, and trust model outputs. Our results highlight the importance of fostering transparency for high-stakes decision-making and provide a scalable pathway for interpretable machine learning on complex, high-dimensional data. Key contributions include the integration of multi-modal dimensionality reduction, robust feature attribution, and end-user focused visualization. Keywords: model interpretability, explainable AI, high-dimensional data, dimensionality reduction, SHAP, LIME, visualization, transparency, machine learning.
