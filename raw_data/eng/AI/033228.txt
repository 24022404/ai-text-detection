This paper proposes a novel framework for 3D object detection that eliminates the need for manually labeled training data by leveraging an open-source simulation environment. The main objective is to address the high cost and scalability issues associated with collecting and annotating large-scale 3D datasets, which are critical for training accurate deep learning models in computer vision and autonomous systems. Our approach utilizes simulated 3D scenes to generate diverse, high-fidelity sensor data (such as LiDAR and RGB images) alongside automatic ground-truth annotations, enabling robust model training without manual intervention. We develop and integrate a pipeline for synthetic data generation and domain adaptation, ensuring that models trained in the simulator achieve competitive performance on real-world benchmarks. Experimental results demonstrate that our method significantly reduces the reliance on manual labels while maintaining, and in some cases improving, detection accuracy compared to traditional supervised methods. This work advances the field by providing a scalable, open-source solution for 3D detection tasks and fosters broader accessibility for research and development. Key contributions include an efficient simulation-to-reality workflow, extensive evaluation on standard datasets, and comprehensive benchmarking against supervision-based baselines. Keywords: 3D detection, manual-label free, simulation, open-source, synthetic data, domain adaptation, autonomous systems, computer vision.
