This paper addresses the challenge of robust target recognition in Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) imagery, particularly when only partial aspect angles are available, resulting in significant pose discrepancies and feature entanglement. We propose a novel Pose Discrepancy Spatial Transformer (PDST) based feature disentangling framework designed to effectively separate pose-related variations from target-intrinsic characteristics. The proposed method leverages a spatial transformer network to model and compensate for pose discrepancies, enabling the extraction of disentangled features that are more invariant to viewpoint changes. Extensive experiments were conducted using benchmark SAR target recognition datasets with limited aspect angle coverage. The results demonstrate that our approach significantly improves recognition accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly in scenarios with incomplete angular information. The system also reveals improved generalization to unseen aspect ranges, highlighting its practical value in real-world SAR applications. The contributions of this work lie in the integration of pose-adaptive spatial transformation and disentangled feature representation, which together advance the reliability of SAR target recognition under challenging viewpoint constraints. Keywords: SAR target recognition, spatial transformer, feature disentangling, pose discrepancy, partial aspect angles, deep learning.
