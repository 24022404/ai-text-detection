The objective of this research is to address the challenging problem of video object segmentation utilizing a novel one-shot weakly supervised approach. Traditional video object segmentation methods often require extensive annotated data, which is time-consuming and labor-intensive. Our approach leverages the availability of minimal ground-truth data by incorporating weak supervision into a one-shot learning framework, significantly reducing the reliance on extensive labeled datasets. The method involves the development of a new algorithm that effectively combines similarity matching techniques with a weak supervision strategy to identify and segment objects in video sequences. Key findings demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive segmentation accuracy while drastically minimizing annotation requirements. In evaluations across several benchmark datasets, our method consistently outperforms existing models by achieving higher precision and recall metrics, particularly in scenarios with limited supervision. The implications of this research are profound, offering a scalable and efficient solution for domains where annotated video data is scarce, such as surveillance, autonomous driving, and robotics. This paper contributes to the field by presenting a novel, more efficient method for video object segmentation, opening pathways for further research into more generalized and flexible segmentation models. Key keywords include video object segmentation, weak supervision, one-shot learning, and similarity matching.
