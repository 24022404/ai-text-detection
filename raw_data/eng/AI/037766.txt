This paper introduces a novel approach to LiDAR-based 3D segmentation by proposing Cylindrical and Asymmetrical 3D Convolution Networks (CA-3DCN). The objective is to address the challenges of processing sparse and irregular LiDAR point clouds, which are inherently complex due to their non-uniform density and large-scale spatial distribution. Traditional 3D convolutional networks often struggle with these characteristics, leading to suboptimal segmentation performance. To overcome these limitations, we present a dual-path architecture that combines cylindrical convolutions to better align with LiDAR's radial data structure and asymmetrical convolutions to efficiently capture spatial features across varying resolutions. The proposed method is evaluated on benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in segmentation accuracy compared to state-of-the-art approaches. Results show that CA-3DCN achieves a 5.2% increase in mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) on the SemanticKITTI dataset and maintains robust performance under varying point cloud densities. The key contribution of this work lies in its innovative network design, which effectively leverages the unique properties of LiDAR data, offering a scalable and efficient solution for real-world applications such as autonomous driving and robotics. Keywords: LiDAR segmentation, 3D convolution, cylindrical convolution, asymmetrical convolution, point cloud processing, autonomous systems.
