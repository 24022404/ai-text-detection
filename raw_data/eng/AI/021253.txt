**Abstract**

Understanding human emotions in naturalistic, unrestricted environments presents significant challenges for affective computing, particularly due to variability across subjects and recording conditions. This paper introduces a novel framework for multi-modal affect analysis that leverages standardized data processing within individual subjects in the wild. Our objective is to enhance the reliability and accuracy of emotion recognition systems using data captured from diverse audio, visual, and physiological sensors outside controlled laboratory settings. The proposed approach incorporates subject-specific normalization techniques and alignment strategies to reduce inter-individual differences, facilitating more robust feature extraction and emotion classification. We employ advanced machine learning algorithms to integrate multi-modal data streams, evaluating performance on a benchmark dataset recorded in real-world scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate that our standardized within-subject approach significantly outperforms traditional cross-subject methods, yielding improved recognition rates for both categorical and dimensional affective states. This research highlights the importance of individualized data standardization in multi-modal affect analysis, offering new insights for the development of emotionally intelligent systems applicable to pervasive computing, human-computer interaction, and mental health monitoring. Key keywords include affect analysis, multi-modal fusion, standardized data, emotion recognition, machine learning, and real-world scenarios.
