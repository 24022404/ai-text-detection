Ensemble methods, particularly those utilizing decision stumps and trees, are widely used in machine learning due to their interpretability and strong performance. This paper investigates the robustness of such ensembles against adversarial attacks, specifically focusing on perturbations bounded by the \ell p-norm. We analyze the certified robustness of ensemble stumps and trees under \ell p-norm attacks, deriving theoretical bounds on their performance. Our approach involves developing novel techniques for bounding the sensitivity of these models to input changes. Empirically, we evaluate the derived robustness guarantees on standard datasets, comparing the performance of different ensemble configurations and \ell p-norm constraints. The results demonstrate a trade-off between model accuracy and certified robustness, with the specific relationship varying according to the ensemble structure and the choice of p. These findings provide valuable insights into designing more robust and reliable machine learning systems.

Keywords: Robustness, Ensemble methods, Decision trees, Certified defense, Adversarial attacks, \ell p-norm, Machine learning.
