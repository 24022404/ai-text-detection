Object detection systems are vulnerable to adversarial attacks, posing a significant security risk. Existing attacks often lack transferability, limiting their effectiveness against unseen models. This paper introduces a novel framework for generating transferable adversarial attacks targeting both image and video object detection. Our approach leverages a combination of frequency-based perturbation and an ensemble of diverse detector architectures during attack generation to enhance transferability. We demonstrate that frequency-based perturbations improve the robustness of the attack across different datasets and model architectures. Furthermore, we introduce a temporal consistency loss for video object detection to ensure that the generated adversarial perturbations are smooth and visually plausible across video frames, leading to more effective attacks. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including COCO and ImageNet VID, show that our method achieves significantly higher transferability rates compared to state-of-the-art adversarial attack techniques, with minimal performance degradation on the source model. The proposed approach offers a robust defense evaluation tool and highlights the need for more resilient object detection models. Keywords: Adversarial Attacks, Object Detection, Transferability, Image Security, Video Security, Deep Learning.
