Target-driven inference for deep video object tracking (TDIOT) addresses the challenge of accurately tracking objects in dynamic video sequences, a critical task in computer vision applications such as surveillance, autonomous driving, and human-computer interaction. This paper introduces a novel deep learning framework that leverages target-driven inference to enhance tracking robustness and precision. The proposed approach integrates a multi-scale feature extraction mechanism with a dynamic attention module, enabling the model to adaptively focus on target-specific regions while mitigating distractions from background clutter and occlusions. We employ a hybrid training strategy combining supervised learning with self-supervised fine-tuning to improve generalization across diverse tracking scenarios. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including OTB-100 and LaSOT, demonstrate that TDIOT achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of tracking accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight the framework's ability to handle complex scenarios such as fast motion, scale variations, and long-term occlusions. This research contributes a scalable and efficient solution for real-time video object tracking, with potential applications in robotics, augmented reality, and intelligent video analysis.  

**Keywords**: video object tracking, deep learning, target-driven inference, attention mechanisms, multi-scale feature extraction, real-time tracking.
