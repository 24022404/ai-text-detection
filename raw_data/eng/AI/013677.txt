Satellite image super-resolution (SR) is a critical task in remote sensing, enabling enhanced image quality for applications such as environmental monitoring, urban planning, and disaster management. This paper addresses the challenge of improving the resolution of satellite imagery using deep learning techniques. We propose a novel deep neural network architecture specifically designed for satellite image SR, leveraging advanced convolutional layers and attention mechanisms to capture fine-grained spatial details. Our approach incorporates a multi-scale feature extraction framework, enabling the network to effectively handle varying resolutions and complex textures inherent in satellite data. To evaluate the performance, we conduct extensive experiments on publicly available satellite image datasets, comparing our method against state-of-the-art SR models. Results demonstrate that our approach achieves superior performance in terms of both quantitative metrics (e.g., PSNR and SSIM) and visual quality, with significant improvements in preserving structural details and reducing artifacts. The proposed model also exhibits robustness to noise and varying illumination conditions, making it suitable for real-world satellite imaging scenarios. This research contributes to the advancement of deep learning-based SR techniques, offering a practical solution for high-quality satellite image reconstruction with potential applications in geospatial analysis and decision-making systems.  

**Keywords**: satellite image super-resolution, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, attention mechanisms, remote sensing, image reconstruction.
