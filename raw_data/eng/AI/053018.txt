Boosting algorithms are widely used for classification tasks, but they often produce poorly calibrated probabilities, hindering their use in applications requiring reliable uncertainty estimates. This paper investigates methods for improving the calibration of probabilities generated by boosting algorithms, focusing on techniques applicable without retraining the base learners. We explore and compare several post-processing calibration methods, including Platt scaling, isotonic regression, and beta calibration, adapting them specifically for the characteristics of boosting outputs. Our experiments, conducted on a diverse set of benchmark datasets, demonstrate that beta calibration consistently provides the most accurate probability estimates across different boosting algorithms, such as AdaBoost and Gradient Boosting Machines (GBM). Specifically, beta calibration significantly reduces calibration error as measured by expected calibration error (ECE) and Brier score. Furthermore, we analyze the conditions under which different calibration methods perform best, providing practical guidelines for selecting the most appropriate calibration technique for a given boosting model. This research contributes to the practical utility of boosting algorithms by enabling the generation of well-calibrated probabilities, leading to more reliable decision-making in various real-world applications.

Keywords: Boosting, Calibration, Probability Estimation, Machine Learning, AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting Machines, Beta Calibration.
