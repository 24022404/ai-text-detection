**Abstract**

Accurate and robust segmentation of lung tissues in computed tomography (CT) scans is critical for early detection and diagnosis of pulmonary diseases. However, the scarcity of diverse and well-annotated datasets, especially those containing various types and sizes of lung nodules, often limits the effectiveness of lung segmentation algorithms. This paper proposes a novel approach to augment CT imaging data by generating highly realistic lung nodule simulations using 3D conditional generative adversarial networks (3D-cGANs). Our method conditions the generative process on anatomical lung context, enabling the synthesis of heterogeneous nodules integrated seamlessly into authentic lung structures. We conducted extensive experiments to evaluate the realism of the simulated nodules and their impact on downstream segmentation models. The integration of the augmented dataset led to statistically significant improvements in segmentation accuracy, especially for nodules of smaller size and irregular shapes, compared to models trained on original or conventionally augmented data. This study demonstrates the effectiveness of 3D-cGAN-based simulation for augmenting medical imaging datasets and enhances the robustness and generalizability of automated lung segmentation. Our approach offers a scalable solution for improving computer-aided diagnosis tools, with applications in clinical screening and disease monitoring. **Keywords:** lung nodule simulation, 3D generative adversarial networks, CT imaging, lung segmentation, data augmentation, medical imaging.
