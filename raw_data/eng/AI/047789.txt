This paper presents a comparative analysis of actor-critic methods and direct policy search techniques in the context of reinforcement learning, with a focus on sample complexity. The objective is to investigate the efficiency of these approaches in terms of the number of samples required to achieve optimal performance. We employ a range of algorithms, including deep deterministic policy gradients and trust region policy optimization, and evaluate their performance on several benchmark tasks. Our results show that actor-critic methods generally exhibit lower sample complexity than direct policy search methods, particularly in high-dimensional state and action spaces. However, we also identify scenarios where direct policy search can be more efficient, such as in cases where the policy landscape is relatively simple. The conclusions drawn from this study have important implications for the design of reinforcement learning systems, highlighting the need for careful consideration of the trade-offs between sample complexity, computational cost, and policy optimization. Key contributions include a comprehensive comparison of actor-critic and direct policy search methods, as well as insights into the factors influencing sample complexity in reinforcement learning. Relevant keywords: reinforcement learning, actor-critic methods, direct policy search, sample complexity, policy optimization.
