This paper introduces a novel framework for combining the strengths of graphical models and neural networks to achieve structured representations and efficient inference. We propose a method for composing graphical models, specifically Markov Random Fields (MRFs), using neural networks to parameterize their potential functions. This approach allows for learning complex dependencies from data while maintaining the interpretability and efficient inference capabilities of graphical models. Our method uses a custom neural network architecture designed to enforce specific structural constraints dictated by the underlying MRF, leading to improved model generalization and faster convergence. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our framework on image segmentation and object recognition tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance with significantly reduced inference time compared to traditional neural network approaches. The results show a superior trade-off between accuracy and computational cost. This hybrid approach offers a promising direction for developing intelligent systems that require both structured knowledge representation and efficient reasoning.

**Keywords:** Graphical Models, Neural Networks, Markov Random Fields, Structured Representations, Inference, Image Segmentation, Object Recognition, Deep Learning.
