This paper revisits the Adam optimization algorithm, a widely-used method for training deep learning models, by introducing a novel perspective based on weighted past gradients. The objective is to improve the convergence and stability of Adam by incorporating a more informed and adaptive approach to gradient weighting. Our method modifies the traditional Adam update rule by assigning weights to past gradients, allowing the algorithm to better capture the underlying dynamics of the optimization landscape. Experimental results demonstrate that our weighted past gradients approach outperforms the standard Adam algorithm in various deep learning tasks, including image classification and natural language processing. The key findings indicate that our method achieves faster convergence, improved stability, and enhanced generalization performance. This research contributes to the ongoing efforts to improve optimization algorithms for deep learning and has potential applications in areas such as computer vision, speech recognition, and recommender systems. Key keywords: Adam optimization, weighted past gradients, deep learning, convergence, stability.
