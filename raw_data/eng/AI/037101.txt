This paper addresses the challenge of generating geometrically consistent 3D meshes from imperfect or incomplete input data, a critical task in applications such as computer graphics, robotics, and augmented reality. We propose a novel deep learning-based framework that leverages geometric consistency constraints to refine and correct 3D mesh representations. Our approach integrates a graph neural network (GNN) architecture with differentiable mesh operations, enabling the model to learn and enforce geometric priors directly from the data. The proposed method is trained on a diverse dataset of 3D shapes, incorporating both synthetic and real-world scans, to ensure robustness across various scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate that our framework significantly improves mesh quality, achieving state-of-the-art performance in terms of geometric accuracy and consistency compared to existing methods. Furthermore, the model exhibits strong generalization capabilities, effectively handling unseen data with varying levels of noise and incompleteness. The implications of this work extend to applications requiring high-fidelity 3D reconstructions, such as virtual prototyping, medical imaging, and autonomous navigation. By bridging the gap between data-driven learning and geometric constraints, this research contributes a scalable and efficient solution for 3D mesh correction.  

**Keywords**: 3D mesh correction, geometric consistency, graph neural networks, deep learning, 3D reconstruction, differentiable mesh operations.
