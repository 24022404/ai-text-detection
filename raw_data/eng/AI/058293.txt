This paper addresses the challenge of achieving high-quality image super-resolution (SR) with minimal computational overhead, a critical requirement for real-time applications on resource-constrained devices. We propose a novel Adaptive Weighted Learning Network (AWLN), a lightweight deep learning framework designed to enhance image resolution while maintaining efficiency. The AWLN leverages an adaptive weighting mechanism that dynamically adjusts the contribution of feature maps during the reconstruction process, enabling the model to focus on the most relevant details for SR. Unlike traditional SR methods, which often rely on computationally expensive architectures, our approach achieves a balance between performance and efficiency by optimizing network parameters and reducing redundancy. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that AWLN outperforms existing lightweight SR models in terms of both peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM), while significantly reducing inference time. The proposed method also exhibits robust generalization across diverse image domains, making it suitable for practical applications such as medical imaging, surveillance, and mobile photography. This work contributes to the field by introducing an innovative, resource-efficient solution for image SR, paving the way for deployment in real-world scenarios where computational resources are limited.  

**Keywords**: Image super-resolution, lightweight networks, adaptive weighting, deep learning, computational efficiency, real-time applications.
