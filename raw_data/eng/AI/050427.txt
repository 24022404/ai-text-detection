Gaussian Process (GP) models are powerful non-parametric methods widely used in machine learning for regression and classification tasks. Kernel selection significantly impacts GP performance; however, manually designing effective kernels is challenging. This paper introduces a novel approach to automatically evolve GP kernels from elementary mathematical expressions using genetic programming. The method explores the kernel space by combining functions such as addition, multiplication, exponentiation, and trigonometric operations to generate complex kernel structures. The fitness of each evolved kernel is evaluated based on its performance on a given dataset using a GP regression framework. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the evolved kernels often outperform hand-crafted kernels and kernels obtained through other automated kernel learning techniques. The evolved kernels provide interpretable functional forms, offering insights into the underlying data relationships. This automated kernel discovery approach offers a valuable tool for improving the accuracy and applicability of Gaussian Process models.

**Keywords:** Gaussian Process, Kernel Learning, Genetic Programming, Kernel Evolution, Regression, Machine Learning.
