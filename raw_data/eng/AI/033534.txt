Objective: Adversarial patch attacks expose vulnerabilities in computer vision systems, but their conspicuous appearance and sensitivity to real-world distributional shifts limit practical evaluation of robustness. This paper introduces a novel transparent patch framework that simultaneously (1) maintains high attack strength under diverse distributional distortions and (2) minimizes visual obtrusiveness to human observers.

Methods: We formulate transparent patch generation as a multi-objective optimization problem combining a classification-driven adversarial loss with a perceptual transparency loss derived from structural similarity metrics. To capture real-world variability, we train patches on a suite of simulated distributional shifts (e.g., noise, blur, weather effects) and augmentations. The optimization employs projected gradient descent with adaptive transparency weighting, yielding patches that remain effective under both clean and corrupted inputs.

Results: On ImageNet and two common corruption benchmarks, our transparent patches achieve an average attack success rate of 82.5% under shift conditions—outperforming state-of-the-art opaque patches (68.3%) by 14.2%. Structural similarity (SSIM) scores indicate a 35% reduction in perceptual distortion compared to prior methods. A user study with 50 participants confirms that our patches are rated significantly less obtrusive (mean rating 2.1/5) than standard opaque attacks (3.8/5).

Conclusion: Transparent patch attacks reveal persistent blind spots in modern image classifiers under distributional shift while preserving stealthiness. Our contributions include (i) a distribution-shift-aware transparent patch generation algorithm, (ii) a human-perception-guided transparency metric, and (iii) an open-source evaluation suite. This work underscores the need for defenses that address both adversarial robustness and practical stealth constraints.  

Keywords: Transparent adversarial patch, distributional shift, obtrusiveness, image classification, adversarial robustness.
