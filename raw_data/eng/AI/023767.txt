Histopathology image analysis is crucial for cancer diagnosis, but appearance variations due to different staining protocols and imaging devices pose significant challenges. This paper introduces a novel domain-adversarial neural network (DANN) approach to mitigate these variations. Our DANN model integrates a domain discriminator with a feature extractor, enabling the learning of domain-invariant features from histopathology images acquired from multiple sources. This process aims to minimize the discrepancy between feature distributions of different domains, enhancing the generalization capability of downstream diagnostic tasks. We evaluated our method on a multi-center dataset of breast cancer histopathology images, demonstrating significant improvements in classification accuracy and robustness compared to models trained without domain adaptation. The results show that our DANN effectively reduces the impact of staining and imaging variations, paving the way for more reliable and generalizable automated histopathology image analysis. Keywords: Histopathology, domain adaptation, domain-adversarial neural network, deep learning, cancer diagnosis, image analysis.
