Forecasting human-object interaction (HOI) in first-person video is crucial for anticipating user intentions and enabling proactive assistance in applications like robotics and augmented reality. This paper introduces a novel approach for jointly predicting motor attention, indicating which objects a user will interact with, and the subsequent actions they will perform. We propose a deep learning model that leverages both visual cues from the video frames and egocentric motion data to infer the user’s focus of attention and predict future actions. The model incorporates a spatio-temporal attention mechanism to identify relevant objects and a recurrent neural network to model the temporal dependencies between attention and actions. Experimental results on a benchmark dataset demonstrate that our joint prediction framework significantly outperforms existing methods in both motor attention prediction accuracy and action forecasting, particularly in scenarios with complex interactions. This work provides a foundation for improved anticipation of human behavior in interactive environments. Keywords: Human-Object Interaction, Action Forecasting, Motor Attention, First-Person Video, Deep Learning, Egocentric Vision.
