**Abstract**

Instance-based generalization presents a promising direction for enhancing the adaptability and performance of reinforcement learning (RL) agents across diverse and complex environments. This paper investigates the integration of instance-based methods into RL frameworks, with the objective of enabling agents to generalize knowledge from specific past experiences to novel situations. We propose an instance-based RL approach that combines episodic memory with approximate nearest-neighbor retrieval, allowing the agent to select and adapt actions based on stored instances similar to the current state. Our method is evaluated on a suite of benchmark tasks, demonstrating improvements in sample efficiency, policy robustness, and adaptability compared to conventional model-free RL algorithms. The results indicate that instance-guided action selection enables the agent to leverage past experiences more effectively, reducing the need for extensive exploration and leading to faster convergence. Furthermore, the study analyzes the scalability of the approach in large and continuous state spaces and discusses the trade-offs between memory usage and generalization capability. The findings contribute new insights into the design of RL agents capable of efficient learning and flexible behavior. Potential applications of this research include robotics, autonomous systems, and decision support, where rapid adaptation to dynamic environments is critical. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, instance-based learning, generalization, episodic memory, sample efficiency, nearest-neighbor methods.
