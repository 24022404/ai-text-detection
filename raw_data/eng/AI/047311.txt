**Abstract:**  
Subgraph recognition is a fundamental problem in graph-based machine learning with wide-ranging applications, including social network analysis, bioinformatics, and anomaly detection. However, existing approaches often struggle with distinguishing relevant subgraph structures from noisy or redundant information. This paper introduces a novel framework, termed Graph Information Bottleneck (GIB), which leverages the information bottleneck principle to enhance subgraph recognition performance. By formulating the subgraph identification task as an optimization problem, GIB maximizes the mutual information between the selected subgraph representations and the target labels, while minimizing the mutual information with irrelevant graph components. Our method integrates message-passing neural networks with a bottleneck regularization scheme, enabling the extraction of compact and informative subgraph embeddings. Experimental results on benchmark graph datasets demonstrate that GIB significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both accuracy and robustness, particularly when faced with large, noisy graphs. The proposed approach provides a principled way to filter out extraneous information, offering improved interpretability and transferability to diverse subgraph recognition tasks. Keywords: graph neural networks, information bottleneck, subgraph recognition, graph representation learning, deep learning.
