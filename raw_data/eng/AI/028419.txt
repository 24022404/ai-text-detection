This study addresses the challenge of personalized glycemic control in septic patients, a critical task for improving patient outcomes in intensive care units. Traditional methods often lack the adaptability required for individual variability in patients with sepsis. We aim to develop a more tailored approach to glycemic management through advanced machine learning techniques.

Methods: We propose a novel framework combining representation learning and reinforcement learning. The approach utilizes deep representation learning to capture complex patient characteristics from electronic health records (EHRs) and employs reinforcement learning to devise personalized insulin dosing strategies. This model continually adapts to the unique physiological responses of each patient, optimizing glycemic control through real-time feedback loops.

Results: Our system demonstrated significant improvements in maintaining optimal blood glucose levels compared to standard protocols. In a series of retrospective evaluations on a large dataset of septic patients, the proposed method reduced hyperglycemic and hypoglycemic episodes by 30% and 25%, respectively. These results highlight the system's ability to adaptively manage glucose levels, improving overall patient safety and reducing the risk of complications.

Conclusion: The integration of representation and reinforcement learning offers a promising direction for personalized healthcare interventions. Our research not only advances the state-of-the-art in glycemic control for septic patients but also sets the stage for broader applications in personalized medicine. The developed framework holds potential for enhancing automated decision-making systems, ultimately paving the way for more effective and individualized clinical treatments.

Keywords: glycemic control, sepsis, representation learning, reinforcement learning, personalized medicine, electronic health records (EHRs).
