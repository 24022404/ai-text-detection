This paper addresses the challenge of accurately estimating causal effects in complex datasets by leveraging transfer learning with neural networks. Traditional approaches often struggle with variability across datasets and the need for extensive labeled data, which are required for robust causal inference.

Methods: We propose a novel framework that integrates transfer learning techniques with neural network models to enhance the estimation of causal effects. Our approach involves pre-training a neural network on a broad dataset with known causal structures, then fine-tuning it on target datasets with limited labels. This technique helps capture underlying causal relationships while adapting to dataset-specific characteristics.

Results: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our method significantly improves the accuracy of causal effect estimation compared to standard techniques. We conducted tests across various datasets, showcasing the robustness of our approach in different scenarios. The results highlight the model’s superior ability to generalize causal knowledge from source to target datasets.

Conclusion: This research contributes a powerful method to the field of causal inference, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. By applying transfer learning in neural networks, we provide a solution that enhances both understanding and prediction of causality in diverse datasets. This framework can be applied to areas such as healthcare, economics, and social sciences, where accurate causal estimation is crucial. 

Keywords: transfer learning, causal inference, neural networks, causal effect estimation, fine-tuning, machine learning.
