Self-supervised learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning meaningful representations without extensive labeled data. However, existing methods often focus solely on global feature extraction, neglecting the importance of spatial localization in representation quality. This paper introduces CAST (Context-Aware Spatial Transformer), a novel framework that enhances self-supervised learning by explicitly incorporating spatial localization into the representation learning process. CAST leverages a multi-task learning approach, combining global feature extraction with a localization task that encourages the model to identify and focus on semantically meaningful regions within an image. We evaluate CAST on benchmark datasets for image classification and object detection, demonstrating significant improvements over state-of-the-art self-supervised methods. Our results show that CAST achieves a 5.2% improvement in linear evaluation accuracy on ImageNet and a 3.8% boost in mean average precision (mAP) on COCO object detection. The framework’s ability to learn spatially aware representations not only enhances downstream task performance but also provides interpretable insights into the model’s decision-making process. This work highlights the importance of spatial context in self-supervised learning and opens new avenues for developing more robust and interpretable representation learning models.  

**Keywords**: self-supervised learning, spatial localization, representation learning, multi-task learning, image classification, object detection.
