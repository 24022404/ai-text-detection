We address the challenge of computing realistic barycenters of natural images to enable high-quality image morphing. While classical Wasserstein barycenters provide a mathematically grounded interpolation of pixel distributions, they often produce artifacts, blurred textures, and semantic inconsistencies when applied directly to high-dimensional image data. To overcome these limitations, we propose a constrained optimal transport framework that incorporates color histogram alignment, spatial smoothness, and patch-level coherence into the barycenter computation. Our method leverages entropic regularization for computational efficiency and employs an alternating optimization scheme that projects intermediate solutions onto constraint sets, thereby preserving local structure and global feature distributions. Experiments on standard image benchmarks demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods, yielding lower transport cost and perceptual distance metrics alongside visually smooth, artifact-free morphing sequences. Ablation studies confirm the individual and combined benefits of each constraint. The resulting framework offers a versatile tool for applications such as animation, data augmentation, and style transfer. Our implementation is publicly released to promote further advances in constrained optimal transport for image processing.  
Keywords: constrained Wasserstein barycenter, optimal transport, image morphing, entropic regularization, natural image interpolation.
