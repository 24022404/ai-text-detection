Objective/Purpose: Structured prediction tasks—from semantic segmentation to sequence labeling—require models that capture complex dependencies among output variables. Existing deep learning approaches typically rely on pairwise or low-order interactions, limiting their ability to model rich output structures. This paper introduces a novel deep learning architecture designed to learn high-order interactions for structured outputs in an end-to-end fashion.

Methods/Approach: We propose a High-Order Interaction Network (HOIN) that integrates a dedicated interaction layer based on tensor factorization into a standard deep encoder. The interaction layer uses low-rank tensor decomposition to efficiently represent and learn third- and higher-order relationships among output units. During training, the entire network—including feature extraction, interaction modeling, and structured inference—is optimized jointly via backpropagation. An auxiliary consistency loss encourages coherent global output configurations.

Results/Findings: We evaluate HOIN on three benchmark tasks: semantic segmentation, dependency parsing, and human pose estimation. Compared to state-of-the-art pairwise-structured models, HOIN achieves relative improvements of 4.5%–7.8% in overall accuracy and F1 score. Ablation studies confirm that each component—tensor interaction layer, consistency loss, and joint training—contributes to performance gains. Moreover, HOIN maintains computational efficiency, with only a 12% increase in inference time over pairwise baselines.

Conclusion/Implications: By explicitly modeling high-order dependencies, HOIN delivers more accurate and globally consistent structured outputs. This approach opens new avenues for complex structured prediction problems in computer vision, natural language processing, and bioinformatics. It demonstrates that end-to-end high-order interaction modeling can be both effective and practical.

Keywords: deep learning, structured output, high-order interaction, structured prediction, tensor factorization, neural networks.
