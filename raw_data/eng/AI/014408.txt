Session-based recommendation systems aim to predict user preferences and provide personalized suggestions based on short-term interactions. Traditional approaches often struggle with dynamic user behavior and sparse data, limiting their effectiveness. This paper introduces a novel framework, Batch-Constrained Distributional Reinforcement Learning (BC-DRL), designed to address these challenges by leveraging distributional reinforcement learning within a batch-constrained setting. The proposed method models the uncertainty in user-item interactions and optimizes recommendations by learning a distribution over potential rewards, ensuring robust performance even with limited data.  

The BC-DRL framework employs a distributional Q-network to estimate the full distribution of future rewards, enabling more accurate predictions of user preferences. Additionally, batch constraints are integrated to stabilize training and prevent overfitting, ensuring the model generalizes well to unseen data. Experiments conducted on real-world datasets demonstrate that BC-DRL outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of recommendation accuracy and user engagement metrics. Key findings reveal significant improvements in precision, recall, and mean reciprocal rank (MRR), highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed approach.  

This research contributes to the field of session-based recommendation by introducing a novel integration of distributional reinforcement learning with batch constraints, offering a scalable and efficient solution for dynamic recommendation scenarios. The framework's ability to handle uncertainty and sparse data makes it particularly suitable for real-world applications, such as e-commerce and content streaming platforms. Keywords: session-based recommendation, reinforcement learning, distributional RL, batch constraints, personalized recommendation, uncertainty modeling.
