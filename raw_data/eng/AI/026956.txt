This paper presents a novel approach to launching Trojan attacks on neural networks by leveraging controlled detoxification in the deep feature space. The objective is to investigate the vulnerability of neural networks to backdoor attacks, where an attacker can manipulate the model's behavior by injecting a trigger into the input data. Our approach utilizes a unique detoxification mechanism to subtly alter the feature space, allowing the attacker to maintain control over the model's output while evading detection. We employ a combination of adversarial training and optimization techniques to craft the Trojan trigger, ensuring its effectiveness and stealthiness. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our method, showcasing significant improvements in attack success rates and reduced detectability compared to existing backdoor attack methods. The findings of this research highlight the importance of robustness and security in neural network design, particularly in applications where trustworthiness is paramount. Key contributions include the introduction of controlled detoxification as a means to enhance Trojan attack potency and the development of a novel framework for evaluating neural network vulnerability to backdoor attacks. Relevant keywords: neural networks, Trojan attacks, backdoor attacks, deep feature space, controlled detoxification, adversarial training, robustness, security.
