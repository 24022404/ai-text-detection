This paper presents an innovative deep vision system designed for accurate animal classification and detection in trail-camera images. The objective is to develop a reliable and explainable model that can automatically adapt to changing environmental conditions through post-deployment retraining. Our approach utilizes a custom convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, leveraging techniques such as transfer learning and attention mechanisms to enhance model performance. The system is trained on a large dataset of trail-camera images, annotated with various animal species, and achieves high accuracy in classification and detection tasks. Key findings indicate that our model outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, with significant improvements in recall and precision. The automatic retraining mechanism enables the system to maintain its performance over time, even in the presence of concept drift. This research contributes to the development of more efficient and effective wildlife monitoring systems, with potential applications in conservation biology, ecology, and wildlife management. The use of explainable AI techniques provides insights into the decision-making process of the model, increasing trust and reliability in the results. Relevant keywords: deep learning, computer vision, animal classification, object detection, explainable AI, trail-camera images, wildlife monitoring.
