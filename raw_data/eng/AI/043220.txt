This study aims to investigate the effectiveness of various evaluation metrics for generative adversarial networks (GANs), a crucial aspect of assessing their performance in generating realistic synthetic data. We employ a comprehensive approach, utilizing multiple evaluation metrics, including inception score, frechet inception distance, and mode score, to assess the quality and diversity of generated samples. Our experiments involve training and evaluating several state-of-the-art GAN architectures on benchmark datasets, such as CIFAR-10 and CelebA. The results show that no single metric can comprehensively capture the performance of GANs, and a combination of metrics is necessary for a more accurate assessment. Our findings have significant implications for the development and evaluation of GANs, highlighting the need for a multi-faceted evaluation framework. This research contributes to the field of deep learning, particularly in the area of generative models, and provides valuable insights for practitioners and researchers working with GANs, with key applications in image and video generation, data augmentation, and style transfer. Key keywords: generative adversarial networks, evaluation metrics, deep learning, synthetic data generation.
