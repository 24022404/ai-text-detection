Face recognition has become a critical area of research in computer vision, with applications ranging from security to personalized user experiences. This paper introduces *Face Transformer*, a novel architecture designed to address the challenges of facial recognition in diverse and complex scenarios. The proposed model leverages the transformer architecture, originally developed for natural language processing, to capture long-range dependencies and intricate patterns in facial features. Unlike traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs), the Face Transformer employs self-attention mechanisms to dynamically focus on salient regions of the face, enabling robust performance across varying poses, lighting conditions, and occlusions.  

The approach involves pre-training the model on a large-scale facial dataset and fine-tuning it for specific recognition tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that the Face Transformer outperforms state-of-the-art methods on benchmark datasets, achieving higher accuracy and lower error rates. Notably, the model exhibits superior generalization capabilities, particularly in scenarios with limited training data.  

The key contribution of this work lies in the adaptation of transformer-based architectures for facial recognition, offering a new direction for research in this domain. The Face Transformer not only enhances recognition accuracy but also provides interpretability through attention maps, which highlight the regions of the face most influential to the model's decisions. This research has significant implications for real-world applications, including surveillance, biometric authentication, and human-computer interaction.  

**Keywords**: Face recognition, transformer, self-attention, computer vision, deep learning, biometrics.
