The increasing adoption of deep learning for tabular data analysis has sparked debates about its effectiveness compared to traditional machine learning methods. This study investigates whether deep learning models consistently outperform classical approaches, such as gradient-boosted decision trees, across diverse tabular datasets. We evaluate the performance of state-of-the-art deep learning architectures, including transformer-based models, against traditional methods on a wide range of datasets, focusing on accuracy, computational efficiency, and interpretability. Our findings reveal that while deep learning models excel in specific scenarios, such as datasets with complex relationships or high dimensionality, traditional methods often achieve comparable or superior performance with significantly lower computational costs. Furthermore, we identify key factors influencing the effectiveness of deep learning, such as dataset size, feature types, and preprocessing requirements. The results challenge the prevailing assumption that deep learning is universally superior for tabular data and emphasize the importance of selecting the right tool for the task. This research provides actionable insights for practitioners, highlighting scenarios where traditional methods remain indispensable and offering guidance on when to leverage deep learning for optimal results.  

**Keywords**: tabular data, deep learning, gradient-boosted decision trees, machine learning, model comparison, computational efficiency.
