This paper introduces LT-GAN, a novel self-supervised Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) designed for enhanced image generation and manipulation detection. The primary objective is to improve GAN training stability and image quality by incorporating a self-supervised task focused on detecting latent space transformations. LT-GAN employs a transformation discriminator, which learns to distinguish between original latent codes and those altered by specific transformations. This approach encourages the generator to produce images that are both realistic and consistent with latent space semantics. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that LT-GAN achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of image quality metrics such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception Score (IS), while also exhibiting improved performance in detecting subtle image manipulations. The self-supervised learning framework significantly enhances the robustness of the GAN against adversarial attacks. Our findings suggest that latent transformation detection is a promising direction for advancing GAN research and improving the security of image-based applications. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, Self-Supervised Learning, Latent Space, Image Manipulation Detection, GAN Training.
