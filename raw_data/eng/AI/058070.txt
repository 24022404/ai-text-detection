This paper investigates the generalization error of generalized linear models (GLMs) in high-dimensional settings where the number of features is comparable to, or larger than, the number of samples. We develop a novel theoretical framework, leveraging tools from random matrix theory and statistical physics, to precisely characterize the asymptotic behavior of the prediction error for GLMs with various activation functions. Our analysis provides explicit formulas for the generalization error as a function of the data distribution, regularization parameters, and the specific form of the GLM. We demonstrate that, in the high-dimensional regime, the generalization performance is highly sensitive to the interplay between the regularization strength and the data's spectral properties. Specifically, we identify critical regularization levels that minimize the prediction error. Furthermore, we compare our theoretical predictions with empirical results on synthetic and real-world datasets, showing excellent agreement. Our findings offer valuable insights into the design and optimization of GLMs in modern machine learning applications.

Keywords: Generalized Linear Models, High-Dimensional Statistics, Generalization Error, Random Matrix Theory, Regularization, Prediction Error.
