The paper addresses the challenges in object detection by exploring the potential advantages of transformer-based architectures, which have revolutionized natural language processing, for use in computer vision tasks. The study aims to harness the capabilities of transformers to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of object detection systems.

Methods: We propose a novel transformer-based object detection model, which integrates multi-head self-attention mechanisms and layered architectures to process visual data. The model is trained on large-scale datasets, utilizing advanced optimization techniques to refine feature extraction and classification processes. Comparative analysis is conducted against traditional convolutional neural network (CNN) approaches to evaluate performance gains.

Results: The proposed transformer-based model demonstrates significant improvements in detection accuracy and processing speed across various benchmark datasets. Key findings include enhanced localization precision and reduced inference latency, yielding superior results compared to existing state-of-the-art CNN methods. This model also showcases robustness in detecting objects under diverse conditions and scales.

Conclusion: This research contributes to the growing field of computer vision by introducing an effective transformer-based approach to object detection. The findings suggest substantial potential for transformers to become central components in future object detection systems, offering improvements in accuracy and computational efficiency. These advancements have broad implications for applications in autonomous driving, surveillance, and real-time video analysis, underscoring the significance of transformer architectures in advancing computer vision technologies.

Keywords: Transformer, Object Detection, Computer Vision, Self-Attention, Deep Learning, Neural Networks, Real-Time Processing.
