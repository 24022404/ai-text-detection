Abstract:

Ensuring fairness in clustering algorithms is a critical challenge as automated decision systems become increasingly influential in various domains such as social services, healthcare, and finance. This paper introduces Variational Fair Clustering, a novel approach designed to mitigate bias in unsupervised learning by incorporating fairness constraints directly into the clustering process. Our method leverages variational inference within a probabilistic framework to model data distributions while enforcing statistical parity across protected groups. We propose a differentiable fairness regularization technique that balances cluster quality with fairness objectives, enabling the discovery of meaningful and unbiased groupings in high-dimensional data. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, effectively reducing disparate impact without significant loss in clustering accuracy. Comparisons with existing fair clustering algorithms reveal competitive trade-offs between cluster purity and fairness metrics. The proposed model is adaptable, scalable, and interpretable, making it suitable for real-world applications where ethical considerations are paramount. Our contributions advance the field of fair machine learning by providing a principled solution to variational clustering with built-in fairness guarantees. 

Keywords: fair clustering, variational inference, bias mitigation, unsupervised learning, fairness constraints, probabilistic modeling, machine learning ethics.
