Autotuning is essential for optimizing the performance and efficiency of exascale applications, which often operate on diverse hardware and workloads. This paper investigates the application of multitask and transfer learning techniques to advance autotuning methodologies for exascale computing environments. We propose a novel framework that leverages multitask learning to jointly learn from multiple autotuning tasks, enabling the sharing of relevant knowledge across different applications and system configurations. Additionally, transfer learning mechanisms are integrated to facilitate the adaptation of previously learned autotuning strategies to new exascale scenarios, significantly reducing the exploration overhead and computational cost. Experimental evaluations are conducted on a set of representative exascale workloads running on heterogeneous supercomputing platforms. The results demonstrate that our approach achieves faster convergence, improved performance gains, and greater robustness compared to traditional autotuning methods that treat each task in isolation. The research highlights the potential of combining multitask and transfer learning to deliver scalable and adaptive autotuning solutions, paving the way for more efficient use of exascale resources in future scientific and industrial applications. Key keywords: autotuning, exascale applications, multitask learning, transfer learning, performance optimization, scalable computing.
