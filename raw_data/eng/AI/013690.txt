**Abstract**

Metric learning aims to construct embedding spaces in which semantically similar samples are close together while dissimilar samples are far apart. Traditional approaches often struggle with high-dimensional data, leading to challenges such as overfitting and suboptimal convergence. This paper introduces a novel "divide and conquer" framework for metric learning, which systematically partitions the embedding space into subspaces, each tailored for capturing distinct semantic relationships. Our method employs an adaptive space decomposition algorithm that assigns data subsets to specialized embedding modules, which are subsequently integrated through a multi-level fusion mechanism. Experiments conducted on benchmark image and text retrieval datasets demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms existing metric learning techniques in terms of both accuracy and scalability. Notably, the divide and conquer strategy enables more effective handling of class imbalance and intra-class variations, resulting in superior generalization. The proposed framework offers a robust solution for large-scale similarity search, face recognition, and recommendation systems. Key contributions include the design of an efficient partitioning algorithm, the introduction of a fusion strategy that preserves global semantic structure, and a comprehensive evaluation highlighting improvements over state-of-the-art baselines. These findings suggest that dividing and conquering the embedding space is a promising direction for advancing metric learning in complex, real-world applications.

**Keywords:** metric learning, embedding space, divide and conquer, adaptive decomposition, similarity search, deep learning, large-scale retrieval
