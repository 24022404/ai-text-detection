Human pose estimation has become a critical task in computer vision for applications ranging from augmented reality to human-computer interaction. This paper addresses the challenges of improving accuracy and efficiency in pose estimation by introducing a novel knowledge-guided deep fractal neural network architecture. 

Methods/Approach: The proposed method leverages fractal neural networks, characterized by their recursive and self-similar structures, to enhance feature extraction capabilities. The model is further augmented with knowledge-guided mechanisms that incorporate prior domain knowledge, refining network predictions and improving accuracy. Advanced training techniques, including data augmentation and multi-scale analysis, are employed to optimize network performance.

Results/Findings: Experiments demonstrate that the knowledge-guided deep fractal neural network outperforms existing state-of-the-art models in various benchmark datasets for human pose estimation. Key findings indicate significant improvements in pose accuracy and processing speed. The network's innovative architecture significantly reduces computational overhead without compromising prediction quality, illustrating its potential for real-time applications.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a novel approach to human pose estimation by integrating fractal neural networks and domain knowledge, offering a unique balance of efficiency and accuracy. The methodology provides significant advancements in computer vision, with potential applications extending to robotics, surveillance, and health monitoring systems. Future work could explore the integration of additional contextual information and the application of this approach to other complex pattern recognition tasks.

Keywords: Human Pose Estimation, Deep Fractal Neural Networks, Knowledge-Guided Mechanisms, Computer Vision, Feature Extraction, Real-Time Applications.
