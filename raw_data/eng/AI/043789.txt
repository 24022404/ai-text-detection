Medical image segmentation is crucial for computer-aided diagnosis, but its reliance on extensive labeled data poses a significant challenge, especially when dealing with rare diseases. This paper introduces a novel semi-supervised few-shot learning approach to address this challenge. Our method leverages a meta-learning framework coupled with a consistency regularization technique to effectively utilize both limited labeled data and abundant unlabeled data. Specifically, we train the model to rapidly adapt to new segmentation tasks with only a few labeled examples, while the consistency regularization ensures consistent predictions on unlabeled data under different perturbations. We evaluate our approach on two publicly available medical image datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in segmentation accuracy compared to existing few-shot and semi-supervised methods. The results show that our approach achieves superior performance with limited labeled data, enabling more accurate and efficient medical image segmentation. This work offers a promising direction for reducing the annotation burden in medical imaging and improving the applicability of AI in healthcare.

**Keywords:** Semi-supervised learning, few-shot learning, medical image segmentation, meta-learning, consistency regularization, deep learning.
