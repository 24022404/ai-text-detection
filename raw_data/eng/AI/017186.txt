This paper introduces Hamiltonian Generative Networks, a novel approach to generative modeling that leverages the mathematical framework of Hamiltonian mechanics to learn complex data distributions. The objective is to develop a more efficient and effective generative model that can capture long-term dependencies and structural relationships in data. Our method employs a hybrid approach, combining the benefits of normalizing flows and energy-based models, to learn a probabilistic representation of the data. The key innovation lies in the use of Hamiltonian dynamics to model the generative process, allowing for more flexible and interpretable representations. Experimental results demonstrate the superiority of our approach over existing state-of-the-art methods, particularly in terms of sample quality and diversity. The proposed framework has significant implications for applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and robotics, and contributes to the development of more advanced generative models. Key contributions include the introduction of Hamiltonian dynamics to generative modeling, improved sampling efficiency, and enhanced model interpretability. Relevant keywords: generative models, Hamiltonian mechanics, normalizing flows, energy-based models, deep learning.
