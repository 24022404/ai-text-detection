The proliferation of image manipulation tools has made colorized images increasingly prevalent, raising concerns about their authenticity and potential misuse. This paper addresses the challenge of detecting colorized images with high accuracy and robust generalization across diverse datasets. We propose a novel approach leveraging Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) tailored specifically for this task. Our method incorporates a multi-scale feature extraction mechanism to capture subtle artifacts introduced during the colorization process, combined with a domain adaptation strategy to enhance generalization across varying image sources. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our model achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in both accuracy and cross-dataset robustness. Key findings reveal that the proposed approach effectively identifies colorized images with a precision of over 95%, even when tested on unseen datasets. The implications of this research extend to applications in digital forensics, media verification, and content authentication, where reliable detection of manipulated images is critical. This work contributes to advancing the field by providing a scalable and generalizable solution for detecting colorized images, paving the way for future research in image integrity verification.  

**Keywords**: Colorized image detection, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), digital forensics, image manipulation, domain adaptation, generalization.
