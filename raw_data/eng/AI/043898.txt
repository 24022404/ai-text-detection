This paper explores the concept of boosting self-supervised learning through knowledge transfer, aiming to enhance the performance of deep neural networks in the absence of labeled data. Our approach leverages pre-trained models as teachers to guide the training of student models, facilitating the transfer of valuable knowledge and features. By employing a novel transfer mechanism, we enable the student models to learn from the teacher's representations, thereby improving their ability to generalize and adapt to new tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing self-supervised learning techniques, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets. The key contributions of this research include the development of a knowledge transfer framework that boosts self-supervised learning, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in improving model robustness and accuracy. This work has significant implications for the development of AI models, particularly in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or unavailable, and highlights the potential of knowledge transfer as a means of enhancing self-supervised learning capabilities. Key keywords: self-supervised learning, knowledge transfer, deep neural networks, representation learning, AI models.
