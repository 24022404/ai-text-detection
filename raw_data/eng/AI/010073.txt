This paper explores the intersection of machine learning (ML) and control theory, presenting a novel framework for designing adaptive control systems. Traditional control methods often struggle with complex, nonlinear dynamics and uncertainties. We propose a data-driven approach that leverages ML algorithms, specifically reinforcement learning and neural networks, to enhance control performance. Our method involves training a neural network to approximate the optimal control policy based on system data, enabling the system to adapt to changing environments and unknown parameters. The algorithm is then integrated with classic control theory paradigms. Results demonstrate significant improvements in tracking accuracy, robustness to disturbances, and overall system stability compared to conventional control techniques. Furthermore, we analyze the theoretical convergence properties of the proposed approach and provide empirical validation through simulations on several benchmark control problems. The findings suggest that the integration of ML and control theory offers a promising direction for developing intelligent and autonomous systems. Keywords: Machine Learning, Control Theory, Reinforcement Learning, Adaptive Control, Neural Networks, System Identification.
