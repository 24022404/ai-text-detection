Video inpainting, the task of filling in missing or corrupted regions in video sequences, has seen significant advances with deep learning techniques. However, current evaluation metrics often fail to capture the nuanced visual quality and temporal coherence crucial for realistic video completion. This paper introduces DEVIL (Diagnostic Evaluation benchmark for Video Inpainting), a novel benchmark designed to provide a more comprehensive and diagnostic evaluation of video inpainting methods. DEVIL comprises a diverse set of video sequences with varying motion complexities, object types, and masking patterns. It incorporates both quantitative metrics, including frame-level and temporal consistency scores, and a qualitative human evaluation study to assess perceptual realism. Experiments conducted on state-of-the-art video inpainting models reveal limitations in their ability to handle complex scenes and maintain temporal coherence, despite achieving high scores on traditional metrics. DEVIL provides a challenging and informative platform for future research, facilitating the development of more robust and visually plausible video inpainting algorithms. Keywords: video inpainting, benchmark, evaluation, diagnostic, temporal coherence, deep learning.
