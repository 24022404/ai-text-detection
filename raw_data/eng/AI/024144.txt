This paper addresses the challenge of modeling long-range dependencies in sequential data, a critical issue for various applications such as natural language processing, time-series analysis, and bioinformatics. Conventional transformer architectures, while highly effective, struggle with lengthy sequences due to substantial memory and computation requirements that scale quadratically with sequence length. To overcome these limitations, we propose Compressive Transformers, an innovative architecture that introduces a memory compression mechanism allowing the efficient storage and retrieval of compressed past activations. Our approach selectively compresses historical representations, maintaining essential information while reducing memory size and computational overhead. Empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that Compressive Transformers achieve competitive or superior performance compared to existing transformer-based models, particularly on tasks requiring the retention of long-term context. Additionally, ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of the compression strategy and its impact on improving both efficiency and capability for long-range sequence modeling. Overall, our work provides a scalable and effective solution for long-range dependency learning, expanding the practical applicability of transformers to more complex and extended sequential tasks. Keywords: compressive transformer, long-range dependencies, sequence modeling, memory compression, deep learning, attention mechanisms.
