Temporal action localization in untrimmed videos remains a challenging task due to complex background content and subtle action boundaries. This paper investigates the development of stronger feature representations to enhance the accuracy and robustness of temporal action localization systems. We propose a novel framework that integrates multi-scale temporal feature extraction with attention-enhanced feature fusion, enabling the model to capture both short-term and long-term temporal dependencies effectively. Our approach employs advanced sequence modeling techniques to refine temporal boundaries and discriminative feature learning mechanisms to suppress irrelevant background noise. Extensive experiments conducted on standard benchmarks demonstrate that our method outperforms existing state-of-the-art approaches in terms of localization accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight significant improvements in both mean Average Precision (mAP) and detection precision across various action categories. Our findings emphasize the importance of strong feature engineering in temporal action analysis and provide practical insights for future research and real-world applications in video understanding, surveillance, and content retrieval. Keywords: temporal action localization, feature representation, video analysis, attention mechanism, sequence modeling, action detection.
