This paper proposes a novel approach to learning discriminative prototypes using Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) for time-series classification tasks. The objective is to develop a robust and efficient method that can handle variable-length sequences and capture complex patterns. Our approach employs a DTW-based distance metric to compare time-series sequences and learn prototypes that maximize class separability. The method utilizes a dynamic programming technique to efficiently compute DTW distances and update prototype representations. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets. The proposed method outperforms existing techniques in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, making it a promising solution for real-world applications. Key contributions include the introduction of a DTW-based prototype learning framework, which enables the discovery of informative and discriminative patterns in time-series data. This work has significant implications for time-series analysis, machine learning, and related fields, with potential applications in areas such as speech recognition, gesture recognition, and anomaly detection. Relevant keywords: Dynamic Time Warping, time-series classification, prototype learning, machine learning, pattern recognition.
