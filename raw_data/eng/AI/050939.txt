**Abstract**

Transfer learning has significantly advanced natural language processing (NLP) by enabling models to leverage knowledge from diverse tasks and domains. However, state-of-the-art approaches often require extensive model parameters and large-scale labeled datasets, limiting their practicality in resource-constrained scenarios. This paper introduces a conditionally adaptive multi-task learning (CA-MTL) framework designed to improve transfer learning efficiency in NLP while minimizing parameter usage and data requirements. The proposed method integrates a conditional adaptation mechanism that dynamically modulates shared representations based on task-specific signals, allowing for more effective information sharing across multiple tasks. We evaluate CA-MTL on a suite of benchmark NLP tasks, including text classification, sequence labeling, and question answering. Experimental results demonstrate that CA-MTL achieves competitive or superior performance compared to conventional multi-task and single-task transfer learning baselines, while utilizing significantly fewer parameters and less labeled data. Our analysis reveals improved generalization, faster convergence, and robust performance in low-resource settings. The proposed approach offers a scalable and resource-efficient solution for NLP transfer learning, with potential applications in domains where computational resources or annotated data are limited. Key keywords: multi-task learning, transfer learning, NLP, parameter efficiency, data efficiency, conditional adaptation, deep learning.
