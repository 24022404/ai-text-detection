This paper introduces XGAN, a novel unsupervised image-to-image translation framework designed to handle many-to-many mappings. The objective is to develop a model that can learn complex, multi-modal relationships between two domains without requiring paired training data. To achieve this, XGAN employs a combination of generative adversarial networks (GANs) and a specially designed loss function that encourages diversity in the generated images. The approach allows for flexible and realistic translations between domains, capturing the inherent variability of the data. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of XGAN in various image-to-image translation tasks, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of visual quality and diversity. The key contributions of this work include the ability to model complex many-to-many relationships and the generation of highly realistic images. XGAN has potential applications in image editing, data augmentation, and style transfer, among others. Key keywords: unsupervised image-to-image translation, many-to-many mappings, generative adversarial networks, GANs, deep learning, computer vision.
