This paper introduces ResNeSt, an advanced neural network architecture aimed at enhancing image classification performance through a novel approach known as split-attention. The research addresses the limitations of conventional attention mechanisms by proposing a more efficient method to distribute and control the focus across different feature map segments.

Methods/Approach: ResNeSt builds upon existing residual networks by integrating the split-attention block, which performs channel-wise feature map aggregation dynamically. This technique allows for simultaneous attention assignments across multiple network pathways, improving both network capacity and computational efficiency. The architecture utilizes grouped convolution operations for parallel information processing, which enhances the flexibility and expressive power of the model.

Results/Findings: The proposed ResNeSt framework demonstrates significant improvements in classification accuracy and computational efficiency compared to traditional ResNet variants and other state-of-the-art models. Extensive experimentation on standard benchmark datasets, such as ImageNet, shows that ResNeSt achieves superior performance, reducing error rates and optimizing resource usage. It consistently outperforms existing models, especially in scenarios that demand high-level feature discrimination and generalization.

Conclusion/Implications: ResNeSt presents a significant step forward in image recognition tasks, offering a robust and scalable solution for various applications in computer vision. The split-attention mechanism introduced can be readily incorporated into other deep learning models, providing a foundation for future research and development in attention-based network architectures. The implications of this study extend to enhanced performance in real-time image processing and potential applications across diverse sectors such as autonomous vehicles, surveillance systems, and medical image analysis.

Keywords: ResNeSt, split-attention, neural networks, image classification, feature map, residual networks, channel-wise aggregation, benchmark datasets.
