Action-object detection in first-person (egocentric) videos is crucial for understanding human activities and enabling context-aware applications. This paper introduces EgoNet, a novel deep learning architecture designed to improve the accuracy and efficiency of action-object detection from egocentric perspectives. EgoNet leverages a two-stream network, processing both appearance and motion cues extracted from video frames. A key innovation is the integration of an attention mechanism that dynamically focuses on relevant objects and actions, mitigating challenges posed by cluttered scenes and viewpoint variations inherent in first-person data. We evaluate EgoNet on benchmark egocentric datasets, demonstrating significant performance gains compared to state-of-the-art methods in terms of mean Average Precision (mAP) and F1-score. Furthermore, EgoNet exhibits improved computational efficiency, making it suitable for real-time applications. Our findings highlight the effectiveness of the proposed architecture for robust action-object detection in egocentric videos, paving the way for enhanced human-computer interaction and activity recognition systems.

Keywords: Egocentric vision, Action-Object Detection, Deep Learning, Attention Mechanism, Video Analysis.
