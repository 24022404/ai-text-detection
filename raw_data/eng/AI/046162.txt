This research addresses the challenge of memory inefficiency and locality issues within Transformer models when applied to time series forecasting, which are pivotal in various domains such as finance, weather prediction, and resource management.

Methods/Approach: We propose an innovative architecture that modifies the traditional Transformer framework to enhance its locality capabilities and alleviate the memory bottleneck encountered during long-sequence processing. Our approach involves a novel combination of segment-wise attention mechanisms and memory-efficient data structures, enabling the model to handle extensive datasets more effectively.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our enhanced Transformer model significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency across several benchmark time series datasets. The proposed model not only reduces memory usage but also improves prediction precision, offering a balance between performance and resource consumption.

Conclusion/Implications: The study provides a substantial advancement in time series forecasting by improving the scalability and localization of Transformer models. This development opens up new possibilities for real-time and large-scale applications across various industries, where traditional models might falter due to their computational limitations. Our work paves the way for further exploration into optimized architectures for efficient temporal data analysis.

Keywords: Transformer, memory bottleneck, time series forecasting, locality, segment-wise attention, computational efficiency.
