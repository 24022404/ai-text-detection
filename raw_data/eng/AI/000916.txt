Rendering realistic atmospheric clouds presents a significant challenge in computer graphics due to the complex light scattering phenomena involved. This paper introduces Deep Scattering, a novel approach that leverages radiance-predicting neural networks to efficiently render clouds with high fidelity. Our method directly learns to predict the outgoing radiance at each point within the cloud volume, bypassing traditional ray marching techniques and costly scattering calculations. We employ a deep neural network architecture trained on synthetic cloud data generated from a physically based rendering engine. This allows the network to capture intricate scattering patterns and dependencies on factors such as cloud density, lighting conditions, and viewing angles. Results demonstrate that Deep Scattering achieves comparable visual quality to state-of-the-art path tracing methods, while offering significant performance improvements, enabling real-time or interactive cloud rendering. The approach effectively handles complex cloud shapes and varying atmospheric conditions. Key contributions include a novel neural network architecture tailored for radiance prediction in participating media, a comprehensive training pipeline for generating realistic cloud data, and a thorough evaluation demonstrating the efficiency and accuracy of the method. Keywords: Cloud Rendering, Neural Rendering, Deep Learning, Radiance Prediction, Atmospheric Scattering.
