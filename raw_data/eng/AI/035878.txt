This paper introduces DyStaB, a novel unsupervised object segmentation approach that leverages dynamic-static bootstrapping to accurately identify and separate objects from backgrounds in images. The objective is to address the challenge of segmenting objects without prior knowledge of their classes or appearances. DyStaB employs a unique methodology that combines dynamic and static cues to bootstrap the segmentation process, utilizing a dual-branch architecture to capture both spatial and temporal information. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of DyStaB, outperforming state-of-the-art unsupervised segmentation methods on benchmark datasets. The key findings highlight the importance of integrating dynamic and static features for robust object segmentation. The proposed approach has significant implications for various applications, including autonomous driving, robotics, and surveillance, where accurate object segmentation is crucial. By providing a robust and efficient unsupervised segmentation framework, DyStaB contributes to the advancement of computer vision and image understanding, with relevant keywords including unsupervised learning, object segmentation, dynamic-static bootstrapping, and computer vision.
