This paper presents a novel method for 3D face reconstruction that leverages color photometric stereo with uncalibrated near point light sources. The primary objective is to address the challenges of accurately reconstructing facial geometry in uncontrolled environments where lighting positions are unknown and close to the subject. Our approach utilizes color information to disentangle surface reflectance from geometric cues, enabling robust normal estimation despite uncalibrated lighting. We propose an optimization framework that simultaneously estimates surface normals, albedo, and light positions from a minimal set of color images. Extensive experiments were conducted on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating our method's ability to achieve high-fidelity 3D face reconstructions with fine-detail preservation. Comparative evaluations reveal significant improvements over traditional calibrated and parallel light photometric stereo techniques, particularly in coping with non-uniform illumination and complex facial features. The proposed framework operates without the need for specialized equipment or pre-calibration, making it suitable for consumer-grade camera systems and practical facial modeling applications. This research advances the state of the art in 3D face reconstruction, with potential impacts in areas such as biometric identification, virtual reality, and digital content creation. Key keywords: 3D face reconstruction, photometric stereo, uncalibrated lighting, near point light, color imaging, surface normal estimation.
