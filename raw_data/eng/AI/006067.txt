This paper addresses the challenge of model misspecification in reinforcement learning (RL) for continuous control tasks. Traditional RL approaches often assume precise models of the environment, which can lead to suboptimal performance when these assumptions are violated. Our research aims to develop a robust RL framework capable of maintaining high performance despite inaccuracies in the environment model.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel reinforcement learning algorithm designed to handle model misspecifications by incorporating a robust optimization technique. The proposed method enhances the agent's adaptability to environment uncertainties by integrating an ensemble of models to estimate potential inaccuracies and adjust the agent's policy in real-time. This approach leverages a combination of model-free and model-based strategies to improve learning stability and generalization across diversified environments.

Results/Findings: Experiments were conducted on a suite of continuous control benchmarks, demonstrating that our robust reinforcement learning algorithm outperforms existing state-of-the-art models in the presence of significant model discrepancies. Our method consistently achieved higher rewards and demonstrated increased resilience to various levels of model misspecification compared to conventional RL algorithms. The results show marked improvements in robustness while maintaining computational efficiency.

Conclusion/Implications: The research presents significant advancements in the field of reinforcement learning by offering a robust solution for scenarios plagued by model misspecification. This work contributes to enhancing the reliability and applicability of RL in real-world settings where exact environmental models are often unavailable. Potential applications span autonomous vehicles, robotics, and any domain requiring stable control in uncertain environments. This study underscores the importance of robust model design in pushing the boundaries of RL applications.

Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Continuous Control, Model Misspecification, Robust Optimization, Policy Adaptation, Autonomous Systems.
