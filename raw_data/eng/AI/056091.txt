Abstract:

Image-to-image translation is a crucial task in computer vision, enabling the transformation of visual content from one domain to another, such as changing sketches to realistic photos or converting day images to night scenes. This paper presents a novel approach leveraging Asymmetric Generative Adversarial Networks (Asymmetric GANs) to address inherent challenges in existing methods, particularly imbalance in domain complexity and feature representation. The proposed model introduces asymmetry in network architecture and loss functions, allowing the generator and discriminator to focus on domain-specific characteristics and adaptively balance fidelity and realism. Experiments are conducted on multiple benchmark datasets across diverse translation tasks, demonstrating that the Asymmetric GAN outperforms traditional symmetric GAN models in terms of both quantitative measures (such as Structural Similarity Index and Inception Score) and qualitative assessments. Notably, our approach excels when dealing with domains exhibiting significant structural or semantic differences. These findings highlight the effectiveness and versatility of asymmetric architectures in advancing image-to-image translation. The implications of this work extend to applications in data augmentation, style transfer, and content enhancement, offering a robust framework for future research in generative modeling and cross-domain visual translation.

Keywords: Asymmetric GAN, image-to-image translation, deep learning, domain adaptation, generative adversarial networks, computer vision
