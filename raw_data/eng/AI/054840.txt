Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) often require extensive training data and computational resources. This paper introduces a novel knowledge transfer approach leveraging both semantic and visual similarities to enhance training efficiency. Our method identifies and transfers knowledge from pre-trained CNNs trained on semantically and visually related source datasets to a target dataset with limited labeled data. We propose a two-stage transfer learning framework: first, semantic relationships between classes are exploited to select relevant source models. Second, visual similarity, assessed by feature distribution alignment in intermediate CNN layers, guides the adaptation of selected models. Experiments on image classification tasks demonstrate that our approach achieves significantly improved accuracy and faster convergence compared to traditional transfer learning methods and training from scratch. The integrated use of semantic and visual information proves effective in identifying and transferring task-relevant knowledge, reducing the need for large-scale target datasets and extensive fine-tuning. Our findings contribute to more efficient and practical CNN training in data-scarce scenarios.

Keywords: Knowledge Transfer, Convolutional Neural Networks, Semantic Similarity, Visual Similarity, Transfer Learning, CNN Training, Feature Alignment.
