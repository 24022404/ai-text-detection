This paper addresses the critical challenge of accurately segmenting overlapping chromosomes in cytogenetic images, a fundamental task for various genetic analyses and diagnosis processes. The proposed study introduces an innovative method termed Adversarial Multiscale Feature Learning (AMFL), designed to enhance the segmentation performance of overlapping chromosomes. The approach leverages an adversarial framework combined with multiscale feature extraction to capture and learn both global and local contextual information effectively. Utilizing a generative adversarial network (GAN)-based architecture, the model generates more precise and robust segmentation outcomes by dynamically adjusting to the varying shapes and overlaps of chromosome clusters. The performance of the AMFL method was evaluated on a publicly available dataset and demonstrated superior segmentation accuracy compared to existing state-of-the-art models, evidenced by higher Intersection over Union (IoU) and F1 metrics. The results indicate significant improvements in distinguishing tightly clustered and partially overlapping chromosome instances. This research thereby contributes a novel technique that holds potential utility in enhancing automated karyotyping and advancing cytogenetic analyses. Future applications may extend to broader medical imaging domains that require precise segmentation of intricate and overlapping structures. Key keywords include chromosome segmentation, adversarial learning, multiscale feature extraction, GAN, and medical image analysis.
