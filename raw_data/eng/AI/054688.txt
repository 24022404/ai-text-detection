This paper presents a novel approach to evolving Gaussian Process (GP) kernels from elementary mathematical expressions, addressing the long-standing challenge of kernel design in machine learning. Our objective is to develop a flexible and automated framework for constructing kernels that can effectively capture complex patterns in data. We propose a method that utilizes evolutionary algorithms to search for optimal kernel combinations, leveraging a library of basic mathematical operations and functions. The results show that our approach can generate kernels that outperform traditional hand-designed kernels in various regression and classification tasks, demonstrating improved predictive accuracy and robustness. Our findings have significant implications for the development of more efficient and adaptive GP models, enabling their application to a broader range of problems in fields such as computer vision, natural language processing, and time-series forecasting. Key contributions include the introduction of a new kernel evolution framework, the demonstration of its effectiveness in real-world datasets, and the potential for automated kernel design in GP models, highlighting the importance of Gaussian Processes, kernel methods, and evolutionary algorithms in machine learning research.
