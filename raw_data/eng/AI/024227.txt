This paper introduces DAGMapper, a novel approach to learning-based mapping that focuses on discovering lane topology for autonomous driving applications. The objective is to accurately map complex road networks by identifying lane connections and topology. Our method employs a directed acyclic graph (DAG) representation to model lane relationships and utilizes a deep learning framework to learn the mapping function. The DAGMapper approach is validated through extensive experiments, demonstrating improved mapping accuracy and robustness compared to existing methods. Key findings include the ability to effectively handle complex intersections and lane merges, as well as improved generalization to unseen environments. The proposed system has significant implications for autonomous vehicle navigation and mapping, enabling more efficient and accurate route planning. By discovering lane topology, DAGMapper contributes to the development of more sophisticated and reliable autonomous driving systems, with potential applications in fields such as robotics, computer vision, and intelligent transportation systems. Keywords: autonomous driving, mapping, lane topology, DAG, deep learning, computer vision.
