This paper presents a novel Multi-Modal Graph Neural Network (MM-GNN) designed to address the challenge of joint reasoning on vision and scene text. The objective is to enable systems to effectively integrate visual and textual information for tasks such as image understanding, scene interpretation, and contextual reasoning. Traditional approaches often treat vision and text as separate modalities, limiting their ability to capture the intricate relationships between them. To overcome this, we propose a unified graph-based framework that constructs a multi-modal graph, where nodes represent visual and textual elements, and edges model their interactions. Our approach leverages graph neural networks (GNNs) to propagate and aggregate information across modalities, enabling the model to learn complex dependencies and perform reasoning tasks. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed MM-GNN outperforms existing methods in tasks such as visual question answering and scene text-based reasoning, achieving state-of-the-art performance. The results highlight the effectiveness of our approach in capturing cross-modal relationships and its potential for applications in autonomous systems, augmented reality, and document analysis. This work contributes a scalable and interpretable framework for multi-modal reasoning, paving the way for future advancements in vision-language integration.  

**Keywords**: Multi-modal learning, graph neural networks, scene text, vision-language integration, joint reasoning, visual question answering.
