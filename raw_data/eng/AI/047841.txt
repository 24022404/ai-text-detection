This paper addresses the challenge of interpreting neural network models used for forecasting aggregated renewable generation, a crucial task for ensuring grid stability and optimizing energy distribution. Our objective is to develop a framework that provides insights into the decision-making process of neural networks, enabling more accurate and reliable predictions of renewable energy output. We propose a novel approach that combines feature attribution methods with layer-wise relevance propagation, allowing for the identification of key input factors influencing forecasted values. Our results show that the proposed framework significantly improves model interpretability, enabling the identification of complex relationships between weather patterns, renewable generation, and forecast errors. The findings of this study have important implications for the development of more transparent and trustworthy neural network models in the field of renewable energy forecasting, ultimately contributing to a more efficient and sustainable energy system. Key contributions include the application of neural network interpretability techniques to renewable energy forecasting, and the demonstration of improved model performance through feature attribution analysis. Relevant keywords: neural network interpretability, renewable energy forecasting, feature attribution, layer-wise relevance propagation, explainable AI.
