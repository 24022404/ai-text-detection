Time series feature extraction plays a critical role in industrial big data applications, enabling predictive maintenance, anomaly detection, and process optimization. However, the computational demands of processing massive time series datasets often exceed the capabilities of single-machine architectures. This paper presents a distributed and parallel framework for efficient time series feature extraction tailored for industrial big data. Our approach leverages Apache Spark to distribute time series data across a cluster of machines and employs a parallel implementation of commonly used feature extraction algorithms, including statistical moments, entropy measures, and frequency-domain features. We introduce a novel data partitioning strategy that minimizes inter-node communication overhead during feature computation. The performance of our framework is evaluated using a real-world industrial dataset from a manufacturing plant, demonstrating significant speedups compared to traditional single-machine implementations. Specifically, we achieve near-linear scalability with the number of worker nodes and a reduction in processing time by up to 80% for large datasets. These results highlight the potential of our distributed and parallel framework to enable real-time analysis and decision-making in industrial big data environments. Keywords: Time series, Feature extraction, Distributed computing, Parallel processing, Big data, Industrial applications, Apache Spark.
