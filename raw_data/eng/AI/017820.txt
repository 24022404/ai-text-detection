Abstract:

Cross-domain and cross-task transfer learning remains a significant challenge in machine learning, particularly due to the inherent differences in data distributions and label spaces across domains and tasks. This paper presents a novel framework that leverages unsupervised clustering to enhance the transferability of learned models. Our approach, termed Clustered Domain Transfer (CDT), automatically infers meaningful data groupings within source and target domains through adaptive clustering algorithms. By aligning these clusters, CDT constructs a shared latent representation that preserves critical task-related structures and reduces distributional discrepancies. We apply our method to multiple benchmark datasets in visual recognition and text classification, demonstrating substantial improvements in both domain adaptation and multitask learning scenarios when compared to traditional transfer learning and domain adaptation techniques. Experimental results showcase increased accuracy, robustness to domain shifts, and better generalization for new tasks. The proposed framework is model-agnostic, scalable, and provides insights into how unsupervised structure discovery can facilitate knowledge transfer. Our findings highlight the potential of clustering as a bridge for effective transfer across heterogeneous domains and tasks, contributing to the advancement of transfer learning methodologies. Keywords: transfer learning, domain adaptation, clustering, multitask learning, unsupervised learning, representation learning.
