Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown remarkable success in solving complex decision-making tasks, yet its ability to generalize across diverse environments remains a significant challenge. This paper addresses the problem of instance-based generalization in RL, where an agent must adapt to unseen environments or tasks that share similarities with its training instances. We propose a novel framework that leverages instance-based learning techniques to enhance the generalization capabilities of RL agents. Our approach integrates memory-augmented neural networks with a dynamic instance retrieval mechanism, enabling the agent to efficiently recall and apply knowledge from past experiences to new scenarios. Through extensive experiments on benchmark environments, we demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms traditional RL algorithms in terms of generalization performance, achieving higher success rates and faster adaptation to novel tasks. The results highlight the importance of instance-based reasoning in RL and provide insights into designing more robust and adaptable learning systems. This work contributes to advancing the field of RL by offering a scalable solution for improving generalization, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and adaptive AI.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Generalization, Instance-Based Learning, Memory-Augmented Networks, Adaptive Systems.
