This paper addresses the critical problem of bias in learned generative models, which can lead to inaccuracies in data synthesis and downstream tasks. The study proposes a novel method for correcting this bias without relying on likelihood computations, which are often infeasible due to complex model structures.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a likelihood-free importance weighting technique that adjusts the output of generative models to reduce bias. This approach leverages importance sampling to reweight generated samples based on their statistical characteristics, eliminating the need for explicit likelihood evaluation and enhancing computational feasibility.

Results/Findings: Our approach was tested across several benchmark datasets, showcasing significant improvement in bias correction over traditional generative modeling techniques. Comparative analysis reveals that our method achieves superior performance in maintaining the statistical integrity of generated samples while preserving model efficiency. Additionally, the importance weighting technique reduced the bias in sample distributions by a notable margin, proving its efficacy.

Conclusion/Implications: By providing a robust framework for bias correction without the computational burden of likelihood calculations, this research contributes a scalable solution for enhancing the reliability of generative models. This method can potentially be applied in various domains, including but not limited to synthetic data generation, fairness in AI applications, and more accurate probabilistic modeling. The introduction of this technique marks a significant step forward in addressing the challenges of bias in generative modeling.

Keywords: Bias correction, generative models, importance weighting, likelihood-free, synthetic data, statistical integrity, AI fairness.
