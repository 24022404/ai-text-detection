Objective: Deep learning models have achieved remarkable accuracy in medical imaging and neurodevelopmental disorder diagnosis but often lack interpretability, limiting their clinical applicability. This study presents a novel framework that combines graph-based data representation with cooperative game theory to efficiently interpret deep neural networks and uncover biomarkers associated with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD).

Methods: We model functional connectivity data derived from resting-state functional MRI as a weighted graph, where nodes represent brain regions and edges capture inter-regional interactions. A tailored interpretation algorithm computes approximate Shapley values for each node and edge, quantifying their contribution to model predictions. To address the combinatorial complexity of exact value calculation, we introduce a Monte Carlo–based sampling strategy that leverages the graph’s sparsity to reduce computational cost by over 70% compared to standard Shapley estimators. The deep classifier is a convolutional graph neural network (GNN) trained to distinguish ASD from neurotypical controls.

Results: On two publicly available ASD cohorts (n=200 and n=150), our method achieved interpretation runtimes under 10 minutes per subject and maintained classification accuracy above 88%. The Shapley-based analysis highlighted reproducible biomarker subgraphs centered on the default mode and salience networks. Feature importance rankings showed statistically significant agreement (p<0.01) with established ASD connectivity alterations.

Conclusions: By integrating graph structure with cooperative game-theoretic attribution, our approach delivers fast, granular interpretability for deep learning models in neuroscience. The identified network biomarkers offer insights into ASD pathophysiology and demonstrate potential for guiding individualized interventions. This framework can generalize to other disorders and complex graph-structured datasets.

Keywords: deep learning, model interpretability, graph neural network, Shapley value, cooperative game theory, autism spectrum disorder, biomarker discovery.
