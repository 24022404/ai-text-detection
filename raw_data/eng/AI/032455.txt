**Abstract:**  
Interpretability remains a critical challenge in machine learning, particularly for complex, black-box models whose decision-making processes are opaque. This paper introduces a Modified Perturbed Sampling Method (MPSM) to enhance Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanation (LIME) frameworks. The proposed approach aims to improve the fidelity and stability of local explanations by generating more representative perturbed samples in the vicinity of the instance under investigation. In contrast to traditional perturbation techniques that may introduce artificial or unrealistic samples, MPSM incorporates adaptive sampling strategies that consider the underlying data distribution and feature dependencies. Experimental evaluations on multiple benchmark datasets, using both classification and regression models, demonstrate that MPSM achieves higher explanation accuracy and better consistency compared to standard LIME. Furthermore, qualitative assessments indicate that the generated explanations align more closely with human intuition and domain knowledge. The findings suggest that the modified sampling approach not only strengthens model interpretability but also provides more actionable insights for stakeholders. This advancement holds potential for broad application in fields requiring transparent AI, such as healthcare, finance, and legal tech.  
**Keywords:** model interpretability, LIME, local explanation, perturbed sampling, explainable AI, machine learning transparency
