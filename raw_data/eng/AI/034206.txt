Objective: Thin structures such as blood vessels, wires, or cracks often occupy only a few pixels in high-resolution images, making their restoration under large missing regions particularly challenging. This paper addresses the problem of blind inpainting for large-scale masks occluding thin structures, where neither the occlusion shape nor the underlying content is known.

Methods: We propose a two-stage deep learning framework that combines adversarial training with reinforcement learning. First, a generative adversarial network (GAN) learns to produce initial inpainting results guided by a structure-preserving loss that emphasizes continuity and thin-line coherence. Second, a reinforcement learning (RL) agent refines these results through sequential patch selection, using a reward function that balances local detail recovery and global consistency. The RL agent adaptively focuses on hard-to-recover regions, dynamically adjusting its inpainting strategy based on feedback from a discriminator network.

Results: Extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets (including medical angiograms and urban aerial imagery) demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art blind inpainting and general image restoration models by 12–18% in structural similarity (SSIM) and reduces thin-line discontinuities by up to 25%. Qualitative evaluations show sharper, more coherent reconstructions of occluded filaments and curves.

Conclusion: By integrating adversarial learning with reinforcement-driven refinement, our approach offers a robust solution for the blind inpainting of thin structures under extensive occlusion. This framework can be readily adapted to applications in medical image analysis, infrastructure inspection, and historical document restoration.  

Keywords: blind inpainting, thin structures, generative adversarial network, reinforcement learning, image restoration.
