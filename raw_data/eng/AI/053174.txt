This paper addresses the challenge of domain generalization, where a model is expected to perform well on unseen domains. The objective is to develop a framework that enables learning to expect the unexpected, allowing for probably approximately correct (PAC) domain generalization. We propose a novel approach that combines adversarial training with meta-learning to learn domain-invariant representations. Our method, termed PAC-DG, utilizes a probabilistic framework to quantify uncertainty and adapt to new domains. Experimental results demonstrate that PAC-DG outperforms state-of-the-art domain generalization methods on benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and robustness. The key findings highlight the importance of uncertainty quantification and adaptation in domain generalization. This research contributes to the development of more robust and generalizable machine learning models, with potential applications in areas such as computer vision, natural language processing, and reinforcement learning. Key keywords: domain generalization, probably approximately correct, meta-learning, adversarial training, uncertainty quantification.
