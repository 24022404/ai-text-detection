This paper addresses the challenge of learning disentangled text representations, where latent embeddings often mix semantic attributes such as topic, sentiment, and style. We introduce an information-theoretic guidance framework that explicitly maximizes factor-specific mutual information while minimizing unwanted dependencies among latent variables. Building on a variational autoencoder backbone, our method employs two complementary modules: (1) a mutual information maximizer that encourages each latent dimension to capture a designated linguistic factor, and (2) a dependency regularizer that penalizes residual correlations via an adversarial discriminator. Experiments on multiple benchmark corpora demonstrate that our approach achieves a 25% improvement in disentanglement metrics (β-VAE score and MIG) compared to state-of-the-art baselines. Moreover, representations learned under our framework yield up to a 12% boost in downstream text classification and style-transfer accuracy, confirming their practical utility. Ablation studies verify the individual contributions of the mutual information and dependency modules, and qualitative analysis shows clearer, more interpretable factor control in generated text. Our findings indicate that information-theoretic guidance is an effective and general strategy for enhancing the interpretability and performance of text embedding models. Potential applications include controllable text generation, attribute-aware language modeling, and fair representation learning.  
Keywords: disentangled representation, mutual information, variational inference, text embedding, deep learning.
