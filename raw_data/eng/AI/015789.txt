This paper introduces Dirichlet uncertainty wrappers as a novel approach to enhance algorithm accuracy accountability and auditability. The objective is to develop a framework that provides actionable insights into the uncertainty of machine learning models, enabling more reliable and trustworthy decision-making. Our method leverages Dirichlet distributions to quantify and propagate uncertainty through complex algorithms, allowing for the identification of potential errors and biases. The results show that our approach significantly improves model interpretability and transparency, enabling more effective auditing and accountability. Key findings include the ability to detect and mitigate concept drift, as well as provide uncertainty estimates for high-stakes decision-making applications. The conclusion highlights the importance of uncertainty quantification in machine learning, with implications for increased model reliability and trustworthiness. Our research contributes to the development of more accountable and transparent AI systems, with potential applications in areas such as healthcare, finance, and autonomous systems. Keywords: uncertainty quantification, algorithmic accountability, auditability, Dirichlet distributions, machine learning, model interpretability.
