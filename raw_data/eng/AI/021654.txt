Online learning with uncertain feedback graphs is a critical area of research in machine learning, where the learner receives partial and potentially noisy information about the environment. This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing decision-making in such scenarios, where the feedback graph—representing the relationship between actions and observed outcomes—is uncertain or incomplete. We propose a novel algorithmic framework that leverages probabilistic modeling and adaptive exploration strategies to handle uncertainty in the feedback structure. Our approach integrates graph-based learning with online optimization techniques, enabling the learner to efficiently update its strategy based on evolving feedback. Through extensive experiments, we demonstrate that our method outperforms existing approaches in terms of regret minimization and convergence speed, particularly in environments with high uncertainty. The results highlight the robustness and scalability of our framework, making it suitable for real-world applications such as recommendation systems, online advertising, and resource allocation. This work contributes to the broader field of online learning by providing a principled and effective solution for handling uncertain feedback graphs, with implications for improving decision-making in dynamic and partially observable environments.  

**Keywords**: online learning, uncertain feedback graphs, probabilistic modeling, adaptive exploration, regret minimization, decision-making.
