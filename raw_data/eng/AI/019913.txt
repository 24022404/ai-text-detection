Deep face recognition systems have witnessed substantial progress in recent years, yet challenges remain in achieving robust performance under varying pose, illumination, and expression conditions. This paper presents a novel approach to boosting deep face recognition accuracy by explicitly disentangling appearance and geometry information within facial representations. Our method introduces a dual-branch deep learning framework where appearance-related features (such as texture and color) are separated from geometry-related features (such as facial structure and shape) at the feature extraction stage. This disentanglement is facilitated through a specialized loss function and architectural innovations that encourage the network to learn invariant and complementary representations. Comprehensive experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms conventional deep face recognition methods, particularly in scenarios involving significant facial variations. The results show improved recognition accuracy, enhanced robustness to occlusions, and better generalization to unseen data. Our findings highlight the importance of disentangling appearance and geometry in face recognition, offering valuable insights for developing more reliable and interpretable biometric systems. Key contributions include the design of the disentangling framework and its effectiveness in real-world, unconstrained environments. Keywords: deep face recognition, disentanglement, facial appearance, facial geometry, deep learning, biometric systems.
