This paper introduces a novel approach to 3D reconstruction and motion estimation of rapidly moving, textured objects from motion blur. The core objective is to recover accurate 3D shapes and motions where traditional methods fail due to significant blurring artifacts. Our method employs a physics-based model that incorporates both camera motion and object motion into the blur formation process. We present an optimization framework that jointly estimates the object's 3D shape, its trajectory, and the underlying texture from a single blurry image. This involves solving a non-linear inverse problem, which we address using a custom iterative algorithm. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world data demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing deblurring and reconstruction techniques, particularly in scenarios with substantial motion blur. We achieve superior accuracy in shape recovery and motion estimation, even with complex object textures. The proposed method has potential applications in high-speed imaging, robotics, and autonomous navigation. Keywords: 3D Reconstruction, Motion Blur, Shape Recovery, Motion Estimation, Inverse Problem, High-Speed Imaging.
