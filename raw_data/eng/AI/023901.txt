Video super-resolution (VSR) aims to enhance the spatial resolution of low-quality video sequences, a critical task for applications in video streaming, surveillance, and medical imaging. While supervised learning methods have shown promising results, they rely heavily on large-scale paired datasets, which are often expensive and time-consuming to acquire. This paper introduces a novel self-supervised adaptation framework for VSR that eliminates the need for paired training data. Our approach leverages temporal consistency and spatial redundancy within video sequences to train a deep neural network without explicit supervision. Specifically, we propose a multi-scale feature extraction module combined with a temporal alignment mechanism to ensure robust learning from unpaired data. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art supervised approaches, with significant improvements in perceptual quality and temporal stability. The proposed framework not only reduces dependency on labeled data but also generalizes well across diverse video domains. This work contributes to advancing self-supervised learning in VSR, offering a practical and scalable solution for real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Video super-resolution, self-supervised learning, temporal consistency, deep learning, unpaired data, multi-scale feature extraction.
