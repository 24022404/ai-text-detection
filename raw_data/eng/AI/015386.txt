This paper addresses the challenge of anomaly detection in Internet of Things (IoT) environments deployed in hierarchical edge computing architectures. Traditional anomaly detection methods often struggle with the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of IoT data and the limited resources available at the edge. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel adaptive anomaly detection framework based on a contextual-bandit approach. Our method leverages contextual information from the IoT devices and the edge computing nodes to dynamically adjust anomaly detection parameters, optimizing performance in real-time. We evaluate our approach using both synthetic and real-world IoT datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in detection accuracy and reduced false alarm rates compared to static and non-adaptive anomaly detection techniques. Furthermore, we analyze the computational overhead of our method, showing its suitability for resource-constrained edge environments. The results highlight the potential of contextual-bandit algorithms for adaptive and efficient anomaly detection in hierarchical edge-based IoT systems, contributing to enhanced security and reliability of IoT deployments.

Keywords: Anomaly Detection, Internet of Things, Edge Computing, Contextual Bandit, Adaptive Learning, IoT Security.
