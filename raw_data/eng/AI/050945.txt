The accurate classification and risk prediction of medical tabular data are critical for improving healthcare decision-making. However, the inherent uncertainty in such data poses significant challenges, particularly in high-stakes applications. This paper addresses the problem of uncertainty estimation in classification and risk prediction tasks, aiming to enhance the reliability of machine learning models in medical contexts. We propose a novel probabilistic framework that integrates uncertainty quantification into traditional classification and regression models, leveraging Bayesian neural networks and ensemble-based methods. Our approach not only provides point predictions but also estimates predictive uncertainty, enabling more informed decision-making. Experiments conducted on diverse medical datasets demonstrate that the proposed framework outperforms baseline methods in terms of both prediction accuracy and uncertainty calibration. Key findings reveal that incorporating uncertainty estimation reduces overconfidence in predictions and improves model robustness, particularly in scenarios with limited or imbalanced data. The results highlight the potential of this approach to support clinical applications, such as disease diagnosis and patient risk stratification, by offering interpretable and reliable predictions. This work contributes to the growing field of trustworthy AI in healthcare, emphasizing the importance of uncertainty-aware models for safe and effective deployment.  

**Keywords**: uncertainty estimation, medical tabular data, classification, risk prediction, Bayesian neural networks, ensemble methods, trustworthy AI.
