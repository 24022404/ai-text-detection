**Abstract**

Monocular depth estimation, the process of inferring scene depth from a single image, is vital for diverse applications such as autonomous vehicles, robotics, and augmented reality. However, achieving accurate depth estimation in general, unconstrained scenes remains challenging due to scene diversity and the limited availability of ground truth depth data. This paper presents RealMonoDepth, a novel self-supervised framework for monocular depth estimation designed to generalize across a wide range of real-world environments. Our approach leverages self-supervised learning by employing a photometric consistency loss between consecutive frames, eliminating the need for expensive ground truth depth annotations. We introduce several innovations, including a multi-scale attention-guided encoder-decoder architecture and an adaptive dynamic masking module that robustly handles occlusions and moving objects. Experimental results on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that RealMonoDepth outperforms existing state-of-the-art self-supervised methods in terms of accuracy and generalization, particularly in challenging outdoor and indoor scenes. Additionally, RealMonoDepth achieves real-time inference speeds, enabling deployment on resource-constrained platforms. Our findings highlight the effectiveness of self-supervised learning for scalable and adaptable monocular depth estimation, paving the way for broader real-world applications in computer vision and robotics.

**Keywords:** Monocular depth estimation, self-supervised learning, real-time inference, computer vision, generalization, deep learning, autonomous systems.
