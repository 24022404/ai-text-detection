Autonomous driving systems rely heavily on accurate and efficient object detection to ensure safety and reliability. However, challenges such as suboptimal anchor generation and class imbalance in datasets often hinder the performance of detection models. This paper addresses these issues by proposing a novel framework that enhances object detection through optimized anchor generation and a tailored approach to mitigate class imbalance. Our method introduces an adaptive anchor generation algorithm that dynamically adjusts anchor sizes and aspect ratios based on the distribution of objects in the dataset, improving localization accuracy. Additionally, we implement a class-aware sampling strategy combined with a focal loss variant to address class imbalance, ensuring balanced learning across all object categories. Experiments conducted on benchmark autonomous driving datasets demonstrate significant improvements in detection accuracy, particularly for underrepresented classes, while maintaining computational efficiency. The proposed framework outperforms existing methods in terms of mean Average Precision (mAP) and robustness in diverse driving scenarios. This research contributes to advancing object detection for autonomous vehicles, offering a scalable solution that can be integrated into real-world systems to enhance safety and performance.  

**Keywords**: object detection, autonomous driving, anchor generation, class imbalance, adaptive algorithms, focal loss, mean Average Precision (mAP).
