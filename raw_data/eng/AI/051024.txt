Understanding the decision-making processes of reinforcement learning (RL) agents, particularly in complex environments like Atari games, remains a significant challenge. This paper benchmarks perturbation-based saliency maps as a method for explaining the behavior of Atari agents. The objective is to evaluate the effectiveness of these saliency maps in identifying critical input features that influence agent decisions. We employ a systematic approach, applying perturbation techniques to modify game frames and analyzing the resulting changes in agent actions. Several state-of-the-art saliency map methods are compared, including occlusion-based and gradient-based approaches, to assess their interpretability and robustness. Our results demonstrate that perturbation-based saliency maps provide meaningful insights into agent behavior, with certain methods outperforming others in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. Additionally, we identify limitations in current approaches, such as sensitivity to noise and scalability issues in high-dimensional environments. The findings highlight the potential of perturbation-based methods for improving the transparency of RL agents, offering valuable implications for both researchers and practitioners in developing more interpretable AI systems. This work contributes to the growing field of explainable AI (XAI) by providing a comprehensive evaluation framework for saliency maps in the context of Atari agents.  

**Keywords**: Explainable AI, saliency maps, reinforcement learning, Atari agents, perturbation techniques, interpretability.
