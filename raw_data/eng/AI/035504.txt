This paper investigates the role of attention mechanisms in generating saliency maps for Atari game-playing agents, introducing a novel approach termed "Free-Lunch Saliency." The objective is to leverage inherent attention layers within deep reinforcement learning models to produce interpretable saliency maps without additional computational overhead. We propose a method that extracts attention weights from transformer-based architectures and visualizes them as saliency maps, providing insights into the decision-making process of the agent. Experiments are conducted on a suite of Atari games using state-of-the-art reinforcement learning frameworks. Results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves comparable or superior interpretability compared to traditional saliency techniques, while maintaining computational efficiency. The findings highlight the potential of attention-based saliency as a lightweight and effective tool for understanding agent behavior in complex environments. This work contributes to the growing field of explainable AI by offering a practical, resource-efficient solution for saliency generation in reinforcement learning systems.  

**Keywords**: Saliency maps, attention mechanisms, reinforcement learning, Atari games, explainable AI, transformer models.
