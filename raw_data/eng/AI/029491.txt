Recent advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) have demonstrated remarkable success in solving complex tasks. However, many RL methods struggle with generalization, particularly in few-shot scenarios where agents must extrapolate to unseen environments or tasks. This paper addresses the challenge of few-shot extrapolation by proposing a novel approach based on structured maximum entropy (MaxEnt) RL. Our method leverages a probabilistic framework to encourage diverse policy exploration, enabling agents to discover multiple solutions to a given problem. By explicitly modeling the structure of the solution space, the approach facilitates robust generalization and adaptability to new contexts. We evaluate our method on a suite of benchmark tasks, including robotic manipulation and navigation, and demonstrate significant improvements in extrapolation performance compared to existing baselines. The results highlight the importance of diversity in policy learning and provide insights into how structured exploration can enhance few-shot generalization. This work contributes to the broader goal of developing RL systems that can efficiently adapt to real-world variability, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and beyond.  

**Keywords**: reinforcement learning, maximum entropy, few-shot learning, generalization, policy diversity, structured exploration.
