Predicting patient mortality using unstructured clinical data remains a significant challenge in healthcare informatics. This study explores the potential of leveraging free-text clinical notes to improve mortality prediction models, addressing the limitations of structured data alone. We propose a novel deep learning framework that integrates natural language processing (NLP) techniques with advanced neural architectures to extract meaningful insights from unstructured text. Our approach employs transformer-based models to encode clinical narratives, combined with a multi-modal fusion mechanism to incorporate structured patient data. Experiments were conducted on a large-scale dataset of de-identified electronic health records (EHRs), demonstrating that our model achieves superior performance compared to traditional methods, with significant improvements in accuracy and recall. Key findings reveal that free-text notes provide critical contextual information, such as patient history and clinical observations, which are often absent in structured formats. The proposed framework not only enhances mortality prediction but also offers interpretable insights into the factors driving predictions, making it a valuable tool for clinical decision support. This research contributes to the growing body of work on AI-driven healthcare analytics, highlighting the importance of unstructured data in improving patient outcomes and advancing precision medicine.  

**Keywords**: mortality prediction, clinical notes, natural language processing, deep learning, electronic health records, healthcare analytics.
