This study aims to investigate the underlying mechanisms of deep transfer learning in medical image analysis, a crucial aspect of contemporary healthcare. By leveraging pre-trained convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and fine-tuning them on medical image datasets, we explore the effectiveness of transfer learning in improving model performance and reducing training time. Our approach utilizes a combination of feature extraction and classification techniques to analyze the transferability of knowledge from natural images to medical images. The results show significant improvements in model accuracy and efficiency, particularly in cases where limited training data is available. Key findings highlight the importance of domain adaptation and the role of batch normalization in facilitating successful transfer learning. This research contributes to the understanding of deep transfer learning mechanisms, providing valuable insights for the development of more accurate and efficient medical image analysis systems. The study's implications extend to various medical imaging applications, including disease diagnosis and image segmentation, with potential benefits for clinical decision-making and patient care. Relevant keywords: deep learning, transfer learning, medical image analysis, convolutional neural networks, domain adaptation.
