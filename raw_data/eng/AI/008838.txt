**Abstract:**

Few-shot learning has emerged as a critical approach for enabling machine learning models to recognize novel classes from limited examples, yet achieving robust generalization across diverse and relationally complex tasks remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces Relational Generalized Few-Shot Learning (RGFSL), a novel framework that extends conventional few-shot learning paradigms by explicitly modeling and leveraging the relational structure among classes and instances. Our approach integrates relational reasoning modules with generalized meta-learning techniques, allowing the model to effectively capture inter-class dependencies and adapt to new tasks with minimal data. We evaluate RGFSL on benchmark datasets in image classification and graph-based scenarios, demonstrating substantial improvements over existing few-shot and meta-learning methods in terms of classification accuracy and adaptability. Analysis reveals that RGFSL offers enhanced robustness to distribution shifts and better scalability to large label spaces. The results underscore the practical potential of combining relational modeling with few-shot generalization, making the proposed framework a promising solution for real-world applications where data scarcity and relational complexity are prevalent. Keywords: few-shot learning, relational reasoning, meta-learning, generalization, machine learning.
