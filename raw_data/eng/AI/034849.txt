This paper presents an unsupervised approach for identifying lane-change maneuvers in naturalistic driving data, specifically focusing on on-ramp merge scenarios. The objective is to develop a method that can accurately detect and characterize lane-change events without relying on labeled data. Our approach utilizes a combination of machine learning algorithms and data mining techniques to analyze large datasets of driving behavior. The results show that our method can effectively identify lane-change maneuvers with high accuracy, outperforming traditional rule-based approaches. Key findings include the ability to detect subtle changes in driver behavior and the identification of unique patterns associated with on-ramp merges. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to provide insights into driver behavior and traffic flow, with potential applications in traffic management, road safety analysis, and autonomous vehicle development. Our approach highlights the importance of unsupervised learning in analyzing complex driving behaviors, and its novelty lies in the integration of data-driven methods for lane-change identification. Relevant keywords include: unsupervised learning, lane-change identification, naturalistic driving data, on-ramp merge analysis, driver behavior analysis, traffic flow modeling, and intelligent transportation systems.
