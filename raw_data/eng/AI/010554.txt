Person re-identification (Re-ID) across different camera views is a challenging problem in the field of computer vision, often hindered by variations in lighting, pose, and background. This paper presents a novel approach to improving the accuracy and robustness of person Re-ID through the introduction of Cluster Loss, a loss function designed to enhance feature discriminability.

Methods/Approach: The proposed Cluster Loss is integrated into a deep learning framework, facilitating the grouping of similar features while separating dissimilar ones in the feature space. This technique is combined with conventional loss functions to achieve a balanced optimization of intra-class compactness and inter-class separability. Our approach leverages a convolutional neural network (CNN) topology, fine-tuned with Cluster Loss to optimize feature embedding for Re-ID tasks.

Results/Findings: Extensive experiments were conducted on standard benchmark datasets for person re-identification, demonstrating that models optimized with Cluster Loss outperform baseline methods. The results indicated significant improvements in rank-1 accuracy and mean Average Precision (mAP), underscoring the effectiveness of the proposed loss function. Comparative analysis shows that Cluster Loss enhances feature learning capability, especially in challenging conditions with substantial intra-class variance.

Conclusion/Implications: This research introduces Cluster Loss as a powerful tool for improving person re-identification systems, contributing to more accurate and efficient identity matching across camera networks. The findings suggest that incorporating Cluster Loss can lead to notable advancements in automated surveillance and monitoring applications. The approach has the potential to be applied in various domains that require robust feature discrimination, furthering the capabilities of computer vision applications.

Keywords: Person Re-Identification, Cluster Loss, Feature Embedding, Deep Learning, Convolutional Neural Networks, Identity Matching, Computer Vision.
