Distributional reinforcement learning (DRL) has shown promise in addressing the limitations of traditional value-based methods by learning a distribution of returns rather than a single expected value. However, the impact of sample size and statistical methods on the performance and stability of DRL algorithms remains underexplored. This paper investigates the interplay between different statistical techniques, specifically focusing on various estimators and sampling strategies within the DRL framework. We analyze the effect of using different estimators such as the empirical mean, quantile regression, and Wasserstein barycenter, on the learned return distributions. We further investigate the influence of different sampling methods, including uniform sampling and prioritized experience replay, on the stability and convergence of DRL agents. Our experiments, conducted on a range of benchmark environments, demonstrate that the choice of estimator and sampling strategy significantly affects the performance and robustness of DRL algorithms. We identify specific combinations of estimators and sampling methods that lead to improved performance and propose guidelines for selecting appropriate statistical tools for DRL. Our findings provide valuable insights into the statistical properties of DRL and offer practical recommendations for designing more effective and reliable DRL agents. Keywords: Distributional Reinforcement Learning, Statistics, Sampling, Reinforcement Learning, Quantile Regression, Experience Replay.
