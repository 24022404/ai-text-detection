Multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) offers a powerful framework for addressing complex decision-making problems with multiple, potentially conflicting, objectives. This paper tackles the challenge of optimizing policies for “picky” customers, where preferences are not explicitly known but revealed through feedback. We introduce a novel MORL algorithm designed to efficiently learn policies that satisfy diverse customer needs while minimizing regret. Our approach leverages a modified upper confidence bound (UCB) exploration strategy tailored for multi-objective settings, balancing exploration of less-known policy spaces with exploitation of promising regions. We derive theoretical regret bounds demonstrating the algorithm's performance and provide exploration complexity guarantees, indicating the number of interactions required to achieve near-optimal policies. Experimental results on benchmark MORL environments validate the effectiveness of our algorithm, showcasing its ability to adapt to varying customer preferences and outperform existing MORL methods in terms of cumulative reward and preference satisfaction. This work contributes to the development of practical MORL solutions for personalized recommendation systems, adaptive robotics, and other applications requiring preference-aware decision-making.

Keywords: Multi-objective Reinforcement Learning, Regret Bound, Exploration Complexity, Preference Learning, Upper Confidence Bound, Reinforcement Learning.
