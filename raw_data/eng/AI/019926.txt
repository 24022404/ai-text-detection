**Abstract:**

Human action recognition is a vital task in fields such as healthcare, sports analytics, and human-computer interaction. Traditional approaches primarily leverage sequential inertial sensor data, which often face challenges related to temporal dynamics and variability. This paper proposes a novel method that encodes inertial sensor data into image representations, enabling the application of advanced image-based deep learning models for action classification. The proposed framework processes raw sensor signals—such as accelerometer and gyroscope readings—using a structured transformation pipeline to generate two-dimensional visual representations that capture both temporal dependencies and sensor correlations. These images are then input to state-of-the-art convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for action recognition. Experimental evaluations on publicly available inertial datasets demonstrate that the image encoding approach achieves superior recognition accuracy compared to traditional sequence-based methods, particularly in complex or noisy environments. Additionally, our method facilitates the use of established computer vision techniques for feature extraction and model interpretability. The results indicate significant improvement in both robustness and generalization across diverse action classes. This work highlights the potential of cross-domain data representation—bridging time-series sensor analytics and image-based machine learning—to enhance the performance and versatility of human action recognition systems. Key keywords: inertial sensors, action recognition, image encoding, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, sensor data transformation.
