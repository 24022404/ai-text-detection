This paper explores the application of Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (Neural ODEs) to model and control dynamics on graph-structured data. The objective is to address the challenge of capturing continuous-time dynamics in complex systems represented as graphs, such as social networks, biological interactions, or transportation systems. Traditional methods often rely on discrete-time approximations, which can limit accuracy and scalability. In this work, we propose a novel framework that leverages Neural ODEs to model the continuous evolution of node and edge features over time, enabling more precise control of graph dynamics. Our approach integrates graph neural networks (GNNs) with Neural ODEs to learn latent representations of the system and predict future states. We evaluate the proposed method on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating its ability to outperform existing discrete-time models in terms of prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight the framework's potential for real-world applications, such as traffic flow optimization, disease spread modeling, and recommendation systems. This research contributes to the growing field of graph-based machine learning by introducing a continuous-time perspective, offering a new direction for controlling and analyzing dynamic systems on graphs.  

**Keywords**: Neural Ordinary Differential Equations, Graph Dynamics, Continuous-Time Models, Graph Neural Networks, System Control.
