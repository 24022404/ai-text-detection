Chromosome segmentation, particularly in overlapping scenarios, remains a critical challenge in medical image analysis, with significant implications for genetic research and diagnostics. This paper introduces a novel adversarial multiscale feature learning framework designed to address the complexities of overlapping chromosome segmentation. The proposed approach leverages a generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture combined with multiscale feature extraction to enhance the model's ability to distinguish and separate overlapping chromosomes in high-resolution microscopy images. The multiscale feature learning component captures both fine-grained details and broader contextual information, while the adversarial training mechanism ensures robust and accurate segmentation outcomes. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing techniques in terms of segmentation accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with high levels of chromosome overlap. The framework achieves a mean intersection-over-union (IoU) score of 0.92, surpassing state-of-the-art methods by 8%. This research contributes to the field by providing a scalable and efficient solution for overlapping chromosome segmentation, with potential applications in automated karyotyping and genetic disorder diagnosis. Key innovations include the integration of adversarial learning with multiscale feature extraction and the development of a loss function tailored to overlapping chromosome scenarios.  

**Keywords**: chromosome segmentation, adversarial learning, multiscale feature extraction, generative adversarial networks (GANs), medical image analysis, overlapping objects.
