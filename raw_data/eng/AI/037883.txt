This paper addresses the challenge of robust subspace discovery in high-dimensional data, a critical task in machine learning and data analysis. Traditional subspace learning methods often struggle with noisy or corrupted data, leading to suboptimal performance. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel approach called Block-diagonal Adaptive Locality-constrained Representation (BALR). BALR integrates block-diagonal structure constraints with adaptive locality preservation to enhance the discriminative power of learned subspaces. The method leverages a self-representation framework to capture the intrinsic geometric structure of data while enforcing block-diagonal patterns to promote clustering-friendly representations. We develop an efficient optimization algorithm to solve the proposed model, ensuring scalability and convergence. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that BALR outperforms state-of-the-art subspace learning methods in terms of clustering accuracy and robustness to noise. The results highlight the effectiveness of BALR in discovering meaningful subspaces, particularly in applications such as image clustering and anomaly detection. This work contributes a robust and adaptive framework for subspace discovery, offering significant potential for advancing data analysis tasks in real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Subspace learning, block-diagonal representation, locality constraints, clustering, high-dimensional data, noise robustness.
