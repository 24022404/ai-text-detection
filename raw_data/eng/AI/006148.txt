Graph spectral learning has emerged as a powerful approach for analyzing complex data structures in information networks, social graphs, and biological systems. However, existing spectral methods often struggle to scale efficiently to large real-world graphs, limiting their practical applicability. This paper introduces GRASPEL, a novel framework for scalable graph spectral learning. Our approach leverages optimized graph partitioning, efficient spectral embedding techniques, and parallel computation to handle graphs with millions of nodes and edges. We present the design and implementation of GRASPEL, detailing algorithms for fast eigen-decomposition, sparse matrix operations, and batch processing. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that GRASPEL achieves comparable or superior accuracy in node classification, link prediction, and community detection tasks, while reducing computation time and memory footprint by an order of magnitude compared to traditional spectral methods. Our findings highlight the effectiveness of spectral learning for large-scale graph analytics and open new possibilities for real-time, data-driven applications in recommendation systems, cybersecurity, and bioinformatics. GRASPEL represents a significant advancement in graph spectral learning, enabling researchers and practitioners to efficiently extract structural insights from massive and complex networked data.

Keywords: graph spectral learning, scalable algorithms, large-scale graphs, spectral embedding, parallel computation, graph analytics.
