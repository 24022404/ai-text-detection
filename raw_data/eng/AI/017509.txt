Understanding human activities in home environments is crucial for various applications, including assistive robotics and smart homes. This paper introduces the Home Action Genome (HAG), a novel framework for cooperative compositional action understanding. HAG decomposes complex activities into atomic actions and models their temporal and spatial relationships. We propose a hierarchical graph representation that captures both the compositional structure of activities and the cooperative interactions between multiple agents. Our approach utilizes a combination of video analysis, object recognition, and human pose estimation to identify and classify individual actions. The framework then leverages graph neural networks to infer the overall activity based on the identified atomic actions and their relationships. Experimental results on a newly collected dataset demonstrate that HAG significantly outperforms existing methods in activity recognition accuracy and provides more detailed and interpretable activity descriptions. The HAG framework offers a promising approach for understanding complex human behaviors in unstructured environments, enabling more effective human-computer interaction.
**Keywords:** Activity Recognition, Home Environment, Compositional Action Understanding, Graph Neural Networks, Human-Computer Interaction.
