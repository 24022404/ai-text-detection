Objective: Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) methods have achieved remarkable successes across diverse domains, yet many algorithms suffer from bias introduced by their sampling schemes, value estimators, or policy updates. This paper presents Unbiased Deep Reinforcement Learning (UDRL), a general training framework designed to eliminate algorithm-specific bias and improve stability and sample efficiency in both existing and future DRL methods.

Methods: UDRL leverages importance-weighted gradient estimators and adaptive correction factors to adjust for distributional mismatches between behavior and target policies. The framework integrates seamlessly with on-policy and off-policy algorithms by wrapping standard loss functions with a bias‐correction layer. Theoretical analysis demonstrates that UDRL yields unbiased policy gradient estimates under mild assumptions, without increasing computational complexity. Key components include a dynamic replay weighting scheme, variance control via clipped importance ratios, and a modular API that supports arbitrary DRL backbones.

Results: We evaluate UDRL on benchmark control tasks in OpenAI Gym (MuJoCo) and Atari 2600 environments. Across six state-of-the-art algorithms (PPO, SAC, DQN, A2C, TD3, Rainbow), UDRL consistently reduces estimation bias, accelerates convergence by 20–35%, and achieves up to 15% higher final rewards compared to baseline implementations. Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of each bias‐correction module and highlight robust performance gains even in high-variance settings.

Conclusion: UDRL provides a unified, plug-and-play solution to bias in deep reinforcement learning, offering both theoretical guarantees and practical improvements. Its algorithm-agnostic design ensures broad applicability, paving the way for more reliable and efficient DRL in robotics, autonomous systems, and beyond.

Keywords: Deep Reinforcement Learning; Unbiased Training; Bias Correction; Importance Weighting; Algorithm-Agnostic Framework; Sample Efficiency.
