This paper presents a novel approach to network clustering using Kernel-Autoregressive Moving Average (ARMA) modeling and the Grassmannian framework, with a specific application to brain-network analysis. The objective is to identify clusters of highly interconnected nodes within complex networks, which is crucial for understanding brain function and dysfunction. Our method leverages the Kernel-ARMA model to capture nonlinear relationships between nodes, and the Grassmannian framework to efficiently compute and analyze the clustering structure. The results show that our approach outperforms existing clustering algorithms in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly in the presence of noise and missing data. The key findings highlight the importance of incorporating nonlinear dynamics and geometric constraints into network clustering, leading to improved insights into brain-network organization and function. This research contributes to the development of advanced network analysis techniques, with potential applications in neuroscience, social network analysis, and other fields involving complex network systems. Key keywords: network clustering, Kernel-ARMA modeling, Grassmannian framework, brain-network analysis, nonlinear dynamics, geometric constraints.
