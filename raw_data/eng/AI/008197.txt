Adversarial training has emerged as a robust defense mechanism against adversarial attacks in machine learning. However, evaluating the true effectiveness of adversarial training remains challenging, particularly due to the inherent variability in classification accuracy across different training runs and attack configurations. This paper presents a rigorous statistical framework for analyzing classification accuracies obtained through adversarial training. We introduce novel statistical tests and confidence intervals tailored to assess the significance of observed accuracy differences, accounting for the impact of hyperparameters and attack parameters. Our approach enables a precise comparison of various adversarial training strategies and provides a more reliable assessment of their performance than traditional point estimates. We demonstrate the utility of our framework through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, revealing nuanced insights into the behavior of adversarial training under different attack scenarios. Key findings include statistically significant differences in robustness depending on the choice of adversary and the importance of considering statistical power when drawing conclusions about the efficacy of defenses. This methodology facilitates more informed design and evaluation of robust machine learning models. Keywords: Adversarial Training, Statistical Analysis, Classification Accuracy, Robustness, Hypothesis Testing, Machine Learning Security.
