Causal inference is a critical area of research in data science and machine learning, enabling the identification of cause-and-effect relationships from observational data. However, expressing and validating causal assumptions remains a significant challenge, often leading to biased or incorrect conclusions. This paper introduces *DoWhy*, a Python library designed to address these challenges by providing a unified framework for causal inference. *DoWhy* simplifies the process of defining causal models, specifying assumptions, and validating them through robustness checks. The library integrates state-of-the-art methods for causal estimation, including propensity score matching, instrumental variables, and counterfactual analysis, while emphasizing transparency and interpretability. Experimental results demonstrate that *DoWhy* effectively reduces errors in causal inference by systematically testing assumptions and providing actionable insights. The framework is evaluated on both synthetic and real-world datasets, showcasing its ability to handle complex causal relationships and improve the reliability of causal conclusions. By bridging the gap between theoretical causal reasoning and practical implementation, *DoWhy* offers a valuable tool for researchers and practitioners in fields such as healthcare, economics, and social sciences. This work contributes to advancing causal inference methodologies, making them more accessible and robust for real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: causal inference, causal assumptions, DoWhy, robustness checks, counterfactual analysis, observational data, Python library.
