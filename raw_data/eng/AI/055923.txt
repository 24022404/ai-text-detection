This paper addresses the challenges inherent in modeling time-series data characterized by structural breaks—abrupt changes in underlying dynamics that often degrade predictive accuracy in traditional learning algorithms. We present an in-depth theoretical analysis and empirical evaluation of second order optimization techniques tailored for time-series scenarios with such non-stationarity. Our approach leverages curvature information from the observed data to adapt dynamically to regime shifts, thereby enhancing model responsiveness and stability. We provide formal proofs establishing the convergence and robustness of our methods in the presence of multiple structural breaks. To validate our approach, we conduct extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets from finance and sensor monitoring domains, comparing the performance of our proposed algorithms with first order and baseline techniques. Results demonstrate significant improvements in detection accuracy, adaptation speed, and forecasting performance. The findings suggest that incorporating second order information yields models better equipped to handle sudden structural changes, presenting a notable advancement in time-series learning for non-stationary environments. Potential applications include economic forecasting, anomaly detection, and adaptive control systems. Key keywords: time-series, structural breaks, second order optimization, non-stationary data, adaptive learning, forecasting.
