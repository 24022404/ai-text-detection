This paper addresses the challenge of policy discrepancy in return-based Deep Q-Networks (DQNs), a critical issue affecting the stability and performance of reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms. The objective is to quantitatively and qualitatively measure the divergence between the target policy and the behavior policy during training, which often leads to suboptimal learning outcomes. To achieve this, we propose a novel framework that integrates a discrepancy metric into the return-based DQN architecture. This metric evaluates the alignment between the expected returns of the target and behavior policies, providing insights into the learning dynamics. Our approach leverages a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical experiments to validate the effectiveness of the proposed metric. Results demonstrate that the framework significantly reduces policy discrepancy, leading to improved convergence rates and higher cumulative rewards compared to traditional DQN variants. Furthermore, the qualitative analysis reveals patterns in policy divergence that can guide future improvements in RL algorithm design. The implications of this research extend to applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and decision-making tasks, where stable and efficient policy learning is essential. This work contributes to the broader understanding of RL training dynamics and offers a practical tool for enhancing the robustness of return-based methods.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Deep Q-Network, Policy Discrepancy, Return-Based Methods, Algorithm Stability, Decision-Making.
