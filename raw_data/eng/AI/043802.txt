Adam, a widely adopted optimization algorithm in deep learning, combines the benefits of adaptive learning rates with momentum. This paper revisits Adam through a novel lens, framing its update rules as a weighted average of past gradients. By explicitly analyzing these weights, we uncover previously unobserved properties that contribute to Adam's empirical success and potential limitations. Our analysis reveals that Adam's weighting scheme can, under certain circumstances, lead to an over-reliance on early gradients, potentially hindering convergence in later stages of training. To mitigate this issue, we propose a modification to Adam's weight decay mechanism, dynamically adjusting the influence of past gradients based on the current training epoch. Experimental results on various benchmark datasets, including image classification and natural language processing tasks, demonstrate that our modified Adam variant consistently outperforms the original algorithm, particularly in scenarios requiring fine-grained optimization. This work provides a deeper understanding of Adam's internal dynamics and offers a practical improvement for enhanced performance and robustness. Keywords: Adam, Optimization, Deep Learning, Gradient Descent, Momentum, Weight Decay.
