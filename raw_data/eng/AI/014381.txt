This paper addresses the challenge of sign language recognition by proposing a novel approach that integrates pose estimation with advanced machine learning models. The primary objective is to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of recognizing sign languages, facilitating better communication for the hearing impaired. Our method employs Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) to capture spatial dependencies and dynamic movements inherent in sign language gestures, utilizing pose data as a foundation. Additionally, we integrate Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) to understand contextual nuances and temporal sequences in sign language expressions. Through the combination of these two models, our approach effectively captures both the spatial configurations and sequential patterns of signs. Experimental results demonstrate that our system outperforms existent methods, achieving higher recognition accuracy across various sign language datasets. The improved performance highlights the potential for real-time applications in assistive technologies. The proposed method advances the field of sign language recognition by providing a robust and scalable solution capable of being extended to other gesture-based communication forms. Key contributions include the hybrid use of GCN for spatial analysis and BERT for temporal context, showcasing their complementary strengths. This research holds significant implications for enhancing accessibility and fostering inclusive communication technologies. Keywords: sign language recognition, pose estimation, Graph Convolutional Networks, GCN, BERT, assistive technology.
