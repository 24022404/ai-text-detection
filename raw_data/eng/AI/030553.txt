This paper addresses the challenge of multi-person 3D pose estimation under severe occlusion, a critical problem in computer vision with applications in surveillance, human-computer interaction, and augmented reality. Existing methods often struggle to accurately estimate poses when individuals are heavily occluded, leading to significant performance degradation. To tackle this issue, we propose a novel framework leveraging gated convolutional networks, which dynamically focus on visible body parts while suppressing irrelevant occluded regions. Our approach integrates a multi-stage architecture that progressively refines pose predictions by combining local and global contextual information. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art techniques, achieving higher accuracy and robustness in occluded scenarios. The results highlight the effectiveness of gated convolutions in handling complex occlusion patterns, offering a significant improvement over traditional approaches. This research contributes a scalable and efficient solution for real-world applications where occlusion is prevalent, paving the way for more reliable 3D pose estimation systems.  

**Keywords**: 3D pose estimation, occlusion handling, gated convolution, multi-person tracking, computer vision.
