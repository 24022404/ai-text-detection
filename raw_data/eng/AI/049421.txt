This paper addresses the challenge of performing transductive inference in higher-order Markov Random Fields (MRFs), which are prevalent in capturing complex dependencies in structured data. We aim to develop an efficient inference framework that can handle the intricate discrete-continuous optimization problems inherent in such models.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel Discrete-Continuous Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) framework tailored for higher-order MRFs. This approach leverages the strengths of ADMM in decomposing complex optimization problems, enabling effective coordination between discrete and continuous variables. The proposed method systematically alternates between discrete label assignment and continuous parameter adjustment while observing the constraints imposed by the MRF structure.

Results/Findings: Our framework was evaluated on various benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. The proposed method effectively balances the trade-off between convergence speed and optimality, showcasing robust transductive inference capabilities across different higher-order MRF configurations.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of Discrete-Continuous ADMM represents a significant advancement in the field of structured prediction, offering a versatile and scalable solution for high-dimensional MRFs. This framework not only enhances the accuracy of transductive inference in complex graphical models but also opens avenues for its application in numerous domains, including computer vision, natural language processing, and network analysis. Our work lays the groundwork for future research in optimizing structured prediction tasks, and we anticipate its broad applicability in advanced computational settings.

Keywords: transductive inference, higher-order Markov Random Fields, discrete-continuous optimization, ADMM, structured prediction.
