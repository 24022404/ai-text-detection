**Abstract:**

Accurate mortality prediction is a critical component in healthcare for optimizing clinical decision-making and improving patient outcomes. Traditional models primarily rely on structured data such as laboratory results and vital signs, which often fail to fully capture the complexity of patient conditions reflected in narrative documentation. This paper presents a novel approach for mortality prediction by leveraging unstructured free-text clinical notes from electronic health records (EHRs). We develop and evaluate a deep learning-based natural language processing (NLP) framework capable of extracting meaningful features from raw clinical narratives without the need for extensive manual annotation or predefined templates. Our method incorporates recurrent neural networks (RNNs) with attention mechanisms to model temporal and contextual relationships in the data. Experiments conducted on a large, real-world intensive care unit (ICU) dataset demonstrate that our approach achieves superior predictive performance compared to traditional structured-feature models, with significant improvements in area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC) and calibration metrics. The findings suggest that utilizing free-text clinical notes provides complementary insights beyond structured data, enabling more accurate and timely mortality risk stratification. Our work underscores the potential of unstructured data integration in clinical predictive analytics and paves the way for more holistic, data-driven healthcare decision support systems. **Keywords:** mortality prediction, free-text clinical notes, unstructured data, deep learning, natural language processing, electronic health records, healthcare analytics.
