Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for analyzing graph-structured data, with applications ranging from social networks to molecular modeling. However, designing optimal GNN architectures for specific tasks remains a challenging and time-consuming process, often requiring extensive domain expertise and computational resources. This paper addresses the problem of simplifying architecture search for GNNs by introducing an automated and efficient framework. Our approach leverages a novel neural architecture search (NAS) algorithm tailored for GNNs, which combines reinforcement learning with lightweight surrogate models to reduce search complexity. The proposed method systematically explores the architecture space, optimizing for both performance and computational efficiency. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our framework achieves competitive accuracy while significantly reducing search time compared to existing methods. Furthermore, the discovered architectures generalize well across diverse tasks, highlighting the robustness of the approach. This work contributes to the field by providing a scalable and accessible solution for GNN architecture design, enabling broader adoption and application of GNNs in real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Graph Neural Networks, Neural Architecture Search, Reinforcement Learning, Automated Machine Learning, Graph-Structured Data.
