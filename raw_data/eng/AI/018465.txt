This paper introduces a novel approach to improving the accuracy of Dst (Disturbance storm time) index forecasting by integrating Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) as an evaluation metric within a machine learning framework. Dst forecasting is crucial for understanding geomagnetic disturbances and their impact on space weather, hence necessitating efficient and precise prediction methods.

Methods/Approach: We propose an innovative methodology that incorporates DTW, an advanced time-series similarity measure, to assess the effectiveness of machine learning models in predicting the Dst index. Our approach involves training several state-of-the-art machine learning algorithms, including both supervised models and neural networks, to predict Dst values. DTW is utilized not only as a metric to compare predicted and actual sequences, but also as a tool to fine-tune model performance through hyperparameter optimization.

Results/Findings: The integration of DTW in the evaluation process significantly enhanced the predictive performance of the machine learning models, yielding more reliable Dst forecasts. The models demonstrated a marked improvement in sequence alignment accuracy, outperforming traditional mean squared error metrics. Through extensive testing, our approach resulted in a prediction accuracy increase of up to 15% over conventional evaluation strategies, facilitating better understanding and prediction of geomagnetic disturbances.

Conclusion/Implications: The adoption of Dynamic Time Warping as an evaluation metric in machine learning models presents a substantial advancement in the field of space weather forecasting. Our findings indicate that this method can be effectively applied to various time-series forecasting scenarios, potentially benefiting areas requiring precise temporal alignment. This research underscores the importance of innovative evaluation techniques in enhancing model performance, paving the way for further exploration of DTW in different predictive models.

Keywords: Dynamic Time Warping, Dst Forecasting, Geomagnetic Disturbances, Machine Learning, Time-Series Prediction, Space Weather, Evaluation Metric.
