This study aims to develop a Bayesian inversion framework for calibrating generative models used in geologic storage of carbon dioxide. The objective is to improve the accuracy of predictive models by quantifying uncertainties in model parameters and characterizing complex geological formations. A novel approach is proposed, combining Bayesian inference with deep generative models to invert for model parameters that match observed data. The methodology involves training a generative model on a dataset of synthetic formations, followed by Bayesian inversion to calibrate the model parameters against real-world data. Results show that the proposed approach can effectively quantify uncertainties in model predictions and improve the accuracy of CO2 storage capacity estimates. The study demonstrates the potential of Bayesian inversion of generative models for geologic carbon sequestration, highlighting its contributions to enhanced decision-making and risk assessment in this critical application. Key findings include improved model calibration, uncertainty quantification, and predictive accuracy, with implications for optimizing CO2 storage operations and reducing environmental risks. Relevant keywords: Bayesian inversion, generative models, geologic carbon storage, uncertainty quantification, carbon sequestration.
