This research addresses the challenging task of single image dehazing by proposing a novel classification-driven approach. The primary objective is to enhance image visibility and clarity in hazy conditions, which is crucial for various applications such as autonomous driving, remote sensing, and computer vision systems.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a classification-driven model that leverages supervised learning techniques to categorize image regions based on haze density and employs targeted dehazing algorithms tailored to each classification. Our approach integrates a convolutional neural network (CNN) to classify haze levels and dynamically applies adaptive dehazing methods to optimize image restoration. This methodology marks a departure from traditional one-size-fits-all dehazing techniques, allowing for more precise and effective haze removal.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our classification-driven model significantly outperforms state-of-the-art dehazing algorithms in terms of both qualitative and quantitative metrics. Enhanced image quality is verified through improved haze density classification accuracy and superior pixel-level restoration performance. The proposed method also showcases robustness across diverse scene types and varying haze conditions.

Conclusion/Implications: The research contributes a novel, classification-oriented framework for single image dehazing, offering improved visual restoration capabilities and potential for real-time applications in computer vision environments. This approach not only advances the field of image processing by addressing haze-specific challenges but also highlights the efficacy of integrating classification processes in enhancing dehazing performance. Key implications include improved intelligence in autonomous systems and enhanced visual quality in surveillance operations.

Keywords: single image dehazing, classification-driven, convolutional neural network, haze density, image restoration, computer vision.
