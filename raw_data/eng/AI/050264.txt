This paper addresses the challenge of moving object detection in event-based vision systems, which differ fundamentally from traditional frame-based cameras by capturing asynchronous, high-temporal-resolution changes in the scene. We propose an efficient and robust approach leveraging k-means clustering to segment spatiotemporal event data for the identification of dynamic objects. Our method transforms the stream of event data into feature vectors that encapsulate both spatial locality and temporal characteristics, enabling the direct application of k-means clustering for unsupervised event grouping. Comprehensive experiments were conducted on publicly available event-based datasets, demonstrating the superiority of our approach in terms of detection accuracy, computational efficiency, and adaptability to diverse lighting and motion conditions compared to existing state-of-the-art techniques. The results indicate significant improvements in real-time performance, making the proposed method well-suited for deployment in resource-constrained applications such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and surveillance systems. Our research highlights the potential of unsupervised learning techniques like k-means clustering to enhance the capability of event-based vision systems for reliable moving object detection. Keywords: event-based vision, moving object detection, k-means clustering, dynamic scenes, unsupervised learning, real-time processing.
