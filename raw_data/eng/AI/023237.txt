This paper introduces a novel approach for learning generative models of tissue organization using supervised Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). The objective is to generate realistic and diverse synthetic tissue samples that mimic the complex structural arrangements observed in biological tissues. Our method employs a supervised GAN framework, where the discriminator is trained to distinguish between real and generated tissue samples, while simultaneously being guided by expert annotations of tissue components. This supervised learning approach ensures that the generated tissues not only exhibit visual realism but also accurately reflect the underlying biological structures. We evaluate our model on multiple datasets of histological images, demonstrating its ability to generate high-quality synthetic tissues with controlled variations in cellular density, spatial arrangement, and tissue morphology. Quantitative analysis, including Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and structural similarity (SSIM) metrics, reveals that our supervised GAN significantly outperforms unsupervised GANs and other generative models in capturing the intricate patterns of tissue organization. The generated synthetic tissues can be valuable for various applications, including training deep learning models for automated tissue analysis, simulating disease progression, and augmenting limited datasets for biomedical research. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, tissue organization, supervised learning, histological images, synthetic data, biomedical imaging.
