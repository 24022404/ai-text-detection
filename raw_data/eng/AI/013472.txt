This paper addresses the challenge of accurately detecting and recognizing texts in complex scene images, a fundamental problem in computer vision and pattern recognition. To enhance performance, we propose a novel approach for verisimilar image synthesis, creating high-fidelity, labeled scene text images that closely mimic real-world conditions. Our synthesis pipeline integrates context-aware rendering, advanced generative models, and domain adaptation techniques to capture variations in illumination, perspective, background clutter, and font styles. By augmenting existing training datasets with these realistic synthetic images, we improve the robustness and generalizability of state-of-the-art text detection and recognition algorithms. Experimental results demonstrate substantial gains in accuracy across multiple public benchmarks, outperforming conventional data augmentation and synthetic image generation methods. The findings highlight the importance of verisimilar synthetic data in bridging the gap between artificial and real-world scenes, facilitating the development of more reliable automated systems for scene text understanding. Our work contributes a scalable methodology for generating training data in domains where annotated images are scarce, with potential applications in document analysis, navigation aids, and mobile visual search. Keywords: verisimilar synthesis, scene text detection, text recognition, generative models, domain adaptation, image synthesis, computer vision.
