Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have achieved remarkable success in diverse computer vision tasks. However, the intricacies of their internal feature representations and the impact of architectural choices on performance remain areas requiring deeper exploration. This paper investigates the role of low-level feature details in CNNs and their contribution to overall network accuracy. We employ a novel feature visualization technique to analyze the activation patterns of different layers within several popular CNN architectures. Our findings reveal that seemingly minor architectural modifications, such as filter size and stride, can significantly alter the learned feature representations and subsequent classification performance. Furthermore, we demonstrate that preserving fine-grained details in early layers is crucial for accurate object recognition, particularly for datasets with high intra-class variation. We present a modified CNN architecture incorporating detail-preserving blocks, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets while maintaining computational efficiency. This research highlights the importance of careful architectural design and provides insights into the inner workings of CNNs, paving the way for more robust and interpretable models. Keywords: Convolutional Neural Networks, Feature Visualization, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, Image Recognition, Architecture Design.
