This paper investigates the application of meta-learning to enhance surrogate modeling in sequential decision making tasks. Traditional surrogate models often struggle to efficiently adapt to changing problem dynamics in real-time decision environments, limiting their effectiveness in optimization and policy learning. To address this challenge, we propose a novel meta-learning framework that enables surrogate models to rapidly adapt to new tasks with minimal data, leveraging prior experience across related decision problems. The approach utilizes neural-based meta-learners trained to capture high-level patterns across task distributions, enabling efficient transfer and adaptation during sequential optimization. Empirical evaluations are conducted on benchmark sequential decision making scenarios, including Bayesian optimization and reinforcement learning environments. Results demonstrate that our meta-learned surrogates achieve superior prediction accuracy and faster convergence compared to conventional surrogate models and standard transfer learning techniques. These improvements lead to more sample-efficient policy updates and more robust decision pipelines. The findings highlight the potential of integrating meta-learning with surrogate modeling to accelerate and strengthen sequential decision making across a range of information technology applications. Key contributions include a flexible meta-learning surrogate architecture, a task-adaptive training protocol, and comprehensive experimental validation. Keywords: meta-learning, surrogate models, sequential decision making, Bayesian optimization, reinforcement learning, task adaptation, neural networks.
