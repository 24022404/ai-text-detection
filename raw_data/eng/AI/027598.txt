Adversarial training has emerged as a critical defense mechanism against adversarial attacks in machine learning models, particularly in classification tasks. However, the statistical robustness of classification accuracies under adversarial training remains underexplored. This paper addresses the challenge of precisely quantifying and analyzing the performance of classifiers trained with adversarial techniques. We propose a novel statistical framework that evaluates classification accuracies under varying adversarial conditions, leveraging rigorous hypothesis testing and confidence interval estimation. Our approach integrates Monte Carlo simulations and bootstrapping methods to account for uncertainty and variability in adversarial scenarios. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our framework provides statistically significant insights into the trade-offs between robustness and accuracy, outperforming traditional evaluation methods. The findings reveal that adversarial training not only enhances model resilience but also introduces measurable statistical patterns in classification performance. This work contributes a robust analytical tool for researchers and practitioners to assess and improve adversarial training strategies, with implications for secure and reliable machine learning systems in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: adversarial training, classification accuracy, statistical analysis, machine learning robustness, hypothesis testing, Monte Carlo simulations.
