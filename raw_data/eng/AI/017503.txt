Objective: As deep neural networks (DNNs) proliferate across diverse applications, adapting a trained model to new architectures—whether for hardware constraints, efficiency gains, or evolving tasks—remains a complex and time-consuming challenge. This paper investigates systematic transformations between DNNs, enabling seamless conversion from a source architecture to a target architecture with minimal retraining.

Methods: We propose a unified framework combining network morphism, weight reparameterization, and knowledge distillation. First, an architecture mapping module aligns layers and parameter shapes between source and target networks. Next, a weight initialization scheme employs linear interpolation and orthogonal projection to transfer learned representations. Finally, a regularized fine-tuning step refines the transformed model on the original task data, enforcing fidelity to source performance.

Results: We validate our approach on image classification (CIFAR-10, ImageNet) and sequence modeling tasks using convolutional, residual, and transformer architectures. Our transformed networks incur under 1% accuracy loss relative to fully trained targets, reduce retraining time by up to 40%, and achieve model size reductions of up to 50%. Comparisons against standard transfer learning and pruning baselines show improved convergence speed and higher final accuracy.

Conclusion: This work delivers a practical toolset for DNN architecture adaptability, facilitating efficient model compression, edge deployment, and continual learning. By preserving task performance through principled transformations, it broadens the flexibility and lifespan of deep learning systems.  
Keywords: deep learning, network morphism, model transformation, weight reparameterization, transfer learning.
