Model compression has emerged as a critical technique to deploy deep learning models efficiently on resource-constrained devices. This paper introduces **LIT (Block-wise Intermediate Representation Training)**, a novel approach for model compression that leverages intermediate representations within neural networks to achieve significant reductions in model size and computational complexity. The proposed method divides the network into blocks and trains each block independently using intermediate feature representations, enabling targeted compression without compromising overall model performance. LIT employs a combination of pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation techniques, tailored to each block's unique characteristics, to optimize the compression process. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that LIT achieves up to **4× compression** with minimal accuracy loss, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods. Furthermore, LIT's modular design allows for seamless integration into various architectures, making it a versatile solution for real-world applications. The findings highlight the potential of block-wise intermediate representation training as a scalable and efficient strategy for model compression, paving the way for deploying advanced deep learning models on edge devices.  

**Keywords**: model compression, intermediate representation, block-wise training, pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, deep learning, edge computing.
