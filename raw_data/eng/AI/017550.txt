This paper addresses the vulnerability of face recognition systems in the physical world to adversarial attacks. Our objective is to design and develop meaningful adversarial stickers that can effectively evade face detection and recognition models in real-world scenarios. We propose a novel approach that utilizes a combination of computer vision and machine learning techniques to generate stickers that are not only adversarial but also visually appealing and meaningful. Our method involves training a generative model to produce stickers that can be worn on the face, allowing individuals to maintain their privacy and security in public spaces. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our approach, with a significant reduction in face recognition accuracy when our stickers are worn. Our findings have important implications for the development of more secure and privacy-preserving face recognition systems, and highlight the need for increased awareness of the potential risks associated with these technologies. Key contributions of this research include the development of a novel adversarial sticker generation method, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in real-world scenarios. Relevant keywords: face recognition, adversarial attacks, computer vision, machine learning, privacy, security.
