Graph Contrastive Learning (GCL) has emerged as a powerful technique for learning node embeddings in graph-structured data. However, existing GCL methods often rely on simple graph augmentations, potentially overlooking crucial structural information encoded in node distances. This paper introduces Distance-wise Graph Contrastive Learning (DGCL), a novel framework that leverages node distance to generate more informative contrastive views. DGCL constructs positive and negative samples based on the proximity and separation of nodes, respectively, enabling the model to learn more robust and distance-aware representations. We propose a novel distance encoding scheme to efficiently capture the structural relationships between nodes. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that DGCL consistently outperforms state-of-the-art GCL methods in node classification and clustering tasks. The results highlight the importance of considering node distance in GCL and showcase DGCL's ability to learn high-quality graph representations. Keywords: Graph Contrastive Learning, Node Embedding, Graph Neural Networks, Distance Encoding, Representation Learning.
