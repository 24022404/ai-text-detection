Boosting algorithms, particularly AdaBoost, have demonstrated impressive performance in classification tasks but are known to produce poorly calibrated probability estimates, which limits their effectiveness in applications that require reliable probability outputs, such as risk assessment and decision-making systems. This paper addresses the challenge of obtaining well-calibrated probabilities from boosting models. We systematically evaluate the calibration quality of raw boosting scores and investigate several post-processing techniques, including Platt Scaling, Isotonic Regression, and Logistic Correction, to improve probability estimation. Comparative experiments are conducted across multiple real-world datasets, measuring performance using standard metrics such as Brier score and log-loss. Results indicate that while boosting alone yields high classification accuracy, its probability outputs are generally overconfident or under-confident. Post-processing approaches, especially Platt Scaling and Isotonic Regression, significantly enhance calibration without sacrificing predictive accuracy. The study highlights best practices for practitioners seeking both accurate classifications and reliable probability estimates from boosting-based models. Our findings contribute to the development of trustworthy machine learning systems and enable broader deployment of boosted models in domains requiring quantifiable decision-making confidence. Key keywords: boosting, probability calibration, Platt Scaling, Isotonic Regression, AdaBoost, machine learning, classification reliability.
