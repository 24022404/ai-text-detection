Abstract:

This paper addresses the challenge of automatic video summarization by proposing a novel approach based entirely on self-attention mechanisms, leveraging the Universal Transformer architecture. Traditional video summarization methods often rely on recurrent or convolutional neural networks, which can struggle to capture long-range dependencies and temporal context efficiently. Our method, inspired by advances in natural language processing, eliminates recurrence and convolutions, instead utilizing multi-head self-attention to model both spatial and temporal relationships within video data. The Universal Transformer’s iterative refinement enables adaptive depth and improved representation of complex video structures. We conduct extensive experiments on benchmark video summarization datasets, demonstrating that our approach achieves superior performance in terms of precision, recall, and F-score when compared to state-of-the-art techniques. Furthermore, the model exhibits enhanced scalability and flexibility in handling varying video lengths. The findings highlight the effectiveness of self-attention and Transformer-based architectures for video understanding tasks. Our method opens new avenues for efficient, accurate, and interpretable video summarization applications in information retrieval, surveillance, and content management systems. 

Keywords: video summarization, self-attention, Universal Transformer, deep learning, temporal modeling, multi-head attention, video processing.
