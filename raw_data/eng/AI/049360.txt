Abstract:

This paper presents a novel approach to image processing by introducing Weighted Nonlocal Total Variation (WNLTV), a method aimed at enhancing image denoising and restoration. Traditional total variation (TV) methods are effective in preserving edges, but often fail to capture nonlocal similarities and can result in loss of fine details. Our proposed WNLTV model integrates adaptive weighting schemes into the nonlocal total variation framework, allowing for more accurate modeling of pixel relationships over larger spatial neighborhoods. The method employs a similarity-based weighting function to selectively enforce regularization based on both spatial distance and intensity similarity, improving the preservation of textures and subtle structures. Extensive experiments were conducted on both synthetic and real images to assess the performance of the proposed technique. Results demonstrate that WNLTV consistently outperforms classic local and nonlocal TV methods in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), structural similarity index (SSIM), and visual quality, especially in challenging noise and artifact scenarios. This research highlights the effectiveness of adaptive, weighted regularization in nonlocal frameworks, providing a valuable tool for various image processing applications, including medical imaging, photography enhancement, and remote sensing. Keywords: image processing, total variation, nonlocal methods, image denoising, weighted regularization, image restoration.
