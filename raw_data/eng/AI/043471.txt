Accurate disease classification from medical images is crucial for timely diagnosis and treatment. However, the lack of explainability in many deep learning models hinders clinical adoption. This paper presents a novel weakly-supervised approach to disease classification that simultaneously generates segmentation maps, enhancing explainability. Our method utilizes only image-level disease labels to train a convolutional neural network (CNN) with a custom loss function that encourages the network to identify and segment disease-related regions. The generated segmentation maps highlight the areas within the image that contribute most to the classification decision, providing visual explanations for the model's predictions. Experiments on chest X-ray datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves comparable or superior classification accuracy to existing methods while offering significantly improved interpretability. Furthermore, the generated segmentation maps correlate well with radiologist annotations, validating the clinical relevance of the explanations. This work contributes a practical and explainable AI solution for disease classification, potentially improving trust and acceptance among clinicians. Keywords: Explainable AI, Disease Classification, Weakly-Supervised Segmentation, Medical Imaging, Deep Learning, Interpretability.
