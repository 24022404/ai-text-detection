Forest biomass estimation is critical for understanding carbon sequestration and ecosystem dynamics. Traditional methods often rely on labor-intensive field surveys or limited-resolution satellite data, which can be time-consuming and costly. This paper proposes a novel approach to construct high-resolution forest biomass prediction maps using radar backscatter data. We introduce a sequential regression framework enhanced by a Conditional Generative Adversarial Network (cGAN) to model the complex relationship between radar signals and biomass. The cGAN-based model leverages its ability to capture spatial dependencies and generate realistic biomass distributions, addressing the challenges of noise and data sparsity in radar measurements. Our method is validated using synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating superior accuracy and robustness compared to conventional regression techniques. The results show significant improvements in biomass prediction accuracy, with a mean absolute error reduction of 15% over existing methods. This research contributes to the field of remote sensing by providing a scalable and efficient solution for large-scale forest biomass mapping, with potential applications in climate change monitoring and sustainable forest management. Key innovations include the integration of cGAN for sequential regression and the generation of high-resolution biomass maps from radar data. Keywords: forest biomass, radar backscatter, conditional generative adversarial network (cGAN), sequential regression, remote sensing, carbon monitoring.
