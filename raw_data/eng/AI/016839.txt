This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the robustness and interpretability of sparse coding and dictionary learning in high-dimensional data analysis. Traditional methods often struggle with noise sensitivity and lack confidence estimation in their representations, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel framework called Confident Kernel Sparse Coding (CKSC), which integrates kernel-based transformations with probabilistic confidence measures. Our approach leverages kernel functions to map data into a higher-dimensional space, enabling more expressive representations, while simultaneously estimating the confidence of each sparse code to improve reliability. We also introduce a corresponding dictionary learning algorithm that optimizes both reconstruction accuracy and confidence consistency. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that CKSC outperforms existing methods in terms of reconstruction error, classification accuracy, and robustness to noise. The proposed framework not only provides a more reliable representation of data but also offers insights into the uncertainty of sparse codes, making it particularly suitable for applications such as image processing, anomaly detection, and bioinformatics. This work contributes to the field by bridging the gap between sparse coding and probabilistic modeling, paving the way for more interpretable and trustworthy machine learning systems.  

**Keywords**: sparse coding, dictionary learning, kernel methods, confidence estimation, probabilistic modeling, high-dimensional data.
