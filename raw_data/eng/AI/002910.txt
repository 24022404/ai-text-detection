This paper presents a novel approach to language-guided navigation, leveraging cross-modal grounding and alternate adversarial learning to improve agent navigation in complex environments. The objective is to enable agents to effectively interpret and execute natural language instructions, enhancing their ability to navigate through unseen environments. Our method employs a cross-modal grounding framework, which establishes a connection between linguistic and visual representations, allowing the agent to better understand the environment and the instructions. Additionally, we introduce an alternate adversarial learning strategy, which promotes the agent to explore and learn from its mistakes, leading to more robust and efficient navigation. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods, achieving significant improvements in navigation success rates and efficiency. The key contributions of this research include the development of a cross-modal grounding framework and the introduction of alternate adversarial learning, which can be applied to various language-guided navigation tasks. Our work has important implications for applications such as robotics, virtual reality, and autonomous systems, and highlights the potential of language-guided navigation in complex environments. Key keywords: language-guided navigation, cross-modal grounding, alternate adversarial learning, robotics, virtual reality.
