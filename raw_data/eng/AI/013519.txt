The proliferation of manipulated facial videos poses a significant threat to online security and trust. This paper presents a novel approach to detect video face manipulation by leveraging an ensemble of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). Our method combines the strengths of multiple CNN architectures, each trained to identify distinct artifacts and inconsistencies introduced during the manipulation process. Specifically, we utilize a combination of MesoNet, XceptionNet, and a custom-designed CNN focusing on frequency domain analysis. The ensemble aggregates the predictions of these individual networks using a weighted averaging scheme optimized for high accuracy and robustness. Experimental results on benchmark datasets, including FaceForensics++ and DeepFake Detection Challenge (DFDC), demonstrate that our ensemble approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, surpassing existing methods in both detection accuracy and generalization ability. Furthermore, we investigate the effectiveness of different ensemble configurations and analyze the contribution of each CNN to the overall performance. The proposed method offers a practical and effective solution for detecting manipulated facial videos, contributing to enhanced online security and trust. Keywords: Face Manipulation Detection, Deepfakes, Convolutional Neural Networks, Ensemble Learning, Video Forensics, Cybersecurity.
