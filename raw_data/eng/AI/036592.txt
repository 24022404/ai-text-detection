This paper introduces *DeepBlindness*, a novel framework for fast and accurate blindness map estimation and blindness type classification from a single color image of outdoor scenes. The objective is to address the challenge of identifying and categorizing visual impairments, such as glare, haze, and low-light conditions, which degrade image quality and hinder computer vision applications. We propose a deep learning-based approach that leverages a multi-task convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture to simultaneously predict pixel-wise blindness maps and classify the type of blindness present in the image. The model is trained on a diverse dataset of outdoor scenes with annotated blindness regions and types, ensuring robustness across various environmental conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that *DeepBlindness* achieves state-of-the-art performance in both blindness map estimation and classification tasks, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The framework operates in real-time, making it suitable for integration into autonomous systems, surveillance, and augmented reality applications. This research contributes a scalable and efficient solution for enhancing visual perception in challenging outdoor environments, with potential implications for improving safety and reliability in AI-driven systems.  

**Keywords**: Blindness map estimation, blindness type classification, deep learning, computer vision, outdoor scene analysis, real-time processing.
