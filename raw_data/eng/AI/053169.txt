The proliferation of frame interpolation techniques has necessitated the need for robust benchmarks to evaluate their performance effectively. This study addresses the challenge of establishing a comprehensive assessment methodology by introducing subjective annotation augmented with artefact amplification to enhance the evaluation process of frame interpolation algorithms.

Methods/Approach: We developed a novel framework that combines subjective human annotation with artefact amplification techniques. The approach amplifies visual artefacts often overlooked by traditional benchmarks, enabling precise qualitative assessment. Participants were tasked with evaluating interpolated frames that underwent an artefact amplification process, thus providing more insightful subjective feedback on algorithm performance.

Results/Findings: The application of artefact amplification in subjective annotation significantly heightened the sensitivity of evaluations, revealing subtleties in frame interpolation quality that standard approaches might miss. Our findings highlight the disparity between current objective metrics and human perception, uncovering nuances critical for the development of higher-quality interpolation techniques.

Conclusion/Implications: This research presents a significant advancement in frame interpolation benchmarking by integrating subjective annotations with artefact amplification. The proposed method offers a more nuanced evaluation framework, bridging the gap between algorithmic performance and perceptual quality. This work is instrumental for researchers and developers in the field of computer vision and visual effects, serving as a resource for improving and validating interpolation algorithms. Key Keywords: frame interpolation, subjective annotation, artefact amplification, benchmarking, visual evaluation.
