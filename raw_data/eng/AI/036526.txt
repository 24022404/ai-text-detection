Motion segmentation in videos captured from moving cameras is a challenging problem in computer vision, crucial for tasks like autonomous driving and robotic navigation. This paper introduces a novel probabilistic model for causal motion segmentation, addressing the limitations of existing methods that often struggle with dynamic backgrounds and complex motions. Our approach combines optical flow analysis with a Bayesian framework to infer the underlying causal structure of motion, distinguishing between camera ego-motion, independent object movements, and noise. The model incorporates temporal dependencies to achieve robust and consistent segmentation over time, enabling a causal interpretation of motion events. We evaluate our method on a variety of challenging video sequences, demonstrating superior performance compared to state-of-the-art techniques in terms of segmentation accuracy and robustness to camera shake. The results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed probabilistic model in capturing the causal relationships between different motion components, leading to improved scene understanding. Keywords: Motion Segmentation, Causal Inference, Moving Camera, Optical Flow, Bayesian Model, Video Analysis.
