This paper explores the application of quantum computing to solve correspondence problems on point sets, a fundamental challenge in computer vision and pattern recognition. We introduce a novel quantum algorithm based on Grover's search and quantum annealing techniques to efficiently identify accurate point correspondences despite noise and outliers. Our approach leverages the inherent parallelism of quantum computation to significantly reduce the computational complexity compared to classical methods. Through simulations and experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets, we demonstrate that our quantum algorithm achieves a quadratic speedup in finding optimal correspondences, particularly as the size of the point sets increases. The results show improved accuracy and robustness compared to traditional iterative closest point (ICP) algorithms and other classical optimization techniques. This research contributes a practical quantum solution for correspondence problems, paving the way for advancements in 3D reconstruction, object recognition, and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM). Keywords: Quantum Computing, Point Set Registration, Correspondence Problem, Grover's Algorithm, Quantum Annealing, Computer Vision.
