Dictionary learning is a powerful technique for representing signals as sparse linear combinations of basis elements learned from data. However, the computational and storage costs associated with dictionary learning can be prohibitive, especially for high-dimensional data and large dictionaries. This paper introduces a novel approach to compressed dictionary learning that significantly reduces these costs while maintaining competitive representation accuracy. Our method integrates techniques from compressive sensing and structured dictionary design to learn dictionaries that are both compact and efficient for sparse coding. Specifically, we leverage randomized sketching to reduce the dimensionality of the training data, followed by a structured dictionary update that promotes sparsity and reduces storage overhead. We demonstrate, through extensive experiments on image and audio datasets, that our compressed dictionary learning method achieves comparable or better performance than traditional dictionary learning algorithms, while requiring significantly less memory and computation time. These results highlight the potential of our approach for deploying dictionary learning in resource-constrained environments and for scaling to very large datasets. Keywords: Dictionary Learning, Compressed Sensing, Sparse Coding, Dimensionality Reduction, Randomized Sketching, Structured Dictionaries.
