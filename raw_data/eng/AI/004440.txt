This paper addresses the challenge of effectively clustering high-dimensional data from multiple viewpoints, a fundamental task in data analysis and machine learning. Traditional methods often struggle with integrating diverse data views, which can limit clustering accuracy and relevance. This study aims to enhance clustering performance by leveraging deep learning alongside collaborative training strategies.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel Deep Embedded Multi-view Clustering (DEMC) framework that utilizes deep neural networks to embed multi-view data into a unified latent space. The framework incorporates a collaborative training mechanism, where each view-specific network collaborates in optimizing a shared clustering objective. This approach facilitates a more cohesive understanding of complex data structures by integrating information from different perspectives.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations on several benchmark datasets demonstrate that our DEMC model significantly outperforms conventional multi-view clustering methods. Notably, the proposed framework achieved enhanced accuracy and robustness across various clustering tasks, showcasing its effectiveness in exploiting the complementary information inherent in multi-view data. Comparative analysis highlights improved clustering quality, with marked gains in both intra-cluster consistency and inter-cluster separation.

Conclusion/Implications: The research introduces an innovative clustering paradigm through deep embedded multi-view processing with collaborative training, contributing to both theoretical and practical advancements in multi-view data analysis. The insights gained from this study have the potential to advance applications in diverse fields such as bioinformatics, image processing, and social network analysis, where multi-view data is prevalent. Future work may explore extending the framework to handle dynamic or evolving multi-view datasets.

Keywords: multi-view clustering, deep learning, collaborative training, data embedding, high-dimensional data, neural networks.
