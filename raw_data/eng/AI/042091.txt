This paper introduces a novel approach to training deep neural networks, dubbed "Go Wide, Then Narrow," which efficiently trains deep thin networks by leveraging the strengths of both wide and narrow architectures. The objective is to address the challenge of training deep thin networks, which often suffer from vanishing gradients and slow convergence. Our method employs a two-stage training process, where a wide network is first trained to capture a broad range of features, and then pruned to form a thin network that is fine-tuned for the specific task. We utilize a combination of knowledge distillation and sparse regularization to preserve the essential information from the wide network. Our results show that this approach outperforms traditional training methods for deep thin networks, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets. The key findings include significant improvements in training speed and accuracy, making our method an attractive solution for applications where computational resources are limited. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to efficiently train deep thin networks, enabling the deployment of accurate models in resource-constrained environments, with potential applications in areas such as edge AI, mobile devices, and embedded systems, and relevant keywords include deep learning, neural networks, model pruning, knowledge distillation, and efficient training.
