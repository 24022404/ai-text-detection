Graph topology inference is a critical task in machine learning, enabling the extraction of relational structures from complex data. This paper presents a comprehensive benchmarking framework to evaluate the performance of state-of-the-art graph topology inference methods. The objective is to address the lack of standardized evaluation protocols, which hinders fair comparisons and progress in the field. We propose a systematic approach, leveraging synthetic and real-world datasets to assess the accuracy, scalability, and robustness of various algorithms, including spectral clustering, graph neural networks, and probabilistic models. Our results demonstrate significant variations in performance across methods, highlighting the strengths and limitations of each under different conditions, such as noise levels and graph sparsity. Key findings reveal that hybrid approaches combining probabilistic reasoning with deep learning outperform traditional methods in most scenarios. The study concludes with practical insights for selecting appropriate inference techniques based on application requirements and data characteristics. This work contributes a reproducible benchmarking framework, fostering advancements in graph-based machine learning and its applications in social networks, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems.  

**Keywords**: graph topology inference, machine learning, benchmarking, graph neural networks, spectral clustering, probabilistic models.
