This paper proposes a novel approach to contrastive representation learning, introducing Center-wise Local Image Mixture (CLIM), a method designed to enhance the quality of learned representations by leveraging local image mixtures in a center-wise manner. The objective is to improve the robustness and generalizability of representations learned through contrastive learning, a crucial task in computer vision. Our approach employs a unique mixture strategy that combines local image regions around multiple centers, promoting the model to capture diverse and complementary features. The results show that CLIM outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods on several benchmarks, demonstrating its effectiveness in learning discriminative and transferable representations. Key findings include significant improvements in image classification, object detection, and segmentation tasks, highlighting the potential of CLIM for real-world applications. By emphasizing local feature diversity and center-wise mixture, CLIM contributes to the advancement of contrastive learning techniques, offering a promising direction for future research in representation learning, self-supervised learning, and computer vision. Relevant keywords: contrastive learning, representation learning, image mixture, computer vision, self-supervised learning.
