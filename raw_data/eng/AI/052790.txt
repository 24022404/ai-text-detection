This paper proposes a novel Transformation Consistent Self-ensembling Model for semi-supervised medical image segmentation, aiming to improve the accuracy and robustness of segmentation tasks with limited labeled data. Our approach combines self-ensembling techniques with transformation consistency to leverage unlabeled data and reduce the need for extensive manual annotation. The model is designed to learn from both labeled and unlabeled images, utilizing a consistency loss function to ensure that the predictions are invariant to random transformations. Experimental results demonstrate that our model outperforms state-of-the-art semi-supervised methods, achieving significant improvements in segmentation accuracy and dice similarity coefficient. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a transformation consistency constraint and a self-ensembling framework, which enables the model to capture complex anatomical structures and generalize well to unseen data. This work has important implications for medical image analysis, enabling more accurate and efficient segmentation of images, and potentially leading to better clinical decision-making. Key keywords: semi-supervised learning, medical image segmentation, self-ensembling, transformation consistency, deep learning.
