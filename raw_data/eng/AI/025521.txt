This paper investigates the utility-based dueling bandit problem through the lens of partial monitoring, a general framework for sequential decision-making under partial feedback. Traditional multi-armed bandit algorithms assume explicit reward signals, while dueling bandits only provide relative feedback via pairwise arm comparisons, complicating optimal strategy identification. We formalize utility-based dueling bandits as a specific instance of partial monitoring games, characterizing their feedback and loss structure within this broader paradigm. Our approach enables the application and adaptation of established partial monitoring algorithms to the dueling bandit setting. We analyze the theoretical performance bounds and demonstrate that this formulation bridges gaps between regret minimization in bandit problems and partial monitoring tasks. Experimental results on both synthetic and real-world datasets validate the effectiveness of our proposed algorithms, showcasing improved regret performance and robustness compared to existing dueling bandit strategies. The findings highlight the potential of leveraging partial monitoring theory to design more efficient online learning algorithms for applications involving preference elicitation and ranking optimization. Keywords: dueling bandits, partial monitoring, online learning, regret minimization, preference learning, bandit algorithms.
