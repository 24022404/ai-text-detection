This paper explores a data fusion methodology for generating high-fidelity 3D models of cultural heritage objects, leveraging the complementary strengths of laser scanning, structured light, and photogrammetry. The objective is to create accurate and complete geometric representations suitable for preservation, restoration, and digital archiving. The proposed approach integrates data acquired from each technique through a multi-stage registration and fusion pipeline. Initially, pairwise registration using Iterative Closest Point (ICP) variants aligns the datasets. Subsequently, a volumetric fusion technique, based on weighted averaging, merges the registered data, minimizing noise and maximizing detail capture. The methodology was applied to several cultural heritage artifacts, demonstrating improved accuracy and completeness compared to models generated using individual techniques. Quantitative evaluation, using ground truth measurements and comparative analysis with existing models, confirms the effectiveness of the fusion approach. The results indicate that this data fusion strategy significantly enhances the quality of 3D models, offering valuable resources for cultural heritage documentation and research. Keywords: Data fusion, 3D modeling, laser scanning, structured light, photogrammetry, cultural heritage, digital preservation.
