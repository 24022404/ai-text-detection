**Abstract**

Weakly supervised semantic segmentation aims to delineate object regions in images using limited supervision, often in the form of image-level labels, rather than costly pixel-wise annotations. In this paper, we propose a novel framework that fully leverages classifier networks to enhance the quality of localization cues for weakly supervised semantic segmentation. Our approach introduces modified cues, generated by systematically adapting the output of classifiers, thereby improving the accuracy and spatial consistency of the initial segmentation seeds. We integrate these refined cues into a segmentation network, enabling end-to-end training that progressively sharpens object boundaries and suppresses noise. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms existing weakly supervised approaches, achieving higher mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and better visual segmentation quality. Furthermore, our framework is efficient, requiring minimal overhead compared to conventional methods. The proposed approach broadens the practical applicability of weakly supervised learning by maximizing the utility of classifier information, paving the way for more accurate semantic segmentation in scenarios where detailed annotations are not feasible.

**Keywords:** weakly supervised segmentation, semantic segmentation, classifier cues, deep learning, image recognition, computer vision
