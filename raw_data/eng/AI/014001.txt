The paper addresses the critical issue of privacy in data publishing by introducing a novel approach called Distribution-Preserving k-Anonymity. The objective is to enhance privacy protection while maintaining the statistical characteristics of the original dataset. Traditional k-Anonymity methods often result in significant data distortion, which impairs the utility of the anonymized data. To overcome this, we propose a new algorithm that not only ensures each individual in the dataset is indistinguishable from at least k-1 others but also carefully preserves the original data distribution. Our method involves an innovative optimization technique that iteratively modifies the dataset to achieve k-Anonymity constraints while minimizing distributional discrepancies. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms existing methods in balancing data utility and privacy, retaining key statistical properties such as mean and variance. The implications of this research are substantial, as it enables organizations to share privacy-preserved datasets without compromising data quality, facilitating more accurate data analysis and decision-making processes. Key contributions of this work include the development of a distribution-preserving algorithm, comparison with established k-Anonymity techniques, and validation through extensive testing on various benchmark datasets. Keywords for this study include privacy preservation, k-Anonymity, data distribution, anonymity algorithms, and data utility.
