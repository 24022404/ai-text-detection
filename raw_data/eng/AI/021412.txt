This paper addresses the challenge of recovering semantic attributes from data with weak supervision, a critical task in enhancing interpretability and usability in various information technology applications. Traditional methods often rely on fully labeled datasets, which are costly and time-intensive to obtain. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel weakly supervised learning framework that leverages limited annotations to infer semantic attributes effectively. Our approach integrates a multi-task learning architecture with attention mechanisms, enabling the model to focus on relevant features while minimizing noise from weakly labeled data. We evaluate the proposed framework on benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in attribute recovery accuracy compared to existing weakly supervised methods. The results highlight the model's robustness and scalability across diverse domains, including image analysis and natural language processing. This research contributes a practical and efficient solution for semantic attribute recovery, offering potential applications in areas such as content recommendation, automated tagging, and human-computer interaction. Key innovations include the integration of attention mechanisms and a multi-task learning paradigm, which collectively enhance the model's ability to generalize from limited supervision. Keywords: weakly supervised learning, semantic attributes, attention mechanisms, multi-task learning, interpretability.
