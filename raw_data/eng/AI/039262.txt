This paper presents a novel approach for semantic video analysis by leveraging deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) enhanced through representation warping. Addressing the challenges of temporal coherence and accurate object recognition in video sequences, we propose a framework that dynamically aligns intermediate CNN representations across consecutive frames. Our method introduces a representation warping module that predicts pixel-wise correspondence using low-cost motion estimation, enabling the network to aggregate semantic features temporally and spatially. Experiments were conducted on benchmark video segmentation and activity recognition datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in accuracy and computational efficiency over traditional frame-by-frame and static fusion CNN models. The results highlight the effectiveness of exploiting temporal continuity via learned feature alignment, leading to more robust and consistent video-level predictions. This approach opens new possibilities for efficient, scalable video understanding in applications such as autonomous driving, surveillance, and content indexing. Key contributions of this work include the development of an end-to-end trainable architecture for representation warping in semantic video CNNs, thorough empirical validation, and a discussion on the broader implications for real-time video analytics. Keywords: semantic video analysis, convolutional neural networks, representation warping, temporal coherence, video segmentation, activity recognition.
