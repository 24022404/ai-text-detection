The increasing reliance on machine learning models in critical applications has highlighted the vulnerability of these systems to adversarial attacks. This paper investigates the concept of *semantic adversarial examples*, a novel class of adversarial inputs that exploit semantic features rather than pixel-level perturbations to deceive models. The objective of this research is to explore how semantic manipulations, such as altering object textures or contextual backgrounds, can lead to misclassification in deep learning models. We propose a systematic approach to generate semantic adversarial examples by leveraging generative adversarial networks (GANs) and semantic segmentation techniques. Our experiments evaluate the robustness of state-of-the-art image classification models against these attacks, demonstrating significant drops in accuracy under semantic perturbations. Results reveal that semantic adversarial examples are not only effective but also more interpretable and realistic compared to traditional adversarial attacks. This work contributes to the field by introducing a new dimension to adversarial robustness research, emphasizing the importance of semantic understanding in model training and defense mechanisms. The findings have implications for improving the reliability of AI systems in real-world scenarios, particularly in domains like autonomous driving and medical imaging.  

**Keywords**: adversarial examples, semantic perturbations, generative adversarial networks, deep learning robustness, interpretability, machine learning security.
