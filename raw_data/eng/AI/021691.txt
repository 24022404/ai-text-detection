This paper investigates the critical parameters that influence the scalability of distributed learning systems, particularly when utilizing large batches and asynchronous updates. The primary objective is to identify the key factors that impact the performance and efficiency of distributed machine learning algorithms in large-scale environments. We propose a novel approach that combines batch size optimization with asynchronous update strategies, leveraging advancements in parallel computing and communication protocols. Our methodology involves a comprehensive analysis of the interplay between batch size, update frequency, and network communication overhead. The results show significant improvements in training time and model accuracy, outperforming traditional synchronous update methods. Key findings indicate that careful tuning of batch size, update intervals, and communication schedules can lead to substantial scalability gains. This research contributes to the development of more efficient and scalable distributed learning systems, with potential applications in large-scale AI model training, edge computing, and real-time data processing. The proposed approach has significant implications for the design of future distributed learning architectures, enabling faster and more accurate model training on large datasets. Relevant keywords: distributed learning, large batches, asynchronous updates, scalability, parallel computing, deep learning.
