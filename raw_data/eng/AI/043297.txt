This paper explores the potential of leveraging Conditional Generative Adversarial Networks (cGANs) to enhance knowledge distillation and transfer learning in image classification and regression tasks. The primary objective is to address the challenge of limited labeled data by generating synthetic samples that preserve the underlying data distribution and semantic features. We propose a novel framework that integrates cGAN-generated samples into the training pipeline, enabling the distillation of knowledge from a pre-trained teacher model to a lightweight student model. The approach involves training a cGAN to produce high-quality, task-specific synthetic images, which are then used to fine-tune the student model. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in classification accuracy and regression performance across multiple benchmark datasets, outperforming traditional data augmentation techniques. The proposed method also exhibits robustness in scenarios with scarce labeled data, making it particularly suitable for real-world applications. Key contributions include a scalable and efficient knowledge transfer framework, a comparative analysis of cGAN-generated samples versus real data, and insights into the interplay between synthetic data quality and model performance. This work highlights the potential of cGANs as a powerful tool for enhancing machine learning models in resource-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: Conditional Generative Adversarial Networks (cGANs), knowledge distillation, transfer learning, image classification, regression, synthetic data, data augmentation.
