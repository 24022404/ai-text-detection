This paper introduces BRP-NAS, a novel prediction-based Neural Architecture Search (NAS) framework that leverages Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to efficiently explore the vast architecture space. The objective is to develop a method that can accurately predict the performance of neural architectures without requiring extensive training, thereby reducing the computational cost associated with traditional NAS methods. BRP-NAS employs a GCN-based encoder to learn compact representations of neural architectures, which are then used to predict their performance on a given task. The results show that BRP-NAS achieves state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets, outperforming existing NAS methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The key findings highlight the effectiveness of GCNs in capturing the structural properties of neural architectures and the importance of prediction-based approaches in NAS. This research contributes to the development of more efficient and effective NAS methods, with potential applications in automated machine learning, computer vision, and natural language processing. Key keywords: Neural Architecture Search, Graph Convolutional Networks, Prediction-based NAS, Automated Machine Learning.
