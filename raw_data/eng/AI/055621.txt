This paper introduces a novel end-to-end image processing and perception framework, dubbed "Dirty Pixels," which revolutionizes the way images are processed and understood by machines. The objective is to develop a system that can learn to perceive and process images in a more human-like manner, by jointly optimizing image processing and perception tasks. Our approach leverages a deep learning-based architecture that integrates image processing techniques, such as denoising and super-resolution, with high-level perception tasks, like object recognition and scene understanding. The results show significant improvements in image quality and perception accuracy, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in various benchmarks. Our framework also demonstrates the ability to generalize to unseen datasets and scenarios, making it a promising solution for real-world applications. The key contributions of this work include the introduction of a new loss function that balances image processing and perception objectives, and the development of a novel attention mechanism that selectively focuses on relevant image regions. This research has important implications for computer vision, robotics, and autonomous systems, and paves the way for future research in end-to-end image processing and perception. Key keywords: image processing, computer vision, deep learning, perception, attention mechanisms.
