Estimating causal effects from observational data is a central challenge in fields such as healthcare, economics, and social sciences. Traditional machine learning methods, while powerful, often falter when faced with limited labeled data or varying data distributions across domains. This paper investigates the application of transfer learning techniques in conjunction with neural networks to enhance the estimation of causal effects. We propose a novel framework that leverages representations learned from large source domains and adapts them for causal inference in target domains with scarce data. Our approach utilizes deep neural architectures augmented with domain adaptation modules to align feature distributions and mitigate confounding bias. Comprehensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing causal inference models, especially in scenarios with domain shift or data scarcity. We further conduct ablation studies to assess the contribution of each component within our framework. The results highlight the robustness and flexibility of transfer learning in improving causal estimation accuracy. This research underscores the potential of integrating transfer learning and deep learning for practical and scalable causal effect estimation, with implications for policy analysis, personalized medicine, and other data-driven decision-making applications. Key keywords: causal inference, transfer learning, neural networks, domain adaptation, treatment effect estimation, deep learning.
