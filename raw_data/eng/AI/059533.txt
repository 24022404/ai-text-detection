This paper introduces a novel framework for constructing superpixel hierarchies, a technique crucial for efficient image segmentation and high-level computer vision tasks. The primary objective is to address the limitations of existing superpixel algorithms, which typically generate only flat segmentations and lack multi-scale adaptability. We propose a hierarchical superpixel model that incrementally merges low-level segments into larger, semantically meaningful regions, thereby enabling robust representation across multiple scales. Our approach leverages graph-based methods and adaptive region merging criteria, allowing for flexible integration of color, texture, and spatial information. Comprehensive experiments on benchmark image datasets demonstrate that our superpixel hierarchy significantly improves segmentation accuracy, boundary adherence, and computational efficiency compared to state-of-the-art flat superpixel algorithms. Furthermore, we showcase the practical benefits of our method in downstream applications such as object recognition, scene parsing, and image editing. In conclusion, the proposed superpixel hierarchy framework offers a scalable and adaptable solution, enhancing both the granularity and interpretability of image representation. Keywords: superpixels, image segmentation, hierarchical clustering, computer vision, region merging, multi-scale analysis.
