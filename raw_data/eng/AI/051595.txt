This paper addresses the challenge of adapting semantic segmentation models to continually changing environments without requiring annotated data for each new scenario. Our objective is to develop an unsupervised model adaptation approach that enables continual learning and improvement of semantic segmentation performance over time. We propose a novel method that leverages self-supervised learning and online adaptation techniques to adjust the model to new, unseen data distributions. Our approach utilizes a combination of adversarial training and consistency regularization to align the model's predictions with the changing environment. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, achieving significant improvements in semantic segmentation accuracy on various benchmark datasets. Our research contributes to the development of more robust and adaptable computer vision systems, with potential applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and surveillance. Key contributions include the introduction of a new unsupervised adaptation framework and the demonstration of its efficacy in real-world scenarios, highlighting the importance of continual learning in semantic segmentation. Relevant keywords: continual learning, semantic segmentation, unsupervised adaptation, self-supervised learning, computer vision.
