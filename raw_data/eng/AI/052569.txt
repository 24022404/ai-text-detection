This paper introduces Almost Boltzmann Exploration (ABE), a novel exploration strategy for reinforcement learning that approximates Boltzmann exploration while significantly reducing computational overhead. Boltzmann exploration, known for its effectiveness in balancing exploration and exploitation, suffers from high computational costs due to the exponential calculation of action probabilities. ABE addresses this limitation by employing a stochastic selection mechanism based on a truncated and rescaled probability distribution, thereby diminishing the necessity for full softmax computations. We rigorously analyze the theoretical underpinnings of ABE, demonstrating its convergence properties and establishing bounds on its approximation error. Empirical evaluations across a range of benchmark environments, including discrete and continuous action spaces, show that ABE achieves comparable or superior performance to traditional Boltzmann exploration and other state-of-the-art exploration methods, while exhibiting substantially reduced computational complexity. This enhanced efficiency makes ABE particularly suitable for deployment in resource-constrained environments and for scaling reinforcement learning to more complex problems. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Exploration Strategy, Boltzmann Exploration, Softmax Approximation, Stochastic Optimization.
