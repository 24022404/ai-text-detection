The proliferation of deep learning has reignited interest in the fundamental question of how data quantity and quality influence the performance of neural models. This paper aims to revisit the concept of the "unreasonable effectiveness of data" within the context of modern deep learning architectures. We systematically investigate the impact of dataset scale, diversity, and noise on model performance across various tasks, including image classification and natural language processing. By conducting extensive experiments with different state-of-the-art neural network architectures and data augmentation techniques, we measure how incremental data increases affect convergence, generalization, and robustness. Our findings reveal that, while model gains scale substantially with larger datasets, the marginal benefits diminish past a critical threshold, and data quality begins to play a more decisive role. Furthermore, we highlight the interplay between model capacity and data volume, illustrating scenarios where larger models can exploit abundant data more effectively. The study also explores strategies for optimal data curation and efficient utilization, providing insights for practitioners who seek to maximize model performance under real-world data constraints. Our analysis reaffirms the pivotal role of data in deep learning, clarifies its limitations, and offers practical guidelines for dataset construction and resource allocation. Key keywords: deep learning, dataset scaling, neural networks, model generalization, data quality, data augmentation.
