Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved remarkable success in various domains, leading to a proliferation of benchmarking frameworks designed to evaluate and compare RL algorithms. This paper presents a comprehensive survey of existing RL benchmarking frameworks, examining their design principles, evaluation metrics, supported environments, and target applications. Our objective is to provide researchers and practitioners with a structured overview to facilitate informed framework selection and identify gaps for future development. We analyze prominent frameworks, categorizing them based on their focus areas, such as robotics, game playing, and simulated environments. The survey evaluates the frameworks based on reproducibility, scalability, and extensibility, highlighting the strengths and weaknesses of each. Furthermore, we discuss the challenges associated with RL benchmarking, including the selection of representative tasks, the impact of hyperparameter tuning, and the need for standardized evaluation protocols. This review identifies key areas for improvement, such as enhanced support for complex, real-world environments and the development of more robust and reliable evaluation metrics. Ultimately, this work contributes a consolidated resource for navigating the landscape of RL benchmarking, fostering more rigorous and reproducible research in the field. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Benchmarking, Frameworks, Evaluation, Reproducibility, Performance.
