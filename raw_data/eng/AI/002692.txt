Adversarial examples pose a significant threat to the robustness of deep learning models, particularly in security-sensitive applications. This paper addresses the challenge of generating adversarial examples that are both fast to compute and exhibit low distortion, thereby maximizing their stealth and impact. We introduce a novel approach, EdgeWalk, which leverages the model's gradient information to efficiently traverse the decision boundary and identify minimally perturbed adversarial examples. Unlike existing methods that rely on iterative optimization or complex distance metrics, EdgeWalk directly manipulates input features along the edges of learned representations, leading to significant speedups in adversarial example generation. Experimental results on benchmark image classification datasets demonstrate that EdgeWalk achieves comparable or superior attack success rates with substantially reduced computational cost and perceptual distortion compared to state-of-the-art methods such as FGSM, PGD, and DeepFool. Our findings highlight the effectiveness of edge-based exploration for crafting fast, low-distortion adversarial examples, with implications for evaluating and improving the robustness of deep learning systems. Keywords: adversarial examples, deep learning, robustness, attack, image classification, security.
