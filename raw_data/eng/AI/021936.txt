The rapid advancement of visual data processing has led to significant breakthroughs in image understanding, yet video analysis remains a challenging and resource-intensive task. This paper addresses the question: *What is a video worth in terms of computational representation?* Building on the success of Vision Transformers (ViTs) for image classification, we propose a novel framework for video understanding that extends the "image as a sequence of patches" paradigm to videos. Our approach treats a video as a spatiotemporal sequence of tokens, leveraging transformer-based architectures to capture both spatial and temporal dependencies efficiently. We introduce a hierarchical tokenization strategy that reduces computational complexity while preserving critical motion and context information. Experiments on benchmark video datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in action recognition and video classification tasks, outperforming traditional convolutional and recurrent models. Additionally, our framework exhibits superior scalability and generalization across diverse video domains. The results highlight the potential of transformer-based models for video understanding, offering a unified and efficient solution for spatiotemporal data processing. This work contributes to the growing field of video analysis by providing a scalable, interpretable, and high-performing approach, with implications for applications in surveillance, autonomous systems, and multimedia retrieval.  

**Keywords**: Video understanding, Vision Transformers, spatiotemporal modeling, action recognition, hierarchical tokenization.
