This paper addresses the challenge of learning Linear Non-Gaussian Acyclic Models (LiNGAM) from high-dimensional datasets where the number of variables exceeds the number of observations, a common scenario in modern data-driven applications. The objective is to develop a robust and scalable approach for causal structure discovery under such conditions. We propose a novel method that combines sparse regularization techniques with an efficient optimization framework to estimate the causal structure in high-dimensional settings. Our approach leverages the inherent sparsity of causal relationships and employs a gradient-based optimization algorithm to handle the computational complexity. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing LiNGAM-based approaches in terms of accuracy and scalability, particularly when the number of variables significantly exceeds the number of samples. The findings highlight the potential of our method for applications in fields such as genomics, finance, and social network analysis, where high-dimensional data is prevalent. This research contributes to advancing causal discovery by providing a practical solution for learning LiNGAM in high-dimensional settings, offering new insights into the challenges and opportunities of causal inference in the era of big data.  

**Keywords**: LiNGAM, causal discovery, high-dimensional data, sparse regularization, causal inference, big data.
