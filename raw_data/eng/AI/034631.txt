Stereo matching, a crucial task in 3D scene understanding, suffers performance degradation when applied to new environments due to domain shift. This paper introduces a novel geometry-aware unsupervised domain adaptation framework to mitigate this issue without requiring labeled target data. Our approach leverages geometric constraints inherent in stereo vision to guide the adaptation process. Specifically, we enforce consistency between the estimated disparity map and the underlying 3D scene geometry through a differentiable geometric loss. Furthermore, we employ an adversarial learning strategy to align feature distributions across the source and target domains, focusing on disparity-related features. This geometry-aware adaptation minimizes the domain gap while preserving the structural integrity of the scene. Experiments on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method significantly improves stereo matching accuracy in the target domain compared to state-of-the-art unsupervised domain adaptation techniques. The proposed framework achieves comparable performance to supervised methods trained with target domain labels, highlighting its effectiveness and potential for real-world deployment. Keywords: Stereo matching, unsupervised domain adaptation, geometric constraints, adversarial learning, 3D reconstruction.
