This paper investigates the challenges of applying Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) to dynamic dispatching problems, focusing on environments with stochastic and time-varying demands. We explore the difficulties in designing effective reward functions that accurately reflect long-term system performance and the complexities of handling high-dimensional state spaces arising from real-world dispatching scenarios. Furthermore, the study examines the instability and sample inefficiency often encountered when training DRL agents in these dynamic environments. Through a series of experiments using a simulated dispatching system, we demonstrate the impact of various factors, including reward shaping, exploration strategies, and network architectures, on the agent's learning process and the quality of dispatching policies. Our findings highlight the need for robust and adaptive DRL algorithms capable of generalizing across diverse and evolving demand patterns. The analysis provides valuable insights for researchers and practitioners seeking to leverage DRL for optimizing dynamic dispatching in domains such as transportation, logistics, and resource allocation. Keywords: Deep Reinforcement Learning, Dynamic Dispatching, Reward Shaping, Exploration, Sample Efficiency, Stochastic Optimization.
