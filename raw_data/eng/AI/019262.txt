Skeleton-based human action recognition has become a pivotal technique in computer vision, providing efficient and robust methods for understanding human activities by analyzing skeletal joint trajectories. However, the growing deployment of such systems in safety-critical applications exposes them to security threats, particularly adversarial attacks that can deceive deep learning models. This paper investigates the vulnerability of skeleton-based human action recognition models to adversarial attacks. We propose a novel attack framework that generates imperceptible perturbations on skeleton joint coordinates in both space and time domains, effectively misleading state-of-the-art action recognition networks. Our approach leverages gradient-based optimization to craft adversarial examples that preserve the physical plausibility of motion, ensuring stealthy manipulations that evade straightforward detection. Experimental evaluations on widely-used benchmark datasets reveal a significant drop in recognition accuracy under attack, outperforming baseline adversarial methods in both effectiveness and subtlety. The results highlight systemic weaknesses in existing models and underscore the urgent need for robust defense mechanisms. Our work contributes new insights into the security challenges of skeleton-based action recognition, and provides a strong foundation for developing resilient recognition systems. Keywords: adversarial attack, skeleton-based action recognition, human activity recognition, deep learning, security, robustness.
