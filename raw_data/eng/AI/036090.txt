This paper addresses the challenge of sparse coral classification, a crucial task for marine conservation efforts. Our objective is to develop an accurate and efficient method for classifying coral species from sparse and varied image data. We propose a deep convolutional neural network (CNN) approach, leveraging transfer learning and fine-tuning techniques to adapt to the unique characteristics of coral images. Our method utilizes a compact and efficient architecture, enabling real-time classification on resource-constrained devices. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving high accuracy and outperforming traditional machine learning methods. The key findings of this research highlight the potential of deep learning for coral classification, with significant implications for automated monitoring and conservation efforts. Our contributions include the development of a novel CNN architecture and the creation of a large, annotated dataset for sparse coral classification. This research has important applications in marine biology, ecology, and conservation, with key keywords including coral classification, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, marine conservation, and image classification.
