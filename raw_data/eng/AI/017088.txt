This paper proposes a novel semi-supervised deep kernel learning approach for regression tasks, leveraging unlabeled data to improve predictive performance by minimizing predictive variance. The objective is to develop a framework that effectively utilizes both labeled and unlabeled data to enhance model generalization. Our method employs a deep kernel learning architecture, combining the strengths of neural networks and kernel methods, to learn informative representations from both labeled and unlabeled datasets. By minimizing the predictive variance of the model, we encourage the learning of more consistent and accurate predictions. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our approach, showing significant improvements in regression performance compared to state-of-the-art supervised and semi-supervised methods. The key contributions of this work include the development of a semi-supervised deep kernel learning framework, the introduction of a predictive variance minimization objective, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in leveraging unlabeled data for improved regression performance. This research has important implications for applications where labeled data is scarce or expensive to obtain, and highlights the potential of semi-supervised learning for improving model accuracy and robustness in real-world regression tasks, particularly in areas such as machine learning, kernel methods, and deep learning.
