Scene Graph Generation (SGG) aims to represent an image as a structured graph, with objects as nodes and relationships as edges. However, current SGG models often suffer from bias towards frequently occurring relationships, leading to poor performance on less frequent, yet important, relationships. To address this, we propose a Target-Tailored Source-Transformation (TTST) framework for SGG. TTST transforms both the visual features and semantic embeddings of source (frequent) relationships to better represent the target (infrequent) relationships. Specifically, we employ a learnable transformation module that leverages attention mechanisms to selectively transfer relevant information from source to target relationships. Furthermore, we introduce a novel loss function that explicitly encourages feature alignment between transformed source and target relationships. Experiments on Visual Genome dataset demonstrate that TTST significantly improves performance on tail relationships, achieving state-of-the-art results while maintaining competitive performance on head relationships. The proposed approach effectively mitigates the bias issue in SGG and offers a promising direction for improving the generation of comprehensive scene graphs. Keywords: Scene Graph Generation, Relationship Prediction, Source Transformation, Imbalanced Data, Computer Vision.
