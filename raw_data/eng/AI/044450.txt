Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for analyzing graph-structured data, but their performance heavily relies on the availability of labeled data, which is often scarce or expensive to obtain. To address this challenge, we propose RRLFSOR (Robust Representation Learning Framework for Self-Supervised Optimization of GCNs), a novel self-supervised learning strategy designed to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of GCNs in scenarios with limited labeled data. Our approach leverages a combination of contrastive learning and graph augmentation techniques to generate robust node representations without requiring extensive labeled datasets. The framework introduces a unique optimization mechanism that dynamically adjusts the learning process based on the graph's structural properties, ensuring adaptability across diverse datasets. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that RRLFSOR outperforms existing self-supervised and semi-supervised methods, achieving significant improvements in node classification accuracy and robustness to noisy data. Furthermore, the framework exhibits strong scalability, making it suitable for large-scale graph analysis tasks. The proposed method not only reduces dependency on labeled data but also provides a generalizable solution for improving GCN performance across various domains, including social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics. This work contributes to advancing self-supervised learning techniques for graph-based models, offering a practical and efficient alternative to traditional supervised approaches.  

**Keywords**: Graph Convolutional Networks, Self-Supervised Learning, Contrastive Learning, Graph Augmentation, Node Representation, Scalability.
