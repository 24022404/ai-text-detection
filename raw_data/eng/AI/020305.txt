This paper addresses the challenge of correcting geometric inaccuracies in 3D meshes, a common problem arising from reconstruction artifacts or simplification processes. We introduce a novel learning-based approach that directly predicts geometrically consistent mesh corrections. Our method employs a graph convolutional network (GCN) architecture to analyze the mesh structure and learn deformation patterns that minimize distortion. The network is trained on a synthetic dataset of distorted meshes with corresponding ground truth corrections, enforcing geometric constraints such as edge length preservation and angular consistency within mesh faces. We demonstrate that our approach effectively reduces geometric errors while maintaining mesh quality. Experimental results show significant improvements in mesh accuracy and fairness compared to existing mesh smoothing and optimization techniques. Key contributions include a novel GCN-based architecture for mesh correction and a training methodology that enforces geometric consistency. Our method has applications in 3D modeling, computer graphics, and reverse engineering.
Keywords: Mesh Correction, Geometric Deep Learning, Graph Convolutional Networks, 3D Reconstruction, Mesh Processing.
