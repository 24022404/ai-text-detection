The rapid advancements in medical imaging highlight the need for precise image segmentation to support diagnostic processes. This research addresses the challenge of automatically designing convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures tailored specifically for medical image segmentation tasks, aiming to improve accuracy and efficiency over manually crafted models.

Methods/Approach: We propose an innovative automated method that utilizes a combination of neural architecture search (NAS) and an optimization framework to generate CNN architectures optimized for segmentation precision and computational efficiency. The process involves leveraging advanced NAS algorithms to explore a vast design space and identifying architectures that provide superior performance for medical imaging modalities such as MRI and CT scans.

Results/Findings: The experimental results demonstrate that our automatically designed CNN architectures outperform several state-of-the-art models in terms of segmentation accuracy and computational cost. Specifically, our approach achieved notable improvements in Dice coefficient and Intersection over Union (IoU) scores across multiple datasets. The automatically generated models also showed enhanced generalization capabilities when applied to different imaging datasets.

Conclusion/Implications: This study contributes significantly to the field of medical image analysis by introducing a robust automated methodology for designing CNNs tailored for segmentation tasks. The ability to automatically generate effective architectures not only accelerates the workflow but also democratizes the development of high-performance models across diverse medical imaging contexts. This opens up new avenues for leveraging AI in healthcare, enhancing diagnostic precision and potentially impacting patient outcomes positively.

Keywords: CNN architectures, medical image segmentation, neural architecture search, automated model design, medical imaging, MRI, CT scans, image analysis.
