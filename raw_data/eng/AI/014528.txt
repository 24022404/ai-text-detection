Generative models have become essential tools in a variety of information technology applications, including image synthesis, data augmentation, and anomaly detection. However, guaranteeing their reliability and robustness, especially under adversarial perturbations, remains a significant challenge. This paper addresses the critical problem of certifying the Lipschitz continuity of generative models, which is closely related to their stability and generalization performance. We introduce a novel framework for provable Lipschitz certification that can be applied to a broad class of generative architectures, such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). Our approach leverages analytical bounds, efficient algorithms, and architectural constraints to certify upper limits on the Lipschitz constant of a given generator. Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, we demonstrate that our certification method rigorously and efficiently estimates Lipschitz constants without sacrificing model expressiveness or quality. Comparisons with existing heuristic methods highlight superior performance in terms of both computational efficiency and certification tightness. The proposed Lipschitz certification enables more robust deployment of generative models in safety-critical and regulated environments. Our contributions advance the field by providing a practical and theoretically sound solution for automated verification of generative model robustness. Key keywords: generative models, Lipschitz continuity, certification, robustness, adversarial attacks, machine learning.
