This paper introduces MeNToS, a novel framework for tracklet association in multi-object tracking (MOT) using a Space-Time Memory Network. The primary objective of this research is to address the challenges of occlusions, appearance changes, and complex motion patterns in dynamic environments, which often degrade the performance of existing MOT systems. To achieve this, MeNToS leverages a memory-augmented neural network that captures both spatial and temporal dependencies across video frames, enabling robust and accurate association of tracklets over time. The proposed approach integrates a memory module that stores and retrieves historical information, allowing the system to maintain long-term consistency in object tracking.  

Experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets, including MOTChallenge, to evaluate the performance of MeNToS. Results demonstrate significant improvements in tracking accuracy and robustness compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly in scenarios with high occlusion rates and crowded scenes. The system achieves a notable reduction in identity switches and fragmentation, while maintaining competitive computational efficiency.  

The key contribution of this work lies in the integration of a memory-based architecture for tracklet association, which enhances the system's ability to handle complex tracking scenarios. MeNToS provides a scalable and effective solution for real-world applications, such as surveillance, autonomous driving, and activity recognition. This research advances the field of MOT by introducing a memory-driven approach that improves both accuracy and reliability in dynamic environments.  

**Keywords**: Multi-object tracking, tracklet association, space-time memory network, occlusion handling, memory-augmented neural networks, MOTChallenge.
