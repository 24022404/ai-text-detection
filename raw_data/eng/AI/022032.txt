Profile face recognition poses significant challenges due to drastic variations in facial appearance caused by pose, leading to reduced recognition accuracy in real-world applications. This paper introduces an Attention-guided Progressive Mapping (APM) framework designed to address the difficulties of recognizing profiles by progressively bridging the appearance gap between frontal and profile face images. The proposed approach leverages an attention mechanism to selectively focus on salient facial regions that are robust to pose changes, allowing for more informative feature extraction. The progressive mapping strategy incrementally aligns profile faces toward a canonical representation through a series of intermediate domains, thus reducing the nonlinear transformation complexity. Extensive experiments are conducted on standard face recognition benchmarks, demonstrating that the APM framework significantly improves recognition accuracy on large pose variations compared to state-of-the-art methods. In addition, ablation studies validate the effectiveness of both the attention module and the progressive mapping stages in enhancing feature consistency. The results indicate that our method provides a practical and scalable solution for profile face recognition, offering promising applications in surveillance, security, and human-computer interaction systems. 

Keywords: face recognition, profile face, attention mechanism, progressive mapping, deep learning, pose variation, feature alignment.
