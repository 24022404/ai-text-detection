This paper addresses the challenge of accurate 3D facial pose tracking using depth data, an essential task in computer vision applications such as virtual reality, augmented reality, and facial recognition. Current approaches often struggle with occlusion and visibility constraints, resulting in reduced tracking performance.

Methods/Approach: To overcome these challenges, we propose a novel Visibility Constrained Generative Model that effectively incorporates visibility maps into the generative process for enhanced 3D facial tracking. Our model leverages a deep learning architecture that integrates depth-based spatial information with an adaptive visibility layer, ensuring robustness against common occlusion scenarios and inaccuracies in depth data capture.

Results/Findings: The proposed method demonstrates superior performance over existing state-of-the-art techniques, achieving significant improvements in both accuracy and real-time capability. The model was evaluated on a comprehensive dataset featuring diverse facial expressions and varying occlusion levels. Results indicate a reduction in tracking errors by up to 30% compared to traditional approaches, highlighting the model's effectiveness in maintaining consistent performance under visibility constraints.

Conclusion/Implications: This research provides critical insights into the development of visibility-aware generative models for 3D pose tracking, offering a robust solution for applications requiring precise facial tracking under challenging conditions. The proposed model's ability to adapt to occlusions and integrate seamlessly with depth sensing technologies opens new avenues for advancements in interactive virtual environments and security systems. Key Keywords: 3D facial pose tracking, depth data, visibility constraints, generative model, occlusion handling, computer vision, deep learning.
