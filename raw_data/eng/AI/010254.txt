This paper explores the application of machine learning techniques to analyze machine data from a Computer-Assisted Telephone Interviewing (CATI) network. The objective is to improve the efficiency and accuracy of data collection and processing in survey research. A novel approach is proposed, utilizing a combination of supervised and unsupervised learning methods to identify patterns and anomalies in the machine data. The methodology involves training models on a large dataset of CATI interactions, incorporating features such as call duration, response rates, and interviewer performance. The results show significant improvements in predicting survey outcomes and detecting potential issues in the data collection process. The findings have important implications for the field of survey research, enabling more effective use of resources and enhanced data quality. The use of machine learning in this context contributes to the development of more efficient and reliable data collection systems, with potential applications in social science research, market research, and beyond. Key keywords: machine learning, CATI, survey research, data analysis, predictive modeling.
