Reinforcement learning agents often struggle with exploration in sparse-reward environments. This paper introduces a novel approach to improve exploration by leveraging human gameplay videos from platforms like YouTube. Our method, Imitation-Augmented Exploration (IAE), trains an agent to imitate successful exploration strategies observed in human play. We first extract key exploratory actions from YouTube videos of humans playing the target game. These actions are then used to guide the agent's exploration during training via a behavior cloning loss combined with a traditional reinforcement learning objective. We evaluate IAE on a suite of challenging hard exploration games. Results demonstrate that agents trained with IAE achieve significantly higher scores and faster learning compared to agents trained with standard exploration techniques. Furthermore, we analyze the impact of different imitation strategies and identify key factors contributing to successful exploration. This work highlights the potential of using readily available human gameplay data to improve the exploration capabilities of reinforcement learning agents. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Exploration, Imitation Learning, Video Games, Behavior Cloning.
