**Abstract:**

Inverse rendering aims to infer scene properties such as geometry, material reflectance, and illumination from observed images, yet traditional approaches often struggle with ambiguities arising from limited cues in single-view settings. This paper presents a novel approach for polarimetric multi-view inverse rendering that leverages polarization imaging across multiple viewpoints to enhance the accuracy and robustness of scene reconstruction. Our method integrates polarimetric information—capturing light polarization states—with conventional RGB data from synchronized multi-view cameras. We develop a unified optimization framework that jointly estimates surface normals, albedo, and lighting by exploiting the additional constraints provided by polarization cues, even in challenging lighting conditions and with complex materials. Extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing inverse rendering methods, yielding more accurate geometry and material separation, particularly in scenarios involving transparent or glossy objects. The proposed method also shows strong generalizability to diverse scene types and acquisition setups. This work advances the state-of-the-art in computational photography and 3D computer vision, making polarimetric multi-view inverse rendering a promising solution for high-fidelity scene reconstruction. **Keywords:** inverse rendering, polarization imaging, multi-view, material estimation, 3D reconstruction, computational photography.
