Understanding human actions in home environments is a critical challenge in computer vision and artificial intelligence, with applications ranging from smart homes to assistive robotics. This paper introduces *Home Action Genome (HAG)*, a novel framework for cooperative compositional action understanding, which addresses the complexity of recognizing and interpreting multi-agent activities in dynamic home settings. Unlike existing approaches that focus on isolated actions or single-agent scenarios, HAG leverages a compositional model that decomposes complex activities into simpler, cooperative sub-actions performed by multiple agents. We propose a hierarchical graph-based representation to capture the relationships and dependencies between agents, objects, and actions, enabling more robust and interpretable action understanding. Our approach integrates state-of-the-art deep learning techniques with graph neural networks to model spatiotemporal interactions effectively. Experiments on a newly curated dataset of home activities demonstrate that HAG outperforms existing methods in action recognition accuracy and generalizability, achieving a 12% improvement in F1-score. The framework also provides interpretable insights into the compositional structure of activities, making it a valuable tool for real-world applications. This research advances the field of action understanding by emphasizing cooperative dynamics and compositional reasoning, paving the way for more intelligent systems capable of understanding complex human behaviors in everyday environments.  

**Keywords**: action understanding, compositional modeling, cooperative actions, graph neural networks, home environments, multi-agent systems.
