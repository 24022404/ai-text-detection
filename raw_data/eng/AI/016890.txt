This paper introduces a novel approach for classification and regression tasks involving functional data, termed Measure Inducing Classification and Regression Trees (MICRT). Traditional tree-based methods often struggle with high-dimensional and continuous functional data due to their inherent complexity and variability. To address this, MICRT leverages measure-theoretic principles to construct decision trees that effectively handle functional inputs by inducing measures over the data space. The proposed method partitions the functional domain into interpretable regions, enabling efficient and accurate predictions. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that MICRT outperforms existing functional data analysis techniques in terms of predictive accuracy and computational efficiency. Furthermore, the method provides interpretable insights into the functional features driving classification or regression outcomes. The implications of this work extend to applications in fields such as healthcare, finance, and environmental monitoring, where functional data analysis is critical. MICRT represents a significant advancement in functional data modeling, offering a robust and scalable solution for real-world challenges.  

**Keywords**: functional data analysis, classification and regression trees, measure theory, interpretable models, predictive modeling.
