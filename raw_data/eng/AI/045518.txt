This paper addresses the challenge of scalability and efficiency in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) by introducing a novel surrogate agent-environment interface. Traditional DRL frameworks often suffer from high computational costs and inefficiencies due to direct interactions between agents and complex environments. To overcome this limitation, we propose a lightweight surrogate model that acts as an intermediary, reducing the computational burden while maintaining the fidelity of environment interactions. Our approach leverages a combination of neural network-based approximation and adaptive sampling techniques to simulate environment dynamics, enabling faster training and improved resource utilization. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves comparable or superior performance to traditional DRL approaches across multiple benchmark environments, with significant reductions in training time and computational overhead. The findings highlight the potential of surrogate interfaces to enhance the scalability of DRL systems, particularly in resource-constrained settings. This work contributes to the growing body of research on efficient DRL frameworks and opens new avenues for deploying reinforcement learning in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: deep reinforcement learning, surrogate model, agent-environment interface, computational efficiency, neural networks, adaptive sampling.
