This paper addresses the challenges of generalization in reinforcement learning (RL), particularly focusing on how RL agents can better adapt to new, unseen environments. Unlike conventional methods that rely solely on reward signals, our research investigates the integration of explicit human instructions to enhance an agent's learning process and generalization capabilities.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel approach that incorporates human instructions into the RL framework, where agents receive both reward signals and structured guidance from humans. This method leverages natural language processing (NLP) techniques to parse and understand instructions, which are then used in combination with traditional RL algorithms. Our approach is evaluated across a series of benchmark environments to assess how effectively human instructions improve agent performance as compared to standard RL models.

Results/Findings: Empirical results indicate that RL agents enhanced with human instructions demonstrate superior generalization abilities, performing significantly better in novel scenarios they have not encountered during training. Our method shows a marked improvement in sample efficiency and adaptability, outperforming existing RL strategies that do not utilize human inputs. The integration of human knowledge allows for accelerated learning and better decision-making processes in complex environments.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings underscore the potential of human instructions as a powerful tool to bridge the gap between RL training environments and real-world applications. This research contributes to the field by introducing a scalable model that combines human and machine intelligence, thus opening new pathways for developing RL systems with enhanced versatility and efficiency. Future work could explore the broader applicability of this method across diverse domains, offering insight into human-AI collaboration in machine learning.

Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Generalization, Human Instructions, Natural Language Processing, Machine Learning, AI Collaboration.
