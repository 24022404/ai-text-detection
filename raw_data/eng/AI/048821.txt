This paper introduces a novel network clustering method leveraging Kernel Autoregressive Moving Average (ARMA) modeling on the Grassmannian manifold for brain network analysis. The objective is to develop a clustering approach that effectively captures the dynamic dependencies and topological structures within complex brain networks. We propose embedding brain network connectivity matrices onto the Grassmannian manifold and subsequently applying Kernel-ARMA models to represent the temporal evolution of network states. This allows us to capture both linear and non-linear dynamic patterns inherent in brain activity. The proposed method is evaluated on both synthetic and real-world fMRI datasets, demonstrating superior performance in identifying functionally relevant brain subnetworks compared to traditional clustering algorithms such as spectral clustering and k-means. Results indicate that Kernel-ARMA modeling on the Grassmannian effectively captures the underlying dynamic characteristics of brain networks, leading to improved clustering accuracy and providing novel insights into brain organization. The proposed framework offers a powerful tool for understanding complex brain functions and identifying potential biomarkers for neurological disorders.

**Keywords:** Network Clustering, Kernel-ARMA, Grassmannian Manifold, Brain Networks, fMRI, Time Series Analysis.
