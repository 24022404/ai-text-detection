This research addresses the challenge of distinguishing controllable factors from uncontrollable ones in complex environments, aiming to enhance autonomous systems' understanding and interaction with the world. Recognizing these factors is critical for developing systems that can autonomously adapt and respond to dynamic conditions.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework utilizing reinforcement learning (RL) and interaction-based scenarios to disentangle factors of variation. Our approach employs a hybrid model combining unsupervised learning techniques with an RL paradigm to effectively identify and manipulate controllable elements within an environment. By simulating diverse interaction scenarios, the system iteratively learns to exploit these factors, improving adaptability and autonomy.

Results/Findings: The proposed framework demonstrates significant improvement in distinguishing and utilizing controllable factors across several simulated environments, outperforming traditional RL methods. Experimental results show increased task efficiency and adaptability, with our model achieving a higher rate of successful task completions compared to baseline models. Furthermore, the system's ability to generalize across different scenarios indicates its robustness and potential for wider applications.

Conclusion/Implications: Our findings underline the importance of interactive learning in understanding environmental dynamics and pave the way for more advanced autonomous systems. The research contributes a novel methodology for disentangling factors of variation, with implications for robotics, automated agents, and any AI system requiring nuanced interactions with its environment. This framework opens new avenues for developing intelligent systems capable of adaptive learning and decision-making in real-world conditions.

Keywords: controllable factors, uncontrollable factors, reinforcement learning, interaction-based learning, environment dynamics, autonomous systems, adaptability.
