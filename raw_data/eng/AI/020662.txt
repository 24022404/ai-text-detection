Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become a standard tool for representation learning on graphs. However, their behavior and limitations are not yet fully understood. This paper introduces a simple spectral failure mode exhibited by GCNs, wherein specific graph structures lead to a significant attenuation of certain frequency components in the learned node embeddings. We demonstrate that this attenuation arises from the spectral properties of the graph Laplacian and the inherent smoothing operation performed by GCN layers. Through theoretical analysis and empirical validation on synthetic and real-world graphs, we show that this failure mode can negatively impact downstream task performance, particularly for tasks relying on higher-order graph information. Furthermore, we propose a simple modification to the GCN architecture that mitigates this spectral attenuation and improves performance on affected graphs. Our findings provide insights into the spectral biases of GCNs and suggest potential directions for developing more robust and spectrally aware graph neural networks.
**Keywords:** Graph Convolutional Networks, Spectral Analysis, Graph Signal Processing, Failure Modes, Graph Neural Networks.
