Graph contrastive learning (GCL) has recently emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning effective node and graph-level representations from structured data, facilitating advancements in applications such as social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics. However, prevailing GCL approaches often exhibit vulnerability to noise, perturbations, and suboptimal augmentation strategies, which can impair the reliability and generalization ability of learned representations. This paper aims to address these challenges by proposing a novel framework for robust graph contrastive learning. Our approach introduces adaptive data augmentation techniques and a stability-regularized contrastive loss function designed to enhance resilience against adversarial perturbations and structural noise. We conduct extensive experiments on multiple benchmark graph datasets, demonstrating that our method achieves superior performance compared to state-of-the-art GCL baselines, especially under noisy and perturbed conditions. The results reveal improved representation quality, enhanced transferability, and more stable performance. Our contributions highlight pathways toward more reliable and robust graph representation learning, laying the foundation for broader deployment of GCL in real-world, noise-prone environments.

Keywords: Graph Contrastive Learning, Robustness, Graph Neural Networks, Data Augmentation, Representation Learning, Adversarial Noise
