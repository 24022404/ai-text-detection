Face attribute manipulation aims to modify specific attributes of a face image while preserving identity. Existing methods often struggle with generating realistic and high-resolution images, especially when manipulating multiple attributes simultaneously. This paper introduces a novel approach that learns residual images representing the difference between the original and manipulated face, enabling more precise attribute control. Our method employs a deep convolutional encoder-decoder network trained to predict these residual images, conditioned on the desired attribute changes. We introduce a novel loss function that combines pixel-level reconstruction, perceptual similarity, and adversarial training to ensure both realism and attribute fidelity. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of visual quality, attribute accuracy, and identity preservation. Furthermore, our model exhibits robustness in handling large attribute changes and complex scenarios. The proposed method offers a promising solution for face editing applications and contributes to advancing the field of image manipulation.

**Keywords:** Face attribute manipulation, image editing, residual learning, deep learning, generative adversarial networks.
