The increasing demand for efficient and accessible deep learning solutions in medical imaging, particularly for 3D segmentation tasks, has highlighted the limitations of GPU-dependent models in resource-constrained environments. This paper introduces **TernaryNet**, a novel deep learning framework designed to accelerate model inference without relying on GPUs by leveraging **sparse and binary convolutions**. TernaryNet addresses the computational inefficiencies of traditional 3D convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by reducing the precision of weights and activations to ternary values (-1, 0, +1), enabling faster computations on CPU-based systems. Our approach integrates a sparse convolution mechanism to minimize redundant operations, further enhancing inference speed. Experiments on benchmark medical 3D segmentation datasets demonstrate that TernaryNet achieves comparable accuracy to state-of-the-art models while reducing inference time by up to **60%** on CPUs. The framework also exhibits significant memory efficiency, making it suitable for deployment in low-resource clinical settings. By eliminating the need for GPUs, TernaryNet provides a cost-effective and scalable solution for real-time medical image analysis, with potential applications in telemedicine and point-of-care diagnostics. This work contributes to the growing field of efficient deep learning by introducing a lightweight yet powerful architecture tailored for medical 3D segmentation tasks.  

**Keywords**: TernaryNet, 3D segmentation, sparse convolutions, binary convolutions, CPU inference, medical imaging, efficient deep learning.
