Face recognition systems are widely deployed in security and surveillance applications, yet they remain vulnerable to adversarial attacks in the physical world. This paper investigates the design and effectiveness of *meaningful adversarial stickers*—visually interpretable perturbations that can deceive face recognition models while maintaining a natural appearance to human observers. We propose a novel approach to generate such stickers by combining adversarial machine learning techniques with perceptual constraints, ensuring that the stickers blend seamlessly into real-world environments. Our method leverages a generative adversarial network (GAN) framework to optimize sticker patterns that are both effective in fooling state-of-the-art face recognition systems and visually meaningful, such as logos or decorative elements. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves a high attack success rate against commercial face recognition systems, outperforming traditional adversarial patch methods. Furthermore, we evaluate the robustness of these stickers under various physical conditions, including lighting changes, viewing angles, and printing quality. The findings highlight the practical risks posed by adversarial stickers and underscore the need for more robust face recognition systems. This work contributes to the growing body of research on physical adversarial attacks and provides insights into the development of defense mechanisms against such threats.  

**Keywords**: adversarial attacks, face recognition, physical-world attacks, meaningful perturbations, generative adversarial networks (GANs), robustness.
