This paper introduces LRPD, a novel framework for long-range 3D pedestrian detection that leverages the complementary strengths of LiDAR and RGB sensors. While existing methods often struggle with accurate detection at extended distances due to limitations in sensor resolution and environmental conditions, LRPD addresses this challenge by integrating multi-modal data fusion and advanced deep learning techniques. The proposed approach employs a dual-branch architecture, where LiDAR data provides precise depth information and RGB images offer rich contextual details. A feature alignment module ensures seamless integration of the two modalities, enhancing the system's ability to detect pedestrians at distances exceeding 100 meters. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that LRPD outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of detection accuracy and robustness, particularly in challenging scenarios such as low-light conditions and occlusions. The framework achieves a significant improvement in average precision (AP) for long-range detection, showcasing its potential for applications in autonomous driving and surveillance systems. By combining the specific strengths of LiDAR and RGB, LRPD sets a new standard for long-range 3D pedestrian detection, offering a scalable and efficient solution for real-world deployment.  

**Keywords**: 3D pedestrian detection, LiDAR, RGB, multi-modal fusion, long-range detection, autonomous driving, deep learning.
