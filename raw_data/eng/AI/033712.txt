This paper proposes BDANet, a novel multiscale convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture designed for building damage assessment from satellite images. The objective is to accurately identify and classify damaged buildings after natural disasters, facilitating rapid response and relief efforts. BDANet employs a cross-directional attention mechanism, allowing the model to focus on relevant features and contextual information from multiple scales and directions. The approach leverages a combination of local and global features, extracted through a hierarchical convolutional framework, to capture subtle damage patterns and distinguish between different damage levels. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of BDANet, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and robustness. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a cross-directional attention module and a multiscale feature extraction strategy, enabling more accurate and efficient building damage assessment from satellite imagery. This work has significant implications for disaster response, urban planning, and infrastructure management, with potential applications in computer vision, remote sensing, and geospatial analysis. Key keywords: building damage assessment, satellite images, convolutional neural networks, cross-directional attention, multiscale feature extraction, disaster response.
