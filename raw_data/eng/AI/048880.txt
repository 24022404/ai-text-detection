This paper presents a comprehensive benchmarking study of reinforcement learning algorithms on real-world robots, aiming to bridge the gap between theoretical advancements and practical applications. Our objective is to evaluate the performance of state-of-the-art reinforcement learning methods, including Deep Q-Networks and Policy Gradient Methods, in robotic control tasks such as navigation and manipulation. We employ a systematic approach, using a combination of simulation-based training and real-world deployment on robotic platforms. Our results show that while reinforcement learning algorithms can achieve remarkable performance in simulated environments, their transferability to real-world robots is often limited by factors such as sensor noise and actuation uncertainty. We identify key challenges and propose potential solutions, including domain randomization and robustification techniques. Our study provides valuable insights into the strengths and weaknesses of reinforcement learning algorithms in real-world robotic applications, highlighting the need for further research in this area. The contributions of this work include a comprehensive benchmarking framework, a thorough analysis of reinforcement learning algorithms on real-world robots, and a discussion of future directions for improving the reliability and efficiency of robotic control systems. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, robotics, benchmarking, Deep Q-Networks, Policy Gradient Methods, real-world deployment.
