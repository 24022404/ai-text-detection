This paper presents an innovative approach to unsupervised learning from narrated instruction videos, addressing the challenge of automatically understanding and extracting valuable information from unstructured video data. Our objective is to develop a system that can learn from instructional videos without manual annotation, enabling applications in areas such as robotic learning, task automation, and intelligent tutoring. We propose a novel framework that leverages computer vision and natural language processing techniques to jointly learn visual and linguistic representations from video and audio streams. Our approach utilizes a multimodal fusion strategy to integrate features from both modalities, allowing the model to discover underlying patterns and relationships between visual actions and narrative descriptions. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method in learning meaningful representations from instruction videos, outperforming existing unsupervised learning approaches. Our research contributes to the development of more intelligent and autonomous systems, with potential applications in human-robot interaction, education, and knowledge acquisition. Key keywords: unsupervised learning, instruction videos, multimodal fusion, computer vision, natural language processing, robotic learning.
