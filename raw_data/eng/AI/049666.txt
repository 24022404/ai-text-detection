This paper introduces a novel approach for detecting gaze preferences in children using a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), with the potential to aid in the early diagnosis of Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). The objective is to develop a robust and automated method for analyzing visual attention patterns, a key indicator of ASD. Our method utilizes a CNN architecture trained on eye-tracking data collected during visual stimuli presentation. The CNN model learns to classify gaze patterns, distinguishing between neurotypical children and those with ASD, based on preferential looking behaviors. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed CNN-based approach, achieving a significant improvement in accuracy compared to traditional machine learning techniques for gaze preference analysis. These findings suggest that the developed system can serve as a valuable tool for clinicians in the early identification of children at risk for ASD, enabling timely intervention and support. Keywords: Autism Spectrum Disorder, Gaze Preference, Convolutional Neural Network, Eye-Tracking, Diagnostics, Visual Attention.
