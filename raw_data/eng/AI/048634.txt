This paper introduces a novel deep neural network architecture designed to model online user behavior by leveraging both implicit and explicit feedback signals. The objective is to capture the complex interplay of short-term and long-term user preferences within sequential interaction data. Our approach integrates a recurrent neural network (RNN) component, specifically Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), for modeling sequential behavior, with a dedicated attention mechanism to explicitly weigh user preferences derived from explicit feedback, such as ratings and reviews. The model learns user embeddings that represent long-term interests, which are then combined with short-term sequential embeddings to predict future actions. Experimental results on several real-world e-commerce datasets demonstrate that our proposed model significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in predicting user actions and item interactions, showing improvements in precision and recall. The findings suggest that explicitly incorporating preference information alongside sequential behavior leads to more accurate user modeling. This research contributes a more nuanced understanding of online user behavior and provides a practical method for enhancing recommendation systems and personalized user experiences.
Keywords: Sequential recommendation, user modeling, deep learning, implicit feedback, explicit feedback, recurrent neural networks, attention mechanism.
