Multi-state autoregressive models are powerful tools for capturing complex temporal dependencies in various domains. However, determining the optimal number of states remains a significant challenge, often relying on computationally expensive grid searches or expert knowledge. This paper introduces a novel data-driven approach for learning the number of states directly from the observed time series data. Our method leverages a Bayesian non-parametric prior, specifically a Dirichlet process, to automatically infer the number of active states during model training. We develop an efficient Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) inference algorithm that allows the model complexity to adapt to the data. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets, including speech and financial time series, demonstrate that our approach accurately estimates the number of states and achieves comparable or superior performance to existing state-of-the-art methods with significantly reduced computational cost. This automated state discovery offers a practical advantage in applying multi-state autoregressive models to complex sequential data analysis.

Keywords: Autoregressive Models, Bayesian Non-parametrics, Dirichlet Process, Time Series Analysis, State-Space Models, MCMC, Model Selection.
