The accurate mapping of complex road networks is a significant challenge in the development of autonomous driving systems. This paper introduces DAGMapper, an innovative approach designed to automatically discover and map lane topology, thereby enhancing the navigation capabilities of self-driving vehicles.

Methods/Approach: DAGMapper leverages a combination of deep learning techniques and directed acyclic graph (DAG) structures to interpret and organize raw map data. The proposed system utilizes convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to process visual inputs and employs a graph-centric model to accurately delineate lane configurations and connections. By integrating spatial and temporal information, DAGMapper constructs an interpretable and reliable lane topology representation.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that DAGMapper surpasses existing methodologies in both accuracy and efficiency when reconstructing lane topologies from diverse road conditions. In benchmark tests, our model achieved significant improvements in lane detection and topology accuracy metrics compared to traditional mapping techniques. The system effectively handles complex scenarios such as lane merges, splits, and intersections, showcasing robust performance across various datasets.

Conclusion/Implications: DAGMapper represents a substantial advancement in autonomous vehicle mapping technology, providing a more precise and scalable solution for real-time lane topology discovery. Its ability to autonomously interpret complex driving environments enhances the safety and reliability of autonomous navigation systems. Potential applications extend beyond self-driving cars to include smart city infrastructure and advanced driver-assistance systems, further contributing to the field of intelligent transportation.

Keywords: DAGMapper, lane topology, autonomous driving, deep learning, directed acyclic graph, mapping technology, CNNs, self-driving vehicles.
