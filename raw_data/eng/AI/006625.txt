This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the resolution of satellite images using deep learning techniques. The objective is to develop an effective method for satellite image super-resolution, enabling the recovery of high-resolution details from low-resolution satellite imagery. To achieve this, a novel approach is proposed, leveraging a custom-designed deep neural network architecture that integrates advanced upsampling modules and perceptual loss functions. The methodology involves training the network on a large dataset of paired low- and high-resolution satellite images, utilizing a combination of pixel-wise and feature-wise loss functions to optimize the super-resolution process. Experimental results demonstrate the superiority of the proposed approach, achieving significant improvements in peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM) compared to state-of-the-art methods. The findings of this research have important implications for various applications, including environmental monitoring, urban planning, and disaster response, where high-resolution satellite imagery is crucial. Key contributions of this work include the design of a robust and efficient deep network architecture for satellite image super-resolution, and the demonstration of its potential to enhance the resolution of satellite images while preserving critical details and textures. Relevant keywords: satellite image super-resolution, deep learning, neural networks, remote sensing, image processing.
