This paper presents a comprehensive survey of the application of deep learning techniques to machine health monitoring, an area critical for predictive maintenance and operational safety in industrial systems. The primary objective is to systematically review recent advances and trends in employing deep learning models—such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and autoencoders—for fault diagnosis, anomaly detection, and remaining useful life (RUL) prediction. We analyze a wide spectrum of methodologies, discussing the process from raw data acquisition through preprocessing to feature extraction and model training. The survey evaluates state-of-the-art deep learning frameworks against traditional machine learning approaches, highlighting improvements in accuracy, robustness, and adaptability to complex sensor data. Additionally, we discuss the challenges of limited labeled data, data imbalance, model interpretability, and real-time deployment constraints encountered in industry settings. Our findings reveal that deep learning models significantly enhance the capability to monitor machine health in dynamic and noisy environments, though issues such as generalization and explainability remain areas for further research. The paper concludes by outlining open challenges and future directions, including the integration of transfer learning, edge computing, and hybrid models, which are poised to accelerate advancements in intelligent machine maintenance. Keywords: deep learning, machine health monitoring, predictive maintenance, fault diagnosis, anomaly detection, remaining useful life, industrial IoT.
