This paper presents an innovative approach to offline meta-reinforcement learning, leveraging online self-supervision to enhance agent adaptability and performance. The primary objective is to develop a framework that enables agents to learn from offline data and adapt to new, unseen environments with minimal online interactions. Our method employs a novel combination of meta-reinforcement learning algorithms and self-supervised learning techniques, allowing the agent to learn a robust and generalizable representation of tasks. The approach is evaluated on a range of benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements in few-shot adaptation and overall performance compared to existing offline meta-reinforcement learning methods. Key findings include the ability of the agent to effectively adapt to new environments with limited online supervision, and the importance of self-supervised learning in improving the agent's representation and transferability. The proposed framework has significant implications for real-world applications, such as robotics and autonomous systems, where offline data is abundant but online interactions are limited. Our research contributes to the development of more efficient and adaptable reinforcement learning systems, with potential applications in areas like meta-learning, transfer learning, and autonomous decision-making. Keywords: meta-reinforcement learning, offline learning, self-supervision, few-shot adaptation, autonomous systems.
