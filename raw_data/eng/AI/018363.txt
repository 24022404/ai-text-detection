This paper introduces AntiDote, a novel attention-based dynamic optimization technique designed to enhance the runtime efficiency of neural networks. The primary objective is to address the issue of computational overhead in deep learning models, which hinders their deployment in resource-constrained environments. AntiDote achieves this by dynamically adjusting the computational resources allocated to different parts of the network, based on their relative importance to the overall task. Our approach utilizes attention mechanisms to identify and prioritize critical components, ensuring that the model's performance is preserved while minimizing unnecessary computations. Experimental results demonstrate that AntiDote outperforms existing optimization methods, achieving significant reductions in latency and energy consumption without compromising accuracy. The key contributions of this research include the development of a flexible and scalable optimization framework, which can be applied to a wide range of neural network architectures and applications. By leveraging attention-based optimization, AntiDote paves the way for more efficient and sustainable deployment of deep learning models in real-world scenarios, with potential applications in areas such as edge computing, autonomous systems, and mobile devices. Key keywords: neural networks, attention mechanisms, dynamic optimization, runtime efficiency, deep learning.
