This paper addresses the challenge of general image recognition in scenarios with limited labeled data by proposing a knowledge-guided multi-label few-shot learning framework. Traditional few-shot learning methods often struggle with multi-label classification tasks due to the complexity of recognizing multiple objects or attributes within a single image. To overcome this limitation, we introduce a novel approach that leverages external knowledge graphs to guide the learning process, enabling the model to better generalize across diverse and unseen categories. Our method integrates a graph-based attention mechanism with a meta-learning framework, allowing the model to effectively capture relationships between labels and utilize prior knowledge for improved recognition. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves significant performance improvements over state-of-the-art methods, particularly in scenarios with extreme label scarcity. The results highlight the potential of incorporating external knowledge to enhance few-shot learning capabilities, offering a promising direction for real-world applications such as medical imaging, autonomous driving, and content-based image retrieval. Key contributions include the development of a scalable knowledge-guided framework and the demonstration of its effectiveness in multi-label few-shot settings.  

**Keywords**: Few-shot learning, multi-label classification, knowledge graphs, image recognition, meta-learning, attention mechanisms.
