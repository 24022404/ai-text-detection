The objective of this research is to address the challenge of 3D multi-object tracking (MOT) in complex environments by introducing GNN3DMOT, a novel approach utilizing Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) with multi-feature learning capabilities. Current 3D MOT methodologies often struggle with accurately tracking multiple objects due to dynamic changes and occlusions in the environment. Our approach leverages the relational modeling strengths of GNNs to effectively capture the interactions between tracked objects over time. By integrating multi-feature learning, GNN3DMOT incorporates various object features such as shape, motion, and appearance, enhancing the robustness and accuracy of object tracking. The model is trained and evaluated on benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance in terms of tracking accuracy and computational efficiency compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. Our findings indicate that GNN3DMOT significantly improves the association of detected objects over consecutive frames, thereby reducing discrepancies in object identity management. The implications of this research are substantial for applications in autonomous vehicles, robot navigation, and surveillance systems, where reliable and efficient 3D object tracking is crucial. The innovative integration of GNNs with multi-feature learning underscores the potential of this approach to advance the field of 3D MOT. Keywords: Graph Neural Networks, 3D Multi-Object Tracking, Multi-Feature Learning, Autonomous Systems, Object Detection.
