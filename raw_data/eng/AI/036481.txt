Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have shown remarkable success in generating high-quality data, but their training often suffers from instability, particularly due to issues like mode collapse and divergence. Spectral normalization has emerged as a widely adopted technique to stabilize GAN training, yet its underlying mechanisms remain underexplored. This paper investigates why spectral normalization effectively stabilizes GANs by analyzing its impact on the Lipschitz continuity of the discriminator. We demonstrate that spectral normalization enforces a controlled gradient norm, preventing the discriminator from overpowering the generator and ensuring smoother optimization dynamics. Building on this analysis, we propose an improved variant of spectral normalization that adaptively adjusts the spectral bounds during training, further enhancing stability and convergence. Experimental results on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, validate our approach, showing improved performance in terms of both training stability and sample quality compared to existing methods. Our findings provide deeper insights into the role of spectral normalization in GANs and offer a practical enhancement for more robust generative modeling.  

**Keywords**: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), spectral normalization, Lipschitz continuity, training stability, adaptive normalization, generative modeling.
