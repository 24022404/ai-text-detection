Gesture recognition is a crucial component in human-computer interaction, enabling intuitive control and communication. This paper introduces a novel path searching method within a weighted graph framework to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of gesture recognition, particularly focusing on identifying important, high-impact actions. Our approach represents gesture sequences as paths in a graph, where nodes correspond to gesture components and edge weights reflect the likelihood of transitions between them. We then employ a modified shortest path algorithm to identify the most probable gesture sequence, emphasizing paths associated with predefined “important actions” by assigning higher weights to their constituent nodes and edges. The system was evaluated on a custom dataset of dynamic hand gestures, demonstrating a significant improvement in recognition accuracy compared to traditional Hidden Markov Model (HMM) and Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) based approaches, especially for complex, multi-step gestures. The results highlight the potential of weighted graph-based path searching for robust and efficient gesture recognition, paving the way for more natural and responsive human-computer interfaces. Keywords: Gesture Recognition, Weighted Graph, Path Searching, Human-Computer Interaction, Dynamic Hand Gestures, Action Recognition.
