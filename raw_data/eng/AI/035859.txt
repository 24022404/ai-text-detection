Abstract:

3D object detection in point clouds has become a critical task in autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality applications. Traditional voting-based methods generate candidate object centers by aggregating information from local regions, yet they often struggle to accurately trace back representative points that contribute significantly to the final object hypotheses, leading to suboptimal detection precision. This paper presents a novel back-tracing mechanism for representative point identification in voting-based 3D object detection frameworks. Our method introduces an explicit back-tracing algorithm that tracks the voting process to pinpoint influential points within the input point cloud. By integrating this mechanism into existing detection networks, we enhance both the localization accuracy and robustness of 3D object proposals. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms standard voting-based baselines, achieving higher mean Average Precision (mAP) and better recall, particularly in challenging and cluttered environments. The proposed method preserves computational efficiency while enabling interpretable detection by highlighting critical points responsible for each object hypothesis. These findings suggest that back-tracing representative points can significantly advance the effectiveness and transparency of 3D object detection in point cloud data. Keywords: 3D object detection, point clouds, voting-based methods, back-tracing, representative points, deep learning, autonomous systems.
