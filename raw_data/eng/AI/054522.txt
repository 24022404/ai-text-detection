The rapid advancements in color image sensor technology demand efficient demultiplexing techniques to enhance image quality and processing speed. This paper addresses the challenge of numerical demultiplexing of color image sensor measurements, which is crucial for achieving high-fidelity color reproduction in modern imaging systems.

Methods/Approach: We introduce an innovative approach leveraging non-linear random forest modeling to perform demultiplexing of sensor data. The proposed method capitalizes on the strengths of random forests in handling non-linear relationships and making accurate predictions based on complex input-output mappings. This model is trained using a comprehensive dataset of sensor measurements, facilitating the prediction of high-quality color images from raw data.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the non-linear random forest model in accurately demultiplexing color sensor measurements. The results exhibit a significant improvement in color fidelity and image clarity compared to traditional linear methods. Additionally, the proposed model maintains computational efficiency, making it suitable for real-time image processing applications.

Conclusion/Implications: The research establishes a novel demultiplexing technique that enhances image quality without compromising processing speed, offering a substantial contribution to the field of color image sensing. Potential applications include digital photography, surveillance, and augmented reality systems. The integration of non-linear random forest modeling in sensor data processing could pave the way for further advancements in computational imaging technologies.

Keywords: numerical demultiplexing, color image sensor, non-linear random forests, image processing, computational imaging, color fidelity, real-time applications.
