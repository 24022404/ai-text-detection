This paper addresses the problem of optimizing morphological structures for robotics through a task-agnostic approach, bypassing the traditional dependency on task-specific designs. Our research explores how morphology can autonomously evolve without predefined targets, aiming for generalized adaptability across various tasks.

Methods/Approach: We present a novel evolutionary framework leveraging machine learning algorithms and genetic programming to facilitate the self-directed evolution of robotic morphologies. The approach integrates a task-agnostic fitness evaluation mechanism that enables the structures to evolve based on intrinsic adaptability and resilience factors, rather than task-specific performance metrics.

Results/Findings: The results demonstrate that the evolved morphologies exhibit enhanced adaptability across multiple simulated environments, outperforming traditional task-specific designs in terms of versatility and efficiency. Comparative analyses highlight a significant reduction in design biases and an increase in developmental robustness, showcasing the potential of this task-agnostic evolution framework.

Conclusion/Implications: The research suggests that task-agnostic morphology evolution can lead to more adaptable and reusable robotic configurations, reducing the need for extensive trial-and-error design processes. This advancement has significant implications for autonomous robotics development, enabling more efficient exploration of morphological space and fostering innovation in fields requiring versatile robotic agents.

Keywords: Morphology Evolution, Task-Agnostic, Robotics, Machine Learning, Genetic Programming, Autonomous Systems.
