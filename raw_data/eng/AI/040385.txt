The proliferation of manipulated images in digital media has necessitated robust methods for fake image detection. This paper addresses the challenge of creating imperceptible adversarial examples that can evade detection by state-of-the-art fake image detectors while maintaining visual fidelity. We propose a novel approach leveraging generative adversarial networks (GANs) and gradient-based optimization techniques to craft adversarial perturbations that are virtually undetectable to the human eye. Our method focuses on minimizing perceptibility by constraining perturbations within a low-frequency domain, ensuring that the adversarial examples remain visually indistinguishable from the original images. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach successfully deceives multiple advanced detection models, achieving a high evasion rate while preserving image quality. Furthermore, we evaluate the robustness of existing detection systems against such adversarial attacks, revealing critical vulnerabilities in current methodologies. This research highlights the need for more resilient detection frameworks and provides insights into the development of adversarial defense mechanisms. The findings have significant implications for enhancing the security and reliability of fake image detection systems in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: adversarial examples, fake image detection, GANs, image manipulation, imperceptible perturbations, deep learning.
