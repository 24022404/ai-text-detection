Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have shown promise in 3D point cloud generation, but often suffer from instability and mode collapse due to the inherent challenges in sampling from high-dimensional spaces. This paper introduces a novel sampling strategy for 3D point cloud GANs, designed to improve both generation quality and training stability. Our approach, dubbed Adaptive Density-Aware Sampling (ADAS), dynamically adjusts the sampling distribution based on the local density of generated point clouds during training. Specifically, ADAS prioritizes sampling in regions of lower density, encouraging exploration of underrepresented modes and mitigating mode collapse. We evaluate ADAS on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing sampling techniques and state-of-the-art 3D point cloud GANs. Quantitative metrics, including the Fréchet Point Cloud Distance (FPD) and Coverage, show significant improvements in generation quality and diversity. Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of the adaptive density estimation in guiding the sampling process. Our findings highlight the importance of informed sampling strategies in 3D GANs and offer a new direction for future research.

Keywords: 3D point clouds, GANs, generative models, sampling, density estimation, adversarial learning.
