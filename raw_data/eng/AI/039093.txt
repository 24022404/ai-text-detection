Anomaly detection is a critical task in various domains, including cybersecurity, industrial inspection, and healthcare, where identifying rare or unexpected patterns is essential. This paper proposes a novel two-stage anomaly detection framework guided by attention maps and enhanced through hard augmentation techniques. The objective is to improve the detection of anomalies in complex datasets by leveraging the interpretability of attention mechanisms and the robustness of data augmentation. In the first stage, an attention map is generated to highlight regions of interest within the input data, focusing on potential anomalies. The second stage employs a hard augmentation strategy to create challenging synthetic samples, enabling the model to learn more discriminative features and improve generalization. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing approaches in terms of detection accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with imbalanced or noisy data. The framework’s ability to integrate attention-guided localization with hard augmentation represents a significant advancement in anomaly detection, offering practical implications for real-world applications. Key contributions include the introduction of a two-stage architecture, the novel use of hard augmentation, and the interpretability provided by attention maps. Keywords: anomaly detection, attention maps, hard augmentation, two-stage framework, interpretability, robustness.
