Glioma classification from MRI data using deep learning models has shown promise, but often lacks interpretability, hindering clinical adoption. This paper introduces an explanation-guided training (EGT) approach to improve both the accuracy and interpretability of a convolutional neural network (CNN) for glioma classification. EGT leverages gradient-based explanation maps to guide the training process, encouraging the network to focus on clinically relevant features within the MRI images. Specifically, we penalize network activations in regions deemed irrelevant by the explanation maps, promoting feature learning in salient areas. Experiments on a benchmark dataset of glioma MRI scans demonstrate that EGT enhances classification accuracy compared to standard CNN training, achieving a statistically significant improvement in F1-score. Furthermore, the resulting explanation maps are more focused on tumor regions, enhancing the model’s interpretability. This approach offers a practical method for developing more reliable and transparent deep learning models for medical image analysis.
Keywords: Glioma classification, MRI, convolutional neural network, explainable AI, explanation-guided training, medical image analysis.
