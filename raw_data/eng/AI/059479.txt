The objective of this research is to investigate the scaling laws applicable to transfer learning processes in the field of machine learning. Traditional scaling laws have provided insights into model performance relative to resource allocation, yet their application in transfer learning remains under-explored. This study aims to fill this gap by systematically analyzing how model size, dataset scale, and computational resources influence the efficacy of transfer learning. Our approach involves utilizing a diverse set of neural networks, examining their performance across various tasks and domains, and identifying underlying patterns and principles. Key findings indicate that transfer learning performance does not linearly correlate with increased resources; instead, it exhibits distinct scaling behaviors that are contingent on task similarity and dataset characteristics. These observations suggest the presence of optimal transfer scenarios where performance gains plateau or even diminish despite additional resources. Our conclusions emphasize the nuanced nature of transfer learning scaling laws and propose guidelines for optimizing resource allocation in practical applications. The contributions of this work lie in enhancing the understanding of transfer learning dynamics and facilitating more efficient deployment of AI models in diverse settings. Key keywords include transfer learning, scaling laws, neural networks, and model optimization.
