This paper explores the application of transfer learning in material classification using convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The objective is to improve the accuracy of material classification by leveraging pre-trained CNN models and fine-tuning them on a smaller dataset of material images. A deep convolutional network architecture is employed, utilizing transfer learning to adapt the features learned from a large-scale image classification task to the material classification problem. The approach involves fine-tuning the pre-trained model on a dataset of images representing various materials, such as wood, metal, and fabric. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves significant improvements in classification accuracy compared to training a CNN from scratch. The findings suggest that transfer learning can effectively capture subtle material textures and patterns, leading to more accurate classification. This research contributes to the development of efficient material classification systems, with potential applications in computer vision, robotics, and interior design. Key contributions include the demonstration of transfer learning efficacy in material classification and the identification of optimal fine-tuning strategies for CNNs in this context. Relevant keywords: transfer learning, convolutional neural networks, material classification, computer vision, deep learning.
