This paper addresses the challenge of effective representation learning in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), a crucial aspect of decision-making and control in complex systems. Our objective is to develop a hierarchical representation learning framework that captures the underlying structure of MDPs, enabling more efficient and scalable decision-making. We propose a novel approach that leverages a hierarchical architecture to learn compact and informative representations of states and actions. Our method utilizes a combination of convolutional and recurrent neural networks to extract features from high-dimensional state spaces and model temporal dependencies. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of learning efficiency and decision-making performance, particularly in large and complex MDPs. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of hierarchical representation learning in MDPs, with significant implications for applications in robotics, game playing, and autonomous systems. Our contributions include a novel hierarchical architecture, a new loss function that encourages compact and informative representations, and a comprehensive evaluation framework for assessing the performance of representation learning methods in MDPs. Key keywords: Markov Decision Processes, Hierarchical Representation Learning, Deep Reinforcement Learning, Autonomous Systems.
