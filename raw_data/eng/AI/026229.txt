The objective of this study is to investigate whether different neural networks, despite varying architectures and initialization, converge to similar internal representations when trained on the same dataset. This question is critical for understanding the generalizability and interpretability of deep learning models. We employ a comparative analysis framework, training multiple neural network architectures—including convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and transformers—on benchmark datasets such as CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. To evaluate representation similarity, we utilize techniques such as canonical correlation analysis (CCA) and centered kernel alignment (CKA). Our findings reveal that while different networks achieve comparable task performance, their learned representations exhibit significant structural differences, particularly in deeper layers. However, certain high-level features show consistent patterns across architectures, suggesting partial convergence in learned representations. These results provide insights into the robustness and transferability of neural network representations, with implications for model interpretability, transfer learning, and ensemble methods. This work contributes to the broader understanding of representation learning in deep neural networks and highlights the importance of architectural choices in shaping learned features.  

**Keywords**: neural networks, representation learning, canonical correlation analysis, centered kernel alignment, deep learning, transfer learning.
