Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have achieved remarkable success in various computer vision tasks, but their high computational cost and memory requirements hinder deployment on resource-constrained devices. This paper introduces SlimConv, a novel approach to reduce channel redundancy in CNNs by strategically flipping weights. Our method identifies and flips weights within convolutional filters to minimize feature map redundancy, effectively compressing the network without significantly impacting accuracy. SlimConv utilizes an iterative process of weight flipping and fine-tuning to optimize the network for minimal redundancy. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, demonstrate that SlimConv achieves significant reductions in the number of parameters and FLOPs compared to state-of-the-art channel pruning techniques, while maintaining comparable or even improved accuracy. Specifically, SlimConv enables higher compression ratios than conventional pruning, demonstrating its efficacy in creating efficient CNN architectures. This work offers a practical solution for deploying complex CNNs on edge devices and contributes to the development of resource-efficient deep learning models.
Keywords: Convolutional Neural Networks, Model Compression, Channel Pruning, Weight Flipping, Deep Learning, Resource-Efficient CNNs.
