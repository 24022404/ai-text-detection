Abstract:

Accurate depth estimation from multiple camera views is a crucial task for various applications in computer vision, such as 3D reconstruction, autonomous navigation, and augmented reality. This paper introduces a novel approach to multi-view depth estimation using Epipolar Spatio-Temporal Networks (ESTN), which leverage both geometric constraints and temporal information inherent in multi-frame video sequences. Our proposed ESTN framework integrates epipolar geometry into a deep neural network architecture, enabling robust correspondence matching across views while exploiting temporal coherence to resolve ambiguities and enhance depth prediction accuracy. We design a specialized attention mechanism that aligns image features along epipolar lines and fuses spatial-temporal context, resulting in improved resilience to occlusions and varying lighting conditions. Extensive experiments are conducted on standard multi-view stereo benchmarks and real-world datasets, demonstrating that our method outperforms existing state-of-the-art approaches in terms of depth estimation accuracy, completeness, and efficiency. The results highlight the ability of ESTN to generate denser and more reliable depth maps, especially in challenging scenarios with dynamic scenes and non-Lambertian surfaces. This research advances the field of 3D computer vision by providing a scalable and effective solution for high-fidelity depth estimation from multi-view imagery, with potential applicability in robotics, virtual reality, and scene understanding. 

Keywords: multi-view depth estimation, epipolar geometry, spatio-temporal networks, deep learning, 3D reconstruction, computer vision.
