Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have shown promise in semi-supervised node classification, but often struggle with linear separability and generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) data. This paper investigates these limitations and proposes a novel graph convolution approach designed to improve both aspects. Our method, termed Adaptive Spectral Graph Convolution (ASGC), learns adaptive filter kernels in the spectral domain, explicitly optimizing for linear separability in the embedded node representations. ASGC leverages a meta-learning framework to enhance OOD generalization by simulating distribution shifts during training. We evaluate ASGC on a range of benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in classification accuracy compared to standard GCNs and other state-of-the-art semi-supervised learning techniques. Furthermore, we show that ASGC exhibits superior robustness and generalization performance under various OOD scenarios. The results highlight the effectiveness of adaptive spectral filtering and meta-learning in addressing the limitations of GCNs for semi-supervised classification, paving the way for more reliable and generalizable graph-based learning models.

Keywords: Graph Convolutional Networks, Semi-Supervised Learning, Out-of-Distribution Generalization, Spectral Graph Theory, Meta-Learning, Node Classification.
