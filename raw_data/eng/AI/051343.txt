Medical image analysis is a critical area in healthcare, yet the scarcity of labeled data often hinders the development of robust deep learning models. This paper addresses the challenge of training effective deep learning models with limited quantities of labeled medical images by introducing a novel surrogate supervision framework. The proposed approach leverages unlabeled data and synthetic annotations to create surrogate labels, enabling the model to learn meaningful representations without extensive manual labeling. We employ a combination of self-supervised learning and domain-specific augmentation techniques to enhance the quality of surrogate supervision. Experiments conducted on benchmark medical imaging datasets demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance compared to fully supervised models, even with significantly fewer labeled samples. The results highlight the framework's ability to generalize across diverse medical imaging tasks, including segmentation and classification. This work contributes a practical solution to the data scarcity problem in medical image analysis, offering a pathway for deploying deep learning in resource-constrained settings. The proposed approach has broad implications for improving diagnostic accuracy and reducing reliance on costly labeled datasets in healthcare applications.  

**Keywords**: medical image analysis, deep learning, surrogate supervision, self-supervised learning, data scarcity, healthcare AI.
