The management and analysis of time-series data is fundamental in various applications, including finance, healthcare, and IoT. However, preserving data privacy while maintaining data utility remains a significant challenge. This study addresses the critical issue of inferential privacy, focusing on the composition properties that balance privacy and accuracy in time-series data.

Methods/Approach: Our approach utilizes a novel framework integrating inferential statistical methods with privacy-preserving mechanisms specifically designed for time-series data. We develop a composite model that combines noise addition techniques with differential privacy principles to ensure robust privacy guarantees. This model is systematically analyzed to understand its impact on data utility across composed sequences of data.

Results/Findings: We demonstrate that our proposed method achieves superior privacy-accuracy trade-offs compared to traditional privacy models. Through empirical experiments on real-world time-series datasets, we validate that our framework can maintain high levels of privacy without significantly degrading the quality of the data for analytical purposes. Our results indicate an improved balance in protecting sensitive information while preserving essential temporal patterns.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a significant advancement in the field of inferential privacy by elucidating the composition properties for time-series data. Our findings offer practical insights into the integration of privacy-preserving measures with data analysis, fostering secure and effective applications in sectors reliant on time-series forecasting and monitoring. The model has the potential to be adapted and improved further for broader applications in privacy-sensitive data ecosystems.

Keywords: time-series data, inferential privacy, differential privacy, data utility, privacy-preserving mechanisms.
