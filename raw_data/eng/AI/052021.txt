Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have achieved state-of-the-art performance in a wide range of computer vision tasks. However, their reliance on padding strategies to maintain spatial dimensions during convolutional operations introduces the risk of "blind spots"—regions in the output where the receptive field may be artificially limited or information may be lost. This paper investigates the impact of standard padding techniques, such as zero-padding and reflection padding, on the feature extraction capabilities of CNNs. Through theoretical analysis and extensive empirical evaluation on benchmark image classification and segmentation datasets, we demonstrate that conventional padding can lead to systematic performance degradation, particularly near image boundaries. We introduce visualization tools to identify and quantify these blind spots, providing insights into their prevalence and effect on network decision-making. Furthermore, we propose alternative padding schemes and architectural modifications to mitigate these issues, leading to consistent improvements in model accuracy and robustness, especially in tasks sensitive to local image features. The findings underscore the importance of careful padding selection in CNN design and open avenues for further research on boundary-aware neural architectures. Keywords: Convolutional Neural Networks, padding, blind spots, image classification, feature extraction, boundary effects, neural network architecture.
