This paper presents a novel Markov Random Field (MRF)-based method for multispectral image fusion, designed to enhance the spatial and spectral quality of fused images. The proposed approach integrates an adaptive edge-guided interpolation technique within the MRF framework to effectively preserve edge structures and minimize spectral distortion during the fusion process. The method dynamically adjusts interpolation parameters in response to local edge information, ensuring that significant image features are maintained while reducing artifacts commonly associated with traditional fusion methods. Extensive experiments were conducted on publicly available multispectral datasets, demonstrating that the proposed technique outperforms conventional methods in terms of both visual quality and quantitative metrics such as peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity (SSIM). Results highlight improved detail preservation and color fidelity, particularly in regions with complex textures and edges. The proposed MRF-based adaptive fusion approach offers promising potential for applications in remote sensing, medical imaging, and surveillance, where high-fidelity multispectral image integration is critical. Key contributions include the integration of adaptive edge-guided interpolation within the MRF model and comprehensive evaluation on multiple datasets, establishing the approach as an effective solution for advanced multispectral image fusion. **Keywords:** MRF, multispectral image fusion, edge-guided interpolation, adaptive algorithm, image quality enhancement, remote sensing, image processing.
