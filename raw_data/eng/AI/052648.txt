Self-supervised learning (SSL) aims to learn representations from unlabeled data by training on pretext tasks. However, performance often degrades when pretext task characteristics significantly differ from downstream tasks. This paper introduces a novel approach to learn pretext-invariant representations, improving generalization across diverse downstream tasks. Our method, Pretext-Invariance Network (PIN), employs an adversarial learning framework to minimize the dependency of learned representations on specific pretext signals. PIN consists of an encoder that learns representations, a pretext classifier that predicts the pretext task, and an invariance discriminator that distinguishes between different pretext tasks based on the learned representations. We train the encoder to generate representations that are informative for the pretext classifier but indistinguishable by the invariance discriminator. Experiments on image classification and object detection benchmarks demonstrate that PIN consistently outperforms existing SSL methods, particularly when downstream tasks exhibit substantial differences from the pretraining pretext task. The learned representations exhibit improved robustness and transferability, leading to superior performance in novel environments. Keywords: Self-Supervised Learning, Representation Learning, Invariance, Adversarial Learning, Transfer Learning.
