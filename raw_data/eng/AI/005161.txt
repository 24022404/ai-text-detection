This paper presents insights from the NIPS 2016 Workshop on Representation Learning in Artificial and Biological Neural Networks (MLINI 2016), which explored the intersection of machine learning and neuroscience. The workshop aimed to address the fundamental challenge of understanding how both artificial and biological systems learn and represent information. A variety of approaches were discussed, including deep learning architectures, unsupervised learning techniques, and biologically inspired models. Key findings highlighted the potential of representation learning to bridge the gap between artificial intelligence and neuroscience, offering new perspectives on feature extraction, hierarchical learning, and generalization. The workshop also emphasized the importance of cross-disciplinary collaboration to advance the development of more efficient and interpretable models. Contributions included novel methodologies for improving representation learning in neural networks, as well as insights into how biological systems can inform the design of artificial systems. The outcomes of this workshop underscore the significance of representation learning in advancing both AI and neuroscience, with implications for applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and cognitive modeling.  

**Keywords**: representation learning, neural networks, deep learning, neuroscience, unsupervised learning, hierarchical learning, cross-disciplinary research.
