This paper addresses the growing need for transparency and interpretability in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) agents by introducing an action-conditional β-Variational Autoencoder (β-VAE) framework tailored for governance and efficacy analysis. The primary objective is to enhance the explainability of agent behaviors in complex environments by disentangling and representing the underlying factors influenced by both observations and action choices. Our approach integrates an action-conditional component into traditional β-VAE architectures, enabling the model to learn latent representations that capture causal relationships between actions and subsequent state transitions. Empirical evaluation was conducted on benchmark DRL tasks, demonstrating that the proposed method not only maintains competitive task performance but also offers more transparent and structured latent spaces. Compared to baseline DRL agents and conventional β-VAE models, our framework provides clearer insights into agent decision-making processes and supports post-hoc interpretability analyses. The results suggest that action-conditional disentanglement facilitates both system auditing and potential regulatory compliance. This research contributes a significant step towards trustworthy and governable DRL systems by bridging the gap between high performance and actionable transparency. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, β-VAE, transparency, interpretability, disentangled representation, action-conditional modeling, agent governance, explainable AI.
