This paper introduces the Encoded Prior Sliced Wasserstein AutoEncoder (EPSWAE), a novel approach for learning latent manifold representations. The EPSWAE leverages the Sliced Wasserstein distance to minimize the discrepancy between the learned latent space and a predefined prior distribution, while simultaneously employing an encoder network to map data samples into this structured latent space. By encoding a prior distribution directly into the autoencoder framework, EPSWAE encourages the formation of well-organized and interpretable latent manifolds. The method is evaluated on synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its ability to generate smoother latent spaces and improved reconstruction quality compared to traditional autoencoders and other Wasserstein autoencoder variants. Results show that EPSWAE facilitates enhanced downstream tasks, such as data generation and representation learning. The key contribution is a stable and efficient method for learning latent representations with explicit prior encoding, offering improvements in both reconstruction accuracy and latent space structure.

Keywords: Autoencoder, Wasserstein distance, Latent space, Manifold learning, Generative models, Representation learning.
