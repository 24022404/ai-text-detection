Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) tasks challenge agents to navigate in real-world environments following natural language instructions. Current VLN models often struggle with generalization to unseen environments due to overfitting to specific layouts and visual features present in the training data. To address this, we propose a novel data augmentation technique, Random Environmental Mixup (REM), which generates new training environments by blending visual features and navigational graphs from randomly selected existing environments. This encourages the agent to learn more robust and generalizable navigation strategies. We evaluate our approach on the Room-to-Room (R2R) and Room-for-Room (RxR) datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that REM significantly improves the agent's navigation performance and generalization ability, achieving state-of-the-art results on unseen environments. Specifically, REM improves success rate by X% on R2R unseen and Y% on RxR unseen splits compared to baseline models. Our findings indicate that manipulating environmental features through mixup augmentation is an effective strategy for enhancing VLN model generalization. Keywords: Vision-Language Navigation, Data Augmentation, Generalization, Environmental Mixup, Reinforcement Learning.
