This paper addresses the objective of predicting toxicity levels in chemical compounds efficiently, leveraging simple features and machine learning techniques. Our approach utilizes shallow neural networks and decision trees to model the complex relationships between molecular structures and their toxicological properties. By employing a combination of molecular descriptors and fingerprint representations as input features, our models achieve high accuracy in predicting toxicity while minimizing computational overhead. The results show that our proposed method outperforms existing state-of-the-art models in terms of prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. Key findings indicate that the use of shallow neural networks and decision trees enables effective feature selection and generalization, leading to improved performance on unseen data. The implications of this research are significant, as it enables the rapid screening of large chemical libraries for potential toxicities, facilitating safer drug discovery and environmental monitoring. Our contributions highlight the potential of simple, interpretable machine learning models in toxicology prediction, with relevant applications in cheminformatics, pharmacology, and environmental science, and key keywords include toxicity prediction, shallow neural networks, decision trees, cheminformatics, and machine learning.
