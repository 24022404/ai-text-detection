Objective: Accurate detection of active wildfires using Sentinel-2 imagery is often hindered by its moderate spatial resolution, limiting the early identification of small or low‐intensity fire events. This study introduces a convolutional neural network (CNN)–based super-resolution framework designed to enhance Sentinel-2 multispectral data and improve active fire detection performance.  
Methods: We develop a deep residual CNN model trained on matched high-resolution (PlanetScope) and Sentinel-2 image pairs to learn a mapping from low- to high-resolution reflectance. The upscaled images are then processed using a modified normalized difference fire index (NDFI) and a machine-learning–based classifier to identify active fire pixels. Model training employs data augmentation and spectral consistency loss to preserve radiometric fidelity across infrared and visible bands.  
Results: On a benchmark dataset spanning diverse vegetation types and fire regimes, our approach achieves a mean peak signal‐to‐noise ratio (PSNR) improvement of 2.8 dB and a structural similarity index (SSIM) gain of 0.12 over bicubic interpolation. Fire detection accuracy increases by 15 %, with a precision of 0.89 and recall of 0.85, outperforming both traditional interpolation and unsupervised super-resolution baselines.  
Conclusion: The proposed CNN-based super-resolution technique significantly enhances Sentinel-2 data utility for active fire monitoring, enabling earlier and more reliable detection of small fire events. This approach offers a scalable tool for wildfire management agencies and supports rapid response systems in environmental monitoring.  
Keywords: convolutional neural network, super-resolution, active fire detection, Sentinel-2, remote sensing, deep learning.
