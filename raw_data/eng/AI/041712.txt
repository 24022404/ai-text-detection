Hyperspectral imagery (HSI) often suffers from high dimensionality and limited labeled data, posing significant challenges for effective analysis and classification. This paper introduces a novel approach, Joint and Progressive Subspace Analysis (JPSA), integrated with Spatial-Spectral Manifold Alignment (SSMA), to address these challenges in semi-supervised hyperspectral dimensionality reduction. The proposed method leverages both labeled and unlabeled data to construct a unified low-dimensional subspace that preserves the intrinsic spatial and spectral relationships within the data. JPSA progressively refines the subspace by iteratively aligning the spatial and spectral manifolds, ensuring robust feature extraction and improved discriminability. Experimental results on benchmark hyperspectral datasets demonstrate that JPSA outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of classification accuracy and computational efficiency. The approach achieves superior performance by effectively balancing the trade-off between dimensionality reduction and information preservation, making it highly suitable for real-world applications such as remote sensing and environmental monitoring. This work contributes a scalable and efficient framework for semi-supervised HSI analysis, offering significant potential for advancing hyperspectral data processing in resource-constrained scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Hyperspectral imagery, dimensionality reduction, semi-supervised learning, manifold alignment, subspace analysis, spatial-spectral features.
