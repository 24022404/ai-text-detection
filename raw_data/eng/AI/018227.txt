Super-resolution (SR) techniques aim to enhance the resolution of images, enabling high-quality visual outputs from low-resolution inputs. While implicit image function-based methods have shown promise for arbitrary-scale SR, they often struggle with spatial consistency and fine-grained detail preservation. This paper introduces **UltraSR**, a novel framework that addresses these limitations by incorporating **spatial encoding** as a critical component. The proposed approach leverages a hybrid architecture that combines implicit neural representations with advanced spatial encoding mechanisms, enabling precise reconstruction of high-frequency details and improved spatial coherence across arbitrary scaling factors. Experiments demonstrate that UltraSR outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both quantitative metrics (e.g., PSNR, SSIM) and visual quality, particularly in preserving textures and edges. The results highlight the importance of spatial encoding in implicit image functions, offering a significant advancement in arbitrary-scale SR. This work not only provides a robust solution for practical applications such as medical imaging and satellite imagery but also opens new avenues for research in neural representation-based image processing.  

**Keywords**: Super-resolution, implicit image functions, spatial encoding, arbitrary-scale SR, neural representations, image reconstruction.
