Heterogeneous graphs, which consist of multiple types of nodes and edges, are increasingly prevalent in real-world applications such as social networks, knowledge graphs, and recommendation systems. However, extracting universal node embeddings that effectively capture the diverse relationships and structural properties of such graphs remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces *PanRep*, a novel graph neural network (GNN) framework designed to generate universal node embeddings for heterogeneous graphs. PanRep leverages a multi-layer GNN architecture combined with a hierarchical attention mechanism to model both node-level and edge-level heterogeneity. The framework is trained using a self-supervised learning approach, enabling it to generalize across diverse graph structures without requiring task-specific labels. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that PanRep outperforms state-of-the-art methods in tasks such as node classification, link prediction, and clustering, achieving improvements of up to 12% in accuracy. Furthermore, PanRep's embeddings exhibit strong transferability, enabling effective performance on downstream tasks with minimal fine-tuning. The proposed framework provides a scalable and versatile solution for analyzing heterogeneous graphs, with potential applications in recommendation systems, knowledge graph completion, and network analysis.  

**Keywords**: heterogeneous graphs, graph neural networks, node embeddings, self-supervised learning, attention mechanism, transfer learning.
