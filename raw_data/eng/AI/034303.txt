This paper presents an end-to-end trainable framework for multi-instance pose estimation using transformer-based architectures. The objective is to address the challenges of accurately detecting and estimating poses for multiple instances in complex scenes, where occlusions and overlapping instances often degrade performance. We propose a novel approach that leverages the self-attention mechanism of transformers to model global dependencies among keypoints, enabling robust pose estimation even in crowded environments. Our method integrates a multi-instance detection module with a pose estimation network, allowing for joint optimization of both tasks. The framework is trained end-to-end, eliminating the need for complex post-processing steps. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in accuracy and robustness compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly in scenarios with high instance density. The proposed approach achieves a 5% higher mean average precision (mAP) on the COCO dataset and shows superior generalization across diverse datasets. This work contributes to the field by introducing a scalable and efficient solution for multi-instance pose estimation, with potential applications in robotics, augmented reality, and human-computer interaction. Key innovations include the integration of transformers for global context modeling and the end-to-end trainable architecture, which simplifies the pipeline while enhancing performance.  

**Keywords**: Pose estimation, transformers, multi-instance detection, end-to-end learning, self-attention, keypoint detection.
