This paper introduces a novel approach for training Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) by integrating self-labeling and self-attention mechanisms, aimed at enhancing the generation performance and stability of GAN models. The objective of our research is to tackle the challenges of mode collapse and unstable training processes commonly observed in GAN frameworks. Our proposed method, referred to as Unified Self-Labeling and Self-Attention GAN (USLSA-GAN), employs a self-labeling strategy that dynamically adjusts class labels based on generated data, thereby promoting diversity and more robust learning. Additionally, a self-attention mechanism is incorporated to improve the model's capability to capture intricate dependencies in data, resulting in higher quality and more coherent outputs. Experimental results demonstrate that USLSA-GAN outperforms state-of-the-art GAN variants in terms of image quality, diversity, and training stability, as evidenced by superior performance metrics and visual assessments. Furthermore, our method reduces the computational complexity associated with the typical training process. In conclusion, the integration of self-labeling and self-attention in GANs presents a significant advancement in generative modeling techniques, offering potential applications in image synthesis, data augmentation, and other AI-driven fields requiring high-fidelity data generation. Keywords: GAN, self-labeling, self-attention, mode collapse, training stability, image synthesis.
