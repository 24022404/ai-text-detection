The burgeoning field of causal discovery aims to unravel the underlying mechanisms in complex systems by identifying causal relationships. Traditional approaches often rely on observational data, which limits their effectiveness in real-world applications where interventions are possible. This paper introduces a novel framework for collaborative causal discovery using atomic interventions to enhance the accuracy and robustness of causal inference.

Methods/Approach: We propose a collaborative approach that integrates atomic interventions—minimal and highly controlled perturbations to the system—with existing causal discovery algorithms. Our method leverages both observational and interventional data to improve the precision of causal structures. The framework is designed to facilitate scalable and efficient collaboration across distributed systems and datasets, using a blend of statistical modeling and machine learning techniques.

Results/Findings: Through extensive experiments, our approach demonstrates significant improvements in identifying true causal relationships compared to traditional methods. Our collaborative causal discovery framework outperforms conventional algorithms particularly in complex, high-dimensional datasets where purely observational methods struggle. Furthermore, the use of atomic interventions allows for a more granular analysis of causal mechanisms, yielding insights that would otherwise remain obscured.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed framework advances the field of causal discovery by providing a robust tool for researchers and practitioners to accurately infer causality in diverse domains. Our findings suggest that incorporating atomic interventions can greatly enhance causal inference, offering potential applications in fields such as biology, economics, and social sciences. This research paves the way for more sophisticated causal models that better reflect the dynamics of real-world systems.

Keywords: Causal discovery, atomic interventions, collaborative framework, observational data, interventional data, machine learning, causal inference.
