This paper addresses the challenge of image de-quantization, a critical task in image processing aimed at recovering high-quality, continuous-tone images from quantized (low-bit-depth) inputs. Traditional methods often struggle with artifacts and loss of detail, particularly in complex textures and fine structures. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel approach leveraging generative models as priors to guide the de-quantization process. Specifically, we employ a pre-trained generative adversarial network (GAN) to model the natural image manifold, enabling the reconstruction of high-fidelity images from quantized data. Our method integrates a gradient-based optimization framework that iteratively refines the output by minimizing a loss function combining perceptual quality and pixel-level accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing techniques in terms of both visual quality and quantitative metrics, such as peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM). The proposed method is particularly effective in preserving fine details and reducing artifacts, making it suitable for applications in medical imaging, satellite imagery, and digital restoration. This work highlights the potential of generative models as powerful priors for image enhancement tasks and opens new avenues for research in image reconstruction and restoration.  

**Keywords**: Image de-quantization, generative models, GAN, image reconstruction, perceptual quality, optimization.
