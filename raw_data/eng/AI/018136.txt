This paper addresses the challenge of classification in imbalanced datasets, where misclassification costs vary depending on the specific example. Traditional decision tree algorithms often fail to account for these example-dependent costs, leading to suboptimal performance in real-world applications. To tackle this issue, we propose an ensemble of example-dependent cost-sensitive decision trees, which integrates cost-sensitive learning directly into the tree construction and ensemble aggregation processes. Our approach leverages a weighted splitting criterion during tree growth, ensuring that each decision node minimizes the expected misclassification cost. Additionally, we employ a cost-sensitive boosting mechanism to enhance the ensemble's ability to generalize across diverse cost scenarios. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing cost-sensitive and ensemble-based approaches, achieving significant improvements in cost minimization and classification accuracy. The proposed framework is particularly effective in applications such as fraud detection and medical diagnosis, where example-dependent costs are critical. This research contributes a novel, scalable solution for handling imbalanced datasets with varying misclassification costs, offering practical insights for deploying cost-sensitive machine learning models in real-world systems.  

**Keywords**: cost-sensitive learning, decision trees, ensemble methods, imbalanced datasets, example-dependent costs, classification.
