This paper proposes a novel spectral-spatial-dependent global learning framework to address the challenges of insufficient and imbalanced hyperspectral image classification. The objective is to develop an effective method that can learn discriminative features from limited and class-imbalanced training data. Our approach integrates a spectral-spatial-dependent module to capture both spectral and spatial information, and a global learning strategy to adaptively weigh the importance of different classes. The results show that our framework outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of overall accuracy, average accuracy, and Kappa coefficient. The key findings indicate that our method can effectively handle insufficient and imbalanced data, and achieve better classification performance. The conclusion is that the proposed framework provides a new perspective for hyperspectral image classification, and has potential applications in remote sensing and environmental monitoring. The novelty of this research lies in its ability to jointly model spectral and spatial dependencies, and to adaptively handle class imbalance. Key keywords: hyperspectral image classification, spectral-spatial-dependent learning, global learning, class imbalance, remote sensing.
