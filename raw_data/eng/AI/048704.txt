This paper investigates the effectiveness of actor-critic and direct policy search methods in reinforcement learning with a specific focus on sample complexity. Sample complexity, defined as the number of samples required to achieve a particular level of performance, is a critical factor in the practical deployment of reinforcement learning systems. The study aims to provide a thorough comparison of these methods to determine which is more efficient under various conditions.

Methods/Approach: We employ a comparative analysis using several benchmark environments and scenarios typical in reinforcement learning. The actor-critic framework is considered using specific algorithms that separate the actor (policy) and critic (value function) elements, while direct policy search methods are implemented using algorithms that optimize policy parameters directly. Both approaches are evaluated based on their sample efficiency and convergence behavior.

Results/Findings: Our experiments reveal that actor-critic methods generally require fewer samples than direct policy search methods to converge to an optimal or near-optimal policy in environments with moderate complexity. However, in environments characterized by high dimensionality or significant non-linearities, direct policy search demonstrates competitive or superior sample efficiency. Additionally, the variance in performance was observed to be broader in actor-critic methods due to their reliance on value function estimates.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings underscore the importance of selecting the appropriate reinforcement learning method based on environment characteristics and computational constraints. This research contributes to the existing body of knowledge by providing insights into the trade-offs between actor-critic and direct policy search algorithms, particularly concerning sample complexity. It can guide practitioners in choosing suitable methods for deploying reinforcement learning in resource-constrained contexts. Potential applications of this work include autonomous systems, where timely and efficient learning is critical. 

Keywords: reinforcement learning, sample complexity, actor-critic, direct policy search, algorithm comparison, convergence behavior.
