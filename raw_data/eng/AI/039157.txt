Abstract:

Distribution inference risks have become increasingly significant as data-driven systems are employed for sensitive and high-stakes decision-making. This paper presents a formal framework for assessing and quantifying the risks associated with inferring underlying data distribution properties from observed or released information. We introduce mathematical definitions to rigorously characterize distribution inference risk and propose novel metrics to evaluate potential exposure in various data sharing and analysis scenarios. Our approach leverages advances in statistical modeling and information-theoretic techniques to formalize risk quantification while accommodating both parametric and non-parametric data models. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our methods through empirical studies on real-world datasets, comparing their performance against existing risk assessment techniques. The results reveal that our framework provides more precise and actionable risk estimates, enabling practitioners to identify vulnerabilities in data release mechanisms and model outputs. We discuss the implications for privacy-preserving machine learning, data anonymization, and secure data sharing, highlighting how our contributions support the design of more robust and trustworthy information systems. Keywords: distribution inference, risk estimation, information security, data privacy, statistical modeling, information theory.
