This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the resolution of brain MRI images, a crucial aspect for accurate diagnosis and treatment planning. Our objective is to develop a novel super-resolution technique that leverages overcomplete dictionaries and nonlocal similarity to improve image quality. We propose a method that utilizes a dictionary learning approach to capture intricate image features, combined with a nonlocal similarity metric to exploit the redundant information within the image. Our approach is tested on a dataset of brain MRI images, yielding significant improvements in image resolution and quality. The results demonstrate that our technique outperforms existing super-resolution methods, with enhanced visual clarity and reduced artifacts. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to effectively integrate dictionary learning and nonlocal similarity, resulting in a powerful tool for brain MRI image enhancement. This work has important implications for medical imaging applications, particularly in the fields of neurology and radiology, where high-resolution images are essential for precise diagnosis and treatment. Key keywords: super-resolution, brain MRI, overcomplete dictionaries, nonlocal similarity, medical imaging, image enhancement.
