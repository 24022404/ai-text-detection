Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has shown remarkable success in solving complex decision-making tasks, yet challenges remain in optimizing policy gradient estimations due to the inherent bias-variance tradeoff. This paper introduces a novel approach that merges deterministic policy gradient estimations with varied bias-variance tradeoffs to enhance the stability and efficiency of DRL algorithms. The proposed method leverages a hybrid framework that dynamically adjusts the weighting of high-bias and low-variance estimators, as well as low-bias and high-variance estimators, to achieve a balanced and effective learning process. Through extensive experiments on benchmark environments, the approach demonstrates significant improvements in convergence speed and final performance compared to traditional deterministic and stochastic policy gradient methods. Key findings reveal that the adaptive tradeoff mechanism not only mitigates the instability issues commonly associated with policy gradient methods but also enhances generalization across diverse tasks. The results highlight the potential of this approach for real-world applications, such as robotics and autonomous systems, where reliable and efficient learning is critical. This work contributes to the advancement of DRL by providing a robust and scalable solution for optimizing policy gradient estimations, paving the way for more effective and adaptable reinforcement learning systems.  

**Keywords**: Deep Reinforcement Learning, Policy Gradient Methods, Bias-Variance Tradeoff, Deterministic Policy Gradients, Adaptive Learning, Convergence Stability.
