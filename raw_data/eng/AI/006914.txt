This paper presents a novel approach to 3D face reconstruction using color photometric stereo under uncalibrated near point light sources. The objective is to develop a robust and accurate method that minimizes the need for precise light source calibration, a common limitation in existing techniques. Our method employs a two-stage optimization process: first, estimating light source parameters and surface normals simultaneously, followed by integrating the normals to obtain the 3D face geometry. We introduce a novel cost function that leverages color information to enhance the accuracy of light source estimation, even with near light sources. Experiments on synthetic and real-world face datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction accuracy compared to existing uncalibrated photometric stereo techniques. The results show improved robustness to variations in lighting conditions and facial reflectance. The proposed method offers a practical solution for 3D face scanning in uncontrolled environments, with potential applications in biometrics, computer animation, and medical imaging.

Keywords: 3D Face Reconstruction, Photometric Stereo, Uncalibrated Lighting, Near Point Lights, Color Imaging, Computer Vision.
