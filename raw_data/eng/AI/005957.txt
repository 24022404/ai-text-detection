Understanding slow modes in dynamical systems is critical for analyzing long-term system behavior in fields such as physics, chemistry, and biology. This paper investigates the capabilities and limitations of time-lagged autoencoders as a data-driven approach for discovering slow collective variables within complex dynamical systems. Time-lagged autoencoders leverage neural network architectures to learn compact representations that emphasize slowly evolving features by training on time-shifted state pairs. We systematically evaluate their effectiveness on benchmark dynamical systems, comparing their performance with traditional methods such as Time-lagged Independent Component Analysis (TICA). Our results demonstrate that time-lagged autoencoders can uncover nonlinear slow modes with higher fidelity under certain conditions, outperforming linear dimensionality reduction approaches in capturing nontrivial dynamical processes. However, our analysis also reveals inherent limitations, including sensitivity to network architecture, time-lag parameter selection, and the potential for spurious feature learning in high-noise or undersampled regimes. We discuss strategies to mitigate these challenges and provide insights into the practical deployment of time-lagged autoencoders for slow mode discovery. This work advances the understanding of deep learning-based methods for dynamical system analysis and outlines promising directions for future research in dimensionality reduction and nonlinear dynamical system modeling.

Keywords: time-lagged autoencoder, slow mode discovery, dynamical systems, dimensionality reduction, deep learning, neural networks, TICA
