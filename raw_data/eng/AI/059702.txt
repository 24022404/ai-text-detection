Abstract:

The rapid evolution of automotive cyber-physical systems (CPS) has intensified the demand for accurate and real-time scene understanding, integral to autonomous driving and advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS). This paper presents a novel framework for accelerating deep neural networks (DNNs) tailored to the unique constraints of automotive environments, including limited computational resources, stringent latency requirements, and safety-critical operations. Our approach combines model compression techniques, such as pruning and quantization, with hardware-aware neural architecture optimization to achieve substantial reductions in inference time and memory consumption without significant loss of accuracy. Experimental evaluations are conducted on real-world automotive datasets and a range of embedded hardware platforms, demonstrating that the optimized networks achieve up to 50% faster inference and 40% lower energy consumption compared to standard DNN baselines, while maintaining state-of-the-art performance in object detection and semantic segmentation tasks. The results highlight not only the efficiency gains but also the practical feasibility of deploying advanced scene understanding capabilities in resource-constrained automotive CPS. Our methodology contributes to safer, more responsive, and energy-efficient autonomous vehicle systems, and can be adapted for a wide range of edge-based scene understanding applications. Keywords: deep neural networks, scene understanding, automotive cyber-physical systems, model compression, real-time inference, embedded systems, autonomous vehicles.
