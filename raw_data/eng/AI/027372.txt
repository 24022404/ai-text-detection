This paper investigates the vulnerability of deep neural networks to membership model inversion attacks, a novel threat that combines membership inference and model inversion techniques. The primary objective is to assess the extent to which adversaries can exploit model outputs to both determine if a specific datapoint was part of the training set (membership inference) and reconstruct sensitive input features (model inversion), thereby posing significant privacy risks. We propose a combined attack framework that leverages confidence scores and gradient information from target models to increase the success of inferring membership while simultaneously generating partial input reconstructions. Experimental evaluations are conducted on benchmark datasets using popular deep network architectures, including convolutional and fully connected models. The results demonstrate that our combined attack significantly outperforms traditional standalone membership inference and model inversion approaches, achieving higher accuracy and reconstruction quality, particularly against unprotected models. Furthermore, we analyze the effectiveness of common defense mechanisms and discuss their limitations in mitigating such combined attacks. These findings highlight the urgent need for more robust privacy-preserving techniques in deep learning systems. Our work provides new insights into attack vectors on machine learning models and offers guidance for the development of stronger defenses. Keywords: deep learning, privacy, membership inference, model inversion, adversarial attacks, neural networks.
