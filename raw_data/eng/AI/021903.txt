Video classification at scale remains a challenging task due to the complexity of processing large volumes of frame-level features while maintaining computational efficiency. This paper introduces **NeXtVLAD**, a novel neural network architecture designed to efficiently aggregate frame-level features for large-scale video classification. The proposed model addresses the limitations of traditional approaches by leveraging a lightweight yet powerful feature aggregation mechanism, which reduces computational overhead without compromising accuracy. NeXtVLAD employs a hierarchical structure to capture both local and global temporal dependencies, enabling robust representation learning across diverse video datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that NeXtVLAD achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets, outperforming existing methods in terms of both accuracy and inference speed. Furthermore, the model's scalability and efficiency make it suitable for real-world applications, such as video recommendation systems and content-based retrieval. This work contributes a significant advancement in video understanding by providing a computationally efficient solution for large-scale video classification tasks.  

**Keywords**: Video classification, feature aggregation, neural networks, large-scale datasets, computational efficiency, NeXtVLAD.
