Offline meta-reinforcement learning (meta-RL) enables rapid adaptation to new tasks by leveraging previously collected datasets. However, its performance is often limited by the distribution shift between the offline data and the online adaptation phase. To address this, we introduce a novel offline meta-RL framework with online self-supervision. Our approach pre-trains a meta-policy offline on a diverse dataset of tasks and then employs self-supervised learning during online adaptation to extract relevant features from the agent's own experience. Specifically, we use contrastive learning to learn state representations that are invariant to task-irrelevant variations while capturing task-specific dynamics. This allows the agent to quickly identify the underlying task and adapt its policy accordingly. We evaluate our method on several challenging meta-RL benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements in sample efficiency and final performance compared to existing offline meta-RL algorithms. The results highlight the effectiveness of online self-supervision for mitigating the distribution shift problem and improving the adaptability of meta-RL agents. Keywords: Meta-Reinforcement Learning, Offline Learning, Self-Supervised Learning, Contrastive Learning, Adaptation.
