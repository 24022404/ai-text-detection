Visual Question Answering (VQA) traditionally depends on large sets of question-answer pairs for training, posing challenges in terms of data collection and model robustness. This paper introduces a novel approach to VQA that eliminates the need for direct question-answer pair training. Our primary objective is to investigate the feasibility of a VQA system that leverages unsupervised learning techniques to understand visual content and derive answers without pre-defined Q&A datasets. We employ a combination of advanced neural network architectures and transfer learning from pre-trained visual models to comprehend and infer answers from images. The proposed method dynamically synthesizes potential questions and derives answers through a context-aware inference mechanism. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive accuracy when compared to traditional VQA methods while significantly reducing the amount of labeled training data required. This research contributes a new paradigm for VQA, facilitating adaptability to diverse visual domains and enhancing scalability for real-world applications. Key innovations include the incorporation of visual context to generate implicit queries and a robust inference model that adapts to varying visual stimuli. Potential applications span areas such as automated visual content analysis, enhanced accessibility tools, and dynamic interactive systems. Key keywords: Visual Question Answering, unsupervised learning, neural networks, transfer learning, context-aware inference.
