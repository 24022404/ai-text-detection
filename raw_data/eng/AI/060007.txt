This paper presents a novel approach to fine-grained urban traffic flow inference, leveraging the structural information of road networks to enhance prediction accuracy. The objective is to develop a model that can effectively capture the complex spatial and temporal dependencies of urban traffic flow. Our method utilizes a graph-based neural network architecture, incorporating road network topology and traffic signal control data to improve the inference of traffic flow. The results show that our approach outperforms existing methods, achieving a significant reduction in mean absolute error and mean squared error. The key findings highlight the importance of integrating road network guidance into traffic flow inference models, enabling more accurate and informed decision-making for urban traffic management. This research contributes to the development of intelligent transportation systems, with potential applications in traffic signal control, route optimization, and congestion mitigation. Key keywords: urban traffic flow inference, road network analysis, graph-based neural networks, traffic signal control, intelligent transportation systems.
