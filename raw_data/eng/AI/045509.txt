Partitioning problems are fundamental in various domains, including data clustering, image segmentation, and network analysis. Traditional approaches often rely on predefined distance metrics, which may not optimally capture the underlying structure of the data. This paper introduces a novel large-margin metric learning framework tailored for partitioning problems. The proposed method learns a task-specific distance metric that maximizes the margin between different partitions, thereby enhancing the quality of the resulting clusters or segments. We formulate the problem as a convex optimization task and develop an efficient algorithm to solve it, leveraging gradient-based techniques and iterative refinement. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of clustering accuracy and robustness to noise. The learned metrics also exhibit strong generalization capabilities across diverse applications. This work contributes a scalable and adaptable solution to partitioning problems, with potential implications for improving performance in unsupervised learning tasks and real-world data analysis scenarios.  

**Keywords**: metric learning, partitioning problems, large-margin optimization, clustering, unsupervised learning.
