Abstract:

This paper addresses the challenge of recovering underlying signals in the presence of unknown noise distributions through the development of nonparametric deconvolution models. Traditional parametric approaches to deconvolution are often limited by strict assumptions regarding noise and signal distributions, which can hinder their applicability in complex, real-world information technology systems. To overcome these limitations, we propose a flexible nonparametric framework that leverages kernel density estimation and empirical characteristic function methods, allowing for accurate signal recovery without prior distributional assumptions. The proposed models are evaluated through simulations and real-world datasets, demonstrating superior performance in terms of mean squared error and reconstruction accuracy compared to conventional parametric deconvolution techniques. Our results indicate robust applicability across diverse domains, such as image processing, signal recovery, and communication systems. The primary contributions of this work include an adaptable and data-driven deconvolution strategy, reduced reliance on restrictive probabilistic models, and improved handling of heterogeneous and unknown noise sources. These findings highlight the practical value of nonparametric deconvolution in modern IT applications, promoting more reliable data recovery and analysis in uncertain environments. Key keywords: nonparametric deconvolution, signal recovery, kernel density estimation, empirical characteristic function, noise robustness, IT applications.
