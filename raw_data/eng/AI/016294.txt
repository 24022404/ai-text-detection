Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) for 3D object detection is a critical challenge in autonomous driving and robotics, where labeled data in target domains are often scarce. This paper introduces **ST3D++**, a novel denoised self-training framework designed to improve the performance of 3D object detection models in unsupervised target domains. The proposed approach addresses the limitations of existing methods by incorporating a robust denoising mechanism to reduce pseudo-label noise during self-training, which is a common issue in UDA tasks. ST3D++ leverages a multi-stage training pipeline, including domain-specific data augmentation, pseudo-label refinement, and iterative model optimization, to enhance the quality of predictions in the target domain. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that ST3D++ achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods by a significant margin. The framework also exhibits strong generalization capabilities across diverse 3D environments, making it a promising solution for real-world applications. This work contributes to advancing UDA techniques for 3D object detection, offering a scalable and effective approach for deploying detection models in unlabeled or under-annotated domains.  

**Keywords**: Unsupervised Domain Adaptation, 3D Object Detection, Self-training, Denoising, Autonomous Driving, Pseudo-label Refinement.
