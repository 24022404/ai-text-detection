This paper addresses the critical challenge of user association in 5G and beyond networks, where the increasing density of small cells and heterogeneous network architectures pose significant optimization problems. Our objective is to develop transferable and distributed user association policies that can efficiently allocate users to base stations, minimizing interference and maximizing network throughput. We propose a novel approach based on machine learning and graph theory, which enables the derivation of optimal user association strategies in a distributed manner. Our method utilizes a transfer learning framework to adapt policies across different network scenarios, ensuring seamless performance under varying conditions. Simulation results demonstrate that our approach outperforms traditional user association methods, achieving significant gains in network throughput and user satisfaction. The key findings of this research highlight the potential of transferable and distributed user association policies to enhance the efficiency and scalability of 5G and beyond networks, with important implications for network design and optimization. Relevant keywords: 5G networks, user association, transfer learning, distributed optimization, network throughput, small cells, heterogeneous networks.
