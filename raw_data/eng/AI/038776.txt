This paper presents a full probabilistic model designed to address the challenges of yes-no type crowdsourcing in multi-class classification tasks. The objective is to improve the accuracy and reliability of crowdsourced labels, which are often noisy and inconsistent due to the varying expertise of annotators. The proposed model leverages a probabilistic framework to estimate the true class labels by integrating the responses of multiple annotators, each providing binary (yes-no) answers to specific questions about the data. The approach incorporates latent variables to model annotator reliability and class priors, enabling robust inference even in the presence of ambiguous or conflicting annotations. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the model outperforms existing methods in terms of classification accuracy and label consistency. The findings highlight the model's ability to effectively aggregate noisy crowdsourced data, making it a valuable tool for applications requiring scalable and cost-effective labeling solutions. This research contributes to the field of crowdsourcing by providing a novel, probabilistic approach that enhances the quality of multi-class classification tasks, with potential applications in areas such as image annotation, sentiment analysis, and data labeling for machine learning.  

**Keywords**: crowdsourcing, probabilistic model, multi-class classification, yes-no labeling, annotator reliability, label aggregation.
