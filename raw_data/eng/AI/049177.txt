This paper addresses the challenge of Sketch-based Image Retrieval (SBIR), focusing on the development of an unsupervised framework to enhance retrieval accuracy without requiring annotated data. The objective is to bridge the semantic gap between freehand sketches and real-world images, which traditionally poses difficulties in accurate matching due to the differing data domains. Our approach introduces an innovative model that leverages generative adversarial networks (GANs) to learn a shared latent space, effectively mapping both sketches and images for comparative analysis. The unsupervised nature of the model is facilitated by an adversarial training process, optimizing the ability to discern semantic similarities without direct supervision. Key findings include a significant improvement in retrieval precision and recall metrics compared to existing supervised methods. This advancement delineates the potential of unsupervised methods in reducing the dependency on extensive labeled datasets, thereby broadening accessibility and applicability. Implications of this research underscore its contribution to fields such as digital content management and augmented reality, where efficient and scalable image retrieval systems are crucial. The study accentuates the novelty of leveraging unsupervised techniques in SBIR, providing a foundation for future developments in cross-modal retrieval tasks. Key keywords include SBIR, unsupervised learning, generative adversarial networks, semantic gap, and image retrieval.
