This research addresses the challenge of developing reliable offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms, specifically in situations characterized by partial coverage. The study focuses on establishing PAC (Probably Approximately Correct) bounds and enhancing the model's performance through novel pessimistic model-based approaches.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a pessimistic model-based offline RL framework that integrates posterior sampling strategies. Our approach accounts for uncertainties in the offline setting by modeling potential discrepancies between the offline data distribution and unexplored areas of the state-action space. A key innovation is the development of PAC bounds that provide probabilistic guarantees on the performance of the learned policy, even when access to exhaustive coverage is limited.

Results/Findings: The proposed method demonstrates superior performance in various RL benchmarks, particularly in environments with incomplete coverage that challenge traditional model-based approaches. Our empirical evaluations show significant improvements in policy reliability and decision-making accuracy compared to state-of-the-art offline RL techniques. The posterior sampling mechanism effectively mitigates overestimation of rewards in unseen states, enhancing overall policy robustness.

Conclusion/Implications: This study contributes to the field of offline RL by offering a theoretical foundation for pessimism under partial coverage, backed by strong empirical results. The insights gained have broader implications, potentially influencing the development of RL systems for real-world applications where data collection is constrained or costly. Keywords related to this work include offline RL, PAC bounds, posterior sampling, pessimistic modeling, and partial coverage.
