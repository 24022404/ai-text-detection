This paper introduces the concept of Wavelet Networks, a pioneering approach to scale-equivariant learning directly from raw waveforms. Traditional neural networks often struggle with effectively capturing variations across different scales and frequencies. Our research aims to address this challenge by leveraging the unique properties of wavelets to enhance learning models' adaptability and performance.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel architecture that integrates wavelet transform techniques into neural network frameworks, thus facilitating scale equivariance and enabling efficient feature extraction from raw signals. The model employs multiresolution analysis to dynamically adjust to varying scales, utilizing wavelet coefficients for training without resorting to preprocessed or external feature inputs. Key to the design is the wavelet transformation layer, which harmonizes with backpropagation to optimize network parameters.

Results/Findings: Our evaluation demonstrates that Wavelet Networks outperform conventional models in various tasks involving time-series data, including signal classification and anomaly detection. The results indicate significant improvements in accuracy and robustness against scale variations compared to state-of-the-art methods. Furthermore, experimental comparisons demonstrate notably reduced computational overhead while maintaining high precision.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of Wavelet Networks signifies a substantial advancement in the domain of raw waveform analysis. By enabling scale-equivariant learning, this approach not only broadens the applicability of machine learning models to more diverse signal processing tasks but also offers insights into developing efficient, energy-conscious neural networks. The potential applications of our findings span across numerous fields, including audio processing, telecommunications, and IoT sensor data analysis. Keywords: Wavelet Networks, Scale Equivariance, Raw Waveforms, Multiresolution Analysis, Signal Classification.
