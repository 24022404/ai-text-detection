Understanding which regions in a video are most relevant to human observers is crucial for a wide range of computer vision applications, including video summarization, saliency detection, and human-computer interaction. This paper presents a novel approach for learning to automatically attend to significant regions in video sequences by leveraging eye fixation data collected from human viewers. We introduce a deep learning framework that integrates spatiotemporal features from video frames with supervised attention mechanisms trained using recorded fixations. By modeling where viewers naturally focus, our method adapts attention maps across both spatial and temporal dimensions, enabling more accurate identification of regions of interest. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our model outperforms existing saliency prediction and attention modeling techniques in terms of precision and recall. Furthermore, ablation studies confirm the importance of incorporating eye fixation data for guiding the attention mechanism. The proposed framework is generalizable and can be easily integrated into downstream video analysis tasks, enhancing performance in areas such as activity recognition, object detection, and video captioning. Our findings highlight the potential of human-driven attention signals for advancing automatic video understanding. Keywords: eye fixations, attention mechanism, video analysis, saliency prediction, deep learning, spatiotemporal modeling.
