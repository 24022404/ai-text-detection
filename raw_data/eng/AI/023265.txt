Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (SNE) has emerged as a fundamental technique for visualizing high-dimensional data by mapping it onto lower-dimensional spaces while preserving neighborhood structures. This tutorial and survey paper provides a comprehensive overview of SNE with an emphasis on both Gaussian and Student-t distribution-based variants. We start by outlining the core objectives of dimensionality reduction and the importance of faithful visual representation in fields such as machine learning, pattern recognition, and data exploration. Our approach involves a detailed exposition of the original SNE algorithm, its mathematical formulation using Gaussian distributions, and the subsequent advancement to t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE), which employs the Student-t distribution to address crowding and cluster separation issues. We summarize the methodological differences, highlight the practical considerations for parameter selection, and present performance comparisons across synthetic and real-world datasets. Key findings indicate that Student-t distribution-based embeddings often yield superior cluster visualization and scalability, while Gaussian-based methods provide more interpretable neighborhood relationships in specific contexts. The paper concludes with insights regarding current challenges, best practices for application, and emerging trends such as parametric embeddings and deep learning integrations. This work serves as both an instructional resource and a reference guide, supporting researchers and practitioners in selecting and refining SNE techniques for effective high-dimensional data analysis. Keywords: dimensionality reduction, SNE, t-SNE, Gaussian distribution, Student-t distribution, data visualization, high-dimensional representation.
