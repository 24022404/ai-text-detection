This paper presents Transformer-XL, an advanced architecture for natural language processing that overcomes the limitations of fixed-length context windows in traditional transformer models. The primary objective is to enable modeling of long-range dependencies more effectively, thereby improving the understanding and generation of coherent language over extended sequences. Transformer-XL introduces a novel segment-level recurrence mechanism combined with an improved relative positional encoding scheme, allowing the model to reuse hidden states from previous segments without context fragmentation. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets for language modeling and sequence learning demonstrate that Transformer-XL significantly outperforms existing transformer-based models in terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency. Empirical results indicate superior performance on long-term dependency tasks, achieving new state-of-the-art results in perplexity measurements. The proposed approach offers enhanced scalability for large-scale language modeling applications and provides deeper insights into the capabilities of attentive neural architectures. Transformer-XL’s ability to capture context beyond fixed-length constraints makes it an essential advancement for tasks such as text generation, document summarization, and question answering. Keywords: Transformer-XL, long-range dependencies, language modeling, recurrence, attention mechanisms, natural language processing.
