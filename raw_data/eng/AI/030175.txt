Abstract:

Tabular data remains a foundational element in numerous domains such as finance, healthcare, and scientific research, yet the optimal approach for modeling this data type continues to be a subject of debate. This paper investigates the effectiveness of deep learning techniques compared to traditional machine learning methods for tabular data analysis. We systematically evaluate a range of deep neural network architectures alongside established algorithms such as gradient boosting machines and random forests across multiple real-world tabular datasets. Our experiments assess performance based on standard classification and regression metrics, model interpretability, and computational efficiency. The results reveal that, despite significant advancements in deep learning, traditional methods often outperform or match deep learning models in both predictive accuracy and resource utilization for tabular data tasks. Additionally, we discuss the practical considerations that influence method selection, including data dimensionality, feature engineering requirements, and model explainability. Our findings underscore the importance of not relying solely on deep learning solutions for tabular data problems and highlight the continued relevance of classic machine learning techniques. This work provides actionable insights for practitioners and researchers seeking effective approaches to tabular data modeling. 

Keywords: tabular data, deep learning, machine learning, gradient boosting, model interpretability, predictive analytics
