This paper presents a novel hybrid approach combining Gradient Boosting Trees (GBT) and Neural Networks (NN) for forecasting operating room (OR) data, aiming to improve accuracy and efficiency in healthcare resource management. The objective is to address the challenges of predicting OR utilization, patient flow, and scheduling, which are critical for optimizing hospital operations. The proposed method leverages the interpretability and robustness of GBT for feature selection and initial predictions, while NN enhances the model's ability to capture complex temporal patterns and nonlinear relationships in the data. The hybrid model is trained and validated on a real-world dataset of OR records, including patient demographics, surgical durations, and resource usage. Experimental results demonstrate that the hybrid approach outperforms standalone GBT and NN models, achieving higher prediction accuracy and lower error rates. Key findings highlight the model's ability to adapt to varying OR conditions and provide actionable insights for hospital administrators. The study contributes to the growing field of healthcare analytics by introducing a scalable and interpretable forecasting framework with potential applications in OR scheduling, resource allocation, and cost optimization. Keywords: Gradient Boosting Trees, Neural Networks, Operating Room Forecasting, Healthcare Analytics, Hybrid Models.
