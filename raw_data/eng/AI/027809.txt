Glioma classification using MRI data is a critical task in medical imaging, with significant implications for diagnosis and treatment planning. However, existing neural network models often lack interpretability, limiting their reliability in clinical settings. This study introduces an explanation-guided training approach to improve the performance and interpretability of neural networks for glioma classification. The proposed method integrates explainable AI techniques, such as gradient-based saliency maps, into the training process to guide the model toward learning more meaningful and clinically relevant features. We evaluate the approach on a publicly available MRI dataset, comparing it with traditional neural network training methods. Results demonstrate that the explanation-guided model achieves higher classification accuracy and provides more interpretable decision-making insights, aligning closely with expert annotations. The findings highlight the potential of incorporating explainability into model training to enhance both performance and trustworthiness in medical AI applications. This work contributes to advancing interpretable AI in healthcare, offering a novel framework for improving neural network models in glioma classification and beyond.  

**Keywords**: glioma classification, MRI, neural networks, explainable AI, saliency maps, medical imaging, interpretability.
