The paper addresses the critical task of improving object detection in autonomous driving systems, focusing on two main challenges: the optimization of anchor generation and the mitigation of class imbalance. These factors significantly impact the accuracy and efficiency of detecting a diverse range of objects in dynamic driving environments.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel approach that enhances anchor generation by incorporating a dynamic adjustment mechanism that adapts to varying object scales and aspect ratios. Additionally, we introduce a weighted loss function that effectively addresses class imbalance by assigning higher importance to underrepresented classes during the training phase. This dual-strategy is implemented within a state-of-the-art convolutional neural network framework to leverage deep learning techniques for improved object detection accuracy.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our optimized anchor generation method significantly improves the precision of object detection, particularly for small and distant objects. Our approach to class imbalance results in improved recall rates for rare and minor classes without compromising the detection performance of more frequent classes. Comparative analysis shows that our method outperforms existing benchmarks, achieving higher mean average precision (mAP) scores in challenging urban scenarios.

Conclusion/Implications: The research contributes to the field of autonomous driving by providing a more robust and adaptable object detection system. The enhanced accuracy and efficiency of our method can potentially lead to increased safety and reliability in autonomous vehicle navigation. The insights gained from optimizing anchor generation and addressing class imbalances can be applied to other domains where precise object detection is critical. Key Keywords: autonomous driving, object detection, anchor generation, class imbalance, convolutional neural network, machine learning.
