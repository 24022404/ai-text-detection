**Abstract**

Parameter inference is a fundamental challenge in modeling complex dynamical systems, particularly when systems exhibit bifurcation phenomena that can lead to abrupt qualitative changes in behavior. This paper proposes a novel methodology that leverages bifurcation diagrams as an informative tool for parameter estimation. Our approach integrates computational bifurcation analysis with advanced optimization algorithms to systematically infer model parameters that best capture observed qualitative system transitions. We implemented our method within a flexible computational framework, applying it to canonical nonlinear dynamical systems, including ecological and biochemical models, where bifurcation structures are prominent. Experimental results demonstrate improved accuracy in parameter inference compared to traditional time-series fitting methods, especially in the presence of limited or noisy data. Moreover, the approach provides enhanced insights into model behavior by explicitly linking parameter values to critical dynamical regimes. The findings highlight the value of bifurcation-based techniques for robust parameter inference in nonlinear systems, with potential applications spanning systems biology, engineering, and applied mathematics. Key innovations include the fusion of bifurcation diagram analysis with parameter optimization and the demonstrated resilience to sparse or imprecise observations. Overall, this work advances the state-of-the-art in parameter inference by harnessing the global dynamical information contained in bifurcation diagrams.  
**Keywords:** parameter inference, bifurcation diagrams, dynamical systems, nonlinear modeling, parameter estimation, optimization, system identification.
