This paper explores the development of Lifelong Reinforcement Learning (LRL) systems, which aim to continuously learn and adapt through diverse tasks over an extended period. We address the pivotal challenge of enhancing LRL systems in retaining valuable knowledge without succumbing to catastrophic forgetting.

Methods/Approach: Our approach integrates novel mechanisms for knowledge retention and transfer within an LRL framework. We introduce an adaptive memory architecture that selectively consolidates experiences while utilizing a task-aware transfer strategy to leverage past learning in novel environments. The framework employs deep reinforcement learning algorithms optimized for cross-task adaptation and incremental learning.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our LRL system achieves superior performance in retaining critical knowledge and transferring skills across multiple domains compared to traditional reinforcement learning models. The evaluation involves standard benchmarks and real-world scenarios, showcasing improved efficiency and adaptability. Moreover, the system exhibits resilience against task interference and forgetting, maintaining a high level of competence over time.

Conclusion/Implications: The research advances the field of lifelong learning in reinforcement learning by presenting a robust methodology for continuous adaptation and knowledge retention. The implications of this work suggest vast potential for applications requiring robust, adaptable AI systems in dynamic environments, such as robotics, autonomous decision-making, and personalized learning platforms. Our findings contribute to the foundational understanding of LRL systems and pave the way for future exploration into more sophisticated adaptive learning systems.

Keywords: Lifelong Reinforcement Learning, Catastrophic Forgetting, Knowledge Transfer, Adaptive Memory, Continuous Learning, AI Systems.
