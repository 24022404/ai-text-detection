This paper introduces R2-B2, a novel framework for Recursive Reasoning-Based Bayesian Optimization, designed to address the challenge of no-regret learning in competitive multi-agent environments. The objective of this work is to enhance decision-making strategies in games by integrating recursive reasoning principles with Bayesian optimization, enabling agents to adaptively learn and optimize their actions while minimizing regret. The proposed approach leverages a hierarchical reasoning model that captures the iterative decision-making processes of agents, combined with a Bayesian optimization framework to efficiently explore and exploit the action space. Experimental results demonstrate that R2-B2 outperforms existing no-regret learning algorithms in terms of convergence speed and cumulative regret reduction across various game-theoretic scenarios, including zero-sum games and cooperative settings. The framework’s ability to balance exploration and exploitation while accounting for opponents’ strategies highlights its potential for applications in autonomous systems, multi-agent reinforcement learning, and strategic planning. This research contributes a scalable and theoretically grounded method for no-regret learning, offering significant advancements in the design of adaptive and intelligent decision-making systems.  

**Keywords**: Bayesian optimization, recursive reasoning, no-regret learning, multi-agent systems, game theory, decision-making.
