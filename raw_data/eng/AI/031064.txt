This paper investigates the generalization error of generalized linear models (GLMs) in high-dimensional settings, where the number of features exceeds the sample size. Our objective is to understand how GLMs behave when the dimensionality of the data increases, and to develop a framework for predicting their performance. We employ a combination of theoretical analysis and numerical simulations to study the generalization error of GLMs, using tools from statistical learning theory and high-dimensional statistics. Our results show that the generalization error of GLMs can be bounded by a quantity that depends on the dimensionality of the data, the sample size, and the regularization parameter. We also demonstrate that our bounds are tight, in the sense that they cannot be improved upon by more than a constant factor. Our findings have important implications for the application of GLMs in high-dimensional settings, such as genomics, finance, and computer vision, where the number of features can be extremely large. Key contributions of this work include the development of a novel framework for analyzing the generalization error of GLMs, and the provision of new insights into the behavior of these models in high-dimensional settings. Relevant keywords: generalized linear models, high-dimensional statistics, generalization error, statistical learning theory, regularization techniques.
