Shot boundary detection (SBD) is a critical task in video analysis, enabling the segmentation of videos into meaningful scenes for applications such as video summarization, indexing, and retrieval. Despite significant advancements, existing methods often struggle with accuracy and robustness due to limited datasets and reliance on single-feature approaches. This paper introduces a novel SBD method leveraging a newly constructed extensive dataset and a mixed-feature approach to address these challenges. The proposed method combines spatial, temporal, and motion-based features to enhance detection accuracy across various types of shot transitions, including abrupt cuts and gradual transitions like fades and dissolves. A comprehensive evaluation is conducted using the newly curated dataset, which includes diverse video genres and transition types, ensuring broader generalizability. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art techniques in terms of precision, recall, and F1-score, particularly in handling complex transitions. The study highlights the importance of dataset diversity and feature integration in improving SBD performance. The findings have significant implications for video processing applications, offering a more reliable and scalable solution for real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Shot boundary detection, video analysis, mixed features, extensive dataset, transition detection, video segmentation.
