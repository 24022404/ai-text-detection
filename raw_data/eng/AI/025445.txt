This paper presents a novel approach to unsupervised visual representation learning by leveraging the temporal continuity of video data through the task of tracking patches across frames. The primary objective is to develop feature representations that capture underlying visual semantics without relying on manual annotation or labeled datasets, a key challenge in scalable computer vision. Our methodology utilizes a tracking framework that automatically discovers and follows distinct image patches within raw video sequences over time, forming positive pairs for training deep neural networks. By encouraging representations of patches that belong to the same physical object or region to remain consistent across frames, the proposed method exploits natural supervision from video dynamics. Experimental evaluations on standard benchmarks demonstrate that features learned via our patch tracking approach significantly outperform existing unsupervised methods on downstream tasks such as object detection and image classification. Furthermore, our method exhibits robust generalization across diverse visual domains. The results highlight the effectiveness of video-based patch tracking in capturing rich, spatial-temporal features and suggest promising directions for further self-supervised learning in computer vision. Keywords: unsupervised learning, visual representation, patch tracking, video analysis, self-supervised learning, deep learning.
