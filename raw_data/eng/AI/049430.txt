Gaze estimation is a critical component in human-computer interaction, with applications ranging from assistive technologies to augmented reality. However, existing methods often struggle with physically unconstrained environments, limiting their practical utility in real-world scenarios. This paper introduces *Gaze360*, a novel framework for robust gaze estimation in the wild. Unlike traditional approaches that rely on controlled settings or head-mounted devices, *Gaze360* leverages a 360-degree camera setup and advanced deep learning techniques to estimate gaze direction without physical constraints. The proposed method combines a multi-task neural network architecture with a self-supervised learning mechanism to handle diverse head poses, lighting conditions, and occlusions. Experimental results demonstrate that *Gaze360* achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets, outperforming existing methods by a significant margin in unconstrained environments. Furthermore, the framework is shown to generalize well across different user demographics and environmental conditions. The implications of this work extend to applications in human-robot interaction, driver monitoring systems, and immersive virtual environments, where accurate and unconstrained gaze estimation is essential. This research contributes a scalable and practical solution to a long-standing challenge in gaze estimation, paving the way for broader adoption in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Gaze estimation, 360-degree cameras, deep learning, human-computer interaction, unconstrained environments.
