This paper introduces LT-GAN, a novel self-supervised Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework designed to enhance image generation through latent transformation detection. The primary objective of LT-GAN is to address the challenge of learning meaningful latent representations in unsupervised settings, which is critical for generating high-quality and diverse images. Unlike traditional GANs, LT-GAN incorporates a self-supervised mechanism that detects and leverages latent transformations to improve the discriminator’s ability to distinguish between real and generated data. The proposed approach involves training the generator and discriminator to jointly learn latent space transformations, enabling the model to capture intricate data distributions more effectively.  

Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and CelebA, demonstrate that LT-GAN outperforms existing GAN variants in terms of image quality and diversity, as measured by metrics such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception Score (IS). Additionally, LT-GAN exhibits improved stability during training, reducing common issues like mode collapse. The results highlight the effectiveness of self-supervised latent transformation detection in advancing GAN-based image synthesis.  

The key contribution of this work lies in its innovative integration of self-supervised learning with GANs, offering a new direction for improving unsupervised generative models. LT-GAN has potential applications in areas such as image editing, data augmentation, and synthetic data generation for training machine learning models. This research underscores the importance of leveraging latent space dynamics to enhance generative modeling, paving the way for future advancements in the field.  

**Keywords**: GAN, self-supervised learning, latent transformation, image generation, unsupervised learning, generative models.
