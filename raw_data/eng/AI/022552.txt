Objective: Many real-world systems exhibit multiple, related network structures that govern dynamic processes such as diffusion or signal propagation. This paper addresses the problem of jointly inferring several graph topologies from observations generated by matrix‐polynomial models, where each graph’s adjacency matrix appears as a polynomial term of measured data.  

Methods: We propose a unified framework that models observed data as outputs of distinct polynomial filters applied to unknown adjacency matrices. By coupling the inference tasks, our method enforces shared structural patterns across graphs while allowing individual deviations. The joint estimation problem is cast as a convex optimization with sparsity and low‐rank regularizers to promote interpretable network structures. We develop an efficient alternating‐direction method of multipliers (ADMM) solver that scales to large networks.  

Results: On synthetic benchmarks, our joint inference approach recovers multiple graph structures with up to 30% higher F1‐score compared to independent estimation methods. We demonstrate robustness to noise and model mismatch, and show that exploiting inter‐graph similarity substantially improves recovery in low‐sample regimes. Application to real-world datasets—including functional brain connectivity under different cognitive tasks and gene regulatory networks under varying conditions—reveals meaningful common motifs and condition-specific links.  

Conclusion: This work introduces the first convex framework for joint inference of multiple graphs from matrix polynomials, providing enhanced accuracy and interpretability. Our approach is broadly applicable in network science, bioinformatics, and signal processing on graphs.  

Keywords: multiple graph inference, matrix polynomials, joint estimation, convex optimization, network structure.
