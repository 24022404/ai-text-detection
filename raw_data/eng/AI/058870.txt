This paper introduces the Canonical Interval Forest (CIF) classifier, a novel approach for time series classification. The objective is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of classification models in handling complex time series data. The CIF classifier combines the strengths of interval-based features and ensemble learning, utilizing a forest of decision trees to identify optimal interval representations of time series patterns. The method is evaluated on a range of benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to state-of-the-art classifiers. Key findings include improved accuracy, particularly on datasets with complex or non-uniform patterns, and reduced computational complexity. The CIF classifier contributes to the field by providing a robust and adaptable solution for time series classification, with potential applications in fields such as finance, healthcare, and IoT analytics. The novelty of the approach lies in its ability to automatically identify and leverage informative intervals in time series data, making it a valuable tool for researchers and practitioners working with temporal data. Relevant keywords: time series classification, ensemble learning, interval-based features, decision trees, CIF classifier.
