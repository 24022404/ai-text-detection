Zero-shot image recognition (ZSL) aims to classify images from categories unseen during training by leveraging semantic information such as class attributes or word embeddings. However, many existing methods treat visual and semantic modalities independently, limiting effective knowledge transfer to novel classes. In this work, we introduce a Graph-based Visual-Semantic Entanglement Network (GVEN) that dynamically fuses visual features and semantic relationships within a unified graph structure. Our approach constructs a class-level semantic graph encoding pairwise relations among both seen and unseen categories. Visual features extracted by a convolutional backbone are projected into this semantic graph via a cross-modal entanglement module, enabling bidirectional information flow. Subsequent graph convolution layers propagate enriched representations across nodes, reinforcing semantic consistency and visual alignment. We evaluate GVEN on three standard benchmarks—AWA2, CUB, and SUN—under both conventional and generalized zero-shot settings. Experimental results demonstrate that GVEN outperforms state-of-the-art methods by up to 6.4% in classification accuracy and exhibits superior robustness to domain shift. Ablation studies confirm the efficacy of the entanglement module and semantic graph construction. Our network offers an end-to-end framework for enhanced cross-modal fusion, with potential applications in image retrieval, fine-grained recognition, and open-vocabulary classification.

Keywords: zero-shot learning, graph convolutional network, visual-semantic embedding, cross-modal entanglement, image recognition.
