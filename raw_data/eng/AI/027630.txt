This paper presents a novel spectral method designed to enhance the learning and inference capabilities of latent variable models, a foundational tool in fields such as natural language processing, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics. The central objective is to address the challenges of scalability and identifiability commonly encountered in traditional latent variable frameworks. Our approach leverages advanced spectral decomposition techniques to efficiently uncover hidden structures in high-dimensional datasets without relying on iterative optimization or strong parametric assumptions. Comprehensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing algorithms in terms of accuracy, computational efficiency, and robustness to noise. Notably, the method achieves precise estimation of model parameters and improved latent variable recovery, even under limited supervision and noisy conditions. This work contributes significant advancements to unsupervised learning methodologies and provides practical implications for deploying latent variable models in large-scale, real-time applications. Keywords: spectral method, latent variable models, matrix decomposition, unsupervised learning, high-dimensional data, scalable algorithms.
