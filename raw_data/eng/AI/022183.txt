Graph representation learning has emerged as a critical area in machine learning, with applications ranging from social network analysis to bioinformatics. However, existing methods often struggle to capture both local and global structural information effectively. This paper introduces **Distance-wise Graph Contrastive Learning (DGCL)**, a novel framework designed to enhance graph representation by leveraging distance-aware contrastive learning. The proposed approach explicitly models node relationships at varying distances, enabling the learning of hierarchical structural patterns. DGCL employs a multi-scale contrastive loss function that optimizes representations by contrasting node pairs based on their topological proximity, ensuring both local neighborhood and global graph-level features are preserved. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that DGCL outperforms state-of-the-art methods in node classification, link prediction, and graph clustering tasks. The results highlight the framework's ability to capture nuanced structural dependencies, offering significant improvements in downstream performance. This research contributes to the advancement of graph-based machine learning by providing a scalable and effective solution for learning robust graph representations, with potential applications in recommendation systems, knowledge graphs, and molecular property prediction.  

**Keywords**: Graph representation learning, contrastive learning, hierarchical structures, node classification, link prediction.
