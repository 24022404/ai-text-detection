The objective of this research is to address the challenge of optimizing neural network architectures by proposing a novel curvature-guided pruning approach named RicciNets, which leverages Ricci Flow, a geometric concept, to enhance model efficiency without compromising performance. Traditional neural network pruning methods often rely on heuristic measures that can lead to suboptimal network configurations. Our approach uses Ricci Flow to compute a curvature measure for network components, identifying and pruning less significant parts of the network based on their geometric contribution to the overall architecture. Extensive experiments were conducted on various high-performance models, demonstrating that RicciNets achieves state-of-the-art results in reducing model size while maintaining, or even improving, baseline performance. Compared to conventional pruning techniques, RicciNets significantly reduces computational overhead and improves generalization through curvature-guided optimization. The implications of this study suggest that incorporating geometric properties such as curvature into neural network design and pruning could open new pathways for creating more efficient AI models. This contribution is pivotal for deploying computationally intensive applications on resource-constrained devices. Overall, the RicciNets framework presents a cutting-edge advancement in neural network pruning, combining principles from differential geometry and machine learning to achieve high-performance, compact neural architectures. Keywords: RicciNets, Neural Network Pruning, Ricci Flow, Geometric Optimization, Curvature, Model Efficiency.
