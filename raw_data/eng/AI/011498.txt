Dictionary learning (DL) aims to find an optimal representation of data as sparse linear combinations of atoms in a learned dictionary. This paper introduces a novel approach to sparse coding within DL, termed Equiprobable Matching Pursuit (EqMP), designed to enhance the uniformity of atom usage. Unlike traditional Matching Pursuit (MP) algorithms that often lead to uneven atom selection frequencies, EqMP promotes a more balanced usage across the dictionary. The method involves modifying the atom selection criterion in MP to favor atoms that have been used less frequently in previous iterations. We present a detailed algorithmic formulation of EqMP and analyze its convergence properties. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that DL with EqMP achieves comparable or superior reconstruction performance compared to standard MP, while significantly improving the equiprobability of atom usage. This balanced usage can lead to more robust and interpretable dictionaries, benefiting applications such as image denoising, feature extraction, and data compression. Keywords: Dictionary Learning, Sparse Coding, Matching Pursuit, Atom Usage, Equiprobability, Signal Processing.
