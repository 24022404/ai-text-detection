This paper introduces a novel approach to inverse rendering that leverages polarimetric and multi-view information to reconstruct 3D geometry and material properties of objects. Our method addresses the ill-posed nature of traditional inverse rendering by incorporating polarization cues, which provide additional constraints on surface normals and reflectance parameters. We present a new framework that integrates multi-view stereo reconstruction with polarimetric data to estimate surface geometry, albedo, and specular reflectance. Specifically, we propose a polarimetric multi-view photometric stereo algorithm that optimizes for surface normals and BRDF parameters jointly. Experimental results on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our method achieves more accurate and robust reconstructions compared to state-of-the-art methods that rely solely on photometric or geometric cues. The improved accuracy in material property estimation facilitates realistic rendering and relighting applications. Keywords: Inverse Rendering, Polarimetry, Multi-View Stereo, 3D Reconstruction, BRDF Estimation, Photometric Stereo.
