**Abstract:**

The increasing integration of wind and solar energy sources into modern power grids necessitates high-resolution spatiotemporal data for accurate forecasting, grid stability, and resource management. However, available renewable energy datasets are often limited in resolution, posing challenges to advanced analysis and modeling. This paper introduces WiSoSuper, a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate and compare super-resolution methods specifically on wind and solar energy data. The benchmark comprises multiple real-world and synthetic datasets, along with standardized evaluation protocols and metrics tailored for renewable energy applications. We assess a range of state-of-the-art super-resolution algorithms—including deep learning models and interpolation baselines—on their ability to enhance the resolution of wind speed and solar irradiance fields. Experimental results indicate that advanced neural network architectures, such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformers, outperform traditional interpolation techniques in preserving key meteorological and spatial features. WiSoSuper provides valuable insights into the strengths and limitations of existing super-resolution approaches in the context of renewable energy. The benchmark and findings aim to guide future research toward more accurate and robust spatiotemporal upscaling methods, with significant implications for energy system forecasting, planning, and policy-making. Key keywords: super-resolution, wind data, solar data, machine learning, benchmark, renewable energy, spatiotemporal forecasting.
