Visual odometry and depth estimation are critical tasks in computer vision, enabling applications such as autonomous navigation and 3D scene reconstruction. This paper presents a novel approach leveraging Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) for both supervised and unsupervised learning of monocular video-based visual odometry and depth estimation. The proposed method addresses the challenges of scale ambiguity and temporal consistency in monocular sequences by integrating temporal dependencies through RNN architectures. Our approach employs a unified framework that jointly optimizes pose estimation and depth prediction, enabling end-to-end training with minimal supervision. For unsupervised learning, we introduce a self-supervised loss function that leverages photometric consistency and geometric constraints across consecutive frames. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves competitive performance in both odometry and depth estimation tasks, outperforming existing unsupervised methods and closely matching supervised baselines. The key contributions of this work include a scalable RNN-based architecture, a robust self-supervised training strategy, and a comprehensive evaluation of its applicability in real-world scenarios. This research advances the state-of-the-art in monocular visual odometry and depth estimation, offering a promising direction for applications in robotics, augmented reality, and autonomous systems.  

**Keywords**: Recurrent Neural Networks, Visual Odometry, Depth Estimation, Monocular Video, Unsupervised Learning, Self-Supervised Learning.
