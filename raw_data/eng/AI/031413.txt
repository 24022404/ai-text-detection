This paper presents a novel approach to reinforcement learning by integrating cover tree data structures with Bayesian reinforcement learning frameworks. The primary objective is to address the challenges of scalability and efficient exploration in high-dimensional state spaces. Our method leverages the hierarchical organization and fast nearest-neighbor queries provided by cover trees to dynamically partition the state-action space, enabling more effective representation and generalization of the agent's experience. We incorporate Bayesian inference to model uncertainty and guide exploration, optimizing policy performance while managing computational complexity. Experimental evaluations on synthetic and benchmark reinforcement learning environments demonstrate that our Cover Tree Bayesian Reinforcement Learning (CT-BRL) algorithm achieves superior sample efficiency and faster convergence compared to conventional methods, particularly in large or continuous domains. The proposed approach offers new insights into scalable reinforcement learning and has potential applications in robotics, adaptive control, and autonomous systems. Key contributions include the development of a hybrid cover tree-based Bayesian model, a novel exploration strategy informed by posterior uncertainty, and empirical validation of improved learning dynamics. Keywords: reinforcement learning, cover tree, Bayesian inference, exploration, scalability, sample efficiency.
