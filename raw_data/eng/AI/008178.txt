The rapid advancement of deepfake technologies has raised significant concerns regarding their misuse, particularly in spreading misinformation and manipulating digital content. This paper addresses the critical challenge of attributing deepfakes to their source models or creators, a task essential for ensuring accountability and trust in digital media. We propose a novel attribution framework that leverages advanced deep learning techniques to identify unique artifacts and patterns left by different generative models during the creation of deepfakes. Our approach combines multi-scale feature extraction with a robust classifier trained on a diverse dataset of deepfakes generated by state-of-the-art models. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves high accuracy in attributing deepfakes to their source models, outperforming existing techniques in both precision and recall. Furthermore, we analyze the generalizability of our framework across various datasets and adversarial scenarios, highlighting its resilience to common evasion tactics. The findings of this research provide a significant step forward in the fight against malicious deepfake usage, offering a reliable tool for forensic analysis and digital content verification. This work contributes to the broader field of digital forensics by introducing a scalable and effective solution for deepfake attribution, with potential applications in cybersecurity, media integrity, and legal investigations.  

**Keywords**: deepfakes, attribution, digital forensics, generative models, deep learning, misinformation, cybersecurity.
