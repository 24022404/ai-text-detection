Electronic Health Records (EHRs) contain vast amounts of patient data, but their high dimensionality and heterogeneity pose significant challenges for analysis. This study explores the effectiveness of representation learning using autoencoders to extract meaningful and compact features from EHRs. We conduct a comparative analysis of various autoencoder architectures, including vanilla, denoising, and variational autoencoders, to evaluate their performance in capturing essential patterns and reducing data complexity. Our experiments utilize a large-scale EHR dataset, focusing on tasks such as patient clustering and predictive modeling. Results demonstrate that denoising autoencoders outperform other variants in preserving critical information while achieving robust dimensionality reduction. Furthermore, the learned representations significantly improve downstream task performance compared to traditional feature engineering methods. This research highlights the potential of autoencoders as a powerful tool for EHR analysis, offering insights into scalable and efficient data representation techniques for healthcare applications. Key contributions include a systematic evaluation framework and practical recommendations for selecting autoencoder architectures tailored to EHR characteristics.  

**Keywords**: Representation learning, autoencoders, electronic health records, dimensionality reduction, healthcare analytics.
