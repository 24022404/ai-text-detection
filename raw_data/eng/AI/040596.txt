Objective: Off-policy evaluation (OPE) in recommendation systems aims to estimate the performance of new policies using logged user interactions. However, imbalance in logged data and selection bias often lead to misleading estimates, undermining reliable policy comparison and deployment decisions.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel debiasing framework that combines refined propensity estimation with a doubly robust correction term. First, we model user–item exposure probabilities via a regularized logistic regression to capture systematic logging biases. Second, we integrate this propensity model into a doubly robust estimator that adjusts both importance-weighted rewards and model-based reward predictions. This hybrid approach mitigates variance from purely importance-weighted methods and bias from direct models.

Results/Findings: On two real-world recommendation datasets, our debiased OPE method reduces mean squared error of policy value estimates by 25–40% compared to standard inverse propensity scoring and by 15–30% against existing doubly robust baselines. Moreover, our approach maintains stable performance under varying degrees of logging bias and data sparsity. Ablation studies confirm the complementary roles of accurate propensity estimation and model-based correction.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed debiasing framework offers a more reliable and scalable solution for offline policy evaluation in recommendation systems. By correcting for logging biases and balancing bias–variance trade-offs, it facilitates safer policy optimization and accelerates iterative development of recommendation algorithms. Our method can be readily integrated into existing OPE pipelines to improve trustworthiness of offline experimentation.

Keywords: off-policy evaluation, recommendation systems, bias correction, propensity scoring, doubly robust estimator, counterfactual learning.
