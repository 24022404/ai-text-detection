This study explores the impact of agent behavior on contract negotiation through the application of reinforcement learning. The primary objective is to investigate how prosocial and selfish agents differ in their negotiation strategies and outcomes. Reinforcement learning is utilized to model the negotiation process, enabling agents to learn and adapt their behaviors to optimize contractual results. Two distinct agent behavior types—prosocial, which prioritizes collective benefits, and selfish, which focuses on individual gains—are analyzed. Experimental results demonstrate significant variations in negotiation outcomes based on agent behavior, with prosocial agents achieving more balanced agreements that enhance collective welfare, while selfish agents attain higher individual payoffs. This research highlights the potential of reinforcement learning in developing intelligent agents capable of sophisticated negotiation tactics. By understanding these behavioral mechanisms, the study contributes to the design of more effective negotiation systems in domains ranging from business contracts to automated trading. Key insights suggest applications in scenarios requiring adaptive and strategic negotiation frameworks, and the findings propose directions for future research in agent-based decision-making. Keywords: Reinforcement learning, contract negotiation, agent behavior, prosocial, selfish, adaptive negotiation.
