Objective: Efficiently retrieving images of complex scenes from large-scale databases remains a challenge due to the high variability of object arrangements and appearances. We introduce a novel framework, Searching Scenes by Abstracting Things, which represents scenes through concise, interpretable abstractions of their constituent objects (“things”) rather than relying on dense visual features.  
Methods: Our approach first generates object proposals using a lightweight detector, then encodes each proposal with five abstract properties—position, size, aspect ratio, dominant color, and semantic category. These properties form a fixed-length, order-invariant descriptor vector for each scene. To match a user query (e.g., a sketch, bounding-box layout, or verbal description), we abstract the query into the same property space and compute scene similarity via a learned metric that balances geometric and semantic alignment.  
Results: On two benchmark datasets (SUN397 and COCO-Scenes), our abstraction-based retrieval achieves a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 0.62, outperforming global CNN features (mAP 0.43) and region-based baselines (mAP 0.50). In zero-shot settings—queries involving unseen object categories—our method maintains 80% of its supervised performance, demonstrating strong generalization. User studies confirm that abstraction-driven results align better with human expectations for spatial layout and object relevance.  
Conclusions: By focusing on high-level object abstractions, our framework offers an interpretable, robust, and modality-agnostic solution for scene search. This work paves the way for lightweight, cross-modal image retrieval systems in applications such as interactive design, visual storytelling, and robotics.  
Keywords: scene retrieval, object abstraction, visual search, cross-modal matching, zero-shot generalization.
