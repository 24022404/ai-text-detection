Causal discovery is a fundamental challenge in understanding complex systems, particularly in domains such as healthcare, economics, and artificial intelligence. This paper addresses the problem of identifying causal relationships in linear acyclic models (LAMs) where the underlying data distributions are arbitrary and not constrained to specific forms, such as Gaussian. Traditional methods often rely on distributional assumptions, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios. We propose a novel approach that leverages advanced statistical techniques and graph-based algorithms to infer causal structures without imposing restrictive distributional constraints. Our method integrates conditional independence testing with a robust search strategy to accurately recover the causal graph. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and scalability, particularly in cases with non-Gaussian or heavy-tailed distributions. The findings highlight the potential of this method for applications in diverse fields where causal inference is critical. This work contributes to the broader understanding of causal discovery by providing a flexible and effective framework for analyzing linear acyclic models with arbitrary distributions.  

**Keywords**: Causal discovery, linear acyclic models, arbitrary distributions, conditional independence, graph-based algorithms, causal inference.
