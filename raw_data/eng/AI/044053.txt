This paper addresses the challenge of fine-grained classification of medical reports, a critical task in healthcare informatics, where accurate categorization of complex clinical data is essential for decision-making and patient care. Traditional supervised learning methods often require extensive labeled datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to obtain in the medical domain. To overcome this limitation, we propose a semi-supervised natural language processing (NLP) approach that leverages both labeled and unlabeled medical reports to improve classification performance. Our method integrates advanced transformer-based language models with a novel self-training mechanism, enabling the model to iteratively refine its predictions using high-confidence pseudo-labels generated from unlabeled data. We evaluate our approach on a publicly available dataset of medical reports, demonstrating significant improvements in classification accuracy compared to fully supervised baselines and other semi-supervised techniques. The results highlight the effectiveness of our method in reducing reliance on labeled data while maintaining high precision in fine-grained categorization. This research contributes to the growing field of medical NLP by providing a scalable and efficient solution for automated report classification, with potential applications in clinical decision support systems and healthcare analytics.  

**Keywords**: semi-supervised learning, natural language processing, medical report classification, transformer models, healthcare informatics.
