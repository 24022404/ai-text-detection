The objective of this research is to address the challenge of 3D object detection in autonomous systems by developing an innovative fusion method that integrates Camera and LiDAR data, known as CLOCs (Camera-LiDAR Object Candidates Fusion). Traditional 3D object detection approaches often rely on either purely monocular vision or LiDAR point cloud data, each having its limitations in terms of depth accuracy and object detection reliability. The CLOCs method leverages the complementary strengths of both sensing modalities by proposing a novel fusion framework that enhances detection accuracy and robustness. Our approach involves generating object candidates independently from camera images and LiDAR scans, followed by a strategic fusion stage that optimizes candidate selection and validation through a deep neural network model. The experimental results demonstrate that the CLOCs fusion algorithm significantly outperforms single modality methods, achieving notable improvements in detection accuracy and precision, particularly in complex environments and under various lighting conditions. This research contributes to the field by offering a scalable and efficient 3D object detection framework that can be readily integrated into existing autonomous systems, potentially enhancing their environmental perception and decision-making capabilities. Key keywords include Camera-LiDAR fusion, 3D object detection, sensor fusion, autonomous vehicles, and deep learning.
