This paper introduces CR-Fill, a novel generative image inpainting approach that leverages auxiliary contextual reconstruction to produce high-quality, semantically coherent results. The objective is to address the challenging problem of image inpainting, where large missing regions need to be filled in a visually plausible manner. Our approach employs a deep learning-based model that combines the strengths of generative adversarial networks (GANs) and contextual attention mechanisms. The CR-Fill method utilizes a two-stage process, first reconstructing the missing context and then filling in the missing regions using this reconstructed context. Experimental results demonstrate that CR-Fill outperforms state-of-the-art image inpainting methods, achieving significant improvements in terms of visual quality, consistency, and accuracy. The proposed approach has important implications for various applications, including image editing, restoration, and generation. Key contributions of this work include the introduction of auxiliary contextual reconstruction, which enhances the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies and semantic relationships, and the development of a novel loss function that balances reconstruction accuracy and visual realism. Overall, CR-Fill represents a significant advancement in generative image inpainting, with potential applications in computer vision, graphics, and artificial intelligence. Keywords: image inpainting, generative models, contextual reconstruction, GANs, deep learning.
