Graph embedding techniques have become essential for analyzing and processing complex relational data in various domains, such as social networks, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems. This paper introduces a novel approach for inductive graph embeddings that leverages locality encodings to capture structural and relational patterns within graphs. Unlike traditional methods that rely on transductive learning, our approach generalizes to unseen nodes and graphs, making it highly scalable and applicable to dynamic environments. We propose a framework that combines locality-sensitive hashing with graph neural networks to encode local neighborhood information efficiently. The model is trained to preserve both local and global graph properties, ensuring robust representations for downstream tasks such as node classification, link prediction, and graph clustering. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art approaches in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The proposed technique also exhibits strong generalization capabilities, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. This work contributes to advancing inductive graph representation learning by providing a scalable and effective solution for real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Graph embeddings, inductive learning, locality encodings, graph neural networks, node classification, link prediction.
