This research addresses the challenge of improving ensemble classifier performance through a novel geometric framework. The study seeks to enhance classification accuracy by effectively leveraging dataset-level insights, thereby overcoming limitations inherent in individual classifiers.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a dataset-level geometric framework that strategically integrates multiple classifiers to form an ensemble. This method focuses on the spatial distribution and relationships of data points across classifiers, utilizing geometric principles to optimize ensemble decision boundaries. The framework is designed to dynamically assess classifier complementarity and redundancy, adapting to the underlying data structure.

Results/Findings: Our experimental results demonstrate a significant improvement in classification accuracy across various benchmark datasets. Compared to traditional ensemble methods, such as bagging and boosting, our geometric framework consistently achieved higher predictive performance. The results also show reduced error rates and an enhanced ability to deal with imbalanced and complex data distributions.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a novel perspective on ensemble learning by incorporating geometric properties at the dataset level. Our findings suggest potential applications in numerous areas, including image recognition, fraud detection, and natural language processing. The introduced framework not only advances the understanding of ensemble classifiers but also offers a powerful tool for practitioners aiming to improve model robustness and accuracy.

Keywords: ensemble classifiers, geometric framework, dataset-level analysis, classification accuracy, data distribution, machine learning.
