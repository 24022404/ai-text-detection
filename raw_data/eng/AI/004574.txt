Video object segmentation (VOS) is a challenging task requiring precise pixel-level annotations for training. This paper introduces a novel one-shot weakly supervised VOS framework to alleviate this annotation burden. Our approach leverages a single frame with weak annotations, such as bounding boxes, to propagate segmentation masks across the entire video sequence. We employ a self-training paradigm using a teacher-student model. The teacher model, trained on the weakly annotated frame, generates pseudo-labels for subsequent frames. A student model is then trained on these pseudo-labels, iteratively improving segmentation accuracy. To address the noise inherent in pseudo-labels, we incorporate a confidence-aware loss function that emphasizes high-confidence regions during student model training. Experimental results on standard VOS datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive performance compared to existing weakly supervised methods and even approaches some fully supervised techniques, despite using only a single weakly annotated frame. This work offers a practical solution for VOS, significantly reducing annotation costs and enabling broader applications. Keywords: Video Object Segmentation, Weakly Supervised Learning, One-Shot Learning, Self-Training, Pseudo-Labeling.
