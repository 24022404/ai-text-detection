In the rapidly evolving field of artificial intelligence, continual learning has emerged as a crucial area of research, necessitating the development of systems capable of continuously adapting to new information without forgetting previous knowledge. This paper addresses the existing gap in effectively categorizing continual learning scenarios, which is critical for developing robust and generalizable models. We propose a comprehensive categorization framework that systematically organizes different continual learning scenarios based on their characteristics and complexity. In parallel, this study underscores the importance of strong baseline methodologies by evaluating a variety of commonly used approaches in these redefined scenarios. Our approach utilizes a combination of state-of-the-art neural network architectures and innovative adaptation techniques to systematically assess each scenario's effectiveness. The findings reveal significant discrepancies in performance across different continual learning approaches, emphasizing the need for strong benchmarks to facilitate advancements in this domain. The proposed categorizations highlight overlooked aspects of current methodologies, suggesting pathways for enhancing scalability and efficiency. Ultimately, the research establishes a structured approach to continual learning, advocating for the use of strong, reliable baselines that can guide future developments in creating more resilient and adaptive AI systems. Key implications include improved model evaluation procedures and the potential for real-world applications in dynamic environments such as robotics, personalized learning, and automated decision-making systems. Keywords: continual learning, AI, neural networks, adaptation techniques, scalable AI systems, baseline methodologies.
