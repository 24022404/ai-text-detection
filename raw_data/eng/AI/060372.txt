This paper presents a novel approach to arbitrary style transfer, leveraging a multi-adaptation network to effectively bridge the gap between content and style images. The objective is to enable flexible and high-quality style transfer without requiring paired training data or elaborate preprocessing. Our method employs a dual-branch architecture, combining a content encoder with a style adapter, to learn multi-level feature representations that capture both the structural and textual information of the input images. The multi-adaptation network is trained using a carefully designed loss function that balances content preservation and style transfer. Experimental results demonstrate the superiority of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance in terms of visual quality and diversity. The proposed method has significant implications for various applications, including image editing, art generation, and data augmentation. Key contributions include the introduction of a multi-adaptation mechanism, enabling more accurate and efficient style transfer, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in handling diverse styles and content images. Relevant keywords: arbitrary style transfer, multi-adaptation network, deep learning, image processing, computer vision.
