This paper introduces ResNeSt, a novel neural network architecture that enhances the performance of image recognition tasks by integrating split-attention mechanisms into residual networks. The primary objective of this work is to improve feature representation and model generalization without significantly increasing computational complexity. ResNeSt achieves this by dividing feature maps into multiple groups and applying channel-wise attention within each group, enabling the network to capture more diverse and discriminative features. The proposed architecture is evaluated on several benchmark datasets, including ImageNet, COCO, and ADE20K, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing models such as ResNet and EfficientNet. Experimental results show that ResNeSt achieves state-of-the-art accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency, making it suitable for both high-performance and resource-constrained environments. The findings highlight the effectiveness of split-attention mechanisms in improving model robustness and scalability. ResNeSt's contributions include a scalable and modular design that can be easily integrated into various computer vision tasks, such as object detection, segmentation, and classification. This research advances the field of deep learning by providing a powerful and efficient framework for visual recognition, with potential applications in autonomous systems, medical imaging, and multimedia analysis.  

**Keywords**: ResNeSt, split-attention, residual networks, image recognition, deep learning, computer vision.
