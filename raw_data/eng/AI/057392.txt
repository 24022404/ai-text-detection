This paper explores the concept of convergent learning, investigating whether different neural networks learn the same representations when trained on identical tasks and datasets. Our objective is to understand the extent to which various architectures converge on similar internal representations, despite differences in their design and initialization. We employ a range of neural network models, including convolutional and recurrent networks, and analyze their learned representations using techniques such as feature visualization and clustering. Our results show that, while different networks exhibit distinct patterns of representation, there are notable similarities and convergences across models, particularly in the later layers of the networks. These findings have significant implications for our understanding of neural network behavior and the development of more efficient and generalizable models. Our research contributes to the ongoing discussion on the nature of neural network representations and highlights the potential for convergent learning to inform the design of more robust and adaptable AI systems, with key applications in areas such as transfer learning, few-shot learning, and neural network interpretability.
