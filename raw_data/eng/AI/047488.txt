This paper introduces a novel approach to reinforcement learning, addressing the challenge of sparse reward environments through learned directed exploration strategies. The primary objective is to enable agents to effectively discover rewarding experiences in complex, unfamiliar environments where traditional exploration methods often fail. Our method employs a meta-learning framework to train an exploration policy that biases the agent's actions towards promising, yet unexplored, states. This learned strategy leverages intrinsic motivation signals, derived from prediction errors and state visitation counts, to guide exploration in a directed manner. We evaluate our approach across a suite of challenging benchmark environments characterized by sparse rewards and deceptive local optima. Experimental results demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing exploration techniques, including random exploration and curiosity-driven methods, in terms of both sample efficiency and asymptotic performance. The learned exploration strategies exhibit strong generalization capabilities, enabling faster adaptation to new and unseen environments. Key contributions include a novel meta-learning framework for directed exploration, an effective combination of intrinsic motivation signals, and empirical validation demonstrating state-of-the-art performance in sparse reward reinforcement learning. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Exploration, Meta-Learning, Intrinsic Motivation, Sparse Rewards, Directed Exploration.
