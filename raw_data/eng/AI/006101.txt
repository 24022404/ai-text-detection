Pedestrian trajectory prediction is a pivotal task in autonomous systems, smart surveillance, and urban planning, requiring accurate modeling of social interactions and spatio-temporal dynamics. This paper introduces Sparse Graph Convolution Network (SGCN), an innovative model designed to enhance pedestrian trajectory prediction by leveraging sparse graph convolutional operations. Unlike existing models that struggle with computational inefficiencies and dense graphs, SGCN effectively captures and processes social and spatial relationships with reduced complexity. By employing a sparse adjacency matrix and specialized convolutional techniques, SGCN efficiently models the interconnected dynamics between pedestrians while significantly lowering computational demands.

The core methodology involves constructing a dynamic graph where nodes represent pedestrians and edges symbolize interactions, dynamically adjusted based on proximity and social behavior. The sparse graph convolutional layers then operate on this graph to predict future trajectories more accurately. Experiments conducted on benchmark trajectory datasets demonstrate that SGCN outperforms state-of-the-art models in terms of prediction accuracy and processing speed, while also requiring less computational resources.

Our findings suggest that incorporating sparsity into graph convolution networks substantially improves the scalability and accuracy of trajectory predictions. The SGCN's ability to seamlessly integrate spatial-temporal features provides robust performance across diverse scenarios. This research contributes a novel approach to trajectory prediction tasks, with potential applications in autonomous navigation systems, pedestrian flow analysis, and crowd management technologies. Keywords: SGCN, pedestrian trajectory prediction, sparse graph convolution, spatio-temporal dynamics, autonomous systems.
