This paper introduces SSD-GAN, a novel Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework designed to measure and enhance the realness of generated images by evaluating both spatial and spectral domains. Traditional GANs primarily focus on spatial features, often overlooking the importance of spectral characteristics, which can lead to artifacts and inconsistencies in generated outputs. SSD-GAN addresses this limitation by integrating a dual-domain evaluation mechanism that simultaneously optimizes spatial fidelity and spectral coherence. The proposed framework employs a multi-branch discriminator architecture, where one branch assesses spatial details such as texture and structure, while the other analyzes spectral properties like frequency distribution and phase alignment. Experimental results demonstrate that SSD-GAN significantly outperforms existing GAN models in terms of image quality, achieving higher realism scores across multiple benchmark datasets. Additionally, the framework exhibits improved stability during training, reducing mode collapse and gradient issues commonly observed in GANs. The findings highlight the importance of incorporating spectral domain analysis in generative models and pave the way for more robust and realistic image synthesis. SSD-GAN has potential applications in areas such as medical imaging, computer vision, and content creation, where high-fidelity image generation is critical.  

**Keywords**: GAN, spatial domain, spectral domain, image synthesis, realism, multi-branch discriminator.
