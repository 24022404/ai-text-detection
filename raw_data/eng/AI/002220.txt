This paper introduces *DAGMapper*, a novel approach for learning to map road networks by discovering lane topology. The objective of this research is to address the challenge of accurately representing complex road structures, such as intersections and multi-lane roads, in a scalable and automated manner. Traditional mapping methods often rely on manual annotations or predefined rules, which are time-consuming and lack adaptability to diverse road environments. To overcome these limitations, *DAGMapper* leverages a graph-based representation, modeling lane connectivity as a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG). The proposed method employs a combination of deep learning and graph neural networks to infer lane topology directly from raw sensor data, such as LiDAR or camera inputs. Experimental results demonstrate that *DAGMapper* achieves state-of-the-art performance in lane topology reconstruction, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and robustness across diverse urban and highway scenarios. The system’s ability to generalize to unseen environments highlights its potential for real-world applications, including autonomous driving and high-definition map generation. This work contributes a scalable and data-driven solution to the mapping problem, paving the way for more efficient and adaptive mapping systems in the future.  

**Keywords**: Lane topology, Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG), graph neural networks, autonomous driving, mapping, deep learning.
