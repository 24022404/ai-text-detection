In the evolving landscape of distributed learning systems, ensuring the robustness and security of learning processes against malicious participants and data anomalies is paramount. This paper introduces Aspis, a novel detection system designed to enhance the integrity and reliability of distributed learning environments.

Methods/Approach: Aspis leverages a combination of anomaly detection algorithms and integrity verification mechanisms to identify and mitigate potential threats within distributed learning networks. The system employs advanced statistical techniques and machine learning models to monitor, assess, and address inconsistencies in both data inputs and model updates. The architecture is designed to seamlessly integrate with existing distributed learning frameworks, providing scalable and effective threat detection without significant overhead.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations of Aspis demonstrate its effectiveness in identifying various types of data poisoning attacks and parameter manipulations, outperforming existing detection methods in both accuracy and efficiency. The system is tested across multiple distributed learning scenarios, showcasing its capability to maintain model performance and convergence rates in the presence of adversarial behavior.

Conclusion/Implications: Aspis represents a significant advancement in the field of distributed learning, offering a robust solution to the challenges of maintaining data integrity and model accuracy. This research presents an important step towards more secure and resilient distributed learning systems, with potential applications in federated learning, collaborative filtering, and large-scale machine learning deployments. By providing a trustworthy detection mechanism, Aspis enhances the reliability and adoption of distributed learning technologies in various sectors.

Keywords: Aspis, distributed learning, anomaly detection, data integrity, federated learning, machine learning security, data poisoning, threat mitigation.
