Person re-identification (re-ID) is a critical task in video surveillance and intelligent security systems, requiring robust matching of individuals across disjoint camera views. Traditional loss functions, such as softmax and triplet loss, often struggle to effectively capture complex intra-class variance and inter-class similarity present in real-world datasets. This paper introduces a novel Support Neighbor Loss (SNL) function tailored for person re-identification, which enhances feature discrimination by simultaneously maximizing positive sample cohesion and enlarging the separation from hard negative samples in the feature space. Our approach constructs a dynamic support set of neighbor samples during training, leveraging the contextual relationships among both positive and negative instances to refine the learned embeddings. Extensive experiments on widely-used person re-ID benchmarks demonstrate that models trained with our SNL outperform state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and robustness. Furthermore, ablation studies show the effectiveness of SNL in improving convergence speed and mitigating the impact of noisy labels. The Support Neighbor Loss provides a simple yet powerful alternative to conventional loss designs, advancing the performance and reliability of deep learning-based person re-identification systems. Keywords: person re-identification, deep learning, loss function, support neighbor loss, feature embedding, surveillance, computer vision.
