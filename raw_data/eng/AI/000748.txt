This paper presents a novel approach for Optical Braille Recognition (OBR) leveraging the Circular Hough Transform (CHT) to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of Braille character detection in scanned documents. The primary objective is to address the challenges of accurately identifying Braille dots, which are often affected by noise, uneven lighting, and document degradation. The proposed method utilizes the CHT to detect circular patterns corresponding to Braille dots, followed by a robust post-processing step to map detected dots into meaningful Braille characters. The approach is evaluated on a diverse dataset of Braille documents, including both printed and embossed samples, under varying conditions. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in recognition accuracy compared to traditional image processing techniques, achieving an average precision of 95.3% and recall of 93.7%. The system’s computational efficiency also makes it suitable for real-time applications. This research contributes to the development of accessible technologies by providing a reliable and scalable solution for Braille-to-text conversion, with potential applications in assistive devices, document digitization, and educational tools.  

**Keywords**: Optical Braille Recognition, Circular Hough Transform, image processing, assistive technology, Braille-to-text conversion.
