Model compression techniques aim to reduce the computational cost and memory footprint of large neural networks. This paper introduces LIT, a novel block-wise intermediate representation training method designed to improve the effectiveness of knowledge distillation for model compression. Unlike existing methods that focus solely on matching the final output or layer-wise features, LIT selectively transfers knowledge from intermediate representations within individual blocks of the teacher network. This approach allows the student network to learn more nuanced and relevant information, leading to improved performance. We evaluate LIT on various image classification tasks using CNN and Transformer-based architectures. Experimental results demonstrate that LIT consistently outperforms existing knowledge distillation techniques, achieving significant improvements in accuracy for compressed models. Furthermore, LIT exhibits robustness across different compression ratios and network architectures. This research offers a new direction for knowledge distillation, emphasizing the importance of block-wise intermediate representation transfer for efficient and effective model compression.
Keywords: Model Compression, Knowledge Distillation, Intermediate Representation, Neural Networks, Deep Learning.
