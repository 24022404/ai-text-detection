This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the robustness and performance of deep learning models by introducing a novel data augmentation technique called GridMask. The method aims to overcome the limitations of existing data augmentation strategies in terms of preserving salient features while contributing to model generalization.

Methods/Approach: GridMask involves the strategic masking of input data using a grid pattern, which dynamically adjusts the size and position of the masked regions. This approach is implemented to obscure parts of the image or data samples, forcing the model to learn discriminative features from incomplete information. Unlike conventional techniques, GridMask provides a more structured and controllable way of data modification, ensuring that critical parts of the input are selectively obscured in a repeatable manner.

Results/Findings: Experiments conducted on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate that GridMask significantly improves the performance of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformer-based architectures. The augmentation technique showed notable enhancements in classification accuracy and robustness against adversarial attacks compared to traditional methods like Cutout and DropBlock. Additionally, GridMask was effective in preserving essential data patterns while diversifying the dataset, which led to improved generalization across unseen data.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of GridMask represents a substantial contribution to the field of data augmentation, providing a versatile tool for researchers and practitioners in machine learning and computer vision. Its ability to enhance model performance with minimal computational overhead highlights its potential applications in various domains requiring high-performing and resilient AI models. Keywords: GridMask, data augmentation, deep learning, model generalization, CNNs, adversarial robustness.
