This paper provides a comprehensive analysis of autoencoders as a powerful tool for representation learning, focusing on their fundamentals, practical applications, and inherent challenges. The objective is to explore how autoencoders can effectively learn meaningful representations of data, particularly in unsupervised and semi-supervised learning tasks. We examine the core architecture of autoencoders, including their encoder-decoder framework, and discuss variations such as denoising, sparse, and variational autoencoders. Through case studies, we demonstrate their application in diverse domains, including image reconstruction, anomaly detection, and dimensionality reduction. Additionally, we address the explainability of autoencoder-generated representations, highlighting methods to interpret their latent spaces and decision-making processes. The study also identifies key challenges, such as scalability, overfitting, and the trade-off between reconstruction accuracy and representation quality. Our findings underscore the versatility of autoencoders in learning robust data representations while emphasizing the need for further research to overcome existing limitations. This work contributes to the growing body of knowledge on representation learning and offers insights into the practical deployment of autoencoders in real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Autoencoders, representation learning, unsupervised learning, explainability, dimensionality reduction, anomaly detection.
