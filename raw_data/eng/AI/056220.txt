Video frame synthesis aims to generate intermediate frames between existing ones, enhancing frame rates and enabling slow-motion effects. This paper introduces a novel Multi-Scale Video Frame-Synthesis Network (MSVFS-Net) designed to improve the quality and consistency of synthesized frames. Our approach utilizes a multi-scale architecture to capture both fine-grained details and global motion patterns within the video sequence. Specifically, we employ a series of convolutional and deconvolutional layers at multiple scales, allowing the network to effectively learn temporal dependencies and spatial relationships. Furthermore, we propose a Transitive Consistency Loss (TCL) that enforces consistency between synthesized frames and their neighboring real frames. TCL ensures that the transformation between two real frames is consistent with the composition of transformations through the synthesized intermediate frame. We evaluate MSVFS-Net on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in PSNR, SSIM, and perceptual quality compared to state-of-the-art methods. Ablation studies validate the effectiveness of the multi-scale architecture and the TCL. Our approach contributes a robust and accurate video frame synthesis method suitable for a wide range of applications, including video editing and high-frame-rate video generation.

Keywords: Video Frame Synthesis, Frame Interpolation, Multi-Scale Network, Transitive Consistency Loss, Deep Learning.
