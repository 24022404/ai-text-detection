This paper introduces ALBA, a novel reinforcement learning (RL) approach for video object segmentation. The objective is to address the limitations of existing supervised methods that require extensive manual annotation. ALBA employs an agent trained via RL to iteratively refine object segmentation masks across video frames. The method utilizes a deep neural network architecture to process visual features and predict actions that improve segmentation accuracy. The reward function is carefully designed to encourage accurate and consistent segmentation while penalizing errors. Experimental results on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate that ALBA achieves state-of-the-art performance compared to existing unsupervised and weakly supervised methods. Specifically, ALBA exhibits improved temporal consistency and robustness to occlusions and motion blur. Furthermore, ALBA demonstrates promising generalization capabilities to unseen video sequences. These findings highlight the potential of RL for automating and improving video object segmentation, reducing reliance on manual labeling efforts. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Video Object Segmentation, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, Unsupervised Learning.
