Graph coarsening, a technique widely used in scientific computing, has gained significant attention in machine learning due to its ability to simplify complex graph structures while preserving essential properties. This paper explores the adaptation of graph coarsening methods from traditional scientific computing applications to modern machine learning tasks, such as graph neural networks (GNNs) and large-scale graph analysis. We propose a novel framework that leverages hierarchical coarsening strategies to reduce computational complexity and improve scalability without compromising accuracy. Our approach integrates multi-level graph representations, enabling efficient feature aggregation and propagation in GNNs. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves competitive performance on benchmark datasets, outperforming existing techniques in terms of both computational efficiency and predictive accuracy. Furthermore, we highlight the versatility of graph coarsening in diverse applications, including social network analysis, recommendation systems, and molecular property prediction. This work bridges the gap between scientific computing and machine learning, offering a scalable and effective solution for handling large-scale graph data. The findings underscore the potential of graph coarsening as a foundational tool for advancing graph-based machine learning models.  

**Keywords**: Graph coarsening, graph neural networks, scalability, hierarchical representation, machine learning, scientific computing.
