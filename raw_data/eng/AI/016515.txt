Semantic image segmentation is a critical task in computer vision, with applications ranging from autonomous driving to medical imaging. However, the increasing reliance on deep learning models for segmentation tasks raises concerns about privacy and security, particularly regarding membership inference attacks (MIAs). This paper investigates the vulnerability of semantic segmentation models to MIAs, where adversaries aim to determine whether a specific image was part of the model's training dataset. We propose *Segmentations-Leak*, a novel framework that systematically evaluates the susceptibility of segmentation models to such attacks. Our approach leverages the unique characteristics of segmentation outputs, such as pixel-wise predictions and feature maps, to infer membership. We also introduce a defense mechanism that incorporates noise injection and differential privacy techniques to mitigate the risk of MIAs without significantly compromising segmentation accuracy. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that state-of-the-art segmentation models are highly vulnerable to MIAs, with our proposed defense achieving a 40% reduction in attack success rates while maintaining competitive segmentation performance. This work highlights the importance of addressing privacy concerns in segmentation tasks and provides actionable insights for developing more secure and privacy-preserving segmentation models.  

**Keywords**: semantic segmentation, membership inference attacks, privacy-preserving machine learning, differential privacy, deep learning, computer vision.
