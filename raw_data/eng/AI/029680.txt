This paper investigates the optimization landscapes encountered in overcomplete representation learning, a foundational approach in information processing, signal reconstruction, and machine learning. Overcomplete representations, where the number of basis elements exceeds the dimensionality of the input data, are widely used to enhance expressiveness and robustness in feature extraction. However, their optimization remains challenging due to the increased complexity and non-convexity of the solution space. In this study, we systematically analyze the geometric and topological properties of optimization landscapes arising from overcomplete models, including dictionary learning and sparse coding frameworks. Utilizing a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical evaluation, we probe the existence and structure of local minima, saddle points, and plateaus, and we assess their implications for convergence behavior and learning efficiency. Our findings reveal that specific initialization strategies and regularization techniques can significantly smooth the landscape, thereby improving convergence rates and solution quality. We further demonstrate, through benchmarking on synthetic and real datasets, that tailored optimization algorithms exploiting the landscape structure consistently outperform generic solvers in both speed and accuracy. This research provides novel insights into the interplay between model redundancy and optimization tractability, offering guidelines for designing more effective overcomplete learning systems. Keywords: overcomplete representation, optimization landscape, sparse coding, dictionary learning, non-convex optimization, representation learning.
