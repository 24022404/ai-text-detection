Image segmentation is a critical task in computer vision with applications in medical imaging, autonomous driving, and object recognition. Watershed algorithms are widely used for image segmentation, but often suffer from over-segmentation. This paper introduces a novel approach to image segmentation that addresses the over-segmentation issue by employing a size-dependent single linkage clustering method on a watershed basin graph. The objective is to merge oversegmented regions based on their size and adjacency relationships. Specifically, watershed transformation is first applied to generate an initial segmentation. A graph is then constructed where nodes represent watershed basins and edges represent adjacency. A size-dependent single linkage clustering algorithm is applied to merge adjacent basins, prioritizing smaller regions for merging to reduce over-segmentation. The performance of the proposed method is evaluated on standard benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in segmentation accuracy compared to traditional watershed and graph-based segmentation methods. The results show the algorithm effectively reduces over-segmentation while preserving meaningful boundaries, leading to more accurate and visually appealing image segmentations. This approach offers a robust and efficient solution for image segmentation with potential applications across various domains.

Keywords: Image Segmentation, Watershed Algorithm, Graph-Based Segmentation, Single Linkage Clustering, Over-segmentation, Computer Vision.
