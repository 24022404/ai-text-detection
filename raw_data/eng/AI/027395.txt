Human pose estimation is a critical task in computer vision with applications in human-computer interaction, augmented reality, and surveillance. This paper presents a novel approach termed Knowledge-Guided Deep Fractal Neural Networks (KG-DFNN) for accurate human pose estimation. The proposed method integrates domain-specific knowledge into a hierarchical fractal network architecture, enabling more effective extraction and representation of multi-scale spatial dependencies inherent in human body structures. The knowledge-guided component incorporates anatomical constraints and kinematic relationships during both training and inference, guiding the network toward physiologically plausible predictions and reducing ambiguities in challenging scenarios such as occlusions and complex articulations.

Extensive experiments were conducted on standard benchmark datasets, demonstrating that KG-DFNN achieves state-of-the-art accuracy, outperforming existing convolutional and transformer-based models in keypoint localization metrics. Ablation studies further reveal that embedding prior knowledge significantly enhances robustness and generalization capability, particularly in diverse environmental conditions and pose variations.

The results highlight the advantages of combining deep learning architectures with structured knowledge to advance pose estimation performance. This work contributes a flexible and scalable framework suitable for real-time human pose analysis in various applications. Key innovations include the integration of knowledge-guided modeling, fractal neural network design, and an effective multi-scale feature fusion strategy. Future research directions include extending the approach to 3D pose estimation and real-world deployment. 

Keywords: human pose estimation, deep learning, fractal neural networks, prior knowledge, multi-scale representation, computer vision.
