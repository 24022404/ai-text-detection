Wide neural networks have gained significant attention due to their ability to achieve high performance in various machine learning tasks. However, understanding the internal mechanisms that drive their success remains a challenge. This paper investigates the phenomenon of *representation mitosis* in wide neural networks, where learned features undergo a splitting process during training, leading to the emergence of specialized sub-features. We propose a novel theoretical framework to model this behavior, leveraging tools from dynamical systems and information theory. Our approach provides a detailed analysis of how representation mitosis contributes to the network's ability to generalize and adapt to complex data distributions. Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, we demonstrate that wide networks exhibiting representation mitosis achieve superior performance compared to their narrower counterparts, particularly in tasks requiring fine-grained feature discrimination. Furthermore, we identify key conditions under which mitosis occurs, offering insights into the design of more efficient and interpretable neural architectures. This work advances the understanding of feature learning in wide networks and highlights the potential for leveraging representation mitosis to improve model scalability and robustness.  

**Keywords**: wide neural networks, representation mitosis, feature learning, generalization, dynamical systems, interpretability.
