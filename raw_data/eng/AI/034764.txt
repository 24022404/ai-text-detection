This paper introduces R2-B2, a novel approach to no-regret learning in games by leveraging recursive reasoning and Bayesian optimization. The objective is to develop an algorithm that can efficiently learn optimal strategies in complex, dynamic environments. R2-B2 achieves this by employing a recursive reasoning framework to model opponents' behaviors and a Bayesian optimization method to adaptively explore the strategy space. Experimental results demonstrate that R2-B2 outperforms state-of-the-art methods in various game settings, exhibiting superior convergence rates and improved regret bounds. The key findings highlight the importance of integrating recursive reasoning and Bayesian optimization for effective no-regret learning. This research contributes to the advancement of artificial intelligence and game theory, with potential applications in autonomous decision-making, multi-agent systems, and human-computer interaction. The proposed approach combines concepts from machine learning, optimization, and game theory, making it a significant step towards developing more sophisticated and adaptive learning algorithms. Key keywords: Bayesian optimization, recursive reasoning, no-regret learning, game theory, artificial intelligence, multi-agent systems.
