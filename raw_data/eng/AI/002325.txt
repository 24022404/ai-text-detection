This paper explores the application of potential-based reward shaping (PBRS) in the context of Sokoban, a classic puzzle game known for its complexity and challenging decision-making requirements. The objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency of reinforcement learning (RL) agents in solving Sokoban levels by leveraging PBRS to guide exploration and improve learning outcomes. We propose a novel reward-shaping framework that incorporates domain-specific heuristics to define potential functions, enabling the agent to prioritize meaningful actions and avoid suboptimal behaviors. Our approach is evaluated using a deep Q-network (DQN) architecture, and the results demonstrate significant improvements in both learning speed and solution quality compared to baseline RL methods without reward shaping. The findings highlight the effectiveness of PBRS in reducing the exploration space and accelerating convergence in complex environments. This work contributes to the broader field of RL by showcasing the practical benefits of reward shaping in puzzle-solving tasks and providing insights into the design of potential functions for similar domains. Key implications include potential applications in automated planning, game AI, and other sequential decision-making problems. Keywords: reinforcement learning, reward shaping, Sokoban, potential-based methods, deep Q-network, puzzle-solving.
