This paper introduces Brainstorming Generative Adversarial Networks (BGANs), a novel multi-agent architecture designed to enable collaborative generative modeling across distributed environments, where agents each possess private, non-shared datasets. The primary objective is to address privacy and data accessibility challenges that impede centralized data collection for training powerful generative models. Our approach leverages a network of generative adversarial networks (GANs), where each agent independently trains on its own data while securely exchanging abstract model insights rather than raw data. Through iterative exchange and consensus mechanisms, BGANs encourage “brainstorming” among agents to enhance collective learning and model performance. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that BGANs significantly improve sample diversity and generative quality compared to isolated GANs and outperform standard federated learning GAN approaches in heterogeneous data scenarios. The results also illustrate the resilience of BGANs to dataset imbalance and the maintenance of data privacy. This framework offers a scalable, privacy-preserving strategy for generating high-quality synthetic data across sectors such as healthcare, finance, and IoT, where data sharing is often restricted. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, Multi-Agent Systems, Distributed Learning, Privacy Preservation, Federated Learning, Synthetic Data Generation.
