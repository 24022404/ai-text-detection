Graph classification is a fundamental task with applications in diverse fields, from drug discovery to social network analysis. This paper introduces a simple yet surprisingly effective baseline algorithm for graph classification, offering a computationally efficient alternative to complex graph neural networks. Our approach, termed "Graph Feature Aggregation Network" (GFAN), aggregates node features using basic statistical measures (mean, variance, max) across the entire graph, feeding the resulting vector into a standard multi-layer perceptron for classification. We evaluate GFAN on several benchmark graph classification datasets, including MUTAG, PTC-MR, and IMDB-BINARY. Results demonstrate that GFAN achieves competitive performance compared to more sophisticated graph neural networks, while requiring significantly less computational resources and training time. Specifically, GFAN achieves comparable or superior accuracy on small to medium-sized datasets and provides a strong baseline for future research. This work highlights the potential of simple feature aggregation techniques for graph classification and offers a practical, efficient solution for real-world applications. Keywords: Graph classification, baseline algorithm, graph neural networks, feature aggregation, machine learning.
