Clustering is a fundamental task in data analysis, with k-means being one of the most widely used algorithms due to its simplicity and efficiency. However, its performance is often limited by sensitivity to initialization and the quality of feature representations. This paper proposes a novel approach to improve k-means clustering by leveraging disentangled internal representations. The proposed method disentangles latent features into independent factors, enabling the algorithm to capture more meaningful and interpretable patterns in the data. We introduce a disentanglement framework that integrates with k-means, optimizing feature representations to enhance clustering accuracy and robustness. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in clustering performance, with our method outperforming traditional k-means and other state-of-the-art clustering techniques. The results highlight the importance of disentangled representations in reducing initialization dependency and improving cluster quality. This work contributes to the field of unsupervised learning by providing a scalable and effective solution for enhancing k-means clustering, with potential applications in data mining, image segmentation, and anomaly detection.  

**Keywords**: k-means clustering, disentangled representations, unsupervised learning, feature optimization, data mining.
