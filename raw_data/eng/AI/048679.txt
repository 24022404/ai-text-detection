This paper addresses the challenge of accurately segmenting clouds in images with varying illumination conditions by introducing a novel approach leveraging High-Dynamic-Range (HDR) imaging technology. The purpose of this research is to enhance cloud detection capabilities by overcoming limitations encountered in standard imaging methods that struggle with extreme contrast scenarios.

Methods/Approach: The proposed method utilizes HDR imaging to capture a wider range of luminance levels, enabling more detailed capture of cloud features. We developed an advanced segmentation algorithm specifically designed to process HDR data, tailored to identify subtle distinctions between clouds and the sky under diverse lighting conditions. The approach incorporates dynamic thresholding and adaptive filtering to optimize segmentation accuracy.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our HDR-based cloud segmentation algorithm significantly outperforms traditional low-dynamic-range imaging techniques, particularly in scenarios with complex lighting variations. The system was tested on multiple datasets containing diverse atmospheric conditions, resulting in improved accuracy and robustness, with performance gains quantified through metrics such as precision, recall, and F1-score.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of HDR imaging for cloud segmentation represents an innovative advancement in environmental monitoring applications. This method provides more reliable data for weather prediction, climate analysis, and remote sensing missions. Our findings suggest that integrating HDR technology into cloud observation systems can greatly enhance their efficacy and accuracy, offering new insights into atmospheric science. This research lays the groundwork for future exploration into HDR applications in various terrestrial and atmospheric imaging tasks.

Keywords: High-Dynamic-Range Imaging, Cloud Segmentation, Adaptive Filtering, Environmental Monitoring, Image Processing, Remote Sensing, Atmospheric Science.
