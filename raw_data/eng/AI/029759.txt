Self-supervised learning (SSL) has demonstrated remarkable success in learning representations from unlabeled data. However, the design of effective data augmentation policies for SSL remains a challenging and computationally expensive task, often relying on manual tuning or complex search algorithms. This paper introduces SelfAugment, a novel framework for automatically discovering augmentation policies tailored to SSL objectives. SelfAugment leverages a differentiable search space of augmentations, guided by a proxy SSL task, to efficiently identify optimal policies. These policies are then applied to train the main SSL model. We demonstrate the effectiveness of SelfAugment on image classification benchmarks, including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet. Our results show that SelfAugment discovers augmentation policies that significantly improve the performance of various SSL algorithms, such as SimCLR and BYOL, achieving state-of-the-art results while reducing the need for manual policy engineering. The discovered policies exhibit interesting properties, adapting to the specific characteristics of the dataset and SSL objective. SelfAugment offers a practical and efficient approach to automate the design of data augmentation strategies for self-supervised learning, paving the way for more robust and generalizable representations. Keywords: Self-Supervised Learning, Data Augmentation, Automatic Policy Search, Representation Learning, Computer Vision.
