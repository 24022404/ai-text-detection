This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing deep neural networks for embedded automation systems, where computational resources are limited. The objective is to improve the efficiency and performance of these networks without sacrificing accuracy. To achieve this, we propose a novel approach called Mean Spectral Normalization, which normalizes the spectral properties of the network's weights to reduce overfitting and improve generalization. Our method is based on a simple yet effective technique that adjusts the weight matrix to have a target spectral distribution, resulting in more stable and efficient training. Experimental results demonstrate that Mean Spectral Normalization outperforms existing normalization techniques, achieving significant reductions in computational cost and memory usage while maintaining state-of-the-art accuracy. The implications of this research are substantial, enabling the deployment of deep neural networks in resource-constrained embedded automation systems, such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and industrial control systems. Key contributions include improved network efficiency, reduced overfitting, and enhanced performance in low-resource environments, making this technique an attractive solution for a wide range of applications in embedded automation and beyond.
