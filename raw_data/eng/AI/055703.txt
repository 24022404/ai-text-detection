This paper introduces Ego-CNN, a novel framework for the distributed and egocentric representation of graphs tailored for the detection of critical structures within complex networks. Traditional graph analysis methods often rely on global information or central processing, which can be inefficient and overlook localized patterns essential for tasks such as vulnerability analysis, anomaly detection, and network optimization. Ego-CNN addresses these limitations by leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in a decentralized, node-centric manner, extracting rich, localized features from ego-networks—subgraphs centered around each node. The proposed approach encodes both structural and attribute-based information, enabling scalable parallel processing across large graphs. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including social, communication, and biological networks, demonstrate that Ego-CNN achieves superior accuracy and efficiency in identifying critical nodes and substructures when compared to state-of-the-art graph neural networks and classic centrality-based algorithms. Our results highlight significant improvements in detection precision, computational scalability, and adaptability to heterogeneous graph types. The implications of this work extend to real-time monitoring systems, infrastructure resilience planning, and enhancing security in networked environments. Keywords: graph neural networks, egocentric representation, convolutional neural networks, critical structure detection, distributed graph processing, network analysis.
