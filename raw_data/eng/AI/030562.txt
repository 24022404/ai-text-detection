Multiple Object Tracking (MOT) systems are critical for applications such as autonomous driving, surveillance, and robotics. While adversarial attacks have been extensively studied in object detection, their impact on MOT systems remains underexplored. This paper presents the first adversarial attack framework specifically designed to target MOT systems, demonstrating that fooling object detection alone is insufficient to disrupt tracking performance. Our approach leverages a novel optimization strategy to generate adversarial perturbations that simultaneously degrade detection accuracy and disrupt tracking consistency across frames. We evaluate the proposed attack on state-of-the-art MOT benchmarks, showing significant reductions in tracking accuracy and robustness. The results reveal vulnerabilities in current MOT systems and highlight the need for more resilient tracking algorithms. This work contributes to the growing body of research on adversarial machine learning by extending the scope of attacks to multi-frame tracking tasks, offering insights for future defenses and system improvements.  

**Keywords**: Multiple Object Tracking, Adversarial Attacks, Object Detection, Robustness, Machine Learning, Computer Vision.
