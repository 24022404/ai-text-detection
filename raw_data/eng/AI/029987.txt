Semi-supervised learning (SSL) offers a promising approach to leverage unlabeled data for improving fine-grained classification, a task often hindered by limited labeled examples. This paper presents a realistic evaluation of SSL techniques on several challenging fine-grained datasets. We investigate the effectiveness of consistency regularization and pseudo-labeling methods, focusing on their sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning and the quality of generated pseudo-labels. Our approach incorporates a novel metric for assessing pseudo-label reliability, allowing for dynamic adjustment of SSL contribution during training. Experimental results demonstrate that while SSL can improve performance compared to supervised learning with limited data, the gains are highly dependent on careful parameter selection and robust pseudo-label filtering. Furthermore, we identify scenarios where SSL can degrade performance due to the introduction of noisy labels. We conclude by providing practical recommendations for applying SSL in fine-grained classification tasks, emphasizing the importance of validation strategies and pseudo-label quality control. Keywords: Semi-Supervised Learning, Fine-Grained Classification, Consistency Regularization, Pseudo-Labeling, Machine Learning, Transfer Learning.
