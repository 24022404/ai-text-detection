This paper presents a novel spectral algorithm for Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), a widely used probabilistic model for topic modeling in text analysis. The objective of this research is to address the computational inefficiencies and scalability challenges associated with traditional inference methods, such as Gibbs sampling and variational inference, particularly when applied to large-scale datasets. Our approach leverages spectral decomposition techniques to estimate the parameters of the LDA model directly from the data, bypassing the need for iterative optimization. The proposed algorithm operates by constructing low-rank approximations of the word co-occurrence matrix and extracting topic-word distributions through eigenvalue decomposition. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the spectral algorithm achieves comparable topic coherence and document classification accuracy to state-of-the-art methods while significantly reducing computational time. Furthermore, the method exhibits robustness to noise and scalability to high-dimensional data, making it suitable for real-world applications such as document clustering, recommendation systems, and information retrieval. This work contributes to the field of topic modeling by introducing a computationally efficient and theoretically grounded alternative to existing inference techniques, with potential implications for advancing large-scale text analysis and machine learning workflows.  

**Keywords**: Latent Dirichlet Allocation, spectral decomposition, topic modeling, text analysis, computational efficiency, machine learning.
