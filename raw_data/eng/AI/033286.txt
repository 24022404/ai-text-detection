Accurate El Niño forecasts are crucial for mitigating the adverse impacts of this climate phenomenon on global weather patterns and economies. This paper introduces a novel approach leveraging Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to model the complex interactions within the climate system and improve El Niño prediction accuracy. We represent the Earth as a graph, where nodes represent geographical locations and edges signify climate-related dependencies. A custom GNN architecture is designed to learn spatiotemporal patterns from historical climate data, incorporating sea surface temperatures, wind patterns, and atmospheric pressure. Our results demonstrate that the GNN-based model outperforms traditional statistical methods and conventional neural networks in predicting El Niño events up to 12 months in advance, achieving a significant reduction in forecast error. This approach captures intricate climate dependencies often overlooked by traditional methods, leading to more reliable and timely warnings. The proposed GNN framework provides a powerful tool for climate scientists and policymakers to better anticipate and prepare for the impacts of El Niño. Keywords: El Niño, Graph Neural Networks, Climate Modeling, Forecasting, Spatiotemporal Analysis, Climate Change.
