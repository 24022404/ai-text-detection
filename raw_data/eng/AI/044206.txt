Adversarial examples—inputs intentionally perturbed to fool deep learning models—have highlighted critical vulnerabilities in image classification. However, their impact on more complex visual tasks such as semantic segmentation and object detection remains less explored. This paper investigates the generation and consequences of adversarial examples targeting leading semantic segmentation and object detection networks. We propose a systematic method to construct imperceptible pixel-level perturbations that can significantly degrade the accuracy of state-of-the-art models, including deep convolutional and transformer-based architectures. Extensive experiments demonstrate that these adversarial attacks not only reduce overall performance metrics, but also cause targeted failures such as mislabeling of specific object classes or suppression of object localization. Furthermore, our results reveal a transferability effect, where adversarial patterns crafted for one model often compromise others with similar structures. The findings underscore the need for enhanced robustness in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and medical image analysis. In conclusion, this work advances the understanding of adversarial threats in semantic segmentation and object detection, and provides a foundation for developing more resilient deep learning systems. 

Keywords: adversarial examples, semantic segmentation, object detection, deep learning, model robustness, security, neural networks
