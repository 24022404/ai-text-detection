Image super-resolution (SR) is a critical task in computer vision, aiming to reconstruct high-resolution images from low-resolution inputs. Traditional SR methods often rely on single-reference learning, which limits their ability to capture diverse texture details and structural information. This paper introduces a novel Attention-based Multi-Reference Learning (AMRL) framework to address these limitations. The proposed approach leverages multiple reference images to enrich the reconstruction process, employing an attention mechanism to dynamically prioritize the most relevant features from each reference. The AMRL framework integrates a transformer-based architecture to model long-range dependencies and enhance feature fusion. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that AMRL outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both quantitative metrics (e.g., PSNR and SSIM) and visual quality. The attention mechanism effectively reduces artifacts and improves texture fidelity, particularly in complex scenarios with varying textures and structures. This work contributes to the field by providing a robust and flexible solution for image super-resolution, with potential applications in medical imaging, satellite imagery, and digital restoration.  

**Keywords**: image super-resolution, attention mechanism, multi-reference learning, transformer, feature fusion.
