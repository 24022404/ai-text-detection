This paper presents a novel approach to deep reinforcement learning by introducing a surrogate agent-environment interface. The objective is to improve the efficiency and scalability of reinforcement learning in complex environments. Our method employs a surrogate model to approximate the environment's behavior, allowing the agent to learn from simulated experiences rather than direct interactions. We utilize a deep neural network to represent the surrogate interface, which is trained concurrently with the reinforcement learning agent. The results show that our approach significantly reduces the number of required environment interactions, leading to faster convergence and improved performance. Key findings include a 30% reduction in training time and a 25% increase in cumulative rewards compared to traditional reinforcement learning methods. The proposed framework has important implications for applications in robotics, game playing, and autonomous systems, where efficient exploration and learning are crucial. Our contributions include the development of a novel surrogate-based reinforcement learning algorithm and its evaluation in various environments, demonstrating its potential to accelerate the development of intelligent agents. Relevant keywords: deep reinforcement learning, surrogate modeling, agent-environment interface, neural networks, robotics, autonomous systems.
