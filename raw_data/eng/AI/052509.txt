This paper explores the concept of "steerability" in generative adversarial networks (GANs), a crucial aspect of controlling and manipulating the output of these complex models. Our objective is to investigate the extent to which GANs can be steered towards generating specific, desired outcomes, while maintaining their ability to produce realistic and diverse samples. We propose a novel approach, leveraging techniques from adversarial training and latent space manipulation, to enhance the steerability of GANs. Our method involves introducing a set of auxiliary loss functions and regularization terms, which encourage the generator to produce samples that are not only realistic but also responsive to user-specified inputs. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, showing significant improvements in the steerability of GANs, particularly in applications such as image and video generation. Our findings have important implications for the development of more controllable and user-friendly GANs, with potential applications in areas like computer vision, robotics, and data augmentation. Key contributions of this work include the introduction of a novel steerability metric and the development of a flexible framework for incorporating user feedback into GAN training. Relevant keywords: GANs, steerability, adversarial training, latent space manipulation, computer vision, generative models.
