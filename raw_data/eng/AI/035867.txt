The rapid advancement of deep learning techniques has led to the proliferation of realistic video face manipulation, raising significant concerns about misinformation and digital security. This paper addresses the critical challenge of detecting manipulated faces in videos by proposing an ensemble-based approach leveraging Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). The objective is to enhance detection accuracy and robustness across diverse manipulation techniques, including deepfakes and face swaps. Our method integrates multiple CNN architectures, each trained on distinct feature representations, to capture subtle artifacts and inconsistencies introduced during manipulation. The ensemble framework combines the outputs of these models using a weighted voting mechanism, ensuring improved generalization and reduced false positives. Experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets, including FaceForensics++ and Celeb-DF, demonstrating that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance with an accuracy of 98.2% and a low false acceptance rate. The results highlight the effectiveness of the ensemble strategy in identifying manipulated content, even under challenging conditions such as compression and varying video quality. This research contributes to the field of digital forensics by providing a reliable and scalable solution for video face manipulation detection, with potential applications in media integrity verification, cybersecurity, and law enforcement.  

**Keywords**: Video face manipulation, deepfake detection, ensemble learning, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), digital forensics, media integrity.
