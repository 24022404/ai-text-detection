This paper introduces PanRep, a novel graph neural network framework designed to extract universal node embeddings in heterogeneous graphs. The objective is to develop a robust and scalable method that can effectively capture the complex relationships and structures present in heterogeneous graphs, which consist of diverse node and edge types. To achieve this, PanRep employs a meta-learning approach, combining graph attention mechanisms and graph convolutional networks to learn node embeddings that are transferable across different graph domains. The results demonstrate that PanRep outperforms state-of-the-art methods in various node classification and clustering tasks, showcasing its ability to learn universal node representations. The key contributions of this research include the development of a heterogeneous graph neural network architecture, the introduction of a meta-learning strategy for learning transferable node embeddings, and the demonstration of PanRep's effectiveness in real-world applications. This work has significant implications for graph representation learning, network analysis, and applications in fields such as social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics, with relevant keywords including graph neural networks, heterogeneous graphs, node embeddings, meta-learning, and graph representation learning.
