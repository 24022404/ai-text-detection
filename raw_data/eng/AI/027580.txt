Moving object detection is a critical task in computer vision, particularly for applications in autonomous systems, surveillance, and robotics. Event-based vision, which captures dynamic changes in a scene asynchronously, offers significant advantages over traditional frame-based methods in terms of efficiency and temporal resolution. This paper presents a novel approach for moving object detection using k-means clustering tailored for event-based vision data. The proposed method leverages the spatiotemporal characteristics of event streams to segment moving objects from the background. By clustering events based on their spatial proximity and temporal consistency, the algorithm effectively identifies and isolates dynamic regions in the scene. Experimental results demonstrate that the approach achieves robust detection performance, even in complex environments with varying lighting conditions and multiple moving objects. Compared to existing methods, the proposed solution offers improved computational efficiency and scalability, making it suitable for real-time applications. The findings highlight the potential of k-means clustering as a lightweight yet effective technique for event-based moving object detection, paving the way for further advancements in this domain.  

**Keywords**: moving object detection, event-based vision, k-means clustering, spatiotemporal analysis, real-time systems.
