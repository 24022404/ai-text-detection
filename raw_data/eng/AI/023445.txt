Graph-based data representation is a powerful tool for modeling complex relationships in various domains, including social networks, bioinformatics, and computer vision. However, traditional graph analysis methods often struggle with scalability and computational efficiency. This paper introduces *Stochastic Graphlet Embedding* (SGE), a novel approach for graph representation that leverages stochastic sampling to efficiently capture local and global structural patterns. The proposed method employs a randomized algorithm to extract and encode graphlets—small subgraphs that serve as building blocks for larger graphs—into a low-dimensional embedding space. By combining stochastic sampling with graphlet-based feature extraction, SGE significantly reduces computational complexity while preserving the discriminative power of graph representations. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that SGE outperforms existing graph embedding techniques in terms of both accuracy and scalability, achieving state-of-the-art performance in graph classification and clustering tasks. Furthermore, the method exhibits robustness to noise and variations in graph structure, making it suitable for real-world applications. The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to balance efficiency and effectiveness, offering a scalable solution for large-scale graph analysis. Potential applications include network anomaly detection, molecular property prediction, and social network analysis. Keywords: graph embedding, graphlets, stochastic sampling, graph representation, graph classification, scalability.
