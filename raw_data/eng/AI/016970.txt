This paper introduces a novel deep learning approach for automatic natural image matting, a challenging task in computer vision aiming to accurately estimate the alpha matte that defines the foreground transparency. Our method employs a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture specifically designed to address the ill-posed nature of matting. The network utilizes a multi-scale feature extraction module combined with an attention mechanism to effectively capture both local details and global contextual information. Furthermore, we propose a new loss function that incorporates gradient constraints to improve the sharpness of the estimated alpha matte, especially around complex boundaries. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art matting algorithms in terms of both quantitative metrics and visual quality. The proposed method exhibits superior robustness to challenging scenarios such as complex hair structures and intricate foreground-background interactions. Our contributions include a novel deep matting architecture, an effective loss function, and comprehensive experimental validation, highlighting the potential for high-quality automatic image matting. Keywords: Image Matting, Deep Learning, Convolutional Neural Network, Alpha Matte, Semantic Segmentation, Computer Vision.
