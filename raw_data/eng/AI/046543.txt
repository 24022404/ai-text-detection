This paper investigates the application of transfer learning using deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for the tasks of gender recognition and age estimation from facial images. The primary objective is to address the challenges associated with limited annotated datasets and the high variability in facial features across different ages and genders. To achieve this, we employ pre-trained deep CNN architectures as feature extractors and subsequently fine-tune them on benchmark datasets specifically curated for gender and age classification. Our approach leverages the representational power of well-established deep models, minimizing the need for extensive data collection and training from scratch. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed transfer learning strategies outperform traditional machine learning and shallow neural methods, achieving significant improvements in classification accuracy and robustness to diverse image conditions. Comparative analyses across several CNN architectures also reveal optimal configurations for both age and gender tasks. These findings underscore the efficacy of transfer learning in facial analysis, presenting a scalable and practical solution for real-world applications such as surveillance, biometric authentication, and demographic analytics. Keywords: transfer learning, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, gender recognition, age estimation, facial analysis, fine-tuning.
