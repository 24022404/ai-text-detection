This paper presents a comparative analysis of Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Independent Component Analysis (ICA) from the perspective of data distribution. The objective is to explore how these dimensionality reduction techniques perform under different data distribution scenarios, particularly focusing on their ability to capture underlying structures and dependencies. PCA, a widely used linear transformation method, is evaluated for its effectiveness in identifying orthogonal components that maximize variance. In contrast, ICA, which aims to separate statistically independent sources, is examined for its capability to uncover non-Gaussian and independent features in the data. The study employs synthetic datasets with varying distributions, including Gaussian, non-Gaussian, and mixed distributions, to assess the performance of both methods. Results indicate that PCA excels in scenarios where data follows a Gaussian distribution, while ICA demonstrates superior performance in non-Gaussian and independent source separation tasks. The findings highlight the importance of selecting the appropriate technique based on the inherent characteristics of the data. This research contributes to a deeper understanding of PCA and ICA, providing practical insights for their application in fields such as signal processing, image analysis, and machine learning.  

**Keywords**: Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Independent Component Analysis (ICA), dimensionality reduction, data distribution, Gaussian distribution, non-Gaussian distribution.
