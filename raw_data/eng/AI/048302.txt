This paper explores the challenge of representing triangle-rich complex networks using low rank approximations. The objective is to investigate the fundamental limits of dimensionality reduction techniques in capturing the intricate structure of networks characterized by a high density of triangles. We employ a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical evaluations to demonstrate the impossibility of low rank representations for such networks. Our approach involves analyzing the spectral properties of triangle-rich networks and comparing them to existing low rank approximation methods. The results show that these networks exhibit a unique set of characteristics that make them resistant to low rank representations, leading to significant losses in structural information. We conclude that traditional dimensionality reduction techniques are insufficient for capturing the complex topology of triangle-rich networks, highlighting the need for novel representation methods that can effectively preserve the network's geometric and topological properties. Our findings have important implications for network analysis, community detection, and visualization, particularly in domains where triangle-rich structures are prevalent, such as social networks and biological systems. Key keywords: low rank representations, triangle-rich networks, complex networks, dimensionality reduction, network analysis.
