This paper investigates the multiview differential geometry of curves, addressing the foundational problem of how spatial curves and their geometric properties are observed and reconstructed from multiple camera views. The objective is to establish a comprehensive mathematical framework that connects the intrinsic differential geometry of a three-dimensional (3D) curve with its observed projections in two-dimensional (2D) images captured from different viewpoints. We introduce novel models and analytical techniques that relate the curvature, torsion, and higher-order differential invariants of a spatial curve to their multiview image representations. Our approach employs concepts from projective geometry and camera calibration to derive explicit expressions for differential geometric features under perspective projection. Experimental results on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our methods in accurately recovering 3D curve geometry from multiple images, even in the presence of noise and viewpoint variation. The findings provide new theoretical insights into multiview geometry, with practical implications for computer vision applications such as 3D reconstruction, motion analysis, and augmented reality. Key contributions include a unified multiview framework for curve differential invariants and algorithms enabling robust geometric reconstruction from multiview observations. Keywords: multiview geometry, differential geometry, 3D curve reconstruction, computer vision, projective invariants, camera calibration.
