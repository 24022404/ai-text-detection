This paper introduces a novel approach to detachable object detection in short-baseline video, addressing the challenge of accurately identifying and segmenting individual objects while determining their relative depth ordering. Our method leverages a deep learning framework that integrates semantic segmentation with stereo depth estimation. Specifically, we employ a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture trained to simultaneously predict pixel-wise semantic labels and disparity maps from adjacent video frames. A subsequent refinement module utilizes the estimated disparity to enforce depth consistency between segmented objects, enabling precise separation and ordering. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in object segmentation accuracy and depth ordering compared to existing methods. Key findings include improved robustness to occlusions and complex scenes, along with efficient processing suitable for real-time applications. This research contributes a novel framework for 3D scene understanding from video, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous navigation, and augmented reality.
**Keywords:** Object Detection, Segmentation, Depth Ordering, Stereo Vision, Deep Learning, Video Analysis.
