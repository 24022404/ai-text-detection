This paper proposes Sparse R-CNN, a novel end-to-end object detection framework that integrates learnable proposals into a single-stage detection model. The objective is to improve detection accuracy and efficiency by eliminating the need for pre-defined anchor boxes and non-maximum suppression. Our approach utilizes a sparse set of learnable proposals, which are optimized jointly with the detection model to selectively focus on regions of interest. Experimental results demonstrate that Sparse R-CNN outperforms state-of-the-art detectors on benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and speed. The key findings include the effectiveness of learnable proposals in reducing false positives and improving detection recall. Our research contributes to the advancement of object detection systems, enabling more accurate and efficient computer vision applications, such as autonomous driving, surveillance, and image analysis. The proposed Sparse R-CNN model has significant implications for real-world applications, highlighting the potential of end-to-end learning and sparse representation for object detection tasks, with relevant keywords including object detection, learnable proposals, end-to-end learning, and computer vision.
