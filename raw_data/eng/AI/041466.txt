This paper introduces Deep Photo Scan, a novel semi-supervised learning approach designed to address real-world degradation issues in smartphone photo scanning. The objective is to develop a robust method for restoring scanned photos to their original quality, despite common degradations such as blur, noise, and distortion. Our approach leverages a combination of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and generative adversarial networks (GANs) to learn from a limited set of labeled data and a large corpus of unlabeled scans. The results show significant improvements in image quality, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in both quantitative and qualitative evaluations. Key findings include the ability to effectively remove noise and blur, correct distortions, and enhance overall image clarity. The Deep Photo Scan method has important implications for preserving and restoring valuable memories and historical documents, and its semi-supervised nature makes it particularly suitable for real-world applications where labeled data is scarce. By advancing the field of image restoration and enhancement, this research contributes to the development of more sophisticated smartphone photo scanning technologies, with potential applications in areas such as digital archiving, cultural heritage preservation, and personal photo management, leveraging key technologies including deep learning, computer vision, and image processing.
