This paper introduces a novel approach for temporal shape super-resolution through intra-frame motion encoding, leveraging high-frame-per-second (high-fps) structured light techniques. The objective of our research is to overcome the temporal resolution limits inherent in conventional 3D shape measurement systems, which often struggle to accurately capture fast-moving objects due to hardware and frame rate constraints. Our method encodes intra-frame object motion into captured structured light patterns by synchronizing high-speed projection and imaging devices. We propose a comprehensive reconstruction algorithm that analyzes the motion-blurred encoded patterns within each frame, enabling the accurate extraction of multiple sub-frame 3D shapes from a single exposure. Experimental results demonstrate that our system significantly improves temporal resolution and reconstructs finer details of dynamic scenes compared to traditional structured light methods. This advancement allows for precise visualization and analysis of rapid object movements, with applications in industrial inspection, biomechanics, animation, and robotics. Our contributions include the development of a robust intra-frame motion encoding strategy, an efficient shape reconstruction pipeline, and a demonstration of the system's enhanced temporal and spatial performance. Keywords: temporal super-resolution, high-fps structured light, 3D reconstruction, intra-frame motion encoding, dynamic scene analysis.
