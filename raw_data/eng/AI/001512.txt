This paper addresses the challenge of achieving controllable invariance in machine learning models, a crucial requirement for enhancing model robustness and fairness across diverse data distributions. We propose a novel framework based on adversarial feature learning, where a model is guided to learn data representations that are invariant to specific nuisance factors while retaining predictive utility for the target task. Our approach introduces an adversarial objective that allows explicit control over the dimensions of invariance, enabling users to specify which features should be deemed invariant and to what extent. Experiments are conducted on benchmark datasets in computer vision and natural language processing to evaluate the effectiveness of our method. The results demonstrate significant improvements in invariance and generalization performance compared to existing techniques, with minimal impact on accuracy. Furthermore, we provide a detailed analysis of the trade-offs between invariance strength and task performance, showcasing the adaptability of our approach. The proposed framework offers a powerful tool for developing more interpretable, robust, and fair machine learning systems. Key keywords include adversarial learning, feature invariance, controllable representation learning, robustness, and fairness in machine learning.
