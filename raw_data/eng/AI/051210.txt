In this paper, we address the challenge of weakly supervised top-down salient object detection, a critical task in computer vision with applications in image segmentation and object recognition. Our objective is to enhance the accuracy of salient object detection using a novel Backtracking Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP)-based image classifier, which integrates both spatial and contextual information effectively. The proposed method employs a dual-stage backtracking mechanism to refine feature representation and improve detection granularity without relying on extensive labeled data. Utilizing a comprehensive dataset, we demonstrate that our SPP-based classifier outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques in terms of precision and recall rates, achieving a significant improvement in detection performance. Our findings reveal that the integration of spatial pyramid pooling with backtracking strategies not only enhances the model's robustness but also allows for better generalization across varying image conditions. The implications of this research extend to various domains, including autonomous driving, video surveillance, and content-based image retrieval, where accurate salient object detection is paramount. Our work provides a foundational framework for future explorations in weakly supervised learning approaches, positioning it as a valuable contribution to the field of computer vision.

Keywords: salient object detection, weakly supervised learning, spatial pyramid pooling, image classification, backtracking mechanism.
