The Adam optimizer has become a cornerstone in training deep learning models due to its adaptive learning rate capabilities. However, its reliance on exponential moving averages of past gradients often leads to suboptimal convergence in certain scenarios. This paper revisits the Adam optimizer by introducing a novel weighted past gradients perspective, which dynamically adjusts the influence of historical gradients based on their relevance to the current optimization landscape. Our approach, termed Weighted Adam (W-Adam), employs a context-aware weighting mechanism to prioritize gradients that align with the current descent direction, thereby enhancing convergence stability and performance. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets and deep neural network architectures demonstrate that W-Adam achieves faster convergence and improved generalization compared to standard Adam and its variants. The proposed method also exhibits robustness in handling non-stationary optimization landscapes, making it particularly suitable for tasks such as fine-tuning pre-trained models and training on noisy datasets. This work contributes a principled enhancement to adaptive optimization techniques, offering a practical solution for improving the efficiency and reliability of deep learning training processes.  

**Keywords**: Adam optimizer, adaptive optimization, weighted gradients, deep learning, convergence stability, gradient descent.
