This paper investigates the challenge of deriving non-monotonic rules from statistical learning models by employing high-utility itemset mining. Traditional rule induction approaches often struggle with non-monotonic relationships due to their inherent complexity and variability.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework that integrates high-utility itemset mining techniques with statistical learning models to identify and extract non-monotonic rules. The methodology leverages the utility-oriented evaluation of itemsets to focus on high-value patterns within the data, which are then analyzed through robust statistical models to uncover underlying non-monotonic interactions.

Results/Findings: Experiments conducted on multiple datasets demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed approach in identifying significant non-monotonic rules that are often overlooked by conventional methods. The results indicate superior performance in terms of precision and recall, showcasing an enhanced capability to capture complex data patterns. Comparisons with existing techniques reveal marked improvements in both rule quality and computational efficiency.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field by providing a novel method for efficiently inducing non-monotonic rules with high interpretability and accuracy. The findings have significant implications for domains where understanding intricate data relationships is crucial, such as market analysis, healthcare, and risk management. The approach lays the groundwork for future exploration of utility-focused methods in advanced machine learning applications.

Keywords: non-monotonic rules, statistical learning models, high-utility itemset mining, data patterns, rule induction, computational efficiency.
