This paper introduces the Deep Differentiable Simplex Layer (DDSL), a novel neural network component designed to learn geometric signals from complex data. The objective is to improve the representation and analysis of geometric shapes and structures in various applications, including computer vision and graphics. Our approach leverages the concept of simplices, fundamental building blocks of geometric shapes, to create a differentiable and learnable layer that can be integrated into deep neural networks. The DDSL layer enables the learning of geometric signals by parameterizing simplices and optimizing their configuration through backpropagation. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of DDSL in learning geometric signals, outperforming existing methods in tasks such as 3D shape reconstruction and mesh segmentation. The proposed layer contributes to the development of more sophisticated geometric deep learning models, with potential applications in fields like robotics, computer-aided design, and medical imaging. Key contributions include the introduction of a novel differentiable simplex layer, improved geometric signal learning, and enhanced performance in geometric deep learning tasks. Relevant keywords: geometric deep learning, differentiable simplex, 3D shape reconstruction, mesh segmentation, neural networks.
