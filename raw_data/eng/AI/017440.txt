This paper addresses the challenge of evaluating conditional image generation models, which are crucial in various applications such as image-to-image translation, image synthesis, and data augmentation. The objective is to develop and compare comprehensive evaluation metrics that can accurately assess the quality and realism of generated images. We propose a multi-faceted approach, combining traditional metrics such as peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM) with more recent perceptual metrics, including Frechet inception distance (FID) and learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS). Our results show that the choice of evaluation metric significantly impacts the assessment of model performance, highlighting the need for a standardized and robust evaluation framework. The key findings of this study contribute to the development of more effective conditional image generation models, with potential applications in computer vision, robotics, and artificial intelligence. By providing a thorough analysis of evaluation metrics, this research aims to advance the field of image generation and facilitate the creation of more realistic and diverse images. Keywords: conditional image generation, evaluation metrics, image synthesis, computer vision, deep learning.
