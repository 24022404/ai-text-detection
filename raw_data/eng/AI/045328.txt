This paper presents a novel approach to gradient boosting by incorporating piece-wise linear regression trees as base learners, rather than the conventional piece-wise constant regression trees. The primary objective is to enhance the predictive accuracy and model expressiveness of gradient boosting algorithms, particularly in applications involving complex, non-linear relationships. The proposed method leverages trees whose leaf nodes represent linear models, allowing for a more flexible partitioning of the input space and improved fitting capabilities. 

To evaluate the effectiveness of this approach, extensive experiments were conducted on benchmark regression datasets. The results demonstrate that gradient boosting with piece-wise linear regression trees consistently outperforms traditional gradient boosted decision trees in terms of both root mean squared error and convergence speed. Comparative analyses highlight that the proposed method reduces overfitting and captures local trends within the data more effectively. Furthermore, an analysis of computational complexity shows that the overhead introduced by fitting linear models at the leaves is offset by faster convergence and improved generalization.

This research advances the state-of-the-art in boosted ensemble methods by introducing a more powerful base learner while preserving the robustness and scalability of the gradient boosting framework. The findings have significant implications for fields such as machine learning, predictive analytics, and data mining, especially in scenarios demanding high model interpretability and performance. Future work will explore extensions to classification tasks and integration with distributed computing environments. 

Keywords: gradient boosting, regression trees, piece-wise linear models, ensemble learning, machine learning, predictive analytics
