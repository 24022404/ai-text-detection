This paper investigates the problem of learning graphical models from observational data. We introduce a novel algorithm based on the multiplicative weights update method for structure learning. Our approach iteratively refines the estimated graph structure by adjusting edge weights based on the observed data's conditional dependencies. Unlike traditional constraint-based or score-based methods, our algorithm provides a computationally efficient way to explore the search space of possible graph structures. We present theoretical guarantees for the algorithm's convergence and demonstrate its effectiveness in identifying the underlying graph structure in synthetic data. Furthermore, we apply our method to real-world biological datasets, showcasing its ability to infer meaningful relationships between genes. The results show that our method achieves competitive performance compared to existing structure learning algorithms while offering improved scalability for high-dimensional datasets. This provides a valuable tool for researchers seeking to uncover causal relationships in complex systems.

Keywords: Graphical models, structure learning, multiplicative weights, conditional independence, causal inference, high-dimensional data.
