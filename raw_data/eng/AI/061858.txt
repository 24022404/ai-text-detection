This paper addresses the challenge of generalization in neural networks used for reinforcement learning, where over-generalization can lead to suboptimal performance. Our objective is to improve the performance of reinforcement learning agents by introducing a novel approach that breaks harmful generalization patterns in neural networks. We propose a method that utilizes a regularization technique to encourage the neural network to focus on specific task-related features, rather than generalizing to irrelevant ones. Our approach is based on a combination of feature selection and reward shaping, which allows the agent to learn more effective policies. Experimental results show that our method outperforms existing state-of-the-art reinforcement learning algorithms in several benchmark environments, demonstrating improved sample efficiency and overall performance. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of controlling generalization in neural networks for reinforcement learning and provide a new perspective on the design of more efficient and effective learning algorithms. Our contributions have significant implications for the development of autonomous systems and decision-making agents, and can be applied to various domains, including robotics, game playing, and recommender systems, with relevant keywords including reinforcement learning, neural networks, generalization, feature selection, and reward shaping.
