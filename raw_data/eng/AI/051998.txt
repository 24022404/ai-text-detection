This paper addresses the challenges in image captioning by exploring the potential of scene graphs, a structured representation capturing objects and their relationships within an image, as a valuable asset for enhancing caption generation. Despite advancements in deep learning models, the complexity of integrating semantic relationships to produce contextually rich descriptions remains a significant hurdle.

Methods/Approach: We present an image captioning framework leveraging scene graphs to systematically interpret visual scenes. The approach involves generating scene graphs from images using a neural network-based model, followed by employing a graph-to-text transformer that translates structured information into descriptive captions. This method is compared against conventional deep learning models to assess the augmentation capabilities provided by scene graphs.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that incorporating scene graphs significantly improves the quality of image captions, resulting in higher scores on standardized metrics such as BLEU, METEOR, and CIDEr. The framework enables better semantic comprehension, thereby generating more coherent and context-aware captions. Additionally, comparison with existing models highlights the framework's superior ability to capture intricate relationships within images.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings reaffirm the relevance and utility of scene graphs in advancing image captioning technologies. By integrating structured representations, the proposed method contributes a meaningful approach to address the limitations of current models, fostering improvements in automated image description tasks. This research provides a foundation for further exploration of scene graphs in other visual understanding applications, promoting richer media interpretation and enhanced human-computer interaction. 

Keywords: Image Captioning, Scene Graphs, Visual Understanding, Semantic Relationships, Neural Networks, Graph-to-Text Transformer.
