Graph clustering, the process of partitioning a graph’s nodes into groups with dense intra-group and sparse inter-group connections, is foundational for numerous applications in information retrieval, data mining, and social network analysis. Despite the extensive literature on clustering algorithms, the theoretical understanding of their consistency properties remains limited. This paper introduces a novel consistency axiom for graph clustering, aiming to formalize the expectations from clustering functions when the graph structure is perturbed in a way that should preserve or reinforce existing clusters. We systematically analyze a broad class of popular graph clustering methods under this new axiom and provide possibility results that characterize which families of algorithms satisfy or violate the proposed property. Our approach includes rigorous axiomatic analysis and illustrative examples, demonstrating both the strengths and boundaries of established methods such as modularity maximization, spectral clustering, and linkage-based techniques. The findings offer valuable insights into the inherent trade-offs faced when designing clustering algorithms that are robust to structural changes in the underlying graph. Our work not only extends the theoretical foundations of graph clustering but also informs practitioners in selecting or designing algorithms that align with desirable consistency guarantees. Keywords: graph clustering, consistency axiom, axiomatic analysis, modularity, spectral clustering, clustering robustness.
