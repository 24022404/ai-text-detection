This paper presents a novel approach called EgoNet for first-person action-object detection, addressing the challenge of accurately identifying actions and associated objects from egocentric video data. The need to enhance real-time interaction and understanding in wearable devices and augmented reality applications drives this research.

Methods/Approach: EgoNet utilizes a deep learning framework tailored for processing egocentric video streams. By integrating convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with attention mechanisms, the system effectively discerns relevant features from dynamic backgrounds, facilitating precise detection of user actions and the objects they interact with. Our approach emphasizes the synergy between motion cues and contextual information, refining the detection accuracy over existing methods.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations on benchmark egocentric video datasets demonstrate that EgoNet achieves superior performance, with notable improvements in detection accuracy and processing speed compared to state-of-the-art models. The system exhibits robust action-object recognition capability in complex scenarios, such as fast motion or occlusion, which are prevalent in first-person perspectives.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed EgoNet framework advances the field of first-person action-object detection, offering significant contributions to enhancing real-time interaction capabilities in wearable technology. Its effectiveness opens new possibilities for applications in augmented and virtual reality, personal assistants, and interactive gaming environments. Future work may explore further optimizations and adaptations of EgoNet for diverse real-world conditions and hardware platforms.

Keywords: EgoNet, first-person action detection, object detection, egocentric video, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, attention mechanisms, augmented reality.
