This paper addresses the problem of estimating the intrinsic dimension of high-dimensional datasets, a crucial task in understanding the underlying structure of complex data. Our objective is to develop a robust and efficient method for intrinsic dimension estimation, which is essential in various applications such as data visualization, anomaly detection, and feature selection. We propose a novel approach based on a combination of geometric and statistical techniques, leveraging the concept of manifold learning to estimate the intrinsic dimension. Our method outperforms existing techniques in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly in the presence of noise and outliers. Experimental results on various benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, with significant improvements in dimension estimation accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods. The proposed technique has important implications for high-dimensional data analysis, enabling better understanding and visualization of complex datasets. Key contributions of this research include the development of a robust intrinsic dimension estimation method, with potential applications in machine learning, data mining, and computer vision. Relevant keywords: intrinsic dimension, manifold learning, high-dimensional data, dimensionality reduction, data analysis.
