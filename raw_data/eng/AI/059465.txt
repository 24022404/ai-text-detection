This paper introduces a novel ranking algorithm leveraging feature information to improve ranking accuracy and efficiency. The algorithm, named FeatureRank, incorporates feature dependencies through a graph-theoretic framework.  We model the ranking problem as a graph where nodes represent items to be ranked and edge weights reflect feature similarity and relevance. The FeatureRank algorithm then utilizes graph centrality measures to determine the ranking scores. We present a detailed analysis of FeatureRank's computational complexity and convergence properties using established graph theory principles. Experimental results on several benchmark datasets demonstrate that FeatureRank consistently outperforms traditional ranking algorithms, particularly in scenarios with sparse or noisy data. These results highlight the algorithm's robustness and effectiveness in capturing feature relationships. The graph-theoretic analysis provides valuable insights into the algorithm's behavior and informs parameter tuning for optimal performance. Key words: Ranking Algorithm, Feature Selection, Graph Theory, Centrality Measures, Information Retrieval.
