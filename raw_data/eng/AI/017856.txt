**Abstract**

Recent advancements in Natural Language Processing (NLP) have predominantly focused on large-scale models trained on extensive computational resources, often making them inaccessible to researchers and practitioners with limited hardware. Addressing this gap, this paper introduces "Small-Bench NLP," a comprehensive benchmark suite specifically designed for evaluating the performance of small-scale NLP models trained on a single GPU. Our objective is to provide a standardized assessment framework for compact models across a diverse array of tasks, including text classification, sentiment analysis, and named entity recognition. The proposed benchmark incorporates a curated set of datasets, task-specific metrics, and baseline results for popular lightweight architectures, ensuring fair and reproducible comparisons. We systematically evaluate several state-of-the-art small models, such as distilled transformers and quantized neural networks, highlighting their strengths and limitations under constrained hardware environments. Experimental results demonstrate that while recent compact models show promising performance, significant trade-offs remain between efficiency and accuracy. Our findings underline the importance of tailored benchmarks for fostering innovation in resource-efficient NLP solutions. Small-Bench NLP serves as an essential tool for the community to advance the development, comparison, and deployment of NLP models suitable for single-GPU and low-resource scenarios. Key keywords: NLP benchmark, small models, single GPU, efficiency, resource-constrained, transformer, evaluation.
