This paper addresses the challenge of segmenting and reconstructing multiple "hedgehog" shapes in complex visual data, a problem with significant applications in medical imaging, robotics, and 3D modeling. Traditional segmentation methods often struggle with overlapping or irregularly shaped objects, particularly when dealing with intricate structures resembling hedgehog spines. To overcome this, we propose a novel extension of the A-expansion algorithm, tailored for handling multiple such shapes simultaneously. Our approach integrates a robust energy minimization framework with a multi-label optimization strategy, enabling precise boundary delineation and accurate shape recovery. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing techniques in terms of segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly in scenarios with high shape complexity and occlusion. The algorithm achieves an average improvement of 15% in boundary precision compared to state-of-the-art methods. This work contributes a scalable and efficient solution for multi-shape segmentation, with potential applications in automated medical diagnostics, autonomous navigation, and 3D reconstruction.  

**Keywords**: A-expansion, multi-shape segmentation, hedgehog shapes, energy minimization, 3D reconstruction, computer vision.
