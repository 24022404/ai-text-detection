This research addresses the challenge of optimizing user association in 5G and beyond networks. With the increasing complexity and density of next-generation networks, efficient user association is crucial for maximizing network performance and ensuring quality of service.

Methods/Approach: We propose novel transferable and distributed user association policies leveraging machine learning techniques. Our approach involves designing policies capable of adapting to varying network conditions and infrastructures, thus ensuring robust performance. The algorithms are trained and validated using a combination of simulated environments and real-world network data, ensuring their applicability to diverse scenarios.

Results/Findings: The proposed policies demonstrate significant improvements in network efficiency and user experience compared to traditional association methods. Our evaluation shows increased data throughput and reduced latency. Furthermore, the distributed nature of our approach reduces computational overhead and enables scalable deployment across large networks.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field by providing scalable, adaptable, and efficient user association strategies for 5G networks, with implications for future network technologies. The transferable nature of the policies enables networks to learn and implement optimal strategies across different settings autonomously, paving the way for more intelligent and responsive network management solutions. Keywords include 5G networks, user association, machine learning, distributed computing, and network efficiency.
