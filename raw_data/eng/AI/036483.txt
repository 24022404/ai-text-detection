Object detection and localization are foundational tasks in computer vision, yet traditional methods often rely on bounding box proposals or segmentation masks, which can be computationally intensive and complex. In this paper, we introduce a novel approach to object representation called "Objects as Extreme Points." Our method models each object based on its extreme spatial coordinates—specifically, the top-most, bottom-most, left-most, and right-most points, which define the minimal enclosing rectangle. By detecting these extreme points using a deep convolutional neural network, we efficiently infer object boundaries without requiring exhaustive region proposals or complex anchor box designs.

We evaluate our approach on established benchmark datasets, demonstrating that it achieves competitive accuracy while significantly reducing inference time compared to conventional bounding box and anchor-based detectors. The method also exhibits robustness across a variety of object shapes and scales, outperforming classical and deep learning baselines in situations with irregular object contours. Our findings indicate that representing objects as extreme points simplifies the object annotation process and enables faster, more precise detection, especially in real-time systems or resource-constrained environments.

This research contributes a streamlined yet powerful alternative to traditional object detection paradigms, offering clear benefits in speed, simplicity, and annotation efficiency. Potential applications include autonomous vehicles, robotics, and augmented reality, where fast and reliable object detection is critical.

Keywords: object detection, extreme points, computer vision, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, region proposals, real-time systems.
