Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have emerged as a powerful framework for generating high-quality synthetic data across various domains. However, estimating the likelihood of generated samples remains a significant challenge, limiting their interpretability and practical applications. This paper addresses the problem of likelihood estimation in GANs by proposing a novel approach that integrates density estimation techniques with adversarial training. Our method leverages a hybrid architecture combining normalizing flows and GANs, enabling accurate likelihood computation while maintaining the generative quality of the model. We evaluate the proposed approach on benchmark datasets, demonstrating its ability to achieve competitive performance in both likelihood estimation and sample generation compared to existing methods. The results highlight the effectiveness of our approach in bridging the gap between generative modeling and probabilistic inference. This work contributes to advancing the interpretability and reliability of GANs, with potential applications in areas such as anomaly detection, data augmentation, and uncertainty quantification.  

**Keywords**: Generative Adversarial Networks, likelihood estimation, normalizing flows, density estimation, probabilistic inference, synthetic data generation.
