This paper addresses the challenge of learning approximate stochastic transition models from data, with the objective of improving the accuracy and efficiency of decision-making processes in complex systems. Our approach utilizes a novel combination of machine learning algorithms and probabilistic modeling techniques to learn compact and informative representations of stochastic transitions. The methodology involves training neural networks to predict transition probabilities and expected outcomes, leveraging techniques such as Bayesian inference and stochastic optimization to handle uncertainty and noise in the data. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in learning accurate transition models from limited data, outperforming existing methods in terms of prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. The proposed framework has significant implications for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and decision-making under uncertainty, enabling more reliable and adaptive behavior in complex environments. Key contributions include the development of a scalable and flexible learning framework, the integration of probabilistic modeling with deep learning, and the demonstration of improved performance in real-world scenarios, with relevant keywords including stochastic transition models, machine learning, Bayesian inference, and decision-making under uncertainty.
