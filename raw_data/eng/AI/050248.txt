This paper presents a comprehensive benchmarking study of state-of-the-art reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms on real-world robotic platforms. The primary objective is to evaluate the effectiveness and reliability of popular RL methods—such as Deep Q-Networks (DQN), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)—when deployed beyond simulated environments and applied directly to physical robots. Our methodology encompasses the selection of diverse robotic tasks, including manipulation, navigation, and locomotion, across multiple hardware platforms. The benchmark protocol accounts for key real-world challenges, such as sensor noise, actuator delays, and safety constraints, that are often absent in simulation-based evaluations. Experimental results demonstrate significant discrepancies in performance between simulation and reality, with some algorithms exhibiting reduced sample efficiency, robustness, and generalization capabilities on actual robots. Moreover, we provide a detailed analysis of failure cases, adaptation requirements, and practical considerations for deployment. The findings yield critical insights into the current limitations and advantages of RL algorithms in real-world robotic applications, highlighting areas for future development and improvement. By establishing standardized benchmarking protocols and presenting openly reproducible results, this study aims to foster systematic progress in bridging the reality gap for reinforcement learning in robotics. Keywords: reinforcement learning, real-world robots, benchmarking, sample efficiency, robotics, deep learning, algorithm evaluation.
