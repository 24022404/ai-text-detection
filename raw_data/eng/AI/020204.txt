This paper addresses the challenge of detachable object detection, aiming to accurately segment and determine the depth ordering of objects from short-baseline video sequences. Our approach leverages a novel combination of computer vision and machine learning techniques to achieve this goal. We propose a deep learning-based model that utilizes a short-baseline stereo setup to estimate depth maps and detect detachable objects. The model is trained on a large dataset of video sequences with annotated ground truth, allowing it to learn effective features for object segmentation and depth ordering. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance in detachable object detection and depth ordering. Our method has significant implications for various applications, including robotics, autonomous driving, and surveillance. Key contributions of this research include the development of a robust and efficient detachable object detection algorithm, the introduction of a new dataset for short-baseline video sequences, and the demonstration of the importance of depth ordering in object detection tasks. Relevant keywords: detachable object detection, short-baseline stereo, depth ordering, computer vision, machine learning, object segmentation.
