**Abstract**

Accurate stereo depth estimation under nighttime conditions poses significant challenges due to the prevalence of dynamic lighting effects, low illumination, and large uninformative image regions. This paper presents a novel joint translation-stereo learning framework specifically designed to address these obstacles in nighttime stereo vision. The proposed approach leverages an end-to-end neural network architecture that simultaneously learns to translate nighttime images into enhanced representations and to refine stereo depth estimation by explicitly modeling the impact of artificial light sources and indistinct image areas. Comprehensive experiments conducted on publicly available nighttime stereo datasets demonstrate that our system outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in both quantitative accuracy and robustness to adverse nighttime artifacts. Notably, our method reduces depth prediction errors in regions affected by glare, reflection, and poor illumination, and effectively mitigates the influence of uninformative areas. The findings emphasize the importance of jointly optimizing image translation and stereo matching tasks, particularly in adverse environments. This research contributes a robust solution for autonomous driving, surveillance, and robotics applications operating under challenging nighttime conditions. Key keywords include stereo depth estimation, nighttime imaging, light effects, uninformative regions, and joint learning.
