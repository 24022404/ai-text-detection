Neural network pruning aims to reduce computational costs and model size by removing redundant parameters. While various pruning strategies exist, a consistent explanation for their success and failure remains elusive. This paper investigates the role of dynamical isometry – the preservation of singular values during signal propagation – as a crucial factor in effective pruning. We hypothesize that pruning methods that maintain dynamical isometry are more likely to preserve network performance. To validate this, we analyze the spectral properties of the Jacobian matrix during training and after pruning, correlating these with the final accuracy. Our findings demonstrate that successful pruning techniques, such as magnitude-based pruning with careful fine-tuning, tend to preserve dynamical isometry, while disruptive methods lead to significant deviations. Furthermore, we propose a novel pruning metric based on dynamical isometry, demonstrating its superior performance in identifying parameters for removal compared to traditional methods. This work provides a theoretical foundation for understanding pruning efficacy and offers a new direction for developing more robust and efficient pruning algorithms. Keywords: Neural Network Pruning, Dynamical Isometry, Jacobian Matrix, Singular Value Decomposition, Network Optimization.
