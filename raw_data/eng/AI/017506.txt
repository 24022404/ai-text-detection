Breast cancer screening aims to detect cancer at an early stage, improving treatment outcomes. However, current methods often face challenges in balancing sensitivity and specificity, leading to both missed cancers and unnecessary biopsies. This paper introduces a novel evaluation metric for breast cancer screening that leverages Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs) to provide a confidence measure alongside predictions. Our approach utilizes the inherent uncertainty quantification of BNNs to develop a metric that prioritizes high-confidence positive cases while penalizing low-confidence predictions. The method was evaluated on a large clinical dataset, demonstrating a significant improvement in the area under the ROC curve (AUC) compared to traditional evaluation metrics based solely on binary classification accuracy. Furthermore, the confidence-based metric allows for a more nuanced assessment of model performance, facilitating the identification of cases where further investigation is warranted. This work offers a valuable tool for developing and evaluating more reliable and clinically relevant breast cancer screening systems, with implications for reducing false positives and improving cancer detection rates. Keywords: Breast Cancer Screening, Bayesian Neural Networks, Confidence Measure, Evaluation Metric, Uncertainty Quantification, Early Detection.
