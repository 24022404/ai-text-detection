The ability to reason effectively within large-scale theories is a critical challenge in artificial intelligence, particularly in domains requiring complex logical inference and decision-making. This paper addresses the problem of enabling AI systems to reason autonomously in large theories without relying on imitation learning or pre-existing human-generated reasoning traces. We propose a novel framework that combines reinforcement learning with symbolic reasoning techniques to guide the exploration of large theory spaces. Our approach leverages a dynamic reward mechanism to prioritize logical consistency and relevance, enabling the system to discover valid reasoning paths independently. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance compared to imitation-based approaches, while significantly reducing reliance on annotated training data. The results highlight the potential of our framework to generalize across diverse domains and scale to increasingly complex theories. This work contributes to advancing autonomous reasoning capabilities in AI systems, with implications for applications in automated theorem proving, knowledge base construction, and decision support systems.  

**Keywords**: autonomous reasoning, reinforcement learning, symbolic reasoning, large theories, logical inference, AI systems.
