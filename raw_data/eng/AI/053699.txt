This paper presents a novel approach to Visual Question Answering (VQA) that eliminates the need for question-answer pair training data. The objective is to develop a VQA model that can accurately answer visual questions without relying on large-scale question-answer datasets. Our method utilizes a multimodal fusion technique, combining visual features extracted from images with textual information from pre-trained language models. The approach leverages self-supervised learning to align visual and textual representations, enabling the model to generate answers to unseen questions. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, achieving competitive performance with state-of-the-art VQA models trained on large question-answer datasets. The key findings highlight the potential of self-supervised learning in VQA, reducing the dependence on annotated data and enabling more efficient model training. This research contributes to the development of more robust and adaptable VQA systems, with potential applications in areas such as image understanding, robotics, and human-computer interaction. Key keywords: Visual Question Answering, Self-Supervised Learning, Multimodal Fusion, Zero-Shot Learning.
