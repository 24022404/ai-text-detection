This paper explores the application of variational models in signal processing using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). The objective is to address the challenge of efficiently processing and analyzing signals represented on irregular graph structures, which are common in real-world applications such as sensor networks, social networks, and biological systems. We propose a novel variational framework that leverages the expressive power of GNNs to model complex dependencies and non-linear relationships within graph-structured signals. Our approach integrates variational inference techniques with GNN architectures, enabling robust signal reconstruction, denoising, and feature extraction. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms traditional signal processing techniques and baseline GNN models in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The findings highlight the potential of variational GNNs for advancing signal processing tasks in domains where data is inherently graph-based. This work contributes a scalable and adaptable framework that can be extended to various applications, including anomaly detection, graph-based compression, and multi-modal signal analysis. Keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Variational Models, Signal Processing, Graph-structured Data, Denoising, Feature Extraction.
