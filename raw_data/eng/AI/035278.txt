This study explores the application of the You Only Look Once (YOLO) object detection framework for marine life identification and monitoring. The objective is to address the challenges of real-time detection and classification of marine species in underwater environments, which are often hindered by low visibility, complex backgrounds, and the dynamic nature of aquatic ecosystems. Leveraging the YOLO architecture, we propose a lightweight and efficient model optimized for underwater imagery. The approach involves fine-tuning the YOLO algorithm using a curated dataset of marine life images, incorporating data augmentation techniques to enhance robustness against environmental variations. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model achieves high accuracy in detecting and classifying marine species, outperforming traditional methods in terms of speed and precision. The system’s real-time capabilities make it suitable for deployment in autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) and marine conservation efforts. This research contributes to advancing underwater computer vision by providing a scalable solution for marine biodiversity monitoring, with potential applications in ecological studies, fisheries management, and habitat preservation. Keywords: YOLO, marine life detection, underwater computer vision, real-time object detection, biodiversity monitoring.
