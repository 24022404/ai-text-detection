This paper addresses the challenge of reasoning in large theories, a crucial task in artificial intelligence and automated reasoning. The objective is to develop a method that enables machines to reason effectively in complex theoretical frameworks without relying on imitation-based approaches. To achieve this, we propose a novel learning-based approach that combines symbolic reasoning with machine learning techniques. Our method utilizes a graph neural network to represent and reason about large theories, allowing the model to learn from existing proofs and generate new ones. The results show that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of proof search efficiency and accuracy, particularly in large and complex theories. The key findings indicate that our model can generalize well to unseen theories and problems, demonstrating its potential for applications in formal verification, knowledge graph reasoning, and other areas of artificial intelligence. The main contribution of this research is the development of a scalable and effective learning-based reasoning framework that can handle large theories without requiring imitation or extensive human expertise, making it a significant step towards more autonomous and efficient reasoning systems. Key keywords: automated reasoning, graph neural networks, machine learning, formal verification, knowledge graph reasoning.
