Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have shown promise in learning representations for graph-structured data, but often struggle with long-range dependencies and computational bottlenecks. This paper introduces GraphiT, a novel approach to encode graph structure directly within Transformer architectures, leveraging self-attention mechanisms for enhanced graph representation learning. GraphiT converts adjacency information into positional encodings, allowing Transformers to effectively capture both local and global graph properties. We demonstrate GraphiT's performance on various graph-based tasks, including node classification, graph classification, and link prediction, using benchmark datasets such as Cora, PubMed, and ogbg-molhiv. Experimental results show that GraphiT achieves significant improvements in accuracy and efficiency compared to traditional GNNs and other graph Transformer variants, particularly on large and complex graphs. The ability to seamlessly integrate graph structure into Transformers opens new avenues for applying these powerful models to graph-related problems, offering a scalable and effective solution for graph representation learning. Keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Transformers, Graph Representation Learning, Positional Encoding, Self-Attention, Graph Classification.
