3D pedestrian detection is crucial for autonomous driving, yet remains challenging due to occlusions and varying sensor quality across different ranges. This paper introduces LRPD, a novel Long Range 3D Pedestrian Detection framework leveraging the complementary strengths of LiDAR and RGB data. LRPD employs a two-stage approach: first, a LiDAR-based proposal network identifies potential pedestrian locations at long ranges; second, an RGB-guided refinement module enhances these proposals by incorporating visual features, specifically addressing ambiguities caused by sparse LiDAR points. A novel attention mechanism selectively fuses LiDAR and RGB information, prioritizing LiDAR data for localization and RGB data for appearance features. Experimental results on the nuScenes dataset demonstrate that LRPD significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, particularly at long ranges (beyond 40 meters) and in scenarios with high occlusion. The proposed method improves detection accuracy by approximately 15% in challenging long-range scenarios, demonstrating its robustness and effectiveness. The LRPD framework offers a promising solution for robust 3D pedestrian detection in complex urban environments, contributing to safer autonomous navigation. Keywords: 3D pedestrian detection, LiDAR, RGB, sensor fusion, long-range detection, autonomous driving.
