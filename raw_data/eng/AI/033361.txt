This paper introduces a novel semi-supervised natural language processing (NLP) approach for fine-grained classification of medical reports. The objective is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of categorizing medical documents when limited labeled data is available. Our method combines a small set of manually labeled reports with a large corpus of unlabeled data using a self-training framework augmented with contextualized word embeddings. Specifically, we leverage pre-trained language models and fine-tune them iteratively with high-confidence pseudo-labels generated from the unlabeled data. Experimental results on a real-world medical report dataset demonstrate that our semi-supervised approach achieves significant performance gains compared to fully supervised models trained solely on labeled data, particularly in classifying reports with subtle diagnostic differences. The proposed method also outperforms traditional semi-supervised learning techniques. This research offers a practical solution for automating the classification of medical reports, reducing manual effort and potentially improving healthcare workflows. Keywords: Semi-supervised learning, natural language processing, medical reports, fine-grained classification, self-training, contextualized embeddings.
