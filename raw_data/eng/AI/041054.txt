Video Super-Resolution (VSR) aims to enhance the spatial resolution of low-resolution videos. While supervised deep learning approaches have achieved remarkable progress, their performance often degrades when applied to videos with different degradation characteristics than those seen during training. This paper introduces a novel self-supervised adaptation framework designed to mitigate this issue. Our method leverages the inherent temporal redundancy present in videos, formulating a self-supervised loss that encourages consistency between enhanced frames. Specifically, we employ a multi-frame recurrent architecture and incorporate a self-attention mechanism to exploit non-local dependencies. Furthermore, we propose a degradation-aware module that dynamically adjusts the network parameters based on estimated blur and noise levels. Experimental results on diverse datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly improves VSR performance on videos with unseen degradation patterns, outperforming existing self-supervised and unsupervised adaptation techniques. Key contributions include the self-supervised consistency loss, the degradation-aware adaptation module, and a comprehensive evaluation demonstrating robustness across varying video characteristics. Keywords: Video Super-Resolution, Self-Supervised Learning, Domain Adaptation, Deep Learning, Video Enhancement.
