Accurate object detection is a critical challenge in computer vision, with bounding box regression being a fundamental component of modern detection systems. This paper addresses the limitations of traditional bounding box regression methods, which often fail to account for prediction uncertainty, leading to suboptimal localization accuracy. We propose a novel approach that integrates uncertainty estimation directly into the bounding box regression process, enabling the model to predict both the bounding box coordinates and their associated confidence levels. Our method leverages a probabilistic framework to model uncertainty, improving robustness to ambiguous or noisy annotations. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including COCO and Pascal VOC, demonstrate that our approach significantly enhances localization precision compared to state-of-the-art methods. The results show a notable reduction in localization errors, particularly for challenging cases involving occluded or partially visible objects. Furthermore, the uncertainty estimates provide valuable insights into the model's confidence, which can be utilized for downstream tasks such as active learning or error analysis. This work contributes to advancing object detection by introducing a principled way to incorporate uncertainty, paving the way for more reliable and interpretable detection systems in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Object detection, bounding box regression, uncertainty estimation, probabilistic modeling, computer vision, localization accuracy.
