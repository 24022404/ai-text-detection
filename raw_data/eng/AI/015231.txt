This paper addresses the challenge of improving the robustness of semantic segmentation models, a crucial task in computer vision. Our objective is to enhance the accuracy and reliability of these models in real-world applications. We propose a novel approach, termed Painting-by-Numbers, which leverages a unique combination of data augmentation techniques and adversarial training to increase model robustness. Our method involves generating synthetic images with randomly painted segments, allowing the model to learn from a diverse set of scenarios and improving its ability to generalize. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly improves the performance of state-of-the-art semantic segmentation models, particularly in the presence of noise and adversarial attacks. Key findings include a notable increase in mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and a reduction in error rates. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to enhance model robustness, making it more suitable for real-world applications such as autonomous driving and medical image analysis. Our approach has significant implications for the development of more reliable and accurate semantic segmentation models, and can be applied to various computer vision tasks, including image segmentation, object detection, and scene understanding, with relevant keywords including semantic segmentation, robustness, adversarial training, and computer vision.
