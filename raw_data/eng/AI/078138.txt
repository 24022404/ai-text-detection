GeoCLR: Georeference Contrastive Learning for Efficient Seafloor Image Interpretation addresses the challenge of efficiently interpreting seafloor imagery by leveraging georeferenced data and advanced machine learning techniques. The objective of this research is to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of analyzing underwater images, which are critical for marine biology, oceanography, and environmental monitoring. We propose a novel contrastive learning model, GeoCLR, which integrates georeferenced data to improve the discernment of features in seafloor images. Our approach utilizes a specialized contrastive learning framework that aligns image features with location-specific information, enabling more precise classifications and semantic segmentations.

Throughout the study, we employed a robust dataset of georeferenced seafloor images to train and evaluate our model's performance. The results indicate that GeoCLR significantly outperforms traditional image interpretation methods, demonstrating superior accuracy in object detection and classification tasks, particularly in heterogeneous and visually complex underwater environments. Comparisons against state-of-the-art models highlight our model's efficiency and robustness, showing notable improvements in processing speed and reduced computational demands.

Concluding, the GeoCLR methodology represents a crucial advancement in the domain of marine image analysis. By integrating contrastive learning with georeferencing, this research contributes a novel paradigm that has the potential to fundamentally streamline and enhance seafloor data interpretation. Applications of this work span environmental conservation, resource management, and scientific exploration, offering a powerful tool for researchers and practitioners in marine science. Key keywords include georeference, contrastive learning, seafloor image interpretation, machine learning, and underwater exploration.
