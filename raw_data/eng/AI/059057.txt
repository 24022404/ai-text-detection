This paper introduces a novel approach for extracting non-monotonic rules from statistical learning models, leveraging high-utility itemset mining techniques. The objective is to improve the interpretability and explainability of complex models by identifying actionable rules that capture non-linear relationships between features and predictions. Our method first discretizes model inputs and then applies a high-utility itemset mining algorithm to discover relevant feature combinations. These itemsets are subsequently transformed into a set of non-monotonic rules, where the confidence of a rule can either increase or decrease with the addition of conditions. Experimental results on several benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in identifying meaningful non-monotonic relationships that are often missed by traditional rule extraction methods. The extracted rules provide valuable insights into the model's decision-making process, facilitating model debugging and refinement. This research contributes to the growing field of explainable AI by providing a practical method for understanding and interpreting complex statistical learning models.

**Keywords:** Explainable AI, Rule Extraction, Non-Monotonic Reasoning, Statistical Learning, High-Utility Itemset Mining, Model Interpretability.
