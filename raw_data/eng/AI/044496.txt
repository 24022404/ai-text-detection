Graph exploration is a fundamental task in information technology, with applications spanning network analysis, recommendation systems, and knowledge discovery. Traditional graph exploration algorithms often lack adaptability when confronted with varying graph structures or domains, limiting their efficacy in new environments. This paper proposes a novel learning-based framework for transferable graph exploration, enabling models to generalize exploration strategies across diverse graph types and tasks. Our approach leverages deep reinforcement learning augmented with graph neural networks to capture rich structural information and facilitate policy transfer. We design a suite of benchmark environments to systematically evaluate the transferability of learned exploration policies between graphs of different sizes, densities, and connectivity patterns. Experimental results demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms conventional heuristics and baseline learning algorithms in terms of exploration efficiency and adaptability, maintaining robust performance on unseen graphs. The findings suggest that the proposed transferable framework can reduce retraining and manual tuning required for each new domain, paving the way for scalable solutions in evolving network applications. Keywords: graph exploration, transfer learning, graph neural networks, reinforcement learning, adaptability, network analysis.
