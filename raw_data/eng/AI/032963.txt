This paper addresses the critical issue of adversarial attacks on time series data, a growing concern due to the increasing use of time series in various data-driven applications such as finance, health monitoring, and climate models. The research aims to explore the vulnerabilities of time series models when exposed to adversarial threats, which can lead to erroneous predictions and have significant real-world consequences.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework to systematically generate and evaluate adversarial attacks against time series models. Our approach involves designing targeted perturbations that can be applied to input data to mislead prediction models, particularly those employing machine learning algorithms such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN). The framework leverages gradient-based optimization techniques to identify minimal changes necessary to achieve desired adversarial outcomes.

Results/Findings: The experimental analysis conducted on various benchmark datasets demonstrates the vulnerability of state-of-the-art time series models to the proposed adversarial attacks. Results show that adversarial modifications, although imperceptible, significantly degrade model performance, with accuracy drops exceeding 40% in some cases. This highlights the need for more robust strategies and defenses in time series applications.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings underscore the critical need for developing more resilient time series models capable of withstanding adversarial attacks. This research contributes valuable insights by pinpointing existing model weaknesses and setting the stage for future work on adversarial defense mechanisms. By raising awareness of this issue, our work opens pathways for secure implementation of time series forecasting in sensitive domains. 

Keywords: adversarial attacks, time series, vulnerability, machine learning, LSTM, CNN, predictive modeling, data security.
