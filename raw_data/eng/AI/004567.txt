This paper introduces a novel Visibility Constrained Generative Model (VCGM) for depth-based 3D facial pose tracking, addressing the challenge of accurately estimating facial poses under varying visibility conditions. The proposed model leverages a generative framework to predict 3D facial landmarks while explicitly incorporating visibility constraints to handle occlusions and partial facial data. By integrating depth information from depth sensors, the approach ensures robust tracking even in scenarios with limited facial visibility. The VCGM employs a hybrid architecture combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to capture spatial and temporal dependencies, enhancing pose estimation accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate that the VCGM outperforms existing methods in terms of tracking precision and robustness, particularly in occlusion-heavy environments. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets, with significant improvements in mean error reduction. This research contributes to advancing real-time 3D facial tracking applications, such as augmented reality, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction, by providing a reliable solution for challenging visibility conditions.  

**Keywords**: 3D facial pose tracking, depth-based tracking, generative models, visibility constraints, occlusion handling, convolutional neural networks, recurrent neural networks.
