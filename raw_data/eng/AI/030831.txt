Goal-oriented visual dialog aims to train an agent (Answerer) to answer a series of questions about an image to fulfill a specific task defined by another agent (Questioner). This paper introduces a novel information-theoretic approach that explicitly models the Questioner's belief state, enabling the Answerer to optimize its responses based on the inferred goal. We formulate the interaction as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) and leverage concepts from information theory, specifically mutual information, to guide the Answerer's policy. The Answerer learns to maximize the information gained by the Questioner regarding the task goal, thereby improving overall task success. Experimental results on the GuessWhat?! dataset demonstrate that our proposed approach significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of task completion rate and dialog efficiency. Furthermore, we show that the learned Answerer policy exhibits improved explainability by aligning its responses with the Questioner's inferred intent. Keywords: Visual Dialog, Goal-Oriented Dialog, Information Theory, Reinforcement Learning, POMDP, Mutual Information.
