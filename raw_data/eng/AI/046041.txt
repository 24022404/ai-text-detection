Reinforcement Learning (RL) faces significant challenges in learning efficient and generalizable representations from high-dimensional observations, which are critical for decision-making in complex environments. This paper introduces Masked Contrastive Representation Learning (MCRL), a novel framework designed to enhance representation learning in RL by leveraging masked autoencoding and contrastive learning. MCRL employs a dual-objective approach: it reconstructs masked observations to capture fine-grained details while simultaneously maximizing the similarity between representations of semantically related states through contrastive loss. This combination enables the model to learn robust and task-relevant features while reducing redundancy in the learned representations. Experiments conducted on benchmark RL environments demonstrate that MCRL outperforms existing representation learning methods in terms of sample efficiency, generalization, and final task performance. The framework is particularly effective in sparse-reward and partially observable settings, where high-quality representations are crucial. The results highlight the potential of MCRL to advance RL applications in real-world scenarios, such as robotics and autonomous systems, by improving the quality of learned representations. This work contributes a scalable and versatile approach to representation learning, bridging the gap between unsupervised learning and RL.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Representation Learning, Masked Autoencoding, Contrastive Learning, Sample Efficiency, Generalization.
