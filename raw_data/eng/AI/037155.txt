Accurate differentiation between crops and weeds is crucial for effective precision agriculture, enabling targeted weed management and optimizing resource use. This paper presents a supervised learning approach for crop-weed classification leveraging color and texture features extracted from field imagery. A dataset composed of various crop and weed species was collected under real-world agricultural conditions, and a comprehensive feature extraction pipeline was developed to capture discriminative color histograms and texture descriptors from the images. Multiple supervised classifiers, including support vector machines and random forests, were trained and evaluated to assess their effectiveness in classification tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves high accuracy in distinguishing crops from weeds, with the combination of color and texture features significantly improving classification performance over single-feature approaches. This research highlights the potential of supervised machine learning for automated, image-based crop-weed recognition and offers insights into feature selection for similar vision-based agricultural applications. The proposed framework can be further integrated into real-time weed detection systems, supporting sustainable and efficient farming practices. Key keywords: supervised learning, crop-weed classification, color features, texture features, machine learning, precision agriculture, image analysis.
