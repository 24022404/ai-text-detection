Person re-identification (Re-ID) aims to match pedestrian images across different camera views, a challenging task due to variations in pose, illumination, and occlusion. This paper introduces a novel cluster loss function designed to improve the discriminative feature learning for Re-ID. Unlike conventional contrastive or triplet losses, the cluster loss encourages feature embeddings of the same identity to form tighter clusters while simultaneously maximizing the separation between different identity clusters. The proposed approach involves iteratively updating cluster centers based on the mean feature embedding of each identity and then penalizing intra-cluster variance and encouraging inter-cluster separation. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including Market-1501, DukeMTMC-reID, and CUHK03, demonstrate that the proposed cluster loss significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving improvements in both Rank-1 accuracy and mAP. Further analysis shows the learned feature embeddings exhibit enhanced robustness to intra-class variations. The cluster loss provides a simple yet effective means of learning discriminative features for person re-identification, with potential applications in video surveillance and intelligent transportation systems. Keywords: person re-identification, cluster loss, deep learning, feature embedding, metric learning.
