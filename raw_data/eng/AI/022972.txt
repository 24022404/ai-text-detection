Probabilistic integration, leveraging random sampling to estimate integrals, has been employed across various scientific domains. This paper investigates the efficacy and applicability of probabilistic integration methods within the context of statistical computation. We explore the theoretical underpinnings of Monte Carlo integration and Quasi-Monte Carlo methods, contrasting their convergence properties and computational costs compared to traditional deterministic quadrature techniques. Our analysis focuses on high-dimensional integration problems prevalent in Bayesian inference and statistical modeling, where deterministic methods often become intractable. We present empirical results demonstrating the performance of different probabilistic integration algorithms on benchmark statistical problems, including approximating marginal likelihoods and posterior expectations. Furthermore, we examine the impact of variance reduction techniques on the accuracy and efficiency of probabilistic integration. Our findings suggest that probabilistic integration offers a viable and, in some cases, superior alternative to deterministic methods for complex statistical computations, especially in high-dimensional settings. We conclude by discussing the potential for future research and the broader implications of probabilistic integration for statistical practice.

**Keywords:** Probabilistic Integration, Monte Carlo, Quasi-Monte Carlo, Statistical Computation, Bayesian Inference, High-Dimensional Integration.
