This paper introduces a novel approach to adversarial imitation learning by leveraging Wasserstein distances. Traditional imitation learning techniques often struggle with mode collapse and training difficulties, posing challenges in learning complex behaviors from demonstrations. This research addresses these limitations through the development of Wasserstein Adversarial Imitation Learning (WAIL), which aims to enhance stability and efficiency.

Methods/Approach: The proposed method employs a Wasserstein distance framework within the realm of generative adversarial networks (GANs) to improve the imitation learning process. By integrating the Wasserstein metric, WAIL attempts to provide a more robust measure of similarity between the policy being learned and the expert demonstrations, thus alleviating the common issues associated with adversarial approaches. The architecture consists of a discriminator and a generator where the discriminator is trained to distinguish between the expert and the generated policies, guided by the Wasserstein metric.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that WAIL significantly enhances the convergence speed and robustness of the imitation learning process compared to existing methods. The performance of WAIL was evaluated on various benchmark tasks, where it exhibited superior capability in capturing complex behaviors with reduced training instability. This approach outperformed traditional adversarial imitation learning frameworks in terms of efficiency and policy accuracy.

Conclusion/Implications: WAIL introduces a promising avenue for imitation learning by effectively mitigating mode collapse and training instabilities through the application of Wasserstein distances. The research presents compelling evidence that this method can be applied to a range of tasks requiring the replication of intricate behaviors, thereby broadening the scope and application of imitation learning in fields such as robotics, game playing, and autonomous systems. Future work may explore extending this method to non-stationary environments and scaling to higher-dimensional spaces. 

Keywords: Wasserstein, adversarial imitation learning, mode collapse, generative adversarial networks, policy learning, automation, robotics.
