The growing demand for efficient video understanding systems has led to significant advancements in deep learning techniques. While traditional approaches often rely on complex architectures tailored for temporal data, this paper explores the hypothesis that an image classifier can suffice for video understanding tasks. We propose a novel framework that leverages pre-trained image classification models, augmented with lightweight temporal aggregation mechanisms, to process video data effectively. Our approach eliminates the need for computationally expensive video-specific architectures, reducing both training time and resource requirements. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves competitive performance in action recognition and video classification tasks, often matching or exceeding state-of-the-art video models. The results highlight the potential of leveraging spatial feature extraction capabilities of image classifiers, combined with minimal temporal modeling, to address video understanding challenges. This work contributes to the field by offering a simpler, more efficient alternative to traditional video analysis methods, with implications for real-time applications and resource-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: Video understanding, image classification, temporal aggregation, deep learning, action recognition.
