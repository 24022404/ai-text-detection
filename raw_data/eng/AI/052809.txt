In the era of big data, the challenge of learning from small datasets remains a critical issue, particularly in domains where data collection is expensive, time-consuming, or ethically constrained. This paper addresses the problem of small sample learning (SSL) and proposes a novel framework to enhance model performance under limited data conditions. Our approach integrates transfer learning, data augmentation, and meta-learning techniques to effectively leverage prior knowledge and generate synthetic data, thereby improving generalization capabilities. We evaluate the proposed framework on multiple benchmark datasets, comparing it against state-of-the-art SSL methods. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with extreme data scarcity. The findings highlight the potential of our approach to bridge the gap between small sample learning and big data applications, offering practical solutions for real-world problems in healthcare, finance, and other data-sensitive domains. This research contributes to advancing SSL methodologies, providing a scalable and efficient framework for learning in data-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: small sample learning, big data, transfer learning, data augmentation, meta-learning, generalization.
