This paper investigates a discrete-time mean field control (MFC) problem where the environment's state influences the dynamics of a large population of agents. We formulate a novel framework where individual agents interact not only through the mean field distribution of agent states but also through a dynamically evolving environment state. This environment state, representing factors external to individual agents, impacts both the agent dynamics and the cost function. We derive the mean field equilibrium conditions, resulting in a system of coupled equations that characterize the optimal control strategy and the evolution of the environment state. Furthermore, we develop a policy iteration algorithm to approximate the solution of this MFC problem. The effectiveness of the proposed framework and algorithm is demonstrated through numerical experiments on a resource allocation problem, showing improved performance compared to traditional MFC approaches that neglect environment states. Key words: Mean Field Control, Discrete-Time Systems, Environment States, Policy Iteration, Large Population Games.
