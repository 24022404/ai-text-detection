Structured Gaussian process (GP) models are widely used for modeling complex, high-dimensional data in various applications, such as spatiotemporal analysis and machine learning. However, traditional GP inference methods often struggle with scalability and computational efficiency, particularly when dealing with structured data. This paper introduces a novel gray-box inference approach that combines the interpretability of white-box models with the flexibility of black-box methods to address these challenges. Our approach leverages domain-specific knowledge to impose structure on the GP prior, enabling efficient inference while maintaining model accuracy. We propose a hybrid framework that integrates structured priors with scalable approximation techniques, such as sparse variational inference, to achieve significant computational savings without sacrificing predictive performance. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing GP inference techniques in terms of both computational efficiency and predictive accuracy. The proposed approach not only advances the state-of-the-art in structured GP modeling but also provides a practical solution for large-scale applications in areas such as environmental monitoring and time-series forecasting. This work highlights the potential of gray-box methods to bridge the gap between interpretability and scalability in probabilistic modeling.  

**Keywords**: Gaussian process, gray-box inference, structured models, scalable inference, spatiotemporal analysis, probabilistic modeling.
