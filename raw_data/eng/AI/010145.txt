Generalized Linear Models (GLMs) are widely used in machine learning and statistics for modeling relationships between variables. However, their performance in high-dimensional settings, where the number of features exceeds the number of observations, remains a critical challenge. This paper investigates the generalization error of GLMs in such high-dimensional regimes, focusing on the interplay between model complexity, data dimensionality, and sample size. We propose a theoretical framework to analyze the generalization bounds of GLMs under sparsity assumptions, leveraging tools from high-dimensional statistics and optimization theory. Our approach incorporates regularization techniques, such as L1 and L2 penalties, to control overfitting and improve model interpretability. Through extensive simulations and empirical evaluations, we demonstrate that our framework provides tighter generalization bounds compared to existing methods, particularly in scenarios with limited data and high feature correlations. The results highlight the importance of regularization and sparsity in achieving robust performance for GLMs in high dimensions. This work contributes to the understanding of generalization in high-dimensional learning and offers practical insights for improving the reliability of GLMs in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Generalized Linear Models, high-dimensional statistics, generalization error, regularization, sparsity, machine learning.
