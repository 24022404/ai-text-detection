**Abstract**

Incremental learning plays a critical role in developing adaptable artificial intelligence systems, enabling models to incorporate new knowledge over time without retraining from scratch or encountering significant performance degradation. This paper introduces DeeSIL (Deep-Shallow Incremental Learning), a novel framework that effectively integrates deep feature extraction with shallow classifiers to address the challenges of incremental learning in large-scale classification tasks. The proposed approach utilizes a pre-trained deep convolutional neural network (CNN) as a consistent feature extractor, while training lightweight shallow classifiers incrementally as new classes are introduced. This hybrid architecture allows DeeSIL to maintain high accuracy and memory efficiency, minimizing catastrophic forgetting without requiring exhaustive retraining or extensive storage of previous data. Experimental evaluations on benchmark image datasets demonstrate that DeeSIL achieves competitive performance compared to existing state-of-the-art incremental learning methods, exhibiting superior scalability and faster adaptation to new tasks. The findings indicate that combining deep representations with flexible shallow classifiers offers a practical solution for incremental learning scenarios, especially where computational resources and data storage are constrained. Potential applications of DeeSIL include dynamic recognition systems, continual learning platforms, and adaptive computer vision solutions. Key keywords: incremental learning, deep learning, shallow classifiers, catastrophic forgetting, continual learning, computer vision.
