This paper explores the vulnerability of Electroencephalography (EEG)-based biometric authentication systems to adversarial attacks. We investigate the use of adversarial deep learning techniques to generate minimally perturbed EEG signals that can successfully impersonate legitimate users. Our approach employs a novel gradient-based optimization method to craft adversarial examples, focusing on preserving the physiological plausibility of the generated EEG signals. We evaluate the effectiveness of our attack on a state-of-the-art deep learning model for EEG biometric identification, demonstrating a significant reduction in authentication accuracy. The results reveal that even subtle, carefully crafted perturbations can compromise the security of these systems. Furthermore, we propose and evaluate several defense strategies, including adversarial training and input sanitization methods, to enhance the robustness of EEG biometric systems against adversarial attacks. Our findings highlight the importance of considering adversarial threats in the design and deployment of EEG-based authentication and contribute to the development of more secure and reliable biometric systems. Keywords: EEG biometrics, adversarial attacks, deep learning, security, authentication, adversarial training.
