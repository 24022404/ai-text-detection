Machine learning models are increasingly used in high-stakes decision-making processes, yet the opacity of many "black box" models raises concerns about fairness, accountability, and trust. This paper argues against relying on post-hoc explainability methods for inherently uninterpretable models in such critical applications. Instead, we advocate for the adoption of intrinsically interpretable models, which offer transparency by design. We explore the limitations of explainability techniques, demonstrating how they can be misleading or unreliable. Through comparative analysis, we show that intrinsically interpretable models, such as rule-based systems and generalized additive models, can achieve comparable or even superior performance to black box models in several high-stakes domains while providing inherent understanding of their decision-making process. Our findings suggest that prioritizing inherently interpretable models is a more robust and ethical approach for ensuring responsible and trustworthy AI in sensitive applications. Keywords: interpretable machine learning, explainable AI, black box models, high-stakes decisions, transparency, accountability.
