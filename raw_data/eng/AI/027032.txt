Graph structured data is increasingly prevalent across diverse domains, yet its vulnerability to adversarial attacks remains a significant concern. This paper investigates novel strategies for crafting adversarial attacks against graph-based machine learning models. We propose a perturbation method, termed Graph Injection Attack (GIA), which strategically injects malicious nodes and edges into the graph structure to degrade model performance. GIA focuses on maximizing the impact on node classification accuracy while maintaining stealth by adhering to structural similarity constraints. We evaluate GIA on several benchmark datasets and graph neural network architectures, demonstrating its superior attack success rate compared to existing state-of-the-art adversarial methods, with an average decrease of 20% in target model accuracy. Furthermore, we analyze the transferability of GIA-generated attacks across different graph models, revealing vulnerabilities that can be exploited for broader security implications. Our findings highlight the critical need for robust defense mechanisms against adversarial manipulation in graph-based learning systems.

Keywords: Adversarial Attack, Graph Neural Networks, Graph Security, Data Poisoning, Node Classification.
