Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved remarkable success in various domains, but its reliance on extensive trial-and-error and complex reward shaping often limits its scalability. This paper explores Evolution Strategies (ES) as a viable, scalable alternative to RL for solving challenging control tasks. We investigate the application of ES, a black-box optimization technique, to train agents in high-dimensional environments. Our approach leverages a population-based search to directly optimize the agent's policy parameters, eliminating the need for explicit value function estimation. We demonstrate that ES can achieve comparable or superior performance to RL algorithms on several benchmark control tasks, while exhibiting significantly better parallelization capabilities and reduced hyperparameter sensitivity. Furthermore, we analyze the sample efficiency and robustness of ES compared to RL, highlighting its potential for deployment in resource-constrained settings. This work contributes to a growing body of evidence suggesting that ES offers a competitive and scalable approach for training intelligent agents, particularly in scenarios where RL faces practical limitations. Keywords: Evolution Strategies, Reinforcement Learning, Scalability, Optimization, Control Tasks, Black-box Optimization, Parallel Computing.
