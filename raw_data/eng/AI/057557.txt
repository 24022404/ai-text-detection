This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the training efficiency and performance of deep generative models by integrating curriculum learning with clustering techniques. The focus is on systematically organizing and presenting training samples to the model, leveraging the structured approach inherent in curriculum learning.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel methodology that combines curriculum learning with a clustering algorithm to improve training processes in deep generative models. The approach involves clustering data into progressively complex subsets and using these clusters to guide the curriculum learning path. This sequential training allows the model to start with simpler data and gradually deal with more complex structures as it evolves.

Results/Findings: Our empirical analysis demonstrates significant improvements in both convergence speed and the quality of generated outputs when utilizing our proposed curriculum-guided clustering method. Compared to traditional training techniques, our approach achieved higher performance metrics, illustrating its effectiveness in refining model training. We conducted extensive experiments across various datasets and model architectures, consistently observing enhancements in generative fidelity and stability.

Conclusion/Implications: The fusion of curriculum learning with clustering introduces an innovative paradigm for training deep generative models, offering both theoretical and practical advancements. This method not only optimizes model training but can also be adapted to other machine learning frameworks, providing a versatile tool for future research. The implications of these findings suggest potential applications in areas such as image synthesis, natural language generation, and beyond, where efficient model training is paramount.

Keywords: curriculum learning, deep generative models, clustering, model training, convergence speed, generative fidelity.
