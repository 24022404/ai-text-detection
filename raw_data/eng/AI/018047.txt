The study aims to address the challenge of learning interpretable discrete representations from complex time series data, which is crucial for applications requiring not only accuracy but also transparency in decision-making processes.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework named SOM-VAE, which integrates the Self-Organizing Map (SOM) with the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to achieve interpretable representation learning. The SOM component is utilized for mapping continuous data into meaningful discrete categories, enhancing interpretability, while the VAE is employed for capturing the intricate temporal dependencies inherent in time series data. This synergy facilitates the transformation of raw data into discrete, human-understandable units without sacrificing modeling performance.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations on benchmark time series datasets demonstrate that SOM-VAE achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art methods while providing more interpretable results. Our approach outperforms existing models in tasks involving classification, clustering, and anomaly detection, and offers significant improvements in model interpretability. The discrete representations developed through our method facilitate clearer insights into underlying data patterns and relationships.

Conclusion/Implications: SOM-VAE presents a significant advancement in the field of time series analysis by offering a method that balances accuracy and interpretability. The framework's ability to generate discrete, interpretable representations opens up new possibilities for applications in domains such as healthcare, finance, and IoT, where understanding model outputs is as crucial as the predictions themselves. This work sets a foundation for future research into interpretable machine learning frameworks capable of handling complex sequential data.

Keywords: SOM, VAE, time series, discrete representation, interpretable machine learning, anomaly detection, classification, clustering.
