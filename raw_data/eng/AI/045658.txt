Object separation is a critical process in image analysis, computer vision, and data segmentation, often challenged by overlapping features and complex backgrounds. This paper presents a semi-automated statistical algorithm designed to enhance object separation efficiency and accuracy in diverse datasets. The proposed method utilizes statistical analysis of pixel distributions and feature clustering, enabling preliminary object segmentation with minimal user intervention. Users provide initial input to guide the algorithm, which then employs adaptive thresholding and unsupervised clustering to distinguish and isolate objects from their surroundings. Comparative evaluations on benchmark image datasets demonstrate that our algorithm outperforms conventional fully-automated approaches in terms of precision, robustness to noise, and adaptability to heterogeneous data. The results indicate substantial reductions in manual post-processing while maintaining high separation accuracy. This semi-automated approach bridges the gap between manual and fully-automated systems, offering flexibility and improved performance in scenarios where complete automation is impractical. The proposed framework holds significant potential for applications in medical imaging, remote sensing, and industrial inspection, where reliable and efficient object separation is essential. Key keywords: object separation, statistical algorithm, semi-automated processing, image segmentation, clustering, adaptive thresholding.
