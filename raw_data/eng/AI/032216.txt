Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) have become a cornerstone of embedded automation systems, enabling real-time decision-making in resource-constrained environments. However, the deployment of DNNs on embedded devices often faces challenges related to computational efficiency and stability during training. This paper introduces Mean Spectral Normalization (MSN), a novel technique designed to enhance the training stability and generalization performance of DNNs for embedded automation. The proposed method normalizes the spectral properties of weight matrices by computing their mean spectral norms, ensuring controlled gradient flow and improved convergence. We evaluate MSN on benchmark datasets and embedded automation tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in reducing training instability and enhancing model robustness. Experimental results show that MSN outperforms traditional normalization techniques, achieving higher accuracy and faster convergence while maintaining computational efficiency. The proposed approach is particularly suited for embedded systems, where resource constraints demand lightweight yet reliable models. This research contributes to the advancement of DNN deployment in automation, offering a practical solution for improving model performance in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Deep Neural Networks, Spectral Normalization, Embedded Automation, Training Stability, Computational Efficiency, Model Robustness.
