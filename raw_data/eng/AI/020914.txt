Understanding human-object interaction is crucial for developing systems capable of perceiving and predicting human behaviors in first-person video scenarios. This research aims to address the challenge of forecasting human-object interactions by jointly predicting motor attention and subsequent actions.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework that leverages state-of-the-art deep learning techniques to simultaneously forecast motor attention and actions. Our approach integrates a temporal convolutional network with attention mechanisms to predict the interaction dynamics from first-person video footage. We utilize a large-scale dataset of first-person videos to train and evaluate our model, ensuring it captures complex, real-world interactions.

Results/Findings: The proposed model demonstrates significant improvements in both accuracy and computational efficiency when compared with existing methods. Our model successfully predicts motor attention and actions in a wide range of scenarios, yielding a prediction accuracy increase of 15% over baseline approaches. Additionally, qualitative assessments show that our model provides more coherent and contextually relevant predictions.

Conclusion/Implications: The joint prediction of motor attention and actions in first-person videos holds significant potential for applications in augmented reality, human-computer interaction, and autonomous systems. Our research provides a foundation for developing more intuitive systems capable of understanding and predicting human behaviors in real-time. Future work will focus on extending the model to accommodate multi-person interactions and further improving prediction robustness.

Keywords: human-object interaction, first-person video, motor attention, action prediction, deep learning, temporal convolutional network, augmented reality, autonomous systems.
