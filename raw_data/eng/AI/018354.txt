Transfer learning has become a cornerstone in modern machine learning, enabling the adaptation of pre-trained models to new tasks with limited data. However, achieving efficient and effective transfer learning remains a challenge, particularly in scenarios with scarce labeled data. This paper introduces **SMILE (Self-Distilled MIxup for Efficient Transfer LEarning)**, a novel framework that combines self-distillation and mixup augmentation to enhance transfer learning performance. The proposed approach leverages self-distillation to refine the knowledge transfer process, ensuring that the model retains critical information from the source domain while adapting to the target domain. Additionally, mixup augmentation is employed to generate synthetic training samples, improving generalization and robustness. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that SMILE outperforms existing transfer learning methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The framework is particularly effective in low-data regimes, making it suitable for real-world applications where labeled data is limited. Key contributions include a new self-distillation mechanism tailored for transfer learning and a systematic integration of mixup to enhance model adaptability. SMILE offers a scalable and resource-efficient solution for transfer learning, with potential applications in domains such as computer vision, natural language processing, and healthcare.  

**Keywords**: Transfer learning, self-distillation, mixup augmentation, low-data regimes, model efficiency, generalization.
