This paper presents a novel approach to articulated shape matching by leveraging Laplacian eigenfunctions combined with unsupervised point registration. The primary objective is to address the challenge of matching complex shapes that undergo significant articulation, which is a common problem in computer vision and shape analysis. We propose a method that utilizes the intrinsic properties of Laplacian eigenfunctions to efficiently capture and represent the geometric and topological characteristics of shapes. Our approach incorporates an unsupervised point registration algorithm that aligns the referenced and target shapes without requiring pre-labeled correspondences or prior knowledge. Comparative analysis demonstrates that our method delivers superior performance in handling non-rigid deformations and varying articulations compared to existing state-of-the-art techniques. The results show significant improvements in accuracy and computational efficiency across a range of benchmark datasets. Our findings suggest that the integration of Laplacian eigenfunctions with unsupervised registration offers a robust solution for articulated shape matching, with potential applications in areas such as animation, medical imaging, and augmented reality. This study contributes to the development of more effective shape analysis tools, emphasizing the importance of intrinsic geometric properties in shape correspondence tasks. Keywords: articulated shape matching, Laplacian eigenfunctions, unsupervised point registration, non-rigid deformation, computer vision.
