LiDAR-based semantic labeling is crucial for autonomous navigation and environmental perception. However, neural network models trained on one LiDAR sensor often exhibit performance degradation when applied to data from different sensors due to variations in point cloud density and sensor characteristics. This paper investigates the cross-sensor portability of several prominent neural network architectures for LiDAR semantic segmentation. We evaluate the performance of PointNet, PointNet++, and DGCNN when trained on data from a high-density LiDAR and tested on data from a lower-density LiDAR, and vice-versa. Our results indicate a significant drop in accuracy for all architectures when transferring between sensors. We further analyze the impact of point cloud density and propose a fine-tuning strategy using a small amount of target sensor data to mitigate the performance gap. The fine-tuning approach demonstrates substantial improvements in cross-sensor portability, achieving up to a 15% increase in Intersection over Union (IoU) on the target sensor dataset. These findings highlight the challenges of cross-sensor generalization in LiDAR-based perception and underscore the importance of domain adaptation techniques for robust deployment. Keywords: LiDAR, Semantic Segmentation, Neural Networks, Cross-Sensor Portability, Domain Adaptation, Point Cloud.
