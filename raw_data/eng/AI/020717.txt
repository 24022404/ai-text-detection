This paper addresses the challenge of capturing long-range dependencies in deep neural networks, a limitation present in traditional convolutional and recurrent architectures primarily focused on local information. We propose a novel non-local neural network framework that introduces non-local operations, allowing each output position to aggregate features from all positions in the input, regardless of their spatial or temporal distance. Our approach is formulated as a generic, end-to-end trainable module that can be seamlessly integrated into existing architectures. To evaluate its effectiveness, we implement our non-local blocks in several computer vision tasks, including video classification and object detection. Experimental results demonstrate that models augmented with non-local operations consistently outperform their baselines, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy with minimal increase in computational cost. The findings indicate that explicitly modeling global context leads to richer feature representations and improved performance in complex tasks involving scenes with intricate and long-range relationships. This work highlights the potential of non-local neural networks as a key advancement in deep learning, with applications in video analysis, image recognition, and sequence modeling. Keywords: non-local neural networks, deep learning, global context, feature aggregation, video classification, object detection.
