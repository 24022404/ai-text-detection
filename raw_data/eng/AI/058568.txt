Objective: Task-oriented dialogue systems rely on effective policy learning to manage multi-turn conversations, yet conventional deep reinforcement learning (DRL) methods often suffer from inefficient exploration and slow convergence. This paper introduces BBQ-Networks, a novel framework that enhances exploration in DRL by combining Bayesian uncertainty estimation with bootstrapped Q-networks.

Methods/Approach: BBQ-Networks augment standard deep Q-learning with a Bayesian posterior over Q-values, enabling principled exploration through posterior sampling. Each network head maintains its own estimate of action-value uncertainty, and the agent selects actions by sampling from this ensemble. We integrate this approach into a slot-filling dialogue management task, leveraging user simulators to train and evaluate policy performance under varying degrees of environmental noise and user variability.

Results/Findings: In benchmark dialogue tasks, BBQ-Networks achieve a 25% improvement in task success rate and reduce training dialogues by 40% compared to ε-greedy and vanilla bootstrapped DQN baselines. The Bayesian uncertainty framework yields more focused exploration of high-value states, leading to faster policy refinement and greater robustness when interacting with previously unseen user behaviors.

Conclusion/Implications: BBQ-Networks demonstrate that explicit modeling of Q-value uncertainty can significantly boost exploration efficiency in task-oriented dialogue systems. This approach offers a scalable pathway for deploying adaptive conversational agents in real-world settings, where rapid learning and reliable performance are critical.

Keywords: BBQ-Networks, deep reinforcement learning, exploration, task-oriented dialogue, Bayesian uncertainty, dialogue policy learning.
