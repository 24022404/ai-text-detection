Time series forecasting is a critical task in various domains, including finance, climate modeling, and resource management. However, accurately capturing long-term trends in time series data remains a significant challenge due to noise, non-stationarity, and complex dependencies. This paper proposes a novel approach leveraging Self-Organizing Maps (SOMs) to extract and forecast long-term trends in time series data. Unlike traditional methods that rely on linear models or deep learning architectures, SOMs provide a robust, unsupervised framework for identifying underlying patterns and reducing dimensionality. The proposed method involves preprocessing the time series data, training the SOM to map high-dimensional data into a low-dimensional representation, and using the resulting topology to forecast future trends. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the SOM-based approach outperforms conventional methods in terms of trend accuracy and computational efficiency. The findings highlight the potential of SOMs as a powerful tool for long-term forecasting, particularly in scenarios where interpretability and scalability are essential. This research contributes to the field by introducing a novel application of SOMs in time series analysis, offering a scalable and interpretable alternative to existing forecasting techniques.  

**Keywords**: Time Series Forecasting, Self-Organizing Maps, Long-Term Trends, Unsupervised Learning, Dimensionality Reduction.
