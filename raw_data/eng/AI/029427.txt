This paper addresses the challenging task of traffic forecasting using the Traffic4cast 2020 dataset. We propose a novel Graph Ensemble Net (GENet) architecture that leverages graph neural networks to model spatial dependencies between road segments and temporal dependencies within time series data. Our approach emphasizes the critical role of feature engineering and loss function design in achieving accurate and robust traffic prediction. Specifically, we explore various feature representations, including historical traffic flow, speed, and external factors like time of day and weather conditions. Furthermore, we investigate the impact of different loss functions, such as Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Huber loss, on model performance and stability. Experimental results demonstrate that GENet, combined with optimized feature engineering and a carefully selected loss function, achieves state-of-the-art performance on the Traffic4cast 2020 benchmark. The findings highlight the importance of holistic design considerations for traffic prediction models, paving the way for more accurate and reliable intelligent transportation systems. Keywords: Traffic forecasting, graph neural networks, ensemble learning, feature engineering, loss function, intelligent transportation systems.
