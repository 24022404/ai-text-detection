This research investigates the application of persistent homology, a tool from topological data analysis, to characterize medical images, specifically focusing on lung and brain cancers. The goal is to enhance the understanding of medical images by extracting topological features that can potentially improve diagnostic and prognostic processes.

Methods/Approach: We employ persistent homology to analyze medical imaging data, capturing topological features that describe the shape and structure inherent in lung and brain cancer images. Our approach involves computing persistence diagrams to represent these features systematically. By utilizing machine learning techniques, we assess the efficacy of these features in distinguishing between cancerous and non-cancerous tissues and in identifying specific types of cancer.

Results/Findings: The study demonstrates that persistent homology features provide significant insights that are complementary to traditional image analysis. The topological features extracted show a promising potential in improving classification accuracy over traditional methods. Notably, persistent homology reveals subtle structural differences that might be overlooked by conventional imaging techniques. Comparative analysis indicates that these features enhance the distinction between cancer types within the lung and brain datasets.

Conclusion/Implications: Our findings suggest that topological features derived from persistent homology can serve as powerful descriptors for medical image characterization, potentially revolutionizing cancer diagnostics. This research contribution lies in introducing a novel application of topological data analysis to the medical imaging domain, with implications for more precise disease classification and tailored treatment approaches. Future work will explore the integration of these topological features with other modalities and their utilization in real-time diagnostic systems.

Keywords: Persistent Homology, Topological Features, Medical Images, Lung Cancer, Brain Cancer, Topological Data Analysis, Machine Learning, Image Classification.
