This paper presents a novel variational approach for learning Markov processes from time series data, addressing the challenge of modeling complex temporal dynamics. Our objective is to develop a flexible and scalable framework for inferring Markov processes from observed data, enabling accurate prediction and analysis of stochastic systems. We propose a variational inference method that leverages a probabilistic formulation to learn the transition probabilities and stationary distribution of the Markov process. Our approach utilizes a deep neural network to approximate the intractable posterior distribution, allowing for efficient learning from large datasets. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method in learning Markov processes from various time series data, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The proposed approach has significant implications for applications such as time series forecasting, anomaly detection, and stochastic modeling, and contributes to the development of more accurate and efficient methods for learning complex temporal dynamics. Key keywords: Markov processes, variational inference, time series data, deep learning, stochastic modeling.
