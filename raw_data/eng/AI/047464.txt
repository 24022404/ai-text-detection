Algorithm accuracy accountability and auditability are crucial for responsible deployment, yet quantifying uncertainty in complex models remains challenging. This paper introduces Dirichlet Uncertainty Wrappers (DUW), a novel approach to provide actionable insights into model confidence. DUW calibrates pre-trained classifiers by mapping their outputs to a Dirichlet distribution, enabling a principled estimation of prediction uncertainty without retraining or modifying the original model architecture. Our method offers a readily applicable framework for flagging potentially incorrect predictions, thereby facilitating human-in-the-loop decision-making and enabling targeted audits. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that DUW consistently improves uncertainty calibration compared to existing post-hoc techniques. Further, we show how DUW can be used to identify data slices where the model exhibits low confidence, highlighting areas for targeted data collection or model refinement. This work contributes a practical, computationally efficient tool for enhancing the trustworthiness and transparency of machine learning systems, promoting responsible AI development and deployment.
Keywords: Uncertainty estimation, Dirichlet distribution, model calibration, accountability, auditability, machine learning.
