This paper addresses the challenge of determining the optimal number of states in multi-state autoregressive models, a crucial aspect of time series analysis. Our objective is to develop a data-driven approach that automatically learns the number of states from the data, eliminating the need for manual specification. We propose a novel method that leverages Bayesian information criteria and cross-validation techniques to identify the optimal model complexity. Our approach is based on a probabilistic framework that integrates model selection and parameter estimation, allowing for efficient exploration of the model space. The results show that our method outperforms traditional techniques in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly in cases where the true number of states is unknown. The key findings of this study highlight the importance of adaptive model selection in multi-state autoregressive models, with significant implications for applications in finance, economics, and signal processing. Our contributions include a new algorithm for data-driven learning of the number of states, which can be used to improve the performance of multi-state autoregressive models in a wide range of fields, including time series forecasting, anomaly detection, and system identification. Key keywords: multi-state autoregressive models, model selection, Bayesian information criteria, cross-validation, time series analysis.
