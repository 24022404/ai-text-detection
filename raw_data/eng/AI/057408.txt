Sparse phase retrieval is a critical problem in signal processing and imaging, aiming to recover sparse signals from magnitude-only measurements. This paper introduces a novel Stochastic Alternating Minimizing (SAM) method to address the challenges of sparse phase retrieval efficiently. The proposed approach leverages a stochastic optimization framework to iteratively minimize a non-convex objective function, alternating between updating the sparse signal and refining the phase estimates. By incorporating stochastic gradient updates, SAM significantly reduces computational complexity while maintaining robust convergence properties. Experimental results demonstrate that SAM outperforms existing methods in terms of both reconstruction accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly for high-dimensional and noisy datasets. The method’s ability to handle large-scale problems makes it a promising tool for applications in computational imaging, microscopy, and machine learning. This work contributes a scalable and effective solution to sparse phase retrieval, offering new insights into the interplay between stochastic optimization and non-convex problems.  

**Keywords**: sparse phase retrieval, stochastic optimization, alternating minimization, non-convex optimization, signal reconstruction.
