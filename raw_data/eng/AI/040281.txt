This paper investigates the robustness of Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures when subjected to adversarial examples, a critical concern in deploying deep learning models for computer vision tasks. The primary objective is to evaluate the vulnerability of ViTs compared to conventional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) under various adversarial attack scenarios. We employ a suite of adversarial attack methods—including Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM), Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), and AutoAttack—to systematically assess the resilience of several ViT variants across standard image classification benchmarks. A comprehensive experimental framework is implemented, incorporating both white-box and black-box attack settings, to measure classification accuracy, perturbation sensitivity, and transferability of adversarial examples. Our findings reveal that while ViTs demonstrate improved robustness against certain low-intensity perturbations, they also exhibit unique failure modes distinct from CNNs, particularly under high-intensity or transfer-based attacks. Furthermore, we explore the impact of adversarial training and input preprocessing as mitigation strategies, highlighting their effectiveness in enhancing ViT robustness. These results provide key insights into the security profile of transformer-based vision models and underscore the necessity for specialized defense mechanisms tailored to their unique architecture. Our work contributes to advancing the understanding of adversarial robustness in vision models and informs the design of more secure AI systems. Keywords: Vision Transformers, adversarial examples, robustness, deep learning, computer vision, adversarial attacks, security.
