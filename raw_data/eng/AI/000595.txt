Single-view depth estimation is a critical challenge in computer vision, with applications ranging from autonomous driving to augmented reality. This paper introduces CAM-Convs (Camera-Aware Multi-Scale Convolutions), a novel convolutional neural network architecture designed to improve depth estimation by explicitly incorporating camera parameters into the learning process. Unlike traditional methods that treat depth estimation as a purely image-based task, CAM-Convs leverage camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters to adaptively adjust feature extraction at multiple scales, enhancing the model's ability to generalize across diverse camera setups. The proposed approach integrates camera-aware mechanisms into convolutional layers, enabling the network to dynamically refine depth predictions based on the specific characteristics of the input camera. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that CAM-Convs achieve state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in both accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the importance of camera-aware modeling in single-view depth estimation and suggest promising applications in real-world scenarios where camera configurations vary significantly. This work contributes a scalable and adaptable framework for depth estimation, advancing the field by bridging the gap between geometric constraints and deep learning-based approaches.  

**Keywords**: Single-view depth estimation, camera-aware convolutions, multi-scale feature extraction, convolutional neural networks, 3D reconstruction.
