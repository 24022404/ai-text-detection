Domain generalization and adaptation are critical challenges in machine learning, particularly when models trained on one domain must perform well on unseen or shifted target domains. This paper addresses these challenges by proposing a novel framework for feature alignment and restoration, designed to enhance model robustness across diverse domains. The proposed approach leverages a dual-stream architecture that simultaneously aligns feature distributions across domains and restores domain-invariant features, ensuring better generalization and adaptation capabilities. We introduce a feature alignment module that minimizes domain discrepancies through adversarial training, coupled with a restoration module that reconstructs essential features to preserve task-relevant information. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, achieving state-of-the-art performance in both domain generalization and adaptation tasks. The results highlight significant improvements in cross-domain accuracy and robustness compared to existing methods. This work contributes a scalable and efficient solution for real-world applications where domain shifts are prevalent, such as autonomous driving, medical imaging, and natural language processing. Key innovations include the integration of feature alignment and restoration, which jointly address domain shift challenges while maintaining model performance. Keywords: domain generalization, domain adaptation, feature alignment, feature restoration, adversarial training, cross-domain learning.
