This paper presents a robust method for image stitching, addressing the challenge of seamlessly combining multiple images into a single, cohesive panorama. The objective is to develop an approach that can accurately align and merge images despite variations in lighting, perspective, and overlap. Our method employs a novel combination of feature extraction and matching techniques, leveraging the strengths of both traditional computer vision and deep learning-based approaches. The results demonstrate significant improvements in stitching accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with low overlap or significant distortion. Key findings include the ability to handle large rotations, translations, and scaling factors, as well as the effective suppression of ghosting and seam artifacts. The proposed method has important implications for applications such as virtual reality, surveillance, and autonomous vehicles, where high-quality image stitching is critical. By introducing a robust and efficient image stitching algorithm, this research contributes to the advancement of computer vision and image processing technologies, with potential applications in fields such as photography, cinematography, and remote sensing. Keywords: image stitching, computer vision, deep learning, feature extraction, panorama reconstruction.
