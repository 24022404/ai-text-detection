Optical motion capture systems are widely used for tracking human movement, but the process of manually labeling markers is time-consuming and prone to errors. This paper addresses the challenge of automating marker labeling in optical motion capture systems through permutation learning. We propose a novel approach that leverages machine learning to predict the correct permutation of marker labels based on their spatial and temporal relationships. Our method employs a deep neural network architecture trained on motion capture data to learn the optimal mapping between unlabeled marker positions and their corresponding labels. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves high accuracy in marker labeling, significantly reducing the need for manual intervention. Compared to existing methods, our solution offers improved robustness to noise and occlusions, making it suitable for real-world applications. The findings highlight the potential of permutation learning to streamline motion capture workflows, with implications for fields such as animation, biomechanics, and robotics. Key contributions include a scalable and efficient labeling framework, as well as insights into the challenges of handling complex marker configurations. This research advances the state of the art in automated motion capture by introducing a data-driven solution that enhances both accuracy and usability.  

**Keywords**: motion capture, marker labeling, permutation learning, deep learning, automation, biomechanics, animation.
