This paper investigates the phenomenon of benign overfitting in the context of ridge function estimation, a problem of significant interest in machine learning and statistical analysis. The objective is to provide a finite sample analysis of this phenomenon, shedding light on the conditions under which it occurs and its implications for model estimation. Our approach employs a combination of theoretical analysis and numerical simulations to study the behavior of ridge function estimators in finite samples. The results show that benign overfitting can occur even in moderately sized samples, leading to accurate predictions despite overfitting. Key findings include the identification of critical parameters that control the onset of benign overfitting and the development of a theoretical framework that explains its occurrence. The conclusions of this research have important implications for the practice of machine learning, highlighting the potential benefits of overfitting in certain scenarios and providing guidance for the design of estimation algorithms. Overall, this study contributes to our understanding of the interplay between model complexity, sample size, and estimation accuracy, with potential applications in areas such as regression analysis, neural networks, and deep learning.
