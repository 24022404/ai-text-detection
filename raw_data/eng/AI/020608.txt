This paper presents LRPD, a novel approach for long-range 3D pedestrian detection that combines the strengths of LiDAR and RGB data. The objective is to improve detection accuracy and range for autonomous vehicles and surveillance systems. Our method leverages the high-resolution 3D point clouds from LiDAR and the texture information from RGB images to detect pedestrians at distances beyond 50 meters. We propose a fusion architecture that integrates LiDAR-based point cloud processing with RGB-based image processing, enabling the model to capture both geometric and appearance cues. Experimental results demonstrate that LRPD outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of detection accuracy and range, with a significant reduction in false positives. The key contributions of this research include the development of a multimodal fusion framework, the introduction of a novel point cloud encoding scheme, and the demonstration of the effectiveness of combining LiDAR and RGB data for long-range pedestrian detection. This work has important implications for the development of autonomous vehicles, smart cities, and surveillance systems, and highlights the potential of multimodal sensing for improving pedestrian detection in complex environments. Key keywords: 3D pedestrian detection, LiDAR, RGB, multimodal fusion, autonomous vehicles, computer vision.
