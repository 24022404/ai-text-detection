In response to the growing threats posed by face spoofing attacks in biometric systems, this research aims to develop an effective method for enhancing motion cues using a novel convolutional neural network (CNN) and long short-term memory (LSTM) architecture to improve face anti-spoofing systems. 

Methods: We propose a hybrid CNN-LSTM model that leverages the spatial processing capabilities of CNNs to capture intricate facial features, while the LSTM components are employed to model temporal dynamics and motion cues critical in distinguishing between live and spoofed faces. This architecture is designed to efficiently process sequential video frames, thereby enabling robust detection of subtle changes associated with face movements indicative of genuine presentations. 

Results: Our experimental evaluation demonstrates that the proposed CNN-LSTM model significantly outperforms traditional face anti-spoofing techniques, achieving higher accuracy rates and reduced false positive occurrences across multiple challenging datasets. Comparative analysis with existing state-of-the-art models highlights our approach's superior capability in identifying sophisticated spoofing attempts, including those involving high-quality, lifelike videos.

Conclusion: The integration of enhanced motion cues into the face anti-spoofing process marks a substantial advancement in biometric security. This study contributes a novel framework that not only bolsters the robustness of face recognition systems against spoofing attacks but also broadens the understanding of temporal patterns essential for differentiating between genuine and fraudulent face access. Potential applications of this research span various sectors including financial services, airport security, and personal device authentication. 

Keywords: Face anti-spoofing, CNN-LSTM architecture, motion cues, biometric security, temporal dynamics, spoofing attacks.
