This paper addresses the challenge of determining the appropriate number of states in multi-state autoregressive models, which are widely used for time series analysis in various domains. Traditional approaches often rely on a priori assumptions, which can lead to suboptimal model performance. The aim is to develop a data-driven method to automatically learn the correct number of states, thus enhancing model accuracy and adaptability.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework that integrates data-driven techniques with state-of-the-art machine learning algorithms to dynamically infer the optimal number of states in multi-state autoregressive models. The approach utilizes a combination of statistical analysis and model selection processes, guided by Bayesian inference principles, to evaluate the potential models and select the most suitable configuration based on real-world data.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations were conducted on a range of synthetic and real-world datasets to validate the performance of the proposed method. The results demonstrate that our framework successfully identifies the correct number of states, leading to significant improvements in predictive accuracy and model robustness compared to traditional fixed-state approaches. The method also shows superior adaptability across different datasets and varying levels of data complexity.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of a data-driven method for learning the number of states in multi-state autoregressive models presents a significant advancement in time series modeling. This approach alleviates the need for manual specification of model parameters, offering an automated and efficient solution that can be applied across diverse applications. The implications of this research extend to numerous fields that rely on time series analysis, providing a pathway for developing more precise and adaptable predictive models.

Keywords: Multi-state autoregressive models, time series analysis, data-driven learning, Bayesian inference, model selection, predictive accuracy.
