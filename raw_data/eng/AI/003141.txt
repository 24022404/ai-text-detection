The increasing demands in surveillance, healthcare, and human-computer interaction have driven research into efficient human action recognition methods. This paper presents a novel approach that utilizes inertial sensor data to facilitate human action recognition through an image encoding methodology.

Methods/Approach: We propose a unique encoding technique that transforms time-series data from inertial sensors into images. The transformation leverages signal processing techniques to preserve temporal dynamics and spatial relationships in the sensor data. The encoded images are then used as inputs for a deep convolutional neural network (CNN) model, specifically designed to classify a range of human actions with high accuracy. 

Results/Findings: Our extensive experimental evaluation demonstrates that the proposed method achieves superior performance when compared to traditional approaches, yielding a significant increase in recognition accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency. The CNN model exhibits robust generalization to diverse datasets and varying action complexities. Additionally, the encoded images provide a visually interpretable representation of the sensor data, enhancing the transparency of the recognition process.

Conclusion/Implications: This research introduces a novel intersection between inertial sensing and image-based machine learning, providing a promising direction for cost-effective and accurate human action recognition. The proposed method could significantly impact areas such as real-time monitoring, intelligent user interfaces, and adaptive assistive technologies. Future work will explore the scalability of this approach and its application to a wider range of sensor types and action categories.

Keywords: Human action recognition, inertial sensors, image encoding, convolutional neural networks, time-series data, deep learning.
