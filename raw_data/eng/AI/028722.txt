**Abstract:**  
Real-time dense 3D reconstruction is a critical task in applications such as robotics, augmented reality, and digital preservation. However, achieving high accuracy in both geometric structure and surface detail under varying illumination remains a significant challenge. This paper proposes a novel framework that combines Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) with multi-spectral photometric stereo to enable robust and accurate real-time dense 3D reconstruction. The proposed approach utilizes SLAM for efficient camera pose estimation and initial sparse mapping, while multi-spectral photometric stereo refines the dense geometry and recovers detailed surface normals by leveraging illumination information across multiple spectral channels. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our system achieves superior reconstruction accuracy and surface detail fidelity compared to traditional SLAM-only or single-spectrum reconstruction methods, while maintaining real-time performance. The integrated framework is validated on both synthetic and real-world datasets, showcasing its adaptability to dynamic environments and varying lighting conditions. These results highlight the potential of combining geometric and photometric cues for enhanced 3D scene understanding, promising broader applications in robotics, virtual reality, and cultural heritage digitization. Key contributions include the seamless integration of SLAM and multi-spectral photometric stereo, real-time processing capability, and improvements in dense 3D reconstruction accuracy.  
**Keywords:** SLAM, multi-spectral, photometric stereo, real-time 3D reconstruction, dense mapping, computer vision, surface recovery.
