Automated quality control (QC) is critical for ensuring the reliability of image segmentation in large-scale biomedical imaging studies. This paper addresses the challenge of scalable, objective QC in the context of the UK Biobank Cardiac Magnetic Resonance (MR) Imaging study, which encompasses tens of thousands of participants and massive volumes of cardiac image data. We propose an end-to-end automated QC framework that leverages advanced machine learning techniques to assess the accuracy of cardiac MR image segmentations. Our system integrates a supervised deep learning model trained on expertly annotated data to detect common segmentation errors and assess image quality metrics, such as boundary consistency and anatomical plausibility. The QC pipeline was evaluated on a substantial subset of the UK Biobank dataset, demonstrating high sensitivity and specificity in identifying suboptimal segmentations when compared to manual expert review. Furthermore, the framework effectively flagged outlier cases and segmentation failures, enabling efficient downstream correction and curation. Our results highlight significant improvements in automation, reproducibility, and scalability of quality control over traditional manual approaches. The approach not only reduces the burden on clinical experts but also enhances data integrity for large-scale studies. This research contributes a robust, generalizable automated QC solution with potential applications in multi-center medical image analysis, population health studies, and other domains reliant on high-quality image segmentation.  
Keywords: automated quality control, image segmentation, cardiac MR imaging, machine learning, UK Biobank, deep learning, biomedical data integrity.
