Conditional image generation has emerged as a critical area in computer vision, enabling the synthesis of realistic images based on specific input conditions. However, evaluating the quality and relevance of generated images remains a significant challenge due to the lack of standardized metrics. This paper addresses this gap by proposing a comprehensive framework for evaluating conditional image generation models. We introduce a set of novel metrics that assess both the fidelity of generated images to the input conditions and their perceptual quality. Our approach combines quantitative measures, such as conditional consistency and diversity, with human perceptual studies to ensure a robust evaluation. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed metrics in distinguishing between state-of-the-art models and providing actionable insights for model improvement. The results highlight the importance of aligning evaluation metrics with the specific goals of conditional image generation tasks. This work contributes to the field by offering a standardized evaluation toolkit, facilitating fair comparisons, and guiding future advancements in conditional image synthesis.  

**Keywords**: conditional image generation, evaluation metrics, perceptual quality, image fidelity, computer vision, generative models.
