Frame interpolation, the task of generating intermediate frames between existing ones, often suffers from artefacts that degrade visual quality. Existing benchmarks frequently rely on objective metrics, which correlate poorly with human perception. This paper introduces a novel subjective annotation methodology for evaluating frame interpolation algorithms, emphasizing artefact amplification to highlight subtle distortions. We present a benchmark dataset annotated using this methodology, where human evaluators were specifically instructed to identify and rate the severity of artefacts in interpolated frames. Our approach facilitates a more perceptually relevant assessment of frame interpolation quality compared to traditional metrics like PSNR and SSIM. Results demonstrate a significant discrepancy between subjective ratings and objective scores, highlighting the limitations of current benchmarks. The proposed benchmark and annotation methodology offer a valuable resource for developing and evaluating frame interpolation algorithms that are better aligned with human visual perception. Keywords: Frame Interpolation, Subjective Evaluation, Artefact Amplification, Benchmark, Human Perception, Video Quality.
