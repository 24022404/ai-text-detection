Deep neural networks (DNNs) have achieved remarkable success across various domains, but their computational and memory demands often hinder deployment in resource-constrained environments. This paper introduces a novel training paradigm, "Go Wide, Then Narrow," designed to efficiently train deep thin networks—architectures with fewer parameters and lower computational overhead. The proposed approach begins by training a wide, over-parameterized network to leverage the benefits of easier optimization and better gradient flow. Subsequently, the model is progressively narrowed through a structured pruning process, retaining only the most critical parameters while maintaining performance. We evaluate our method on benchmark datasets, demonstrating that deep thin networks trained using this paradigm achieve competitive accuracy compared to their wider counterparts, with significantly reduced computational costs. Our results highlight the effectiveness of this approach in balancing model efficiency and performance, making it particularly suitable for edge devices and real-time applications. The key contributions of this work include a scalable training framework, insights into the relationship between network width and trainability, and a practical solution for deploying lightweight yet accurate models. Keywords: deep learning, neural network pruning, efficient training, model compression, edge computing.
