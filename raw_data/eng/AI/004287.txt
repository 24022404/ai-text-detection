The objective of this paper is to address the growing concerns surrounding the risks associated with distribution inference in machine learning environments. As data-driven decision-making becomes increasingly prevalent, understanding the potential privacy and security risks from model inferences based on observed data distributions is critical. Our approach introduces a formal framework to quantify and evaluate these distribution inference risks. By leveraging statistical models and advanced computational techniques, the framework allows for the estimation of privacy leakages that may arise when inferring sensitive information from data distributions. The results of our study demonstrate the ability of the proposed methodology to effectively identify and estimate the extent of potential risks, offering comparisons with existing models that highlight its superior accuracy and reliability. Our findings emphasize the need for enhanced scrutiny in vulnerable areas of data management, proposing concrete measures to mitigate these risks. The research significantly contributes to the field of data privacy by providing a novel and robust tool for assessing and managing distribution inference risks. Key implications include strengthening the privacy assurances of machine learning systems and guiding future policy development. This work opens new avenues for the practical application of distribution inference risk management across various sectors, ensuring more secure and private data utilization. Key keywords: distribution inference, privacy risks, data management, machine learning, security, statistical models, risk estimation.
