Object detection and pose estimation are critical tasks in computer vision, with applications spanning robotics, autonomous vehicles, augmented reality, and scene understanding. Traditionally treated as separate problems, recent advancements in deep learning have enabled joint object detection and pose estimation using monocular vision—i.e., single-camera input—offering cost-effective and scalable solutions for 3D perception. This survey paper systematically reviews the current state-of-the-art approaches for simultaneously detecting objects and estimating their 6-DoF (degrees of freedom) poses from monocular images. We analyze popular model architectures, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs), transformer-based models, and end-to-end learning pipelines that integrate detection and pose estimation within a unified framework. The survey evaluates key challenges such as scale ambiguity, occlusions, and dataset limitations in monocular settings, while summarizing benchmark datasets and evaluation metrics commonly used in this field. We highlight trends in algorithmic design, such as multi-task learning, geometric reasoning, and synthetic data augmentation, and compare the performance of representative methods on standard benchmarks. Our findings reveal notable progress in accuracy, real-time inference, and robustness, yet underscore ongoing challenges in generalization and handling complex real-world scenarios. We conclude by identifying open research directions and promising avenues for future innovation. Keywords: joint object detection, pose estimation, monocular vision, 6-DoF, deep learning, computer vision, multi-task learning.
