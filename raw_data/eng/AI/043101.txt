This paper aims to provide a comprehensive tutorial and survey on Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (SNE), a powerful dimensionality reduction technique widely used for data visualization. We focus on its underlying principles and explore the use of Gaussian and Student-t distributions for optimizing the embeddings, highlighting the distinctions and applications of each.

Methods/Approach: We examine the theoretical foundation of SNE, discussing its ability to preserve local data structures in low-dimensional spaces. By delving into the mathematical formulations, we compare the implementations and benefits of employing Gaussian and Student-t distributions within the SNE framework. The analysis includes a detailed breakdown of algorithmic steps, regularization techniques, and computational considerations.

Results/Findings: Through a series of experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets, we evaluate the performance of Gaussian-SNE and t-SNE. Our findings reveal significant differences in how each distribution handles outliers and density variations. The Student-t distribution's robustness in managing crowding effects and maintaining data spread enables a more faithful representation of high-dimensional data.

Conclusion/Implications: This paper elucidates the strengths and limitations of Gaussian and Student-t based SNE approaches, providing insights into their suitability for various data types and visualization tasks. Our survey and tutorial serve as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners looking to apply these methods in data-driven domains such as bioinformatics, social network analysis, and machine learning. By highlighting the nuances of SNE, we contribute to the broader understanding and advancement of dimensionality reduction techniques.

Keywords: Stochastic Neighbor Embedding, SNE, t-SNE, Gaussian distribution, Student-t distribution, dimensionality reduction, data visualization, machine learning.
