This paper addresses the challenge of recovering the 3D shape, texture, and motion of fast-moving objects from motion-blurred images, a problem that remains underexplored in computer vision. Traditional methods often struggle with highly dynamic scenes due to the loss of high-frequency details caused by motion blur. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel framework that leverages the inherent information within motion-blurred images to reconstruct textured 3D shapes and estimate object motion. Our approach integrates a physics-based motion blur model with deep learning techniques, enabling the joint optimization of shape, texture, and motion parameters. The framework employs a differentiable rendering pipeline to simulate motion blur and iteratively refines the 3D reconstruction using gradient-based optimization. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing techniques in both synthetic and real-world datasets, achieving higher accuracy in shape recovery and motion estimation. The proposed approach is robust to varying levels of motion blur and can handle complex textures and fast-moving objects. This work contributes to advancing 3D reconstruction in dynamic scenes, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and augmented reality.  

**Keywords**: 3D reconstruction, motion blur, textured shape recovery, motion estimation, differentiable rendering, computer vision.
