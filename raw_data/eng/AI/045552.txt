This paper reexamines the architecture selection process in Differentiable Neural Architecture Search (NAS), a crucial aspect of automating the design of deep neural networks. Our objective is to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of NAS by introducing a novel selection strategy that leverages the differentiable nature of the search space. We propose a method that combines reinforcement learning with gradient-based optimization to identify the most promising architectures. Our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of computational cost and model performance, achieving state-of-the-art results on several benchmark datasets. The key findings of our research highlight the importance of carefully designing the architecture selection process in NAS, and demonstrate the potential of differentiable methods to accelerate the search for optimal neural architectures. Our work contributes to the advancement of NAS and has significant implications for the development of more efficient and scalable deep learning models, with potential applications in areas such as computer vision, natural language processing, and autonomous systems. Key keywords: Differentiable NAS, Architecture Selection, Reinforcement Learning, Gradient-based Optimization, Deep Learning.
