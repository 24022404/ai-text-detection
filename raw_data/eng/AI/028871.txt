Temporal Knowledge Graphs (TKGs) capture rich, time-evolving relational data essential for applications such as event prediction, recommendation systems, and temporal reasoning. However, existing approaches to learning on TKGs typically require abundant historical instances for effective model training, limiting their applicability in scenarios where new temporal facts or entities emerge with minimal prior data. This paper addresses this challenge by proposing a novel one-shot learning framework tailored for temporal knowledge graphs. Our approach leverages meta-learning strategies to enable rapid adaptation to new temporal facts from a single or very limited example, integrating temporal encoding with relation- and entity-agnostic representations. We design a temporal meta-learning algorithm that dynamically adjusts to novel entities and evolving relationships, thereby enhancing few-shot inference capabilities. Experimental evaluations on benchmark TKG datasets demonstrate that our model significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in one-shot temporal link prediction and event forecasting tasks, particularly in sparse data regimes. The results highlight the robustness and generalizability of the proposed framework for real-world, dynamic knowledge graph applications. This work introduces one-shot learning principles to temporal knowledge graphs, advancing the ability to reason over time-dependent data with minimal supervision. Keywords: temporal knowledge graph, one-shot learning, meta-learning, temporal reasoning, few-shot learning, event prediction.
