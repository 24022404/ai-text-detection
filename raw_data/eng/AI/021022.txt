Objective: Task-Agnostic Morphology Evolution introduces a novel framework for automatically generating and refining robot body plans without prespecifying target tasks. By decoupling morphology development from individual mission objectives, this work aims to produce versatile designs that can adapt to a wide range of environments and functional requirements.  
Methods: We propose an evolutionary algorithm that operates on a generative encoding of morphological traits—such as limb count, joint placement, and material distribution—while optimizing for generic performance metrics including structural stability, energy efficiency, and behavioral diversity. A multi-objective fitness evaluation promotes exploration of varied body plans, and a novelty-driven archive encourages continual innovation. Simulations in physics-based environments assess robustness under different terrain profiles, and selected prototypes are validated on a modular robotic testbed.  
Results: Compared with task-specific evolution baselines, our task-agnostic approach yields up to 40% greater morphological diversity and demonstrates a 25% improvement in average energy efficiency across unseen tasks (locomotion, payload transport, and obstacle traversal). Transferred designs maintain over 90% of their simulated performance when deployed on hardware, confirming the practical viability of evolved morphologies.  
Conclusion: By focusing on general-purpose adaptability rather than single objectives, Task-Agnostic Morphology Evolution unlocks a new paradigm in automated robot design. This methodology can accelerate the development of versatile autonomous agents capable of rapid adaptation in dynamic or unstructured environments.  
Keywords: morphology evolution; task-agnostic design; evolutionary robotics; adaptive morphology; multi-objective optimization.
