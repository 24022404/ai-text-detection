The objective of this paper is to address the challenges and explore the current advancements in applying deep learning techniques to remote sensing tasks, particularly when limited training data is available. Remote sensing data, characterized by its high dimensionality and complexity, often requires sophisticated analysis methods. However, the effectiveness of deep learning models can be significantly hindered by the scarcity of annotated datasets, a common issue in this field. This study examines existing approaches, such as data augmentation, transfer learning, and semi-supervised learning, which have been employed to mitigate the challenges posed by limited data. We assess the performance and limitations of these methods through empirical analysis and identify critical gaps in current research. Our findings highlight that while notable progress has been made, there remains substantial room for improvement, specifically in enhancing model robustness and generalization capabilities. We propose potential directions for future research, including the exploration of novel architectures and hybrid models that can effectively integrate domain knowledge. This paper contributes to the field by providing a comprehensive overview of the state-of-the-art, identifying existing gaps, and suggesting innovative solutions to propel the use of deep learning in remote sensing with limited data. Keywords include deep learning, remote sensing, limited training data, data augmentation, and transfer learning.
