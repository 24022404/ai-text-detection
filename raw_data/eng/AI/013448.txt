This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised video object segmentation, a critical task in computer vision that aims to automatically identify and segment moving objects in video sequences without human annotations. We propose a novel framework that leverages instance embedding transfer to facilitate accurate segmentation in the absence of labeled training data. The approach utilizes pre-trained instance embedding networks, originally developed for static image segmentation, and adapts them to the dynamic and complex nature of video data. By extracting robust instance-level features and propagating them temporally using motion cues, our method ensures consistent segmentation of objects across frames. Experimental results on benchmark video segmentation datasets demonstrate that our technique outperforms existing unsupervised methods in both segmentation accuracy and temporal stability, narrowing the performance gap with supervised approaches. The proposed framework is computationally efficient, scalable to long video sequences, and readily adaptable to new, unseen environments. This work highlights the potential of transfer learning and instance embeddings in advancing unsupervised video analysis and offers a promising direction for applications in autonomous driving, video surveillance, and robotics. Keywords: unsupervised video object segmentation, instance embedding, transfer learning, feature propagation, computer vision.
