Interpreting and explaining machine learning models have become critical tasks as these systems increasingly inform decisions in diverse domains such as healthcare, finance, and autonomous systems. This paper provides a comprehensive mini-tour of the landscape—often referred to as the “machine learning zoo”—covering prominent interpretability and explainability methods. We begin by outlining the core challenges faced in understanding complex models, with a focus on the trade-offs between accuracy and transparency in algorithms ranging from linear regression to deep neural networks. Our approach systematically examines both model-specific and model-agnostic techniques, including feature importance, partial dependence plots, LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations), and SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations). We evaluate these methods through empirical experiments on benchmark datasets, assessing their explanatory power, computational efficiency, and ability to reveal model biases. Key findings highlight the advantages of hybrid strategies that combine global and local interpretability, and we demonstrate how these approaches enhance user trust and enable debugging in AI-driven applications. In conclusion, our study offers practical guidance on selecting and applying interpretability tools, emphasizing the importance of transparent AI for ethical and reliable deployment. Keywords: interpretability, explainability, machine learning, model transparency, LIME, SHAP, AI ethics.
