Frame interpolation is a critical task in video processing, aiming to generate intermediate frames between existing ones to enhance video quality and smoothness. However, evaluating the performance of frame interpolation methods remains challenging due to the lack of robust benchmarks that account for subjective visual quality. This paper introduces a novel approach to subjective annotation for frame interpolation benchmarks by leveraging artefact amplification. The proposed method systematically amplifies interpolation artefacts, such as blurring, ghosting, and distortion, to make subtle imperfections more perceptible to human annotators. A diverse dataset of video sequences is used, and a crowd-sourced annotation framework is implemented to collect subjective quality ratings. The results demonstrate that artefact amplification significantly improves the consistency and reliability of subjective evaluations compared to traditional methods. Furthermore, the benchmark enables a more nuanced comparison of state-of-the-art frame interpolation algorithms, highlighting their strengths and weaknesses in handling specific artefact types. This work contributes a valuable tool for the research community, offering a more accurate and human-centric evaluation framework for frame interpolation technologies. The implications extend to applications in video compression, streaming, and computer vision, where high-quality frame interpolation is essential.  

**Keywords**: frame interpolation, subjective annotation, artefact amplification, video quality assessment, benchmark evaluation.
