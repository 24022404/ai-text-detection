This paper presents a novel approach to improving the efficiency and convergence of the Greedy-GQ algorithm, a popular reinforcement learning method for off-policy policy evaluation and control. While Greedy-GQ has shown promise in handling large-scale problems, its performance is often hindered by high variance in gradient estimates, leading to slower convergence rates. To address this limitation, we propose Greedy-GQ with Variance Reduction (Greedy-GQ-VR), which integrates variance reduction techniques to stabilize gradient updates and accelerate learning. We provide a rigorous finite-time analysis of the proposed algorithm, demonstrating its improved sample complexity compared to the standard Greedy-GQ. Our theoretical results establish that Greedy-GQ-VR achieves a faster convergence rate under both linear and non-linear function approximation settings. Empirical evaluations on benchmark reinforcement learning tasks confirm the practical effectiveness of the proposed method, showcasing significant reductions in training time and variance. The findings highlight the potential of variance reduction techniques in enhancing the scalability and robustness of reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly in high-dimensional and stochastic environments. This work contributes to the growing body of research on efficient reinforcement learning, offering a practical solution for real-world applications requiring stable and fast-converging policy optimization.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement learning, Greedy-GQ, variance reduction, finite-time analysis, policy optimization, sample complexity.
