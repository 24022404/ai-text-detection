This paper introduces SOM-VAE, a novel framework that combines Self-Organizing Maps (SOM) with Variational Autoencoders (VAE) to enable interpretable and discrete representation learning for time series data. The primary objective is to address the challenges of learning meaningful and interpretable representations from high-dimensional and sequential datasets, which are crucial for tasks such as anomaly detection, segmentation, and forecasting. Our approach integrates the topology-preserving properties of SOM into the VAE architecture, facilitating the discovery of discrete latent states while maintaining reconstruction fidelity. We evaluate SOM-VAE on multiple real-world and synthetic time series datasets, demonstrating its capability to capture underlying sequential patterns and provide visually interpretable discrete embeddings. Comparative experiments show that SOM-VAE outperforms existing methods in clustering quality and interpretability without sacrificing predictive performance. The results suggest that SOM-VAE can serve as a valuable tool for the analysis and understanding of complex temporal data in domains such as finance, healthcare, and sensor networks. Key contributions include the design of a unified SOM-VAE architecture, a robust training scheme for discrete state learning, and extensive experiments validating interpretability and effectiveness. Keywords: representation learning, time series, variational autoencoder, self-organizing map, discrete embeddings, interpretability, anomaly detection.
