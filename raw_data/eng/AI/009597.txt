This paper introduces Mean Spectral Normalization (MSN), a novel technique designed to enhance the robustness and efficiency of deep neural networks deployed in resource-constrained embedded automation systems. The objective is to mitigate performance degradation caused by limited computational resources and data scarcity. MSN normalizes the weight matrices of neural network layers by constraining the mean of their singular values, unlike traditional spectral normalization that focuses solely on the largest singular value. This approach offers a more stable and less aggressive regularization, preserving essential feature representations. Experimental results on image classification and object detection tasks, executed on embedded platforms, demonstrate that MSN consistently outperforms standard spectral normalization and other regularization methods in terms of accuracy and convergence speed, particularly when training with limited data. Furthermore, MSN reduces computational overhead during inference, facilitating real-time performance on embedded devices. The findings suggest that MSN provides a practical and effective solution for deploying accurate and efficient deep learning models in embedded automation applications, representing a significant step toward reliable AI-driven automation. Keywords: Spectral Normalization, Deep Learning, Embedded Systems, Automation, Regularization, Neural Networks.
