Near-field distance estimation using surround-view fisheye cameras is crucial for autonomous driving and robotics applications. This paper introduces SVDistNet, a novel self-supervised learning framework for accurate distance prediction without relying on labeled data. SVDistNet leverages the geometric constraints inherent in multi-camera systems to generate pseudo-ground truth distances. Our approach employs a Siamese network architecture with a spatial transformer module to handle the distortions introduced by fisheye lenses and efficiently fuse features from multiple cameras. A novel loss function, incorporating both photometric and geometric consistency, guides the network to learn robust distance representations. Experimental results on a large-scale driving dataset demonstrate that SVDistNet achieves state-of-the-art performance in near-field distance estimation compared to existing self-supervised methods. Furthermore, we show that SVDistNet generalizes well to unseen environments and camera configurations. This work provides a practical and scalable solution for deploying accurate distance estimation systems using readily available surround-view fisheye cameras.
Keywords: Self-Supervised Learning, Distance Estimation, Fisheye Cameras, Surround View, Autonomous Driving.
