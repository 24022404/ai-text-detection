This paper addresses the challenge of making reliable predictions in few-shot learning scenarios by employing a novel approach based on conformal prediction integrated with auxiliary tasks. Few-shot learning requires models to generalize from a limited amount of data, often leading to high uncertainty in predictions. Our research aims to enhance prediction reliability and accuracy in these constrained settings.

Methods/Approach: We propose a framework that combines few-shot learning with conformal prediction methods. To improve model performance, we introduce auxiliary tasks that provide additional context and structure for the learning process. The auxiliary tasks are carefully selected to compliment the primary task, fostering improved learning efficiency. The conformal prediction technique is then applied to generate prediction sets with quantified uncertainty, ensuring that the predictions are both accurate and reliable within few-shot contexts.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms conventional few-shot learning approaches, particularly in circumstances where data availability is limited. The integration of auxiliary tasks aids in producing narrower prediction sets without compromising coverage guarantees, as shown in comparative studies across various datasets and domains.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field of machine learning by presenting a robust methodology for few-shot conformal prediction, effectively combining auxiliary tasks with predictive models to enhance prediction confidence and accuracy. The proposed approach has potential applications in areas requiring rapid adaptation to new tasks with minimal data, such as personalized medicine, adaptive user interfaces, and real-time data analysis. Key keywords include few-shot learning, conformal prediction, auxiliary tasks, prediction reliability, and uncertainty quantification.
