This paper addresses the challenge of simultaneously performing object detection and pose estimation, a key task in computer vision, by leveraging the capabilities of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). The study aims to evaluate and compare the effectiveness of different CNN architectures in achieving high accuracy in these dual tasks.

Methods/Approach: We examine several state-of-the-art CNN models, implementing a joint learning framework that encapsulates object detection and pose estimation within a unified network. The architectures under consideration include variations in depth, complexity, and feature extraction techniques, allowing for a comprehensive analysis. The study also incorporates data augmentation and transfer learning to enhance model performance.

Results/Findings: The experimental results demonstrate that certain CNN architectures significantly outperform others in jointly detecting and estimating object poses with high precision. Comparative analysis reveals that models with enhanced feature extraction capabilities exhibit superior performance, showing improved accuracy and robustness in diverse and complex scenarios. Key metrics, including detection accuracy and pose estimation error, are used to benchmark the models.

Conclusion/Implications: This comparative study contributes valuable insights into the suitability of different CNN architectures for the task of joint object detection and pose estimation. The findings suggest that integrating efficient feature extraction techniques is critical to enhancing the network's overall performance. The study lays the groundwork for further research into optimized CNN models, with potential applications spanning robotics, augmented reality, and autonomous systems.

Keywords: Convolutional Neural Networks, object detection, pose estimation, computer vision, joint learning, deep learning, model comparison.
