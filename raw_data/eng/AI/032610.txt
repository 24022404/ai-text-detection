**Abstract:**

This paper presents Weight-of-Evidence 2.0, an advanced framework that enhances traditional weight-of-evidence (WOE) transformation techniques through the integration of shrinkage methods and spline-based binning strategies. The objective is to address common challenges in risk modeling and credit scoring, such as overfitting, inefficient variable discretization, and suboptimal predictive performance. Our approach leverages shrinkage regularization to stabilize WOE estimates, particularly in the presence of sparse or imbalanced data, reducing the risk of overfitting and variance. Additionally, we introduce spline-binning, which employs spline functions to generate more flexible and data-driven bin edges, capturing non-linear relationships between predictors and the target outcome without manual intervention. Experimental evaluations on multiple real-world credit datasets demonstrate that Weight-of-Evidence 2.0 significantly improves model discrimination and calibration compared to conventional WOE binning, quantile binning, and other regularized approaches. The results highlight superior performance in terms of area under the ROC curve (AUC) and log-loss metrics, while maintaining interpretability crucial for regulatory compliance. The proposed methodology offers a practical and scalable solution for practitioners in credit risk, fraud detection, and other domains requiring robust, interpretable variable transformation. Keywords: weight-of-evidence, shrinkage, spline-binning, risk modeling, credit scoring, variable transformation, interpretability, model regularization.
