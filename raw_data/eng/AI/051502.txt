This paper explores the potential of unsupervised environment design to elicit emergent complexity and facilitate zero-shot transfer in reinforcement learning agents. We introduce a novel framework where agents are trained in environments automatically generated by an adversarial process, encouraging the development of robust and generalizable skills. Our method avoids reliance on predefined task specifications, instead fostering complexity through a competitive dynamic between the agent and the environment generator. Experimental results demonstrate that agents trained under this unsupervised regime exhibit significantly improved performance on unseen target tasks compared to those trained with traditional, fixed environments. Furthermore, we analyze the emergent behaviors and strategies adopted by these agents, providing insights into the mechanisms underlying successful zero-shot transfer. This research contributes a new perspective on reinforcement learning by shifting the focus from task-specific optimization to the development of broadly applicable intelligence through autonomously generated environmental challenges. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Unsupervised Learning, Environment Design, Emergent Complexity, Zero-shot Transfer, Artificial Intelligence.
