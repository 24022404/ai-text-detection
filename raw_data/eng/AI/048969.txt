This paper addresses the challenge of error detection within noisy knowledge graphs, which are often compromised by incomplete or inaccurate information due to automated data integration. We propose a novel approach that guides graph embedding models using path-ranking methods to improve their effectiveness in identifying erroneous entities and relations. Our methodology integrates path-based ranking algorithms, which capture explicit structural patterns and semantic dependencies, with established graph embedding techniques, enabling more robust learning of latent graph representations. Experimental evaluations were conducted on multiple benchmark datasets featuring synthetic and real-world noise, demonstrating that our guided embeddings significantly outperform baseline embedding models and traditional path-based detectors in both precision and recall for error identification tasks. The results highlight improved detection accuracy for varied error types, including missing links and incorrect relationships, while maintaining scalability for large-scale graphs. This hybrid strategy leverages the strengths of both symbolic reasoning and statistical representation learning, providing a practical tool for enhancing knowledge graph reliability. Our work contributes new insights into combining path-aware reasoning with deep learning-based embeddings, suggesting practical applications in knowledge base construction, semantic web, and data quality management. Keywords: knowledge graphs, error detection, graph embeddings, path-ranking, noisy data, machine learning.
