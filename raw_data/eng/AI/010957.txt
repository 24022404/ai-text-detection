Effective cache management is crucial for optimizing performance in various computing systems. Traditional cache replacement policies, such as LRU and LFU, often struggle to adapt to dynamically changing workloads. This paper introduces RLCache, a novel automated cache management framework powered by reinforcement learning (RL). RLCache employs a deep Q-network (DQN) agent to learn optimal cache replacement strategies by interacting with the caching environment. The agent observes cache states, including hit rates, eviction costs, and item frequencies, and selects actions corresponding to cache eviction decisions. The system is trained using a reward function that balances hit rate maximization and eviction cost minimization. Experimental results demonstrate that RLCache significantly outperforms conventional cache replacement policies across a range of workloads, achieving up to a 30% improvement in hit rate and a 15% reduction in eviction costs. Furthermore, RLCache exhibits strong generalization capabilities, adapting effectively to unseen workloads. These findings highlight the potential of RL-based techniques for automated and adaptive cache management, offering a promising direction for enhancing system performance and resource utilization. Keywords: Cache management, Reinforcement learning, Deep Q-network, Cache replacement policies, Automated optimization.
