This paper investigates the impact of entropy regularization on policy optimization in reinforcement learning. We explore how varying entropy bonus coefficients affects both the exploration-exploitation trade-off and the final performance of learned policies. Our approach involves conducting a series of experiments across several benchmark environments, using both on-policy and off-policy algorithms, specifically PPO and SAC, respectively. The results demonstrate a non-monotonic relationship between the entropy bonus and policy performance. Specifically, we find that while a moderate entropy bonus often accelerates initial learning and prevents premature convergence to suboptimal policies, excessively high entropy can hinder learning and lead to unstable behavior. Furthermore, we observe that the optimal entropy bonus varies significantly depending on the environment and the choice of reinforcement learning algorithm. The findings suggest that careful tuning of entropy regularization is crucial for achieving robust and efficient policy optimization. These insights contribute to a better understanding of entropy's role in reinforcement learning and offer practical guidelines for selecting appropriate entropy bonus values. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Entropy Regularization, Policy Optimization, Exploration-Exploitation, PPO, SAC.
