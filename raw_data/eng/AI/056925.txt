This paper introduces Patch Shortcuts, a novel approach for efficiently identifying vulnerabilities in black-box systems using interpretable proxy models. Our method addresses the challenge of understanding and mitigating security flaws when direct access to system internals is limited. Patch Shortcuts constructs simplified, patch-based proxy models that mimic the behavior of the black-box system, enabling rapid exploration of the input space. By focusing on localized input patches, we generate more interpretable models compared to traditional global approximation techniques. We evaluate Patch Shortcuts on a range of vulnerability detection tasks, demonstrating its ability to effectively uncover adversarial examples and security weaknesses. Our results show that Patch Shortcuts achieves comparable vulnerability detection rates to existing methods while significantly reducing computational overhead and enhancing the interpretability of detected vulnerabilities. The generated interpretable proxy models provide valuable insights into the root causes of vulnerabilities, facilitating the development of targeted defense strategies. This research contributes a practical and efficient technique for auditing and securing black-box systems. Keywords: black-box vulnerability, proxy model, interpretability, adversarial example, security, patch-based, shortcut learning.
