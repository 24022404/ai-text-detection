This paper addresses the challenge of human pose estimation in ancient vase paintings, a domain characterized by unique artistic styles and deteriorated image quality. We introduce a novel approach, Perceptually-grounded Style Transfer Learning (PG-STL), to enhance the performance of pose estimation models in this challenging context. PG-STL leverages style transfer techniques to bridge the domain gap between synthetic training data and real-world vase painting images. Crucially, a perceptual loss function is incorporated to preserve structural integrity and anatomical plausibility during style transfer, preventing distortions that could negatively impact pose estimation accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate that PG-STL significantly improves the performance of state-of-the-art pose estimation models on a newly curated dataset of annotated ancient vase paintings, achieving a 15% increase in mean average precision compared to models trained without style transfer. These findings highlight the effectiveness of PG-STL in adapting pose estimation models to stylistically unique and historically significant visual data, offering valuable implications for art history analysis and digital humanities research. Keywords: Pose Estimation, Style Transfer, Deep Learning, Ancient Art, Computer Vision, Digital Humanities.
