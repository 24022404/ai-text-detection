Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models across distributed devices while preserving data privacy. However, the selection of clients in FL often introduces significant variance and biases, which can degrade model performance and fairness. This paper introduces *Clustered Sampling*, a novel client selection strategy designed to reduce variance and enhance representativity in FL systems. The proposed approach leverages clustering techniques to group clients based on their data distributions, ensuring a more balanced and representative subset of participants in each training round. We evaluate the method using extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, comparing it against traditional random and stratified sampling techniques. Results demonstrate that Clustered Sampling achieves lower variance in model updates, faster convergence, and improved generalization performance across diverse client populations. Furthermore, the approach is computationally efficient and scalable, making it suitable for large-scale FL deployments. This work contributes to the advancement of FL by addressing critical challenges in client selection, paving the way for more robust and equitable distributed learning systems.  

**Keywords**: Federated Learning, Client Selection, Clustered Sampling, Variance Reduction, Representativity, Distributed Machine Learning.
