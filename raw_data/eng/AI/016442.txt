This paper investigates instance-based generalization in reinforcement learning (RL), addressing the challenge of enabling agents to effectively transfer learned knowledge to novel, unseen states. We introduce a novel approach that leverages a memory of past experiences, weighted by similarity to the current state, to inform action selection. Our method employs a learned embedding space to quantify state similarity, enabling generalization beyond simple feature overlap. The algorithm dynamically adjusts the weighting of past experiences based on their relevance and the agent’s uncertainty. We evaluate our approach on a suite of benchmark RL environments, demonstrating significant improvements in generalization performance compared to state-of-the-art model-free and model-based RL algorithms. Specifically, our agent exhibits faster learning and higher asymptotic performance in environments with varying dynamics and reward structures. These results highlight the potential of instance-based methods for achieving robust generalization in RL, offering a promising direction for developing agents that can adapt quickly to new situations. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Generalization, Instance-Based Learning, Memory, Embedding, Transfer Learning.
