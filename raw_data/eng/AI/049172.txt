Graph representation learning has emerged as a critical area in machine learning, enabling the extraction of meaningful features from graph-structured data. However, unsupervised graph representation learning often struggles to capture cluster-level information, which is essential for tasks such as node classification and community detection. This paper introduces **Graph InfoClust**, a novel unsupervised framework designed to leverage cluster-level node information for enhanced graph representation learning. The proposed approach integrates a cluster-aware objective function into the graph neural network (GNN) architecture, enabling the model to learn representations that preserve both local and global structural properties. By maximizing mutual information between node representations and cluster-level summaries, Graph InfoClust effectively captures hierarchical relationships within the graph. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that Graph InfoClust outperforms existing unsupervised methods in node classification and clustering tasks, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the importance of incorporating cluster-level information in unsupervised graph learning and provide a foundation for future research in this direction. This work contributes to advancing unsupervised graph representation learning by offering a scalable and interpretable framework with broad applications in social network analysis, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems.  

**Keywords**: graph representation learning, unsupervised learning, graph neural networks, clustering, mutual information, node classification.
