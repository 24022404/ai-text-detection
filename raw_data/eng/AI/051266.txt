This paper explores the application of variational approximations using Fisher divergence, aiming to improve the accuracy and efficiency of approximate inference in complex probabilistic models. Our approach leverages the Fisher divergence as a alternative to traditional Kullback-Leibler divergence, enabling more flexible and robust approximations. We propose a novel algorithm that combines variational inference with Fisher divergence minimization, allowing for better capturing of underlying model structures. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, showing improved performance compared to existing variational approximation techniques. The key findings of this research highlight the potential of Fisher divergence-based variational approximations for enhancing model accuracy and reducing computational costs. Our contributions have important implications for machine learning and artificial intelligence applications, particularly in areas such as Bayesian inference, deep learning, and probabilistic modeling. Key keywords: variational inference, Fisher divergence, approximate inference, probabilistic modeling, Bayesian inference, machine learning.
