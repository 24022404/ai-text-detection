This paper introduces a novel approach termed Auxiliary Augmentation with GNT-Xent Loss (AAG) for self-supervised representation learning, addressing the challenge of feature extraction without labelled data. The primary goal is to enhance the quality of learned representations in neural networks by leveraging auxiliary data augmentation techniques.

Methods/Approach: We propose the AAG framework, which integrates auxiliary augmentation methods with a new contrastive loss function known as GNT-Xent Loss. The framework utilizes self-supervised learning paradigms to enable models to learn robust features that are both discriminative and generalizable. The GNT-Xent Loss builds upon traditional contrastive loss by incorporating gradient normalization techniques, which aid in stabilizing training and improving convergence.

Results/Findings: Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves superior performance compared to existing self-supervised learning methods. The integration of auxiliary augmentations with the GNT-Xent Loss significantly enhances the model's ability to capture intricate patterns in data, resulting in better classification and representation quality. Quantitative assessments show improved metrics in terms of accuracy and representation similarity across diverse tasks.

Conclusion/Implications: The AAG framework and the introduction of the GNT-Xent Loss contribute to the domain of self-supervised learning by offering a more effective and computationally efficient approach to representation learning. This research paves the way for developing more sophisticated methods that can operate without reliance on large volumes of labeled data, with potential applications in areas such as image retrieval, natural language processing, and video analysis.

Keywords: self-supervised learning, representation learning, auxiliary augmentation, GNT-Xent Loss, contrastive learning, feature extraction, neural networks.
