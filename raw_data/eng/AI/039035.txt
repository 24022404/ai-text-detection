Time series classification (TSC) is a crucial task with applications across diverse domains.  Interval-based methods, particularly random interval forests, have shown promise, but often suffer from suboptimal interval selection and feature extraction. This paper introduces the Canonical Interval Forest (CIF) classifier, a novel approach that enhances interval-based TSC by incorporating canonical interval selection and improved feature engineering. CIF employs a dynamic programming strategy to identify the most informative intervals based on class separability.  Furthermore, it integrates a richer set of statistical features extracted from these intervals, including mean, standard deviation, slope, and correlation measures.  Experiments on a comprehensive set of benchmark datasets demonstrate that CIF achieves significantly higher classification accuracy compared to state-of-the-art interval-based and other TSC algorithms.  CIF also exhibits robustness to noise and varying time series lengths.  These results highlight the effectiveness of canonical interval selection and enhanced feature representation for time series classification.  Our findings suggest that CIF offers a powerful and efficient solution for TSC, advancing the state-of-the-art in this field.
Keywords: Time Series Classification, Interval Forests, Canonical Intervals, Feature Engineering, Machine Learning.
