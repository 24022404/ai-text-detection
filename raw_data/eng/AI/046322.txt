The research investigates the efficacy of supervised and unsupervised deep learning methods for anomaly detection in images. The primary objective is to evaluate and compare these two approaches in identifying anomalies within diverse image datasets common in industrial quality control, medical imaging, and surveillance.

Methods: This study employs advanced deep learning architectures, including Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for supervised learning and Autoencoders for unsupervised learning. The supervised method involves training a classifier with labeled data containing normal and anomalous samples, while the unsupervised method utilizes reconstruction errors to detect deviations in unlabeled datasets. Key performance indicators include detection accuracy, false positive rate, and computational efficiency.

Results: Experimental outcomes demonstrate that supervised methods achieve higher accuracy in datasets where anomalies are well-represented during training. Conversely, unsupervised methods, particularly those incorporating variational frameworks, exhibit superior performance in scenarios with limited labeled data, showing robustness to variations in the data distribution. Both methods outperform traditional statistical approaches, highlighting the impact of deep learning techniques in anomaly detection.

Conclusion: This study presents critical insights into the selection of deep learning strategies for anomaly detection tasks. While supervised learning is preferred when anomaly examples are accessible and labeled, unsupervised methods provide flexible, high-quality anomaly detection in unlabeled scenarios. The research underlines the importance of method selection based on dataset characteristics and application needs, facilitating advancements in fields where early and accurate anomaly detection is crucial. Potential applications include automatic defect detection in manufacturing and early diagnosis in medical images.

Keywords: anomaly detection, supervised learning, unsupervised learning, deep learning, CNN, Autoencoder, image analysis, industrial quality control, medical imaging, surveillance systems.
