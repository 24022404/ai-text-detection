This paper presents a novel seismic inverse modeling method leveraging Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to enhance subsurface property reconstruction from seismic data. Traditional inverse modeling approaches often struggle with high computational cost, sensitivity to noise, and limited ability to capture complex data distributions. To address these challenges, we propose a GAN-based framework, where the generator learns to produce plausible subsurface models conditioned on observed seismic responses, while the discriminator evaluates the similarity between generated and real geological models. The proposed approach is trained on a dataset of synthetic seismic measurements and corresponding subsurface structures, enabling the model to learn complex nonlinear mappings inherent in seismic inversion tasks. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves higher reconstruction accuracy, improved noise robustness, and better generalization compared to conventional inversion techniques and other deep learning-based models. Furthermore, the GAN-based inverse modeling provides realistic and geologically consistent estimations, reducing artifacts and ambiguity in the recovered models. These findings indicate the potential of generative models for advancing seismic data interpretation and have practical implications for reservoir characterization, hydrocarbon exploration, and geophysical imaging. Key keywords: seismic inversion, generative adversarial network, deep learning, subsurface modeling, seismic data interpretation.
