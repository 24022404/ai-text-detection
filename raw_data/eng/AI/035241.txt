Detecting small objects in complex scenes remains a significant challenge in computer vision. This paper introduces an efficient coarse-to-fine non-local (ECF-NL) module designed to improve the detection accuracy of small objects while minimizing computational overhead. Our approach leverages a coarse-grained global attention mechanism to rapidly identify regions of interest likely to contain small objects, followed by a fine-grained non-local operation within these regions to capture subtle contextual cues. This hierarchical attention strategy reduces the computational cost associated with traditional non-local methods. We evaluate the ECF-NL module on benchmark datasets containing small objects, demonstrating significant improvements in detection accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly for objects occupying a small percentage of the image. The results show that the ECF-NL module effectively enhances feature representations for small objects, leading to more robust and accurate detection. The proposed module offers a practical solution for small object detection in resource-constrained environments.

Keywords: Small Object Detection, Non-Local Module, Attention Mechanism, Computer Vision, Deep Learning.
