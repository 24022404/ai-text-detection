Accurate estimation of vehicle pose and shape from visual data is a critical challenge in computer vision, with applications in autonomous driving, augmented reality, and robotics. This paper introduces *DirectShape*, a novel framework for direct photometric alignment of 3D shape priors to improve vehicle pose and shape estimation. Unlike traditional methods that rely on sparse keypoints or indirect optimization, *DirectShape* leverages dense photometric consistency between observed images and rendered 3D models, enabling precise alignment under varying lighting and occlusion conditions. The proposed approach integrates a differentiable rendering pipeline with a neural shape prior, allowing end-to-end optimization of pose and shape parameters. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that *DirectShape* achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in both accuracy and robustness. The framework’s ability to handle complex real-world scenarios, such as partial occlusions and dynamic lighting, highlights its practical applicability. This work advances the field by providing a scalable and efficient solution for vehicle pose and shape estimation, with potential extensions to other object categories.  

**Keywords**: 3D reconstruction, photometric alignment, vehicle pose estimation, shape priors, differentiable rendering, computer vision.
