This paper presents NoTeacher, a novel semi-supervised learning approach for the classification of radiology images. The objective is to improve the accuracy of image classification in medical diagnostics, where labeled data is scarce and expensive to obtain. NoTeacher introduces a unique teacher model that deviates from traditional mean-teacher architectures, allowing for more effective knowledge transfer from labeled to unlabeled data. Our method leverages a combination of consistency regularization and self-supervised learning to enable the model to learn from both labeled and unlabeled images. Experimental results demonstrate that NoTeacher outperforms state-of-the-art semi-supervised learning methods on several radiology image classification benchmarks, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and robustness. The key contributions of this research include the development of a novel teacher model, the application of semi-supervised learning to radiology images, and the demonstration of improved performance in real-world medical image classification tasks. This work has important implications for the development of AI-assisted medical imaging diagnostics, enabling more accurate and efficient image analysis with limited labeled data. Key keywords: semi-supervised learning, radiology images, medical image classification, NoTeacher, mean-teacher architecture, consistency regularization, self-supervised learning.
