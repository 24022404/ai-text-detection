This paper addresses the challenge of transferring robot learning from simulation to real-world environments, focusing on scenarios with variable length inputs. The objective is to develop a robust and adaptable framework that enables robots to learn from simulated experiences and effectively apply this knowledge in real-world settings with diverse input sequences. Our approach combines reinforcement learning with domain adaptation techniques, utilizing a novel architecture that can handle variable length inputs and mitigate the sim-to-real gap. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, showing significant improvements in robot performance and adaptability when faced with changing input lengths and environmental conditions. The key findings highlight the importance of incorporating input length variability into the learning process, leading to more reliable and generalizable robot behaviors. This research contributes to the advancement of sim-to-real transfer learning, with potential applications in areas such as robotics, autonomous systems, and human-robot interaction, and paves the way for further exploration of variable length input handling in robot learning, with relevant keywords including reinforcement learning, domain adaptation, sim-to-real transfer, and robot learning.
