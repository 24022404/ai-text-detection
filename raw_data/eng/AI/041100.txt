Intrinsic dimension estimation is a critical task in information technology, particularly in the analysis and interpretation of high-dimensional data commonly encountered in fields such as machine learning, computer vision, and data mining. This paper investigates novel methodologies for accurately estimating the intrinsic dimension—the minimum number of variables needed to represent the underlying structure—of complex datasets. We introduce a data-driven approach that leverages local neighborhood analysis, manifold learning, and dimensionality reduction techniques to provide robust and scalable intrinsic dimension estimates. The proposed method is evaluated against standard benchmark datasets and compared with existing estimation algorithms, demonstrating improved accuracy, computational efficiency, and resilience to noise and outliers. Our findings indicate that the method effectively uncovers the true dimensionality of both synthetic and real-world datasets, facilitating better data visualization, feature selection, and algorithmic performance in downstream tasks. The research contributes a significant advancement to unsupervised learning and exploratory data analysis, offering practical tools for data scientists and engineers to better characterize, interpret, and process high-dimensional data. Key terms: intrinsic dimension, dimensionality reduction, manifold learning, data analysis, high-dimensional data.
