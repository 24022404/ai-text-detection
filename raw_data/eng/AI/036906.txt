This paper introduces Conditionally Adaptive Multi-Task Learning, a novel approach to transfer learning in Natural Language Processing (NLP) that achieves improved performance with fewer parameters and less training data. The objective is to address the limitations of existing multi-task learning methods, which often require large amounts of data and parameters to achieve optimal results. Our approach employs a conditional adaptation mechanism that selectively adapts pre-trained models to specific tasks, leveraging shared knowledge across tasks while minimizing parameter overhead. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art transfer learning techniques, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and efficiency. Key findings include a reduction of up to 30% in required parameters and a decrease of up to 25% in training data needs, without sacrificing performance. The proposed approach has important implications for NLP applications, enabling more efficient and effective transfer learning in resource-constrained settings. Our research contributes to the development of more adaptable and data-efficient NLP models, with potential applications in areas such as language translation, sentiment analysis, and text classification, using techniques like transformer-based architectures and fine-tuning.
