This paper introduces FIERY, a novel approach to predict future instances in bird's-eye view from surround monocular cameras. The objective is to forecast the trajectory of objects, such as pedestrians, cars, and cyclists, in a 3D environment, enabling enhanced autonomous driving capabilities. Our method leverages a combination of computer vision and machine learning techniques, utilizing a surround-view camera system to capture a 360-degree view of the environment. We propose a deep learning architecture that predicts future instances by learning spatial and temporal relationships between objects. The results show significant improvements in prediction accuracy and robustness compared to existing methods. Our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets, demonstrating its potential for real-world autonomous driving applications. The key contributions of this work include the introduction of a novel camera configuration, a robust prediction model, and a comprehensive evaluation framework. This research has important implications for the development of autonomous vehicles, robotics, and smart cities, with potential applications in traffic forecasting, collision avoidance, and intelligent transportation systems. Key keywords: autonomous driving, bird's-eye view, future instance prediction, monocular cameras, computer vision, machine learning.
