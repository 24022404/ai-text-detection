The study explores the novel application of deep neural networks (DNNs) as Locality Sensitive Hash (LSH) functions in the context of manifold learning. This research addresses the challenge of efficiently mapping high-dimensional data onto lower-dimensional spaces while preserving local data structures.

Methods/Approach: The proposed method leverages the unique architecture of deep neural networks to perform manifold learning through LSH-like behavior. We design a DNN structure that iteratively learns to cluster data points based on their inherent geometrical proximity. The network is trained using a supervised learning approach where the objective is to minimize the discrepancy between the input space and the output hashed representations.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our approach to using DNNs as LSH functions captures the underlying manifold structures more effectively than traditional LSH methods. Performance evaluations across various high-dimensional datasets indicate significant improvements in both the accuracy and computational efficiency of data retrieval tasks. Comparisons with existing manifold learning techniques highlight the superior ability of our model to generalize across different data distributions.

Conclusion/Implications: This research provides a promising new perspective on the role of deep learning in manifold learning. By incorporating the principles of LSH within neural networks, we offer a solution that improves data representation and retrieval tasks. The findings open avenues for further research into hybrid models that utilize deep learning for other hashing-based applications. Potential applications of this approach include pattern recognition, image retrieval, and large-scale data analysis. 

Keywords: Deep Neural Networks, Locality Sensitive Hashing, Manifold Learning, Data Retrieval, High-Dimensional Data, Machine Learning, Data Representation.
