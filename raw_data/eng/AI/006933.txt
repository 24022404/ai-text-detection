This paper introduces *YuruGAN*, a novel framework for generating *yuru-chara* mascot designs using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) tailored for small datasets. *Yuru-chara*, or regional mascots, are culturally significant in Japan, but their design process often requires significant creativity and time. To address this, we propose a GAN-based approach that leverages clustering techniques to enhance the quality and diversity of generated mascots, even with limited training data. Our method involves preprocessing a small dataset of *yuru-chara* images, applying clustering to group similar designs, and training a GAN model to generate new mascots that preserve the unique characteristics of each cluster. Experimental results demonstrate that *YuruGAN* outperforms traditional GAN models in terms of visual quality, diversity, and cultural relevance, as validated by both quantitative metrics and user evaluations. The framework’s ability to produce high-quality designs from small datasets makes it a valuable tool for designers and local governments seeking to create culturally authentic mascots efficiently. This research contributes to the growing field of AI-driven creative design, offering a scalable solution for generating culturally significant artwork with limited resources.  

**Keywords**: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), *Yuru-chara*, small dataset, clustering, mascot design, AI-driven creativity.
