**Abstract:**  
Accurate relative pose estimation between RGB-D scans in scenes with minimal or non-overlapping views remains a significant challenge in computer vision and robotics. This paper introduces a novel approach, Extreme Relative Pose Estimation via Scene Completion, that tackles the problem of aligning RGB-D scans under conditions of severe viewpoint disparity, partial occlusion, and limited shared content. Our method leverages a deep learning-based scene completion model to generate plausible reconstructions of occluded or missing regions from each RGB-D scan, effectively synthesizing imaginary overlap and context. The completed scenes are then used to estimate the relative pose by matching geometric and semantic cues, improving robustness in extreme cases where traditional feature-based or direct registration methods fail. Extensive experiments on publicly available RGB-D datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms prior methods in terms of accuracy and success rate, particularly in scenarios with less than 10% overlap. These results highlight the potential of scene completion as an enabling technology for reliable pose estimation in challenging real-world conditions, with applications in SLAM, 3D mapping, and autonomous navigation. Key keywords: relative pose estimation, RGB-D, scene completion, deep learning, 3D reconstruction, low-overlap registration.
