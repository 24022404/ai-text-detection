This paper addresses the challenge of explaining groups of points in low-dimensional representations, a crucial task in understanding complex data structures. Our objective is to develop a methodology that can identify and interpret clusters of points in reduced dimensional spaces, enhancing the explainability and transparency of dimensionality reduction techniques. We propose a novel approach that leverages a combination of geometric and statistical methods to detect and characterize groups of points in low-dimensional representations. Our method utilizes a density-based clustering algorithm and a feature attribution technique to provide insights into the relationships between data points and their corresponding low-dimensional embeddings. The results show that our approach can effectively identify meaningful groups of points and provide interpretable explanations for their formation. Our research contributes to the development of more transparent and trustworthy dimensionality reduction methods, with potential applications in data visualization, anomaly detection, and feature selection. Key keywords: dimensionality reduction, explainability, clustering, low-dimensional representations, feature attribution.
