This paper investigates the training of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) using word observations, aiming to enhance the modeling of textual data for natural language processing applications. Traditional RBMs, widely used for unsupervised feature learning, typically operate on binary or real-valued input data, which limits their direct applicability to text, where observations are word occurrences or counts. To address this limitation, we propose a modified RBM framework capable of effectively representing discrete word data by adjusting the energy function and sampling procedures to accommodate categorical inputs. We describe the mathematical formulation and training methodology, including an adaptation of contrastive divergence for word-based datasets. Experimental results on benchmark text corpora demonstrate that our word-based RBM significantly improves the quality of learned word representations and document clustering performance compared to standard binary RBMs. The findings highlight the model’s ability to capture semantic relationships and latent structures within text. This work contributes a novel approach for leveraging RBMs in natural language processing tasks, opening possibilities for more effective unsupervised learning on large-scale textual data. Keywords: Restricted Boltzmann Machine, word observations, text modeling, natural language processing, unsupervised learning, document representation.
