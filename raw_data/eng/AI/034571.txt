This paper investigates the theoretical and practical aspects of transformations between deep neural networks (DNNs), focusing on the feasibility and implications of converting one network architecture into another. The objective is to explore whether such transformations can preserve or enhance model performance, reduce computational overhead, or enable interoperability across different architectures. We propose a systematic approach leveraging mathematical mappings and optimization techniques to facilitate these transformations, ensuring minimal loss of information and maintaining functional equivalence. Our experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method across various architectures, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformers, with results showing comparable or improved performance metrics post-transformation. The findings reveal that such transformations are not only feasible but also offer practical benefits in scenarios like model compression, cross-platform deployment, and architecture migration. This research contributes to the broader understanding of DNN interoperability and provides a foundation for future work on efficient model adaptation and reuse. Key insights include the potential for reducing training costs and enabling seamless integration of diverse AI systems. Keywords: deep neural networks, model transformation, architecture conversion, optimization, interoperability, AI systems.
