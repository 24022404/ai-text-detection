Meta-reinforcement learning (meta-RL) seeks to equip agents with the ability to rapidly adapt to new tasks using prior experience, yet standardized benchmarks for evaluating structured task learning and adaptation are limited. This paper introduces Alchemy, a novel platform designed to provide a highly structured and compositional set of tasks for meta-reinforcement learning research. Alchemy presents agents with a series of environments characterized by latent rules and shifting contingencies, requiring nuanced exploration, inference, and flexible decision-making. We detail the architecture of the Alchemy task suite, describing its capacity to sample procedurally generated scenarios with controllable complexity and systematic variation in underlying task structure. Experimental results demonstrate that Alchemy poses significant challenges to existing meta-RL algorithms, revealing distinct strengths and limitations in their abilities to model uncertainty, transfer knowledge, and generalize compositional task elements. Additionally, we compare agent performance to human strategies, highlighting gaps in current meta-RL methods. The Alchemy suite thus offers a rigorous and extensible benchmark for advancing the development and assessment of meta-learning architectures. Our contributions underscore the importance of structured task distributions in benchmarking progress towards more generalizable and robust reinforcement learning agents.

Keywords: meta-reinforcement learning, structured tasks, generalization, benchmark, task compositionality, exploration, adaptation, artificial intelligence.
