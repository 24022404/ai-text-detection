This paper introduces GTNet, a novel Generative Transfer Network designed to tackle the challenging task of zero-shot object detection. The objective is to enable detectors to recognize objects from unseen classes without requiring any prior training data. To achieve this, GTNet employs a generative approach, leveraging a transfer learning framework to learn a shared feature space between seen and unseen classes. The method utilizes a combination of generative adversarial networks and transfer learning to synthesize features for unseen classes, allowing the detector to generalize to new objects. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of GTNet, outperforming state-of-the-art zero-shot object detection methods on benchmark datasets. The key findings highlight the importance of generative modeling in zero-shot learning, enabling the detection of objects from unseen classes with significant improvements in accuracy. GTNet contributes to the advancement of zero-shot object detection, paving the way for applications in autonomous systems, surveillance, and robotics, where detecting unseen objects is crucial. Key keywords: zero-shot object detection, generative transfer network, transfer learning, generative adversarial networks, computer vision, object recognition.
