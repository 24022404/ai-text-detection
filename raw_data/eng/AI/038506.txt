Graph contrastive learning (GCL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning representations of graph-structured data, leveraging self-supervised techniques to capture structural and semantic information. However, existing GCL methods often struggle with robustness, particularly in the presence of noisy or incomplete graph data. This paper addresses this challenge by proposing a novel framework, Robust Graph Contrastive Learning (RGCL), designed to enhance the resilience and generalization capabilities of graph representation learning. Our approach introduces a multi-view augmentation strategy that generates diverse graph views while preserving essential structural properties, coupled with a noise-robust contrastive loss function to mitigate the impact of data imperfections. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that RGCL outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of robustness and accuracy across various downstream tasks, including node classification and link prediction. The results highlight the framework's ability to maintain performance under adversarial conditions and noisy inputs. This work contributes to advancing the field of graph representation learning by providing a more reliable and adaptable solution for real-world applications, where data quality is often suboptimal.  

**Keywords**: Graph Contrastive Learning, Robustness, Self-Supervised Learning, Graph Representation Learning, Noise Resilience.
