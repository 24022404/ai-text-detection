Deep neural networks have achieved remarkable success across various domains, yet their complex architectures often result in limited interpretability, hindering broader adoption and trust in critical applications. This paper presents a novel divide-and-conquer approach for understanding the internal mechanisms of deep networks. Our method systematically decomposes deep models into smaller, functionally coherent modules using a combination of hierarchical clustering and feature attribution techniques. By independently analyzing these submodules, we provide intuitive visualizations and quantitative insights into their specific contributions to overall network predictions. Experimental results on image classification and natural language processing benchmarks demonstrate our approach’s effectiveness in revealing latent structures and decision pathways within widely used deep architectures, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformers. Compared to existing black-box interpretability methods, our divide-and-conquer framework achieves higher explanatory power while maintaining scalability to large models. This research contributes a scalable and generalizable strategy for dissecting deep networks, empowering researchers and practitioners to better interpret, debug, and optimize AI systems. Keywords: deep learning, neural network interpretability, divide-and-conquer, model decomposition, explainable AI.
