This paper introduces a novel logic-based approach to generatively defined discriminative modeling, aiming to bridge the gap between generative and discriminative methodologies. We propose a framework where logical rules, expressed in a probabilistic logic, define the generative process. These rules explicitly model the dependencies between features and class labels. The inference is performed by learning the parameters of the probabilistic logic program, which subsequently informs a discriminative classifier. Our approach leverages the expressiveness of logic to encode prior knowledge and constraints, resulting in more interpretable and robust models. We evaluate our method on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to traditional discriminative models, especially in scenarios with limited training data and noisy features. The results highlight the potential of logic-based generative modeling to enhance discriminative classification. This research contributes to the field of machine learning by providing a new perspective on combining the strengths of generative and discriminative paradigms. Keywords: Discriminative Modeling, Generative Models, Probabilistic Logic Programming, Machine Learning, Classification, Knowledge Representation.
