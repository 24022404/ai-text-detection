Semantic image synthesis, a critical task in computer vision, aims to generate realistic images from semantic layouts. While recent advancements have shown promising results, challenges remain in achieving high-quality outputs with computational efficiency. This paper introduces a novel approach leveraging Class-Adaptive Normalization (CAN), a technique designed to enhance the synthesis process by dynamically adjusting normalization parameters based on semantic class information. Our method integrates CAN into a generative adversarial network (GAN) framework, enabling fine-grained control over image generation while reducing computational overhead. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of both image quality and inference speed. Quantitative evaluations reveal significant improvements in metrics such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and structural similarity (SSIM), while qualitative results highlight enhanced visual fidelity and detail preservation. The findings underscore the effectiveness of class-adaptive normalization in semantic image synthesis, offering a scalable solution for applications in virtual environments, augmented reality, and content creation. This work contributes to the field by introducing a computationally efficient and adaptable framework that bridges the gap between realism and performance in image synthesis tasks.  

**Keywords**: Semantic image synthesis, Class-Adaptive Normalization, GAN, computer vision, image generation, computational efficiency.
