This paper addresses the challenge of sample efficiency in reinforcement learning (RL) within contextual decision processes (CDP). We investigate model-based RL strategies, focusing on learning an explicit model of the environment to improve decision-making. Our primary contribution is the derivation of Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) bounds for model-based RL in CDPs, demonstrating a significant reduction in sample complexity compared to model-free approaches. Specifically, we prove that under certain conditions, model-based methods achieve exponential improvements in sample efficiency. We introduce a novel algorithm that leverages context to learn an accurate environment model, enabling faster policy optimization. Empirical evaluations on benchmark CDP tasks validate our theoretical findings, showcasing the superior performance and sample efficiency of our model-based approach compared to state-of-the-art model-free algorithms. These results highlight the potential of model-based RL for solving complex decision-making problems with limited data.
Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Model-based RL, Contextual Decision Processes, PAC Bounds, Sample Complexity, Markov Decision Processes.
