The objective of this research is to address the complexity and opacity of deep learning models applied to multiscale systems by leveraging mutual information to enhance model interpretability. Our approach integrates mutual information techniques to identify and elucidate the relationships between input variables and model predictions, providing a clearer understanding of how these inputs impact outcomes across different scales. Through the implementation of our method, we utilized a combination of scalable deep learning architectures and mutual information metrics to extract insights on feature importance and predictive contributions. The results indicate that our approach significantly improves the explainability of deep learning models, offering a more transparent view of feature interactions within multiscale systems. We benchmark our method against traditional explainability techniques and demonstrate superior performance in terms of clarity and insight generation. The conclusions of this study highlight the potential for applying mutual information to unlock advanced interpretability in deeply complex models, thus contributing to more trustable AI systems. Our findings present valuable implications for fields reliant on multiscale modeling, such as environmental science, material engineering, and bioinformatics, where understanding the underlying decision-making processes is crucial. Key keywords in this study include mutual information, explainable AI, deep learning, multiscale systems, and model interpretability.
