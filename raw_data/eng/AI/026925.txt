Duplicate data removal is a critical step in various information technology applications, including data preprocessing, information retrieval, and database management, to ensure data quality and efficiency. Traditional duplicate detection methods often rely on fixed heuristics or pairwise comparisons, which can struggle to capture complex dependencies within sequences of data records. This paper introduces a novel approach, Sequential Context Encoding (SCE), for enhancing duplicate removal by leveraging contextual relationships among data entries. The proposed method utilizes deep sequence modeling techniques—specifically, a combination of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and attention mechanisms—to encode the contextual information present in data sequences. Extensive experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets from multiple domains, demonstrating that our model significantly outperforms classic rule-based and popular machine learning approaches in both precision and recall. Additionally, our approach shows robustness in handling large-scale datasets with noisy and heterogeneous data characteristics. The results highlight the effectiveness of sequential context encoding in resolving ambiguities that arise in complex or non-trivial duplicate scenarios. This research advances the state-of-the-art in duplicate removal and presents practical implications for data integration, knowledge graph construction, and large-scale data cleaning systems. Keywords: duplicate removal, sequential context encoding, deep learning, data cleaning, attention mechanism, information retrieval.
