Transmitting a de Bruijn sequence of order k via satellite presents unique challenges, especially when continuous positioning and the ability to quickly locate any length-k subsequence are required. Conventional approaches rely on techniques such as linear feedback shift registers, on-off modulation, and substantial look-up tables. However, these methods come with notable limitations, including dependence on primitive polynomials, a suboptimal coding rate of 0.5, and the exponential growth of complexity as k increases. In response to these constraints, chapter 4 introduces an innovative encoding method based on the FKM algorithm, which stands out for its ability to generate each symbol in constant amortized time. Complementing this is a novel decoder, which significantly enhances the existing algorithm by Kociumaka, Radoszewski, and Rytter, allowing for much faster and more efficient positioning of subsequences. This improvement not only outperforms traditional look-up table methods but also contributes to the practical feasibility of using de Bruijn sequences in satellite communication systems.
