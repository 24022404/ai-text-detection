This paper introduces ExtremeWeather, a comprehensive large-scale climate dataset designed to facilitate semi-supervised detection, localization, and understanding of extreme weather events. The objective is to provide a robust foundation for developing and evaluating machine learning models capable of accurately identifying and analyzing severe weather conditions. Our approach involves curating a diverse dataset that encompasses a wide range of extreme weather events, including hurricanes, wildfires, floods, and droughts, leveraging satellite imagery, sensor data, and climate models. The dataset is characterized by its large-scale, high-resolution, and multi-modal nature, making it an ideal resource for training and testing deep learning architectures. Our results demonstrate the effectiveness of ExtremeWeather in enabling state-of-the-art performance in extreme weather event detection and localization tasks. The key findings highlight the importance of leveraging large-scale climate datasets and semi-supervised learning techniques for improving the accuracy and reliability of extreme weather event prediction systems. The contributions of this research have significant implications for climate monitoring, disaster response, and environmental sustainability, with potential applications in fields such as meteorology, emergency management, and ecological conservation. Key keywords: extreme weather events, semi-supervised learning, climate dataset, machine learning, satellite imagery, climate modeling.
