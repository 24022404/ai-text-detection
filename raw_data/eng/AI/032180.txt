Objective: Graph representation learning (GRL) methods—including graph neural networks (GNNs) and embedding techniques—have demonstrated strong performance on benchmark tasks, yet their practical adoption is hindered by unresolved computational, data-related, and interpretability challenges. This study aims to systematically quantify these obstacles to guide both researchers and practitioners.  
Methods: We introduce a unified benchmarking framework that evaluates leading GRL models (e.g., GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT, and node2vec) across diverse real-world datasets drawn from social networks, biological interactions, citation graphs, and e-commerce relations. Our framework measures scalability (runtime and memory consumption), robustness (sensitivity to noise and domain shifts), and interpretability (feature attribution stability), employing standardized protocols for hyperparameter tuning and edge sampling.  
Results: Experimental results reveal substantial trade-offs: GNN-based approaches achieve superior predictive accuracy but incur quadratic growth in memory usage on high-density graphs, while shallow embedding methods scale more efficiently yet degrade sharply under node‐feature perturbations. We further show that skewed degree distributions lead to unstable learned representations, and that minor hyperparameter variations can cause up to 15% drop in downstream performance.  
Conclusion: By quantifying key limitations of current GRL techniques, our study delivers actionable insights on model selection, data preprocessing, and resource allocation. We outline best practices for mitigating scalability bottlenecks and improving robustness, and we highlight open research directions—such as dynamic graph handling and explainable GRL—to accelerate real-world deployment.  
Keywords: graph representation learning, graph neural networks, scalability, robustness, interpretability, benchmarking.
