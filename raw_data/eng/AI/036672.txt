This paper introduces a novel approach for generating 3D visualizations from video frame sequences. The primary objective is to reconstruct a 3D representation of dynamic scenes captured in video, addressing the limitations of traditional multi-view stereo and structure-from-motion techniques when applied to monocular video. Our method employs a deep learning framework that combines convolutional neural networks for feature extraction with a differentiable rendering module to iteratively refine the 3D structure. Specifically, we train a model to predict depth maps from individual frames and subsequently fuse these depth maps into a consistent 3D point cloud using a learned volumetric representation. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of reconstruction accuracy and visual fidelity compared to existing methods. The resulting 3D visualizations exhibit improved temporal coherence and reduced artifacts, enabling more realistic and immersive experiences. This research contributes a robust and efficient solution for 3D video understanding, with potential applications in virtual reality, augmented reality, and autonomous navigation.

Keywords: 3D reconstruction, video processing, deep learning, computer vision, differentiable rendering.
