This paper addresses the challenge of policy tracking in reinforcement learning (RL) under dynamically changing environments. The objective is to develop a theoretical framework that ensures stable and efficient adaptation of policies when the underlying environment evolves over time. We introduce an adiabatic theorem tailored for Temporal Difference (TD) learning, a widely used RL algorithm, to guarantee convergence of policy updates under slow environmental changes. Our approach leverages the adiabatic property, which ensures that the learning process remains close to the optimal policy trajectory as the environment transitions. We provide a rigorous mathematical analysis of the proposed theorem, demonstrating its applicability to both discrete and continuous state-action spaces. Experimental results on benchmark RL environments validate the effectiveness of our method, showing improved policy tracking performance compared to traditional TD-learning approaches. The findings highlight the robustness of the adiabatic framework in maintaining policy stability and adaptability, offering significant implications for real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous systems, and dynamic resource allocation. This work contributes a novel theoretical foundation for policy tracking in RL, bridging the gap between theoretical guarantees and practical implementation in dynamic settings.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Temporal Difference Learning, Adiabatic Theorem, Policy Tracking, Dynamic Environments, Convergence Analysis.
