Out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization poses a significant challenge in reinforcement learning (RL), where agents often fail to perform well in environments that differ from those seen during training. This paper addresses the problem of learning effective representations that enable RL agents to generalize to novel, unseen scenarios. We propose a representation learning framework that explicitly disentangles environment-invariant features from environment-specific noise using a combination of contrastive learning and domain adversarial training. Our approach is evaluated on a suite of benchmark RL tasks where the test environments include systematic variations not present in the training environments. Experimental results demonstrate that agents utilizing the learned representations consistently outperform baseline methods in OOD generalization, achieving higher cumulative rewards and more stable policy performance. The findings highlight the importance of robust representation learning in enhancing RL agent adaptability. Our methodology offers a promising avenue for deploying RL systems in dynamic real-world settings where environmental shifts are inevitable. Key contributions include a novel unsupervised feature extraction strategy for RL, extensive empirical validation, and insights into the mechanisms that drive generalization beyond the training distribution. Keywords: reinforcement learning, out-of-distribution, generalization, representation learning, domain adaptation, contrastive learning.
