Zero imputation, a common strategy for handling missing data in neural network training, can introduce significant bias, particularly when data sparsity is high. This paper investigates the detrimental effects of zero imputation on model performance and proposes a novel correction method. We demonstrate that replacing missing values with zeros distorts the underlying data distribution, leading to suboptimal learning and compromised generalization capabilities. To mitigate this sparsity bias, we introduce a data-driven adjustment technique that recalibrates imputed values based on observed data patterns. Our approach adaptively estimates and compensates for the distortions caused by zero imputation, resulting in improved accuracy and robustness. Experiments conducted on diverse datasets with varying degrees of sparsity demonstrate that our correction method consistently outperforms zero imputation and other standard imputation techniques. These findings highlight the importance of carefully addressing missing data in neural network training and provide a practical solution for enhancing model performance in sparse data scenarios. Keywords: Zero Imputation, Missing Data, Neural Networks, Sparsity Bias, Data Imputation, Machine Learning.
