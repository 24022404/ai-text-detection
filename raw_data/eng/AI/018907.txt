The objective of this study is to address the challenge of synthesizing dynamic talking face animations by developing a novel method called FACIAL (Facial Animation with Implicit Attribute Learning). The research seeks to improve the realism and coherence of facial animations generated from audio inputs by incorporating implicit learning of facial attributes.

Methods/Approach: FACIAL utilizes an advanced deep learning framework that integrates audio-visual synchronization models with implicit attribute learning mechanisms. The approach leverages generative adversarial networks (GANs) combined with attention mechanisms to dynamically capture and reproduce subtle facial expressions and movements contextual to the audio input. The system optimizes attribute learning without direct supervision, allowing for realistic and personalized talking face synthesis.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that FACIAL generates high-quality talking face animations that surpass existing methods in terms of visual realism and lip-sync accuracy. Comparative evaluations against state-of-the-art techniques show significant improvements in preserving facial attributes and expression details across different speaking styles and expressions. User study results indicate increased viewer satisfaction and engagement with animations produced by FACIAL.

Conclusion/Implications: This study introduces a robust framework for synthesizing dynamic talking faces, contributing to advancements in virtual reality, human-computer interaction, and digital communication systems. By achieving superior synthesis quality through implicit attribute learning, FACIAL has the potential to enhance applications in entertainment, gaming, and virtual social platforms, offering more immersive user experiences. Key innovations include the integration of implicit learning mechanisms and improved modeling of facial dynamics.

Keywords: Dynamic talking face, facial animation, implicit attribute learning, GANs, deep learning, audio-visual synchronization.
