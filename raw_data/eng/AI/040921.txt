This paper presents a supervised learning approach for accurate crop-weed classification using color and texture features extracted from agricultural field images. The objective is to develop an efficient method for distinguishing between crops and weeds, enabling targeted and precise weed control. We employ a combination of color-based features, derived from different color spaces (RGB, HSV, Lab), and texture-based features extracted using Gray-Level Co-occurrence Matrices (GLCM). These features are then used to train several supervised learning classifiers, including Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest, and k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN). Experimental results on a diverse dataset of crop and weed images demonstrate that the Random Forest classifier achieves the highest accuracy, surpassing 95% in distinguishing crop from weed. The proposed method offers a robust and effective solution for automated weed detection, leading to reduced herbicide use and improved crop yields. Keywords: Crop-weed classification, supervised learning, color features, texture features, Random Forest, precision agriculture.
