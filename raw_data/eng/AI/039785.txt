Pedestrian detection is a critical task in computer vision, with applications ranging from autonomous driving to surveillance systems. Despite significant advancements, challenges such as occlusions, varying scales, and complex backgrounds persist. This paper introduces SSA-CNN (Semantic Self-Attention Convolutional Neural Network), a novel framework designed to enhance pedestrian detection accuracy by integrating semantic context and self-attention mechanisms. The proposed approach leverages a dual-path architecture: one path focuses on extracting spatial features through convolutional layers, while the other employs a self-attention module to capture long-range dependencies and contextual relationships within the image. By combining these pathways, SSA-CNN effectively addresses the limitations of traditional CNNs in handling occluded and small-scale pedestrians. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, such as Caltech and CityPersons, demonstrate that SSA-CNN outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving higher precision and recall rates. The results highlight the effectiveness of semantic self-attention in improving detection robustness, particularly in challenging scenarios. This research contributes to the field by providing a scalable and efficient solution for real-world pedestrian detection systems, with potential applications in smart cities and autonomous vehicles.  

**Keywords**: Pedestrian detection, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Self-attention, Semantic context, Computer vision, Autonomous systems.
