This paper investigates the sample complexity of episodic fixed-horizon reinforcement learning, a crucial aspect of decision-making and control in complex environments. The objective is to understand how the number of samples required to achieve a desired level of performance scales with the horizon length and other problem parameters. We employ a model-based approach, leveraging techniques from statistical learning theory to derive bounds on the sample complexity of various reinforcement learning algorithms. Our results show that the sample complexity grows polynomially with the horizon length, and we identify key factors influencing this growth, including the transition dynamics and reward structure. The findings have significant implications for the design of efficient reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly in applications where data collection is costly or time-consuming. Our work contributes to the understanding of the fundamental limits of reinforcement learning and provides insights into the development of more sample-efficient methods, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and other domains. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, sample complexity, episodic fixed-horizon, model-based learning, statistical learning theory.
