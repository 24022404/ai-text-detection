Electronic Health Records (EHRs) contain a wealth of information for improving healthcare delivery and research. However, their high dimensionality and complex structure pose significant challenges for effective analysis. This paper investigates the use of autoencoders, a class of neural networks for unsupervised representation learning, to extract meaningful features from EHR data. We comparatively evaluate several autoencoder architectures, including vanilla, sparse, and variational autoencoders, on real-world EHR datasets. The models are assessed based on their ability to reconstruct input data and their performance on downstream tasks such as patient classification and risk prediction. Results demonstrate that variational autoencoders achieve superior performance in capturing the underlying data distribution, leading to improved feature representations and enhanced predictive accuracy compared to other autoencoder variants and traditional methods. This study highlights the potential of autoencoders for unlocking valuable insights from EHR data, facilitating more informed clinical decision-making and personalized healthcare. Keywords: Electronic Health Records, Autoencoders, Representation Learning, Machine Learning, Healthcare, Deep Learning.
