Neural Architecture Search (NAS) automates the design of neural networks, but its computational cost remains a significant challenge. This paper investigates how the supernet, a large, over-parameterized network containing many subnetworks, facilitates efficient NAS. We analyze the training dynamics of supernets and demonstrate that weight sharing and joint training enable rapid evaluation of candidate architectures. Our approach involves training a supernet using a novel sampling strategy that promotes exploration of diverse architectures and improves subnetwork ranking correlation. We evaluate the performance of architectures searched within the trained supernet on image classification tasks using CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets. Results show that our supernet-based NAS achieves comparable or superior performance to state-of-the-art NAS methods while significantly reducing computational cost. The findings highlight the critical role of the supernet in enabling efficient and effective neural architecture search, providing insights for future NAS algorithm development.
**Keywords:** Neural Architecture Search, NAS, Supernet, Weight Sharing, Deep Learning, Architecture Optimization.
