Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been widely adopted for sequential data processing, but they often struggle with long-term dependencies due to vanishing gradients and memory limitations. This paper introduces **RotLSTM**, a novel RNN architecture that incorporates a rotating memory mechanism to enhance information retention and retrieval over extended sequences. The **objective** of this research is to address the challenges of long-term dependency in traditional RNNs by dynamically rotating and updating memory states, ensuring more efficient utilization of memory resources. The proposed **method** integrates a rotating memory module into the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) framework, enabling the network to selectively preserve and discard information based on its relevance. Experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets for sequential tasks, including language modeling and time-series prediction. The **results** demonstrate that RotLSTM outperforms traditional LSTM and other state-of-the-art RNN variants in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly for tasks requiring long-term context. The **conclusion** highlights the potential of RotLSTM to advance applications in natural language processing, speech recognition, and other domains reliant on sequential data analysis. This work contributes a novel approach to memory management in RNNs, offering a scalable solution for handling long-term dependencies.  

**Keywords**: Recurrent Neural Networks, LSTM, rotating memory, sequential data, long-term dependencies, memory management.
