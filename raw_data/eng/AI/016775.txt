Robustness to variations in object position, orientation, and scale remains a critical challenge in computer vision and image classification tasks. This paper introduces SI-Score, a novel image dataset meticulously designed for the fine-grained analysis of model sensitivity to object location, rotation, and size. The SI-Score dataset comprises high-resolution images featuring systematically varied objects across multiple classes, allowing controlled evaluation of robustness in state-of-the-art recognition systems. We detail the dataset construction process, including object selection, manipulation protocols, and annotation standards, ensuring reproducibility and consistency. Through comprehensive benchmarking with leading convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and vision transformers, we demonstrate that existing models exhibit significant performance degradation under controlled spatial transformations. Our analyses provide quantitative metrics, enabling the direct comparison of model robustness and facilitating the diagnosis of failure modes. SI-Score serves as a valuable resource for advancing research on spatial invariance in computer vision, offering insights for the development and evaluation of more robust algorithms. The dataset and evaluation scripts are made publicly available to support continued progress in the field. Keywords: image dataset, robustness, object location, rotation, size, computer vision, spatial invariance, model benchmarking.
