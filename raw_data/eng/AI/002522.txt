Object segmentation is a fundamental task in computer vision with wide-ranging applications, yet progress remains limited by the scarcity of annotated data for supervised learning. This paper introduces DyStaB, an unsupervised object segmentation framework that leverages a novel dynamic-static bootstrapping approach. DyStaB operates by first distinguishing dynamic regions—objects exhibiting movement—across video frames, then propagates this information to identify static objects using spatiotemporal consistency. The method employs motion cues from optical flow to segment moving entities and self-supervised clustering techniques to transfer learned boundaries to static scene components. Experiments conducted on multiple challenging benchmark datasets demonstrate that DyStaB achieves superior segmentation performance compared to existing unsupervised and weakly-supervised methods, particularly in scenarios with complex object backgrounds and varying motion patterns. The results highlight DyStaB’s robustness and adaptability without the need for manual labels or pre-trained models. This research contributes a scalable and generalizable solution for object segmentation, opening new possibilities for large-scale and label-efficient applications in areas such as video analysis, scene understanding, and autonomous systems. Key keywords include: unsupervised segmentation, object detection, video analysis, dynamic-static bootstrapping, self-supervised learning, motion analysis, and computer vision.
