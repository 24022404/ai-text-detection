Accurate distance estimation in near-field environments is critical for applications such as autonomous driving and robotics, particularly when using surround-view fisheye cameras. This paper introduces *SVDistNet*, a novel self-supervised learning framework designed for near-field distance estimation on fisheye camera systems. Unlike traditional methods that rely on labeled datasets or complex calibration procedures, *SVDistNet* leverages geometric constraints and temporal consistency within fisheye video sequences to train without explicit supervision. The proposed approach integrates a multi-scale feature extraction network with a fisheye-specific distortion-aware module, enabling robust distance prediction even in highly distorted regions. Experimental results demonstrate that *SVDistNet* achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets, outperforming existing supervised and unsupervised methods by a significant margin. The framework also exhibits strong generalization capabilities across diverse environments and camera configurations. This work contributes a practical, scalable solution for near-field perception, reducing dependency on costly annotated data while maintaining high accuracy. Potential applications include advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS), parking assistance, and robotic navigation.  

**Keywords**: fisheye cameras, self-supervised learning, distance estimation, surround-view systems, near-field perception, autonomous driving.
