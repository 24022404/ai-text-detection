**Abstract**

The ability to flexibly modify visual attributes in natural scene images has important applications in fields such as computer graphics, virtual reality, and image editing. This paper addresses the challenge of manipulating specific scene attributes—such as weather, lighting, or seasonal context—through a novel hallucination-based framework. We propose a generative adversarial network (GAN) model designed to perform targeted attribute editing by learning disentangled representations of scene characteristics. The model leverages a conditional latent space in conjunction with semantic guidance to enable precise and realistic attribute manipulations without the need for paired training data. Extensive experiments on benchmark scene datasets demonstrate that our approach can convincingly alter targeted attributes while preserving overall scene consistency and visual realism, outperforming existing image-to-image translation and attribute manipulation methods in both quantitative metrics and human evaluations. These results highlight the effectiveness of hallucination as a tool for fine-grained control in natural scene synthesis. Our framework offers significant potential for interactive image editing, content creation, and data augmentation in computer vision tasks. **Keywords:** scene attribute manipulation, image synthesis, hallucination, generative adversarial networks, image editing, conditional generation.
