This paper introduces a novel approach to imitation learning, termed Third-Person Imitation Learning (TPIL), addressing the limitations of traditional methods that rely on first-person demonstrations. TPIL enables an agent to learn from observations of another agent performing a task, even when the observer's viewpoint differs significantly. We propose a framework that integrates a viewpoint transformation module with a reinforcement learning component, allowing the agent to infer the demonstrator's actions from a third-person perspective. This involves training a neural network to map observed states to the demonstrator's estimated actions, which are then used to guide the agent's policy learning. We evaluate TPIL in simulated robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating its superior performance compared to existing imitation learning techniques, particularly when dealing with substantial viewpoint disparities. Results indicate that TPIL significantly improves learning efficiency and task completion rates. Key contributions include a robust viewpoint adaptation mechanism and its effective integration with imitation learning, paving the way for more versatile and practical robot learning scenarios. Keywords: Imitation Learning, Reinforcement Learning, Third-Person Perspective, Viewpoint Adaptation, Robot Learning.
