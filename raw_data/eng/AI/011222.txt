This survey provides a comprehensive overview of machine learning–based approaches for detecting malicious URLs, a critical challenge in cybersecurity. We first outline the growing threat posed by phishing, malware distribution, and spam URLs, and then describe our systematic review methodology, which covers over 100 peer-reviewed studies from the past decade. We categorize existing work according to feature extraction techniques (e.g., lexical, host-based, content-based, and network-behavioral features) and classification models (including decision trees, random forests, support vector machines, deep neural networks, and ensemble methods). Our analysis identifies key trends: tree-based and ensemble models consistently achieve high detection rates, while deep learning methods are gaining traction for their ability to capture complex URL patterns without extensive feature engineering. We also compare evaluation metrics and benchmark datasets, revealing common challenges such as class imbalance, concept drift, and the need for real-time processing. Finally, we highlight open research directions, including adversarial robustness, explainable detection models, and lightweight on-device implementations. By synthesizing current practices and pinpointing gaps, this survey offers researchers and practitioners a clear roadmap for advancing machine learning solutions in malicious URL detection.  
Keywords: malicious URL detection, machine learning, feature engineering, classification algorithms, survey.
