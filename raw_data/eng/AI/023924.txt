This paper introduces a novel approach to learning navigation costs from demonstration using semantic observations. The objective is to enable robots to navigate efficiently in complex environments by learning from human demonstrations without relying on precise geometric maps. Our method utilizes a deep neural network to map raw semantic observations, such as object labels and spatial relationships, directly to navigation cost values. This learned cost function is then used within a path planning algorithm to generate efficient and semantically informed trajectories. We demonstrate that our approach outperforms traditional geometric costmaps and imitation learning techniques in simulated environments. Specifically, our method achieves a 15% reduction in path length and a 20% improvement in navigation success rate compared to baseline approaches. The key contribution is a framework for learning navigation costs from demonstration that effectively leverages semantic scene understanding, enabling robots to plan paths that are both efficient and aligned with human preferences. This approach has the potential to improve robot navigation in unstructured and dynamic environments. Keywords: Robot Navigation, Imitation Learning, Semantic Mapping, Costmap Learning, Deep Learning, Path Planning.
