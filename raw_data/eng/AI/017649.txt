This study investigates human visual perception of synthetic faces, exploring the boundaries between reality and artificiality. Our objective is to understand how individuals distinguish between real and generated faces, and what factors influence their perceptions. We employed a mixed-methods approach, combining psychophysical experiments with machine learning-generated synthetic faces. The results show that participants often struggle to differentiate between real and synthetic faces, with a significant portion of synthetic faces being perceived as more realistic than actual human faces. Our findings have important implications for the development of artificial intelligence, computer vision, and graphics, highlighting the need for more sophisticated methods to detect and generate realistic faces. This research contributes to the ongoing discussion on the potential applications and consequences of synthetic face technology, including its impact on social media, security, and human-computer interaction. Key keywords: synthetic faces, human visual perception, machine learning, artificial intelligence, computer vision, graphics.
