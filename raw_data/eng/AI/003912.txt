**Abstract:**

Generalized zero-shot learning (GZSL) presents a significant challenge in computer vision by requiring models to recognize both seen and unseen classes without prior examples from unseen categories. This paper introduces a novel Two-Level Adversarial Visual-Semantic Coupling (TL-AVSC) framework designed to enhance performance in GZSL tasks. The proposed method employs a dual-layer adversarial architecture: one layer aligns visual features with semantic representations to bridge the modality gap, while the second adversarial layer enforces robust discrimination between seen and unseen classes within the shared embedding space. By jointly optimizing these adversarial objectives, TL-AVSC effectively mitigates the semantic ambiguity and domain bias inherent in conventional zero-shot learning approaches. Extensive experiments on standard GZSL benchmarks demonstrate that TL-AVSC achieves superior accuracy and balanced performance across seen and unseen classes compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. The results highlight the efficacy of two-level coupling in fostering generalized recognition capabilities. This work contributes a robust and scalable solution that advances the state of the art in zero-shot recognition, with broad implications for applications in image classification, semantic learning, and intelligent visual systems. **Keywords:** zero-shot learning, adversarial learning, visual-semantic embedding, domain adaptation, image classification.
