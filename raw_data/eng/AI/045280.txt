Time series data is ubiquitous across various domains, yet extracting meaningful and interpretable representations remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces SOM-VAE, a novel framework that combines the strengths of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Self-Organizing Maps (SOMs) to learn discrete, interpretable representations of time series data. The proposed approach leverages the generative capabilities of VAEs to model complex temporal dependencies while utilizing SOMs to enforce a structured, discrete latent space that enhances interpretability. We evaluate SOM-VAE on multiple real-world time series datasets, demonstrating its ability to outperform existing methods in terms of reconstruction accuracy and clustering performance. Additionally, the discrete representations generated by SOM-VAE provide intuitive insights into the underlying patterns of the data, making it a valuable tool for exploratory analysis and decision-making. The results highlight the framework's potential for applications in anomaly detection, forecasting, and domain-specific interpretability tasks. This work contributes a new perspective on representation learning by bridging the gap between deep generative models and interpretable discrete structures.  

**Keywords**: Time series, Variational Autoencoders, Self-Organizing Maps, Representation Learning, Interpretability, Discrete Latent Space.
