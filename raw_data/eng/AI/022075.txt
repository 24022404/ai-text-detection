This paper introduces a novel approach to hyperspectral dimensionality reduction, addressing the challenge of effectively analyzing high-dimensional hyperspectral images (HSIs) with limited labeled samples. The proposed method aims to enhance the performance of semi-supervised learning by leveraging the spatial and spectral characteristics of the data.

Methods/Approach: We propose the Joint and Progressive Subspace Analysis (JPSA) technique, combined with spatial-spectral manifold alignment, as a robust framework for hyperspectral dimensionality reduction. JPSA progressively identifies relevant subspaces by integrating spatial and spectral information, promoting both discrimination and representation power. The method employs manifold alignment to ensure that the high-dimensional data's spatial and spectral correlations are accurately preserved and utilized in a lower-dimensional representation.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations on widely-used hyperspectral datasets demonstrate that the proposed JPSA method significantly outperforms traditional dimensionality reduction techniques. Our approach effectively enhances classification accuracy with fewer labeled samples, showcasing superior performance in both computational efficiency and accuracy. Comparative analyses highlight the superiority of JPSA in maintaining the intrinsic structure of HSIs while achieving robust classification results.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of JPSA with spatial-spectral manifold alignment presents a significant advancement in hyperspectral image analysis, offering a powerful tool for semi-supervised learning. This research contributes to the field by providing a scalable and effective solution for dimensionality reduction, with potential applications in environmental monitoring, agriculture, and remote sensing. The findings underscore the importance of integrating spatial and spectral data characteristics for improved analysis of complex hyperspectral datasets.

Keywords: JPSA, hyperspectral dimensionality reduction, semi-supervised learning, spatial-spectral manifold alignment, hyperspectral images, high-dimensional data, classification accuracy.
