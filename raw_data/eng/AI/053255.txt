This paper investigates the spectral foundations underlying the widely used word2vec algorithm, a cornerstone in natural language processing for generating dense vector representations of words. The primary objective is to analyze the mechanisms by which word2vec models, particularly the Skip-gram with Negative Sampling (SGNS) variant, implicitly capture semantic relationships through spectral properties of co-occurrence matrices derived from large text corpora. We approach this problem by reformulating word2vec's optimization objective and exploring its connection to matrix factorization techniques, such as Singular Value Decomposition (SVD). Our analysis highlights how the learned embeddings correspond to lower-dimensional representations that preserve principal spectral characteristics of word co-occurrence statistics. Empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets reveal a close alignment between the subspaces spanned by word2vec embeddings and those obtained from explicit spectral decomposition methods, demonstrating that word2vec efficiently approximates spectral features relevant for downstream tasks. Furthermore, we discuss the implications of these findings for enhancing the interpretability, stability, and efficiency of embedding models. The research provides new insights into the theoretical basis of popular word embedding methods and opens pathways for the development of improved representation learning algorithms in natural language processing. Keywords: word2vec, word embeddings, spectral analysis, matrix factorization, NLP, representation learning.
