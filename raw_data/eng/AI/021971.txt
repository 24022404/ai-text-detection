**Abstract**

Reversible image merging is a pivotal process in low-level machine vision, enabling the integration of multiple image regions while preserving the ability to recover original structures. This paper investigates a novel framework for reversible image merging, addressing the challenge of information loss that typically impedes downstream vision tasks such as segmentation, object recognition, and feature extraction. The proposed approach leverages adaptive region-based merging algorithms that utilize feature similarity metrics and boundary preservation techniques to ensure both effectiveness and reversibility. Experimental evaluations are conducted on diverse image datasets, demonstrating that our method achieves high fidelity in image reconstruction after merging and competitive performance in standard low-level vision benchmarks. Comparisons with conventional irreversible merging algorithms reveal our approach’s superiority in maintaining crucial image details and supporting iterative processing. The results highlight significant benefits for applications requiring both compact image representation and lossless analysis capabilities. This work contributes an efficient, information-preserving mechanism for image merging, with direct implications for areas such as image compression, real-time robotic perception, and medical imaging. Key keywords: reversible image merging, low-level machine vision, image segmentation, image reconstruction, region-based algorithms, feature preservation.
