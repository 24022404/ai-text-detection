This paper explores the concept of learnability in the context of the Information Bottleneck (IB) method, a theoretical framework for understanding the trade-off between complexity and accuracy in machine learning models. The objective is to investigate the conditions under which a model can efficiently learn a compressed representation of the input data while preserving relevant information. We propose a novel approach based on a variational approximation of the IB objective, leveraging advances in deep learning to optimize the information bottleneck. Our method, termed Learnable Information Bottleneck (LIB), utilizes a neural network to parameterize the bottleneck and learn a probabilistic representation of the data. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of LIB in achieving a better balance between compression and accuracy compared to existing IB methods. The key findings highlight the importance of learnability in the IB framework, enabling the discovery of informative features and improved generalization performance. This research contributes to the development of more efficient and effective machine learning models, with potential applications in areas such as data compression, feature selection, and transfer learning, and is relevant to keywords such as information theory, deep learning, and representation learning.
