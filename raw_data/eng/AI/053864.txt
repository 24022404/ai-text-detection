Causal discovery from observational data is a challenging problem, particularly in high-dimensional settings where the number of variables exceeds the number of observations. This paper addresses the problem of learning causal structures under the Linear Non-Gaussian Acyclic Model (LiNGAM) when the data is high-dimensional. We propose a novel algorithm, termed Sparse-LiNGAM, that integrates sparse regression techniques with the LiNGAM framework to effectively identify the underlying causal relationships. Specifically, we utilize L1 regularization to enforce sparsity in the estimated causal coefficient matrix, thereby mitigating the risk of overfitting in high-dimensional data. We present theoretical guarantees demonstrating the consistency of Sparse-LiNGAM under certain sparsity conditions. Empirical evaluations on synthetic datasets and benchmark biological datasets show that Sparse-LiNGAM outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and robustness, especially when the number of variables is significantly larger than the number of observations. This research provides a valuable tool for causal inference in complex systems with limited data.
Keywords: Causal Discovery, LiNGAM, High-Dimensional Data, Sparse Regression, L1 Regularization, Causal Inference.
