In the field of machine learning, the scarcity of annotated data and the challenge of domain shifts significantly hinder model generalization and performance. This paper addresses these critical issues by introducing *Extreme Consistency*, a novel framework designed to enhance model robustness and adaptability in low-resource and cross-domain scenarios. The proposed approach leverages self-supervised learning and consistency regularization techniques to maximize the utility of limited labeled data while effectively bridging domain gaps. By enforcing consistency across diverse data augmentations and pseudo-labeling strategies, the framework reduces reliance on extensive annotations and improves generalization across unseen domains. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that *Extreme Consistency* outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both low-resource and domain-shift settings, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and robustness. The findings highlight the framework's potential for real-world applications, particularly in domains where labeled data is scarce or domain adaptation is critical. This research contributes a scalable and efficient solution to two of the most pressing challenges in machine learning, paving the way for more resilient and adaptable AI systems.  

**Keywords**: self-supervised learning, domain adaptation, consistency regularization, low-resource learning, pseudo-labeling, machine learning.
