**Abstract**

Selecting appropriate data sources is a critical challenge in Bayesian learning, where data quality and relevance significantly influence model accuracy and computational efficiency. This paper proposes a novel near-optimal data source selection framework designed to enhance Bayesian learning performance by systematically identifying and prioritizing the most informative and reliable data sources from a heterogeneous collection. Our approach formulates the data selection problem as an optimization task, leveraging information-theoretic measures and probabilistic modeling to assess and rank candidate sources based on their expected contribution to posterior inference. We introduce an efficient algorithm that balances computational complexity with selection quality, achieving near-optimal selection without exhaustive evaluation. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms baseline strategies in terms of predictive accuracy, robustness to noise, and computational resource usage. The proposed framework effectively addresses data redundancy and source uncertainty, leading to more reliable Bayesian inference. These findings suggest substantial benefits for applications in data integration, sensor networks, and large-scale machine learning. This work advances the state of the art in data source selection by enabling scalable, informed, and reliable learning within the Bayesian paradigm.

**Keywords:** Bayesian learning, data source selection, probabilistic modeling, optimization, information theory, machine learning, scalability.
