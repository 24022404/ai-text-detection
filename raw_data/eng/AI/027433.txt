LiDAR segmentation remains a critical challenge in autonomous systems, where precise environmental modeling is key to navigation and obstacle detection. This paper introduces a novel approach utilizing Cylindrical and Asymmetrical 3D Convolution Networks (CA3D-CN) to enhance segmentation accuracy and efficiency in LiDAR point cloud data.

Methods/Approach: The proposed CA3D-CN leverages cylindrical partitioning to better capture geometric features across varying scales and asymmetrical convolutions to adapt to LiDAR’s inherent spatial patterns. Unlike traditional 3D convolutional networks, this architecture efficiently processes large-scale, sparse LiDAR data by focusing computational resources on vital regions, while asymmetrical layers dynamically adjust to local shape variations, improving segmentation precision.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations conducted on standard LiDAR datasets demonstrate that the CA3D-CN outperforms existing state-of-the-art models in terms of segmentation accuracy and processing speed. Key findings include a significant reduction in computational overhead and improved adaptability to complex environments, validated through robust benchmark comparisons.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of cylindrical and asymmetrical convolutions reshapes the landscape of 3D data segmentation by offering enhanced scalability and adaptability, making it ideal for real-time autonomous applications. The research contributes a fresh perspective and methodology that can be applied to various 3D data processing tasks, potentially advancing capabilities in areas such as robotics, self-driving vehicles, and augmented reality.

Keywords: LiDAR segmentation, cylindrical convolution, asymmetrical convolution, 3D convolution networks, autonomous systems, point cloud processing.
