This paper reexamines the role of scene graphs in image captioning, a task that involves generating descriptive text from visual content. The objective is to investigate the effectiveness of incorporating scene graphs into image captioning models, with a focus on improving caption accuracy and relevance. Our approach utilizes a novel graph-based architecture that integrates visual features and semantic relationships extracted from the scene graph. Experimental results demonstrate that our model outperforms state-of-the-art captioning systems, particularly in scenarios where complex object interactions and contextual understanding are crucial. The key findings highlight the importance of structured knowledge representation in image captioning, and our contributions include a new dataset with annotated scene graphs and a robust evaluation framework. This research has significant implications for applications such as image search, visual question answering, and human-computer interaction, and paves the way for further exploration of graph-based models in computer vision and natural language processing, with relevant keywords including scene graphs, image captioning, graph neural networks, and multimodal learning.
