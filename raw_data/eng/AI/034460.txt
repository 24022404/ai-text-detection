This paper introduces NeXtVLAD, a novel neural network architecture designed to efficiently aggregate frame-level features for large-scale video classification. The objective is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of video classification models by developing a robust and scalable feature aggregation method. NeXtVLAD employs a combination of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and a modified VLAD (Vector of Locally Aggregated Descriptors) encoding scheme to extract and aggregate frame-level features. The approach is evaluated on several large-scale video classification benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements in classification accuracy and computational efficiency compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. The key findings highlight the effectiveness of NeXtVLAD in capturing complex temporal relationships and spatial hierarchies in videos. The proposed architecture has important implications for real-world applications, including content-based video retrieval, recommendation systems, and autonomous surveillance. By leveraging NeXtVLAD, researchers and practitioners can develop more accurate and efficient video classification models, enabling better decision-making and improved user experiences. Key keywords: video classification, neural networks, feature aggregation, VLAD, CNNs, large-scale video analysis.
