This paper introduces a novel game-theoretic optimization algorithm based on the Nikaido-Isoda function, leveraging gradient information to enhance convergence and stability. Traditional game-theoretic solvers often struggle with high-dimensional strategy spaces and complex payoff structures. Our approach addresses these limitations by incorporating gradient-based updates derived from the Nikaido-Isoda function, effectively guiding players towards Nash equilibria. We demonstrate the efficacy of our algorithm through a series of experiments on benchmark game problems, including congestion games and network resource allocation, achieving significant improvements in convergence speed and equilibrium quality compared to existing methods like fictitious play and gradient descent ascent. Specifically, our method exhibits superior performance in finding stable Nash equilibria in non-convex games. This work contributes a robust and efficient optimization technique applicable to various domains requiring game-theoretic solutions, such as multi-agent systems and economic modeling.
**Keywords:** Game Theory, Optimization, Nikaido-Isoda Function, Nash Equilibrium, Gradient Descent, Multi-Agent Systems.
