Understanding causal relationships in complex systems is a fundamental challenge in information technology, with significant implications for fields such as healthcare, finance, and recommendation systems. This paper introduces Graph Intervention Networks (GIN), a novel framework for causal effect estimation that leverages the structural properties of graph-based models to identify, represent, and quantify causal dependencies among variables. Our approach utilizes a combination of graph neural networks and intervention modeling techniques to learn the underlying causal graph and estimate treatment effects from observational and experimental data. Through extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets, we demonstrate that GIN outperforms existing baseline methods in accuracy and robustness, particularly in the presence of confounders and non-linear relationships. The results reveal that our method provides improved interpretability and predictive performance for counterfactual inference tasks. This research highlights the potential of integrating graph-based learning with intervention analysis to advance causal inference methodologies, enabling more informed decision-making in complex IT systems. Key keywords: causal inference, graph neural networks, intervention, treatment effect estimation, counterfactual analysis, deep learning.
