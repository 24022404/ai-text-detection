This paper presents a scalable algorithm for anomaly detection that leverages learning-based controlled sensing to optimize detection accuracy and resource efficiency in large-scale information systems. The primary objective is to address the challenges of identifying anomalies within high-dimensional and dynamic data streams, where traditional detection methods may suffer from computational bottlenecks or reduced sensitivity. Our approach integrates machine learning techniques with adaptive sensing strategies, allowing the system to prioritize data sources and allocate sensing resources dynamically based on real-time feedback. The proposed algorithm employs a reinforcement learning framework to learn optimal sensing policies that maximize anomaly detection rates while minimizing computational and data acquisition costs. Experimental evaluations conducted on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms conventional fixed-sensing and random sampling methods in terms of detection accuracy, scalability, and efficiency. The results show that the learning-based controlled sensing not only accelerates the identification of rare events but also adapts to evolving data distributions without manual intervention. This research contributes a novel framework to the fields of anomaly detection, active learning, and intelligent sensing, with potential applications in cybersecurity, industrial monitoring, and large-scale data analytics. Keywords: anomaly detection, scalable algorithm, controlled sensing, machine learning, reinforcement learning, resource optimization.
