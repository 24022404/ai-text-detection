Sign language recognition (SLR) is a critical area of research aimed at bridging communication gaps for the hearing-impaired community. This paper addresses the challenge of accurately recognizing sign language gestures by leveraging pose-based features and advanced deep learning techniques. We propose a novel framework that combines Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) and Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) to model the spatial and temporal dynamics of sign language gestures. The GCN captures the structural relationships between key body joints, while BERT processes sequential pose data to understand temporal dependencies. Our approach eliminates the need for handcrafted features, relying solely on pose estimation data extracted from video sequences. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing approaches in terms of accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the effectiveness of integrating spatial and temporal modeling for SLR tasks. This research contributes to the development of more accessible and efficient communication tools, with potential applications in real-time translation systems and assistive technologies.  

**Keywords**: Sign Language Recognition, Graph Convolutional Networks, BERT, Pose Estimation, Deep Learning, Temporal Modeling.
