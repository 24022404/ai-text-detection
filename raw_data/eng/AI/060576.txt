Multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) addresses the challenge of optimizing multiple, often conflicting objectives simultaneously, a critical requirement in real-world applications such as personalized recommendation systems and resource allocation. This paper investigates the problem of accommodating "picky" customers—users with highly specific and diverse preferences—in MORL frameworks. We propose a novel algorithm that balances exploration and exploitation to efficiently learn policies tailored to such users. Our approach leverages a regret-bound analysis to quantify the trade-offs between competing objectives, ensuring robust performance across diverse user preferences. Through extensive simulations, we demonstrate that our method achieves significantly lower regret compared to existing MORL techniques, particularly in scenarios with high-dimensional preference spaces. Additionally, we analyze the exploration complexity of our algorithm, providing theoretical guarantees on its efficiency in discovering optimal policies. The results highlight the algorithm's ability to adapt to varying user demands while maintaining computational tractability. This work contributes to the advancement of MORL by offering a scalable and user-centric solution for applications requiring fine-grained preference alignment. Key implications include improved customer satisfaction in recommendation systems and enhanced adaptability in dynamic decision-making environments.  

**Keywords**: multi-objective reinforcement learning, regret bound, exploration complexity, personalized recommendation, user preferences, optimization.
