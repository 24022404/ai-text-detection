Reasoning within large, complex theories poses a significant challenge for automated theorem provers. This paper introduces a novel reinforcement learning approach to guide proof search in such environments, circumventing the need for potentially limiting imitation learning strategies. Our method leverages a graph neural network architecture to represent the theoretical landscape and learns a policy to strategically select axioms for forward reasoning. We train the agent using a reward function that incentivizes reaching a proof state without relying on expert demonstrations. Empirical evaluations on challenging theorem proving benchmarks demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing automated theorem provers and imitation learning baselines, achieving a higher success rate and generating shorter proofs. Furthermore, analysis reveals that the learned policy exhibits emergent reasoning strategies, suggesting that our approach can discover novel and efficient proof techniques. This work opens avenues for developing more robust and autonomous reasoning systems capable of tackling complex theoretical problems.

**Keywords:** Theorem Proving, Reinforcement Learning, Graph Neural Networks, Automated Reasoning, Axiom Selection.
