Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for a wide range of graph-based machine learning tasks, including node classification, link prediction, and community detection. However, despite their success, their behavior under certain spectral conditions of input graphs remains underexplored. This paper investigates a simple yet previously uncharacterized spectral failure mode affecting GCNs, where the network's ability to capture structural information degrades due to specific eigenvalue distributions in the adjacency or Laplacian matrices of the graph. Through rigorous theoretical analysis and empirical evaluation on synthetic and real-world datasets, we demonstrate that GCNs are particularly vulnerable to spectral concentration and shrinkage phenomena, leading to diminished learning capacity and reduced predictive performance. Our findings highlight the limitations of standard GCN architectures when exposed to graphs with narrow spectral gaps or atypical spectral signatures. We further present diagnostic tools for detecting such failure modes and discuss potential architectural modifications and preprocessing strategies to mitigate these issues. This work provides new insights into the spectral dynamics of GCNs, offering guidance for designing more robust graph neural network models. Key keywords: Graph Convolutional Networks, spectral analysis, eigenvalues, failure mode, graph neural networks, robustness.
