Continual learning (CL) aims to enable machine learning models to learn sequentially from non-stationary data streams without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. Despite significant progress, existing CL methods often struggle with catastrophic forgetting and scalability in complex environments. This paper investigates variational continual learning (VCL), a probabilistic framework that leverages variational inference to address these challenges. We propose an improved VCL approach that enhances model stability and performance by introducing a novel regularization mechanism and adaptive task-specific priors. Our method is evaluated on benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance in terms of accuracy and forgetting rates compared to state-of-the-art CL techniques. Additionally, we provide a theoretical analysis to better understand the role of variational inference in mitigating forgetting and improving generalization. The results highlight the potential of our approach for real-world applications, such as autonomous systems and personalized AI, where continual adaptation is critical. This work contributes to the broader understanding of probabilistic methods in CL and offers practical insights for developing robust and scalable continual learning systems.  

**Keywords**: continual learning, variational inference, catastrophic forgetting, probabilistic models, adaptive priors, machine learning.
