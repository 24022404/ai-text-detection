This paper addresses the challenge of generating images from scene graphs, a crucial task in computer vision and graphics. Our objective is to learn canonical representations that enable the effective translation of scene graphs into realistic images. We propose a novel approach that leverages a combination of graph convolutional networks and generative adversarial networks to learn a compact and informative representation of scene graphs. Our method, termed Canonical Scene Graph Representation (CSGR), learns to encode scene graphs into a canonical space that captures the essential structure and semantics of the graph. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance in scene graph to image generation tasks. Our findings show that CSGR outperforms existing methods in terms of image quality, diversity, and consistency with the input scene graph. The proposed approach has significant implications for applications such as image synthesis, data augmentation, and visual reasoning, and contributes to the advancement of scene graph understanding and image generation techniques. Key keywords: scene graph, image generation, graph convolutional networks, generative adversarial networks, canonical representation.
