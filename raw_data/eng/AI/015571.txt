**Abstract:**

Deep video completion aims to restore missing or corrupted regions of video sequences in a temporally consistent and visually plausible manner. In this paper, we introduce Onion-Peel Networks, a novel deep learning framework designed specifically for video completion tasks. The proposed approach leverages a hierarchical, layered architecture that incrementally reconstructs missing content from the outer boundaries inward, mimicking the natural "peeling" process of an onion. This design allows the model to effectively exploit spatial and temporal dependencies across video frames, leading to more stable and coherent inpainting results. We implement our method using an encoder-decoder backbone with multiple iterative refinement stages, each responsible for filling progressively deeper masked regions while maintaining context consistency. Extensive experiments on benchmark video datasets demonstrate that Onion-Peel Networks outperform existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of both quantitative metrics, such as PSNR and SSIM, and qualitative assessments of visual realism. Furthermore, our model exhibits superior generalization to diverse video content and mask patterns. The results highlight the effectiveness of hierarchical region reconstruction and provide valuable insights for future developments in video restoration, editing, and augmented reality applications. Key keywords: deep video completion, Onion-Peel Networks, hierarchical reconstruction, video inpainting, temporal consistency.
