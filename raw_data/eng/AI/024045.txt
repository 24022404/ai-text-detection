Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown significant promise in solving complex decision-making problems, but its performance often depends on the choice of function approximation. Kernel-based methods have emerged as a powerful tool for RL due to their ability to model complex, high-dimensional environments. This paper addresses the challenge of deriving regret bounds for kernel-based RL algorithms, which quantify the performance gap between the learned policy and the optimal policy over time. We propose a novel framework that leverages kernel embeddings to approximate value functions and policies, ensuring efficient exploration and exploitation in continuous state-action spaces. Our approach combines kernel ridge regression with optimistic exploration strategies to achieve sublinear regret bounds under mild assumptions on the kernel and the environment. Theoretical analysis demonstrates that the proposed algorithm achieves a regret bound of \( O(\sqrt{T}) \), where \( T \) is the number of time steps, matching the best-known bounds for kernel-based RL. Empirical evaluations on benchmark environments confirm the effectiveness of the method, showing improved sample efficiency and scalability compared to existing approaches. This work contributes to the theoretical understanding of kernel-based RL and provides practical insights for deploying such algorithms in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Kernel Methods, Regret Bounds, Function Approximation, Optimistic Exploration.
