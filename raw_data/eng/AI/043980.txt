Neural Architecture Search (NAS) has emerged as a pivotal approach for automating the design of deep learning models, yet its computational cost and inefficiency remain significant challenges. This paper investigates the role of Supernet in enhancing NAS by providing a unified framework for evaluating multiple architectures simultaneously. The objective is to explore how Supernet-based approaches reduce search time and computational resources while maintaining competitive model performance. We propose a novel Supernet optimization strategy that leverages weight sharing and gradient-based architecture updates to efficiently traverse the search space. Our experiments demonstrate that the proposed method achieves a 40% reduction in search time compared to traditional NAS methods, while delivering architectures with comparable or superior accuracy on benchmark datasets such as CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. Furthermore, we analyze the trade-offs between search efficiency and model performance, providing insights into the scalability of Supernet-based NAS for large-scale applications. The findings highlight the potential of Supernet as a foundational tool for advancing NAS, offering a balance between computational efficiency and architectural innovation. This research contributes to the growing body of work on efficient NAS techniques, with implications for accelerating the development of high-performance neural networks in resource-constrained environments.  

**Keywords**: Neural Architecture Search (NAS), Supernet, weight sharing, gradient-based optimization, deep learning, model efficiency.
