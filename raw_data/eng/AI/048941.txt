This paper surveys the recent advances in deep learning methodologies applied to automatic threat detection within X-ray security imaging. The objective is to provide a comprehensive overview of how deep learning techniques are addressing the challenges of identifying concealed weapons, explosives, and other prohibited items in baggage and cargo screening. We examine various deep learning models, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and transformer-based architectures, analyzing their strengths and weaknesses in feature extraction, object detection, and classification accuracy within X-ray images. The survey encompasses studies that demonstrate enhanced detection rates, reduced false alarm rates, and improved processing times compared to traditional image processing methods. Furthermore, we discuss the impact of transfer learning, data augmentation, and adversarial training in improving the robustness and generalization capabilities of deep learning models for X-ray security. Finally, the paper highlights the limitations of current approaches, including challenges associated with limited datasets, domain adaptation, and the detection of novel threats. We conclude by outlining potential future research directions aimed at developing more effective and reliable automatic threat detection systems. Keywords: Deep Learning, X-ray Security Imaging, Threat Detection, Convolutional Neural Networks, Object Recognition, Airport Security.
