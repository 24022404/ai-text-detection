This paper presents a novel approach to image inpainting by leveraging frequency domain priors to enhance the quality and accuracy of reconstructed images. Traditional inpainting techniques often struggle to preserve structural consistency and fine details, especially in regions with complex textures or patterns. Our method addresses these challenges by integrating frequency domain analysis—specifically, the use of discrete Fourier transform (DFT) features—into the inpainting process, which guides the restoration of lost image content based on global and local frequency characteristics. The proposed algorithm utilizes a hybrid framework combining spatial domain deep learning architectures with a frequency domain prior module. Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, our approach demonstrates significant improvements in restoring both low- and high-frequency image components, resulting in superior visual fidelity compared to existing spatial-only inpainting methods. Quantitative evaluations reveal notable gains in metrics such as Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM). These findings highlight the effectiveness of frequency-aware priors in mitigating common artifacts and ensuring more realistic image completions. This work provides a promising direction for applications in computer vision, such as image editing, restoration of damaged photographs, and semantic content generation. Key keywords: image inpainting, frequency domain, priors, deep learning, DFT, reconstruction, computer vision.
