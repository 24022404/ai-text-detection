Medical image segmentation is a critical task in healthcare, enabling precise diagnosis and treatment planning. However, obtaining large-scale annotated medical datasets is challenging due to the time-consuming and costly nature of manual labeling. This paper proposes a novel *Transformation Consistent Self-ensembling Model (TCSM)* for semi-supervised medical image segmentation, leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data to improve segmentation accuracy. The proposed approach integrates a self-ensembling framework with transformation consistency regularization, ensuring robustness to geometric transformations such as rotation, scaling, and flipping. By enforcing consistency between predictions of the student and teacher networks under different transformations, TCSM effectively utilizes unlabeled data to enhance model generalization. Experiments on benchmark medical image datasets demonstrate that TCSM outperforms state-of-the-art semi-supervised methods, achieving significant improvements in segmentation accuracy, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. The results highlight the model's ability to reduce dependency on extensive annotations while maintaining high performance. This work contributes to advancing semi-supervised learning in medical imaging, offering a practical solution for scenarios where labeled data is scarce. The proposed method has potential applications in various medical domains, including tumor detection, organ segmentation, and disease diagnosis.  

**Keywords**: Semi-supervised learning, medical image segmentation, transformation consistency, self-ensembling, deep learning, healthcare AI.
