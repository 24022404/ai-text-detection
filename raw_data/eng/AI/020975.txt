Urban traffic flow inference is critical for efficient transportation management and smart city development. However, existing methods often struggle to achieve fine-grained accuracy due to the complexity of urban road networks and sparse data availability. This paper proposes a novel approach for fine-grained urban traffic flow inference guided by road network topology. The proposed method leverages graph neural networks (GNNs) to model the spatial dependencies within the road network, integrating multi-source data such as traffic sensors, GPS trajectories, and road attributes. A hierarchical attention mechanism is introduced to capture both local and global traffic patterns, enabling precise inference at a fine-grained level. Experimental results on real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, achieving significant improvements in inference accuracy and robustness. The findings highlight the importance of road network structure in traffic flow modeling and provide a scalable solution for urban traffic management. This research contributes to advancing intelligent transportation systems and offers practical insights for urban planners and policymakers.  

**Keywords**: traffic flow inference, road network, graph neural networks, urban transportation, smart cities, hierarchical attention.
