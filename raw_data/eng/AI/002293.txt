**Abstract:**  
Image segmentation in complex outdoor environments remains a significant challenge, particularly under variable lighting conditions and in visually cluttered cross-country scenes. This paper addresses the problem by focusing on image segmentation of cross-country landscapes captured in the infrared (IR) spectrum, which provides resilience against common visual distractions such as shadows and camouflage. We propose a novel segmentation framework that leverages advanced deep learning architectures, specifically a tailored convolutional neural network (CNN), adapted for the unique characteristics of IR imagery. The approach incorporates pre-processing techniques for thermal noise reduction and contrast enhancement, followed by supervised learning using annotated IR datasets representing diverse terrain types, vegetation, and man-made structures. Experimental results demonstrate substantial improvements in segmentation accuracy, especially in challenging scenarios where traditional RGB-based methods perform poorly. Comparative analysis with existing state-of-the-art segmentation algorithms highlights significant gains in both mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and processing speed. The findings underscore the potential of IR-based segmentation for applications in autonomous navigation, military reconnaissance, and environmental monitoring. This research contributes a new benchmark for cross-country scene analysis in the IR domain, advancing the reliability of vision systems under adverse and variable conditions.  
**Keywords:** image segmentation, infrared imaging, cross-country scenes, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, autonomous navigation, thermal image analysis
