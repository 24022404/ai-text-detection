This paper addresses the challenge of policy tracking in dynamic environments where the optimal policy may change over time, a common scenario in reinforcement learning applications. We propose an adiabatic theorem for policy tracking using Temporal Difference (TD) learning, drawing an analogy to adiabatic processes in physics to formalize how TD-learning adapts to smoothly evolving policies. Our approach models the policy changes as a time-dependent process and rigorously analyzes the conditions under which TD algorithms can reliably follow and adapt to policy shifts while maintaining accurate value estimation. Through theoretical analysis and empirical experiments on benchmark reinforcement learning tasks, we demonstrate that under suitably slow policy variations, TD-learning algorithms remain stable and effectively track the changing optimal policy with tractable convergence guarantees. The findings provide actionable insights for deploying TD-learning methods in real-world systems where policy adaptation is critical, such as adaptive control and non-stationary environments. This work advances understanding of the dynamic behavior of policy evaluation algorithms and establishes a foundation for robust reinforcement learning in non-static settings. **Keywords:** reinforcement learning, policy tracking, adiabatic theorem, temporal difference learning, non-stationary environment, adaptive control.
