This paper addresses the challenge of improving the generalization and stability of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) by introducing a controllable orthogonalization framework during training. The primary objective is to regulate the degree of orthogonality among neural network weights, mitigating issues such as redundant feature learning and vanishing/exploding gradients. Our approach incorporates a novel regularization technique that enables dynamic adjustment of the orthogonalization strength, providing flexibility to adapt to various network architectures and learning tasks. Through extensive experimentation on benchmark datasets involving image classification and natural language processing tasks, we demonstrate that our method leads to enhanced model robustness, faster convergence, and superior accuracy compared to standard and fixed orthogonalization strategies. Furthermore, ablation studies reveal the impact of controllable orthogonalization across different network depths and learning rates. The proposed framework is easily integrable with existing deep learning workflows and introduces negligible computational overhead. This research offers new insights into optimizing DNN training and paves the way for broader adoption of adaptive regularization methods in modern machine learning. Keywords: controllable orthogonalization, deep neural networks, regularization, training stability, generalization, adaptive algorithms.
