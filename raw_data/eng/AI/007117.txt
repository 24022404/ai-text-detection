Image segmentation is a crucial process in computer vision, impacting numerous applications from medical imaging to autonomous vehicles. This paper addresses the challenge of efficient and accurate image segmentation through the development of a novel Multi-Threshold technique utilizing histogram sampling.

Methods/Approach: The proposed approach involves an innovative application of histogram sampling to determine multiple thresholds for segmenting images. By analyzing the distribution of pixel intensities, our method dynamically selects threshold values that partition the image into distinct regions. This technique is designed to reduce computational complexity while maintaining high fidelity in the segmented output.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our Multi-Threshold technique significantly enhances segmentation accuracy compared to traditional single-threshold methods. Performance evaluations on benchmark datasets showed improved delineation of complex image elements with reduced computational time. The proposed method also outperformed existing multi-threshold algorithms in terms of precision and processing efficiency, as evidenced by quantitative metrics such as the Dice coefficient and processing time.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a robust image segmentation method that leverages histogram sampling for multi-thresholding, offering a balance of accuracy and efficiency. The technique's adaptability makes it suitable for various applications requiring precise image partitioning with minimal computational overhead. Future work could explore integrating this approach with machine learning models for further improvements, particularly in real-time image processing scenarios.

Keywords: image segmentation, multi-threshold technique, histogram sampling, pixel intensity distribution, computer vision, computational efficiency.
