This paper addresses the challenge of improving the efficiency of knowledge transfer in Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) training by leveraging semantic and visual similarities. Traditional CNN training often requires extensive labeled datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to acquire. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel approach that integrates semantic relationships and visual features to enhance knowledge transfer across related tasks. Our method employs a dual-path architecture, where one path focuses on extracting semantic embeddings from textual data, while the other extracts visual features from images. These embeddings are then aligned using a similarity-based loss function, enabling the model to transfer knowledge more effectively between domains. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves superior performance compared to existing methods, reducing the need for large labeled datasets while maintaining high accuracy. The proposed framework not only improves training efficiency but also generalizes well to unseen tasks, making it a valuable tool for applications in computer vision and beyond. This work contributes to the field by introducing a scalable and resource-efficient solution for knowledge transfer in CNNs, with potential applications in areas such as medical imaging, autonomous systems, and multimedia analysis.  

**Keywords**: Convolutional Neural Networks, Knowledge Transfer, Semantic Embeddings, Visual Features, Similarity Learning, Efficient Training.
