This paper addresses the challenge of personalized glycemic control in septic patients, a critical aspect of intensive care management. The objective is to develop a data-driven approach that optimizes insulin therapy while minimizing the risk of hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia. We propose a novel framework combining representation learning and reinforcement learning (RL) to model the complex dynamics of glucose regulation in sepsis. Representation learning is employed to extract meaningful features from high-dimensional clinical data, capturing patient-specific physiological patterns. These features are then used to train an RL agent that dynamically adjusts insulin dosages based on real-time patient responses. The proposed method is evaluated on a large-scale dataset of septic patients, demonstrating superior performance in maintaining glycemic stability compared to traditional protocols and baseline RL approaches. Key findings include a significant reduction in glycemic variability and improved adherence to target glucose ranges. The framework’s adaptability to individual patient profiles highlights its potential for clinical deployment. This research contributes to the growing field of AI-driven healthcare by offering a scalable and interpretable solution for personalized glycemic control, with implications for improving patient outcomes in critical care settings.  

**Keywords**: Glycemic control, sepsis, reinforcement learning, representation learning, personalized medicine, intensive care, AI in healthcare.
