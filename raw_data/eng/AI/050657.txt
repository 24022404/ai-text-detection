Domain adaptation and task transfer are critical challenges in machine learning, particularly when labeled data is scarce in the target domain or task. This paper introduces a novel framework, *Learning to Cluster for Transfer* (LCT), which leverages unsupervised clustering to enhance cross-domain and cross-task transferability. The proposed approach focuses on learning cluster structures that are invariant across domains, enabling effective knowledge transfer without requiring extensive labeled data. LCT employs a dual-objective optimization strategy, combining clustering loss with domain-invariant feature learning, to align source and target distributions while preserving task-relevant structures. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that LCT outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both domain adaptation and task transfer scenarios, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the importance of clustering as a mechanism for capturing transferable representations, offering a scalable and efficient solution for real-world applications where domain shifts and task variations are prevalent. This work contributes a new perspective on transfer learning, emphasizing the role of unsupervised learning in bridging domain and task gaps.  

**Keywords**: domain adaptation, task transfer, unsupervised clustering, transfer learning, invariant representations, machine learning.
