Hyperspectral imaging (HSI) captures detailed spectral information across numerous bands, enabling precise analysis in applications such as remote sensing and environmental monitoring. However, the high dimensionality of hyperspectral data often leads to computational inefficiency and redundancy. This paper addresses the challenge of band selection in HSI by proposing an attention-based convolutional neural network (CNN) framework. The proposed method leverages attention mechanisms to identify and prioritize the most informative spectral bands, reducing dimensionality while preserving critical information. The CNN architecture is designed to capture spatial-spectral features, and the attention module dynamically weights bands based on their relevance to the task. Experiments conducted on benchmark hyperspectral datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms traditional band selection methods in terms of classification accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight the effectiveness of attention mechanisms in enhancing feature extraction and reducing redundancy in hyperspectral data. This research contributes a novel, efficient solution for band selection, with potential applications in remote sensing, agriculture, and environmental monitoring.  

**Keywords**: hyperspectral imaging, band selection, attention mechanisms, convolutional neural networks, dimensionality reduction, remote sensing.
