The increasing demand for energy-efficient and adaptable neural networks in real-time applications has driven the need for runtime-throttleable systems. This paper addresses the challenge of dynamically adjusting the computational resources of neural networks during inference to balance performance and energy consumption. We propose a novel framework that enables runtime throttling by leveraging adaptive pruning and quantization techniques, allowing the network to scale its computational complexity based on resource availability or application requirements. Our approach integrates a lightweight control mechanism that dynamically adjusts the network's depth and precision without requiring retraining or significant overhead. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves up to a 40% reduction in energy consumption with minimal accuracy degradation compared to static models. The results highlight the framework's ability to maintain competitive performance while offering flexibility for deployment in resource-constrained environments, such as edge devices and IoT systems. This work contributes to the growing field of energy-efficient AI by providing a practical solution for runtime-adaptive neural networks, paving the way for more sustainable and scalable AI applications.  

**Keywords**: runtime throttling, neural networks, adaptive pruning, quantization, energy efficiency, edge computing.
