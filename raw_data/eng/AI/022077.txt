This paper introduces RSINet, a novel Rotation-Scale Invariant Network designed to enhance the robustness and accuracy of online visual tracking systems. Visual object tracking in dynamic environments often faces challenges due to abrupt changes in target orientation and scale, which can significantly degrade tracker performance. To address these issues, RSINet integrates a multi-branch feature extraction module that concurrently learns rotation and scale-invariant representations from input video frames. The proposed architecture leverages a hybrid attention mechanism to dynamically emphasize relevant spatial features, enabling precise localization under complex motion scenarios. Experimental evaluation on multiple public benchmark datasets demonstrates that RSINet consistently outperforms state-of-the-art tracking methods, particularly in sequences involving rapid target rotation and significant scale variation. The system achieves notable improvements in tracking accuracy, robustness, and real-time operation. The results confirm the effectiveness of the rotation-scale invariant approach in practical scenarios such as surveillance, autonomous driving, and robotic vision. RSINet’s adaptable framework promises enhanced deployment in a wide range of computer vision applications where reliable and efficient online tracking is critical. Key keywords: visual tracking, object tracking, rotation-scale invariance, deep neural networks, attention mechanisms, real-time tracking.
