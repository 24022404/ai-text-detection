This paper addresses the challenge of dynamic configuration in modular software systems, where adapting module composition and behavior is essential to meet varying operational contexts and requirements. We propose a novel metareasoning framework that enables on-the-fly system configuration by leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) in conjunction with rich contextual representations of the software environment. Our approach models the configuration task as a sequential decision-making process, where the RL agent continuously observes both system states and external variables, encoded through learned contextual embeddings, to optimize module selection and parameterization in real-time. Experimental evaluations on multiple modular software architectures demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms traditional rule-based and static configuration strategies in terms of adaptability, efficiency, and overall system performance. Furthermore, ablation studies highlight the critical importance of incorporating rich contextual information and demonstrate the flexibility of our framework across diverse software domains. Our findings suggest that integrating metareasoning with RL and advanced context modeling can empower modular software systems with self-optimization capabilities, paving the way for more autonomous, resilient, and context-aware IT solutions. Keywords: metareasoning, modular software systems, on-the-fly configuration, reinforcement learning, contextual representations, self-adaptive systems.
