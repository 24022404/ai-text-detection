Affective computing relies heavily on effective analysis of physiological and behavioral data, yet current methods often require large volumes of labeled data and are limited by single-modality or single-corpus approaches. This paper addresses these challenges by proposing an unsupervised multi-modal representation learning framework for affective computing, utilizing heterogeneous wearable sensor data collected from multiple datasets. Our approach integrates signals such as electrodermal activity, heart rate, and accelerometer measurements through a cross-modal encoder architecture, enabling the model to learn robust, generalized representations of affective states without the need for manual annotation. By aggregating and aligning data from diverse corpora, the system overcomes issues of domain variability and enhances cross-domain generalization. Experimental results demonstrate that the learned representations significantly improve the performance of downstream emotion recognition tasks, outperforming conventional single-modal and supervised baselines in cross-dataset scenarios. Additionally, we provide analysis on the interpretability and transferability of the extracted representations. This work highlights the potential of unsupervised, multi-modal, and multi-corpus learning in advancing scalable and generalizable affective computing applications. Key contributions include a novel fusion framework, comprehensive benchmarking on heterogeneous wearable datasets, and demonstrated gains in emotion recognition without labeled data. Keywords: affective computing, unsupervised learning, multi-modal, wearable sensors, representation learning, multi-corpus, emotion recognition.
