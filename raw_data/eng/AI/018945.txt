This paper addresses the challenging problem of multi-camera association, specifically, linking the identities of individuals across non-overlapping camera views. Current methods often struggle with variations in appearance, illumination, and pose, leading to fragmented trajectories. We propose a novel approach that leverages 3D localization information to significantly improve association accuracy. Our method integrates pedestrian detection and pose estimation within each camera view, followed by triangulation to obtain 3D locations. These 3D locations are then used to constrain the association process, effectively filtering out spurious matches based on spatial proximity. We introduce a graph-based association framework that incorporates both appearance features and 3D location cues. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods by a significant margin, particularly in crowded scenes and challenging camera configurations. The results highlight the effectiveness of incorporating 3D localization for robust multi-camera association, paving the way for more reliable video surveillance and activity analysis applications. Keywords: Multi-camera Tracking, Person Re-identification, 3D Localization, Camera Networks, Object Association.
