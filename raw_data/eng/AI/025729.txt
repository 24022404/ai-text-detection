Data anonymization techniques, such as k-anonymity, aim to protect individual privacy while enabling data sharing and analysis. However, traditional k-anonymity can distort the original data distribution, leading to inaccurate analytical results. This paper introduces a novel distribution-preserving k-anonymity approach that minimizes information loss and maintains data utility. Our method utilizes a clustering algorithm based on earth mover's distance (EMD) to group similar records, followed by a generalization process that preserves the overall data distribution. We evaluate our approach on several real-world datasets, comparing its performance against existing k-anonymity techniques in terms of information loss, data utility, and privacy protection. The results demonstrate that our distribution-preserving method significantly reduces information loss while maintaining strong k-anonymity and improving the accuracy of analytical tasks. This approach provides a valuable tool for privacy-preserving data sharing and analysis, enabling more reliable insights from anonymized datasets.
