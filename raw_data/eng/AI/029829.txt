This paper addresses the challenge of learning visual relations from weakly-supervised data, a critical task in computer vision with applications in image understanding and scene analysis. Traditional methods often rely on fully annotated datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to obtain. To overcome this limitation, we propose an explanation-based weakly-supervised learning framework that leverages graph networks to model and infer visual relationships. Our approach integrates a graph-based representation of visual entities and their interactions, enabling the system to learn relational patterns from limited supervision. The framework employs a novel explanation mechanism that guides the learning process by identifying and emphasizing the most informative relational cues in the data. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance compared to fully-supervised approaches while significantly reducing the need for annotated data. The results highlight the effectiveness of graph networks in capturing complex visual relationships and the potential of explanation-based learning for weakly-supervised tasks. This work contributes to advancing visual relationship understanding by providing a scalable and efficient solution for real-world applications, such as autonomous systems and multimedia analysis.  

**Keywords**: weakly-supervised learning, visual relations, graph networks, explanation-based learning, computer vision.
