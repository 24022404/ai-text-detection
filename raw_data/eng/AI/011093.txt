The growing complexity of network systems calls for innovative solutions to effectively detect anomalies. This paper introduces GEE, a Gradient-based Explainable Variational Autoencoder, designed to enhance network anomaly detection by providing both high accuracy and interpretability.

Methods/Approach: GEE leverages the capabilities of variational autoencoders (VAEs) to model network traffic behavior while integrating a gradient-based approach to facilitate explainability. The GEE model applies a novel gradient attribution method to highlight input features contributing to detected anomalies, thus offering insight into the decision-making process. This combination aims to balance the trade-off between performance and interpretability, often found in traditional anomaly detection models.

Results/Findings: Extensive experiments on benchmark network datasets demonstrate that GEE outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of anomaly detection accuracy, achieving a superior balance between precision and recall metrics. Additionally, the explainability component was validated through user studies, confirming the model's ability to provide intuitive and relevant feature attributions for detected anomalies. Comparative analyses further illustrate GEE's robustness and efficiency in processing network traffic data.

Conclusion/Implications: The development of GEE marks a significant advancement in the realm of anomaly detection systems by merging explainability with high-performance detection capabilities. This research contributes to the growing field of interpretable machine learning and offers potential applications in network security monitoring, real-time intrusion detection, and compliance auditing. Future work will explore the scalability of GEE to accommodate the increasing volume and diversity of network data, paving the way for more resilient and trustworthy AI-driven network management solutions.

Keywords: Anomaly Detection, Explainable AI, Variational Autoencoder, Network Security, Gradient Attribution, Interpretable Machine Learning.
