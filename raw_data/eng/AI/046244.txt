This paper addresses the challenge of effectively clustering complex brain networks by introducing a novel methodology that harnesses Kernel-ARMA (AutoRegressive Moving Average) modeling and the Grassmannian framework. The objective of this research is to improve the accuracy and reliability of brain-network clustering, an important task in understanding neurological patterns and disorders. Our approach leverages the statistical power of ARMA models to capture temporal dependencies within network data, enhanced through kernelization to handle non-linear dynamics inherent in brain networks. We further employ the Grassmannian representation to facilitate robust clustering in the manifold space, which inherently respects the geometric properties of network data. The proposed method significantly outperforms traditional clustering techniques, as demonstrated in experiments involving real-world brain-network datasets, showing superior capability in distinguishing intricate neural patterns. Our findings indicate that this hybrid methodology not only enhances clustering performance but also provides deeper insights into brain connectivity and its variations across different cognitive states. This research contributes to the fields of network analysis and neuroinformatics by presenting a powerful tool that can be applied in clinical settings for brain disorder diagnostics and monitoring, potentially advancing precision medicine. Key keywords include network clustering, Kernel-ARMA, Grassmannian, brain networks, neural patterns, neuroinformatics.
