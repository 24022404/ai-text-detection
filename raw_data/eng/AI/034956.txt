The paper explores the integration of neuroscience principles to enhance the generalization capabilities of reinforcement learning (RL) agents. It addresses the fundamental challenge of creating agents that can adapt to a wide variety of tasks, emulating the flexibility and efficiency observed in biological learning systems.

Methods/Approach: Our approach introduces a novel framework that incorporates neuroscience-derived principles, such as synaptic plasticity and neuro-modulatory mechanisms, into existing RL paradigms. The framework leverages computational models inspired by brain structures to dynamically adjust learning rates and strategies based on environmental feedback and agent experiences. A series of experiments were conducted using various benchmarks to assess the adaptability and performance enhancements of the proposed RL agents.

Results/Findings: The implementation of neuroscience principles led to significant improvements in agent generalization, evidenced by higher adaptability to previously unseen environments compared to conventional RL baselines. Our findings demonstrate that the enhanced agents exhibited faster convergence rates and improved decision-making capabilities, particularly in complex, dynamic tasks where traditional methods often falter.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by introducing biologically-inspired methodologies that significantly bolster agent flexibility and performance. The results suggest a promising direction for future RL systems that require generalization across diverse task sets. Potential applications of this work include autonomous systems, adaptive AI in dynamic environments, and any domain requiring robust, flexible learning agents. These findings lay the groundwork for further exploration into the synergistic integration of neuroscience and artificial intelligence.

Key Keywords: reinforcement learning, neuroscience, generalization, biologically-inspired AI, synaptic plasticity, adaptive learning agents.
