This paper addresses the critical challenge of dataset bias in the emerging field of visual emotion recognition. While deep learning models have shown promise in automatically inferring emotions from images, their performance is often hampered by biases present in existing datasets, leading to poor generalization across diverse visual contexts. This study investigates the impact of such biases, focusing on inherent correlations between specific visual attributes and emotion labels. We propose a novel debiasing framework based on adversarial learning, which aims to disentangle emotional content from confounding factors. Our approach incorporates a bias detector to identify and suppress spurious correlations during feature extraction. We evaluate our method on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in generalization performance compared to state-of-the-art models, particularly in cross-dataset validation scenarios. The results highlight the importance of addressing dataset bias in visual emotion recognition and provide a promising direction for developing more robust and reliable emotion-aware systems. Keywords: visual emotion recognition, dataset bias, adversarial learning, debiasing, deep learning, generalization.
