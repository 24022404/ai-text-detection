Bid rigging, a form of collusion in procurement auctions, poses significant challenges to market fairness and economic efficiency. Traditional detection methods often rely on manual analysis or rule-based systems, which are time-consuming and prone to errors. This paper proposes a novel deep learning-based approach to automatically detect bid rigging by identifying patterns indicative of cartel behavior. Leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs), we develop a model trained on historical bidding data to classify participants as either collusive or non-collusive. The model captures subtle irregularities in bid distributions, timing, and pricing strategies that are characteristic of cartel activities. Experimental results on real-world procurement datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method, achieving a detection accuracy of 92.3%, significantly outperforming conventional statistical approaches. The findings highlight the potential of deep learning in enhancing antitrust enforcement and ensuring competitive markets. This research contributes to the growing body of work applying artificial intelligence to regulatory challenges, offering a scalable and efficient solution for detecting bid rigging in large-scale procurement systems.  

**Keywords**: Bid rigging, cartel detection, convolutional neural networks, deep learning, procurement auctions, antitrust enforcement.
