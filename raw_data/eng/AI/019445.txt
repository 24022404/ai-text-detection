The purpose of this research is to address the challenges associated with training deeper Graph Convolutional Neural Networks (GCNNs) and to unlock their full potential in graph-based applications. Traditional GCNNs often suffer from performance degradation as layers increase, limiting their effectiveness for deep network architectures. We propose a novel training methodology that integrates advanced optimization techniques and refined initialization strategies to enhance the stability and performance of deep GCNNs. Our approach includes the utilization of improved normalization layers and adaptive learning rate adjustments, tailored specifically for hierarchical graph structures. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our training regime significantly outperforms existing methods, achieving higher accuracy and efficiency in various benchmark datasets. Key findings include stabilized training processes, reduced overfitting, and improved generalization in deeper networks. The implications of this research suggest transformative possibilities for deploying deeper GCNNs in complex tasks such as social network analysis, molecular chemistry, and recommendation systems. Our contributions provide a foundation for future work towards the development of deeper and more effective graph-based neural networks. Keywords: Graph Convolutional Neural Networks, deep learning, training optimization, stability, graph structures.
