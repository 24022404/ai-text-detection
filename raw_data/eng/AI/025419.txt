In the realm of dynamic systems, the ability to accurately identify and infer the structure of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) from data plays a crucial role in modeling and understanding complex phenomena. This research addresses the challenge of deriving the underlying ODE structure directly from observational data, a task that traditional approaches find difficult without extensive prior knowledge or assumptions.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel method leveraging a hybrid model that integrates machine learning techniques—specifically neural networks—with traditional mathematical frameworks to infer the structure of ODEs. Our approach employs a data-driven algorithm coupled with symbolic regression to discover the functional forms and parameters that govern the system dynamics.

Results/Findings: The proposed method was evaluated on a range of simulated datasets and benchmark problems representing various physical and biological processes. The results demonstrate that our method not only accurately infers the structure of ODEs but also outperforms existing techniques in terms of robustness and precision, providing high fidelity reconstructions with minimal data inputs.

Conclusion/Implications: This study presents a significant advancement in the automated inference of differential equation structures, offering a powerful tool for scientists and engineers in fields such as physics, biology, and engineering. The enhanced capability to deduce system dynamics with limited prior knowledge has potential applications in real-time monitoring, predictive modeling, and control systems design, which can transform how complex dynamic behaviors are understood and managed.

Key Keywords: Ordinary Differential Equations, structure inference, machine learning, neural networks, symbolic regression, system dynamics, data-driven modeling.
