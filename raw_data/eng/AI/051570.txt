This paper addresses the challenge of forecasting human-object interactions (HOIs) in first-person video by jointly predicting motor attention and future actions. The ability to anticipate interactions is critical for applications such as assistive robotics, augmented reality, and human-computer interaction. We propose a novel framework that integrates spatiotemporal features from video sequences with attention mechanisms to model the relationship between visual cues and motor actions. Our approach leverages a dual-stream architecture, combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for spatial feature extraction and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for temporal dynamics, enhanced by a motor attention module that predicts regions of interest for future interactions. Experiments conducted on benchmark first-person video datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing approaches in both action prediction and attention localization tasks. The results highlight the importance of joint modeling for accurate HOI forecasting, achieving state-of-the-art performance in terms of precision and recall. This work contributes to advancing the understanding of human-object interaction dynamics and provides a robust foundation for real-world applications requiring predictive capabilities in dynamic environments.  

**Keywords**: Human-object interaction, first-person video, motor attention, action prediction, spatiotemporal modeling, deep learning.
