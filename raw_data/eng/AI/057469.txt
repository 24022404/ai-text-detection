Data augmentation is a pivotal technique in enhancing the performance and generalization of object detection models. However, the selection and combination of augmentation strategies often rely on heuristic or empirical approaches, leading to suboptimal results. This paper introduces ADA, a novel framework that models data augmentation as a game-theoretic process, providing a principled approach to selecting augmentation policies for object detection tasks. By formulating the interaction between the augmentation policy and the object detection model as a two-player game, ADA dynamically balances between improving model robustness and maintaining data fidelity. We present a rigorous methodology for solving this game using equilibrium analysis and adaptive policy updates, allowing for automated and context-aware augmentation strategy selection. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that ADA significantly outperforms traditional augmentation methods and recent automated augmentation systems in both accuracy and robustness across various detection architectures. The proposed approach offers new insights into the strategic design of data augmentation, presenting a robust, adaptable, and theoretically grounded solution with broad applicability in computer vision tasks. Keywords: data augmentation, object detection, game theory, policy optimization, computer vision, robustness.
