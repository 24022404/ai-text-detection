Pedestrian trajectory prediction is a critical challenge in autonomous systems, particularly for applications like self-driving cars and robotics. Traditional methods often struggle with modeling complex social interactions and dynamic environments. This paper introduces **SGCN (Sparse Graph Convolution Network)**, a novel approach designed to address these limitations by leveraging sparse graph representations to efficiently capture pedestrian interactions and spatial dependencies. The proposed model constructs a sparse graph where nodes represent pedestrians and edges encode their interactions, significantly reducing computational overhead compared to dense graph-based methods. SGCN employs a lightweight graph convolution mechanism to process these sparse structures, enabling real-time performance without sacrificing accuracy. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, such as ETH and UCY, demonstrate that SGCN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. Key findings reveal a 12% improvement in average displacement error (ADE) and a 15% reduction in final displacement error (FDE) compared to existing approaches. The results highlight SGCN's ability to handle crowded scenarios and its potential for integration into real-world autonomous systems. This work contributes a scalable and efficient solution for pedestrian trajectory prediction, paving the way for safer and more reliable navigation in dynamic environments.  

**Keywords**: Pedestrian trajectory prediction, sparse graph convolution, autonomous systems, social interactions, real-time performance.
