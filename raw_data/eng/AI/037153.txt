This paper addresses the challenge of sequential budgeted learning, where a learner must make decisions under a limited budget. The objective is to develop a framework that optimizes the trade-off between exploration and exploitation while respecting the budget constraint. We propose Confidence-Budget Matching, a novel approach that integrates confidence estimates with budget allocation to guide the learning process. Our method utilizes a probabilistic model to quantify uncertainty and allocate the budget effectively. Experimental results demonstrate that Confidence-Budget Matching outperforms existing methods in terms of cumulative reward and budget efficiency. The key findings highlight the importance of balancing exploration and exploitation in sequential decision-making under budget constraints. This research contributes to the field of sequential learning and has potential applications in areas such as recommender systems, autonomous systems, and decision-making under uncertainty. Key keywords: sequential budgeted learning, confidence estimation, budget allocation, exploration-exploitation trade-off, probabilistic modeling.
