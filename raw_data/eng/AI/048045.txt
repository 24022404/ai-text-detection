This paper introduces Foothill, a novel quasiconvex regularization technique designed to optimize the deployment of deep neural networks (DNNs) in edge computing environments. The objective is to address the challenge of efficiently processing DNNs on resource-constrained edge devices while maintaining model accuracy. Our approach involves a regularization method that adaptively prunes and quantizes DNN models, reducing computational complexity without compromising performance. Experimental results demonstrate that Foothill outperforms existing regularization techniques, achieving significant reductions in model size and inference time while preserving accuracy. The key findings highlight the effectiveness of Foothill in enabling the efficient edge computing of DNNs, with potential applications in real-time image classification, object detection, and natural language processing. By leveraging quasiconvex optimization, Foothill contributes to the development of more efficient and scalable edge AI solutions, making it an important innovation in the field of deep learning and edge computing, with relevant keywords including edge AI, deep neural networks, quasiconvex regularization, model pruning, and quantization.
