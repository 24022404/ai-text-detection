Probabilistic integration offers a novel perspective for tackling complex statistical computation problems, particularly where traditional numerical integration methods face scalability and uncertainty quantification challenges. This paper investigates the role of probabilistic integration within the broader framework of statistical computation, focusing on its ability to model numerical error as part of the inferential process. We present a detailed analysis of probabilistic numerical methods, such as Bayesian quadrature, which treat the approximation of integrals as inference problems by leveraging probabilistic models over functions and adaptively allocating computational resources. Through theoretical analysis and empirical evaluation on synthetic and real-world datasets, we compare probabilistic integration methods against conventional Monte Carlo and deterministic quadrature techniques. Results demonstrate that probabilistic approaches provide not only accurate integral estimates but also robust uncertainty quantification, which is beneficial for downstream statistical tasks including model selection, parameter estimation, and uncertainty propagation. We further discuss applications in Bayesian inference and machine learning, where quantifying integration uncertainty enhances decision-making and model robustness. The paper concludes that probabilistic integration constitutes a significant advancement in statistical computation, suggesting promising directions for future research and practical implementations in data science and artificial intelligence. Key keywords: probabilistic integration, Bayesian quadrature, statistical computation, uncertainty quantification, numerical methods, Bayesian inference.
