This paper addresses the challenge of robust line detection in noisy and outlier-prone environments, a critical task in computer vision and image processing. We propose a novel GPU-based approach for efficiently computing the 2D Least Median of Squares (LMedS) estimator, a robust statistical method for fitting lines to data. Traditional CPU-based implementations of LMedS are computationally intensive, limiting their applicability in real-time systems. Our method leverages the parallel processing capabilities of modern GPUs to significantly accelerate the computation, enabling real-time performance without sacrificing robustness. We introduce an optimized algorithm that reduces memory overhead and maximizes parallelism, achieving a speedup of up to 20x compared to conventional CPU implementations. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate the method's effectiveness in detecting lines accurately, even in the presence of significant noise and outliers. The proposed approach is particularly well-suited for applications requiring fast and reliable line detection, such as autonomous navigation, augmented reality, and industrial automation. This work contributes a practical, high-performance solution to the problem of robust line fitting, bridging the gap between computational efficiency and statistical reliability.  

**Keywords**: GPU acceleration, Least Median of Squares, robust line detection, parallel computing, computer vision, outlier rejection.
