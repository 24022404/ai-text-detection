Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for representation learning, reducing reliance on labeled data. However, the performance of SSL models can be limited by the quality and quantity of the unlabeled data. This paper introduces a novel knowledge transfer framework to boost the performance of SSL by leveraging information from pre-trained models on related tasks. Our approach, Knowledge-Enhanced Self-Supervised Learning (KESL), transfers relevant features and structural knowledge from a source model to guide the learning process of a target SSL model. KESL employs a cross-modal distillation technique to align feature spaces and promote knowledge adaptation. Experiments on image classification and object detection benchmarks demonstrate that KESL significantly improves the accuracy and convergence speed of various SSL algorithms, including SimCLR and MoCo. The results show a consistent performance gain of up to 15% compared to standard SSL methods. These findings suggest that knowledge transfer is a promising direction for enhancing the effectiveness of self-supervised representation learning, especially in data-scarce scenarios. Keywords: Self-Supervised Learning, Knowledge Transfer, Representation Learning, Distillation, Unlabeled Data.
