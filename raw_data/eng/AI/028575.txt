The purpose of this research is to enhance the quality and resolution of brain MRI images, which is crucial for precise medical diagnostics and treatment planning. The challenge lies in effectively increasing resolution while preserving detailed structural information inherent in brain images.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel super-resolution technique that utilizes overcomplete dictionaries and nonlocal similarity to improve image quality. Our approach leverages the concept of overcomplete dictionaries to capture diverse image details and represents the high-resolution image as a sparse combination of these dictionary elements. Additionally, we incorporate nonlocal similarity principles to ensure spatial coherence and enhance texture details by exploiting repetitive patterns within the image dataset.

Results/Findings: The proposed method demonstrates superior performance in enhancing brain MRI images when compared to conventional super-resolution techniques. Quantitative evaluations reveal notable improvements in resolution and structural fidelity, as indicated by several metrics including peak signal-to-noise ratio and structural similarity index. Our approach also surpasses other state-of-the-art techniques in preserving critical anatomical details, which is essential for accurate medical interpretation.

Conclusion/Implications: Our research provides significant contributions to the field of medical imaging by offering an effective solution for enhancing MRI image resolution. The integration of overcomplete dictionaries with nonlocal similarity not only achieves better image quality but also opens new avenues for applications in advanced medical diagnostics and therapeutic interventions. This method has the potential to greatly influence clinical workflows by facilitating enhanced image interpretation and potentially reducing the need for repeated scans. Key Keywords: Super-Resolution, Brain MRI, Overcomplete Dictionaries, Nonlocal Similarity, Medical Imaging.
