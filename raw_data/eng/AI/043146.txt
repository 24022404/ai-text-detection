This paper addresses the challenge of improving image-text matching by proposing a novel framework that leverages recurrent visual embedding to express visual objects in images analogously to how words are represented in textual data. The proposed method decomposes images into constituent visual objects, which are then sequentially embedded using a recurrent neural network (RNN)-based architecture. This sequential embedding captures relationships and contextual dependencies among objects, enabling the generation of a visual representation that closely parallels the structure and semantics of natural language sentences. Experimental evaluations were conducted on benchmark datasets for image-text retrieval, demonstrating that our approach achieves superior performance compared to existing image-text matching models, particularly in accurately capturing fine-grained relationships between image regions and descriptive text. The results indicate that treating objects in images as sequential visual tokens contributes to more robust cross-modal alignment. This research introduces a unified perspective for multimodal representation learning and offers significant advancements for applications such as visual question answering, image captioning, and cross-modal retrieval. Key keywords include image-text matching, recurrent visual embedding, object representation, cross-modal retrieval, and multimodal learning.
