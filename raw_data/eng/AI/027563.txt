Zero-shot image recognition (ZSL) aims to classify images of unseen classes by leveraging semantic relationships between seen and unseen categories. This paper introduces a novel **Graph-based Visual-Semantic Entanglement Network (GVSEN)** to address the challenges of aligning visual features with semantic embeddings in ZSL tasks. GVSEN employs a graph neural network (GNN) to model the intricate relationships between visual and semantic domains, enabling effective knowledge transfer from seen to unseen classes. The proposed framework integrates a visual-semantic attention mechanism to enhance feature representation and a graph-based entanglement module to capture hierarchical dependencies among classes. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including CUB, SUN, and AWA2, demonstrate that GVSEN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both conventional and generalized ZSL settings. The results highlight the model's ability to generalize across diverse datasets and its robustness in handling domain shifts. This work contributes to advancing zero-shot learning by providing a scalable and interpretable framework for visual-semantic alignment, with potential applications in image retrieval, autonomous systems, and multimedia analysis.  

**Keywords**: Zero-shot learning, graph neural networks, visual-semantic alignment, image recognition, attention mechanism.
