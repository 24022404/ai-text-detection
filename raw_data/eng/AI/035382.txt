Federated learning (FL) enables collaborative model training without direct data sharing, but client selection remains a critical challenge. Existing random selection strategies often suffer from high variance due to heterogeneous data distributions across clients. To address this, we introduce Clustered Sampling, a novel client selection method that leverages client similarity to improve both variance reduction and representativity. Clustered Sampling groups clients into clusters based on data distribution similarity and then samples clients from each cluster. This ensures that the selected clients better represent the global data distribution while reducing the variance in model updates. We evaluate Clustered Sampling on benchmark datasets with varying degrees of data heterogeneity. Our results demonstrate that Clustered Sampling consistently outperforms state-of-the-art client selection methods in terms of convergence speed, model accuracy, and fairness. Specifically, Clustered Sampling achieves up to 20% improvement in accuracy compared to random selection, especially in highly heterogeneous settings. This method offers a practical and effective approach for enhancing the performance and robustness of federated learning systems.

Keywords: Federated Learning, Client Selection, Clustered Sampling, Data Heterogeneity, Variance Reduction, Representativity.
