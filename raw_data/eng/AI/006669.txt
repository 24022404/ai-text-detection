This paper introduces a novel approach for 3D reconstruction using Deep Implicit Moving Least-Squares (DIMLS) functions, addressing the challenges of accurately representing complex geometries and surfaces from sparse or noisy point cloud data. The proposed method combines the strengths of implicit surface modeling and deep learning to enhance reconstruction quality and computational efficiency. By leveraging a neural network to learn the Moving Least-Squares (MLS) function, the approach adaptively captures local geometric details while maintaining global smoothness. The framework is trained on diverse datasets to ensure robustness across various shapes and topologies. Experimental results demonstrate that DIMLS outperforms traditional MLS-based methods and state-of-the-art deep learning techniques in terms of reconstruction accuracy, particularly in handling sharp features and irregular surfaces. Additionally, the method exhibits superior scalability and generalization capabilities, making it suitable for real-world applications such as medical imaging, robotics, and computer-aided design. This work contributes a significant advancement in 3D reconstruction by integrating implicit surface modeling with deep learning, offering a versatile and efficient solution for high-fidelity geometric representation.  

**Keywords**: 3D reconstruction, implicit surfaces, Moving Least-Squares, deep learning, point cloud processing, geometric modeling.
