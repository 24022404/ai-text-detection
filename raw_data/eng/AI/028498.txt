The accurate detection and labeling of equipment and tools in real-world environments is a critical challenge in computer vision, with applications ranging from industrial automation to augmented reality. This paper introduces ALET (Automated Labeling of Equipment and Tools), a comprehensive dataset designed to address the limitations of existing datasets in tool detection. ALET comprises high-resolution images of tools in diverse, unconstrained settings, annotated with precise bounding boxes and labels. To establish a baseline, we evaluate state-of-the-art object detection models, including YOLOv8 and Faster R-CNN, on the ALET dataset, highlighting their performance in terms of accuracy and robustness. Our experiments demonstrate that ALET significantly improves tool detection in complex, real-world scenarios compared to existing datasets. Furthermore, we present a practical use case in industrial maintenance, where ALET-enabled detection systems enhance efficiency and safety by automating tool identification. The dataset, baseline models, and evaluation framework are made publicly available to foster further research in this domain. This work contributes to advancing tool detection technologies, offering a valuable resource for both academia and industry.  

**Keywords**: tool detection, computer vision, object detection, dataset, industrial automation, YOLOv8, Faster R-CNN.
