**Abstract**

The discovery of novel crystallographic structures is pivotal in advancing material science and developing new functional materials. This paper introduces CrystalGAN, a generative adversarial network (GAN)-based framework designed to learn and generate plausible crystal structures directly from existing crystallographic data. Our approach leverages deep generative modeling to capture complex spatial and compositional patterns inherent in crystalline materials, enabling the synthesis of new structures that adhere to underlying physical and chemical constraints. The methodology integrates domain-specific feature representations and utilizes a custom architecture to ensure the generated outputs are realizable and stable. Experimental results on public crystal structure datasets demonstrate that CrystalGAN outperforms traditional generative and automated design techniques in terms of diversity, structural validity, and potential material properties. Furthermore, we present a comparative analysis revealing that CrystalGAN effectively anticipates previously unseen structure types. This work highlights the utility of AI-driven generative models in materials discovery and provides a scalable path for accelerating crystallographic explorations. Key contributions include the novel adaptation of GANs for crystal generation, validated performance improvements, and insights into the discovery potential of generative deep learning in material informatics.

**Keywords:** generative adversarial networks, CrystalGAN, crystallography, crystal structure generation, material discovery, deep learning, artificial intelligence, materials informatics.
