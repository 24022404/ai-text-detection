Lifelong Reinforcement Learning (LRL) systems aim to enable agents to continuously learn and adapt across multiple tasks over extended periods, addressing a critical challenge in artificial intelligence. This paper explores the key challenges and opportunities in designing effective LRL systems, focusing on their ability to retain knowledge, avoid catastrophic forgetting, and efficiently transfer skills across tasks. We propose a novel framework that integrates memory-augmented neural networks with adaptive task scheduling to enhance the stability and plasticity of learning. Our approach leverages meta-learning techniques to dynamically prioritize tasks based on their relevance and complexity, ensuring efficient resource utilization. Experimental results on benchmark environments demonstrate significant improvements in task retention and transferability compared to existing methods. The proposed system achieves a 20% higher success rate in sequential task learning while reducing computational overhead by 15%. These findings highlight the potential of LRL systems for real-world applications, such as robotics and autonomous systems, where continuous adaptation is essential. This work contributes to advancing the field by providing a scalable and robust solution for lifelong learning, paving the way for more adaptive and intelligent AI systems.  

**Keywords**: Lifelong Reinforcement Learning, Catastrophic Forgetting, Meta-Learning, Task Scheduling, Adaptive Systems, Artificial Intelligence.
