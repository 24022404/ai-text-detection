This paper introduces Graph Filtration Learning, a novel approach to graph-structured data analysis. The objective is to develop an efficient and effective method for learning representations of graphs by incorporating filtration techniques, which capture topological features at multiple scales. Our approach utilizes a combination of graph neural networks and persistent homology to learn robust and informative graph representations. The method is evaluated on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating improved performance in graph classification and clustering tasks compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. Key findings include the ability to capture subtle topological patterns and robustness to noise and structural variations. The proposed Graph Filtration Learning framework contributes to the advancement of graph learning techniques, with potential applications in computer vision, network analysis, and bioinformatics. By highlighting the importance of topological features in graph-structured data, this research paves the way for further innovations in graph representation learning, graph neural networks, and topological data analysis, with relevant keywords including graph learning, persistent homology, graph neural networks, and topological data analysis.
