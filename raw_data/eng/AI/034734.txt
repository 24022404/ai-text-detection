The detection of fake images generated by AI models has become increasingly important. However, existing detection methods are vulnerable to adversarial attacks. This paper investigates the creation of imperceptible adversarial examples that can effectively evade state-of-the-art fake image detectors. We propose a novel adversarial attack strategy based on a frequency-aware perturbation generation, which carefully crafts small, high-frequency noises imperceptible to human eyes but significantly reduces the detection accuracy of deep learning-based detectors. Experimental results on several benchmark datasets, including those generated by GANs and diffusion models, demonstrate that our approach can successfully fool existing detectors with minimal visual artifacts. Specifically, our attack achieves a success rate of over 90% while maintaining a Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) of over 0.98, indicating high perceptual similarity to the original fake images. This highlights a critical vulnerability in current fake image detection techniques and underscores the need for more robust and secure detection mechanisms. Keywords: Adversarial examples, fake image detection, deep learning, adversarial attacks, image forensics, GANs, diffusion models.
