This paper presents a novel approach to manipulating attributes of natural scenes through hallucination, a technique that leverages advanced generative models to synthesize realistic images. The objective is to enable flexible and efficient editing of scene attributes, such as object appearance, lighting, and weather conditions, without requiring extensive manual labeling or editing. Our method employs a deep learning-based framework that combines the strengths of generative adversarial networks (GANs) and variational autoencoders (VAEs) to hallucinate missing or desired attributes in a given scene. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in generating high-quality, photorealistic images with manipulated attributes, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods. The key findings of this research highlight the potential of hallucination-based techniques for various applications, including image editing, computer vision, and robotics. Our contributions include the development of a novel hallucination framework, the introduction of a new evaluation metric for assessing attribute manipulation, and the demonstration of the approach's versatility in handling diverse scene attributes. The proposed method has significant implications for the field of computer vision, enabling more efficient and creative image editing and manipulation. Key keywords: hallucination, generative models, scene attributes, image editing, computer vision, GANs, VAEs.
