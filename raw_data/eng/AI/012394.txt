This paper addresses the challenge of ultra high-resolution image inpainting by introducing a novel approach called Contextual Residual Aggregation (CRA). The objective is to develop an effective method for restoring missing regions in high-resolution images while preserving contextual consistency and fine details. Our approach combines the strengths of deep learning and traditional image processing techniques, utilizing a multi-scale residual aggregation framework to capture long-range dependencies and subtle texture patterns. The CRA model is trained on a large dataset of high-resolution images and achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM). Our results show that CRA outperforms existing image inpainting methods, particularly in scenarios with large missing regions or complex textures. The proposed method has important implications for various applications, including image editing, restoration, and generation. Key contributions of this work include the introduction of a contextual residual aggregation module, a novel loss function that balances reconstruction accuracy and perceptual quality, and a comprehensive evaluation framework for ultra high-resolution image inpainting. Relevant keywords: image inpainting, ultra high-resolution images, deep learning, contextual residual aggregation, image restoration.
