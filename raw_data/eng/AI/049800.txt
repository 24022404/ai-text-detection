**Abstract**

Gesture recognition plays a critical role in enabling intuitive human-computer interaction, but reliably identifying important or context-relevant actions remains a challenge, especially in complex environments with similar or overlapping gestures. This paper presents a novel gesture recognition framework that emphasizes the detection of important actions by employing a path searching method within a weighted graph. The proposed approach models gesture sequences as nodes and transitions on a graph, where edge weights are dynamically assigned based on the significance and correlation of adjacent actions. By leveraging an efficient path searching algorithm, the system prioritizes gesture sequences that represent important or target actions, leading to improved recognition accuracy for critical gestures. Experimental results on publicly available gesture datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms conventional sequence matching and deep learning-based classifiers, particularly in scenarios where distinguishing similar gestures is crucial. The findings highlight significant improvements in recognition precision and robustness, with minimal computational overhead. This research contributes a scalable and adaptable solution for gesture-based systems in applications such as sign language interpretation, surveillance, and smart device control. Key contributions include the integration of weighted graph modeling with path search optimization for gesture recognition and the focused detection of important actions. Keywords: gesture recognition, important action detection, weighted graph, path searching, human-computer interaction, action recognition.
