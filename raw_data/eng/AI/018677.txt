Video Object Segmentation (VOS) is a critical task in computer vision, enabling the identification and tracking of objects in video sequences. Traditional methods often rely on extensive labeled data, which is time-consuming and resource-intensive to obtain. This paper addresses the challenge of One-Shot Weakly Supervised Video Object Segmentation, where only a single annotated frame is available for training. We propose a novel framework that leverages weakly supervised learning to reduce dependency on large-scale labeled datasets. Our approach integrates a lightweight attention mechanism with a temporal consistency module to enhance object localization and tracking across frames. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance compared to fully supervised approaches, with significant reductions in annotation effort. The results highlight the potential of weakly supervised techniques in practical applications such as video surveillance, autonomous driving, and augmented reality. This work contributes to advancing efficient and scalable solutions for video understanding tasks, paving the way for broader adoption in real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Video Object Segmentation, Weakly Supervised Learning, One-Shot Learning, Attention Mechanism, Temporal Consistency, Computer Vision.
