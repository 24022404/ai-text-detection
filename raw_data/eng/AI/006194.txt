This paper presents GNN3DMOT, a novel framework based on Graph Neural Networks (GNN) for accurate and robust 3D multi-object tracking (MOT) in dynamic environments. The objective of this study is to address the persistent challenges in associating objects over time in 3D space, particularly in scenarios with occlusions, varying object motion, and ambiguous spatial-temporal relationships. Our proposed approach leverages multi-feature learning by integrating appearance, motion, and geometric information into a unified graph representation. Each object detection is modeled as a node, and pairwise relationships are encoded as graph edges, enabling the GNN to learn complex dependencies and interactions among objects. The multi-feature fusion mechanism improves discriminative power, facilitating more precise data association and trajectory prediction. Comprehensive experiments on public 3D MOT benchmarks demonstrate that GNN3DMOT outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, achieving higher tracking accuracy and superior robustness in challenging conditions. Ablation studies further validate the effectiveness of our multi-feature learning strategy and graph-based modeling. This research highlights the potential of GNN-based architectures for advancing 3D multi-object tracking and offers a scalable, generalizable solution for real-world applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and intelligent surveillance. Key keywords: multi-object tracking, 3D tracking, Graph Neural Networks, multi-feature learning, data association, autonomous systems.
