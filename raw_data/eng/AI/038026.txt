The purpose of this study is to investigate the impact of individual rater style on the uncertainty associated with deep learning models used for medical imaging segmentation. In medical imaging, accurate segmentation is critical for diagnostics and treatment planning, yet the inherent variability in manual annotations poses significant challenges in training robust and reliable models. Our approach involves analyzing the segmentation performance of deep learning models trained on datasets annotated by different raters, while quantifying the uncertainty using advanced uncertainty estimation techniques. We employed methods such as Monte Carlo Dropout and Deep Ensembles to capture both predictive uncertainty and model variance, revealing how individual rater styles contribute to the overall uncertainty in segmentation outcomes. Results demonstrate a notable variation in model performance based on rater-dependent annotations, highlighting the variability and potential biases introduced by individual rater styles. The findings underscore the importance of considering rater variability when developing and deploying deep learning models in medical imaging. Our work contributes to the field by providing insights into the critical relationship between manual annotation styles and deep learning model uncertainty, ultimately guiding improvements in model robustness. This research has profound implications for enhancing the accuracy and reliability of medical image segmentation, potentially leading to improved clinical decision-making and patient care. Key keywords include medical imaging, deep learning, uncertainty estimation, segmentation, and model robustness.
