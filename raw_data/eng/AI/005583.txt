This paper addresses the challenge of learning complex likelihood functions by utilizing Conditional Normalizing Flows (CNFs), a framework that enables expressive modeling of multivariate distributions conditioned on auxiliary data. The objective is to enhance the accuracy and flexibility of probabilistic modeling in fields such as natural language processing and image analysis.

Methods/Approach: We employ CNFs, which integrate normalizing flows with conditional inputs, to form transformations that achieve tractable likelihood estimation and effective sampling. The modeling capabilities of CNFs are boosted by leveraging neural networks to adaptively transform simpler distributions into the complex target distributions conditioned on external variables.

Results/Findings: Extensive experiments demonstrate that CNFs achieve superior performance in learning likelihoods compared to traditional methods, such as Gaussian mixtures and basic normalizing flows. Our findings reveal that CNFs can capture intricate data dependencies and achieve significant improvements in likelihood estimation accuracy. Furthermore, the proposed method exhibits competitive performance on benchmark datasets, showing its robustness across various applications.

Conclusion/Implications: The research introduces a novel enhancement to probabilistic models through the use of CNFs, offering higher adaptability and precise likelihood estimation. The integration of conditional inputs within normalizing flows represents a substantial advancement in modeling complex distributions. These improvements can be directly applied to a range of tasks, including generative modeling and uncertainty quantification in AI systems, thereby contributing significantly to the fields of machine learning and data analysis.

Keywords: Conditional Normalizing Flows, Likelihood Estimation, Probabilistic Modeling, Neural Networks, Generative Modeling.
