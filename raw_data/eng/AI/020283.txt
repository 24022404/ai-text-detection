Scene flow estimation in 3D point clouds is a critical task for applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality. This paper introduces **FlowNet3D**, a novel deep learning framework designed to accurately estimate scene flow directly from raw 3D point cloud data. Unlike traditional methods that rely on handcrafted features or intermediate representations, FlowNet3D leverages a hierarchical architecture to capture both local and global motion patterns in dynamic 3D scenes. The proposed model employs a combination of point-based feature extraction and flow embedding layers, enabling robust and efficient learning of complex motion dynamics.  

Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including KITTI and FlyingThings3D, demonstrate that FlowNet3D achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The model shows strong generalization capabilities across diverse scenarios, including sparse and noisy point clouds. Furthermore, ablation studies highlight the contributions of key architectural components to the overall performance.  

The results underscore the potential of FlowNet3D as a foundational tool for 3D scene understanding and motion analysis. Its ability to process raw point clouds without requiring voxelization or meshing makes it highly practical for real-world applications. This work advances the field of 3D vision by providing a scalable and effective solution for scene flow estimation, paving the way for future research in dynamic 3D environments.  

**Keywords**: Scene flow, 3D point clouds, deep learning, motion estimation, autonomous systems, FlowNet3D.
