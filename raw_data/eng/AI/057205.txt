This paper proposes a novel Spatially and Temporally Efficient Non-local Attention Network (STEN) for video-based person re-identification, aiming to improve the accuracy and efficiency of existing methods. The objective is to develop a framework that can effectively capture spatial and temporal cues from video sequences to identify individuals across different cameras. Our approach utilizes a non-local attention mechanism to model long-range dependencies between frames, combined with a spatially efficient module to reduce computational costs. Experimental results demonstrate that our STEN network achieves state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and speed. The key findings indicate that our network can efficiently handle large-scale video data and provide robust re-identification results. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to balance spatial and temporal efficiency, making it suitable for real-world applications. The proposed STEN network has significant implications for surveillance, security, and smart city applications, where fast and accurate person re-identification is crucial. Key keywords: person re-identification, non-local attention, video-based, spatially efficient, temporally efficient, deep learning.
