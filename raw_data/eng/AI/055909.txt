Face recognition systems are vulnerable to adversarial attacks, but many current attacks are limited to the digital domain or require specific environmental conditions. This paper explores the creation of physically realizable and robust adversarial stickers that can consistently disrupt face recognition systems in real-world scenarios. We introduce a novel algorithm for generating adversarial stickers, optimizing for both transferability across different face recognition models and robustness to variations in lighting, pose, and distance. Our method leverages a combination of gradient-based optimization and perceptual loss functions to craft stickers that are visually subtle yet highly effective in causing misclassifications. Experimental results demonstrate that our stickers significantly reduce the accuracy of state-of-the-art face recognition systems in physical world tests, outperforming existing physical adversarial attack methods. The findings highlight the potential security risks posed by adversarial examples in face recognition and the need for developing more robust defense mechanisms. Keywords: adversarial attack, face recognition, physical security, adversarial sticker, machine learning, robustness.
