Shape collections are fundamental in various applications such as computer graphics, medical imaging, and 3D data analysis. However, effective representation of these collections is often hindered by variations in orientation, scale, and structure among shapes. This paper addresses the challenge by introducing alignment-aware linear models designed to optimally represent and analyze diverse shape datasets. Our method incorporates an integrated alignment process into the linear modeling framework, enabling joint optimization of shape alignment and basis construction. This approach significantly improves upon traditional principal component analysis (PCA) and other linear models, which typically require pre-aligned data and can suffer from inaccuracies due to misalignment. We evaluate our model on several large-scale 3D shape benchmarks, demonstrating superior reconstruction accuracy, compactness, and robustness to misaligned inputs. Comparative experiments highlight the model’s capability in capturing complex shape variations and streamlining downstream tasks such as shape retrieval, clustering, and interpolation. The proposed alignment-aware linear model offers a scalable, efficient, and interpretable solution for representing shape collections, paving the way for more effective analysis and generation of geometric data in information technology and computer vision. Key contributions include a unified alignment-learning framework, enhanced performance on noisy and real-world datasets, and broad applicability to shape-based machine learning tasks. Keywords: shape representation, alignment, linear models, principal component analysis, 3D shape analysis, computer vision.
