Human attribute recognition (HAR) is a critical task in computer vision with applications in surveillance, human-computer interaction, and biometrics. However, existing methods often struggle with distractions caused by background clutter, occlusions, or irrelevant features, leading to suboptimal performance. This paper proposes a novel Distraction-Aware Feature Learning (DAFL) framework for HAR, leveraging a coarse-to-fine attention mechanism to enhance attribute recognition accuracy. The proposed approach first employs a coarse attention module to identify and suppress irrelevant regions in the input image, followed by a fine-grained attention module that focuses on discriminative features for attribute classification. By iteratively refining attention maps, the model effectively minimizes the impact of distractions while emphasizing relevant visual cues. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including PETA and RAP, demonstrate that DAFL outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of recognition accuracy and robustness. The framework achieves significant improvements in handling complex scenarios with occlusions and cluttered backgrounds. This research contributes a scalable and efficient solution for HAR, with potential applications in real-world systems requiring precise attribute identification.  

**Keywords**: Human attribute recognition, attention mechanism, distraction-aware learning, coarse-to-fine attention, computer vision, feature learning.
