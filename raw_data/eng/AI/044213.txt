This paper introduces a novel approach to Visual Question Answering (VQA) that circumvents the need for traditional question-answer (QA) training data. Our method leverages pre-trained vision-language models and a carefully designed prompting strategy to enable zero-shot VQA capabilities. By formulating the VQA task as a masked language modeling problem, we guide the model to infer answers directly from the visual context and question without explicit QA pairs during fine-tuning. Specifically, we fine-tune a pre-trained model using only image-caption pairs and an auxiliary task focused on reasoning over visual attributes. Experimental results on standard VQA benchmarks demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive performance compared to models trained on extensive QA datasets, particularly in out-of-distribution settings. The findings suggest that learning general visual-semantic relationships can be effectively transferred to VQA, reducing reliance on costly and task-specific annotations. This work contributes a paradigm shift in VQA, paving the way for more efficient and adaptable models. Keywords: Visual Question Answering, Zero-Shot Learning, Vision-Language Models, Prompting, Masked Language Modeling, Image Captioning.
