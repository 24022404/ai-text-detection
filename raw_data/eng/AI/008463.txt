This paper investigates the problem of embedding relational data into Euclidean space while preserving essential structural properties. Our objective is to characterize the types of relations that can be reliably represented in Euclidean embeddings, focusing on identifying necessary and sufficient conditions for low-distortion embeddings. We employ techniques from metric embedding theory, graph theory, and spectral analysis to analyze the embeddability of various relation types, including hierarchical, proximity, and equivalence relations. Specifically, we examine the relationship between the spectral properties of the relation’s adjacency matrix and the dimensionality required for faithful Euclidean embedding. Our findings demonstrate that relations exhibiting specific spectral decay properties can be embedded with bounded distortion. We provide a novel spectral bound that accurately predicts the embeddability of a relation based on its eigenvalue distribution. We further validate our theoretical results through experiments on synthetic and real-world relational datasets, illustrating the practical implications of our findings for network analysis, data visualization, and machine learning applications. The research contributes to a deeper understanding of the limitations and possibilities of Euclidean embeddings for representing complex relational structures. Keywords: Metric Embedding, Relational Data, Euclidean Space, Spectral Analysis, Dimensionality Reduction, Graph Embedding.
