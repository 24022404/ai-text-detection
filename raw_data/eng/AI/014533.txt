This paper presents a novel meta-learning approach for learning transferable representations that can be effectively applied to a single target domain. The objective is to develop a model that can leverage knowledge from multiple source domains to improve performance on a specific target domain, without requiring multiple target domains for training. Our method employs a unique combination of meta-learning algorithms and domain adaptation techniques to learn representations that are both domain-invariant and task-specific. The approach is evaluated on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in performance compared to existing methods. Key findings include the ability to adapt to new target domains with limited labeled data and improved robustness to domain shift. The proposed framework has important implications for real-world applications where data is scarce or expensive to label, and highlights the potential of meta-learning for developing more versatile and generalizable models. Relevant keywords: meta-learning, transfer learning, domain adaptation, representation learning, few-shot learning.
