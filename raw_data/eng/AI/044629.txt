Graph representation learning has emerged as a critical area in machine learning, enabling the extraction of meaningful features from complex graph-structured data. This paper addresses the challenge of learning effective node and graph representations by leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. The objective is to develop a novel framework that optimizes representation learning through adaptive exploration and decision-making processes. We propose a reinforcement learning-based approach that dynamically navigates the graph structure, identifying optimal paths and relationships to enhance feature extraction. The method integrates graph neural networks (GNNs) with RL algorithms, enabling the model to iteratively refine its representations based on reward signals derived from downstream tasks such as node classification and link prediction. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in performance compared to traditional graph representation learning methods, achieving higher accuracy and robustness. The proposed framework also exhibits superior scalability and adaptability to diverse graph types, including social networks, citation graphs, and biological networks. This research contributes to the advancement of graph-based machine learning by introducing a reinforcement-driven paradigm that enhances representation quality and generalizability. The findings have broad implications for applications in recommendation systems, drug discovery, and network analysis, where accurate graph representations are crucial for decision-making. Keywords: graph representation learning, reinforcement learning, graph neural networks, node classification, link prediction.
