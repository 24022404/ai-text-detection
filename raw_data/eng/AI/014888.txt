This paper proposes a novel approach to stochastic gradient algorithms on matrix manifolds, leveraging Riemannian geometry to adaptively optimize the learning process. The objective is to improve the convergence and stability of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) on non-Euclidean spaces, such as matrix manifolds. Our method employs a Riemannian metric to define a adaptive step-size scheme, allowing the algorithm to adjust to the local geometry of the manifold. We utilize a stochastic gradient algorithm with a retraction-based update rule, ensuring that the iterates remain on the manifold. The results demonstrate that our Riemannian adaptive stochastic gradient algorithm outperforms existing methods in terms of convergence rate and accuracy, particularly in applications involving large-scale matrix optimization problems. The key contributions of this work include the development of a novel adaptive step-size scheme and the application of Riemannian geometry to stochastic gradient algorithms on matrix manifolds. Our approach has important implications for machine learning and optimization problems, including low-rank matrix completion, tensor decomposition, and neural network training. Key keywords: Riemannian geometry, stochastic gradient algorithms, matrix manifolds, adaptive optimization, non-Euclidean spaces.
