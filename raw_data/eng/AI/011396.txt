This research addresses the challenge of enabling machine learning models to reason efficiently within large and complex theoretical frameworks without relying on traditional imitation learning techniques, which often require extensive human-labeled data.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel approach that bypasses imitation by utilizing reinforcement learning and unsupervised learning strategies. Our model is trained through self-exploration within a specified theoretical domain, leveraging a reward system that guides it towards valid and optimized reasoning paths. This system incorporates deep neural networks and curriculum learning to progressively enhance reasoning capabilities.

Results/Findings: The proposed method significantly outperforms existing imitation-based models on benchmarks for large theories, showcasing superior reasoning accuracy and speed. Experiments demonstrate that our approach achieves more generalized reasoning skills, reducing dependency on extensive datasets typically required for theory learning. Performance evaluations reveal a remarkable scalability and adaptability to various theoretical fields.

Conclusion/Implications: Our findings underline the potential for autonomous reasoning systems that learn without human imitation, paving the way for more efficient AI applications in fields requiring complex decision-making, such as mathematical proof verification and scientific hypothesis testing. This research contributes to the advancement of AI reasoning technologies by promoting scalable learning solutions that are less reliant on labeled data.

Keywords: Reasoning, Machine Learning, Large Theories, Reinforcement Learning, Unsupervised Learning, Autonomous Systems, AI Models.
