This paper explores the use of score function features for discriminative learning within matrix and tensor frameworks, addressing the challenge of efficiently capturing discriminative patterns in high-dimensional data. We propose a novel approach that leverages score functions to extract meaningful features, enabling enhanced classification and representation learning. The methodology integrates matrix and tensor decompositions to model complex relationships in data, ensuring scalability and computational efficiency. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in classification accuracy and robustness compared to traditional feature extraction methods, particularly in scenarios involving high-dimensional and sparse datasets. The proposed framework also exhibits superior performance in handling multi-modal data, showcasing its versatility across diverse applications. Key contributions include the development of a unified score function-based feature extraction technique and its extension to tensor-based representations, offering a scalable solution for modern machine learning tasks. This work provides valuable insights into the potential of score function features for advancing discriminative learning, with implications for applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and beyond.  

**Keywords**: score function, discriminative learning, matrix decomposition, tensor framework, feature extraction, high-dimensional data, machine learning.
