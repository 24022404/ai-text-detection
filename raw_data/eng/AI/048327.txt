This paper presents a novel approach to night vision obstacle detection and avoidance using bio-inspired vision sensors. The objective is to address the challenges of low-light environments, where traditional vision systems often struggle with accuracy and reliability. Inspired by the visual mechanisms of nocturnal animals, we propose a bio-inspired vision sensor model that enhances sensitivity to low-light conditions while maintaining computational efficiency. The system integrates advanced image processing algorithms and neural network-based object detection to identify and classify obstacles in real-time. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves a 25% improvement in detection accuracy compared to conventional night vision systems, with a processing latency of less than 50 milliseconds per frame. The system's robustness is validated across diverse nighttime scenarios, including urban and rural environments. This research contributes to the development of autonomous navigation systems, particularly for drones and self-driving vehicles, by providing a reliable solution for obstacle detection in low-visibility conditions. The implications extend to applications in surveillance, search-and-rescue operations, and industrial automation, where enhanced night vision capabilities are critical.  

**Keywords**: bio-inspired vision sensors, night vision, obstacle detection, autonomous navigation, low-light environments, neural networks.
