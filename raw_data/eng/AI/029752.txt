The synthesis and editing of realistic person images have significant applications in virtual reality, gaming, and digital content creation. However, existing methods often struggle with maintaining fine-grained control over specific attributes while preserving the overall realism of the generated images. This paper introduces **PISE (Person Image Synthesis and Editing)**, a novel framework based on a **decoupled Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)** architecture. PISE disentangles the synthesis process into two distinct stages: attribute-specific feature extraction and image generation. This decoupling enables precise manipulation of individual attributes, such as clothing, pose, and facial features, without compromising the quality of the final output. Our approach leverages a multi-branch encoder to capture attribute-specific details and a unified generator to synthesize coherent and realistic images. Extensive experiments demonstrate that PISE outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of image quality, attribute control, and editing flexibility. The framework achieves superior performance on benchmark datasets, with significant improvements in perceptual metrics and user evaluations. The results highlight the potential of PISE for applications in personalized avatar creation, virtual try-on systems, and interactive image editing tools. This work contributes a robust and scalable solution for person image synthesis and editing, paving the way for more intuitive and realistic digital human representations.  

**Keywords**: Person image synthesis, image editing, GAN, decoupled learning, attribute manipulation, virtual try-on.
