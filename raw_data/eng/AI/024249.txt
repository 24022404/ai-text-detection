Accurate and efficient detection of crops and weeds is critical for precision agriculture, enabling targeted interventions that optimize resource use and improve crop yields. However, the lack of large, high-quality datasets tailored for agricultural environments remains a significant challenge. This paper presents an automatic model-based dataset generation framework designed to produce synthetic datasets for fast and accurate crop and weed detection. The proposed approach leverages advanced 3D reconstruction techniques and domain randomization to generate highly realistic synthetic images that mimic diverse agricultural conditions, including varying lighting, soil types, and plant growth stages. A deep learning-based detection model is then trained on the synthetic dataset and evaluated against real-world agricultural imagery. Experimental results demonstrate that the synthetic dataset significantly improves detection accuracy, achieving a mean average precision (mAP) of 92.3% on real-world test data, outperforming models trained on manually annotated datasets. The framework also reduces the time and cost associated with manual dataset creation, making it a scalable solution for precision agriculture applications. This research contributes a novel, cost-effective method for dataset generation, paving the way for more robust and adaptable crop and weed detection systems in smart farming.  

**Keywords**: precision agriculture, synthetic dataset, 3D reconstruction, domain randomization, crop detection, weed detection, deep learning.
