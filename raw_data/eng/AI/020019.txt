This paper addresses the challenge of correcting erroneous 3D mesh models by introducing a novel learning-based approach that prioritizes geometric consistency. The objective is to develop a method that can accurately repair and refine mesh models, preserving their geometric integrity and structural coherence. Our approach leverages a deep neural network architecture that learns to predict corrections to faulty mesh elements, ensuring that the resulting models are topologically valid and geometrically consistent. The methodology involves training the network on a large dataset of paired defective and corrected meshes, allowing it to learn the patterns and relationships that define geometric consistency. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, showing significant improvements in mesh quality and accuracy compared to existing correction methods. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a geometric consistency loss function and a mesh refinement strategy that enables the generation of high-quality, error-free mesh models. This work has important implications for various applications, including computer-aided design, 3D printing, and virtual reality, where accurate and consistent mesh models are crucial. Key keywords: geometric consistency, mesh correction, deep learning, 3D modeling, mesh refinement.
