This paper presents a novel approach to self-supervised graph representation learning, focusing on predicting global context to enhance node and graph embeddings. The objective is to develop a method that can effectively capture structural and semantic information in graphs without relying on labeled data. Our approach utilizes a graph neural network framework, incorporating a global context prediction task to learn informative representations. The model is trained to predict global graph properties, such as graph motifs and community structures, which helps to capture long-range dependencies and contextual relationships between nodes. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing self-supervised graph learning approaches, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets. The proposed approach has significant implications for various applications, including graph classification, node classification, and link prediction. Key contributions of this work include the introduction of a global context prediction task, which improves the quality of learned representations, and the development of a flexible framework that can be easily adapted to different graph-structured data. Relevant keywords: self-supervised learning, graph neural networks, global context prediction, graph representation learning, node embeddings.
