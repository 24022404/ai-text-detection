This study aims to develop a global method for identifying trees outside of closed-canopy forests using medium-resolution satellite imagery. Our approach combines machine learning algorithms with spectral and spatial analysis of satellite data to detect and classify trees in non-forest areas. The methodology involves training a model on a large dataset of satellite images with manually annotated tree locations, and then applying the trained model to medium-resolution satellite imagery. Results show that our method achieves high accuracy in identifying trees, with an overall accuracy of 85% and a tree detection rate of 80%. The approach outperforms existing methods, particularly in areas with complex terrain and mixed land cover. Our research contributes to the field of remote sensing and environmental monitoring, providing a valuable tool for mapping and managing trees outside of closed-canopy forests. The method has significant implications for understanding ecosystem services, biodiversity, and carbon sequestration, and can be applied to various fields, including forestry, ecology, and conservation. Key keywords: tree detection, medium-resolution satellite imagery, machine learning, remote sensing, environmental monitoring.
