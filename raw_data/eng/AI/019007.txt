Spectral clustering is a widely used technique for graph partitioning, particularly in sparse graphs where traditional methods often struggle. This paper introduces a unified framework for spectral clustering that addresses key challenges in sparse graph analysis, such as scalability and computational efficiency. The proposed approach integrates advanced matrix factorization techniques with adaptive graph sparsification methods to reduce computational complexity while maintaining clustering accuracy. We evaluate the framework on a variety of benchmark datasets, including real-world sparse graphs from social networks and biological systems. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing spectral clustering algorithms in terms of both runtime and clustering quality, achieving up to a 30% improvement in accuracy on highly sparse graphs. Furthermore, the framework is shown to be robust to noise and scalable to large-scale datasets. The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to unify and optimize spectral clustering for sparse graphs, offering a practical solution for applications in network analysis, community detection, and beyond. This research advances the state-of-the-art in graph clustering and provides a foundation for future developments in spectral methods for sparse data.  

**Keywords**: spectral clustering, sparse graphs, matrix factorization, graph sparsification, network analysis, community detection.
