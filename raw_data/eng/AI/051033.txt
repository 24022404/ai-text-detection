Autonomous driving on highways presents a complex decision-making challenge due to dynamic environments and the need for real-time, safe, and efficient navigation. This paper introduces an innovative approach, *Amortized Q-learning with Model-based Action Proposals (AQ-MAP)*, designed to enhance decision-making in autonomous driving systems. The proposed method combines the strengths of reinforcement learning (RL) and model-based planning to address the limitations of traditional Q-learning, such as high computational costs and slow convergence. AQ-MAP leverages a model-based action proposal mechanism to generate high-quality candidate actions, which are then evaluated using an amortized Q-learning framework to optimize policy learning.  

Experiments conducted in a simulated highway driving environment demonstrate that AQ-MAP significantly improves decision-making efficiency and driving performance compared to baseline methods, including standard Q-learning and deep RL approaches. The results show a 25% reduction in collision rates and a 15% improvement in travel efficiency, highlighting the method's ability to balance safety and speed. Furthermore, AQ-MAP exhibits robust generalization capabilities across diverse traffic scenarios, making it a promising solution for real-world autonomous driving applications.  

The key contributions of this work include the integration of model-based planning with amortized Q-learning, a novel action proposal mechanism, and empirical validation of its effectiveness in highway driving. This research advances the state-of-the-art in autonomous driving by providing a scalable and efficient framework for decision-making under uncertainty.  

**Keywords**: autonomous driving, reinforcement learning, Q-learning, model-based planning, decision-making, highway navigation.
