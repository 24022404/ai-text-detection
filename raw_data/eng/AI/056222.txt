This paper addresses the challenge of interpretability in reinforcement learning (RL), proposing a novel framework that integrates ensemble methods to enhance the transparency and reliability of RL agents. The objective is to bridge the gap between state-of-the-art RL performance and the need for explainable decision-making in critical applications such as healthcare, autonomous vehicles, and finance. The proposed approach constructs an ensemble of RL models whose collective behavior is analyzed to extract actionable insights and interpretable explanations for agent actions. We employ techniques such as majority voting, uncertainty estimation, and feature importance analysis to provide both global and local interpretability of the learned policies. Experimental evaluation is conducted on benchmark RL environments, where our ensemble-based method is compared against traditional single-agent models and existing interpretable RL techniques. Results indicate that our framework achieves competitive or superior performance while substantially improving the clarity of policy decisions and identifying sources of agent uncertainty. The findings demonstrate that ensemble methods can facilitate trust and adoption of RL systems by making their inner workings more accessible to domain experts and stakeholders. This work paves the way for the deployment of interpretable and trustworthy RL solutions in safety-critical and regulated settings. Keywords: reinforcement learning, interpretability, ensemble methods, explainable AI, uncertainty estimation, policy analysis.
