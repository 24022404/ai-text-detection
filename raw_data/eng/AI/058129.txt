This paper addresses the challenges of few-shot object detection, particularly the need for generalized models that can incrementally learn to detect new object categories with minimal labeled data. This problem is critical in scenarios where data acquisition is costly or time-consuming.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework that integrates a meta-learning approach with a dynamically adaptive model architecture. By leveraging a combination of transfer learning and few-shot learning paradigms, the proposed system is capable of incrementally learning new object classes with limited examples. Our model uses a unique hierarchical meta-learning strategy to enhance generalization capabilities across diverse domains and object categories.

Results/Findings: Experiments demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing few-shot and incremental detection methodologies on benchmark datasets. We observe superior generalization to unseen object categories and improved performance in detecting objects with variable visibility and context. The model's adaptability, precision, and efficiency in resource-constrained environments highlight its practical applicability.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a robust solution for generalized and incremental few-shot object detection, paving the way for broader applications in fields such as autonomous systems, robotics, and remote sensing, where rapid and adaptable object recognition is essential. Our findings suggest that the proposed model can reduce the dependency on large annotated datasets, facilitating efficient real-world deployments.

Key Keywords: Few-shot object detection, incremental learning, meta-learning, transfer learning, object recognition, adaptive model architecture.
