Continual semantic segmentation is a critical challenge in computer vision, particularly in dynamic environments where models must adapt to evolving data distributions without requiring labeled data. This paper addresses the problem of unsupervised model adaptation for continual semantic segmentation, enabling models to generalize across domains and tasks without manual annotation. We propose a novel framework that leverages self-supervised learning and domain adaptation techniques to iteratively refine the model's understanding of new data. Our approach combines contrastive learning for feature alignment with a memory-efficient replay mechanism to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in unsupervised continual learning settings, outperforming existing approaches in terms of segmentation accuracy and adaptability. The results highlight the framework's ability to maintain high performance across diverse domains while minimizing computational overhead. This work contributes to advancing unsupervised learning in semantic segmentation, offering a scalable solution for real-world applications such as autonomous driving and robotics, where continuous adaptation is essential.  

**Keywords**: continual learning, semantic segmentation, unsupervised adaptation, domain adaptation, self-supervised learning, contrastive learning.
