The proliferation of probabilistic programming has augmented the ability to model complex stochastic processes, yet efficient inference within these programs remains a significant challenge. This research proposes a novel approach to optimize the inference process for probabilistic programs through meta-learning, aiming to enhance accuracy and efficiency.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a meta-learning framework that automatically learns an inference algorithm tailored for specific probabilistic programs. Our approach leverages gradient-based meta-learning techniques to adapt standard Monte Carlo and variational inference methods. By incorporating a recurrent neural network (RNN) as a meta-learner, we iteratively refine inference strategies to achieve optimal performance across a distribution of tasks.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our meta-learned inference algorithm significantly outperforms traditional inference techniques in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. We provide quantitative evaluations on various benchmark probabilistic programs, illustrating improved posterior approximation and reduced convergence times. The algorithm shows robust generalization across different models and complexity levels.

Conclusion/Implications: This study marks a substantial contribution to the field of probabilistic programming by introducing an adaptive inference mechanism that learns from experience. Our method not only enhances existing probabilistic programming frameworks but also opens new avenues for applying machine learning to automate and improve inference strategies in complex stochastic systems. Future work will explore extending this approach to broader classes of probabilistic models and real-world applications. Key Keywords: meta-learning, inference algorithm, probabilistic programming, Monte Carlo, variational inference, recurrent neural network (RNN).
