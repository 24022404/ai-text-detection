This paper addresses the challenge of accurately computing total kidney volume (TKV) from computed tomography (CT) images in patients with Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD), a condition where kidney volume is a crucial metric for disease progression assessment. Traditional manual or semi-automatic methods are time-consuming and can be error-prone, necessitating a more automated and precise approach.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel application of Multi-Task 3D Convolutional Neural Networks (3D CNNs) to automate the TKV computation process. Our approach simultaneously segments kidney structures and estimates kidney volume by leveraging the capability of 3D CNNs to capture spatial features across three dimensions. The network architecture was designed to incorporate multi-task learning, improving the model's efficiency and accuracy by performing segmentation and volume computation concurrently.

Results/Findings: The proposed model demonstrated superior performance in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency when benchmarked against state-of-the-art techniques for TKV estimation. The 3D CNN achieved high precision and recall in segmenting kidney tissues, leading to a reliable volume computation with an insignificant margin of error compared to manual methods. Additionally, our approach showed robustness across a diverse dataset, indicating strong potential for generalization and clinical application.

Conclusion/Implications: This study introduces an innovative method for automated TKV computation in ADPKD patients, highlighting the model's accuracy and operational efficiency. The implementation of Multi-Task 3D CNNs represents a significant advancement in medical imaging analysis, potentially reducing the burden on healthcare professionals and increasing the reliability of kidney volume assessments. Future work may focus on expanding this approach to other renal conditions and further optimizing the network's architecture. Key keywords include: ADPKD, 3D CNN, multi-task learning, kidney volume computation, CT imaging.
