Objective: Effective deployment of machine learning (ML) models in clinical settings remains a critical challenge due to complexities in data governance, interpretability, and integration with existing workflows. This study presents a comprehensive framework that guides healthcare organizations through the end-to-end process of clinical ML deployment, ensuring safety, reliability, and user acceptance.

Methods: We developed a modular pipeline that addresses key phases—data acquisition and preprocessing, model development and validation, regulatory and ethical compliance, system integration, and post‐deployment monitoring. The pipeline incorporates privacy-preserving techniques (e.g., federated learning), interpretable model architectures, and human‐in‐the‐loop evaluations. We validated the framework on two real‐world case studies: predicting hospital readmission risk and automating diabetic retinopathy screening.

Results: Compared to conventional ad hoc deployment strategies, our framework reduced model integration time by 35% and improved clinician trust scores by 20% in user surveys. Federated learning approaches achieved on‐site model performance within 2% of centralized baselines while maintaining patient data locality. Interpretability modules enabled faster error analysis, decreasing false‐positive alerts by 15% in clinical pilot tests. Regulatory checklists and audit logs streamlined institutional review board approvals, cutting administrative overhead by 40%.

Conclusion: Our structured deployment framework offers a scalable, transparent pathway for translating clinical ML research into practice. By emphasizing data privacy, interpretability, and stakeholder engagement, it bridges gaps between prototype models and routine clinical use. This work paves the way for more robust, ethical, and widely adoptable ML solutions in healthcare.

Keywords: clinical machine learning, deployment framework, interpretability, data privacy, healthcare informatics
