This paper addresses the challenge of recognizing printed text in ultra-low-resolution images, a critical problem in fields like archival digitization and automated document processing. Standard Optical Character Recognition (OCR) systems struggle with low-quality images, necessitating a specialized approach to accurately extract textual information.

Methods: We introduce an innovative end-to-end OCR approach specifically designed for ultra-low-resolution printed text images. Our solution leverages an enhanced deep learning model, combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for effective feature extraction and a recurrent neural network (RNN) for sequence prediction. The system is trained on a uniquely curated dataset of low-resolution text images, employing data augmentation techniques to improve robustness and generalization.

Results: Our model significantly outperforms existing OCR systems on benchmarks involving low-resolution images, yielding a marked improvement in character recognition accuracy and processing speed. Comparative analysis with traditional methods demonstrates our approach’s superior ability to handle severe resolution constraints, providing clear advantages in both accuracy and computational efficiency.

Conclusion: This research presents a novel, highly effective solution for OCR in ultra-low-resolution settings, expanding the applicability of OCR technology to previously challenging scenarios. Our approach offers substantial improvements over traditional methods and opens up new possibilities for digitizing and processing degraded textual content. Potential applications span various domains, including historical document preservation, automated reading of scanned documents, and resource-constrained environments such as mobile devices.

Keywords: Optical Character Recognition (OCR), ultra-low-resolution images, deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), text recognition, document digitization.
