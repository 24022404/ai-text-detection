The accurate classification of blood cells is critical for diagnosing various medical conditions, yet manual analysis remains time-consuming and prone to human error. This study presents an end-to-end computer vision-based approach for multi-class classification of blood cells, leveraging deep learning techniques to automate and enhance diagnostic accuracy. We propose a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture optimized for high-resolution microscopy images, incorporating data augmentation and transfer learning to address challenges such as class imbalance and limited labeled datasets. The model is trained and validated on a publicly available dataset comprising multiple blood cell types, including red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, with an accuracy of 98.7% and superior precision-recall metrics compared to existing methods. The system also exhibits robustness to variations in image quality and lighting conditions, making it suitable for real-world clinical applications. This research contributes a scalable and efficient solution for automated blood cell analysis, with potential implications for improving diagnostic workflows in healthcare settings. Key innovations include the integration of advanced preprocessing techniques and a lightweight model architecture that balances accuracy and computational efficiency. Keywords: blood cell classification, computer vision, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, medical diagnosis, image processing.
