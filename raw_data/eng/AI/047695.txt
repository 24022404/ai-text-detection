This paper presents a novel approach to reinforcement learning by introducing Deep Ordinal Reinforcement Learning (DORL), which leverages ordinal information to improve learning efficiency and policy performance. Traditional reinforcement learning methods typically operate under the assumption of real-valued rewards and treat outcomes as scalar feedback. In contrast, our approach models the reward signals and value functions as ordinal variables, capturing the inherent ranking or preference among outcomes without requiring precise numerical values. We propose a deep neural network architecture specifically designed to process ordinal data and integrate it within both value-based and policy-based reinforcement learning frameworks. Experimental evaluations on standard benchmark environments, including Atari and OpenAI Gym tasks, demonstrate that DORL outperforms conventional reinforcement learning algorithms in terms of sample efficiency, robustness to noisy or imprecise reward signals, and overall policy quality. The results highlight that leveraging ordinal information can lead to more stable learning dynamics and improved generalization across tasks. This research opens new directions for robust decision-making in domains where rewards are naturally ordinal or where obtaining accurate scalar rewards is challenging. Keywords: deep reinforcement learning, ordinal data, value function, sample efficiency, policy optimization, ordinal rewards.
