This paper investigates the application of potential-based reward shaping to the classic puzzle game Sokoban, aiming to enhance the learning efficiency and performance of reinforcement learning (RL) agents. Sokoban poses significant challenges for RL due to its sparse rewards and complex state space, often leading to slow convergence and suboptimal policy learning. To address these issues, we introduce a potential-based reward shaping framework that incorporates domain knowledge into the reward structure while preserving the original optimal policies. Our methodology builds on defining a suitable potential function that guides the agent toward intermediate goals without altering the fundamental solution paths. We evaluate the approach using a deep Q-network (DQN) as the baseline agent and analyze performance metrics such as learning speed, solution optimality, and generalization to unseen levels. Experimental results demonstrate that potential-based reward shaping leads to faster convergence, higher success rates, and improved exploration compared to conventional sparse reward settings. Our findings suggest that the careful design of reward shaping functions can significantly impact RL agent performance in challenging environments like Sokoban, paving the way for more efficient training in other goal-driven tasks. Keywords: Reinforcement learning, reward shaping, Sokoban, potential functions, deep Q-networks, sparse rewards, game AI.
