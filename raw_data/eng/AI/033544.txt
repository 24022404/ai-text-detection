This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised video object segmentation by introducing a novel approach based on instance embedding transfer. The objective is to enable accurate segmentation of primary objects in videos without requiring manual annotations. Our method leverages a pre-trained instance embedding model and transfers its knowledge to the target video domain through a self-supervised learning framework. The approach utilizes a combination of contrastive learning and temporal consistency constraints to adapt the embeddings to the video data. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. Key findings include improved segmentation accuracy and robustness to variations in object appearance and motion. The proposed method contributes to the field of computer vision by providing a flexible and efficient solution for unsupervised video object segmentation, with potential applications in areas such as autonomous driving, surveillance, and video editing. Relevant keywords: unsupervised video object segmentation, instance embedding, transfer learning, self-supervised learning, contrastive learning.
