This paper investigates the convergence of temporal difference (TD) learning for policy tracking in non-stationary Markov Decision Processes. Policy tracking aims to adapt a learning agent's policy to match a time-varying target policy. We introduce an adiabatic theorem that provides sufficient conditions for TD-learning to accurately track the target policy, assuming the target policy changes slowly over time. Our analysis focuses on linear TD-learning and establishes a bound on the tracking error, demonstrating that the error is proportional to the rate of change of the target policy. The theoretical findings are validated through numerical experiments on benchmark reinforcement learning tasks. These results offer insights into the stability and adaptability of TD-learning algorithms in dynamic environments, contributing to the development of robust reinforcement learning systems.
**Keywords:** Reinforcement Learning, Temporal Difference Learning, Policy Tracking, Non-stationary MDP, Adiabatic Theorem, Convergence Analysis.
