Bayesian networks are powerful probabilistic graphical models widely used for reasoning under uncertainty. However, learning their structure from data remains a challenging task, particularly due to the combinatorial nature of directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). This paper addresses the problem of DAG structure learning by exploring continuous optimization techniques that avoid the need for discrete search. We propose a novel framework that leverages gradient-based methods to optimize a smooth, differentiable score function over the space of DAGs. Our approach eliminates the need for explicit acyclicity constraints by incorporating a penalty term that enforces DAG properties during optimization. Through extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets, we demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance in terms of structure recovery and scalability compared to traditional discrete optimization approaches. The results highlight the effectiveness of continuous optimization in simplifying the learning process while maintaining accuracy. This work contributes to advancing the field of Bayesian network learning by providing a practical and efficient alternative to conventional methods, with potential applications in causal inference, decision-making systems, and probabilistic modeling.  

**Keywords**: Bayesian networks, DAG structure learning, continuous optimization, gradient-based methods, probabilistic graphical models.
