**Abstract:**

This paper revisits the Adam optimization algorithm, one of the most widely used methods for training deep neural networks, through the novel perspective of weighted past gradients. The objective is to better understand Adam’s update mechanism by analyzing how it accumulates and weights historical gradients over time. We propose a unified framework that decomposes Adam’s moving averages into explicit weight assignments for prior gradients, allowing for a clearer interpretation of its adaptive learning behavior. Through both theoretical analysis and empirical experiments, we demonstrate that this perspective sheds light on Adam’s strengths and limitations, particularly in terms of convergence stability and performance with sparse or non-stationary data. Comparisons with related algorithms, such as RMSProp and SGD with momentum, further highlight how weighted gradient accumulation impacts optimization dynamics. Our findings suggest guidelines for adjusting Adam’s hyperparameters to better suit various machine learning tasks. In conclusion, viewing Adam from the weighted past gradients viewpoint not only provides deeper insights into its operation but also opens avenues for developing improved adaptive optimization methods. Keywords: Adam optimizer, adaptive learning rate, neural network training, gradient weighting, stochastic optimization, machine learning.
