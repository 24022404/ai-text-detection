Semi-supervised classification is a critical challenge in machine learning, particularly when labeled data is scarce. This paper introduces a novel approach, *Masked Label Prediction* (MLP), which unifies message passing mechanisms for semi-supervised learning tasks. The proposed model leverages a unified framework to predict masked labels in a graph-based structure, enabling efficient information propagation between labeled and unlabeled nodes. By integrating a self-supervised learning objective, MLP enhances the model's ability to generalize from limited labeled data while maintaining robustness across diverse datasets. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that MLP outperforms existing methods in terms of classification accuracy and scalability. The results highlight the model's effectiveness in handling sparse labels and its potential for real-world applications, such as social network analysis and recommendation systems. This work contributes a scalable and adaptable solution to semi-supervised classification, bridging the gap between graph-based and self-supervised learning paradigms.  

**Keywords**: Semi-supervised learning, graph neural networks, message passing, masked label prediction, self-supervised learning, classification.
