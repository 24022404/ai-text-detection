Single image super-resolution (SISR) aims to reconstruct high-resolution images from their low-resolution counterparts, a task critical for applications in medical imaging, satellite imagery, and digital restoration. While deep learning-based methods have dominated this field, they often require extensive computational resources and large datasets for training. This paper explores the surprising effectiveness of texture transfer techniques for SISR, offering a lightweight and efficient alternative. We propose a novel approach that leverages texture synthesis algorithms to transfer high-frequency details from reference textures to low-resolution images, enhancing their visual quality without the need for complex neural networks. Our method combines patch-based texture matching with adaptive blending to ensure seamless integration of transferred details. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art deep learning models, particularly in scenarios with limited computational resources or training data. The findings highlight the potential of texture transfer as a complementary tool in the SISR pipeline, offering a balance between computational efficiency and image quality. This work opens new avenues for resource-efficient super-resolution techniques, with implications for real-time applications and edge computing environments.  

**Keywords**: single image super-resolution, texture transfer, patch-based synthesis, computational efficiency, image enhancement.
