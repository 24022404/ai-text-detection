This paper introduces Deep Ordinal Reinforcement Learning (DORL), a novel framework designed to enhance reinforcement learning by integrating ordinal scales in decision-making processes. Traditional reinforcement learning approaches typically rely on cardinal information, which may not effectively capture the nuances of many real-world scenarios where only ordinal rankings are available or desirable.

Methods/Approach: We propose a new model that incorporates ordinal data into reinforcement learning by adapting existing deep learning architectures. Specifically, our approach involves the integration of ordinal regression techniques with deep Q-networks, enabling an agent to learn effective policies based on ranking information rather than precise numerical rewards. This framework is implemented using a combination of neural networks and ordinal regression loss functions, ensuring the agent's learning mechanism is attuned to ordinal distinctions.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that DORL significantly outperforms traditional reinforcement learning models in environments where data is naturally or more appropriately represented ordinally. By conducting a series of benchmark tests on varied environments, ranging from simple mazes to complex game scenarios, we observed improved efficiency and accuracy in the agent's decision-making capabilities. Comparative analysis also shows that DORL achieves superior convergence rates and robustness in learning with ordinal data.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed deep ordinal reinforcement learning framework provides a groundbreaking method for enhancing the flexibility and applicability of reinforcement learning in real-world applications. Its ability to effectively handle ordinal information opens new avenues for research and potential applications in fields such as autonomous driving, healthcare management, and resource allocation problems, where ordinal data often prevails. This research contributes to the ongoing exploration of integrating learning models with non-traditional data types, underscoring the versatility of reinforcement learning paradigms. Keywords: ordinal data, reinforcement learning, deep learning, Q-networks, ordinal regression.
