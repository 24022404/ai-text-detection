Autotuning exascale applications presents a significant challenge due to the complexity of optimizing performance across diverse hardware and software configurations. This paper investigates the application of multitask and transfer learning techniques to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of autotuning strategies. We propose a novel approach that leverages knowledge gained from tuning a set of related applications to accelerate the autotuning process for new, unseen applications within the exascale domain. Our method involves training a shared model on multiple tasks, where each task represents the optimization of a specific application, followed by fine-tuning the model on a target application with limited data. Experiments conducted on a range of high-performance computing benchmarks demonstrate that our multitask transfer learning approach achieves significant speedup in autotuning convergence and improved performance compared to traditional single-task autotuning methods. Specifically, we observe an average 20% reduction in tuning time and a 10% improvement in application performance. These results highlight the potential of multitask and transfer learning to overcome the challenges of autotuning complex exascale applications, enabling more efficient utilization of computational resources.

Keywords: Autotuning, Exascale Computing, Multitask Learning, Transfer Learning, Performance Optimization, High-Performance Computing.
