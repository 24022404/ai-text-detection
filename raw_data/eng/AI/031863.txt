This paper addresses the challenge of inference in structured Gaussian process models, where the complexity of the model and the size of the dataset often render traditional methods infeasible. Our objective is to develop an efficient and scalable approach for performing inference in these models. We propose a gray-box inference method that leverages the structure of the model to reduce the computational cost of inference, while still providing accurate estimates of the model parameters. Our approach combines the benefits of black-box inference methods, which are flexible but computationally expensive, with the efficiency of white-box methods, which are fast but limited in their applicability. The results show that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, outperforming existing methods on several benchmark datasets. The key contributions of this work are the development of a novel inference algorithm that exploits the structure of the model, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in a range of applications, including spatial modeling and time-series forecasting. Our approach has significant implications for the application of Gaussian process models to large and complex datasets, and highlights the importance of structured inference methods in machine learning. Key keywords: Gaussian process models, gray-box inference, structured inference, machine learning, Bayesian inference.
