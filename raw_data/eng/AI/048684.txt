The detection of anomalies in high-dimensional data is a critical task across various domains, from cybersecurity to finance. This paper presents the Extended Isolation Forest (EIF), an advancement of the original Isolation Forest algorithm designed to enhance anomaly detection accuracy and robustness. The EIF introduces two major extensions: improved random partitioning techniques and adaptive splitting criteria, which address the limitations of standard Isolation Forests when dealing with complex or high-dimensional datasets. Our method dynamically adjusts to data distributions, reducing bias introduced by axis-parallel splits and effectively isolating outliers that traditional approaches often overlook. Extensive experiments were conducted on both synthetic and real-world benchmark datasets, demonstrating that the Extended Isolation Forest consistently outperforms baseline methods in terms of precision, recall, and overall detection rates. The proposed algorithm also maintains computational efficiency, rendering it practical for large-scale and real-time applications. The findings suggest the EIF is a reliable and scalable solution for unsupervised anomaly detection, with significant implications for machine learning, data mining, and cybersecurity analytics. Key contributions include the novel adaptive mechanisms for data partitioning and comprehensive evaluations confirming the model’s superiority in diverse scenarios. Keywords: anomaly detection, Extended Isolation Forest, unsupervised learning, high-dimensional data, adaptive algorithms, data mining.
