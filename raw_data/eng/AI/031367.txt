In volumetric medical imaging, accurate segmentation is critical for diagnosis and treatment planning. However, fully supervised approaches demand extensive labeled data, which are often limited and difficult to obtain. This study introduces momentum contrastive voxel-wise representation learning as a method to improve semi-supervised volumetric medical image segmentation, aiming to reduce dependence on labeled datasets while maintaining high segmentation accuracy.

Methods/Approach: The proposed method employs a momentum contrastive learning framework to enhance voxel-wise representation learning by constructing a dynamic model that adapts to unlabeled data. It leverages a dual-branch architecture consisting of an encoder trained using momentum contrast and a robust segmentation head to perform volumetric segmentation. The model captures fine-grained spatial and contextual information, thereby improving the segmentation performance even with minimal labeled data. Key features of the approach include a contrastive loss function that enhances voxel representation and a momentum update mechanism to stabilize learning.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations on benchmark volumetric medical imaging datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing semi-supervised methods, achieving superior segmentation accuracy with significantly reduced labeled training data. Comparative analyses highlight improved voxel-wise consistency and boundary delineation, with metrics showing enhanced precision and recall. The effectiveness of the momentum contrastive learning framework in maintaining performance stability across various medical imaging modalities is also validated.

Conclusion/Implications: This research provides a novel contribution to the domain of medical image segmentation by introducing a momentum contrastive representation learning approach that excels in semi-supervised settings. The method’s ability to minimize labeled data requirements without compromising accuracy offers promising implications for clinical practice, potentially fostering more accessible and efficient medical diagnosis processes. The findings underscore the potential applications of this methodology in diverse medical scenarios, advocating for further exploration in different imaging contexts.

Key Keywords: Momentum Contrastive Learning, Voxel-wise Representation, Semi-supervised Learning, Volumetric Medical Image Segmentation, Medical Imaging, Contrastive Loss.
