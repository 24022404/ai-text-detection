The objective of this research is to address the critical challenge of detecting occluded prohibited items in X-ray security inspections, a task essential for enhancing safety measures in transportation hubs and other secure facilities. Current systems struggle with accurately identifying items obscured by other objects, leading to potential security lapses. To overcome this limitation, we introduce a novel benchmark dataset specifically designed to tackle occlusion issues in X-ray imagery, encompassing a wide spectrum of prohibited items under diverse occlusion scenarios. Our methodological contribution is the development of a De-occlusion Attention Module (DAM), which enhances feature extraction capabilities and improves item detection accuracy even in complex visibility conditions. The DAM leverages advanced attention mechanisms to selectively focus on informative regions of the image, effectively reconstructing obscured details. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves superior performance compared to existing models, reflected by improved detection accuracy and reduced false-negative rates. The research highlights the potential of integrating the DAM within existing X-ray inspection frameworks, significantly improving system reliability and security standards. Our findings not only present a robust framework for occlusion-challenged environments but also contribute vital data and tools for future studies in automated security inspection systems. Key keywords associated with this study include X-ray security inspection, occluded item detection, de-occlusion, attention mechanisms, and image processing benchmarks.
