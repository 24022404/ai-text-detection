The objective of this research is to introduce and evaluate a novel approach termed Non-Transferable Learning (NTL) for model verification and authorization, addressing security and intellectual property challenges in deploying AI models. Traditional models often face risks related to unauthorized replication or misuse, necessitating robust mechanisms to ensure controlled access and use. The proposed NTL approach integrates cryptographic techniques with machine learning frameworks to safeguard model integrity while allowing legitimate verification processes. Our method involves embedding non-transferable tokens within model architectures, ensuring that any unauthorized transfer or copying results in model non-functionality or errors in deployment. Experimental evaluation demonstrates that NTL retains model performance with minimal overhead while significantly enhancing security features compared to conventional transfer learning and model verification methods. Results show that our approach prevents unauthorized usage, ensuring proprietary models retain their operational exclusivity and intellectual value. The implications of NTL extend to secure deployment in sensitive applications, such as financial technology and bioinformatics, where model authenticity and protection against tampering are paramount. Our study contributes to the field by providing a scalable, effective solution for AI model owners to maintain control over their intellectual assets, fostering trust and reliability in AI systems. Key terms include model security, authorization, cryptographic integration, and non-transferable tokens.
