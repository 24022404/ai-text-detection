In high-stakes decision-making contexts, the reliance on black box machine learning models poses significant ethical and operational challenges due to their lack of interpretability. This paper aims to address the pressing need for transparency in machine learning applications, particularly in sectors such as healthcare, finance, and criminal justice, where decisions can profoundly impact lives. We propose an innovative framework that prioritizes the use of interpretable machine learning models over their opaque counterparts. Our approach integrates model selection and evaluation criteria tailored to enhance interpretability without sacrificing performance. Through a series of empirical studies, we demonstrate that interpretable models not only provide insights into decision-making processes but also achieve comparable accuracy to traditional black box models. Key findings reveal that stakeholders in high-stakes environments prefer transparent models that allow for easier validation and trust, leading to improved user confidence and ethical compliance. Ultimately, this research contributes to the discourse on responsible AI practices, recommending a paradigm shift toward interpretable models as a standard for high-stakes applications. This study underscores the potential of interpretable machine learning to foster accountability while delivering robust performance in critical decision-making processes. 

Keywords: interpretable models, black box machine learning, high-stakes decisions, ethical AI, model transparency.
