Entity resolution (ER), the task of identifying and linking records that refer to the same real-world entity, is a critical challenge in data integration and cleaning. Traditional ER methods often rely on exhaustive pairwise comparisons, which are computationally expensive and impractical for large datasets. This paper introduces a cost-effective variational active learning framework for entity resolution, designed to minimize annotation costs while maintaining high accuracy. The proposed approach leverages a variational autoencoder (VAE) to model the latent representations of entity pairs, enabling efficient uncertainty sampling for active learning. By iteratively selecting the most informative samples for human annotation, the method significantly reduces the number of required labeled instances compared to passive learning strategies. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves competitive accuracy with up to 60% fewer annotations, outperforming state-of-the-art active learning baselines. The approach is particularly effective in scenarios with limited labeled data, making it a practical solution for real-world ER tasks. This work contributes a novel, scalable, and cost-efficient method for entity resolution, with potential applications in data cleaning, record linkage, and knowledge graph construction.  

**Keywords**: Entity resolution, active learning, variational autoencoder, cost-effective, data integration, uncertainty sampling.
