Recent advancements in single image super-resolution (SISR) have primarily focused on deep learning architectures and loss functions to generate high-quality high-resolution images from their low-resolution counterparts. However, challenges remain in reconstructing fine texture details and achieving perceptually realistic outputs. This paper investigates a novel approach leveraging texture transfer techniques to enhance SISR performance. By integrating a texture transfer module that utilizes self-similarity and external high-quality texture databases, our method selectively enhances local textures while preserving global image structure. Experimental evaluations on standard SISR benchmarks demonstrate that our approach achieves superior peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM) scores compared to state-of-the-art convolutional neural network (CNN) based models. Furthermore, perceptual studies indicate a significant improvement in image realism and fine detail reconstruction. This research highlights the "unreasonable effectiveness" of incorporating texture transfer, even with lightweight models, for producing visually compelling super-resolved images. The findings suggest that texture-aware methods represent a promising direction for future SISR research and practical applications in fields such as photography, medical imaging, and remote sensing. Keywords: super-resolution, texture transfer, deep learning, image enhancement, perceptual quality.
