Mortality prediction is critical in healthcare, yet often relies on structured data, which can be incomplete or delayed. This paper explores the potential of unstructured clinical notes for enhanced mortality prediction. We propose a novel approach leveraging transformer-based language models, specifically fine-tuning BERT on a large corpus of clinical notes to capture nuanced contextual information. Our method incorporates techniques for handling the inherent noise and variability within free-text data, including specialized tokenization and attention mechanisms. We evaluate our model on a large, real-world dataset of patient records, demonstrating significant improvements in prediction accuracy compared to traditional models using structured data alone and other state-of-the-art NLP methods. The results indicate that free-text clinical notes offer valuable insights for mortality prediction, potentially enabling earlier intervention and improved patient outcomes. This work highlights the feasibility and effectiveness of using unstructured data for critical healthcare tasks, paving the way for more comprehensive and timely risk assessment.

Keywords: Mortality Prediction, Clinical Notes, Natural Language Processing, BERT, Transformer, Unstructured Data, Healthcare, Machine Learning
