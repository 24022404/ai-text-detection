This paper proposes a novel Topology-Aware Generative Adversarial Network (TAGAN) designed to jointly predict multiple brain graphs from a single brain graph. The objective is to address the challenge of predicting complex brain connectivity patterns from limited data. Our approach utilizes a generative adversarial framework that incorporates topological information of brain graphs, enabling the model to learn and preserve the underlying structural relationships. The TAGAN model is trained on a single brain graph and generates multiple brain graphs that capture diverse connectivity patterns. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of TAGAN in predicting brain graphs with high accuracy and fidelity, outperforming existing methods. The key findings highlight the importance of topology-aware modeling in brain graph prediction, with potential applications in neuroimaging analysis, brain disorder diagnosis, and personalized medicine. This research contributes to the development of advanced generative models for brain graph analysis, with implications for the field of neuroscience and neuroinformatics, and is relevant to keywords such as brain graph prediction, generative adversarial networks, topology-aware modeling, and neuroimaging analysis.
