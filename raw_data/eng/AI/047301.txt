This paper presents a novel approach to adversarial learning through the introduction of Generative Adversarial Forests (GAFs), designed to address the challenges of model stability and training efficiency in traditional adversarial frameworks. The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness of generative models against adversarial attacks while improving the conditioning of learned representations. We propose a hybrid methodology that integrates decision tree ensembles with adversarial training mechanisms, thereby leveraging the strengths of both structured learning and generative modeling. 

Our experiments demonstrate that GAFs outperform conventional adversarial models in terms of both stability and quality of generated samples, as evidenced by improved performance on benchmark datasets. Key findings indicate that GAFs not only achieve superior robustness against adversarial perturbations but also produce samples with higher diversity and fidelity. 

In conclusion, the introduction of Generative Adversarial Forests significantly contributes to the field of adversarial learning by providing a new framework that mitigates issues related to mode collapse and overfitting. This work opens avenues for more reliable applications of generative models in security-sensitive domains such as image synthesis, domain adaptation, and anomaly detection. 

Keywords: Generative Adversarial Forests, adversarial learning, model stability, decision trees, generative models.
