Semi-supervised hashing has emerged as a promising approach for efficient similarity search in large-scale datasets, leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data. This paper introduces a novel **Pairwise Teacher-Student Network (PTSN)** for semi-supervised hashing, designed to enhance the quality of hash codes by effectively utilizing pairwise similarity information. The proposed framework consists of two key components: a teacher network that generates pseudo-labels for unlabeled data based on pairwise relationships, and a student network that learns robust hash functions by minimizing the discrepancy between labeled and pseudo-labeled data. To achieve this, we employ a contrastive learning strategy that optimizes the similarity preservation between data pairs while reducing the impact of noisy pseudo-labels. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that PTSN outperforms state-of-the-art semi-supervised hashing methods in terms of retrieval accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the effectiveness of leveraging pairwise relationships in semi-supervised settings, offering significant improvements in scenarios with limited labeled data. This work contributes to advancing semi-supervised hashing techniques, with potential applications in large-scale image retrieval, recommendation systems, and data compression.  

**Keywords**: Semi-supervised hashing, pairwise similarity, teacher-student network, contrastive learning, hash code learning, similarity search.
