This study aims to develop a predictive model for determining extubation readiness in extremely preterm infants by analyzing cardiorespiratory behavior. To address the challenges of limited and imbalanced datasets, we employed undersampling and bagging techniques in conjunction with decision trees. Our approach utilized a combination of clinical variables and machine learning algorithms to identify key predictors of successful extubation. The results showed that the proposed model achieved high accuracy and reliability in predicting extubation readiness, outperforming traditional methods. The use of undersampling and bagging techniques improved the robustness and generalizability of the model, allowing for more accurate predictions in this high-risk population. This research contributes to the development of personalized and data-driven approaches for neonatal care, highlighting the potential of machine learning in improving clinical outcomes for extremely preterm infants. Key keywords: extubation readiness, preterm infants, decision trees, undersampling, bagging, machine learning, cardiorespiratory behavior.
