This paper introduces *Deep Scattering*, a novel approach for rendering realistic atmospheric clouds using radiance-predicting neural networks. The objective of this research is to address the computational challenges and visual inaccuracies associated with traditional cloud rendering techniques, which often rely on physically-based simulations or heuristic approximations. To achieve this, we propose a deep learning-based framework that leverages a neural network trained to predict radiance distributions directly from cloud density fields. The network architecture incorporates multi-scale feature extraction and attention mechanisms to capture the complex scattering and absorption behaviors of light within volumetric cloud structures.  

Our experiments demonstrate that *Deep Scattering* significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of rendering speed and visual fidelity, achieving near-photorealistic results in real-time applications. Quantitative evaluations show a 40% reduction in rendering time compared to state-of-the-art Monte Carlo-based approaches, while maintaining comparable accuracy. Additionally, the proposed method generalizes well to diverse cloud formations and lighting conditions, making it suitable for applications in gaming, virtual reality, and weather simulation.  

The key contribution of this work lies in the integration of neural networks with physically-inspired rendering, enabling efficient and high-quality cloud visualization without sacrificing realism. This approach opens new possibilities for real-time rendering of complex atmospheric phenomena and highlights the potential of machine learning in advancing computer graphics.  

**Keywords**: atmospheric rendering, neural networks, radiance prediction, real-time rendering, volumetric scattering, cloud simulation.
