Video action classification is a critical task in computer vision, with applications ranging from surveillance to human-computer interaction. However, existing methods often struggle to effectively leverage multimodal data, such as visual and audio cues, due to challenges in modality alignment and feature fusion. This paper introduces a novel framework, Mutual Modality Learning (MML), designed to enhance video action classification by jointly learning and integrating complementary information from multiple modalities. The proposed approach employs a cross-modal attention mechanism to dynamically align and weight features from visual and audio streams, enabling the model to focus on the most relevant information for each action. Additionally, a modality-specific contrastive loss is introduced to ensure robust feature representation across modalities. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including UCF101 and HMDB51, demonstrate that MML achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods by significant margins. The results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed framework in capturing intricate relationships between modalities and improving classification accuracy. This work contributes to the field by providing a scalable and efficient solution for multimodal video analysis, with potential applications in real-time action recognition and video understanding systems.  

**Keywords**: Video action classification, multimodal learning, cross-modal attention, contrastive loss, feature fusion.
