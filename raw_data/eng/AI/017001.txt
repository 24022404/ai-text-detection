This paper presents a comprehensive comparative study of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), a class of artificial intelligence models that have gained prominence for their ability to generate realistic data. The objective of this research is to analyze and compare various GAN architectures and their respective performance in diverse applications such as image generation, data augmentation, and anomaly detection. Our approach involves implementing a selection of prominent GAN variants, including but not limited to Deep Convolutional GAN (DCGAN), Wasserstein GAN (WGAN), and StyleGAN, across standardized datasets to evaluate their efficiency and effectiveness. Key findings of this study reveal significant variations in output quality, training stability, and computational resource requirements among the examined GAN models. The results demonstrate that certain architectures excel in specific tasks while others offer a balanced performance across multiple applications. In conclusion, this study not only provides insights into the strengths and limitations of different GAN models but also guides future research efforts in selecting the most appropriate architecture for specific uses. The implications of our findings suggest potential enhancements in GAN deployment for practical applications, contributing to the advancement of AI-driven content generation. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, DCGAN, WGAN, StyleGAN, AI models, image generation, data augmentation.
