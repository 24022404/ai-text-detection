This paper introduces a novel approach to monocular video visual odometry and depth estimation using a recurrent neural network (RNN) framework. Our method addresses the challenges of unsupervised and supervised learning by leveraging temporal coherence in video sequences to simultaneously predict camera pose and scene depth. The proposed network architecture incorporates a recurrent layer to effectively capture and exploit long-range dependencies between consecutive frames, leading to improved accuracy and robustness compared to existing single-frame methods. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including KITTI, showing competitive results in both supervised and unsupervised settings. Furthermore, we analyze the impact of different recurrent cell types and training strategies on the overall performance. Key findings highlight the importance of temporal context in learning accurate and consistent depth maps and pose estimations. The research contributes a robust and versatile framework for monocular visual odometry and depth estimation, suitable for applications in autonomous navigation and robotics.
Keywords: Visual Odometry, Depth Estimation, Recurrent Neural Networks, Unsupervised Learning, Monocular Vision, Deep Learning.
