Graph matching is a fundamental problem in computer science with applications in areas such as computer vision, bioinformatics, and network analysis. This paper addresses the challenge of computing optimal assignments for approximate graph matching efficiently. We propose a novel linear-time algorithm that significantly reduces the computational complexity compared to existing methods, which often rely on quadratic or higher-order time complexities. Our approach leverages a combination of graph decomposition techniques and dynamic programming to achieve optimal assignments while maintaining high accuracy in matching. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art algorithms in terms of both runtime and matching precision across various benchmark datasets. The algorithm's linear-time complexity makes it particularly suitable for large-scale graph matching tasks, enabling real-time applications in domains requiring rapid and accurate graph comparisons. This work contributes a scalable and efficient solution to the graph matching problem, with potential implications for improving performance in tasks such as image recognition, molecular structure alignment, and social network analysis.  

**Keywords**: graph matching, linear-time algorithm, optimal assignments, dynamic programming, large-scale graphs.
