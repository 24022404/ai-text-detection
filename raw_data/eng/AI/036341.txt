This study aims to address the challenge of evaluating various reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms on real-world robotic platforms to understand their practical performance and applicability. The inherent complexity and unpredictability of real-world environments necessitate a comprehensive benchmarking process to identify the most effective RL techniques for robotic applications.

Methods/Approach: We conducted a series of experiments using a diverse set of RL algorithms, including both traditional and state-of-the-art models, on physical robots tasked with executing complex, real-world tasks. The platforms involved encompass a range of robots differing in size, functionality, and operational environment. The evaluation process incorporated a standardized set of metrics focusing on learning efficiency, adaptability, and robustness.

Results/Findings: The study highlights significant performance variations among the tested RL algorithms, with some models demonstrating superior adaptability and efficiency in dynamic environments. Notably, algorithms incorporating model-based learning and sophisticated exploration strategies exhibited improved performance metrics compared to others. These findings underscore the importance of algorithm choice in robotic RL applications, particularly concerning speed of convergence and adaptability to environmental variations.

Conclusion/Implications: The research provides valuable insights into the strengths and limitations of different RL algorithms when applied to real-world robotic scenarios. The results can guide practitioners in selecting appropriate RL techniques for specific robotic applications, ultimately enhancing the deployment of intelligent robotic systems in practical settings. This benchmarking serves as a foundation for further exploration and optimization of RL algorithms tailored for robotic applications. Key keywords include reinforcement learning, real-world robotics, algorithm benchmarking, model-based learning, and robotic adaptability.
