De Bruijn graphs have become integral to various modern applications in biology, playing a crucial role in genome assembly processes. Tools such as EULER-USR and innovative techniques developed by Pevzner and colleagues have significantly advanced the field by addressing and resolving one of the chief challenges—repeats in DNA fragment assembly. These methods enable efficient and accurate reconstruction of entire genomes from fragmented sequencing data, which is often complicated by recurring DNA sequences. The utility of De Bruijn graphs extends beyond assembly, having been adapted for global DNA sequence alignment, thereby improving our ability to compare extensive complex sequences. Additionally, these graph-based approaches form the backbone of several recent studies into DNA storage, underscoring their continued relevance and versatility in computational biology.
