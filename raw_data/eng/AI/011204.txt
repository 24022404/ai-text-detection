This paper introduces a Multi-Subspace Neural Network (MSNN) framework for improved image recognition tasks. The primary objective is to address the challenges of intra-class variation and feature redundancy commonly encountered in traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The proposed MSNN architecture decomposes the feature space into multiple subspaces, enabling the network to capture diverse discriminative patterns across different dimensions. Each subspace is processed by dedicated subnetworks, and their outputs are fused to generate a comprehensive image representation. Extensive experiments were conducted on several benchmark image recognition datasets, demonstrating that MSNN consistently outperforms standard CNN-based approaches in terms of classification accuracy and robustness to noisy data. The results show notable improvements particularly in scenarios involving complex and heterogeneous visual categories. The study further investigates the efficiency of subspace selection and its effect on computational complexity, revealing that the MSNN achieves higher performance with reduced parameter overhead compared to conventional models. These findings highlight the effectiveness and scalability of leveraging multi-subspace learning for advanced computer vision systems. Potential applications include object recognition, facial analysis, and automated medical imaging analysis. Keywords: image recognition, neural networks, multi-subspace learning, feature representation, deep learning.
