This paper introduces DoWhy, a framework designed to address the challenges of expressing and validating causal assumptions in data analysis. The objective is to provide a transparent and systematic approach to causal inference, enabling researchers to explicitly state and test their assumptions. DoWhy achieves this through a simple, yet powerful, language for causal models, allowing users to formalize their hypotheses and identify potential biases. The framework employs a combination of statistical and machine learning techniques to validate causal assumptions, ensuring the reliability of the results. Key findings demonstrate the effectiveness of DoWhy in various domains, including healthcare and social sciences, where causal inference is crucial. The conclusion highlights the significance of DoWhy in promoting rigorous and reproducible causal analysis, with potential applications in decision-making and policy-making. By providing a robust and user-friendly framework, DoWhy contributes to the advancement of causal inference, making it more accessible and trustworthy. Relevant keywords: causal inference, causal assumptions, DoWhy, transparency, reproducibility, statistical analysis, machine learning.
