This paper addresses the challenge of bridging 3D visual data and natural language by proposing Part2Word, a novel framework for learning joint embeddings of point clouds and descriptive text. The objective is to enhance cross-modal understanding by aligning semantic components of 3D objects—captured as point cloud parts—with textual descriptors at the word level. To achieve this, Part2Word leverages deep neural architectures: a point cloud encoder segments and represents object parts, while a parallel text encoder processes associated descriptions. Through a part-to-word matching mechanism and a contrastive learning objective, the model encourages semantic correspondences between geometric structures and language. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that Part2Word significantly outperforms existing methods on cross-modal retrieval and fine-grained part annotation tasks, showcasing improved alignment and interpretability between modalities. The results highlight the model's ability to facilitate tasks such as 3D object search by natural language queries and part-level captioning. Part2Word’s approach offers important implications for multi-modal representation learning, enabling more intuitive human-computer interaction in 3D modeling, computer vision, and robotics applications. Keywords: point cloud, joint embedding, cross-modal learning, 3D object recognition, natural language processing, contrastive learning, part segmentation.
