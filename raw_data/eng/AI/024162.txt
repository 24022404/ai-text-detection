This paper investigates the application of posterior sampling methods in reinforcement learning (RL) contexts, particularly evaluating worst-case regret bounds to assess performance efficiency. The study aims to enhance our understanding of how stochastic decision-making techniques perform under adversarial scenarios, addressing gaps in current RL frameworks related to computational efficiency and robustness.

Methods: We propose a novel approach leveraging posterior sampling to derive and analyze regret bounds in reinforcement learning environments. Our method incorporates Bayesian updates and explores the asymptotic behavior of regret, offering a direct comparison with other established RL strategies, such as Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) and Epsilon-Greedy algorithms. Theoretical analysis is conducted to evaluate its efficacy against worst-case adversities.

Results: Our findings demonstrate that the proposed posterior sampling approach achieves competitive regret bounds, closely approximating the optimal rates under varying conditions. Notably, our method exhibits significant improvements in computational complexity over traditional strategies, while maintaining a high degree of robustness in adversarial setups. Detailed simulations corroborate these theoretical predictions, showcasing superior performance across a range of simulated environments.

Conclusion: The research contributes a new perspective on implementing posterior sampling for RL, emphasizing its potential for achieving near-optimal performance with manageable computational resources. This framework broadens the applicability of RL solutions in complex, adversarial domains, offering promising insights for future developments in autonomous systems and decision-making algorithms. Keywords such as reinforcement learning, posterior sampling, regret bounds, Bayesian methods, and adversarial environments highlight the core themes of this study.
