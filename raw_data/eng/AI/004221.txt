This paper presents a novel approach to learning inference algorithms for probabilistic programs using meta-learning. The objective is to develop a flexible and efficient method for approximating posterior distributions in complex probabilistic models. Our approach involves training a meta-model to learn the structure of probabilistic programs and generate effective inference algorithms. We employ a model-agnostic meta-learning algorithm that can adapt to various probabilistic programming languages and models. The results show that our meta-learned inference algorithm outperforms traditional inference methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency. Our approach has significant implications for applications in artificial intelligence, machine learning, and data science, enabling more efficient and accurate probabilistic modeling and inference. Key contributions include the development of a meta-learning framework for probabilistic programs, improved inference accuracy, and reduced computational cost. Relevant keywords: meta-learning, probabilistic programming, inference algorithms, model-agnostic meta-learning, artificial intelligence, machine learning.
