Motion segmentation in videos captured by moving cameras presents a significant challenge due to the complex interplay of object and camera motion. This paper introduces a novel probabilistic model designed to address causal motion segmentation in dynamic scenes. The proposed approach leverages a probabilistic framework to distinguish between independently moving objects and background motion caused by camera movement. By integrating motion cues and spatial-temporal dependencies, the model effectively identifies and segments moving objects in real-time video sequences. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly in scenarios with rapid camera motion and occlusions. The findings highlight the model’s robustness and its potential applications in autonomous navigation, surveillance, and video analysis. This research contributes a scalable and efficient solution to motion segmentation, offering significant advancements in handling dynamic environments with moving cameras.  

**Keywords**: motion segmentation, probabilistic model, moving camera, dynamic scenes, real-time video analysis, autonomous navigation.
