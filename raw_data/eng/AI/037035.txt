This paper introduces Graph InfoClust, a novel approach to unsupervised graph representation learning that leverages cluster-level node information to improve the quality of learned node embeddings. The objective is to develop a method that can effectively capture the structural and semantic properties of graphs without relying on labeled data. Our approach employs a clustering-based framework to identify densely connected node groups, which are then used to inform the representation learning process. By incorporating cluster-level information, Graph InfoClust is able to learn more informative and discriminative node representations. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines on several benchmark datasets. The key findings highlight the importance of considering cluster-level node information in graph representation learning, leading to improved performance in downstream tasks such as node classification and graph clustering. Our research contributes to the development of more advanced graph representation learning techniques, with potential applications in areas like social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics. Key keywords: graph representation learning, unsupervised learning, node embeddings, clustering, graph neural networks.
