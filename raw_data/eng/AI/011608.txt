Weakly supervised semantic segmentation (WSSS) aims to train segmentation models using only image-level labels, reducing annotation costs. Current methods often rely on class activation maps (CAMs) generated from image classification networks as cues, which typically highlight only the most discriminative parts of objects, leading to incomplete object coverage. To address this, we propose a novel framework that fully utilizes the classifiers for WSSS by modifying and enhancing the cues extracted from them. Specifically, we introduce a multi-stage approach that refines CAMs through iterative expansion and suppression, leveraging both object and background information. Furthermore, we incorporate a novel cue enhancement module that leverages the classifier's feature representations to generate more complete and accurate cues. Our method demonstrates significant improvements in segmentation accuracy on benchmark datasets, including PASCAL VOC 2012 and COCO, surpassing state-of-the-art WSSS approaches. The results highlight the effectiveness of fully leveraging classifier information for generating high-quality pseudo-labels, leading to improved weakly supervised semantic segmentation. Keywords: Weakly Supervised Semantic Segmentation, Class Activation Maps, Image Classification, Deep Learning, Computer Vision.
