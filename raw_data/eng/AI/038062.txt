The rapid advancement of video generation techniques has sparked interest in leveraging structured representations for improved synthesis. This paper introduces the *Generative Video Transformer (GVT)*, a novel framework that treats objects in video sequences as semantic units akin to words in natural language processing. The primary objective is to explore whether object-centric representations can enhance the quality and interpretability of generated videos. GVT employs a transformer-based architecture that integrates object detection and tracking mechanisms to extract and model object-level dynamics, enabling the generation of coherent and contextually rich video content.  

Our approach involves a two-stage process: first, objects are detected and tracked across frames to form a spatiotemporal object graph; second, a transformer model is trained to predict object trajectories and interactions, which are then rendered into video sequences. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that GVT outperforms existing video generation methods in terms of visual fidelity, temporal consistency, and semantic alignment. Notably, the object-centric approach provides interpretable intermediate representations, offering insights into the generative process.  

The key contribution of this work lies in bridging the gap between object-level understanding and video generation, paving the way for applications in video editing, simulation, and augmented reality. By treating objects as the fundamental building blocks of video, GVT opens new avenues for research in structured video synthesis and understanding.  

**Keywords**: Video generation, transformer models, object-centric representation, spatiotemporal modeling, generative AI.
