This paper investigates the consistency of semi-supervised learning (SSL) algorithms on graphs, focusing on two prominent methods: the probit and one-hot approaches. The objective is to evaluate the theoretical and practical performance of these methods in achieving consistent predictions across graph-structured data, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. We propose a unified framework to analyze the convergence properties of both methods under varying graph topologies and label distributions. Through extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets, we demonstrate that the probit method exhibits superior consistency in low-label regimes, while the one-hot method performs better in scenarios with moderate label availability. Our findings reveal that the choice of SSL algorithm significantly impacts prediction stability and accuracy, with the probit method showing robustness to graph sparsity and noise. The results provide valuable insights for practitioners in selecting appropriate SSL techniques for graph-based applications, such as social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics. This study contributes to the growing body of research on SSL by offering a comparative analysis of two widely used methods and highlighting their strengths and limitations in different contexts.  

**Keywords**: semi-supervised learning, graph-based learning, probit method, one-hot method, consistency, graph topology, label distribution.
