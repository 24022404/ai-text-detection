Objective: We introduce Head2Head, a video-based neural framework for high-fidelity head synthesis and reenactment. Our goal is to generate photorealistic talking heads that faithfully preserve identity, expression, and motion dynamics from arbitrary source and driving videos.

Methods: Head2Head employs a two-stage pipeline. First, a lightweight encoder extracts 3D-aware facial features—including pose, expression coefficients, and appearance embeddings—from input frames. Second, a generator network conditioned on these features produces output frames using a novel Temporal Consistency Module that enforces smooth transitions and reduces flicker. Adversarial training with a multi-scale discriminator and perceptual loss ensures sharp details and realistic skin texture, while a geometry-guided attention mechanism aligns key facial regions across time.

Results: We evaluate Head2Head on several public talking-head benchmarks, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance in identity preservation (identity cosine similarity > 0.95), expression accuracy (landmark error < 2 px), and frame quality (FID score 18.7). Comparative user studies indicate a 35% preference over existing GAN-based reenactment methods. Our system runs at 25 fps on a single GPU with 512×512 resolution, enabling real-time applications.

Conclusion: Head2Head advances video-driven head synthesis by combining 3D semantic guidance with temporal regularization, yielding stable, high-resolution outputs suitable for telepresence, virtual avatars, and film production. Its modular design allows easy integration with downstream tasks like voice-driven animation or AR filters.

Keywords: video-based neural synthesis; talking-head reenactment; temporal coherence; 3D head modeling; generative adversarial network; real-time performance.
