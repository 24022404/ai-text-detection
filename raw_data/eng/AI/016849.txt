Point cloud analysis has become increasingly crucial for applications in 3D vision, robotics, and autonomous systems. Traditional convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures are ineffective on point clouds due to their irregular, unordered nature. This paper introduces KPConv, a flexible and deformable convolutional operator specifically designed for point cloud processing. KPConv represents convolutional kernels as sets of learnable points, known as kernel points, enabling the convolution operation to adapt to the local geometric structure of the data. The proposed method supports both rigid and deformable kernel configurations, allowing for greater adaptability to complex 3D shapes. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets for tasks such as semantic segmentation and object classification demonstrate that KPConv achieves superior accuracy compared to existing point-based and voxel-based methods. The results highlight improvements in both segmentation quality and computational efficiency. KPConv’s ability to capture intricate spatial patterns while maintaining flexibility renders it effective for a wide range of 3D vision applications. This work offers a novel and practical approach for applying deep learning to unstructured point cloud data, with significant implications for autonomous navigation, industrial inspection, and augmented reality systems.

Keywords: KPConv, point cloud, 3D vision, deformable convolution, deep learning, semantic segmentation, kernel points
