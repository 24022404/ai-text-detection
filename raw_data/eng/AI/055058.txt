**Abstract**

Early stopping is a widely-used technique to prevent overfitting in deep neural network training, typically requiring a separate validation set and regular monitoring of validation loss. However, allocating a validation set can be costly in data-limited scenarios and may decrease the effective size of the training data. In this work, we introduce a novel channel-wise early stopping method that eliminates the need for a validation set by leveraging Non-Negative Kernel (NNK) polytope interpolation. Our approach monitors the geometric evolution of feature channels during training by modeling each channel’s behavior through the lens of NNK polytopes, allowing us to identify the optimal stopping point when overfitting begins to emerge. We evaluate our method across multiple benchmark datasets and architectures, demonstrating competitive or superior performance compared to traditional early stopping methods that utilize a validation set. Notably, our channel-wise approach adapts to the learning dynamics of individual layers, offering improved generalization and potentially shorter training times. The proposed method presents a new direction for resource-efficient model selection and training, especially in scenarios where every data point is valuable. Key contributions include the first application of NNK polytope interpolation for early stopping, a channel-specific monitoring mechanism, and extensive empirical validation.  
**Keywords:** Early stopping, validation set, deep learning, channel-wise analysis, NNK polytope, model generalization, training efficiency
