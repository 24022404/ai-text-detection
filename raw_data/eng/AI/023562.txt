This paper presents a novel approach to 3D parametric wireframe extraction utilizing distance fields, addressing the challenge of accurately reconstructing complex 3D models from raw data. Our objective is to develop an efficient and robust method for extracting wireframes, which are essential for various applications in computer-aided design, engineering, and architecture. We propose a distance field-based technique that leverages the advantages of implicit surface representations to generate high-quality wireframes. Our approach employs a combination of geometric and topological analysis to identify and extract the underlying wireframe structure from the distance field. The results demonstrate significant improvements in accuracy and efficiency compared to existing methods, particularly in handling complex and noisy datasets. The key contributions of this research include a robust and efficient wireframe extraction algorithm, improved handling of topological ambiguities, and the ability to generate high-quality wireframes for a wide range of 3D models. This work has important implications for applications such as 3D modeling, computer-aided design, and robotics, where accurate and efficient wireframe extraction is crucial. Key keywords: 3D reconstruction, wireframe extraction, distance fields, implicit surface representation, computer-aided design.
