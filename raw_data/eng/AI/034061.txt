Deep face recognition has achieved remarkable progress, yet remains challenged by variations in appearance (e.g., illumination, expression) and geometry (e.g., pose, occlusion). This paper introduces a novel approach to boost deep face recognition performance by explicitly disentangling appearance and geometry features. We propose a Disentangled Representation Learning Network (DRL-Net) that leverages adversarial learning and feature factorization to extract independent appearance and geometry representations from face images. Specifically, we utilize a shared encoder to extract features, followed by two separate branches to learn appearance and geometry representations, respectively. Adversarial training enforces the independence of these representations. Furthermore, we introduce a novel Geometry-Aware Attention Module (GAAM) to selectively focus on geometrically important regions, enhancing the robustness of our model to pose and occlusion variations. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including LFW, CFP-FP, and AgeDB-30, demonstrate that our DRL-Net significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods. The disentangled representations and the GAAM contribute to improved accuracy and robustness in face recognition, offering a promising direction for handling challenging variations in real-world scenarios. Keywords: Face Recognition, Disentanglement, Adversarial Learning, Attention Mechanism, Deep Learning.
