**Abstract:**

3D shape reconstruction from ambiguous and sparse 2D sketches remains a significant challenge in computer vision and computer graphics. This paper presents a novel approach utilizing multi-view convolutional neural networks (MVCNNs) to accurately infer detailed 3D geometries from single or multiple hand-drawn sketches. Our method first encodes input sketches from multiple viewpoints using 2D convolutional networks, capturing essential structural and stylistic cues. These multi-view features are then fused and processed to predict a voxel-based 3D shape representation. Comprehensive evaluations on both synthetic and real-world sketch datasets demonstrate that our model outperforms existing single-view and volumetric approaches in terms of reconstruction accuracy and consistency, particularly when handling complex or incomplete sketches. The results highlight the robustness of our network to sketch variations and its ability to generalize across different object categories. This research offers a significant advancement for sketch-based modeling, computer-aided design, and intuitive human-computer interaction. Key contributions include the integration of multi-view feature learning with convolutional architectures, extensive quantitative and qualitative analyses, and discussion of potential applications in digital content creation.  
**Keywords:** 3D reconstruction, sketch-based modeling, multi-view convolutional networks, deep learning, voxel representation, computer vision.
