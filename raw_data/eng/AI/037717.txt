Bounding box regression is a critical component in object detection tasks, yet achieving precise localization remains a challenge due to misalignment between predicted and ground-truth boxes. This paper introduces SCALoss, a novel loss function designed to improve bounding box regression by explicitly aligning the sides and corners of predicted boxes with their ground-truth counterparts. Unlike traditional loss functions that focus solely on minimizing coordinate differences, SCALoss incorporates geometric constraints to ensure better alignment of box boundaries. The proposed method leverages a combination of side alignment and corner alignment terms, enabling the model to learn more accurate spatial relationships. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including COCO and Pascal VOC, demonstrate that SCALoss significantly enhances localization accuracy compared to state-of-the-art loss functions. The results show consistent improvements in mean Average Precision (mAP) across various object detection frameworks, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed approach. SCALoss offers a simple yet powerful solution for improving bounding box regression, with potential applications in autonomous driving, surveillance, and medical imaging. This work contributes to advancing object detection by addressing the limitations of existing loss functions and providing a more robust framework for precise localization.  

**Keywords**: Bounding box regression, object detection, loss function, SCALoss, geometric alignment, localization accuracy.
