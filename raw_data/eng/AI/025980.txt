This paper introduces a novel context-aware, video-based risk descriptor designed to enhance cyclist safety. Our approach addresses the limitations of existing risk assessment methods by incorporating real-time visual data and contextual information to provide a more comprehensive understanding of potential hazards. We employ a deep learning model trained on a large dataset of cyclist videos annotated with environmental and traffic conditions to identify and classify risk factors. The system analyzes video frames to detect objects (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians), predict their trajectories, and assess the likelihood of collisions, while simultaneously integrating contextual data such as weather conditions, road type, and time of day to refine risk assessments. Experimental results demonstrate that our system achieves significantly higher accuracy in predicting cyclist risk compared to baseline models relying solely on contextual data or simple object detection. The proposed risk descriptor offers a valuable tool for developing advanced cyclist safety systems, including adaptive warning systems and autonomous vehicle interactions with cyclists, ultimately contributing to a reduction in cyclist accidents. Keywords: Cyclist Safety, Risk Assessment, Video Analysis, Context Awareness, Deep Learning, Computer Vision.
