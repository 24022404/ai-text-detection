Volumetric image segmentation is critical in medical imaging, yet its reliance on extensive labeled data poses a significant challenge. This paper introduces a novel few-shot segmentation approach leveraging 'Squeeze & Excite' (SE) attention mechanisms to guide the segmentation of volumetric images with limited annotations. Our method incorporates SE blocks to dynamically recalibrate channel-wise feature responses, enhancing the model's ability to focus on salient features relevant to target objects, even with only a few labeled examples. We propose a meta-learning framework where the model learns to generalize from a set of support images and corresponding segmentation masks to segment unseen query images. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our SE-guided approach achieves superior segmentation accuracy compared to existing few-shot segmentation techniques, particularly in scenarios with extremely limited labeled data. Specifically, our method shows significant improvements in Dice score and Intersection over Union (IoU) metrics. The findings highlight the potential of SE attention in improving the efficiency and effectiveness of few-shot learning for volumetric image segmentation, reducing the annotation burden in medical image analysis. Keywords: Few-shot segmentation, Volumetric images, Squeeze & Excite, Attention mechanism, Meta-learning, Medical imaging.
