Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) have achieved remarkable success across various domains, yet their robustness to adversarial perturbations remains a critical concern. This paper addresses the challenge of quantifying the robustness of DNNs by introducing a novel probabilistic framework grounded in Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) learning theory. The proposed approach leverages PAC-based bounds to estimate the likelihood of a DNN maintaining correct predictions under input perturbations, providing a rigorous and scalable method for robustness analysis. Unlike traditional deterministic methods, our probabilistic framework accounts for uncertainty and variability in adversarial scenarios, offering a more comprehensive evaluation of model reliability. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of the approach, showing significant improvements in robustness estimation compared to existing techniques. Furthermore, the method is computationally efficient, making it suitable for large-scale DNNs. This work contributes to the field by bridging the gap between theoretical robustness guarantees and practical applications, enabling safer deployment of DNNs in real-world systems. Key implications include enhanced trustworthiness of AI systems and improved resilience against adversarial attacks.  

**Keywords**: Deep Neural Networks, Robustness Analysis, PAC Learning, Adversarial Perturbations, Probabilistic Framework, AI Trustworthiness.
