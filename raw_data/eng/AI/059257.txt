This paper presents a novel approach to exploration in complex environments by leveraging a latent dynamics model to maximize information gain. The objective is to develop an efficient exploration strategy that adapts to the underlying dynamics of the system, enabling agents to learn effective representations and make informed decisions. Our approach employs a probabilistic latent variable model to capture the dynamics of the environment, and an information-theoretic objective to guide exploration. The method involves training a latent dynamics model using a variational inference framework, and then using this model to plan exploratory actions that maximize the mutual information between the agent's observations and the latent state. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing exploration methods in terms of learning efficiency and final performance, particularly in environments with complex dynamics. The key contributions of this work include a novel latent dynamics model for exploration, and a theoretically grounded approach to information-maximizing exploration. Our approach has significant implications for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and other areas where efficient exploration is critical. Key keywords: latent dynamics model, information-maximizing exploration, probabilistic latent variable models, variational inference, mutual information, exploration strategies.
