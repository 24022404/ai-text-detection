This paper presents a novel approach to designing runtime-throttleable neural networks, enabling dynamic adjustment of computational resources to meet varying performance and efficiency requirements. Our objective is to develop a flexible and adaptive neural network architecture that can be seamlessly throttled during runtime, allowing for optimal trade-offs between accuracy, latency, and energy consumption. We propose a modular and hierarchical framework that leverages a combination of knowledge distillation, pruning, and quantization techniques to achieve throttleability. Our approach is evaluated on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in efficiency and adaptability compared to state-of-the-art methods. The results show that our runtime-throttleable neural networks can achieve up to 50% reduction in computational cost while maintaining comparable accuracy. This research contributes to the development of more efficient and scalable AI systems, with potential applications in edge computing, real-time processing, and resource-constrained devices. Key keywords: neural networks, runtime throttleability, adaptive computing, knowledge distillation, pruning, quantization, edge AI.
