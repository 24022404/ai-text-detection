This paper addresses the challenge of estimating mutual information in contrastive representation learning, a crucial component in deep learning frameworks. Our objective is to develop an efficient and accurate method for estimating mutual information, which is essential for learning effective representations. We propose a novel approach, Decomposed Mutual Information Estimation, which decomposes the mutual information into simpler, more manageable components, allowing for more accurate estimation. Our method utilizes a combination of neural networks and information-theoretic techniques to estimate the mutual information between inputs and representations. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of estimation accuracy and computational efficiency. The key findings of our research include improved representation learning capabilities, enhanced robustness to noise and outliers, and increased scalability to high-dimensional data. Our contributions have significant implications for contrastive learning, self-supervised learning, and representation learning, with potential applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and recommender systems. Key keywords: mutual information estimation, contrastive representation learning, deep learning, neural networks, information theory.
