This paper explores the interaction between deep learning-based object detectors and Siamese network-based trackers in video surveillance systems. Our objective is to enhance tracking accuracy and robustness by leveraging the strengths of both components. We propose a novel framework that dynamically adjusts the detector's confidence threshold based on the tracker's performance, creating a feedback loop for improved object association. Specifically, when the tracker's confidence in its prediction decreases, the system lowers the detection threshold to recover potentially missed objects. Conversely, when the tracker performs reliably, the detection threshold is raised to minimize false positives. We evaluate our approach on challenging video surveillance datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in tracking accuracy, particularly in scenarios with occlusions, cluttered backgrounds, and frequent object disappearances. Experimental results show a 15% increase in overall MOTA score compared to baseline methods with static detection thresholds. This adaptive interaction strategy offers a practical solution for enhancing the performance of video surveillance systems.
Keywords: Object detection, Siamese tracking, video surveillance, deep learning, adaptive thresholding, object tracking.
