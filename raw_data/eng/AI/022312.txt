This paper presents a novel approach to reinforcement learning, combining the strengths of cover tree data structures with Bayesian inference to create a more efficient and adaptive decision-making framework. The objective is to improve the exploration-exploitation trade-off in complex, high-dimensional environments. Our method, Cover Tree Bayesian Reinforcement Learning, utilizes a cover tree to partition the state space, reducing the computational complexity of Bayesian updates. We employ a Bayesian neural network to model the uncertainty of the environment, allowing for more informed exploration and exploitation. Results show that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of cumulative reward and sample efficiency, particularly in environments with large state spaces. The key findings highlight the importance of balancing exploration and exploitation using Bayesian principles and efficient data structures. This research contributes to the development of more robust and adaptive reinforcement learning algorithms, with potential applications in robotics, game playing, and autonomous systems, and is relevant to areas such as Bayesian reinforcement learning, decision-making under uncertainty, and efficient exploration strategies.
