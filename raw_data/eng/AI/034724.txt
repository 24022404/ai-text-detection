Few-shot learning aims to recognize new classes with limited labeled examples. This paper introduces Sparse Spatial Transformers (SST), a novel attention mechanism designed to improve feature alignment and reduce overfitting in few-shot classification tasks. SST leverages a sparse attention map to selectively focus on relevant spatial regions within feature maps, thereby enhancing discriminative feature extraction and reducing the influence of noisy background information. The proposed method is integrated into a meta-learning framework, where a spatial transformer network learns to align features between support and query images in an episodic training paradigm. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including miniImageNet and tieredImageNet, demonstrate that SST achieves state-of-the-art performance compared to existing few-shot learning approaches. Specifically, SST exhibits improved accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with extremely limited training data. The results highlight the efficacy of sparse attention in learning transferable feature representations for few-shot learning. Key words: Few-shot learning, Meta-learning, Spatial Transformer, Attention Mechanism, Sparse Attention, Image Classification.
