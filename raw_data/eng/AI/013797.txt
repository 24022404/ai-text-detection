Objective: Sparse phase retrieval seeks to recover signals with few nonzero components from intensity-only measurements, a problem central to imaging, communications, and optics. This paper introduces a novel stochastic alternating minimizing method (SAM) that addresses the computational and sample-efficiency challenges of existing approaches. 

Methods: SAM alternates between two key steps—phase estimation and sparse signal update—using randomly selected measurement subsets at each iteration. In the phase‐estimation step, current signal estimates are combined with measured magnitudes to form linearized subproblems. In the sparse‐update step, a truncated gradient descent with hard‐thresholding enforces sparsity. By processing mini-batches of measurements, SAM reduces per-iteration cost and memory requirements. We provide theoretical analysis proving that, under standard sub‐Gaussian measurement assumptions, SAM converges linearly to the true support and achieves exact recovery with high probability when the number of measurements scales as O(k log n), where k is the sparsity level and n the signal dimension.

Results: Experiments on synthetic and real‐world datasets demonstrate that SAM outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both recovery accuracy and runtime. In particular, SAM attains exact support recovery with 20–30% fewer measurements and reduces computation time by up to 50% compared to batch alternating minimization and convex relaxation techniques. Robustness tests under additive noise further validate its practical utility.

Conclusion: The proposed stochastic alternating minimizing method offers a scalable, efficient, and theoretically grounded solution for large-scale sparse phase retrieval. Its low memory footprint and fast convergence make it well suited for real-time imaging systems, high-throughput microscopy, and communication channels relying on intensity measurements. 

Keywords: sparse phase retrieval, alternating minimization, stochastic optimization, hard-thresholding, compressive sensing.
