This paper addresses the challenge of generating accurate and contextually relevant captions for images captured in heavy rain conditions, a task complicated by visual distortions and reduced image clarity. Traditional captioning models often struggle with such scenarios due to their reliance on standard visual features that fail to account for weather-induced noise. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel encoder learning framework that leverages semantic visual feature matching to enhance the quality of image captions under heavy rain. Our approach integrates a dual-path encoder architecture, combining a weather-robust feature extraction module with a semantic alignment mechanism to ensure that the generated captions align closely with the underlying visual content. The proposed model is trained on a curated dataset of heavy rain images paired with human-annotated captions, enabling it to learn robust representations of rain-affected scenes. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in caption accuracy and relevance compared to state-of-the-art methods, with a 15% increase in BLEU-4 score and a 20% improvement in semantic coherence. The findings highlight the effectiveness of semantic visual feature matching in addressing the unique challenges posed by adverse weather conditions. This work contributes to advancing image captioning technologies for real-world applications, particularly in autonomous systems and environmental monitoring, where accurate scene understanding in challenging weather is critical.  

**Keywords**: Image captioning, heavy rain, semantic feature matching, encoder learning, weather-robust models, visual distortions.
