As the number of simultaneous connections increases, the data expose how system efficiency adapts or degrades, revealing the limitations and capabilities intrinsic to the M1 Pro architecture. At lower connection counts, latency naturally trends toward the lower percentiles, indicating the processor’s ability to handle requests swiftly and efficiently with minimal delay—demonstrating the proficiency of the performance and efficiency cores working in tandem. Meanwhile, requests per second and throughput are maximized, affirming the system’s high concurrency handling and resource utilization within this optimal range. However, as connection numbers escalate, the summary statistics show a rise in median and higher-end latencies, accompanied by increasing standard deviation. This trend points to variability in response time, possibly attributed to resource contention or scheduling limits as the system copes with greater demand. The 99th percentile latency, particularly, serves as a stark indicator of worst-case response scenarios, illuminating potential choke points or architectural stress zones within the CPU or memory subsystems under heavy load. Through this granular approach to performance measurement, the analysis not only captures a complete spectrum—from routine to outlier experiences—but also underscores the real-world impact on user-facing applications that depend on consistent and reliable server responses. By contextualizing throughput and req/sec alongside latency across these percentiles, the evaluation portrays the processor's shifting sweet spot—balancing high efficiency with the onset of diminishing returns or bottlenecks as connection workloads grow.
