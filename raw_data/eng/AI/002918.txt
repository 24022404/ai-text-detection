This paper explores the concept of unsupervised environment design as a means to foster emergent complexity and achieve zero-shot transfer in artificial intelligence systems. The objective is to create environments that can autonomously generate diverse and complex scenarios, allowing agents to learn generalizable skills without explicit supervision. Our approach leverages a novel combination of generative models and reinforcement learning to design environments that adaptively challenge agents, promoting the development of robust and transferable abilities. Results demonstrate that agents trained in these environments exhibit improved performance on unseen tasks, showcasing the potential for zero-shot transfer. Key findings highlight the importance of environment complexity and diversity in facilitating the emergence of generalizable skills. The implications of this research contribute to the development of more autonomous and adaptable AI systems, with potential applications in areas such as robotics, game playing, and simulated learning environments. Keywords: unsupervised learning, environment design, emergent complexity, zero-shot transfer, reinforcement learning, generative models, artificial intelligence.
