Spatial mapping is a critical task in fields such as robotics, geospatial analysis, and environmental monitoring, where accurate modeling of spatial phenomena is essential. This paper presents a novel approach to spatial mapping by leveraging Gaussian Processes (GPs) enhanced with nonstationary Fourier features. Traditional GPs with stationary kernels often struggle to capture complex, varying patterns in real-world spatial data. To address this limitation, we introduce a nonstationary kernel representation using Fourier feature expansions that adapt their spectral properties across the spatial domain. Our method enables efficient learning and inference by providing a scalable approximation to nonstationary GPs, making it suitable for large and heterogeneous datasets. Through extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world spatial mapping benchmarks, we demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms conventional GP models and state-of-the-art stationary feature techniques in terms of prediction accuracy and robustness to nonuniform data distributions. The proposed framework offers a flexible and efficient solution for high-fidelity spatial mapping, with potential applications in autonomous navigation, environmental monitoring, and urban planning. Key contributions include the integration of adaptive Fourier features into GP modeling and a thorough evaluation of their impact on spatial prediction tasks. Keywords: spatial mapping, Gaussian processes, nonstationary kernels, Fourier features, spatial modeling, machine learning, robot mapping.
