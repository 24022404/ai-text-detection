The paper presents a novel approach titled Pareto GAN, designed to enhance the representational power of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for modeling heavy-tailed distributions, which are prevalent in various real-world datasets. Traditional GANs often struggle to accurately capture such distributions, limiting their effectiveness in certain applications.

Methods/Approach: The proposed Pareto GAN introduces a unique extension to conventional GAN architectures by integrating a Pareto distribution-based modeling framework. This approach leverages the characteristics of the Pareto distribution to better represent data with heavy tails. The architecture incorporates specialized components that dynamically adapt the generative process to account for the thick-tailed behavior, ensuring robust performance across diverse datasets.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that Pareto GANs significantly outperform traditional GAN models in capturing the tail behavior of heavy-tailed distributions. Quantitative metrics and visualizations showcased improved fidelity in the generated samples, offering a marked increase in the realism and diversity of produced outputs. Comparisons with existing state-of-the-art GAN variants illustrate the superior adaptability of Pareto GANs in handling intricate distribution patterns.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of Pareto GANs provides a powerful tool for applications requiring precise modeling of heavy-tailed data, such as financial forecasting, environmental data analysis, and network traffic modeling. This research contributes to the GAN landscape by addressing a critical gap, opening avenues for further exploration in complex data synthesis and enhancing the applicability of GANs in domains characterized by non-standard distributions.

Keywords: Pareto GAN, heavy-tailed distributions, Generative Adversarial Networks, data modeling, distribution characteristics.
