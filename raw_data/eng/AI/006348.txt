Unsupervised skeleton-based action recognition remains a challenging task due to the lack of labeled data and the complexity of modeling spatiotemporal dynamics. This paper introduces a novel framework, Prototypical Contrast and Reverse Prediction (PCRP), designed to address these challenges by leveraging unsupervised learning techniques. The proposed approach combines two key components: (1) Prototypical Contrast, which enhances feature representation by contrasting skeletal sequences against learned prototypes, and (2) Reverse Prediction, which reconstructs the original sequence from its temporal reverse to capture fine-grained motion patterns. By integrating these mechanisms, PCRP effectively learns discriminative features without requiring labeled data. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that PCRP outperforms existing unsupervised methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance in skeleton-based action recognition. The framework’s ability to generalize across diverse datasets highlights its robustness and potential for real-world applications, such as human-computer interaction and surveillance systems. This work contributes a scalable and efficient solution to unsupervised action recognition, paving the way for further advancements in the field.  

**Keywords**: unsupervised learning, skeleton-based action recognition, prototypical contrast, reverse prediction, spatiotemporal modeling.
