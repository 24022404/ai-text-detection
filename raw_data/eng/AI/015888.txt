Accurate forecasting of geomagnetic disturbances, particularly the Disturbance Storm Time (Dst) index, is critical for mitigating the impacts of space weather on technological systems. Traditional evaluation metrics often fail to capture the temporal alignment and variability inherent in Dst forecasts. This paper introduces Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) as a novel evaluation metric for assessing the performance of machine learning-based Dst forecasting models. Unlike conventional metrics such as Mean Absolute Error (MAE) or Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), DTW accounts for temporal shifts and non-linear patterns in time series data, providing a more robust assessment of forecast accuracy. We employ a machine learning framework utilizing Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks to generate Dst forecasts and compare their performance using both traditional metrics and DTW. Experimental results demonstrate that DTW offers a more nuanced evaluation, revealing insights into the temporal alignment of predicted and observed Dst values that are overlooked by standard metrics. The findings highlight the potential of DTW as a complementary tool for evaluating time series forecasts in space weather applications, offering improved alignment with real-world operational needs. This research contributes to advancing the evaluation methodologies for geomagnetic forecasting and underscores the importance of temporal alignment in predictive modeling.  

**Keywords**: Dynamic Time Warping, Dst index, geomagnetic forecasting, machine learning, LSTM, time series evaluation.
