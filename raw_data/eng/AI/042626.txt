This paper introduces a novel approach for fast synthetic LiDAR rendering by leveraging spherical UV unwrapping of equirectangular Z-buffer images. The objective is to address the computational inefficiencies and inaccuracies in existing LiDAR simulation methods, which often struggle with real-time performance and realistic depth representation. Our method utilizes a spherical UV mapping technique to unwrap equirectangular Z-buffer images, enabling efficient depth-to-point cloud conversion while preserving spatial consistency. By integrating this approach with a GPU-accelerated pipeline, we achieve significant improvements in rendering speed without compromising accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms traditional LiDAR simulation techniques, reducing rendering time by up to 40% while maintaining high fidelity in point cloud generation. The approach is particularly advantageous for applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality, where real-time LiDAR data synthesis is critical. This work contributes a scalable and efficient solution for synthetic LiDAR rendering, paving the way for enhanced simulation frameworks in complex 3D environments.  

**Keywords**: LiDAR rendering, spherical UV unwrapping, equirectangular Z-buffer, point cloud generation, GPU acceleration, autonomous driving.
