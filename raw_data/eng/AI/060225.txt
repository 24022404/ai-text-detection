Graph-based semi-supervised learning (SSL) has gained significant attention for its ability to leverage both labeled and unlabeled data in structured domains. However, existing methods often struggle with achieving robust linear separability and generalizing to out-of-distribution (OOD) data. This paper introduces a novel graph convolution framework designed to address these challenges. Our approach integrates adaptive feature aggregation and regularization techniques to enhance the discriminative power of learned representations, ensuring improved linear separability across diverse graph structures. Additionally, we propose a domain-invariant learning mechanism that enhances OOD generalization by minimizing distributional discrepancies between training and testing environments. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art graph-based SSL techniques in terms of classification accuracy and robustness to distribution shifts. The results highlight the framework's potential for real-world applications, such as social network analysis and recommendation systems, where labeled data is scarce and data distributions are often non-stationary. This work contributes to advancing graph-based SSL by providing a scalable and generalizable solution for complex, real-world scenarios.  

**Keywords**: Graph Convolution, Semi-Supervised Learning, Linear Separability, Out-of-Distribution Generalization, Feature Aggregation, Domain Adaptation.
