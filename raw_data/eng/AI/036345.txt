Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) for object detection aims to bridge the gap between labeled source domains and unlabeled target domains, enabling models to generalize effectively across different data distributions. However, most existing UDA methods rely heavily on access to source data during adaptation, which may not always be feasible due to privacy, storage, or computational constraints. This paper introduces a novel framework for unsupervised domain adaptive object detection that operates entirely without source data, addressing a critical limitation in current approaches. Our method leverages a self-supervised learning paradigm combined with domain-invariant feature extraction to align target domain features with a pre-trained source model. By employing a progressive adaptation strategy and pseudo-label refinement, the framework achieves robust performance without requiring access to the original source dataset. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing source-free methods and achieves competitive performance compared to traditional UDA techniques that utilize source data. The proposed framework not only reduces dependency on source data but also opens new avenues for privacy-preserving and resource-efficient domain adaptation in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Unsupervised Domain Adaptation, Object Detection, Source-Free Learning, Self-Supervised Learning, Pseudo-Labeling, Domain-Invariant Features.
