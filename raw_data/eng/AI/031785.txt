Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) offers a promising approach to solving complex, long-horizon tasks, but often suffers from significant sample inefficiency. This paper introduces a novel data-efficient HRL framework that addresses this challenge by leveraging intrinsic motivation and shared latent space exploration. Our method learns a hierarchy of policies, where high-level policies set goals for low-level policies, facilitating temporally extended exploration. A key innovation is the use of a shared latent space for both goal representation and action encoding, enabling efficient transfer of knowledge between levels of the hierarchy. We further enhance exploration by incorporating an intrinsic reward signal that encourages the discovery of novel states within the latent space. Experimental results on benchmark robotic manipulation tasks demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing HRL algorithms in terms of sample efficiency and overall performance. The proposed framework learns faster and achieves higher rewards, showing potential for real-world applications. Keywords: Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning, Data Efficiency, Intrinsic Motivation, Latent Space, Exploration, Robotic Manipulation.
