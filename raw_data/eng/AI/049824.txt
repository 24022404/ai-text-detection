This paper introduces ICON, a novel approach to learning regular maps by enforcing inverse consistency. The objective is to improve the accuracy and robustness of geometric correspondence learning, a critical task in computer vision. ICON leverages the principle that transformations between two shapes should be invertible; thus, the composition of a forward and backward mapping should ideally result in the identity transformation. We formulate a loss function that explicitly penalizes deviations from this inverse consistency, guiding the network to learn more geometrically plausible and accurate maps. Experiments on shape matching and deformation transfer tasks demonstrate that ICON significantly outperforms existing methods, producing more regular and accurate mappings, particularly in the presence of noise or significant geometric variations. Key findings reveal that enforcing inverse consistency regularizes the learned map space, leading to improved generalization and robustness. ICON represents a significant advance in geometric correspondence learning, offering a powerful framework for various applications, including shape analysis, animation, and medical image registration. Keywords: geometric correspondence, inverse consistency, shape matching, deformation transfer, regularization, neural networks.
