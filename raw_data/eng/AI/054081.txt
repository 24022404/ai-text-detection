This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing decision-making in reinforcement learning by proposing a novel approach that effectively combines policy gradient and Q-learning algorithms. Conventional policy gradient methods offer stable policy improvements through direct optimization of expected rewards but often suffer from high variance and slow convergence. In contrast, Q-learning provides efficient value estimation and off-policy learning but may struggle with instability in complex environments. Our proposed hybrid method leverages the strengths of both techniques by integrating the gradient-based policy updates with value-based action evaluations, enabling efficient exploration and robust learning. We detail the architecture, update rules, and training paradigm of our combined approach, demonstrating its capability to balance stability and efficiency. Experimental results on a suite of benchmark control tasks indicate that our method achieves higher sample efficiency and faster convergence compared to pure policy gradient or Q-learning approaches. Furthermore, the proposed framework exhibits improved robustness across environments with varying degrees of stochasticity. These findings highlight the potential of combining policy-based and value-based reinforcement learning strategies to advance the state-of-the-art in AI-driven sequential decision-making. Keywords: reinforcement learning, policy gradient, Q-learning, hybrid algorithms, sample efficiency, sequential decision-making.
