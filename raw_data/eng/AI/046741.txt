This paper presents a novel approach to animating pictures using Eulerian motion fields, addressing the challenge of generating realistic motion from static images. Our objective is to create a framework that can effectively capture and simulate complex motions, enabling the animation of diverse scenes and objects. We propose a method that leverages Eulerian motion fields to model motion as a continuous field, allowing for efficient and flexible animation. Our approach utilizes a combination of computer vision and machine learning techniques to estimate motion fields from a single image, which are then used to generate animated sequences. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method in producing high-quality animations with realistic motion. The key contributions of this research include a novel Eulerian motion field representation and a robust animation framework, enabling a wide range of applications in fields such as computer graphics, video production, and virtual reality. Our work has significant implications for the development of more sophisticated and realistic animation tools, and we highlight the potential for future research in this area, including the integration of our approach with other animation techniques and the exploration of new applications. Key keywords: Eulerian motion fields, picture animation, computer vision, machine learning, computer graphics.
