This paper presents a novel topological approach to spectral clustering, a widely used technique for data partitioning in information technology and machine learning. The primary objective is to address limitations in conventional spectral clustering methods, which often struggle with complex, high-dimensional datasets and arbitrary cluster shapes. Our approach integrates topological data analysis (TDA) with traditional spectral clustering by leveraging persistent homology to capture underlying geometric and topological properties within the data. By constructing a weighted graph that encodes both similarity and topological persistence features, we perform spectral embedding and partitioning using the graph Laplacian. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our topological spectral clustering method outperforms standard algorithms in terms of cluster quality, robustness to noise, and the ability to detect non-convex structures. The proposed framework offers enhanced interpretability and scalability, making it suitable for applications such as image segmentation, anomaly detection, and bioinformatics. Our work highlights the potential of merging topological insights with spectral methods, contributing a powerful tool to the repertoire of unsupervised learning approaches. Keywords: spectral clustering, topological data analysis, persistent homology, graph Laplacian, unsupervised learning, data partitioning.
