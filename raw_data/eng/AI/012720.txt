The accurate reconstruction of high-resolution wind and solar data is critical for optimizing renewable energy systems and improving forecasting models. This paper introduces *WiSoSuper*, a novel benchmark for evaluating super-resolution methods applied to wind and solar datasets. The objective of this study is to assess the performance of state-of-the-art super-resolution techniques in enhancing the spatial and temporal resolution of low-resolution renewable energy data. We evaluate a range of approaches, including deep learning-based models such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and generative adversarial networks (GANs), as well as traditional interpolation methods. Our experiments utilize a comprehensive dataset comprising wind speed, solar irradiance, and meteorological variables across diverse geographical regions. Results demonstrate that deep learning-based methods significantly outperform traditional techniques, achieving up to a 30% improvement in reconstruction accuracy. Furthermore, we identify key challenges, such as handling data sparsity and capturing complex spatial dependencies, which remain critical for future advancements. The *WiSoSuper* benchmark provides a standardized framework for comparing super-resolution methods and highlights the potential of AI-driven approaches in renewable energy applications. This work contributes to the growing field of energy informatics by offering insights into the development of more accurate and efficient renewable energy forecasting systems.  

**Keywords**: super-resolution, wind data, solar data, renewable energy, deep learning, energy forecasting, benchmark.
