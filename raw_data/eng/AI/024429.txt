Objective: Meta-learning aims to train models that rapidly adapt to new tasks with minimal data, yet many approaches struggle with scalability and generalization. This paper investigates how fine-tuning pre-trained models serves as an effective meta-learning strategy across diverse domains.

Methods: We propose FineMeta, a two-stage framework combining large-scale pre-training with task-specific fine-tuning. In the first stage, a neural network is trained on a broad distribution of base tasks to learn a transferable initialization. In the second stage, this initialization is fine-tuned on novel tasks using a few-shot dataset. We analyze the method both theoretically—showing that fine-tuning approximates gradient-based meta-update rules—and empirically through experiments on image classification, reinforcement learning, and natural language understanding benchmarks.

Results: FineMeta consistently outperforms state-of-the-art meta-learning algorithms such as model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML) and prototypical networks. On standard few-shot image classification (5-way, 1-shot and 5-shot), our approach yields relative accuracy improvements of 4–8%. In reinforcement learning tasks with sparse rewards, FineMeta achieves faster convergence and higher final returns. In natural language tasks, it reduces adaptation time by 30% while maintaining or improving performance. Ablation studies confirm that the pre-training stage significantly enhances sample efficiency and robustness to distribution shifts.

Conclusion: Our findings demonstrate that fine-tuning a well-chosen initialization not only simplifies meta-learning but also scales more effectively than specialized meta-optimizers. FineMeta bridges pre-training and meta-learning, offering a practical and high-performance solution for rapid adaptation in resource-constrained scenarios.  
Keywords: Meta-Learning, Fine-Tuning, Transfer Learning, Few-Shot Learning, Neural Network Initialization.
