Accurate classification of primary tumor origins in lung nodules is critical for effective diagnosis and treatment planning in oncology. This study addresses the challenge of distinguishing between primary lung tumors and metastatic nodules using spectral computed tomography (CT) imaging. We propose a novel transfer learning-based approach to leverage pre-trained deep learning models for enhanced classification performance. A convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, fine-tuned with spectral CT data, is employed to extract discriminative features from multi-energy imaging. The model is trained and validated on a dataset comprising spectral CT scans of lung nodules with confirmed pathological diagnoses. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior classification accuracy compared to traditional machine learning techniques and non-spectral CT-based approaches. The model achieves an accuracy of 92.5% and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.96, outperforming existing methods by a significant margin. This research highlights the potential of spectral CT combined with transfer learning to improve the precision of tumor origin classification, offering valuable insights for clinical decision-making and personalized cancer treatment. The findings underscore the importance of advanced imaging technologies and AI-driven solutions in modern oncology.  

**Keywords**: Spectral CT, lung nodules, tumor classification, transfer learning, deep learning, oncology, medical imaging.
