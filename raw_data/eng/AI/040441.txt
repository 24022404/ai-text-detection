Abstract:

Accurate medical image segmentation is essential for clinical diagnosis and treatment planning, yet obtaining large-scale annotated datasets remains a major challenge due to the high cost and expertise required for manual labeling. To address this limitation, this paper proposes a Transformation Consistent Self-ensembling Model (TCSEM) for semi-supervised medical image segmentation. Our approach leverages both labeled and abundant unlabeled data by integrating a self-ensembling strategy with transformation consistency regularization. Specifically, the model encourages consistent segmentation predictions across multiple geometric and intensity-preserving transformations of input images, promoting robust feature learning and improved generalization. An end-to-end convolutional neural network architecture forms the backbone of the framework, utilizing pseudo-label generation and teacher-student networks to augment the effectiveness of unlabeled data. Extensive experiments on benchmark medical imaging datasets, including MRI and CT scans, demonstrate that TCSEM achieves superior segmentation accuracy compared to existing fully supervised and semi-supervised methods, particularly when labeled data is scarce. Our results indicate significant improvements in metrics such as the Dice similarity coefficient and intersection-over-union. The proposed method provides a practical and efficient solution for enhancing medical image segmentation with minimal annotation effort, and holds potential for broader applications in various computer-aided diagnosis systems. 

Keywords: semi-supervised learning, self-ensembling, transformation consistency, medical image segmentation, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, pseudo-labeling.
