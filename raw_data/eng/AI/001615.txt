The ability to model long-range dependencies in sequential data is a critical challenge in machine learning, particularly for tasks such as natural language processing and time-series analysis. This paper introduces Compressive Transformers, a novel architecture designed to address the limitations of traditional transformer models in handling long sequences. The proposed approach incorporates a compressive memory mechanism that efficiently stores and retrieves past information, enabling the model to capture dependencies over extended contexts without excessive computational overhead. We evaluate the performance of Compressive Transformers on benchmark datasets for language modeling and sequential prediction tasks, demonstrating significant improvements in both accuracy and memory efficiency compared to existing methods. Our results show that the model achieves state-of-the-art performance while maintaining scalability, making it suitable for real-world applications requiring long-range sequence modeling. This work contributes a robust and efficient solution to the problem of long-range dependency modeling, with potential applications in areas such as document summarization, video analysis, and financial forecasting.  

**Keywords**: Compressive Transformers, long-range sequence modeling, memory efficiency, transformer architectures, natural language processing, time-series analysis.
