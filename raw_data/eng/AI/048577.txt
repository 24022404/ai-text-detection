Universal adversarial attacks pose a significant threat to the robustness of machine learning models, as they can deceive a wide range of models with a single perturbation. This survey paper provides a comprehensive overview of the current state-of-the-art in universal adversarial attacks, focusing on their methodologies, applications, and implications for model security. We begin by outlining the objective of understanding how these attacks exploit vulnerabilities across diverse models and datasets. The paper then systematically reviews the key approaches used to generate universal perturbations, including optimization-based techniques, generative models, and transferability-based methods. We analyze the effectiveness of these attacks across various domains, such as image classification, natural language processing, and reinforcement learning, highlighting their ability to generalize across different architectures. The findings reveal that universal adversarial attacks are highly effective, even against models with robust defenses, raising critical concerns about the reliability of machine learning systems in real-world applications. Finally, we discuss the broader implications of these attacks, emphasizing the need for more resilient model designs and defense mechanisms. This survey contributes to the field by consolidating existing knowledge, identifying research gaps, and providing insights into future directions for mitigating universal adversarial threats.  

**Keywords**: Universal adversarial attacks, machine learning, model robustness, adversarial perturbations, transferability, defense mechanisms.
