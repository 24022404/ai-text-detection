Accurate lung nodule segmentation is crucial for early diagnosis and treatment of lung cancer. However, the scarcity of labeled data with diverse nodule characteristics poses a significant challenge. This paper introduces a novel approach for generating realistic lung nodule simulations within Computed Tomography (CT) scans using a 3D Conditional Generative Adversarial Network (cGAN). The proposed cGAN is conditioned on lung anatomy and nodule attributes (e.g., size, shape, location) to synthesize nodules seamlessly integrated into existing CT volumes. A comprehensive evaluation demonstrates that training lung segmentation models with these synthetically augmented datasets leads to significant improvements in segmentation robustness, particularly for small and challenging nodules. This method effectively addresses the data scarcity issue and enhances the performance of lung nodule segmentation algorithms, offering a valuable tool for improving lung cancer screening and diagnosis.
