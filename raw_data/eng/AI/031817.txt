**Abstract**

Semi-supervised learning seeks to leverage both labeled and unlabeled data to improve model performance, addressing the high cost and limited availability of labeled datasets. This paper introduces Pseudo-Representation Labeling (PRL), a novel framework for semi-supervised learning that utilizes intermediate feature representations as an auxiliary source of supervision. The proposed method generates pseudo-labels not only at the output layer but also at selected representation layers within the model, reinforcing consistency between the model's predictions and its learned features. We evaluate PRL using standard benchmarks in image classification and text analysis, comparing its performance against established semi-supervised algorithms. Experimental results demonstrate that PRL consistently improves accuracy and robustness, particularly when labeled data is scarce. The approach outperforms traditional pseudo-labeling and consistency regularization methods, highlighting the advantage of leveraging representational signals from multiple model depths. Our findings suggest that pseudo-representation labeling can significantly enhance semi-supervised learning models with minimal computational overhead. The proposed framework opens new avenues for research in representation learning, semi-supervised techniques, and scalable AI systems. Key keywords: semi-supervised learning, pseudo-labeling, representation learning, deep learning, model regularization.
