This paper proposes a novel approach to metric learning by dividing and conquering the embedding space, aiming to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of distance metric learning. Our objective is to develop a method that can learn compact and informative embeddings, enabling accurate similarity measurements between data points. We introduce a hierarchical framework that recursively partitions the embedding space into smaller subspaces, each focusing on a specific subset of the data. This approach allows for a more nuanced and localized learning of the metric, adapting to the varying densities and structures of the data. Our method, termed Hierarchical Metric Learning (HML), leverages a combination of clustering, dimensionality reduction, and metric learning techniques to learn a multi-scale representation of the data. Experimental results demonstrate that HML outperforms state-of-the-art metric learning methods on several benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in clustering accuracy, retrieval precision, and computational efficiency. The key contributions of this work include a novel hierarchical framework for metric learning, an effective method for learning multi-scale embeddings, and a comprehensive evaluation of the approach on various datasets. Our research has important implications for applications such as image retrieval, recommendation systems, and data clustering, where accurate distance metric learning is crucial. Key keywords: metric learning, embedding space, hierarchical clustering, dimensionality reduction, multi-scale representation.
