This paper addresses the challenge of solving bipartite matching problems using neural network-based approaches, a critical task in optimization and resource allocation. Traditional algorithms, while effective, often struggle with scalability and adaptability in dynamic environments. We propose a novel neural architecture designed to learn and optimize bipartite matching by leveraging graph neural networks (GNNs) and reinforcement learning (RL). Our method encodes the bipartite graph structure into a latent representation, enabling the model to generalize across varying problem sizes and constraints. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves competitive performance compared to classical algorithms, such as the Hungarian algorithm, while significantly reducing computational overhead in large-scale scenarios. Furthermore, the model exhibits robust adaptability to real-world applications, including task assignment and recommendation systems. This work contributes to the growing field of neural combinatorial optimization by providing a scalable and efficient solution to bipartite matching, with potential implications for AI-driven decision-making in logistics, healthcare, and beyond.  

**Keywords**: bipartite matching, graph neural networks, reinforcement learning, combinatorial optimization, neural architecture, scalability.
