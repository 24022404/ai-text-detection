This paper investigates the capabilities and limitations of reinforcement learning (RL) benchmarks by employing random weight guessing as a baseline comparative technique. The objective is to assess how well current RL environments distinguish between learned agent behavior and the outcomes generated by agents with untrained, randomly initialized neural network policies. Our methodology involves systematically applying random policy networks across a variety of standard RL benchmarks, such as those in the Atari and OpenAI Gym suites, and rigorously evaluating their performance metrics. The results reveal intriguing insights: in several environments, randomly weighted agents achieve non-trivial scores, occasionally outperforming poorly tuned or naive RL agents. This finding highlights potential gaps in benchmark design and underscores the need for more discriminative evaluation protocols. Our analysis provides a comprehensive characterization of benchmark sensitivity to agent optimization, raising important questions about what constitutes meaningful RL progress. We conclude by proposing recommendations for future benchmark development and evaluation methodologies. Keywords: reinforcement learning, benchmarks, random weight guessing, policy evaluation, neural networks, agent performance.
