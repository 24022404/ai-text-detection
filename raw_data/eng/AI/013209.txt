Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Independent Component Analysis (ICA) are fundamental dimensionality reduction techniques widely used in data analysis. This paper investigates and compares PCA and ICA from the perspective of data distribution, focusing on how each method transforms data based on its underlying statistical properties. PCA seeks orthogonal components that maximize variance, while ICA aims to find independent components that minimize statistical dependence. We analyze the performance of both methods on synthetic and real-world datasets with varying distributional characteristics, including Gaussian, non-Gaussian, and mixed distributions. Our results demonstrate that PCA effectively captures the dominant modes of variation in Gaussian data, but ICA excels in identifying independent sources in non-Gaussian data where independence is a crucial feature. Furthermore, we illustrate the impact of data preprocessing, such as whitening, on the performance of both algorithms. These findings provide insights into selecting the appropriate dimensionality reduction technique based on the statistical properties of the data, contributing to improved data representation and feature extraction. Keywords: PCA, ICA, dimensionality reduction, data distribution, independent components, principal components, feature extraction.
