Weakly-supervised lesion segmentation in Computed Tomography (CT) scans is a challenging task due to limited annotated data. This paper introduces a novel co-segmentation framework to address this issue by leveraging image-level labels. Our approach trains a segmentation network by exploiting the co-occurrence patterns of lesions across multiple CT volumes. Specifically, we employ a multi-instance learning (MIL) strategy to predict lesion presence and location, followed by an iterative refinement process using a conditional random field (CRF) to improve segmentation accuracy. Experiments on a benchmark dataset of liver lesion CT scans demonstrate that our method achieves competitive segmentation performance compared to existing weakly-supervised techniques, while requiring significantly less manual annotation. Furthermore, we show that our co-segmentation approach effectively captures inter-lesion dependencies, leading to improved segmentation consistency. The proposed method offers a practical solution for lesion segmentation in scenarios with limited labeled data, potentially improving the efficiency of clinical diagnosis and treatment planning.

**Keywords:** Weakly-supervised learning, lesion segmentation, co-segmentation, computed tomography, multi-instance learning, conditional random field.
