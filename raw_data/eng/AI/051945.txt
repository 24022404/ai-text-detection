This paper explores the critical landscape of interpretability and explainability within machine learning, offering a concise "mini-tour" of prominent techniques across diverse model types. We investigate methods for understanding how complex models, often considered "black boxes," arrive at their predictions. Our approach involves surveying a spectrum of interpretability techniques, ranging from model-agnostic methods like LIME and SHAP, applicable across various algorithms, to model-specific techniques tailored for linear models, decision trees, and neural networks. We comparatively analyze these techniques, focusing on their strengths, limitations, and applicability to different problem domains. The findings highlight the trade-offs between interpretability, accuracy, and computational cost associated with each method. Ultimately, this survey provides a valuable resource for practitioners seeking to enhance the transparency and trustworthiness of their machine learning deployments. Keywords: Interpretability, Explainability, Machine Learning, LIME, SHAP, Model-Agnostic, Transparency.
