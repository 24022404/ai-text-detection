This paper introduces a novel algorithm for learning linear non-Gaussian graphical models that incorporate multidirected edges, extending the capabilities of traditional graphical models. The primary objective is to accurately represent causal relationships in complex systems where direct feedback loops or instantaneous effects are present. Our method leverages the unique statistical properties of non-Gaussian data, employing independent component analysis (ICA) and a novel edge orientation procedure that accounts for multidirected connections. The proposed algorithm, MultidirectedICA, iteratively refines the graph structure by evaluating conditional independence tests and utilizing a scoring function that penalizes model complexity. Empirical results on synthetic datasets demonstrate that MultidirectedICA outperforms existing methods in terms of structural accuracy, particularly in networks with high connectivity and feedback loops. Furthermore, we apply MultidirectedICA to a gene regulatory network inference problem, showcasing its ability to identify potential regulatory pathways and feedback mechanisms. Key contributions include a statistically sound framework for learning graphical models with multidirected edges and an efficient algorithm that scales well to moderately sized networks. Keywords: Graphical Models, Causal Inference, Non-Gaussian Data, Multidirected Edges, Independent Component Analysis, Network Inference.
