Image segmentation is a fundamental task in computer vision, yet it remains challenging in the presence of intensity inhomogeneity, where pixel intensities vary significantly across an image. This paper introduces a novel local active contour model designed to address this issue effectively. The proposed method leverages localized region-based energy functions to adaptively handle intensity variations, ensuring accurate segmentation even in complex scenarios. Unlike traditional global models, our approach incorporates a local intensity fitting term that dynamically adjusts to inhomogeneous regions, improving robustness and precision. The model is implemented using a level set framework, enabling efficient numerical computation and smooth boundary evolution. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world images demonstrate the model's superior performance compared to existing methods, particularly in handling intensity inhomogeneity and preserving object boundaries. The proposed approach achieves higher segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency, making it suitable for applications in medical imaging, remote sensing, and industrial inspection. This work contributes a significant advancement in image segmentation by addressing a critical limitation of conventional techniques, paving the way for more reliable and adaptive solutions in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Image segmentation, active contour model, intensity inhomogeneity, level set method, local region-based energy.
