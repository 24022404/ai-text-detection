This paper investigates the application of reinforcement learning (RL) techniques for efficient spectrum sharing in radar systems. With increasing demand for wireless communications, optimizing spectrum utilization is crucial. The study aims to explore how RL can enhance spectrum management capabilities in radar environments, maintaining performance while reducing interference with other spectrum users.

Methods/Approach: We employ various reinforcement learning algorithms, including Q-learning and Deep Q-Networks (DQN), to develop strategies for spectrum sharing. These methods enable adaptive decision-making in complex and dynamic spectrum environments. Our approach involves creating a simulation environment that models radar spectrum usage and interaction with other wireless services, allowing us to evaluate the effectiveness of the RL algorithms.

Results/Findings: The experimental results demonstrate that RL techniques significantly improve spectrum utilization compared to traditional methods. Q-learning and DQN show promising results in managing dynamic spectrum access, with a noticeable reduction in interference levels and an increase in data throughput for radar operations. The RL-based models outperform baseline strategies, highlighting their potential in balancing spectrum sharing and radar system performance.

Conclusion/Implications: This study underscores the potential of reinforcement learning as a powerful tool for spectrum sharing in radar systems. The successful implementation of RL algorithms can lead to more efficient spectrum usage, contributing to the advancement of radar and communication technologies in congested spectral environments. Our findings open opportunities for deploying intelligent spectrum management systems in real-world radar operations, paving the way for enhanced coexistence with communication networks. 

Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Spectrum Sharing, Radar Systems, Q-learning, Deep Q-Networks, Spectrum Management, Interference Reduction.
