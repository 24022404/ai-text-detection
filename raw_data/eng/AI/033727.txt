The development of self-driving systems relies heavily on understanding real-world traffic scenarios, which are often complex and dynamic. This paper addresses the challenge of extracting traffic primitives—fundamental units of traffic behavior—directly from naturalistically logged data, a critical step for improving autonomous driving algorithms. We propose a novel data-driven approach that leverages unsupervised learning techniques to identify and categorize traffic primitives without relying on predefined rules or extensive manual labeling. Our method processes raw sensor data, including vehicle trajectories and environmental context, to automatically detect patterns such as lane changes, accelerations, and decelerations. The approach is validated using a large-scale dataset collected from real-world driving scenarios, demonstrating its ability to accurately extract and classify traffic primitives. Results show that our method outperforms traditional rule-based systems in terms of adaptability and scalability, particularly in handling diverse and unstructured driving environments. The extracted primitives provide valuable insights for training self-driving models, enabling more robust and context-aware decision-making. This work contributes to the advancement of autonomous driving by offering a scalable and efficient solution for traffic behavior analysis, with potential applications in simulation, testing, and real-time system optimization.  

**Keywords**: traffic primitives, self-driving systems, unsupervised learning, naturalistic driving data, autonomous vehicles, trajectory analysis.
