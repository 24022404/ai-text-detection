Scene text recognition remains a challenging problem in computer vision due to complex backgrounds, varying fonts, and distortions present in natural images. This paper introduces a novel approach—Semantic Reasoning Networks (SRN)—to enhance the accuracy of scene text recognition by integrating both visual and semantic context. The proposed model employs a dual-branch architecture: a convolutional backbone for robust feature extraction and a semantic reasoning module that leverages sequence-level dependencies to infer plausible textual content. Through end-to-end training, the SRN effectively combines local visual cues with global semantic information, enabling improved recognition of ambiguous or low-quality text. Experimental evaluations on standard benchmarks, including ICDAR and Street View Text datasets, demonstrate that our method achieves superior accuracy compared to state-of-the-art models, especially in scenarios with occlusions and noise. These findings highlight the importance of semantic reasoning in scene text interpretation, offering significant improvements in performance and robustness. Our research contributes a scalable and adaptable framework for real-world applications such as autonomous driving, document analysis, and assistive technologies. Keywords: scene text recognition, semantic reasoning, deep learning, optical character recognition, computer vision.
