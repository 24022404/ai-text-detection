The False Positive Rate (FPR) represents the fraction of real negative cases that a model mistakenly classifies as positive, reflecting errors where the model fails to identify the negative class accurately. A lower FPR signals increased model accuracy for negative cases, as fewer negatives are incorrectly labeled as positives. The complement of the FPR is specificity, which quantifies the model’s ability to correctly recognize and predict negative cases, highlighting the importance of minimizing false positives in evaluations.
