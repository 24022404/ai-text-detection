This paper addresses the challenge of object detection and segmentation in complex visual scenes by introducing a novel approach that represents objects as extreme points. Traditional methods often rely on bounding boxes or pixel-wise segmentation, which can be computationally intensive and less precise. Our proposed method leverages extreme points—the top, bottom, left, and right extremities of an object—to define its spatial extent and shape. This representation simplifies the detection process while maintaining high accuracy. We develop a deep learning framework that predicts extreme points directly from input images, enabling efficient and robust object localization. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive performance in terms of both speed and accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods. Furthermore, the extreme-point representation offers flexibility for downstream tasks such as instance segmentation and object tracking. The key contribution of this work lies in its simplicity and effectiveness, providing a lightweight yet powerful alternative for object detection in real-world applications. This research has significant implications for areas such as autonomous driving, robotics, and video surveillance, where efficient and accurate object localization is critical.  

**Keywords**: object detection, extreme points, instance segmentation, deep learning, computer vision.
