The increasing complexity of exascale applications demands efficient and scalable autotuning solutions to optimize performance across diverse computational environments. This paper explores the application of multitask and transfer learning techniques to enhance the autotuning process for exascale applications. The objective is to address the challenge of tuning parameters in high-performance computing (HPC) systems by leveraging shared knowledge across tasks and transferring insights from related domains. We propose a novel framework that integrates multitask learning to simultaneously optimize multiple performance metrics and transfer learning to adapt pre-trained models to new, resource-intensive applications. Our approach employs advanced machine learning algorithms to reduce the search space and improve convergence rates during the tuning process. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in tuning efficiency, with up to a 30% reduction in computational overhead compared to traditional methods. The framework also exhibits robust generalization capabilities, enabling effective parameter optimization across different architectures and workloads. This research contributes to the advancement of autotuning methodologies by introducing a scalable, data-driven solution that bridges the gap between theoretical models and practical exascale applications. The proposed framework has the potential to accelerate the deployment of exascale systems, offering a pathway to achieving optimal performance in next-generation HPC environments.  

**Keywords**: autotuning, exascale computing, multitask learning, transfer learning, high-performance computing, machine learning, parameter optimization.
