Neural networks, while achieving state-of-the-art performance in numerous tasks, often operate as black boxes, hindering trust and interpretability. This paper introduces LioNets, a novel approach for local interpretation of neural network decisions by decoding the penultimate layer's activations. LioNets leverage a learned mapping to transform these activations into human-understandable features relevant to the input. Specifically, we train a separate decoder network to reconstruct input features from the penultimate layer representation. These reconstructed features serve as local explanations, highlighting the input aspects most influential in the network's decision. We evaluate LioNets on image classification and natural language processing tasks, demonstrating its ability to generate meaningful and faithful explanations. Experiments show that LioNets provide insights consistent with human intuition and outperform existing gradient-based methods in identifying relevant input features. The proposed method offers a valuable tool for understanding and debugging neural networks, fostering greater transparency and trust in AI systems. Keywords: neural network interpretability, explainable AI, penultimate layer decoding, feature visualization, local explanation.
