This paper addresses the problem of graph classification, a critical task in numerous applications such as social network analysis, bioinformatics, and chemistry. While many complex models have been proposed to tackle this problem, they often require significant computational resources. Our research aims to introduce a simple yet effective baseline algorithm that can be used for graph classification, offering an accessible alternative to more complex methodologies.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel baseline algorithm that leverages basic graph properties and a linear classification model to classify graphs. Our approach combines structural graph features such as degree distribution and clustering coefficients with a straightforward feature extraction process. The model is designed to be computationally efficient, enabling application to large datasets with minimal resource requirements.

Results/Findings: Experiments demonstrate that our baseline algorithm achieves competitive performance compared to several state-of-the-art graph classification models on benchmark datasets. Despite its simplicity, the algorithm manages to maintain robustness and accuracy across various graph types. Our findings suggest that for many practical applications, our simpler model can yield satisfactory results with significantly lower computational overhead.

Conclusion/Implications: This research highlights the potential of using straightforward methodologies for complex tasks like graph classification, promoting accessibility and resource efficiency. Our simple baseline algorithm serves as an effective tool for graph classification, especially in scenarios where resources are limited. The approach can be easily integrated into existing systems, offering a practical solution for practitioners and researchers. This work encourages further exploration into the balance between computational simplicity and classification performance in graph-based tasks.

Keywords: graph classification, baseline algorithm, computational efficiency, feature extraction, structural graph properties, machine learning.
