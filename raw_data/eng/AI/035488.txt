Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training agents to make sequential decisions in complex environments. However, a critical challenge in RL is the exploration-exploitation trade-off, where agents must balance discovering new strategies with leveraging known ones to maximize rewards. This paper provides a comprehensive survey of exploration methods in RL, categorizing and analyzing the most prominent techniques developed to address this challenge. We review traditional approaches, such as epsilon-greedy and Boltzmann exploration, as well as advanced methods, including intrinsic motivation, curiosity-driven exploration, and model-based strategies. Additionally, we discuss recent innovations leveraging deep learning, such as exploration through uncertainty estimation and meta-learning. The survey highlights the strengths and limitations of each method, supported by empirical comparisons across benchmark environments. Our findings reveal that no single exploration strategy universally outperforms others, emphasizing the importance of context-specific adaptation. This work contributes to the RL community by offering a structured overview of exploration techniques, identifying open challenges, and suggesting future research directions to enhance the efficiency and scalability of RL agents. Keywords: reinforcement learning, exploration-exploitation trade-off, intrinsic motivation, curiosity-driven learning, deep reinforcement learning.
