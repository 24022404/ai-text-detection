Trust in machine learning classifiers is a critical concern as these models are increasingly deployed in real-world applications ranging from healthcare to autonomous vehicles. This paper investigates methodologies to quantitatively assess and enhance the trustworthiness of classifiers, focusing on both their predictive performance and reliability under diverse conditions. We systematically analyze existing trust metrics, such as confidence calibration and robustness to adversarial inputs, and introduce a novel framework that integrates uncertainty estimation and interpretability techniques. Our approach leverages ensemble models and model-agnostic explanation methods to provide transparent justifications for classifier decisions, thus empowering end-users to make informed judgments about when and how to trust automated predictions. Experimental evaluation on benchmark datasets demonstrates that classifiers equipped with our trust framework achieve improved calibration, provide clearer confidence signals, and are more robust to data shifts compared to conventional models. These findings underscore the importance of combining accuracy, transparency, and robustness in trust assessment. Our work contributes actionable guidelines and a modular toolkit for developers and practitioners seeking to deploy trustworthy classifiers in safety-critical environments. Key keywords: classifier trust, uncertainty estimation, model interpretability, robustness, confidence calibration, machine learning reliability.
