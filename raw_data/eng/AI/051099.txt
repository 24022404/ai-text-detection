This paper introduces LEGAN, a novel framework for disentangled manipulation of directional lighting and facial expressions in facial images. Our approach leverages human perceptual judgements to train a generative adversarial network (GAN) that explicitly separates the latent spaces corresponding to lighting direction and expression variations. We collected a unique dataset of paired facial images with varying lighting and expressions, accompanied by human ratings of perceived lighting direction. This data informs a perceptual loss function that encourages the GAN to generate images consistent with human perception. The LEGAN architecture incorporates disentanglement constraints during training, allowing independent control over lighting and expression attributes at inference time. Experimental results demonstrate that LEGAN achieves superior disentanglement and more realistic image manipulation compared to state-of-the-art GAN-based methods. Qualitative and quantitative evaluations, including user studies, confirm the effectiveness of our approach. The proposed method enables precise control over facial image appearance for applications in image editing, virtual avatar creation, and realistic rendering.

Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, Facial Expression Manipulation, Directional Lighting, Image Disentanglement, Perceptual Loss, Human Perception.
