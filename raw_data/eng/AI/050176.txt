This paper introduces MADE, a novel exploration method for reinforcement learning environments that focuses on maximizing deviation from previously explored regions. Traditional exploration techniques often suffer from insufficient coverage of the state space, leading to suboptimal policy learning and poor generalization. MADE addresses this challenge by dynamically modeling the agent's exploration history and explicitly encouraging policies that venture into state-action spaces with high deviation from past trajectories. The proposed approach leverages a statistical deviation metric computed over sampled features, enabling scalable and adaptive exploration in both discrete and continuous domains. Experimental evaluations across a variety of benchmark environments demonstrate that MADE consistently achieves superior exploration efficiency compared to widely used methods such as epsilon-greedy, intrinsic curiosity, and count-based exploration. The results show significant improvements in cumulative reward, sample efficiency, and discovery of novel states, particularly in sparse-reward or deceptive environments. In conclusion, MADE provides a robust and generalizable exploration strategy, offering substantial advancements for reinforcement learning agents in complex and high-dimensional tasks. Key keywords: Reinforcement learning, exploration, state space, deviation maximization, sample efficiency, sparse reward.
