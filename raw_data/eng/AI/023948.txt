Skin detection plays a crucial role in various computer vision applications, including face detection, gesture recognition, and image filtering. This paper presents a comparative study of several statistical skin detection algorithms applied to a dataset of sub-continental human images. The objective is to evaluate the performance of these algorithms in the context of diverse skin tones and varying illumination conditions prevalent in sub-continental populations. We examine algorithms based on color spaces such as RGB, YCbCr, and HSV, employing statistical models like Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) and single Gaussian models trained on a representative skin sample. The algorithms are evaluated using standard metrics, including precision, recall, and F1-score, on a newly compiled dataset of images depicting individuals from various sub-continental regions. Our findings indicate significant performance variations across the tested algorithms, with GMM-based approaches generally exhibiting superior accuracy compared to single Gaussian models. We analyze the impact of color space selection and parameter tuning on detection accuracy. The results highlight the challenges of robust skin detection in diverse populations and provide valuable insights for selecting appropriate algorithms for specific application scenarios. Keywords: Skin Detection, Statistical Modeling, Gaussian Mixture Models, Color Spaces, Sub-Continental, Image Processing.
