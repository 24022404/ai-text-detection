This paper addresses the challenge of improving sample efficiency in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) by exploring the co-adaptation of algorithmic and implementational innovations within inference-based DRL frameworks. We propose a novel approach that combines a modified inference algorithm with optimized hardware implementation techniques to accelerate policy learning. The algorithmic innovation involves a tailored approximate inference method that reduces the computational complexity of policy updates, while the implementational innovation focuses on efficient parallelization strategies on modern GPU architectures. Experimental results on benchmark control tasks demonstrate significant improvements in sample efficiency and training time compared to existing inference-based DRL algorithms. Specifically, our approach achieves a 2x speedup in training time and a 30% reduction in the number of samples required to reach target performance levels. These findings highlight the potential of co-designing algorithmic and implementational aspects to overcome the limitations of traditional DRL methods. Keywords: Deep Reinforcement Learning, Inference, Co-Adaptation, Algorithmic Innovation, Hardware Acceleration, Sample Efficiency.
