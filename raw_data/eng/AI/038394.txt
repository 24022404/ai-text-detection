This paper addresses the challenge of analyzing and classifying driving styles using primitive driving patterns and Bayesian nonparametric approaches. The objective is to develop a robust framework that can accurately identify and differentiate driving behaviors without relying on predefined categories or extensive labeled data. To achieve this, we propose a novel methodology that leverages unsupervised learning techniques, specifically Bayesian nonparametric models, to extract and cluster primitive driving patterns from raw sensor data. These patterns are then used to infer driving styles, enabling a more flexible and adaptive analysis compared to traditional rule-based or parametric methods. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach, showcasing its ability to accurately classify driving styles across diverse datasets. The framework outperforms existing methods in terms of adaptability and scalability, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. The findings highlight the potential of Bayesian nonparametric approaches in driving behavior analysis, offering valuable insights for applications in autonomous driving, driver assistance systems, and transportation safety. This research contributes to the field by introducing a data-driven, unsupervised solution that enhances the understanding of driving styles and their underlying patterns.  

**Keywords**: driving style analysis, primitive driving patterns, Bayesian nonparametric models, unsupervised learning, autonomous driving, transportation safety.
