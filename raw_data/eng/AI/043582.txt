Content-Based Image Retrieval (CBIR) systems often rely on multiple visual features, such as color, texture, and shape, to improve retrieval accuracy. However, determining the optimal weights for these features remains a significant challenge, as their importance varies across queries and datasets. This paper proposes a novel framework for automatic feature weight determination in multi-feature CBIR systems. Our approach integrates indexing techniques with pseudo-relevance feedback (PRF) to dynamically adjust feature weights based on the relevance of retrieved images. The indexing mechanism accelerates the retrieval process, while PRF refines the weights by analyzing top-ranked results to better align with user intent. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms traditional fixed-weight and static weighting approaches, achieving higher precision and recall rates. The results highlight the effectiveness of combining indexing and PRF for adaptive feature weighting, offering a robust solution for improving CBIR performance. This research contributes to the advancement of intelligent image retrieval systems by providing a scalable and efficient method for handling diverse visual features, with potential applications in multimedia databases, medical imaging, and e-commerce.  

**Keywords**: Content-Based Image Retrieval (CBIR), feature weighting, pseudo-relevance feedback, indexing, multi-feature analysis, image retrieval systems.
