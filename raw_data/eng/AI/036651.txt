Object detection from unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) presents unique challenges due to factors such as varying altitudes, lighting conditions, and occlusions. This paper introduces a novel deep nuisance disentanglement approach to enhance the robustness of object detection in UAV-captured imagery. The proposed method leverages a multi-branch neural network architecture to disentangle and mitigate the impact of environmental nuisances, such as scale variations, motion blur, and background clutter, while preserving critical object features. By employing adversarial training and domain adaptation techniques, the model learns to generalize across diverse UAV environments without requiring extensive labeled data. Experimental results on benchmark UAV datasets demonstrate significant improvements in detection accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly in challenging scenarios with high nuisance variability. The proposed approach achieves a mean average precision (mAP) improvement of 8.2% over baseline models, showcasing its effectiveness in real-world UAV applications. This research contributes to advancing robust object detection for UAVs, with potential applications in surveillance, disaster response, and precision agriculture. Key innovations include the disentanglement framework and its ability to adapt to dynamic aerial environments, offering a scalable solution for real-time UAV-based detection systems.  

**Keywords**: object detection, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), deep learning, nuisance disentanglement, adversarial training, domain adaptation, robust AI.
