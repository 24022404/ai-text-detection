This paper addresses the challenge of modeling complex relationships in data using hypergraph learning, a powerful framework for capturing higher-order interactions beyond pairwise connections. Traditional graph-based methods often fall short in representing multi-way relationships, which are prevalent in real-world applications such as social networks, recommendation systems, and biological networks. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel approach called *Hypergraph Learning with Line Expansion* (HLLE). The method leverages line expansion techniques to transform hypergraphs into a more computationally tractable format while preserving their structural properties. Our approach introduces an efficient algorithm that optimizes node embeddings by incorporating hyperedge information, enabling better representation learning for downstream tasks. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that HLLE outperforms existing hypergraph learning methods in terms of accuracy and scalability, achieving significant improvements in tasks such as node classification and link prediction. The proposed method also exhibits robustness to sparse and noisy data, making it suitable for real-world applications. This work contributes to the advancement of hypergraph learning by providing a scalable and effective framework for modeling higher-order interactions, with potential applications in diverse domains such as network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics.  

**Keywords**: Hypergraph learning, line expansion, higher-order interactions, representation learning, node classification, link prediction.
