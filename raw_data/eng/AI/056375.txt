**Abstract**

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a powerful framework for enabling collaborative machine learning across distributed entities without the need for centralized data aggregation. However, traditional FL approaches often assume homogeneous data distributions and lack mechanisms to address data scarcity or domain divergence across clients. This paper introduces a novel Secure Federated Transfer Learning (SFTL) framework that integrates federated learning with advanced transfer learning techniques to facilitate effective knowledge sharing between diverse clients while preserving data privacy and security. The proposed approach utilizes secure multi-party computation and homomorphic encryption to ensure that sensitive data remains confidential throughout training, while adaptive transfer learning modules dynamically align and transfer knowledge between source and target domains. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that SFTL achieves significant improvements in model accuracy and convergence speed compared to standard federated and transfer learning models, especially in non-IID (non-independent and identically distributed) and data-sparse scenarios. The framework's robust security guarantees are validated through comprehensive threat modeling and empirical analysis. Our findings highlight the potential of SFTL to enable privacy-preserving, cross-domain collaboration in sensitive fields such as healthcare, finance, and smart city applications. Key contributions include the design of the secure transfer mechanism and empirical evaluation of its effectiveness and scalability. Keywords: federated learning, transfer learning, privacy-preserving, homomorphic encryption, secure computation, domain adaptation, collaborative AI.
