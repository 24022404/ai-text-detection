The paper introduces RRLFSOR, a novel self-supervised learning strategy designed to enhance the efficiency and performance of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs). As GCNs become increasingly pivotal in handling graph-structured data across various domains, developing a robust and efficient training methodology is crucial.

Methods/Approach: The RRLFSOR strategy leverages a unique framework that minimizes the reliance on labeled data by incorporating reinforcement learning techniques into the graph learning process. This approach optimizes feature selection and enhances node representation through iterative refinement. The algorithm incorporates cycle-consistent learning and node-level supervision to improve learning dynamics and reduce overfitting.

Results/Findings: Experiments demonstrate that RRLFSOR significantly outperforms traditional supervised learning techniques and current self-supervised methods in benchmark datasets. The strategy results in higher accuracy, faster convergence times, and improved robustness to noise within the data. Comparative analysis illustrates the superior performance of RRLFSOR in maintaining high-quality representations with minimal labeled data.

Conclusion/Implications: The research presents significant advancements in self-supervised learning for graph-based models, offering a compelling alternative to existing supervised approaches that often require substantial labeled datasets. RRLFSOR has potential applications in fields such as social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics, where graph-based data is prevalent. By reducing the dependency on extensive labeled data, this approach could facilitate more efficient and scalable solutions in various practical applications.

Keywords: RRLFSOR, Graph Convolutional Networks, self-supervised learning, reinforcement learning, feature selection, node representation, graph learning.
