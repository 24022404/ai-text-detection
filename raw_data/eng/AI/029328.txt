The growing complexity of urban energy systems necessitates innovative approaches to demand response and energy management. This paper introduces *CityLearn*, a standardized framework designed to advance research in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) for urban energy optimization. The primary objective of *CityLearn* is to provide a unified platform for evaluating and comparing MARL algorithms in realistic urban energy scenarios, addressing the lack of standardized benchmarks in this domain. The framework integrates diverse energy systems, including residential, commercial, and industrial buildings, and simulates real-world demand response challenges such as peak load reduction and renewable energy integration.  

We present a modular architecture that supports customizable environments, enabling researchers to test and refine MARL algorithms under varying conditions. The framework also includes a comprehensive evaluation toolkit to assess algorithm performance in terms of energy efficiency, cost savings, and scalability. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of *CityLearn* in facilitating the development of robust MARL solutions, outperforming traditional rule-based methods in reducing energy consumption and operational costs.  

The implications of this work are significant for both academia and industry, offering a scalable and reproducible platform for advancing MARL applications in urban energy management. By standardizing research practices, *CityLearn* aims to accelerate innovation and foster collaboration in the development of intelligent energy systems.  

**Keywords**: multi-agent reinforcement learning, demand response, urban energy management, energy optimization, smart cities, renewable energy integration.
