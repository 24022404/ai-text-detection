This paper addresses the challenge of color constancy in digital images, where the goal is to remove the effects of illumination and recover the true colors of objects. Our objective is to develop a novel approach to color constancy using a bag of color features, which can effectively capture the color distribution of an image. We propose a method that extracts a set of color features from an image and uses them to estimate the color of the illuminant, allowing for more accurate color constancy. Our approach utilizes a combination of color histograms and texture features to create a robust representation of the image's color content. Experimental results show that our method outperforms existing color constancy algorithms, particularly in scenes with complex lighting conditions. The key findings of this research include the development of a new color feature extraction technique and the demonstration of its effectiveness in improving color constancy. This work contributes to the field of computer vision and has potential applications in image processing, object recognition, and color-based image retrieval, with relevant keywords including color constancy, bag of features, color histograms, and image processing.
