Video summarization is a critical task in multimedia analysis, aiming to condense lengthy videos into concise, informative summaries. Traditional approaches often rely on handcrafted features or supervised learning, which may lack adaptability across diverse tasks and datasets. This paper introduces a novel meta-learning framework for task-driven video summarization, enabling models to generalize effectively across varying summarization objectives and video domains. Our approach leverages meta-learning to train a model that can rapidly adapt to new tasks with minimal labeled data, addressing the limitations of conventional methods. We employ a combination of reinforcement learning and attention mechanisms to optimize summarization policies, ensuring task-specific relevance and coherence. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our framework outperforms existing methods in terms of summarization accuracy, adaptability, and computational efficiency. The proposed approach not only enhances the quality of video summaries but also reduces the dependency on large annotated datasets, making it highly practical for real-world applications. This research contributes to advancing video summarization by introducing a flexible, task-driven solution with broad implications for multimedia analysis, content retrieval, and automated video editing.  

**Keywords**: Meta-learning, video summarization, task-driven, reinforcement learning, attention mechanisms, multimedia analysis.
