Reinforcement Learning (RL) in parameterized action spaces presents unique challenges due to the complexity of simultaneously learning discrete actions and their associated continuous parameters. This paper proposes a hierarchical approach to address these challenges, enabling more efficient and scalable learning in such environments. The proposed method decomposes the problem into two levels: a high-level policy for selecting discrete actions and a low-level policy for determining the corresponding continuous parameters. By leveraging this hierarchical structure, the approach reduces the complexity of the action space and improves learning efficiency. We evaluate the method on benchmark environments with parameterized action spaces, demonstrating significant improvements in performance compared to traditional RL algorithms. The results highlight the method's ability to achieve higher rewards, faster convergence, and better generalization across diverse tasks. This research contributes to advancing RL techniques for complex decision-making scenarios, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and game AI. Key innovations include the integration of hierarchical policies and the efficient handling of mixed discrete-continuous action spaces. Keywords: reinforcement learning, parameterized action spaces, hierarchical policies, decision-making, autonomous systems.
