This paper introduces a novel self-supervised approach for learning joint representations of language and noisy real-world videos. Our objective is to bridge the semantic gap between textual descriptions and visual content without relying on manual annotations. We propose a framework that leverages readily available video data and associated noisy captions to learn meaningful correspondences. This is achieved through a contrastive learning objective that encourages alignment between video segments and their corresponding textual descriptions while remaining robust to noise. The model learns to map videos and text into a shared embedding space, where semantically similar content is located closer to each other. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance compared to existing supervised and weakly-supervised approaches in video retrieval and text-to-video matching tasks. The learned representations also exhibit strong transfer learning capabilities to downstream tasks such as video captioning and action recognition. Our self-supervised approach offers a scalable and cost-effective solution for learning multimodal representations from readily available noisy web data, which can significantly benefit various vision-language applications. Keywords: self-supervision, video understanding, language grounding, contrastive learning, multimodal representation.
