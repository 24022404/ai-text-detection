Color constancy remains a fundamental challenge in computer vision, impacting tasks such as object recognition and image analysis under varying illumination conditions. This paper presents a novel approach leveraging the Bag of Color Features (BoCF) framework to enhance computational color constancy. The proposed method represents images using a collection of local color descriptors, encoding the distribution and relationships of color features across the scene. By constructing compact and informative histograms of these color features, the system effectively models image statistics that are robust to changes in lighting. Extensive experiments were conducted on standard color constancy benchmarks, comparing the BoCF approach with established methods. Results demonstrate that the BoCF framework achieves competitive or superior performance in estimating the correct illumination source, particularly in complex environments with mixed or non-uniform lighting. The method also exhibits robustness to spatial variations and noise in natural scenes. Our findings highlight the value of treating color information analogously to visual words in the bag-of-features paradigm, enabling flexible and scalable solutions for color constancy. This research contributes a new perspective and toolkit for image pre-processing tasks, with potential applications in digital photography, autonomous vehicles, and advanced visual perception systems. Keywords: color constancy, bag of features, computer vision, color descriptors, illumination estimation, image processing.
