Generalized zero-shot learning (GZSL) aims to recognize instances from both seen and unseen classes, posing a significant challenge due to the inherent data imbalance. This paper introduces a novel Two-Level Adversarial Visual-Semantic Coupling (TAVSC) framework to address this challenge. TAVSC leverages adversarial learning to bridge the visual and semantic domains at both global and local levels. At the global level, we employ a domain discriminator to align the feature distributions of seen and unseen classes. At the local level, we introduce a conditional discriminator that focuses on fine-grained feature alignment based on semantic attributes, enabling better discrimination between classes. This two-level adversarial coupling allows for more effective knowledge transfer from seen to unseen classes while mitigating the bias towards seen classes during classification. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that TAVSC significantly outperforms existing GZSL methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance in both seen and unseen class recognition. The proposed approach offers a promising direction for improving the robustness and accuracy of GZSL models, with potential applications in image recognition, object detection, and other computer vision tasks.

**Keywords:** Generalized Zero-Shot Learning, Adversarial Learning, Visual-Semantic Embedding, Domain Adaptation, Image Recognition.
