**Abstract**

Video super-resolution (VSR) aims to enhance the quality of low-resolution video sequences by reconstructing high-resolution frames, a challenging task due to the inherent tradeoff between capturing spatial and temporal information. This paper investigates whether a fundamental tradeoff exists between spatial and temporal resolution in state-of-the-art VSR models. We systematically analyze various architectural designs and fusion strategies employed to integrate spatial frame quality with temporal motion consistency. By benchmarking representative spatial-centric, temporal-centric, and hybrid VSR frameworks on multiple standard datasets, we quantitatively assess the impact of model design choices on performance metrics such as PSNR, SSIM, and temporal warping error. Our experiments reveal that while certain methods excel in enhancing spatial details, they may compromise temporal coherence, and vice versa. However, we also identify novel fusion strategies that substantially mitigate this tradeoff, achieving a more balanced improvement across both dimensions. These findings provide key insights into the interplay of spatial and temporal processing in VSR, guiding future research toward developing more effective, balanced VSR algorithms. Our study contributes new benchmarks, comparative analyses, and recommendations for the design of next-generation video super-resolution systems. **Keywords:** video super-resolution, spatial-temporal tradeoff, deep learning, image enhancement, temporal coherence, spatial resolution, hybrid models.
