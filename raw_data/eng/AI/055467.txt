Objective: Counterfactual explanations offer intuitive insights into machine learning decisions by identifying minimal changes needed to alter a model’s prediction. However, existing approaches often overlook two critical aspects—uncertainty estimation and out-of-distribution (OOD) detection—leading to potentially misleading or invalid explanations. This paper systematically investigates common pitfalls in quantifying uncertainty around counterfactuals and detecting when they fall outside the training distribution.  

Methods: We first analyze why standard uncertainty measures (e.g., predictive variance from ensembles or Bayesian networks) may misrepresent reliability in counterfactual scenarios. We then introduce a unified framework combining conformal prediction for calibrated uncertainty intervals with density-based OOD detection tailored to the counterfactual search space. Our approach leverages model-agnostic density estimators to flag unrealistic counterfactuals and applies split-conformal calibration to guarantee statistical validity of uncertainty bounds.  

Results: Empirical evaluation on benchmark tabular and image datasets demonstrates that our method reduces miscalibrated explanations by up to 35% compared to state-of-the-art baselines. OOD detection accuracy for counterfactual samples improves by 20–30%, effectively filtering out implausible or adversarial alterations. A user study further confirms that practitioners rate our enriched explanations as more trustworthy and actionable.  

Conclusion: By highlighting common failure modes and proposing practical solutions, this work advances the reliability of counterfactual explanations in real-world deployments. Our modular framework can be integrated with existing explainability pipelines, ensuring both rigorous uncertainty quantification and robust OOD safeguards.  

Keywords: Counterfactual Explanations; Uncertainty Estimation; Out-of-Distribution Detection; Conformal Prediction; Model Interpretability.
