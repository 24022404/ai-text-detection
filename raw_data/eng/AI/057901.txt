Spectral methods and deep learning have traditionally been viewed as distinct paradigms for solving complex computational problems. This paper introduces *Spectral Inference Networks* (SpINets), a novel framework that unifies deep learning with spectral techniques to leverage the strengths of both approaches. The primary objective is to address the limitations of conventional deep learning models in capturing global structures and the computational inefficiencies of traditional spectral methods. SpINets integrate spectral decomposition into neural network architectures, enabling efficient learning of both local and global features in high-dimensional data. We propose a hybrid model that combines graph-based spectral embeddings with deep neural networks, utilizing a scalable optimization algorithm to ensure computational feasibility. Experimental results demonstrate that SpINets outperform state-of-the-art methods in tasks such as graph-based semi-supervised learning and image classification, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and convergence speed. The framework also exhibits robustness to noise and scalability to large datasets. This work contributes a new perspective on bridging spectral theory and deep learning, offering a versatile tool for applications in data analysis, computer vision, and graph-based learning. Key innovations include the seamless integration of spectral embeddings into neural networks and a novel training paradigm that preserves spectral properties while optimizing for task-specific objectives. Keywords: spectral learning, deep learning, graph neural networks, spectral embeddings, hybrid models.
