Land cover mapping from remote sensing imagery benefits from the availability of labeled data. However, generalizing models trained on one geographic area to another remains challenging due to domain shift. This paper introduces a novel, semi-supervised domain adaptation technique leveraging Bayesian principles within a deep learning framework to address this issue. Our approach uses a convolutional neural network (CNN) backbone, modified to incorporate uncertainty estimation inspired by Bayesian methods, allowing the model to quantify its confidence in predictions. We further employ a semi-supervised learning strategy, incorporating unlabeled data from the target domain to refine feature representations and reduce domain discrepancy. Experimental results on multi-source satellite imagery demonstrate that our method significantly improves land cover classification accuracy in the target domain compared to existing domain adaptation techniques and standard supervised learning. The proposed method offers a robust and accurate solution for land cover mapping in data-scarce regions, enhancing the applicability of remote sensing data for environmental monitoring and resource management.

Keywords: Domain Adaptation, Semi-Supervised Learning, Bayesian Deep Learning, Land Cover Mapping, Remote Sensing, Convolutional Neural Networks.
