This paper addresses the challenging problem of multi-person 3D pose estimation in the presence of severe occlusion, where existing methods often struggle to accurately detect and track human poses. Our approach leverages gated convolution to effectively handle occlusions and improve pose estimation accuracy. We propose a novel architecture that integrates gated convolutional layers to selectively focus on visible body parts and suppress occluded regions. Our method is evaluated on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in pose estimation accuracy, particularly in scenarios with heavy occlusion. The results show that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving a substantial reduction in error rates. This research contributes to the development of more robust and accurate 3D pose estimation systems, with potential applications in fields such as computer vision, robotics, and human-computer interaction. Key contributions include the introduction of gated convolution for occlusion handling and the demonstration of its effectiveness in multi-person 3D pose estimation. Relevant keywords: 3D pose estimation, multi-person tracking, gated convolution, occlusion handling, computer vision, deep learning.
