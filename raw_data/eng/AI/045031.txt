Abstract:

Approximate inference is a fundamental challenge in probabilistic graphical models, particularly when dealing with high-dimensional data where exact solutions are computationally infeasible. This paper introduces Bucket Renormalization, a novel algorithmic framework designed to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of approximate inference. The proposed method builds upon traditional bucket elimination techniques by integrating recursive renormalization procedures that systematically reduce computational complexity while preserving marginal distributions. Experimental evaluation demonstrates that Bucket Renormalization consistently outperforms existing inference methods, such as variational inference and loopy belief propagation, in terms of both runtime and solution quality across a range of benchmark datasets. The approach exhibits robust scalability and maintains high approximation accuracy even in models with complex dependencies. Our findings underscore the potential of Bucket Renormalization to serve as a practical and effective tool for large-scale probabilistic reasoning in machine learning, artificial intelligence, and data mining applications. Keywords: approximate inference, graphical models, bucket elimination, renormalization, probabilistic reasoning, scalability.
