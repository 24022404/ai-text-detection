Reinforcement Learning (RL) often requires extensive interaction with environments to achieve optimal performance, which can be computationally expensive and data-intensive. This paper addresses the challenge of data efficiency in RL by proposing a novel pretraining framework that learns robust representations from limited data. Our approach leverages unsupervised pretraining techniques to extract meaningful features from raw observations, enabling faster and more effective policy learning in downstream RL tasks. We introduce a hybrid model combining contrastive learning and self-supervised objectives to pretrain representations that generalize across diverse environments. Experimental results demonstrate that our method significantly reduces the sample complexity of RL algorithms, achieving competitive performance with up to 50% fewer interactions compared to state-of-the-art baselines. Furthermore, the pretrained representations exhibit strong transferability to unseen tasks, highlighting their versatility. This work contributes to advancing data-efficient RL by providing a scalable and generalizable solution for pretraining, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and other domains where data collection is costly or limited.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Pretraining, Data Efficiency, Representation Learning, Contrastive Learning, Self-Supervised Learning.
