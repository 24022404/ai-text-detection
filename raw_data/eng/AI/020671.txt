The burgeoning utilization of facial recognition technology poses significant privacy concerns, necessitating robust methods for face anonymization. This paper presents DeepPrivacy, a novel approach leveraging generative adversarial networks (GANs) to anonymize faces in images while preserving non-facial features.

Methods/Approach: We developed DeepPrivacy using a state-of-the-art GAN architecture, specifically designed to obscure identifiable facial features without compromising the integrity of the surrounding non-facial regions. The methodology employs an adversarial training process involving a generator network tasked with modifying facial features and a discriminator network focused on distinguishing anonymized images from original ones, refining the results iteratively.

Results/Findings: DeepPrivacy successfully achieves high-quality face anonymization, effectively balancing privacy enhancement and visual coherence. The evaluation results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms traditional anonymization techniques, both in terms of privacy preservation and visual quality, as assessed by automated metrics and human judgment.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a significant advancement in privacy protection within image and video data, offering a viable solution for scenarios requiring anonymization without sacrificing data utility. The implications of this work extend to various applications, including social media, surveillance footage processing, and any domain demanding the safeguarding of individual privacy through sophisticated yet efficient techniques.
