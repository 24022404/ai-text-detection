The optimization of large-scale classification tasks in machine learning often requires efficient handling of softmax computations, particularly when dealing with high-dimensional output spaces. This paper addresses the challenge of reducing computational complexity and memory usage in softmax-based models by proposing an efficient sampled softmax implementation for TensorFlow. Our approach leverages a novel sampling strategy that dynamically adjusts the probability distribution of candidate classes during training, ensuring better gradient estimation and faster convergence. We introduce a lightweight, scalable algorithm that integrates seamlessly with TensorFlow's existing framework, enabling users to easily adopt it for tasks such as language modeling, recommendation systems, and large-scale classification. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves significant improvements in training speed and memory efficiency compared to traditional softmax and existing sampled softmax techniques, without compromising model accuracy. Furthermore, the proposed solution is robust across various datasets and architectures, making it a versatile tool for practitioners. This work contributes to the growing body of research on efficient machine learning by providing a practical, open-source implementation that bridges the gap between theoretical advancements and real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: sampled softmax, TensorFlow, large-scale classification, machine learning, optimization, gradient estimation.
