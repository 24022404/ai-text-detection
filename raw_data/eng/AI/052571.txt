Algorithms, while often perceived as objective, can inadvertently perpetuate and amplify societal biases present in the data they are trained on. This paper investigates the implicit bias inherent in any algorithm, focusing on establishing a quantifiable relationship between the algorithm's margin and the resulting bias. We introduce a novel framework for bounding algorithmic bias by analyzing the margin distribution, offering a theoretical upper bound on the bias exhibited by the algorithm's predictions. Our approach is model-agnostic, applicable to a wide range of classification algorithms. We demonstrate, both theoretically and empirically, that algorithms with larger margins tend to exhibit lower bias. Experiments on benchmark datasets with known biases validate our theoretical findings and highlight the effectiveness of margin-based regularization techniques in mitigating algorithmic bias. This work provides a practical tool for assessing and mitigating bias in algorithmic decision-making, contributing to the development of fairer and more equitable AI systems. Keywords: Algorithmic Bias, Margin, Fairness, Classification, Machine Learning, Bias Mitigation.
