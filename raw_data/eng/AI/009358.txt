This study aims to investigate the effectiveness of a two-stage feature transfer approach in deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for the classification of diffuse lung diseases. The proposed method involves transferring features from a pre-trained CNN model to a target model in two stages, allowing for a more efficient and accurate representation of lung disease characteristics. Our approach utilizes a combination of convolutional and fully connected layers to extract and refine features, which are then used for disease classification. Experimental results demonstrate that the two-stage feature transfer method outperforms traditional single-stage transfer learning approaches, achieving a significant improvement in classification accuracy. The findings of this research contribute to the development of more accurate and reliable computer-aided diagnosis systems for diffuse lung diseases, highlighting the potential of deep learning techniques in medical image analysis. Key contributions include the introduction of a novel two-stage feature transfer method, the evaluation of feature representation in deep CNNs, and the application of this approach to a critical medical imaging task, with relevant keywords including deep learning, convolutional neural networks, feature transfer, lung disease classification, and medical image analysis.
