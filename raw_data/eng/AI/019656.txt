This paper presents a novel approach to dictionary learning by introducing an equiprobable matching pursuit strategy, aiming to address the problem of sparse representation in high-dimensional signal processing. Traditional dictionary learning algorithms often suffer from biased atom selection, leading to suboptimal sparsity and reconstruction quality. Our method modifies the atom selection process during sparse coding by enforcing equiprobable usage of dictionary atoms, thereby promoting diversity and preventing overfitting to frequently selected atoms. We develop an iterative algorithm that integrates equiprobable matching pursuit with conventional dictionary update steps, ensuring that each atom contributes equally during the sparse approximation process. Experimental evaluations on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves improved reconstruction accuracy and enhanced representation sparsity compared to standard matching pursuit and orthogonal matching pursuit methods. Furthermore, the learned dictionaries exhibit greater robustness to noise and better generalization in downstream tasks such as image denoising and audio signal reconstruction. Our findings suggest that incorporating equiprobable atom selection into the dictionary learning pipeline provides a significant advantage for applications requiring compact and expressive feature representations. Key terms: dictionary learning, sparse representation, matching pursuit, equiprobable selection, signal reconstruction.
