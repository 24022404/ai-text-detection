This paper presents a novel mobile recognition system designed to identify Wikipedia-featured sites through the integration of deep learning techniques and crowd-sourced photographic data. The primary objective is to enable users to automatically recognize significant landmarks—curated as Wikipedia-featured sites—directly from images captured on mobile devices. Our approach leverages convolutional neural networks (CNNs) trained on a large and diverse set of crowd-sourced images corresponding to Wikipedia-featured locations worldwide. The mobile application processes captured images in real time, extracts robust visual features, and matches them against our curated database. To address challenges of variable lighting, angles, and device capabilities, we implement data augmentation and model optimization strategies, ensuring accurate recognition on diverse mobile hardware. Experimental results demonstrate superior accuracy and efficiency compared to conventional landmark recognition methods, with the system achieving over 90% top-1 identification rate across a global test set. The study reveals that augmenting training data with globally crowd-sourced imagery significantly enhances recognition performance and geographic coverage. This work contributes a scalable, user-friendly solution for place-based knowledge discovery and mobile heritage engagement. Potential applications extend to tourism, education, and augmented reality, facilitating seamless connections between physical sites and digital cultural information. Key keywords: deep learning, mobile recognition, convolutional neural networks, crowd-sourced imagery, landmark identification, Wikipedia, augmented reality, image classification.
