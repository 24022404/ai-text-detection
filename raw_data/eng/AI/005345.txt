This paper presents a novel approach to language inference using multi-head automata trained through reinforcement learning. The objective is to develop an efficient and accurate method for inferring regular languages from a set of positive and negative samples. Our approach employs a multi-head automaton, which extends traditional automata by allowing multiple reading heads to process the input string in parallel. We utilize reinforcement learning to train the automaton, enabling it to learn optimal inference strategies. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing language inference algorithms in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The key findings include improved performance on complex languages and the ability to handle large input alphabets. Our research contributes to the field of language inference by introducing a new paradigm that leverages the strengths of both multi-head automata and reinforcement learning. The proposed approach has potential applications in natural language processing, compiler design, and formal language theory, and is particularly relevant to areas such as regular language inference, automata theory, and machine learning. Key keywords: language inference, multi-head automata, reinforcement learning, regular languages, natural language processing.
