Visual Speech Recognition (VSR) has gained significant attention for its potential in human-computer interaction and accessibility applications. However, recognizing speech from unseen or new classes remains a challenging task due to the lack of labeled training data. This paper addresses the problem of zero-shot learning in VSR by leveraging Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to synthesize realistic visual speech representations for unseen classes. We propose a novel framework that combines GANs with a cross-modal embedding approach to generate synthetic visual speech data for new classes, enabling the model to generalize effectively without requiring additional labeled examples. Our method employs a two-stage training process: first, a GAN is trained to generate high-quality visual speech features, and second, a zero-shot learning model is fine-tuned using the synthesized data. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of recognition accuracy and robustness, achieving state-of-the-art performance in zero-shot VSR tasks. The proposed framework not only advances the field of VSR but also provides a scalable solution for recognizing new classes in resource-constrained scenarios. This work highlights the potential of GANs in addressing data scarcity challenges and opens new avenues for research in zero-shot learning and visual speech recognition.  

**Keywords**: Visual Speech Recognition, Zero-shot Learning, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Cross-modal Embedding, Synthetic Data Generation.
