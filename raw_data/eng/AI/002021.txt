This research introduces FD-GAN, an innovative generative adversarial network designed to address the challenge of face demorphing, specifically targeting the restoration of an accomplice's facial image from a morphed composite. This work aims to enhance the effectiveness of digital forensics and security by accurately retrieving individual facial features from morphed biometric data.

Methods/Approach: The FD-GAN framework employs a novel adversarial training strategy that includes a generator for producing demorphed images and a discriminator to assess the authenticity and fidelity of the reconstructed images. The model leverages a combination of face synthesis techniques and deep learning methodologies to extract and reconstruct the distinct facial descriptors of contributing individuals in a morph.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that FD-GAN significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and visual quality of the reconstructed images. The approach exhibits superior performance in disentangling the blended features, achieving higher fidelity reconstructions, and maintaining essential individual traits. Quantitative analysis indicates marked improvements in metrics such as identity preservation score and demorphing accuracy.

Conclusion/Implications: The FD-GAN framework presents a significant advancement in the field of digital forensics and biometric identification. By efficiently restoring individual facial identities from morphed images, this research contributes to improved verification and identification processes in security applications. Our results highlight the potential of generative adversarial networks in addressing complex demorphing challenges, paving the way for future developments in biometric research. Key keywords include face-demorphing, generative adversarial network, biometric security, image reconstruction, deep learning.
