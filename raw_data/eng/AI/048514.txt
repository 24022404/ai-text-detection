The increasing integration of renewable energy sources into power systems necessitates accurate and interpretable forecasting models to ensure grid stability and efficient energy management. This study addresses the challenge of forecasting aggregated renewable generation by leveraging neural networks while emphasizing model interpretability. We propose a novel framework that combines advanced deep learning architectures with explainable AI (XAI) techniques to provide both high predictive accuracy and transparency in decision-making. Our approach utilizes a hybrid model integrating long short-term memory (LSTM) networks and attention mechanisms, trained on historical weather and generation data. To enhance interpretability, we employ layer-wise relevance propagation (LRP) and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) to identify key features influencing predictions. Experimental results demonstrate that our model achieves superior forecasting accuracy compared to traditional methods, with a mean absolute error reduction of 15% on benchmark datasets. Furthermore, the interpretability analysis reveals critical dependencies on weather patterns and temporal dynamics, offering actionable insights for grid operators. This research contributes to the field by bridging the gap between high-performance forecasting and interpretability, enabling more reliable and transparent renewable energy integration.  

**Keywords**: renewable energy forecasting, neural networks, interpretability, LSTM, attention mechanisms, explainable AI (XAI), SHAP, grid stability.
