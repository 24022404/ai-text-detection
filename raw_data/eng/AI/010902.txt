This paper investigates the convergence of learned representations across diverse neural network architectures. We examine whether networks trained on identical tasks and datasets develop similar internal representations, irrespective of their structural differences. Our approach involves training several convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformer-based models on image classification tasks, followed by analyses of their hidden layer activations using techniques such as representational similarity analysis (RSA) and centered kernel alignment (CKA). Results indicate a degree of convergence in early layers, particularly in networks trained with similar hyperparameters. However, significant divergence emerges in deeper layers, suggesting architecture-specific specialization. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of neural network generalization and the role of architecture in shaping learned representations. The implications of this research extend to neural architecture search, transfer learning, and the interpretability of deep learning models. Keywords: neural networks, representation learning, convergence, representational similarity analysis, deep learning, generalization.
