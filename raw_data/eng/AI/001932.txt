This paper introduces a novel shift-invariant sparse coding method for audio classification. The objective is to improve the accuracy and robustness of audio classification systems, particularly in the presence of time shifts and variations in audio signals. Our approach utilizes a learned dictionary of acoustic atoms and incorporates a shift-invariant constraint during the sparse coding process, allowing for more flexible and accurate representations of audio events. We propose an efficient algorithm based on iterative soft thresholding to solve the resulting optimization problem. Experimental results on several benchmark audio datasets, including environmental sound classification and music genre recognition, demonstrate that our proposed method achieves significant performance improvements compared to traditional sparse coding techniques and other state-of-the-art audio classification methods. The improved shift invariance leads to better generalization and robustness to variations in audio signals. This research contributes a more robust and accurate feature extraction technique for audio classification, with potential applications in various audio-related tasks.

**Keywords:** Audio classification, sparse coding, shift invariance, feature extraction, machine learning, signal processing.
