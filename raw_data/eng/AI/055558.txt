Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have achieved remarkable success in various computer vision tasks. However, their high computational cost and memory footprint, primarily due to channel redundancy, remain significant challenges. This paper introduces **SlimConv**, a novel approach to reduce channel redundancy in CNNs by leveraging **weights flipping**, a technique that minimizes redundant feature maps while preserving model performance. The proposed method systematically identifies and eliminates redundant channels by analyzing the correlation between convolutional filters and their flipped counterparts. SlimConv is implemented as a lightweight module that can be seamlessly integrated into existing CNN architectures without requiring extensive retraining. Experimental results on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, demonstrate that SlimConv achieves a significant reduction in model size and computational complexity while maintaining competitive accuracy. Compared to state-of-the-art channel pruning methods, SlimConv reduces parameters by up to 40% and FLOPs by 35%, with minimal accuracy loss. The findings highlight the potential of SlimConv to enable more efficient and scalable CNN deployments, particularly in resource-constrained environments such as edge devices. This work contributes to the growing field of model compression and provides a practical solution for optimizing CNNs without sacrificing performance.  

**Keywords**: Convolutional Neural Networks, Channel Redundancy, Model Compression, Weights Flipping, SlimConv, Efficient Inference.
