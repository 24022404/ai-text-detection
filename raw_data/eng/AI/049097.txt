**Abstract:**  
Adversarial Domain Randomization is proposed as a novel technique to enhance the robustness and generalization capabilities of machine learning models when transferring from synthetic to real-world environments. Traditional domain randomization introduces variability into simulated data to bridge the reality gap; however, it often lacks systematic mechanisms for selecting impactful variations. In this work, we introduce an adversarial framework where a generator produces synthetic data with diverse, challenging perturbations, while a discriminator evaluates the effectiveness of these variations in preventing model overfitting to simulation-specific features. Our approach iteratively refines the synthetically generated environments, focusing on maximizing domain discrepancy in a controlled manner. Experimental results, conducted on benchmark computer vision tasks, demonstrate that models trained with adversarially randomized domains achieve higher accuracy and greater resilience to real-world distribution shifts, compared to standard and random domain randomization techniques. This method enhances transfer learning, reduces the need for extensive real-world data collection, and is broadly applicable to fields such as robotics, autonomous driving, and visual perception systems. Keywords: adversarial learning, domain adaptation, domain randomization, synthetic-to-real transfer, machine learning robustness, computer vision.
