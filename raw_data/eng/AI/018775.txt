This paper introduces ResOT, a novel framework for resource-efficient oblique trees designed to address the challenges of neural signal classification. The objective of this research is to develop a lightweight yet accurate model capable of processing high-dimensional neural data with minimal computational overhead. Traditional methods often struggle with balancing accuracy and resource consumption, particularly in real-time applications. To overcome this, ResOT employs an innovative oblique tree architecture that optimizes feature selection and decision boundaries through a combination of sparse regularization and adaptive splitting techniques. The proposed approach is evaluated on benchmark neural signal datasets, demonstrating superior classification accuracy while significantly reducing memory and computational requirements compared to state-of-the-art methods. Results indicate that ResOT achieves a 15% improvement in resource efficiency without compromising performance, making it highly suitable for deployment in resource-constrained environments such as wearable devices and embedded systems. The implications of this work extend to applications in brain-computer interfaces, medical diagnostics, and real-time neural monitoring, where efficient and accurate signal classification is critical. This research contributes a scalable and practical solution to the growing demand for lightweight machine learning models in neural signal processing.  

**Keywords**: oblique trees, neural signal classification, resource efficiency, sparse regularization, lightweight models, brain-computer interfaces.
