Abstract:

Image stitching, a fundamental technique in computer vision and digital photography, involves the seamless combination of multiple overlapping images to produce a wide-view panorama. However, stitching accuracy is often compromised by challenges such as perspective distortion, parallax, and varying illumination. This paper introduces a robust method for image stitching that leverages a combination of feature detection, adaptive matching, and multi-band blending. Our approach utilizes advanced feature descriptors to enhance keypoint extraction under diverse image conditions. An adaptive matching algorithm is proposed to accurately align images with significant viewpoint or illumination variations, followed by geometric verification to reduce mismatches. We further employ multi-band blending to mitigate visible seams and achieve photorealistic transitions between images. Experimental evaluations on a range of challenging image datasets demonstrate that our method consistently achieves higher stitching accuracy and visual quality compared to existing algorithms. The proposed system shows resilience to common artifacts such as ghosting and misalignments, making it suitable for large-scale panoramic construction and real-world applications in photogrammetry, virtual tours, and 3D scene reconstruction. Our findings contribute a robust and efficient solution to image stitching, advancing both academic research and practical deployment in information technology. Keywords: image stitching, feature detection, adaptive matching, multi-band blending, panorama, computer vision.
