Zero-shot learning (ZSL) aims to enable models to recognize objects from unseen classes by leveraging semantic attributes. However, existing methods often struggle with effectively capturing localized attribute representations, which are crucial for fine-grained recognition. This paper introduces a simple yet highly effective approach for localized attribute representations in ZSL. Our method focuses on extracting and aligning attribute-specific features from localized regions of images, enhancing the model's ability to generalize to unseen classes. We propose a lightweight architecture that integrates spatial attention mechanisms to identify and emphasize attribute-relevant regions without requiring extensive computational resources. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in both conventional and generalized ZSL settings. The results highlight the importance of localized attribute representations in improving zero-shot recognition accuracy. This work contributes a practical and scalable solution for ZSL, with potential applications in domains requiring fine-grained classification, such as medical imaging and autonomous systems.  

**Keywords**: zero-shot learning, localized attribute representations, spatial attention, fine-grained recognition, semantic attributes.
