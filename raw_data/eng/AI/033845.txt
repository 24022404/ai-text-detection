The research investigates the limitations of the reconstruction error as a stopping criterion in Contrastive Divergence (CD), a key algorithm in training energy-based models, specifically Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs). It seeks to propose alternative stopping criteria that enhance both training efficiency and model accuracy.

Methods: We explored various potential stopping criteria grounded in statistical measures and model behavior rather than relying solely on the reconstruction error. These included examining the likelihood increase, parameter convergence, and the stability of the hidden unit activation patterns during training. The comparative analysis was conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of these criteria in different training conditions and datasets.

Results: Our experiments demonstrated that reliance solely on reconstruction error could sometimes result in suboptimal convergence, leading to poor generalization. The alternative criteria exhibited significant improvements, with the likelihood-based criterion often providing the most balanced trade-off between training time and performance. The proposed methods also reduced the risk of overfitting encountered with prolonged training under traditional stopping measures.

Conclusion: This study provides crucial insights and a novel approach to stopping criteria in CD, emphasizing their impact on the training process of RBMs. By presenting viable alternatives to reconstruction error, the research contributes significantly to enhancing the robustness and efficiency of training energy-based models. These findings hold potential applications in various domains that utilize RBMs and similar architectures, from machine learning to computational neuroscience.

Keywords: Contrastive Divergence, Stopping Criteria, Reconstruction Error, Energy-Based Models, Restricted Boltzmann Machines, Likelihood Estimation, Model Convergence.
