Visual object tracking remains a challenging problem, particularly in scenarios involving occlusion, illumination changes, and complex object deformations. This paper introduces a novel Spatially Supervised Recurrent Convolutional Neural Network (SSRCNN) architecture for robust and accurate visual tracking. The proposed SSRCNN integrates spatial supervision into a recurrent convolutional framework to effectively learn both spatial and temporal feature representations. Specifically, spatial supervision utilizes location priors to guide feature learning within each convolutional layer, enhancing the network's ability to discriminate between the target object and background clutter. The recurrent architecture, based on Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), allows the network to model temporal dependencies across video frames, improving robustness to occlusions and appearance variations. We evaluate the SSRCNN on several challenging benchmark datasets, including OTB-2015, VOT2016, and VOT2018. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed SSRCNN achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing trackers in terms of accuracy, robustness, and overall tracking success rate. The spatially supervised training enables the network to learn more discriminative features, while the recurrent architecture effectively handles temporal variations. The SSRCNN presents a significant advancement in visual object tracking, with potential applications in autonomous navigation, video surveillance, and human-computer interaction. Keywords: Visual Object Tracking, Recurrent Neural Networks, Convolutional Neural Networks, Spatial Supervision, Deep Learning.
