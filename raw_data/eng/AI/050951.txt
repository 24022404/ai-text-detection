The rapid advancement of deep learning has significantly impacted remote sensing applications, yet challenges persist when training data is limited. This paper investigates the state-of-the-art in deep learning techniques tailored for remote sensing tasks under data scarcity, identifying critical gaps and proposing potential solutions. We review recent methodologies, including transfer learning, data augmentation, and self-supervised learning, which have shown promise in mitigating the limitations of small datasets. Additionally, we evaluate the performance of these approaches across various remote sensing tasks, such as land cover classification, object detection, and change detection. Our findings reveal that while existing methods improve model generalization, they often struggle with domain adaptation and feature representation in low-data regimes. Furthermore, we highlight the need for more robust evaluation frameworks and benchmark datasets to better assess model performance under constrained conditions. This study contributes to the field by providing a comprehensive analysis of current advancements, identifying key research gaps, and suggesting future directions to enhance the applicability of deep learning in remote sensing with limited training data.  

**Keywords**: deep learning, remote sensing, limited training data, transfer learning, data augmentation, self-supervised learning, domain adaptation.
