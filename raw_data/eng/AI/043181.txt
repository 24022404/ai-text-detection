This paper presents a novel approach for automatic highlight detection in figure skating videos by estimating blink probability as an indicator of viewer engagement. The objective is to improve the identification of memorable and attention-grabbing moments without the need for extensive manual annotation. The proposed method leverages computer vision techniques to analyze facial expressions, focusing on eye-blink patterns, which are computationally correlated with audience attention and excitement. A deep learning model is trained to estimate blink probability frames throughout figure skating performances, with high-probability intervals hypothesized to correspond with video highlights.

To validate the effectiveness of this approach, the system is tested on a curated dataset of figure skating recordings, comparing detected highlights with ground truth annotations collected from human viewers. Experimental results demonstrate that blink probability estimation significantly enhances highlight detection accuracy compared to conventional motion-based or scene-change detection methods. Quantitative metrics indicate superior precision and recall rates, showcasing the method’s potential for more intuitive and viewer-centric sports video summarization.

This research contributes to the field of video understanding by integrating affective computing principles into the highlight extraction process, moving beyond purely visual or audio cues. The results have potential applications in automated content editing, personalized video summaries, and enhanced sports broadcasting. Key technologies utilized include deep learning, computer vision, video summarization, and affective computing.
