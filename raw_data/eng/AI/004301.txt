Cross-domain adaptation aims to transfer knowledge from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain. Existing methods often treat all source samples equally, neglecting the varying importance of individual samples for effective transfer. This paper introduces Importance Filtered Cross-Domain Adaptation (IFCDA), a novel framework that selectively filters source samples based on their relevance to the target domain. IFCDA utilizes a learned importance weighting mechanism that assigns higher weights to source samples exhibiting similar characteristics to the target domain, thereby mitigating negative transfer effects. We employ a discrepancy-based metric to quantify the similarity between source and target samples, guiding the importance filtering process. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that IFCDA significantly outperforms state-of-the-art domain adaptation methods, achieving substantial improvements in classification accuracy. The results highlight the crucial role of sample importance in cross-domain adaptation and validate the effectiveness of the proposed filtering approach. Keywords: Domain Adaptation, Transfer Learning, Importance Weighting, Machine Learning, Classification.
