This research addresses the challenge of causal discovery in linear acyclic models (LAMs) where variables follow arbitrary distributions, a problem critical for understanding complex systems beyond simplistic assumptions of Gaussianity. Methods/Approach: We propose a novel algorithm that leverages mathematical optimization techniques to detect causal relationships in LAMs without constraints on variable distribution. Unlike traditional methods, our approach accommodates various distributions, expanding its applicability to more diverse datasets. Results/Findings: We demonstrate that our model outperforms existing state-of-the-art algorithms in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency across several benchmark datasets with non-Gaussian structures. Our findings reveal superior capability in identifying underlying causal structures when traditional assumptions are violated. Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes significantly to the field by providing a more versatile tool for causal inference. The adaptability of our approach makes it suitable for application in domains like economics, biology, and social sciences, where data often deviate from classical distributional assumptions. Key Keywords: causal discovery, linear acyclic models, arbitrary distributions, causal inference, optimization algorithm, non-Gaussian data.
