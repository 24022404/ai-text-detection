Affective computing, which aims to recognize and interpret human emotions, has gained significant attention due to its applications in healthcare, human-computer interaction, and personalized services. However, existing approaches often rely on supervised learning, which requires extensive labeled data, limiting their scalability and adaptability across diverse datasets. This paper addresses this challenge by proposing an unsupervised multi-modal representation learning framework tailored for affective computing using multi-corpus wearable data. Our approach integrates physiological signals (e.g., heart rate, skin conductance) and contextual data (e.g., motion, environment) from wearable devices to capture nuanced emotional states without requiring labeled annotations. We employ a novel contrastive learning mechanism combined with cross-modal alignment to learn robust and generalizable representations across heterogeneous datasets. Experiments conducted on multiple publicly available wearable datasets demonstrate that our framework achieves state-of-the-art performance in emotion recognition tasks, outperforming existing unsupervised methods by a significant margin. Furthermore, the learned representations exhibit strong transferability, enabling effective emotion prediction on unseen datasets with minimal fine-tuning. The proposed method not only reduces dependency on labeled data but also enhances the interpretability of multi-modal affective signals. This work contributes to advancing unsupervised learning in affective computing and paves the way for scalable, privacy-preserving emotion recognition systems in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: affective computing, unsupervised learning, multi-modal representation learning, wearable data, contrastive learning, emotion recognition.
