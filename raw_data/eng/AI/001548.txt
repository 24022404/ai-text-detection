This paper presents a novel approach to improving El Niño forecasts by representing the world's climate system as a graph and leveraging graph neural networks (GNNs). The objective is to enhance the accuracy of El Niño predictions, which are crucial for mitigating the effects of climate-related disasters. Our approach utilizes a graph-based model to capture complex relationships between climate variables, such as sea surface temperatures, atmospheric pressures, and ocean currents. We employ a customized GNN architecture to learn spatial and temporal patterns in the graph-structured data, allowing for more accurate predictions of El Niño events. The results show significant improvements in forecast accuracy compared to traditional methods, with a reduction in error rates of up to 30%. Our study demonstrates the potential of graph neural networks in climate modeling and highlights the importance of representing complex systems as graphs to uncover hidden patterns and relationships. Key contributions include the development of a graph-based framework for climate forecasting and the application of GNNs to a real-world problem with significant societal impacts. Relevant keywords: graph neural networks, El Niño forecasts, climate modeling, graph-based methods, spatial-temporal patterns.
