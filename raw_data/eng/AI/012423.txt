This paper addresses the challenge of American Sign Language (ASL) fingerspelling recognition under dynamic and unconstrained environments, known colloquially as "in the wild." With growing demand for inclusive communication technologies, accurately interpreting ASL fingerspelling in diverse settings becomes essential for enhancing interaction with the Deaf and Hard of Hearing communities.

Methods/Approach: We developed a robust recognition system utilizing a deep learning framework, specifically designed to handle the variability introduced by natural backgrounds and diverse lighting conditions. The model leverages convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to process spatial information, alongside recurrent neural networks (RNNs) that capture temporal sequences. A large-scale dataset was collected, featuring a variety of users performing fingerspelling in real-world scenarios, ensuring a comprehensive training set that reflects real-life variability.

Results/Findings: Our approach demonstrated superior performance compared to existing methods, achieving high accuracy in recognizing fingerspelling sequences amidst complex backgrounds and varying lighting. Extensive testing indicated that the model maintains robustness across different conditions, which has previously posed significant challenges in ASL recognition tasks. The system's ability to generalize across diverse subjects and environments marks a significant advancement.

Conclusion/Implications: The innovative framework presented offers significant contributions to the field of sign language recognition, particularly for applications requiring operation in non-controlled settings. This research holds potential for application in real-time translation devices, educational tools, and accessibility services, promoting more inclusive technological solutions. Keywords include ASL fingerspelling, deep learning, CNN, RNN, and sign language recognition in the wild.
