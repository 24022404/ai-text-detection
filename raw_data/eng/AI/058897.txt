This paper revisits the prevalent use of softmax activation combined with the cross-entropy loss in neural network classifiers, offering a new perspective by interpreting this architecture as a mutual information estimator. The primary objective is to investigate the theoretical and practical implications of viewing the standard classifier not just as a discriminative model but as an estimation tool for the mutual information between input features and class labels. We analytically derive the relationship between the cross-entropy minimization process and mutual information maximization, and empirically validate our findings through experiments on benchmark image and text classification datasets. Our analysis reveals that the softmax-cross-entropy framework implicitly encourages feature representations that maximize mutual information, leading to improved discriminability and robustness. Furthermore, we propose enhancements to the traditional loss function by integrating explicit mutual information objectives and demonstrate their effectiveness in raising classification accuracy and resistance to adversarial perturbations. These results suggest a paradigm shift in the design and understanding of neural network classifiers by highlighting the mutual information perspective and its potential to inspire future model developments. Key keywords include neural network classifier, softmax, cross-entropy, mutual information, representation learning, and classification accuracy.
