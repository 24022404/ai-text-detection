Cervical cancer remains a significant health concern globally, often diagnosed through histopathological examination of cervical tissue slides. This study addresses the challenge of automating the classification of cervical cancer in whole-slide histopathology images using a convolutional neural network (CNN) approach, aiming to improve diagnostic accuracy and efficiency.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel CNN-based model specifically designed for the classification of cervical cancer in whole-slide images. The model incorporates advanced feature extraction techniques tailored to capture the intricate patterns and characteristics present in histopathology slides. We employed a comprehensive dataset of labeled whole-slide images, preprocessed to enhance image quality and segmentation accuracy, to train and validate the model.

Results/Findings: The CNN-based model demonstrated superior performance in classifying cervical cancer images, achieving an accuracy rate of 94.5%, which surpasses existing methods. Additionally, the model proved effective in differentiating between various grades of cervical cancer, showcasing its robustness and versatility. Comparative analysis with traditional machine learning methods and other deep learning models highlights the substantial improvements in classification precision and computational efficiency.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a significant advancement in the field of medical image analysis by providing an effective, automated solution for cervical cancer classification. The proposed CNN model not only enhances diagnostic accuracy but also has the potential to be integrated into clinical workflows, thereby facilitating timely and accurate cervical cancer screening. Future work will focus on extending this approach to other types of cancer and improving real-time processing capabilities. Key Keywords: cervical cancer, CNN, whole-slide histopathology images, image classification, automated diagnosis.
