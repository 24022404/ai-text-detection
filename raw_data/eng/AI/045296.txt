Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have achieved remarkable success in various applications, but their vulnerability to adversarial attacks remains a critical concern. This paper introduces **CNN-Cert**, an efficient framework designed to certify the robustness of CNNs against adversarial perturbations. The primary objective of CNN-Cert is to provide formal guarantees on the stability of CNN predictions under input perturbations, ensuring reliable deployment in safety-critical domains.  

The proposed framework leverages a novel combination of interval bound propagation and optimization techniques to compute tight robustness bounds for CNNs. Unlike existing methods, CNN-Cert significantly reduces computational overhead while maintaining high certification accuracy. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and MNIST, demonstrate that CNN-Cert achieves state-of-the-art performance in certifying robustness, outperforming existing approaches in both efficiency and scalability.  

Key findings reveal that CNN-Cert can handle larger networks and more complex datasets with minimal computational cost, making it suitable for real-world applications. The framework also provides insights into the trade-offs between robustness and model complexity, offering practical guidelines for designing robust CNNs.  

In conclusion, CNN-Cert represents a significant advancement in the field of adversarial robustness certification, providing a scalable and efficient solution for ensuring the reliability of CNNs. Its contributions include a novel certification methodology, improved computational efficiency, and practical implications for deploying robust AI systems in critical applications.  

**Keywords**: Convolutional Neural Networks, Adversarial Robustness, Certification Framework, Interval Bound Propagation, Optimization Techniques.
