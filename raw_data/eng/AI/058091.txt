This paper presents a novel approach to reconstructing 3D scenes from unstructured photo collections using Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF). The objective is to enable high-quality, photorealistic reconstructions from unconstrained images captured in the wild. Our method leverages a modified NeRF architecture, incorporating robust feature extraction and adaptive sampling strategies to handle diverse, real-world photography conditions. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in reconstruction fidelity and robustness compared to existing methods, particularly in scenes with complex lighting, reflections, and occlusions. The key findings highlight the effectiveness of our approach in reconstructing detailed, coherent 3D models from casually captured photos. This research contributes to the development of more versatile and widely applicable 3D reconstruction techniques, with potential applications in fields such as computer vision, robotics, and virtual reality. By advancing the state-of-the-art in NeRF-based reconstruction, our work enables more accurate and efficient 3D modeling from uncontrolled, real-world image datasets, paving the way for broader adoption of this technology in various industries. Key keywords: NeRF, neural radiance fields, 3D reconstruction, photo collections, computer vision.
