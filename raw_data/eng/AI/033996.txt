This study aims to develop a personalized glycemic control system for septic patients using representation and reinforcement learning techniques. The objective is to create a model that can learn from patient data and adapt to individual responses to insulin therapy, improving glycemic control and reducing the risk of hypoglycemia. Our approach combines machine learning algorithms with clinical expertise to represent patient states and reinforcement learning to optimize insulin dosing strategies. The results show that our system outperforms traditional methods, achieving tighter glycemic control and reducing the incidence of hypoglycemia. The key findings highlight the potential of artificial intelligence and machine learning in personalized medicine, particularly in critical care settings. This research contributes to the development of more effective and patient-centered glycemic control systems, with implications for improved patient outcomes and reduced healthcare costs. The study's novelty lies in its integration of representation and reinforcement learning for personalized glycemic control, offering a promising solution for septic patients. Key keywords: personalized medicine, glycemic control, septic patients, reinforcement learning, representation learning, artificial intelligence, machine learning, insulin therapy.
