This paper explores the challenge of training agents capable of generalizing to complex, unseen environments through unsupervised environment design (UED). Traditional reinforcement learning approaches often struggle with zero-shot transfer, as agents are typically trained on fixed or handcrafted environments, limiting their adaptability. To address this, we propose a novel framework that leverages UED to automatically generate a curriculum of increasingly complex environments, enabling agents to learn robust policies that generalize to novel scenarios. Our approach employs a generative model to design environments dynamically, balancing difficulty and diversity to foster emergent complexity in agent behavior. We evaluate our method on a suite of challenging tasks, demonstrating significant improvements in zero-shot transfer performance compared to baseline methods. The results highlight the potential of UED to unlock scalable and adaptive learning in artificial intelligence systems, with implications for real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous systems, and adaptive decision-making. Key contributions include a scalable environment generation mechanism, a theoretical analysis of emergent complexity, and empirical evidence of superior generalization capabilities. Keywords: unsupervised environment design, zero-shot transfer, emergent complexity, reinforcement learning, curriculum learning, adaptive systems.
