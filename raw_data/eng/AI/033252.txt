This paper introduces a novel Multi-Scale Video Frame-Synthesis Network (MS-VFSN) designed to address the challenges of generating high-quality intermediate video frames in dynamic scenes. The proposed framework leverages a multi-scale architecture to capture both fine-grained details and global motion patterns, enabling robust frame synthesis across varying temporal resolutions. A key innovation is the introduction of a Transitive Consistency Loss (TCL), which enforces temporal coherence by ensuring consistency across synthesized frames over multiple time steps. This loss function significantly improves the stability and accuracy of frame predictions, particularly in scenarios with complex motion and occlusions. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that MS-VFSN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of both visual quality and quantitative metrics, such as Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM). The proposed approach has broad applications in video interpolation, slow-motion generation, and video compression, offering a scalable and efficient solution for real-world video processing tasks.  

**Keywords**: Video frame synthesis, multi-scale networks, transitive consistency loss, temporal coherence, video interpolation, motion estimation.
