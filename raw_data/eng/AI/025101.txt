This paper explores a novel approach to tracking road users by leveraging Constraint Programming (CP). Traditional tracking methods often struggle with complex scenarios involving occlusions, noisy sensor data, and diverse object behaviors. We propose a CP-based framework that models the tracking problem as a set of constraints representing physical laws, sensor limitations, and object interactions. The approach formulates the tracking task as a Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP), where the positions and trajectories of road users are variables, and the constraints ensure consistency with observations and domain knowledge. Our CP model incorporates constraints for motion models, sensor measurements, and inter-object relationships (e.g., collision avoidance). The framework is evaluated on synthetic and real-world traffic datasets, demonstrating improved tracking accuracy and robustness compared to conventional Kalman filter-based methods, particularly in challenging scenarios with partial occlusions and erratic movements. The results show a reduction in tracking errors by up to 20% in dense traffic environments. This work contributes a flexible and expressive approach to road user tracking, offering advantages in handling complex interactions and incorporating diverse sources of information. Keywords: Constraint Programming, Road User Tracking, Intelligent Transportation Systems, Constraint Satisfaction Problem, Traffic Monitoring, Sensor Fusion.
