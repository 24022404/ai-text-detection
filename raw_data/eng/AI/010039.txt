Low-rank matrix recovery is a fundamental problem in information technology, with applications in data compression, collaborative filtering, and signal processing. Traditional optimization methods encounter challenges in scalability and convergence speed, particularly when initializing randomly. This paper presents a novel approach utilizing Riemannian gradient descent to achieve fast global convergence for low-rank matrix recovery tasks, even when starting from random initializations. Our method leverages the geometry of the low-rank matrix manifold to ensure efficient descent by projecting updates onto the Riemannian manifold, thus maintaining the low-rank structure throughout optimization. Through rigorous theoretical analysis, we establish global convergence guarantees under mild conditions, significantly relaxing common assumptions about initialization. Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms classical gradient descent and other state-of-the-art algorithms in terms of convergence speed and recovery accuracy. The results confirm the effectiveness of Riemannian optimization techniques for large-scale matrix recovery problems and highlight their suitability for practical deployment where initialization cannot be carefully controlled. This work advances the field by offering a robust, scalable, and theoretically sound solution for low-rank matrix recovery, paving the way for further development in machine learning, computer vision, and data analysis. Key keywords: low-rank matrix recovery, Riemannian gradient descent, global convergence, random initialization, manifold optimization.
