This paper presents a novel approach to inverse design of inorganic materials using generative adversarial networks (GANs) for efficient sampling of chemical space. The objective is to develop a computational framework that can effectively explore the vast chemical space and identify optimal material compositions with desired properties. Our method employs a GAN-based architecture to generate new material candidates, which are then evaluated using physics-based models to predict their properties. The results show that our approach can significantly accelerate the discovery of new inorganic materials with improved performance, outperforming traditional sampling methods. Key findings include the successful identification of novel material compositions with enhanced optical and electrical properties. The proposed framework has important implications for the field of materials science, enabling the rapid design and optimization of materials for a wide range of applications, including energy storage and conversion. Our research contributes to the development of more efficient and effective methods for inverse design of materials, highlighting the potential of GANs in this field. Relevant keywords: GANs, inverse design, inorganic materials, chemical space, materials science, machine learning.
