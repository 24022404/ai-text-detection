This paper introduces *Part2Word*, a novel framework for learning joint embeddings of 3D point clouds and textual descriptions by aligning parts of objects to corresponding words. The objective is to bridge the semantic gap between 3D data and natural language, enabling applications such as cross-modal retrieval, 3D object captioning, and human-robot interaction. Our approach leverages a transformer-based architecture to encode point cloud segments and text tokens into a shared embedding space. By matching fine-grained object parts (e.g., chair legs or car wheels) to their textual counterparts, the model learns semantically meaningful representations. We evaluate *Part2Word* on benchmark datasets for 3D-text alignment, demonstrating superior performance in retrieval accuracy and semantic consistency compared to existing methods. The results highlight the effectiveness of part-to-word matching in capturing detailed object-text relationships. This work contributes a scalable and interpretable framework for multimodal learning, with potential applications in robotics, augmented reality, and 3D scene understanding.  

**Keywords**: 3D point clouds, text embeddings, multimodal learning, transformer models, part-to-word matching, cross-modal retrieval.
