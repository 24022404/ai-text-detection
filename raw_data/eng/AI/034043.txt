This study investigates the presence of artificial fingerprints in images generated by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). The objective is to determine whether GANs inadvertently introduce unique patterns or anomalies that can be used to distinguish generated images from real ones. We employ a deep learning approach, utilizing a custom-designed convolutional neural network to analyze images produced by various GAN architectures. Our results show that GAN-generated images exhibit distinct characteristics, such as inconsistent texture patterns and abnormal frequency spectra, which can be exploited to identify them as artificial. The findings have significant implications for image forensics, digital watermarking, and AI-generated content detection. This research contributes to the development of more sophisticated methods for detecting GAN-generated images, highlighting the importance of considering artificial fingerprints in the design and evaluation of GAN models. Key keywords: GANs, artificial fingerprints, image forensics, deep learning, digital watermarking, AI-generated content detection.
