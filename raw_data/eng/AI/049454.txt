This paper explores the application of posterior sampling to reinforcement learning, with a focus on deriving worst-case regret bounds. The objective is to develop a framework that balances exploration and exploitation in complex, uncertain environments. We employ a Bayesian approach, utilizing posterior sampling to guide the decision-making process. Our method involves maintaining a posterior distribution over possible environments and sampling from this distribution to select actions. Theoretical analysis of this approach yields worst-case regret bounds, providing a guarantee on the performance of the algorithm. Key findings indicate that posterior sampling achieves a regret bound of O(√(T)), where T is the number of time steps, outperforming existing methods in certain scenarios. The conclusions drawn from this research contribute to the understanding of reinforcement learning and posterior sampling, highlighting the potential for improved performance in complex decision-making tasks. This work has implications for applications in robotics, game playing, and other areas where reinforcement learning is applied, with key keywords including reinforcement learning, posterior sampling, regret bounds, Bayesian methods, and decision-making under uncertainty.
