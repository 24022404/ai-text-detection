Hierarchical reinforcement learning (HRL) leverages temporal abstraction to solve complex, long-horizon tasks. However, defining effective subgoals remains a challenge. This paper introduces a novel approach to learn goal embeddings for HRL through self-play. Our method trains a high-level policy to propose goal embeddings and a low-level policy to achieve these goals, both optimized jointly through interaction. The high-level policy learns to propose increasingly challenging goals, while the low-level policy learns to accomplish them. We use a curriculum learning strategy based on the success rate of the low-level policy to guide the self-play process. Experimental results across several benchmark environments demonstrate that our approach significantly improves performance compared to existing HRL methods and single-level reinforcement learning algorithms. The learned goal embeddings capture meaningful task decompositions, leading to faster learning and improved generalization. Our findings suggest that self-play is a powerful tool for automatically discovering effective subgoal representations in HRL.
