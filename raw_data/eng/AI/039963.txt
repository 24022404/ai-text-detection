This paper addresses the challenge of learning relational representations within the realm of artificial intelligence, emphasizing the significant impact of incorporating logic-based methods into auto-encoding frameworks. The research aims to explore and advance the understanding of how auto-encoding logic programs can be effectively utilized to capture and model complex relational structures in data.

Methods/Approach: We propose an innovative method that integrates auto-encoders with logic programming techniques to form a novel auto-encoding logic program model. This approach leverages the strengths of both neural networks and logical reasoning, facilitating the learning of structured representations from relational datasets. Our framework applies a hybrid learning algorithm that combines symbolic logic rules with deep learning architectures, enabling the extraction of meaningful patterns and relationships.

Results/Findings: The experimental results demonstrate the capability of our proposed model to outperform traditional neural architectures when dealing with relational data. The model achieved notable improvements in accuracy and generalization across a series of benchmarks and tasks, including knowledge graph completion and relational inference. Additionally, comparisons with existing methods highlight the superior efficiency and robustness of our approach in capturing complex relational dependencies.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of logic programming into auto-encoding mechanisms presents a promising avenue for advancing the field of relational learning. Our contributions offer a novel paradigm for relational representation, with potential applications spanning various domains, including knowledge management, semantic web technologies, and AI inferences. This study sets the foundation for future exploration into hybrid models that synergize symbolic and connectionist approaches, opening up opportunities for more interpretable and reliable AI systems.

Keywords: relational representations, auto-encoders, logic programming, neural networks, hybrid learning, knowledge graph, AI inference.
