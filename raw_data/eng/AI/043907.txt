Skeleton-based action recognition has gained prominence due to its viewpoint invariance and low computational cost. However, real-world data often suffers from noise, impacting the accuracy of existing Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) based methods. This paper introduces a novel Predictively Encoded Graph Convolutional Network (PEGCN) designed for robust action recognition in noisy environments. PEGCN leverages a predictive encoding module that anticipates future skeletal poses based on historical data, effectively mitigating the impact of noisy or missing joints. The predicted poses are then fused with the observed data to create a more complete and accurate representation of the action. We evaluate PEGCN on benchmark datasets, including NTU RGB+D 60, NTU RGB+D 120, and a synthetically corrupted version of these datasets. Results demonstrate that PEGCN significantly outperforms state-of-the-art GCN-based methods, particularly in noisy conditions, achieving a relative improvement of up to 5% in accuracy. This highlights the effectiveness of predictive encoding in enhancing the robustness of skeleton-based action recognition systems. Our findings suggest PEGCN offers a promising approach for deploying action recognition systems in real-world scenarios where data quality is variable.
Keywords: Action Recognition, Graph Convolutional Network, Skeleton Data, Noise Robustness, Predictive Encoding, Deep Learning.
