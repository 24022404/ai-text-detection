This research addresses the limitations of the conventional Softmax with Cross-Entropy framework in neural network classifiers. It aims to explore how these classifiers can be redefined as mutual information estimators to enhance model interpretability and performance.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel methodology that reconceptualizes the role of the Softmax function in conjunction with Cross-Entropy loss. By employing information-theoretic principles, we reformulate the classification problem to focus on mutual information estimation. Our approach leverages a rigorous analytical model to quantitatively assess the informational content shared between inputs and their corresponding predictive outcomes, thereby optimizing the decision-making process of neural networks.

Results/Findings: The proposed framework demonstrated a consistent improvement in classification accuracy and robustness across multiple benchmark datasets when compared to traditional approaches. Our findings indicate that treating the neural network classifier as a mutual information estimator leads to better alignment with theoretical maximum likelihood estimations. Furthermore, our model provides enhanced insights into uncertainty and decision boundaries, thus offering more reliable predictions.

Conclusion/Implications: The study presents a transformative approach to neural network classifiers by aligning them with mutual information principles, offering unique benefits for understanding and improving model decision processes. This advancement opens pathways to more informed and efficient learning systems that can be utilized in diverse applications such as image recognition, language processing, and beyond. The results underscore the potential for rethinking fundamental machine learning algorithms to achieve superior performance and deeper insights.

Keywords: Softmax, Cross-Entropy, Mutual Information, Neural Network Classifier, Information Theory, Classification Accuracy, Model Robustness.
