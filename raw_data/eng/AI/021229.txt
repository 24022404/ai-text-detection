This paper presents an innovative approach to unsupervised monocular stereo matching, addressing the challenge of estimating depth from a single image. Our objective is to develop a method that can accurately infer depth information without requiring ground truth depth data or supervision. We propose a novel framework that leverages a deep neural network architecture, combining a generative model with a discriminative model to learn the underlying structure of the scene. Our approach utilizes a self-supervised loss function that enables the network to learn from monocular images, eliminating the need for explicit depth labels. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets. The key findings of this research include improved depth estimation accuracy, robustness to varying lighting conditions, and the ability to generalize to unseen environments. This work contributes to the field of computer vision, particularly in applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and 3D reconstruction, where accurate depth estimation is crucial. Key keywords: unsupervised learning, monocular stereo matching, depth estimation, self-supervised learning, computer vision.
