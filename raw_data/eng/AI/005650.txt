Few-shot learning presents significant challenges in Information Technology, requiring models to generalize effectively from limited labeled examples. This paper introduces Sparse Spatial Transformers (SST), a novel approach designed to enhance few-shot learning performance by enabling models to focus selectively on the most discriminative spatial features within input data. Leveraging a sparsity-inducing mechanism within the spatial transformer network, SST dynamically identifies and attends to crucial regions while suppressing irrelevant information, thus improving representation efficiency and robustness. We implement SST within a convolutional neural network architecture and evaluate its effectiveness on standard few-shot image classification benchmarks. Experimental results demonstrate that SST surpasses conventional spatial transformers and other state-of-the-art few-shot learning methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. Further analysis reveals that the induced sparsity leads to more interpretable feature localization and reduced overfitting in low-data regimes. Overall, the proposed Sparse Spatial Transformers provide an effective and scalable solution for enhancing few-shot learning capabilities, with potential applications in areas such as image recognition, medical imaging, and autonomous systems where labeled data is scarce. Key keywords: few-shot learning, spatial transformers, sparsity, image classification, neural networks.
