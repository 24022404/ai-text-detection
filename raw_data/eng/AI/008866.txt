This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing sequential decision-making processes in dynamic environments, where traditional methods often struggle with computational complexity and scalability. We propose a meta-learning framework to develop surrogate models that efficiently approximate decision policies, reducing the need for extensive computational resources while maintaining high accuracy. Our approach leverages meta-learning to adaptively train lightweight surrogate models on a diverse set of decision-making tasks, enabling rapid generalization to new, unseen scenarios. The framework incorporates a novel architecture that combines reinforcement learning with meta-optimization techniques, ensuring robust performance across varying problem domains. Experimental results demonstrate that our surrogate models achieve competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art methods, with significant reductions in computational overhead. The proposed approach is particularly effective in resource-constrained environments, such as real-time systems and large-scale optimization problems. This research contributes a scalable and efficient solution for sequential decision-making, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and industrial automation. Key innovations include the integration of meta-learning with surrogate modeling and the development of a task-adaptive training pipeline. Keywords: meta-learning, surrogate models, sequential decision-making, reinforcement learning, optimization, computational efficiency.
