Lifelong reinforcement learning (LRL) aims to develop agents that continuously learn and improve over their entire lifespan, adapting to new tasks and environments without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. This paper investigates key challenges and potential solutions in LRL systems. We present a novel experience replay mechanism, termed "Curriculum-guided Replay," which prioritizes the storage and retrieval of experiences based on their relevance to the current learning objective and their potential for mitigating catastrophic forgetting. Furthermore, we introduce an adaptive exploration strategy that balances exploration of new environments with the exploitation of existing knowledge. Experimental results across a range of simulated robotic control tasks demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing LRL algorithms in terms of learning speed, asymptotic performance, and resistance to catastrophic forgetting. The findings highlight the importance of intelligent experience management and adaptive exploration in building robust and effective lifelong learning agents. These insights contribute to the development of more adaptable and autonomous AI systems capable of operating in dynamic and unpredictable environments.

Keywords: Lifelong Learning, Reinforcement Learning, Catastrophic Forgetting, Experience Replay, Exploration-Exploitation.
