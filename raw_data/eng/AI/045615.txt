This paper investigates the generalization error behavior of generalized linear models (GLMs) when applied to high-dimensional data sets. Understanding the performance of GLMs in high dimensions is critical as these models are widely used in applications that involve large and complex data structures. 

Methods: We employ advanced statistical techniques to model the generalization error of GLMs, utilizing both theoretical analysis and empirical simulations. Our approach includes the derivation of asymptotic error bounds, taking into account the dimensionality of the data and the complexity of the model. We explore various parameter settings and sparsity conditions to evaluate the performance across different high-dimensional scenarios.

Results: Our findings reveal that the generalization error of GLMs is significantly influenced by the ratio of dimensionality to sample size, as well as the sparsity level of the underlying data. Specifically, we identify critical thresholds where the generalization error transitions from manageable levels to more substantial increases. Comparisons with other commonly used models demonstrate the relative strengths and weaknesses of GLMs in handling high-dimensional data.

Conclusion: The study provides new insights into the scalability and robustness of GLMs in high-dimensional contexts, contributing to more informed model selection and implementation in data-rich environments. The implications are particularly relevant for fields like bioinformatics, finance, and text processing, where GLMs are frequently employed. Our analysis highlights potential improvements in model formulation to enhance performance, paving the way for future research in high-dimensional statistics.

Keywords: generalized linear models, high dimensionality, generalization error, statistical analysis, asymptotic bounds, sparsity, model performance.
