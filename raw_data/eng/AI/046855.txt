Conditional Normalizing Flows (CNFs) have emerged as a powerful framework for modeling complex probability distributions by leveraging invertible transformations. This paper addresses the challenge of learning accurate likelihoods in high-dimensional spaces, where traditional methods often struggle with scalability and expressiveness. We propose a novel approach that integrates CNFs with conditional probability estimation, enabling efficient and flexible modeling of data distributions conditioned on auxiliary variables. Our method employs a series of bijective transformations parameterized by neural networks, which are trained to maximize the likelihood of observed data under the conditional distribution. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms existing methods in terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency. Key findings reveal significant improvements in density estimation tasks, particularly in scenarios involving complex dependencies between variables. The results highlight the potential of CNFs for applications in generative modeling, anomaly detection, and uncertainty quantification. This work contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in probabilistic modeling by providing a scalable and versatile framework for learning conditional likelihoods.  

**Keywords**: Conditional Normalizing Flows, Likelihood Estimation, Probabilistic Modeling, Density Estimation, Generative Models, Neural Networks.
