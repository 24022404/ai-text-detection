This paper introduces NightVision, a novel approach to generating nighttime satellite imagery from infra-red observations. The objective is to produce high-quality visible-light images of the Earth's surface at night, leveraging infra-red data captured by satellites. Our method employs a deep learning-based framework, utilizing a combination of convolutional neural networks and generative adversarial networks to learn the mapping between infra-red and visible-light images. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of NightVision in generating realistic and detailed nighttime images, outperforming existing methods in terms of visual quality and accuracy. Our approach has significant implications for various applications, including environmental monitoring, urban planning, and disaster response. The key contributions of this research lie in its ability to harness infra-red observations to produce nighttime imagery, enabling new possibilities for satellite-based Earth observation. Relevant keywords: nighttime satellite imagery, infra-red observations, deep learning, generative models, Earth observation, remote sensing.
