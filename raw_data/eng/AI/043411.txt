This paper introduces an ante-hoc approach for inducing semantic grouping of latent concepts to enhance the interpretability and explainability of machine learning models. The objective is to address the challenge of understanding complex model decisions by organizing latent representations into semantically meaningful groups, thereby providing intuitive explanations for predictions. We propose a novel framework that leverages unsupervised learning techniques to cluster latent concepts based on their semantic similarity, combined with a supervised mechanism to align these groupings with human-interpretable features. The approach is evaluated on benchmark datasets, demonstrating its ability to improve model transparency without compromising predictive performance. Results show that the proposed method outperforms existing post-hoc explanation techniques in terms of both interpretability and computational efficiency. The findings highlight the potential of ante-hoc semantic grouping as a practical solution for deploying explainable AI systems in real-world applications, particularly in domains requiring high-stakes decision-making. This work contributes to the growing field of explainable AI by offering a scalable and interpretable framework for understanding latent representations in machine learning models.  

**Keywords**: Explainable AI, latent concepts, semantic grouping, ante-hoc interpretability, unsupervised learning, machine learning.
