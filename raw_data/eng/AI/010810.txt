This paper presents a novel approach to multi-view depth estimation, leveraging Epipolar Spatio-Temporal Networks (ESTNs) to accurately predict depth maps from multiple camera views. The objective is to address the challenges of traditional stereo matching methods, which often struggle with textureless regions, occlusions, and varying lighting conditions. Our approach employs a deep learning-based framework, incorporating epipolar geometry and spatio-temporal features to effectively model the relationships between different views. The ESTN model is trained on a large dataset of multi-view images and ground truth depth maps, allowing it to learn robust features and improve depth estimation accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, outperforming state-of-the-art approaches in terms of depth accuracy and robustness. The proposed ESTN architecture has significant implications for various applications, including 3D reconstruction, autonomous driving, and virtual reality. Key contributions of this research include the introduction of a novel epipolar-based network architecture, the incorporation of spatio-temporal features for improved depth estimation, and the demonstration of superior performance on challenging multi-view datasets. Relevant keywords: multi-view depth estimation, epipolar geometry, spatio-temporal networks, deep learning, 3D reconstruction.
