Crowd counting is a critical task in computer vision with applications in public safety, urban planning, and event management. However, accurately estimating crowd density in highly congested scenes remains challenging due to scale variations, occlusions, and complex backgrounds. This paper introduces HA-CCN, a Hierarchical Attention-based Crowd Counting Network, designed to address these challenges by leveraging multi-scale feature extraction and attention mechanisms. The proposed model integrates a hierarchical architecture that captures both local and global contextual information, combined with a novel attention module to focus on salient regions and suppress irrelevant background noise. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, such as ShanghaiTech and UCF_CC_50, demonstrate that HA-CCN achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of mean absolute error (MAE) and mean squared error (MSE). The results highlight the effectiveness of the hierarchical attention mechanism in improving counting accuracy, particularly in dense and cluttered environments. This research contributes a robust and scalable solution for real-world crowd counting applications, with potential implications for enhancing surveillance systems and crowd management strategies.  

**Keywords**: Crowd counting, hierarchical attention, computer vision, deep learning, density estimation, surveillance systems.
