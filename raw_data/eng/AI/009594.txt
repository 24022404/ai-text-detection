This paper introduces *Probability Functional Descent* (PFD), a novel framework that unifies Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Variational Inference (VI), and Reinforcement Learning (RL) under a single probabilistic optimization paradigm. The objective of this work is to address the fragmented theoretical foundations of these methods by providing a cohesive mathematical perspective that bridges their underlying principles. PFD leverages functional gradient descent on probability spaces to optimize divergence measures, enabling a unified treatment of adversarial training, variational approximation, and policy optimization. The proposed approach is demonstrated through theoretical analysis and empirical validation across multiple domains, including image generation, probabilistic modeling, and decision-making tasks. Results indicate that PFD not only recovers existing algorithms as special cases but also enables the development of new hybrid methods with improved stability and performance. The framework offers significant implications for advancing research in machine learning by providing a common language for analyzing and designing algorithms across these traditionally distinct areas. Key contributions include a generalized optimization strategy, theoretical insights into the connections between GANs, VI, and RL, and practical applications demonstrating the versatility of PFD. Keywords: Probability Functional Descent, GANs, Variational Inference, Reinforcement Learning, Optimization, Machine Learning.
