This paper presents a novel application of Second-Order Component Analysis (SOCA) for fault detection in complex systems. The primary objective of our research is to address the limitations of traditional fault detection methods, which often struggle with high-dimensional data and correlated process variables typical in modern industrial environments. We propose an enhanced analytical framework leveraging SOCA to extract and analyze second-order statistical features from large-scale process datasets. The proposed approach involves constructing a second-order component model that captures subtle correlations and variance structures, enabling the identification of abnormal patterns indicative of incipient faults. Comprehensive experiments were conducted on benchmark industrial datasets, where the SOCA-based method demonstrated superior fault detection performance compared to standard Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and other conventional techniques. Notably, our approach achieved higher accuracy in early fault diagnosis and reduced false alarm rates, thereby improving system reliability and safety. The findings highlight the potential of SOCA as a powerful tool for real-time monitoring and fault management in cyber-physical and industrial automation systems. Our work contributes a scalable and effective methodology for fault detection, with implications for process monitoring, predictive maintenance, and intelligent decision support. Keywords: second-order component analysis, fault detection, process monitoring, industrial systems, data-driven methods, anomaly detection.
