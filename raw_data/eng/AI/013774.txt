This paper addresses the challenge of creating interpretable and structured representations of high-dimensional data through supervised topological maps. Traditional unsupervised methods often fail to incorporate domain-specific knowledge, limiting their applicability in tasks requiring precise control over data organization. To overcome this, we propose a novel supervised approach that integrates labeled data into the construction of topological maps, enabling the preservation of both geometric and semantic relationships. Our method leverages a hybrid framework combining graph-based techniques with deep learning to optimize map generation while maintaining computational efficiency. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms existing unsupervised methods in terms of classification accuracy and interpretability, achieving a 15% improvement in clustering quality. Furthermore, the generated maps exhibit enhanced robustness to noise and scalability to large datasets. This work contributes a practical tool for applications such as data visualization, feature extraction, and domain-specific knowledge representation, offering a significant step forward in the development of interpretable machine learning models.  

**Keywords**: supervised learning, topological maps, interpretability, graph-based methods, deep learning, data visualization.
