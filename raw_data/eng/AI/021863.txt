Objective: Emotional body gesture recognition plays a critical role in affective computing and human–computer interaction, yet the field lacks a unified overview of existing techniques, datasets, and evaluation protocols. This survey aims to provide a comprehensive analysis of methods for recognizing emotional states from full-body gestures, highlight current trends, and identify open challenges for future research.

Methods/Approach: We conducted a systematic literature review of 120 peer-reviewed articles published between 2010 and 2023. Each work was categorized according to data modality (RGB video, depth maps, motion capture), feature representation (skeletal kinematics, appearance-based descriptors, spatiotemporal embeddings), and algorithmic approach (classical machine learning, deep neural networks, graph-based models). We also examined publicly available datasets and standardized performance metrics to enable fair comparisons.

Results/Findings: Our analysis reveals a clear shift toward end-to-end deep architectures—particularly convolutional and graph neural networks—that leverage both spatial and temporal information. However, most studies rely on small, lab-controlled datasets with limited cultural and environmental variability. Fusion of multimodal cues (e.g., gestures plus physiological signals) shows promise but remains underexplored. We further identify biases in annotation protocols and inconsistencies in evaluation practices.

Conclusion/Implications: This survey provides the first holistic taxonomy of emotional body gesture recognition methods and pinpoints key research gaps, including the need for large-scale in-the-wild datasets, real-time inference, cross-cultural validation, and privacy-preserving designs. By consolidating existing knowledge and laying out future directions, we aim to foster more robust, generalizable, and ethically responsible systems.

Keywords: emotional body gesture recognition, affective computing, human–computer interaction, deep learning, feature extraction, multimodal fusion, benchmarks.
