Generalized Few-Shot Learning (GFSL) aims to recognize both seen (base) and novel classes with limited labeled examples for the novel classes. This paper addresses the critical challenge of learning effective relational knowledge for distinguishing between base and novel classes in GFSL scenarios. We introduce a novel Relational Generalized Few-Shot Learning (RGFSL) framework that explicitly models the relationships between instances and classes using a graph-based approach. Specifically, we construct a graph where nodes represent instances and classes, and edges represent the relationships between them, learned through a dedicated relation module. Our approach enables the propagation of knowledge from base classes to novel classes, facilitating accurate classification even with limited data. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our RGFSL framework significantly outperforms existing GFSL methods in terms of classification accuracy and generalization ability. The results highlight the importance of relational reasoning in GFSL and showcase the potential of our framework for real-world applications. Keywords: Few-Shot Learning, Meta-Learning, Graph Neural Networks, Relational Learning, Generalization.
