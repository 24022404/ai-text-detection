This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing interpretability in object recognition models by presenting a novel approach utilizing Graph Capsule Networks (GCNs). Despite their success, traditional capsule networks often lack the ability to provide clear insights into their decision-making processes, rendering them as black boxes.

Methods/Approach: To overcome this limitation, we propose an innovative model architecture that integrates graph-based computations within capsule networks, enhancing both the interpretability and performance of object recognition systems. The incorporation of graph structures allows for the explicit modeling of part-whole relationships among objects, facilitating a more transparent decision-making process. The proposed method employs graph embedding techniques to capture complex spatial and structural dependencies within the input data, ensuring a comprehensive understanding of object features.

Results/Findings: Empirical evaluations on standard object recognition benchmarks demonstrate that our Interpretable Graph Capsule Networks outperform conventional capsule networks in terms of recognition accuracy while providing significant improvements in model interpretability. Our approach effectively reveals the importance of individual input attributes and their interactions, offering valuable insights into the recognition process. Compared to state-of-the-art methods, our model achieves superior performance in both accuracy and explainability metrics.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of graph structures into capsule networks not only enhances object recognition capabilities but also brings about a substantial improvement in model interpretability. This research contributes a meaningful advancement in the development of transparent and reliable machine learning models. The implications extend to various applications requiring high-stakes decision-making, where understanding and trust in AI systems are paramount. Key keywords include Graph Capsule Networks, interpretability, object recognition, graph embedding, and feature interactions.
