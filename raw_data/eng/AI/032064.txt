Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms often encounter environments containing exogenous state variables and rewards—that is, elements that evolve independently of the agent’s actions and provide no useful information for policy optimization. The presence of such exogenous factors can unnecessarily increase the state and reward space complexity, slowing down learning and degrading performance. This paper addresses the challenge of systematically identifying and eliminating exogenous state variables and reward components to enhance RL efficiency. We propose a novel framework that processes observed trajectories to discover statistical dependencies, isolating endogenous variables and rewards relevant to the agent's decision-making process. Our method combines information-theoretic analysis with feature selection techniques to automatically detect variables uncorrelated with the agent's actions or future returns. We validate the approach on synthetic and benchmark RL environments, demonstrating substantial reductions in state space dimensionality, faster convergence rates, and improved policy performance compared to conventional approaches. The results underscore the importance of model simplification in complex environments and highlight a principled path toward more scalable and interpretable RL systems. Potential applications include robotics, automated systems, and large-scale decision processes where environmental noise and irrelevant dynamics often hinder learning. Keywords: reinforcement learning, exogenous variables, reward decomposition, state space reduction, feature selection, policy optimization.
