Conditional image generation has seen significant advancements, yet robust evaluation remains a challenge. This paper investigates the efficacy of various evaluation metrics in assessing the quality and fidelity of generated images conditioned on specific inputs. We analyze widely used metrics, including Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Inception Score (IS), and Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS), alongside newer metrics designed to capture conditional accuracy. Our methodology involves training several conditional image generation models using diverse datasets and architectures, followed by a comparative analysis of metric scores and human perceptual evaluations. Results indicate that while FID and IS correlate with overall image quality, they often fail to capture fine-grained conditional accuracy. LPIPS demonstrates better sensitivity to structural similarity, but can be susceptible to adversarial perturbations. We propose a combined metric approach leveraging the strengths of each to provide a more comprehensive evaluation framework. This research contributes to a deeper understanding of evaluation metric limitations and offers practical guidelines for selecting and combining metrics to accurately assess conditional image generation models. Keywords: Conditional Image Generation, Evaluation Metrics, FID, IS, LPIPS, Image Quality Assessment, Generative Models.
