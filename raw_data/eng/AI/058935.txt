Face anti-spoofing remains a fundamental challenge in biometric security, particularly due to significant performance drops when models are deployed across unseen domains with varying lighting, backgrounds, and spoofing techniques. This paper introduces a novel domain generalization framework leveraging pseudo-domain labels to enhance the robustness of face anti-spoofing systems. Our approach automatically generates pseudo-domain labels for training samples by clustering intrinsic domain features, enabling the explicit modeling of domain-specific information without relying on predefined domain annotations. We employ a multi-branch network architecture where shared feature extraction is complemented by domain-specific classifiers, trained jointly using the pseudo-domain labels to encourage domain-invariant representations. Extensive experiments conducted on multiple public datasets highlight substantial improvements in cross-domain generalization compared to existing baselines, demonstrating higher accuracy and reduced overfitting to source domains. The proposed method offers a scalable solution for real-world deployments where the diversity of presentation attacks and domain shifts cannot be anticipated in advance. Our findings suggest that integrating pseudo-domain information is an effective strategy for enhancing the adaptability and security of face anti-spoofing technologies. Keywords: domain generalization, face anti-spoofing, pseudo-domain label, biometric security, deep learning, presentation attack detection.
