Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning meaningful representations from unlabeled data, reducing the reliance on costly annotated datasets. This paper addresses the challenge of learning pretext-invariant representations, which remain robust across diverse pretext tasks while capturing essential semantic features. We propose a novel framework that leverages contrastive learning and task augmentation to ensure invariance to pretext transformations, enabling the model to generalize better to downstream tasks. Our approach introduces a dynamic pretext task generator that creates diverse and challenging transformations, coupled with a loss function designed to minimize task-specific biases. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in representation quality, outperforming existing SSL techniques in tasks such as image classification and object detection. The results highlight the importance of pretext invariance in improving generalization and robustness. This work contributes to advancing SSL by providing a scalable and effective solution for learning task-agnostic representations, with potential applications in domains such as computer vision, natural language processing, and beyond. Key keywords include self-supervised learning, pretext tasks, contrastive learning, representation learning, and task invariance.
