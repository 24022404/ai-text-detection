This paper introduces Sparsely Activated Networks, a novel neural network architecture designed to improve computational efficiency and reduce memory usage. The objective is to develop a model that can achieve state-of-the-art performance while minimizing the number of active neurons during inference. Our approach involves a combination of sparse activation functions and adaptive thresholding techniques to selectively activate neurons based on input patterns. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms traditional dense networks in terms of parameter efficiency and computational speed, while maintaining competitive accuracy on benchmark datasets. The key findings highlight the potential of sparsely activated networks for edge AI applications, real-time processing, and energy-constrained devices. Our research contributes to the development of more efficient and scalable neural networks, with implications for a wide range of applications, including computer vision, natural language processing, and autonomous systems. Key keywords: sparse neural networks, efficient AI, adaptive thresholding, edge AI, real-time processing.
