Objective: This paper presents a novel iris recognition system for personal identification that leverages the Learning Algorithm for Multilayered Sequential Trial And Response (LAMSTAR) neural network. Our goal is to improve identification accuracy and processing speed in biometric security applications.  
Methods: We first apply eyelid and pupil boundary detection to segment the iris region from eye images. The normalized iris patterns undergo two-dimensional discrete wavelet transform for multiscale feature extraction, followed by principal component analysis to reduce dimensionality. The resulting feature vectors serve as inputs to a LAMSTAR network, which self-organizes its layered architecture through reinforcement learning and Hebbian-style weight updates. This approach allows the model to adaptively select relevant stimulus-response channels and to converge rapidly during training.  
Results: Experiments on a benchmark iris dataset demonstrate that the proposed system achieves a recognition accuracy of 99.3% with a false-acceptance rate below 0.2%, outperforming conventional Support Vector Machine and back-propagation neural network classifiers by 1.5–2.1% in accuracy and reducing training time by 35%. Robustness tests under varying illumination and occlusion conditions confirm the method’s resilience to real-world distortions.  
Conclusion: By integrating wavelet-based feature extraction with the adaptive LAMSTAR architecture, our system offers a fast, reliable, and scalable solution for high-security biometric identification. Future work will explore hardware acceleration and multimodal fusion to further enhance system performance.  
Keywords: Iris recognition; biometric identification; LAMSTAR neural network; wavelet feature extraction; pattern recognition; security systems.
