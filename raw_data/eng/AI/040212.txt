Neural Architecture Search (NAS) has shown promise in automating the design of neural networks. Differentiable NAS methods, in particular, offer efficiency by learning architectures through gradient descent. However, existing differentiable NAS approaches often suffer from suboptimal architecture selection due to issues such as architecture collapse and instability during training. This paper rethinks the architecture selection process in differentiable NAS by introducing a novel regularization technique that promotes diversity among candidate architectures throughout the optimization. Our approach, termed "Diversified Architecture Regularization" (DAR), encourages the network to explore a wider range of architectural possibilities, preventing premature convergence to a single, potentially suboptimal architecture. We evaluate DAR on several benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, and demonstrate significant improvements in the performance of the searched architectures compared to state-of-the-art differentiable NAS methods. The results indicate that promoting architectural diversity is crucial for effective architecture selection in differentiable NAS, leading to more robust and higher-performing neural network designs. Keywords: Neural Architecture Search, Differentiable NAS, Architecture Selection, Regularization, Deep Learning.
