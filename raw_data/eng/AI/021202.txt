Face attribute manipulation is a crucial task in computer vision, enabling the editing of facial images to modify characteristics such as age, gender, and expression while maintaining overall identity and realism. This paper introduces a novel approach for face attribute manipulation by learning residual images—compact representations that encode only the necessary changes needed to achieve the desired modification. Our method leverages deep convolutional neural networks to generate residuals that are selectively added to input face images, resulting in highly realistic and attribute-consistent outputs. Unlike traditional approaches that often reconstruct faces from scratch or use entire image transformations, our model isolates attribute-specific adjustments, reducing artifacts and preserving fine-grained facial details. We evaluate our approach on multiple benchmark datasets and demonstrate its superiority in both quantitative metrics and visual quality when compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. Extensive experiments highlight the effectiveness of residual learning in controlling attribute intensity, achieving smooth transitions, and enabling multi-attribute editing. The results underscore the potential of residual image learning for broader applications in image editing, virtual avatars, and privacy protection. Keywords: face attribute manipulation, residual learning, deep learning, image editing, convolutional neural networks, computer vision.
