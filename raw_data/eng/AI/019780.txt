This paper investigates the theoretical properties of posterior sampling for reinforcement learning (RL), focusing on deriving worst-case regret bounds. We present a novel analysis of posterior sampling algorithms, specifically Thompson Sampling, within the framework of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). Our approach leverages concentration inequalities and martingale techniques to establish finite-time regret bounds that hold with high probability. The analysis provides a theoretical guarantee on the exploration-exploitation trade-off inherent in RL. We derive a regret bound that scales polynomially with the number of states, actions, and the planning horizon. Furthermore, we demonstrate that under certain conditions, posterior sampling achieves near-optimal regret compared to existing algorithms. The results highlight the efficacy of Bayesian methods in RL for achieving sample-efficient learning. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Posterior Sampling, Thompson Sampling, Regret Bound, Markov Decision Processes, Bayesian Learning.
