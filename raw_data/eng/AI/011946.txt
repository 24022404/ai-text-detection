Understanding contact interactions and motion trajectories is fundamental for advancing robotic manipulation, human-computer interaction, and automated video analysis. This paper presents a novel framework for extracting detailed contact points and motion paths from manipulation videos, enabling in-depth analysis of object interactions. The proposed method leverages a combination of computer vision techniques, deep learning-based object tracking, and motion segmentation algorithms to identify hands, objects, and their contact events over time. Our approach automatically detects grasping, pushing, and releasing actions, and reconstructs corresponding motion trajectories with high spatial and temporal precision. Extensive experiments on publicly available and custom-collected manipulation video datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and accuracy of our system, outperforming existing baselines in terms of contact detection fidelity and motion estimation. The results highlight the potential of our framework for facilitating robotic learning from videos, improving action recognition, and enriching datasets for manipulation research. This work paves the way for robust and scalable extraction of actionable physical interaction information from raw visual data, contributing to advancements in robotics, human-robot collaboration, and intelligent video analysis.

Keywords: contact detection, motion extraction, manipulation videos, computer vision, deep learning, object tracking, human-object interaction, robotics.
