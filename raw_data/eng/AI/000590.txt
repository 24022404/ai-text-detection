The Extended Isolation Forest (EIF) algorithm addresses limitations in the original Isolation Forest (IF) method for anomaly detection, particularly in handling high-dimensional data and complex distributions. This paper introduces a novel approach that extends the IF framework by incorporating random hyperplanes for data partitioning, enabling more robust and accurate anomaly detection across diverse datasets. The proposed method improves upon the original IF by reducing bias in axis-parallel splits and enhancing the detection of anomalies in non-linear and irregularly structured data. Experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets, comparing EIF with traditional IF and other state-of-the-art anomaly detection techniques. Results demonstrate that EIF achieves superior performance in terms of precision, recall, and F1-score, particularly in high-dimensional scenarios. The algorithm's computational efficiency remains comparable to the original IF, making it scalable for large-scale applications. This research contributes a significant advancement in unsupervised anomaly detection, with potential applications in fraud detection, network security, and industrial monitoring. Key innovations include the use of hyperplane-based partitioning and a refined scoring mechanism, which collectively improve the interpretability and reliability of anomaly detection. Keywords: anomaly detection, isolation forest, high-dimensional data, unsupervised learning, hyperplane partitioning.
