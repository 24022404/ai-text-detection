Co-salient object detection (CoSOD) aims to simultaneously identify common salient objects within a group of images, leveraging inter-image relationships to enhance detection accuracy. This paper introduces a novel Group Collaborative Learning (GCL) framework for CoSOD, designed to explicitly model and exploit collaborative information among images. Our approach constructs a group affinity graph to capture inter-image relationships and employs a message-passing mechanism to propagate contextual information across the group. This collaborative learning process enables the model to effectively distinguish co-salient objects from background clutter by leveraging shared features and contextual cues. We introduce a novel collaborative loss function that encourages consistency in co-saliency predictions across the image group. Extensive experiments on several benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed GCL framework significantly outperforms existing CoSOD methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance in terms of precision, recall, and F-measure. The results highlight the effectiveness of our group-level collaborative learning approach in improving the accuracy and robustness of co-salient object detection. Keywords: Co-salient object detection, collaborative learning, group affinity, message passing, computer vision.
