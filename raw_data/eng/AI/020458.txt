This paper introduces Cover Tree Bayesian Reinforcement Learning (CTBRL), a novel framework designed to enhance decision-making in complex, high-dimensional environments. The primary objective of this research is to address the scalability and computational efficiency challenges inherent in traditional Bayesian Reinforcement Learning (BRL) methods. To achieve this, we propose a hybrid approach that integrates cover trees—a hierarchical data structure for efficient nearest-neighbor search—with Bayesian inference techniques. This combination enables faster exploration of state-action spaces while maintaining probabilistic reasoning capabilities.  

Our methodology involves constructing a cover tree to represent the environment's state space, which reduces the computational overhead of updating posterior distributions. We then employ a modified Thompson sampling strategy to balance exploration and exploitation effectively. Experimental results on benchmark environments demonstrate that CTBRL outperforms existing BRL methods in terms of convergence speed and computational efficiency, particularly in scenarios with large state-action spaces.  

The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to scale Bayesian Reinforcement Learning to more complex problems without sacrificing accuracy. This advancement has significant implications for real-world applications, such as robotics, autonomous systems, and adaptive control, where efficient decision-making under uncertainty is critical. The proposed framework opens new avenues for research in scalable reinforcement learning and probabilistic modeling.  

**Keywords**: Bayesian Reinforcement Learning, Cover Trees, Thompson Sampling, Scalability, Decision-Making, Probabilistic Models.
