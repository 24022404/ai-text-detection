Off-policy reinforcement learning (RL) holds the promise of learning optimal policies from previously collected or external data. However, its effectiveness is often hampered by distributional shift, leading to instability and poor performance. This paper introduces a novel approach, Learning Routines for Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning (LRORL), which aims to mitigate this issue by identifying and leveraging consistent, high-reward sequences of actions, termed "routines," within the off-policy data. LRORL employs a clustering algorithm to discover these routines and integrates them into the policy learning process as auxiliary actions. By biasing the policy towards these learned routines, LRORL encourages exploration of promising state-action trajectories. We evaluate LRORL on a suite of benchmark control tasks, demonstrating significant improvements in sample efficiency and final performance compared to state-of-the-art off-policy RL algorithms. Our results highlight the potential of learning and exploiting behavioral patterns within off-policy datasets to accelerate and stabilize reinforcement learning. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Off-Policy Learning, Routines, Imitation Learning, Behavioral Cloning, Clustering.
