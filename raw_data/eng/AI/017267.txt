Hamiltonian Generative Networks (HGNs) offer a novel approach to generative modeling by integrating Hamiltonian dynamics into the training process. This paper introduces HGNs, which leverage Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs) to constrain the latent space evolution, promoting more stable and physically plausible sample generation, particularly for sequential data. The method uses an encoder-decoder architecture where the latent space is governed by a learned Hamiltonian, ensuring energy conservation and smoother transitions. We demonstrate that HGNs, trained with a custom loss function incorporating both data likelihood and Hamiltonian constraints, achieve superior performance in generating complex time-series data compared to traditional generative adversarial networks (GANs) and variational autoencoders (VAEs). Specifically, experiments on benchmark datasets, including pendulum motion and human activity data, show that HGNs yield lower reconstruction errors and improved long-term prediction accuracy. These findings highlight the potential of incorporating Hamiltonian dynamics into generative models for enhanced stability, interpretability, and the generation of physically realistic data. Keywords: Generative Modeling, Hamiltonian Neural Networks, Hamiltonian Dynamics, Time-Series Generation, Deep Learning, Energy Conservation.
