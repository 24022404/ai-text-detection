This paper addresses the challenge of learning robust visual correspondences across time-varying scenes without relying on extensive labeled data. We propose a novel framework that leverages the inherent cycle-consistency of time to establish reliable correspondences between frames in video sequences. By exploiting the temporal continuity of objects and scenes, our approach enforces consistency constraints across cyclic time intervals, enabling the model to learn meaningful correspondences in a self-supervised manner. The proposed method integrates a deep neural network architecture optimized for temporal feature extraction and correspondence estimation, combined with a cycle-consistency loss function that ensures temporal coherence. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing self-supervised methods in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly in dynamic and occluded scenarios. The framework’s ability to generalize across diverse datasets highlights its potential for applications in video analysis, 3D reconstruction, and autonomous systems. This work contributes a scalable and efficient solution for correspondence learning, reducing the dependency on annotated data while advancing the state-of-the-art in temporal correspondence estimation.  

**Keywords**: temporal correspondence, cycle-consistency, self-supervised learning, video analysis, deep learning, feature matching.
