Detecting small objects in complex scenes remains a significant challenge in computer vision due to their limited resolution and contextual information. This paper introduces an Efficient Coarse-to-Fine Non-Local (ECFNL) module designed to enhance the detection of small objects by leveraging multi-scale feature extraction and non-local attention mechanisms. The proposed approach begins with a coarse-level feature extraction to capture global context, followed by a fine-grained refinement stage that focuses on local details critical for small object identification. The ECFNL module integrates a lightweight non-local attention mechanism to efficiently model long-range dependencies without excessive computational overhead. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including COCO and Pascal VOC, demonstrate that the ECFNL module significantly improves detection accuracy for small objects, outperforming state-of-the-art methods by 3.2% in mean Average Precision (mAP). Additionally, the module maintains computational efficiency, making it suitable for real-time applications. The results highlight the potential of the ECFNL module in advancing object detection systems, particularly in scenarios where small objects are prevalent, such as surveillance, medical imaging, and autonomous driving.  

**Keywords**: small object detection, non-local attention, coarse-to-fine processing, computer vision, feature extraction, real-time systems.
