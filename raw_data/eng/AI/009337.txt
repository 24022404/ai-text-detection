This paper addresses the challenge of inferring the structure of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) from observed data, a crucial problem in various fields such as physics, biology, and engineering. Our objective is to develop a novel approach that can accurately identify the underlying ODEs that govern complex systems. We propose a machine learning-based method that utilizes a combination of symbolic regression and neural networks to learn the structure of ODEs from data. Our approach is able to handle noisy and incomplete data, and can infer ODEs with non-linear and non-polynomial terms. The results show that our method outperforms existing techniques in terms of accuracy and robustness, and can be applied to a wide range of problems, including population dynamics, chemical kinetics, and electrical circuits. The key contributions of this research are the development of a flexible and scalable framework for inferring ODEs, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in real-world applications. Our work has important implications for fields such as systems biology, control theory, and data-driven modeling, and can be used to discover new mathematical models that can inform and improve our understanding of complex systems. Key keywords: ordinary differential equations, machine learning, symbolic regression, neural networks, data-driven modeling.
