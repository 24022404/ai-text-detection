Recent advances in video understanding have primarily relied on specialized architectures that process spatiotemporal features, often at significant computational cost. This paper investigates whether an image classifier, with appropriate adaptations, can serve as a sufficient model for video understanding tasks. We propose a framework that leverages pre-trained image classifiers applied to individual video frames, aggregating their predictions through simple yet effective temporal fusion strategies, such as majority voting and attention-based weighting. Experimental evaluations on standard video datasets, including action recognition and event detection benchmarks, demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive or superior performance compared to many dedicated video models, while offering substantial improvements in efficiency and model simplicity. Our findings suggest that, for many video analysis tasks, complex temporal modeling may be unnecessary, and carefully designed image classification pipelines can deliver robust results. This work contributes to the ongoing exploration of efficient deep learning models in multimedia analysis and opens avenues for resource-constrained video understanding applications. Keywords: video understanding, image classification, temporal fusion, action recognition, deep learning, efficient models.
