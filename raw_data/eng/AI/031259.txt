This paper presents a novel reinforcement learning (RL) approach for optimizing optical thin-film design, a critical challenge in photonics and materials science. Traditional methods for designing thin-film structures often rely on iterative trial-and-error processes or heuristic algorithms, which can be time-consuming and suboptimal. To address these limitations, we propose an RL-based framework that leverages deep Q-learning to efficiently explore and optimize the design space of thin-film layers. The method incorporates a reward mechanism tailored to maximize optical performance metrics, such as reflectance, transmittance, and absorption, while minimizing fabrication constraints. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed RL approach outperforms conventional optimization techniques, achieving superior optical properties with fewer iterations. Furthermore, the framework exhibits robustness across a wide range of material configurations and wavelength spectra. This research contributes to the advancement of automated design tools in photonics, offering a scalable and adaptive solution for complex optical engineering problems. The proposed method has potential applications in solar cells, anti-reflective coatings, and optical sensors, paving the way for more efficient and innovative thin-film technologies.  

**Keywords**: reinforcement learning, optical thin-film design, deep Q-learning, photonics, optimization, optical performance.
