This paper presents an overview and synthesis of the NIPS 2016 Workshop on Representation Learning in Artificial and Biological Neural Networks (MLINI 2016), which aimed to bridge insights between machine learning and neuroscience in the context of neural representations. The primary objective was to foster interdisciplinary dialogue and highlight breakthroughs in understanding how artificial and biological neural systems encode, process, and learn representations. The workshop featured a variety of approaches, including deep learning algorithms, unsupervised and supervised model training techniques, analyses of neural activity in biological brains, and computational frameworks for comparing representational structures. Key findings discussed include novel algorithms for learning robust feature representations, comparative analyses of representations in deep networks and neural circuits, and new interpretability techniques that link artificial neural activations to patterns in brain data. The discussions also emphasized the importance of transfer learning, representation disentanglement, and the biological plausibility of deep learning models. The workshop’s cross-disciplinary contributions illuminate shared challenges and methodologies, paving the way for more biologically inspired artificial intelligence and improved understanding of neural information processing. These advances have implications for AI research, neuroscience, and applications such as pattern recognition, cognitive modeling, and brain-computer interfaces. Keywords: representation learning, neural networks, deep learning, artificial intelligence, neuroscience, interpretability, transfer learning, biological plausibility.
