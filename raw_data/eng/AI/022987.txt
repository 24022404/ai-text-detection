K-shot learning aims to rapidly adapt models to new tasks with limited labeled data. This paper addresses the challenge of overfitting in deep networks during K-shot learning, which often hinders generalization performance. We propose a novel regularization technique, termed Feature Space Entropy Regularization (FSER), designed to encourage diverse and informative feature representations. FSER minimizes the entropy of feature activations across different tasks, effectively preventing the model from relying on task-specific spurious correlations. We integrate FSER into a meta-learning framework and evaluate its performance on several benchmark datasets, including miniImageNet and tieredImageNet. Results demonstrate that our approach achieves significant improvements in accuracy and robustness compared to state-of-the-art K-shot learning methods. Specifically, FSER improves average accuracy by 3-5% on 5-shot classification tasks. Furthermore, we show that FSER enhances the model's ability to generalize to unseen tasks and domains. This work contributes a practical and effective regularization strategy for improving the efficiency and generalization of deep networks in K-shot learning scenarios. Keywords: K-shot learning, meta-learning, regularization, deep learning, generalization, entropy minimization.
