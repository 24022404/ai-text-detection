This paper presents a novel approach to 3D reconstruction using Deep Implicit Moving Least-Squares (DIMLS) functions. The objective is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of 3D modeling from sparse and noisy point cloud data. Our method leverages a deep learning framework to learn implicit representations of 3D surfaces, combining the benefits of moving least-squares techniques with the expressive power of neural networks. The DIMLS approach enables the reconstruction of complex geometries and detailed surface features, outperforming traditional methods in terms of reconstruction quality and robustness. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving state-of-the-art performance on various benchmark datasets. The proposed method has significant implications for applications in computer vision, robotics, and computer-aided design, enabling the creation of high-fidelity 3D models from imperfect or incomplete data. Key contributions include the introduction of a novel implicit representation framework, the development of a deep learning-based moving least-squares algorithm, and the demonstration of improved 3D reconstruction accuracy and efficiency. Relevant keywords: 3D reconstruction, deep learning, implicit surfaces, moving least-squares, computer vision, geometry processing.
