This paper introduces a novel approach to language-based image editing using recurrent attentive models, addressing the challenge of accurately interpreting and executing textual instructions to modify visual content. The proposed method leverages a recurrent neural network (RNN) architecture enhanced with attention mechanisms to dynamically focus on relevant image regions based on natural language descriptions. By integrating multimodal learning, the model aligns textual inputs with corresponding visual features, enabling precise and context-aware image transformations. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate the model's ability to perform diverse editing tasks, such as object addition, removal, and attribute modification, with high accuracy and robustness. Comparative evaluations against existing methods highlight significant improvements in both qualitative and quantitative metrics, including user satisfaction and task completion rates. The findings underscore the potential of recurrent attentive models in advancing language-guided image editing, offering practical applications in creative design, automated content generation, and assistive technologies. Key contributions include the development of a scalable framework for multimodal alignment and the introduction of a new attention mechanism tailored for fine-grained image-text interactions. Keywords: image editing, recurrent neural networks, attention mechanisms, multimodal learning, natural language processing.
