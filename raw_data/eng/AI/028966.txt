Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown remarkable success in various domains, but its effectiveness often hinges on the efficiency of exploration and the quality of the learned policies. Off-policy RL methods, which leverage data collected from different policies, offer a promising approach to improve sample efficiency. However, these methods face challenges in effectively learning and generalizing routines that capture long-term behaviors. This paper addresses the problem of learning robust routines in off-policy RL to enhance decision-making in complex environments. We propose a novel framework that integrates routine discovery with off-policy learning, enabling agents to identify and exploit recurring patterns in their interactions with the environment. Our approach employs a combination of temporal abstraction and hierarchical policy learning to decompose tasks into reusable routines, which are then optimized using an off-policy RL algorithm. Experimental results on benchmark environments demonstrate that our method significantly improves learning efficiency and policy performance compared to existing off-policy approaches. The framework also exhibits better generalization capabilities, particularly in tasks requiring long-term planning and adaptability. This research contributes to advancing off-policy RL by introducing a systematic way to learn and utilize routines, offering potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and other domains requiring efficient and adaptive decision-making.  

**Keywords**: Reinforcement Learning, Off-Policy Learning, Routine Discovery, Temporal Abstraction, Hierarchical Policies, Decision-Making.
