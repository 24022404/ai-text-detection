This paper addresses the challenge of pedestrian detection in thermal imagery, a crucial task in various applications such as security surveillance and autonomous driving. Traditional visual light-based detection methods are often unreliable in low-light conditions, underscoring the need for robust thermal image analysis.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel approach that integrates synthesized images into the pedestrian detection process within thermal imagery. Our method leverages state-of-the-art machine learning and image synthesis techniques to enhance the training dataset with high-quality synthetic samples. By utilizing a deep learning model specifically designed for thermal data, we aim to improve detection accuracy and resilience against environmental variability.

Results/Findings: The proposed method was evaluated on benchmark datasets and demonstrated superior performance compared to conventional thermal detection methods. Our model achieved a significant increase in detection accuracy and robustness, particularly under challenging conditions such as partial occlusions and varied backgrounds. Comparative analysis with existing techniques highlights the effectiveness of incorporating synthesized images into the thermal imagery detection framework.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field by introducing a robust framework for pedestrian detection that effectively utilizes synthesized thermal images. Our findings suggest potential applications in enhancing safety and operational efficiency in nighttime surveillance and autonomous systems. Future work can extend these findings by exploring additional synthetic data generation techniques and real-time deployment scenarios.

Keywords: Pedestrian detection, thermal imagery, synthesized images, machine learning, deep learning, security surveillance, autonomous driving.
