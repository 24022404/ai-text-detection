**Abstract:**

Face restoration aims to recover high-quality face images from degraded observations, often encountering unknown and complex distortions in real-world scenarios. This paper presents a novel blind face restoration framework leveraging deep multi-scale component dictionaries to effectively address the challenges posed by unknown degradation. Our approach decomposes facial images into multiple semantic components, such as eyes, nose, and mouth, and learns hierarchical feature representations at different spatial scales. These representations are stored in specialized deep dictionaries, enabling adaptive reconstruction tailored to each facial region’s needs. The restoration process utilizes a selective synthesis strategy, dynamically retrieving the most appropriate features from the learned dictionaries to enhance image fidelity and restore structural details. Experimental results on standard face restoration benchmarks demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms state-of-the-art approaches in terms of both visual quality and quantitative metrics, particularly for faces undergoing severe blur, noise, or compression artifacts. The proposed solution exhibits strong generalization to a variety of real-world degradations without prior knowledge of the distortion types, verifying its robustness and practicality. Our findings highlight the effectiveness of deep multi-scale component dictionaries for blind face restoration and open new directions for high-fidelity face image enhancement technologies. 

**Keywords:** blind face restoration, deep learning, multi-scale dictionaries, image reconstruction, face enhancement, semantic decomposition.
