Vehicle re-identification (Re-ID) has emerged as a crucial task in intelligent transportation systems, enabling the tracking and recognition of vehicles across multiple non-overlapping cameras or locations. This paper presents a comprehensive study of current methods in vehicle re-identification, focusing on both traditional feature-based approaches and advanced deep learning models. We systematically review algorithms that leverage color, texture, and shape descriptors, as well as state-of-the-art convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and attention mechanisms designed for robust feature extraction. Various datasets and evaluation metrics prevalent in the field are analyzed to provide a standardized framework for comparison. Through extensive experiments, we benchmark several representative methods on widely used vehicle Re-ID datasets, highlighting the strengths and limitations of each. The results demonstrate that deep learning-based approaches outperform traditional methods, particularly in handling varying viewpoints, lighting conditions, and occlusions. Furthermore, we discuss the integration of auxiliary information such as license plates and spatio-temporal cues to further enhance identification accuracy. The paper concludes by identifying ongoing challenges, such as scalability and privacy concerns, and suggests directions for future research. Keywords: vehicle re-identification, intelligent transportation systems, deep learning, feature extraction, computer vision, CNN, surveillance.
