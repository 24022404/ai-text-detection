This research addresses the challenge of effectively modeling online user preferences by transitioning from implicit to explicit feedback mechanisms. Traditional recommendation systems often struggle with accurately capturing both sequential behaviors and the nuanced long-short term preferences of users, which can limit their performance and personalization capabilities.

Methods/Approach: We propose a deep neural network architecture specifically designed to model user interaction data. By leveraging both implicit and explicit feedback, our approach utilizes recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to capture sequential user behaviors and attention mechanisms to discern long-term and short-term preferences. The model is trained on a large dataset of online interactions, optimizing for both prediction accuracy and the personalization of recommendations.

Results/Findings: The experimental results demonstrate that our model significantly improves the accuracy of user preference predictions compared to baseline methods. Specifically, the incorporation of explicit feedback enabled the system to better understand user intent, resulting in enhanced recommendation quality. Furthermore, the attention mechanisms successfully identified and weighted relevant user behaviors, contributing to an adaptable and dynamic recommendation system.

Conclusion/Implications: This study presents a novel framework for integrating implicit and explicit feedback in user preference modeling, offering substantial improvements over existing approaches. The proposed system is particularly beneficial in applications requiring personalized content delivery, such as online retail and media streaming services. Future work may explore further refinement of the model to support real-time user interaction data and extend its applicability to diverse domains.

Keywords: implicit feedback, explicit feedback, deep neural networks, sequential behaviors, long-short term preferences, recommendation systems, personalization, user interaction data.
