Visual Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful paradigm for image representation and processing in computer vision, leveraging the success of transformer architectures originally designed for natural language processing. This paper explores the application of token-based image representation, where images are divided into patches and treated as sequences of tokens, enabling transformers to capture global dependencies and spatial relationships effectively. We propose a novel framework that enhances the efficiency and scalability of ViTs by introducing adaptive tokenization strategies and lightweight attention mechanisms. Our approach addresses key challenges such as computational complexity and memory usage, making it suitable for real-time applications. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance in image classification, object detection, and segmentation tasks, outperforming traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and existing transformer-based models. The findings highlight the potential of token-based image processing to revolutionize computer vision, offering improved accuracy and flexibility for diverse applications. This research contributes to advancing the understanding of transformer architectures in visual domains and provides a foundation for future innovations in scalable and efficient vision systems.  

**Keywords**: Visual Transformers, Token-based Representation, Computer Vision, Attention Mechanisms, Image Processing, Adaptive Tokenization.
