**Abstract:**

Detecting localized hue modifications is a critical task in digital image forensics, especially for verifying visual authenticity in the era of pervasive photo editing. This paper presents a novel approach to hue modification localization based on pair matching techniques. The proposed method formulates the detection problem as identifying inconsistencies between pixel pairs across an image, leveraging statistical and structural similarities to highlight regions where hue alterations have occurred. Our approach integrates a matching network that compares potential pairs, quantifies hue disparities, and generates a probability map indicating tampered areas. Extensive experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets containing both natural images and synthetically manipulated samples. Results demonstrate superior localization accuracy and robustness of our method compared to conventional techniques, including those based on handcrafted features and convolutional neural networks. The findings suggest that pair matching excels in capturing subtle hue manipulation artifacts while maintaining computational efficiency. This work provides a reliable tool for forensic analysts and content verification systems, with broad implications for digital image integrity assessment, media authentication, and the development of anti-fake technologies. Key keywords: image forensics, hue modification, pair matching, tampering localization, digital image authentication.
