**Abstract**

Efficient and accurate object detection using Light Detection and Ranging (LIDAR) sensors is vital for autonomous driving and smart transportation systems. Cooperative perception, where multiple LIDAR-equipped agents share sensor information, enhances detection performance but poses challenges in bandwidth-constrained environments due to the high volume of data. This paper presents a novel bandwidth-adaptive feature sharing framework for cooperative LIDAR object detection, optimizing the balance between communication load and detection accuracy. Our method dynamically selects and compresses informative features from local LIDAR data based on real-time network bandwidth conditions. A multi-resolution feature extraction module and adaptive prioritization algorithm are integrated to identify and transmit the most relevant features to neighboring agents. Experimental results on standard autonomous driving datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly reduces communication overhead—by up to 60%—while maintaining or even improving detection accuracy compared to existing feature sharing strategies. The proposed solution enables scalable and robust cooperative LIDAR detection in bandwidth-limited vehicular networks, with broad applicability to intelligent transportation, connected vehicles, and autonomous fleet operations. Key contributions include dynamic bandwidth adaptation, resource-aware feature selection, and empirical validation in realistic cooperative scenarios. 

**Keywords:** cooperative perception, LIDAR object detection, bandwidth adaptation, feature sharing, autonomous vehicles, connected transportation, sensor fusion.
