Wide neural networks, characterized by an abundance of parameters, often exhibit complex internal representations. This paper introduces the concept of "representation mitosis," a phenomenon wherein the internal representations of a wide neural network spontaneously bifurcate into distinct, functionally specialized subpopulations during training. We investigate this phenomenon through empirical analysis of various network architectures and datasets, demonstrating its prevalence and impact on generalization performance. Our methods involve analyzing the similarity and correlation structure of hidden layer activations throughout training. Results indicate that representation mitosis leads to increased robustness and improved out-of-sample accuracy compared to networks without this characteristic. Furthermore, we explore the factors influencing the onset and degree of representation mitosis, including network width, learning rate, and regularization techniques. Finally, we discuss the implications of representation mitosis for understanding the optimization dynamics of wide neural networks and its potential applications in designing more efficient and robust deep learning models. Keywords: neural networks, representation learning, generalization, wide networks, optimization, mitosis.
