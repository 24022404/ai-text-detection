In this model, binary de Bruijn sequences are encoded using sequences of beacon pulses, with a key innovation to address timing jitter that can arise from long sequences without pulses. Instead of simply mapping a '1' or '0' to the presence or absence of a pulse, two pulse slots are assigned to each bit: an on-on pair represents a '1', while an on-off pair represents a '0', generating what is known as the HdB sequence. This encoding strategy effectively eliminates the problem of consecutive no-pulses, thereby enhancing signaling reliability. However, it doubles the length of the sequence, requiring 2n pulse slots to represent a sequence of length n, and to determine the position within the sequence, a received subsequence of 2 logn pulse slots is necessary.
