The primary objective of this research is to enhance the transparency and interpretability of reinforcement learning (RL) agents through the development of an innovative framework named Action-Conditional β-VAE. The study addresses the challenges of understanding and governing the decision-making processes of deep reinforcement learning systems, which are often perceived as black boxes due to their complexity and opaque nature. The proposed method integrates a variational autoencoder (VAE) into the reinforcement learning paradigm, conditioned on the agent's actions, to enable a more interpretable representation of its decision-making policy. This approach facilitates the disentanglement of latent variables in the policy space, thus providing clearer insights into the agent's behavior. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of the Action-Conditional β-VAE framework by showcasing improved interpretability without sacrificing performance. The model was tested on a suite of benchmark deep RL environments, highlighting its ability to elucidate the underlying policy structure and reveal the critical factors influencing the agent's actions. The findings underscore the potential of this approach to advance the field of transparent reinforcement learning, offering significant contributions towards the effective governance of autonomous agents. The study holds implications for diverse applications, from autonomous systems to complex decision-making processes, where transparency and accountability are paramount. Keywords include reinforcement learning, transparency, variational autoencoder, interpretability, autonomous agents.
