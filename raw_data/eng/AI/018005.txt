This paper addresses the challenge of identifying and attending to relevant regions in videos, a critical task for applications such as video understanding, summarization, and human-computer interaction. While existing methods often rely on handcrafted features or heuristic approaches, we propose a novel deep learning framework that learns to predict eye fixation patterns, which are strong indicators of human attention. Our approach leverages a combination of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to model spatiotemporal dependencies in video data, enabling the system to dynamically focus on salient regions. We evaluate our model on publicly available eye-tracking datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods in terms of fixation prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight the effectiveness of our approach in capturing human-like attention mechanisms, offering potential applications in video analysis, autonomous systems, and assistive technologies. This work contributes to the growing field of attention modeling by providing a data-driven solution that bridges the gap between human visual perception and machine understanding of video content.  

**Keywords**: video attention, eye fixation prediction, deep learning, spatiotemporal modeling, human-computer interaction.
