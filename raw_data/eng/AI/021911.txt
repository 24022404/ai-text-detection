The paper addresses the challenge of classifying time series data by introducing a novel approach that leverages the Sinkhorn divergence of topological signature estimates. This method aims to improve the accuracy and efficiency of time series classification, which is crucial for various applications in fields such as finance, healthcare, and IoT.

Methods/Approach: The proposed approach utilizes topological signature estimates derived from persistent homology, a tool from topological data analysis, to capture the intrinsic structure of time series data. Sinkhorn divergences, a regularized form of the Wasserstein distance, are employed to measure the similarities between these signatures, providing a computationally efficient distance metric for classification. The integration of these techniques is designed to handle the noise and variability inherent in time series data.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms traditional time series classification techniques, both in terms of classification accuracy and computational efficiency. The results indicate significant improvements when compared to existing methods, highlighting the robustness of the topological signature estimates paired with Sinkhorn divergence across diverse datasets.

Conclusion/Implications: This study presents a novel contribution to time series classification by combining topological data analysis with advanced regularized transportation metrics. The findings suggest that the method is capable of capturing complex temporal patterns, thus offering potential applications in areas needing precise and efficient time series analysis. The research opens pathways for further exploration into the integration of topological features in machine learning frameworks.

Key Keywords: time series classification, Sinkhorn divergence, topological signature estimates, persistent homology, Wasserstein distance, topological data analysis.
