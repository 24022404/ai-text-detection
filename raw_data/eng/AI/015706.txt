This paper introduces an innovative approach to unsupervised meta domain translation, enabling the transfer of knowledge across disparate domains without requiring labeled data. The objective is to develop a flexible and adaptable framework that can learn to translate data from one domain to another, facilitating cross-domain applications and improving model generalizability. Our method leverages meta-learning techniques to learn a domain-agnostic representation, which is then fine-tuned for specific domain translation tasks using a novel unsupervised loss function. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in various domain translation benchmarks. The key findings highlight the importance of meta-learning in capturing domain-invariant features and the potential of unsupervised domain translation for real-world applications. This research contributes to the advancement of domain adaptation and transfer learning, with implications for areas such as computer vision, natural language processing, and robotics, and is particularly relevant to topics like few-shot learning, unsupervised learning, and cross-domain transfer.
