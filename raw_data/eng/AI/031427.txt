This research addresses the challenge of effectively classifying large-scale videos by aggregating frame-level features into a compact and informative video representation. The study aims to improve the efficiency and performance of video classification models in rapidly growing video datasets.

Methods/Approach: We introduce NeXtVLAD, a novel neural network architecture designed to aggregate frame-level features with improved computational efficiency and accuracy. This model extends the VLAD (Vector of Locally Aggregated Descriptors) by incorporating a series of enhancements that optimize the pooling layer, making it suitable for deep learning frameworks. NeXtVLAD utilizes a multi-head attention mechanism to capture complex feature relationships across frames and employs a scalable fusion strategy for robust aggregation.

Results/Findings: The experimental evaluations demonstrate that NeXtVLAD significantly outperforms existing frame-level feature aggregation methods in large-scale video classification tasks. Tested on benchmark datasets, our model exhibits superior accuracy and computational efficiency, achieving a noteworthy reduction in model size and execution time without compromising classification performance. Comparisons with state-of-the-art approaches highlight NeXtVLAD's ability to maintain high performance at a reduced computational cost.

Conclusion/Implications: The NeXtVLAD model presents a powerful and efficient solution for aggregating video features, offering substantial improvements in both speed and accuracy for large-scale video classification. This research contributes to the field by providing an innovative approach to feature aggregation, with potential applications ranging from video content analysis to real-time video retrieval systems. The model's scalability and efficiency make it a valuable asset for handling ever-growing video datasets in various technological domains.

Keywords: NeXtVLAD, video classification, neural network, feature aggregation, large-scale, efficiency, frame-level features, model optimization.
