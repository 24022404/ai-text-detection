Microwave imaging (MWI) is a promising technique for brain anomaly detection due to its non-ionizing nature and potential for low-cost implementation. However, achieving high-resolution MWI is challenging due to the complex electromagnetic properties of the brain and the limited number of measurement antennas. This paper introduces a novel approach utilizing a Graph Attention Network (GAT) to enhance MWI for brain anomaly detection. Our method represents the MWI reconstruction problem as a graph, where nodes correspond to image pixels and edges represent spatial relationships. The GAT learns adaptive weights for these edges, effectively capturing long-range dependencies and improving image reconstruction quality. We train the GAT using simulated microwave data from realistic brain models with and without anomalies. Results demonstrate that the proposed GAT-based MWI method significantly improves anomaly localization accuracy and reduces image artifacts compared to traditional reconstruction algorithms. The proposed method achieves a higher contrast-to-noise ratio and lower reconstruction error, indicating superior performance in detecting and characterizing brain anomalies. This work contributes a novel application of graph neural networks to MWI, offering a potential pathway toward more accurate and efficient brain imaging systems.

Keywords: Microwave Imaging, Brain Anomaly Detection, Graph Attention Network, Deep Learning, Image Reconstruction.
