Resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) provides valuable insights into brain functional connectivity. Analyzing these complex relationships requires sophisticated methods capable of capturing intricate patterns. This paper introduces a novel approach leveraging Graph Isomorphism Networks (GINs) for improved rs-fMRI functional connectivity analysis. We construct brain networks from rs-fMRI data, treating brain regions as nodes and functional connections as edges, then employ GINs to learn node embeddings that effectively represent functional connectivity patterns. Our approach enhances the identification of subtle but significant connectivity differences between subject groups. The results demonstrate that GIN-based analysis outperforms traditional methods, such as correlation-based connectivity measures and conventional graph neural networks, in classification accuracy and sensitivity to nuanced connectivity changes. This method offers a promising avenue for investigating neurological disorders and understanding brain function. Keywords: rs-fMRI, functional connectivity, Graph Isomorphism Network, brain network, graph neural network, neuroimaging.
