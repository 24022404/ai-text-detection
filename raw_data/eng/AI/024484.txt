Vertebral body segmentation is a crucial step in spine analysis for clinical diagnosis, treatment planning, and surgical navigation. This study presents an initial experience with the GrowCut algorithm for semi-automatic segmentation of vertebral bodies from medical imaging data, emphasizing its workflow, performance, and practical applications. The proposed approach integrates GrowCut—a region-growing technique enhanced by user-labeled seeds—within a structured workflow that guides users in efficiently delineating vertebral structures in computed tomography (CT) scans. Our evaluation involved multiple datasets, assessing segmentation accuracy, processing time, and user interaction requirements. Results indicate that GrowCut offers a high degree of segmentation precision while substantially reducing manual effort compared to traditional slice-by-slice annotation. Furthermore, streamlined integration into visualization tools supports real-time feedback and editing, broadening its utility in clinical and research settings. The study highlights the potential of GrowCut-based workflows to facilitate reliable vertebral body segmentation, with implications for 3D modeling, quantitative morphometry, and preoperative planning. Keywords: vertebral segmentation, GrowCut, medical imaging, CT, workflow automation, 3D reconstruction, spine analysis.
