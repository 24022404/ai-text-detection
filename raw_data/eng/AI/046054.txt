This paper presents a novel approach to game theoretic optimization using a gradient-based Nikaido-Isoda function. The objective is to develop an efficient method for finding equilibria in multi-player games, addressing the challenges of non-convexity and high dimensionality. Our approach employs a gradient-based optimization technique to minimize the Nikaido-Isoda function, which measures the distance to the equilibrium. We utilize a machine learning framework to learn the game dynamics and compute the gradients of the Nikaido-Isoda function. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method in converging to the equilibrium in various game scenarios, outperforming existing methods in terms of computational efficiency and accuracy. The key findings highlight the importance of incorporating game theoretic insights into optimization algorithms, enabling the development of more robust and adaptive decision-making systems. Our research contributes to the advancement of game theory and optimization, with potential applications in fields such as artificial intelligence, economics, and robotics, particularly in areas like multi-agent systems, mechanism design, and autonomous decision-making, leveraging key concepts like gradient descent, game equilibrium, and Nikaido-Isoda function.
