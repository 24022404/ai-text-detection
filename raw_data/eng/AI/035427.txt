Gaussian Processes (GPs) are widely used in machine learning for regression and classification tasks due to their flexibility and probabilistic nature. However, selecting an appropriate kernel function, which defines the covariance structure of the GP, remains a critical challenge. This paper introduces a novel approach to automatically evolve GP kernels from elementary mathematical expressions using genetic programming. The proposed method leverages a symbolic representation of kernel functions and employs evolutionary algorithms to explore the space of possible kernels, optimizing for model performance on specific datasets. Experiments demonstrate that the evolved kernels outperform traditional handcrafted kernels, such as the Radial Basis Function (RBF) and Matérn kernels, in terms of predictive accuracy and generalization. Furthermore, the approach uncovers interpretable kernel structures that provide insights into the underlying data patterns. This work contributes to the field of automated machine learning by offering a scalable and data-driven solution to kernel design, with potential applications in areas such as time-series forecasting, spatial modeling, and uncertainty quantification.  

**Keywords**: Gaussian Processes, kernel evolution, genetic programming, automated machine learning, symbolic regression.
