This paper addresses the challenge of structuring digital images using a limited color palette, which has significant applications in areas such as compression, cartoonization, and non-photorealistic rendering. We propose a novel learning-based framework that efficiently reconstructs structured representations of images while restricting the output to a predefined number of colors. Our approach leverages a deep neural architecture designed to identify key semantic regions and assign optimal colors, effectively capturing the essential structure and content of the original image within a simplified chromatic scheme. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms traditional color quantization and clustering algorithms in preserving image contours, object boundaries, and perceptual quality, especially with very few colors. Additionally, the framework exhibits strong generalization across diverse datasets and enables user-guided adjustments for artistic or application-specific requirements. Our findings highlight the potential of intelligent color structuring for resource-constrained environments, style transfer, and efficient visual communication. Keywords: image structuring, color quantization, deep learning, semantic segmentation, few-shot learning, image compression, non-photorealistic rendering.
