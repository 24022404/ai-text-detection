Graph classification is a fundamental task in machine learning with applications in social network analysis, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems. Despite the complexity of existing methods, there is a need for simple yet effective baseline algorithms to benchmark performance and provide foundational insights. This paper introduces a straightforward and computationally efficient algorithm for graph classification, leveraging basic graph properties and feature aggregation techniques. The proposed approach extracts key structural and statistical features from graphs, such as node degrees, clustering coefficients, and centrality measures, and employs a lightweight classifier for prediction. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the algorithm achieves competitive performance compared to more complex models, while significantly reducing computational overhead. The results highlight the potential of simple baselines in graph classification, offering a practical starting point for further research and development. This work contributes to the field by providing a transparent, interpretable, and scalable solution, which can serve as a reference for evaluating advanced graph learning techniques.  

**Keywords**: graph classification, baseline algorithm, feature aggregation, computational efficiency, interpretability.
