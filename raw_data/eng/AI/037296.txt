This paper addresses the challenge of initializing and calibrating monocular visual-inertial odometry systems, which are crucial for various applications such as robotics, augmented reality, and autonomous vehicles. The objective is to develop a robust and efficient method for online initialization and extrinsic spatial-temporal calibration of these systems. Our approach combines computer vision and inertial measurement unit (IMU) data to achieve accurate and reliable state estimation. We propose a novel algorithm that leverages the complementary strengths of visual and inertial cues to initialize the system and calibrate the spatial-temporal parameters. The results show that our method outperforms existing approaches in terms of accuracy, robustness, and computational efficiency. Key findings include the ability to handle various types of motion and scenes, as well as the capability to adapt to changing environmental conditions. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to provide a reliable and efficient solution for monocular visual-inertial odometry, with potential applications in fields such as robotics, computer vision, and autonomous systems. Relevant keywords include: visual-inertial odometry, monocular vision, IMU calibration, spatial-temporal calibration, computer vision, robotics, and autonomous systems.
