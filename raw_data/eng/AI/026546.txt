Remote sensing data provides a wealth of information for applications such as environmental monitoring, agriculture, and urban planning. However, the lack of labeled datasets often limits the effectiveness of supervised learning approaches. This paper introduces *Seasonal Contrast*, an unsupervised pre-training framework designed to leverage uncurated remote sensing data by exploiting the inherent temporal variations in seasonal changes. The proposed method utilizes a contrastive learning approach to learn meaningful representations from multi-temporal satellite imagery without the need for manual annotations. By aligning features across different seasons and contrasting them with unrelated samples, the model captures discriminative patterns that generalize well to downstream tasks. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that *Seasonal Contrast* outperforms existing unsupervised methods in tasks such as land cover classification and object detection. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance while significantly reducing the dependency on labeled data. This work highlights the potential of leveraging temporal dynamics in remote sensing data for unsupervised representation learning, offering a scalable and cost-effective solution for real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: unsupervised learning, remote sensing, contrastive learning, seasonal variation, representation learning, satellite imagery.
