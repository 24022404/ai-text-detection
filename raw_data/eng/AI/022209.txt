Image captioning, the task of generating descriptive text for images, has seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning techniques. However, existing models often struggle to align textual descriptions with specific visual details, leading to generic or inaccurate captions. This paper proposes a novel **Text-guided Attention Model (TAM)** for image captioning, which leverages textual guidance to enhance the alignment between visual and linguistic features. The model employs a dual-attention mechanism, where one stream focuses on visual features while the other dynamically adjusts attention based on the generated text, ensuring more contextually relevant captions. We evaluate TAM on benchmark datasets such as MS COCO and Flickr30k, demonstrating its superior performance over state-of-the-art methods in terms of both accuracy and diversity of generated captions. Experimental results show improvements in BLEU, METEOR, and CIDEr scores, highlighting the model's ability to produce more precise and human-like descriptions. The proposed approach not only advances the field of image captioning but also has potential applications in assistive technologies, content generation, and multimedia retrieval. Key innovations include the integration of text-guided attention and the ability to dynamically refine visual focus during caption generation. Keywords: image captioning, text-guided attention, deep learning, visual-language alignment, dual-attention mechanism.
