Image inpainting, the task of filling in missing regions of an image, remains a challenging problem, particularly when dealing with complex structures and textures. This paper introduces CR-Fill, a novel generative image inpainting approach that leverages auxiliary contextual reconstruction to improve the coherence and realism of generated content. Our method employs a two-stage process: first, a coarse inpainting network predicts an initial filling; second, a refinement network reconstructs contextual patches surrounding the inpainted region, encouraging global consistency. By incorporating contextual reconstruction as an auxiliary task, the refinement network learns to better understand the surrounding image context, leading to more plausible and visually appealing inpainting results. We evaluate CR-Fill on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in image quality and structural integrity compared to state-of-the-art methods. Quantitative evaluations using metrics such as PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, along with qualitative assessments, confirm the superior performance of CR-Fill. Our approach demonstrates the effectiveness of auxiliary contextual reconstruction for enhancing generative image inpainting, offering a promising direction for future research. Keywords: Image Inpainting, Generative Models, Contextual Reconstruction, Deep Learning, Image Completion.
