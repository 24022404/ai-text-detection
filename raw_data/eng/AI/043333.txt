Autoregressive models are increasingly deployed in critical applications, making their robustness to adversarial attacks paramount. This paper investigates the vulnerability of autoregressive models to attacks that manipulate the environment, rather than directly perturbing the model’s input. We introduce a novel optimization framework to craft optimal environmental manipulations that maximize the prediction error of the autoregressive model. Our method leverages gradient-based techniques to identify subtle yet effective environmental changes that, when perceived by the model, lead to significant deviations from expected outputs. We evaluate our attack on various autoregressive models across different domains, including time series forecasting and signal processing. Results demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms baseline attacks, achieving a substantial increase in prediction error with minimal environmental perturbation. Furthermore, we analyze the transferability of the crafted attacks across different model architectures and datasets. These findings highlight a significant security vulnerability in autoregressive models and underscore the need for robust defense mechanisms against environmental manipulation. Keywords: Autoregressive Models, Adversarial Attack, Environmental Manipulation, Optimization, Time Series, Robustness.
