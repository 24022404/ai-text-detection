This paper presents a novel semi-automated statistical algorithm designed to efficiently separate objects within complex images. The objective is to address the challenges of object separation in computer vision, where traditional methods often struggle with accuracy and computational efficiency. Our approach combines machine learning techniques with statistical modeling to develop a robust and adaptable algorithm. The method utilizes a two-stage process, initially employing edge detection and region growing to identify potential object boundaries, followed by a statistical refinement step to optimize separation accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in object separation accuracy and reduced computational requirements compared to existing fully automated methods. The algorithm's performance is evaluated on a diverse set of images, showcasing its versatility and potential applications in fields such as image processing, robotics, and autonomous systems. Key contributions include the introduction of a semi-automated framework that balances accuracy and efficiency, making it suitable for real-world applications where manual intervention is feasible. Relevant keywords for this research include object separation, statistical algorithm, machine learning, image processing, and computer vision.
