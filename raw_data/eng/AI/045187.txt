Network anomaly detection is a critical challenge in cybersecurity, requiring robust and interpretable methods to identify malicious activities. This paper introduces GEE (Gradient-based Explainable Variational Autoencoder), a novel framework designed to enhance anomaly detection while providing interpretable insights into detected anomalies. GEE leverages a gradient-based approach within a variational autoencoder (VAE) architecture to improve detection accuracy and generate explainable outputs. The proposed method integrates gradient analysis with latent space representations, enabling the identification of anomalous patterns and their underlying causes. Experiments conducted on benchmark network datasets demonstrate that GEE outperforms traditional anomaly detection methods in terms of precision, recall, and F1-score. Furthermore, the explainability component of GEE offers actionable insights, making it a valuable tool for cybersecurity analysts. The results highlight the potential of GEE to advance anomaly detection systems by combining high performance with interpretability, paving the way for more transparent and reliable cybersecurity solutions.  

**Keywords**: anomaly detection, variational autoencoder, explainable AI, gradient analysis, cybersecurity, network security.
