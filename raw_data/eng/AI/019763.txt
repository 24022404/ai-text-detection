Semantic segmentation is a critical task in computer vision, enabling precise pixel-level understanding of images. However, accurately delineating object boundaries remains a significant challenge due to the inherent complexity of edge detection and the loss of fine-grained details in traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs). This paper introduces **Boundary Neural Fields (BNF)**, a novel framework designed to enhance semantic segmentation by explicitly modeling object boundaries. BNF integrates a boundary-aware module into the segmentation pipeline, leveraging both local and global contextual information to refine edge predictions. The proposed approach employs a dual-stream architecture, where one stream focuses on semantic feature extraction while the other specializes in boundary detection, ensuring high precision in object delineation. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including Cityscapes and PASCAL VOC, demonstrate that BNF achieves state-of-the-art performance, with significant improvements in boundary accuracy and overall segmentation quality. The results highlight the effectiveness of the boundary-aware approach, particularly in complex scenes with overlapping objects and fine structures. This work contributes to advancing semantic segmentation by addressing a key limitation in existing methods, offering potential applications in autonomous driving, medical imaging, and scene understanding. Key innovations include the integration of boundary-specific learning and the development of a unified framework for joint semantic and boundary optimization. Keywords: semantic segmentation, boundary detection, convolutional neural networks, computer vision, Boundary Neural Fields.
