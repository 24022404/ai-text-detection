Abstract:  
Copy-move image forgery is a prevalent technique in digital manipulation where a segment of an image is duplicated and inserted elsewhere within the same image to conceal or fabricate information. The reliable detection of such forgeries is critical for ensuring the integrity of digital visual content. This paper introduces a novel approach to copy-move forgery detection based on evolving circular domains coverage. The proposed method partitions the image into overlapping circular domains that dynamically evolve to more accurately capture structural redundancies resulting from copy-move operations. Feature extraction is performed within these adaptive circular regions, enhancing sensitivity to both small and irregularly shaped copied areas. The approach leverages advanced similarity metrics and robust matching algorithms to discriminate between genuine duplications and similar background patterns. Extensive experiments on standard benchmarking datasets demonstrate the method’s effectiveness, achieving superior detection accuracy and robustness compared to existing block-based and keypoint-based techniques. The results show improved performance, particularly in challenging scenarios involving complex textures, post-processing operations such as rotation or scaling, and low-contrast forgeries. This research contributes a flexible and efficient framework for tamper detection in digital images, offering potential applications in digital forensics, content authentication, and media security.  
Keywords: Image Forgery Detection, Copy-Move Forgery, Circular Domains, Digital Image Forensics, Tampering Localization, Image Authentication
