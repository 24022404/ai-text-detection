This paper presents a comprehensive benchmarking study on word image recognition, addressing the need for standardized evaluation metrics and performance comparisons across diverse datasets. The objective is to assess the effectiveness of state-of-the-art recognition models in handling variations in font styles, resolutions, and noise levels commonly encountered in real-world scenarios. We evaluate several deep learning-based approaches, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformer-based architectures, on widely used word image datasets such as IIIT5K, SVT, and ICDAR. Our experiments highlight the strengths and limitations of each model, with a focus on accuracy, robustness, and computational efficiency. Results demonstrate that transformer-based models achieve superior performance in handling complex text variations, while CNNs remain competitive in scenarios with limited computational resources. The study also identifies key challenges, such as low-resolution images and irregular fonts, that require further research. This work contributes a systematic framework for benchmarking word image recognition systems, providing valuable insights for researchers and practitioners aiming to improve text recognition technologies in applications like document digitization and optical character recognition (OCR).  

**Keywords**: word image recognition, benchmarking, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, transformers, OCR, text recognition.
