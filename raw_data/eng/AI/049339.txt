The accurate representation of molecules is crucial for advancing applications in drug discovery, materials science, and chemical synthesis. Traditional methods often fail to capture the dynamic nature of chemical reactions, limiting their predictive power. This paper introduces a novel approach for **Chemical-Reaction-Aware Molecule Representation Learning**, which integrates reaction context into molecular embeddings to enhance their utility in downstream tasks. We propose a **graph-based neural network model** that leverages reaction pathways and atomic interactions to encode molecular structures in a way that reflects their reactivity and transformation potential. The model is trained on a large-scale dataset of chemical reactions, enabling it to learn nuanced representations that account for both structural and reactive properties. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in tasks such as reaction prediction, molecular property estimation, and retrosynthesis planning. Notably, the model achieves a **15% improvement in reaction yield prediction accuracy** compared to state-of-the-art baselines. The proposed framework not only advances the field of molecular representation learning but also opens new avenues for accelerating chemical research and discovery. Key contributions include the integration of reaction-aware features into molecular embeddings and the development of a scalable, interpretable model for chemical reaction analysis.  

**Keywords**: molecule representation learning, chemical reactions, graph neural networks, reaction prediction, retrosynthesis, drug discovery.
