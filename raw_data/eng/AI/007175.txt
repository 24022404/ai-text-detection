This paper introduces a novel obstacle detection and avoidance system for night vision applications, leveraging the unique capabilities of bio-inspired vision sensors. Addressing the limitations of traditional cameras in low-light environments, our approach utilizes a silicon retina sensor, mimicking the function of biological retinas, to enhance dynamic range and temporal resolution. We present a processing pipeline that combines the sensor's asynchronous output with event-based algorithms for efficient obstacle detection. The system's performance was evaluated through real-world experiments in diverse nocturnal environments, demonstrating a significant improvement in detection range and response time compared to conventional image-based methods. The bio-inspired approach enables robust obstacle avoidance for autonomous navigation in challenging low-light conditions, offering potential benefits for robotics, surveillance, and automotive safety. Key keywords: bio-inspired vision, event cameras, obstacle detection, night vision, autonomous navigation, silicon retina.
