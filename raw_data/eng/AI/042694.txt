**Abstract:**  
Sparse neural network training seeks to reduce model complexity and resource consumption while maintaining high performance. Traditional pruning techniques often lead to irrecoverable loss of important weights, limiting model adaptability and efficacy. This paper proposes a novel approach called Boosting Pruning Plasticity with Neuroregeneration, which introduces a neuroregenerative mechanism inspired by biological neural systems to enhance the plasticity of sparsely trained models. Our method integrates dynamic sparse training with periodic regrowth of pruned connections, allowing the model to adaptively recover and reallocate capacity to important features during learning. Experimental results across multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach not only achieves superior accuracy compared to conventional sparse training and static pruning methods but also improves training stability and convergence rates. Moreover, our neuroregeneration strategy leads to more robust generalization in low-resource settings and maintains efficiency in terms of memory footprint and computational costs. These findings indicate that enhancing network plasticity through controlled regeneration can significantly benefit sparse model optimization, offering promising avenues for applications in edge devices and resource-constrained environments.  
**Keywords:** sparse training, pruning, neural network plasticity, neuroregeneration, adaptive optimization, efficient deep learning.
