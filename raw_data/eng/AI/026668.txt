The growing need for accurate and efficient time series classification algorithms in various domains such as finance, healthcare, and environmental monitoring has led to a demand for advanced methods capable of handling diverse data patterns. This paper introduces the Canonical Interval Forest (CIF) classifier, an innovative approach aimed at improving classification performance and computational efficiency in the analysis of time series data.

Methods/Approach: The CIF classifier leverages the concept of interval-based analysis, where key features are extracted from subsegments of time series data. By constructing a forest of decision trees that analyze these intervals, the CIF effectively captures both local and global data patterns. The model utilizes novel feature selection and ensemble learning techniques to enhance its predictive accuracy while maintaining a balanced computational load.

Results/Findings: Empirical evaluation of the CIF classifier on a diverse set of benchmark time series datasets demonstrates its superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. The results highlight the model's ability to achieve higher classification accuracy and robustness, with significant improvements in speed and scalability observed across varying dataset sizes and complexities.

Conclusion/Implications: The CIF classifier represents a substantial contribution to the field of time series analysis, offering a novel tool that combines accuracy with efficiency. Its scalable and adaptable framework makes it suitable for applications in sectors where rapid and precise time series classification is critical. The advancements embodied in CIF open new avenues for research and application in real-time data processing and decision-making systems.

Keywords: time series classification, Canonical Interval Forest, interval-based analysis, decision trees, ensemble learning, feature selection.
