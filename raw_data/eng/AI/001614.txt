This paper aims to investigate the problem of estimating the effects of continuous-valued interventions, a crucial task in causal inference and decision-making. We propose a novel approach using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to model the complex relationships between interventions and outcomes. Our method, dubbed GAN-CE, leverages the adversarial training process to learn a robust and generalizable representation of the intervention-outcome space. We evaluate the performance of GAN-CE on synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its ability to accurately estimate the effects of continuous-valued interventions and outperform existing methods. The key findings of this research highlight the potential of GANs in causal inference, particularly in scenarios where traditional methods struggle with complex and high-dimensional data. Our contributions include the development of a flexible and scalable framework for estimating intervention effects, which can be applied to various domains, such as healthcare, economics, and social sciences. The proposed approach has significant implications for decision-making and policy evaluation, enabling practitioners to better understand the consequences of continuous-valued interventions. Key keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, causal inference, continuous-valued interventions, intervention effects, decision-making.
