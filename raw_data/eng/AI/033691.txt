Inferring graph topology from observed data is a fundamental problem in various scientific domains. This paper addresses the critical need for standardized benchmarks to evaluate and compare machine learning methods developed for graph topology inference. We introduce a novel suite of synthetic and real-world benchmark datasets, encompassing diverse graph structures and data modalities, designed to rigorously assess the performance of existing and future algorithms. Our benchmarks incorporate graph generation models with varying characteristics, including scale-free, small-world, and random graph topologies, coupled with node feature generation processes mimicking real-world data. We evaluate a range of state-of-the-art machine learning techniques, including both unsupervised and supervised approaches, on these benchmarks, providing a comprehensive comparative analysis. Our results highlight the strengths and weaknesses of different methods across various graph characteristics, revealing crucial insights into their generalization capabilities and computational efficiency. We publicly release the benchmark datasets and evaluation code to facilitate future research and promote the development of more robust and reliable graph topology inference algorithms. This work contributes a valuable resource to the machine learning community, enabling standardized evaluation and accelerating progress in graph-based data analysis.
Keywords: Graph topology inference, benchmarks, machine learning, graph neural networks, evaluation, graph structure learning.
