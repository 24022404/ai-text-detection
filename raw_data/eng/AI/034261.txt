This paper investigates the problem of enabling multiple agents to learn cooperative behaviors within complex environments using policy search methods. The primary objective is to develop a framework in which agents, operating with limited information, autonomously discover and refine strategies that maximize joint performance. We propose a novel multi-agent policy search algorithm that leverages gradient-based optimization to facilitate real-time cooperation among agents. Our approach incorporates decentralized learning, allowing each agent to update its policy based on local observations and occasional communication, balancing exploration and coordination effectively. We evaluate our method on benchmark multi-agent environments, including cooperative navigation and resource allocation tasks, and demonstrate significant improvements in cumulative team rewards and learning stability compared to traditional reinforcement learning and independent policy search baselines. The results indicate enhanced scalability and faster convergence to optimal cooperative strategies. Our findings emphasize the potential of policy search techniques for advancing multi-agent reinforcement learning, with implications for robotics, distributed control, and autonomous systems. Keywords: multi-agent systems, policy search, cooperative learning, reinforcement learning, decentralized optimization.
