This paper explores the application of persistent homology, a technique from topological data analysis, to characterize medical images for lung and brain cancer diagnosis. We propose a novel approach to extract topological features from computed tomography (CT) scans of lung tumors and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans of brain tumors, representing image structures using persistent homology. These topological features capture essential information about the shape and connectivity of tumor regions. We applied our method to two case studies: differentiating between benign and malignant lung nodules, and classifying different grades of brain tumors. Results demonstrate that persistent homology-based features can achieve high accuracy in distinguishing between these categories, offering a complementary approach to traditional image analysis techniques. This research highlights the potential of topological data analysis to provide robust and informative features for medical image analysis, aiding in early and accurate cancer diagnosis.
Keywords: Persistent Homology, Topological Data Analysis, Medical Imaging, Lung Cancer, Brain Cancer, Image Analysis, Feature Extraction.
