This paper introduces **CORE (Color Regression)**, a novel approach for accurately predicting and regressing multiple colors in fashion garments. The objective of this research is to address the challenge of color variability in fashion datasets, where garments often exhibit complex color combinations that are difficult to model using traditional methods. To achieve this, we propose a deep learning-based framework that leverages convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and regression techniques to predict color distributions across multiple garment regions. The model is trained on a diverse dataset of fashion images, annotated with precise color labels, and incorporates a multi-task learning strategy to handle the variability in color patterns. Experimental results demonstrate that CORE outperforms existing methods in color prediction accuracy, achieving a 15% improvement in mean absolute error (MAE) on benchmark datasets. Furthermore, the framework is robust to variations in lighting, texture, and garment design, making it suitable for real-world applications in e-commerce, virtual try-on systems, and fashion recommendation engines. The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to handle multiple colors simultaneously, providing a scalable solution for color analysis in fashion. This research has significant implications for enhancing user experience in online shopping and advancing the automation of fashion-related tasks.  

**Keywords**: Color regression, fashion garments, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, multi-task learning, e-commerce, virtual try-on.
