This paper addresses the challenge of learning linear non-Gaussian graphical models with multidirected edges, a problem that remains underexplored in the field of causal inference and graphical modeling. Traditional approaches often assume Gaussianity or restrict edges to single directions, limiting their applicability to real-world scenarios where complex dependencies and non-Gaussian distributions are prevalent. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel framework that integrates linear structural equation modeling with non-Gaussian noise assumptions, enabling the identification of multidirected edges in directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). Our method leverages independent component analysis (ICA) to estimate the underlying causal structure, combined with a robust optimization algorithm to handle the increased complexity introduced by multidirected edges. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and scalability, particularly in scenarios with high-dimensional data and non-linear dependencies. The proposed framework not only advances the theoretical understanding of non-Gaussian graphical models but also provides practical tools for applications in fields such as bioinformatics, finance, and social network analysis. This work highlights the importance of relaxing traditional assumptions in graphical modeling and opens new avenues for research in causal discovery.  

**Keywords**: Linear non-Gaussian models, multidirected edges, causal inference, structural equation modeling, independent component analysis, directed acyclic graphs.
