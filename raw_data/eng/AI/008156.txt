The study aims to address the challenge of accurate and efficient leaf recognition, a crucial task in botanical sciences, by leveraging the potential of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for feature extraction to improve classification performance.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel methodology utilizing convolutional neural networks to automatically extract discriminative features from leaf images for classification purposes. The approach involves training CNN models on a large dataset of leaf images, enabling the model to learn intricate patterns and morphological details characteristic of different leaf species. The extracted features are then fed into a classification algorithm to perform species identification.

Results/Findings: Our experimental evaluation demonstrates that the CNN-based feature extraction significantly outperforms traditional hand-crafted feature methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The proposed system achieved a high recognition rate across various leaf types, demonstrating robustness against variations in lighting, orientation, and scaling. Comparative studies with existing techniques show a notable improvement in classification accuracy, confirming the efficacy of the convolutional feature extraction approach.

Conclusion/Implications: The research contributes a powerful and automated solution for leaf recognition using CNN-derived features, offering a significant advancement over traditional methods that rely on manual feature engineering. The implications of this work extend to enhanced botanical research, biodiversity monitoring, and ecological studies, facilitating rapid and accurate plant species identification. Future work will explore scaling the model to accommodate an even broader range of plant species and improving real-time processing capabilities.

Keywords: leaf recognition, convolutional neural networks, CNN, feature extraction, botanical sciences, plant species identification.
