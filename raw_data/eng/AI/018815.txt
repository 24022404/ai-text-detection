Pedestrian detection in thermal imagery is a critical task for applications such as surveillance, autonomous driving, and nighttime safety systems. However, the scarcity of annotated thermal datasets and the challenges of varying environmental conditions often hinder the performance of detection models. This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a novel approach that leverages synthesized thermal images to enhance the robustness of pedestrian detection systems. We introduce a generative adversarial network (GAN)-based framework to generate realistic thermal images from visible-light datasets, effectively augmenting the training data. The synthesized images are then used to train a deep learning-based detection model, which incorporates a multi-scale feature fusion mechanism to improve detection accuracy under diverse conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms traditional methods, achieving a 12% improvement in detection precision and a 15% reduction in false positives on benchmark datasets. Furthermore, the model exhibits strong generalization capabilities across different thermal imaging devices and environmental settings. This research contributes to advancing thermal-based pedestrian detection by providing a scalable solution for dataset augmentation and robust model training, with potential applications in safety-critical systems.  

**Keywords**: pedestrian detection, thermal imagery, image synthesis, GAN, deep learning, multi-scale feature fusion, robustness.
