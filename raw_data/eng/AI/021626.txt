Policy optimization is a critical component of reinforcement learning (RL), where the goal is to maximize cumulative rewards by iteratively improving decision-making policies. However, the role of entropy in policy optimization remains underexplored, particularly in balancing exploration and exploitation. This study investigates the impact of entropy regularization on the stability, convergence, and performance of policy optimization algorithms. We propose a novel framework that systematically analyzes the effects of entropy at different stages of training, leveraging both theoretical insights and empirical evaluations. Our approach integrates entropy-based adjustments into state-of-the-art RL algorithms, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), to evaluate their robustness across diverse environments. Experimental results demonstrate that entropy regularization significantly enhances exploration in sparse-reward settings, leading to faster convergence and improved policy performance. Furthermore, we identify optimal entropy levels that mitigate premature convergence to suboptimal policies while maintaining computational efficiency. This research provides actionable insights for designing more effective RL algorithms, particularly in complex, real-world applications where exploration is critical. The findings underscore the importance of entropy as a tunable parameter in policy optimization, offering a pathway to more adaptive and scalable RL systems.  

**Keywords**: reinforcement learning, policy optimization, entropy regularization, exploration-exploitation tradeoff, Proximal Policy Optimization, Soft Actor-Critic.
