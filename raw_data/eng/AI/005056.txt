Abstract:

Medical image analysis plays a critical role in the diagnosis and management of cancer, yet traditional feature extraction techniques often fail to capture the complex morphological variations present in tumor structures. This paper explores the application of persistent homology, a method from topological data analysis, to extract and quantify robust topological features from medical images, with a focus on lung and brain cancer case studies. We designed a computational framework that computes persistence diagrams from imaging data, capturing multiscale topological signatures that reflect the underlying structure of tumors. Using publicly available MRI and CT scan datasets for lung and brain cancers, we evaluated the discriminative power of these topological features in classifying malignancy and predicting clinical outcomes. Our approach was compared against conventional radiomics and machine learning-based image characterization methods. The experimental results demonstrated that persistent homology features provide complementary and, in some cases, superior performance in differentiating between cancer types and grades, as well as in prognostic modeling. The findings indicate that topological features can capture morphological nuances not easily accessible with existing techniques, offering a promising direction for improving automated medical image analysis. This study underscores the potential of persistent homology as an innovative tool in computational oncology, with implications for early detection, diagnosis, and personalized treatment planning. 

Keywords: persistent homology, topological data analysis, medical image analysis, lung cancer, brain cancer, cancer classification, radiomics, machine learning
