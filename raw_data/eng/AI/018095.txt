Communication bottlenecks often impede the scalability of distributed learning algorithms. Error feedback (EF) is a popular technique for reducing communication costs by compressing gradients, but it can suffer from slow convergence or instability. This paper introduces a novel gradient compression method, termed Variance-Reduced Error Feedback (VREF), designed to overcome these limitations. VREF incorporates a variance reduction mechanism into the EF framework, effectively mitigating the error accumulation that hinders convergence. We provide theoretical convergence guarantees for VREF under various settings, demonstrating its superiority over standard EF. Empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets and models, including image classification tasks with ResNet architectures, show that VREF achieves significantly faster convergence rates and higher accuracy compared to state-of-the-art EF variants. Furthermore, VREF exhibits robustness to heterogeneous data distributions and is readily applicable to diverse distributed learning scenarios. These results highlight VREF as a promising alternative to existing gradient compression techniques, offering improved communication efficiency and convergence performance in distributed learning.

Keywords: Distributed Learning, Error Feedback, Gradient Compression, Variance Reduction, Communication Efficiency, Convergence Analysis.
