In the realm of graphical models, understanding complex relationships in multivariate data presents a significant challenge, particularly when dealing with non-Gaussian distributions, which are common in real-world datasets. This study seeks to develop a framework for learning linear non-Gaussian graphical models that incorporate multidirected edges, allowing for more nuanced representation of dependencies in data that traditional models with unidirectional connections may not capture.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel algorithm that leverages techniques from causal inference and graph theory to learn the structure of graphical models characterized by multidirected edges. Utilizing a combination of Independent Component Analysis (ICA) and customized constraint-based methods, the proposed model is equipped to detect and represent linear dependencies in non-Gaussian contexts. 

Results/Findings: Our approach demonstrates superior performance in capturing the underlying structure of data compared to traditional graphical models. Through a series of experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets, we validate the capability of the proposed model to accurately learn graphical structures with multidirected edges, showcasing its resilience to variations in data distribution shapes. Furthermore, performance benchmarks reveal that our model outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and scalability.

Conclusion/Implications: This research introduces a robust framework for learning linear non-Gaussian graphical models, expanding the potential for more comprehensive data analysis in fields such as economics, biology, and network science. By incorporating multidirected edges, our model provides a significant advancement in the capability to model complex dependencies, offering valuable insights and applications in the interpretation of non-conventional data patterns. Key Keywords: graphical models, non-Gaussian distributions, multidirected edges, causal inference, Independent Component Analysis (ICA).
