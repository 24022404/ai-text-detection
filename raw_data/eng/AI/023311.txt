Invariant networks are designed to produce consistent outputs despite transformations in the input data, a property crucial for various applications including image recognition and robotics. This paper investigates the universality of invariant networks, exploring the theoretical limits of their representational power. We present a novel framework for analyzing the ability of these networks to approximate any continuous invariant function. Our approach leverages tools from group representation theory and harmonic analysis to characterize the function space spanned by invariant network architectures. We demonstrate that under certain conditions, invariant networks are universal approximators for invariant functions, meaning they can approximate any continuous invariant function to arbitrary accuracy. Furthermore, we identify specific architectural constraints that are necessary to achieve universality. Experimental results on benchmark datasets corroborate our theoretical findings, showing that networks satisfying these constraints exhibit superior performance compared to standard invariant architectures. These results contribute to a deeper understanding of the capabilities and limitations of invariant networks, offering guidelines for designing more effective and theoretically sound architectures. Keywords: Invariant Networks, Universality, Group Representation Theory, Neural Networks, Machine Learning, Invariance, Equivariance.
