This paper presents a novel variational approach for learning Markov processes from time series data, addressing the challenge of accurately modeling complex stochastic systems. The proposed method leverages variational inference to approximate the underlying transition dynamics of Markov processes, enabling efficient learning even with limited or noisy data. By formulating the problem within a probabilistic framework, our approach optimizes a variational lower bound to infer the parameters of the Markov model, ensuring both computational efficiency and scalability. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms traditional techniques in terms of accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with sparse observations. Furthermore, the approach provides interpretable insights into the learned transition dynamics, making it valuable for applications in fields such as finance, biology, and robotics. The key contribution of this work lies in its ability to combine the flexibility of variational methods with the theoretical rigor of Markov processes, offering a powerful tool for time series analysis and prediction. Keywords: Markov processes, variational inference, time series analysis, probabilistic modeling, stochastic systems.
