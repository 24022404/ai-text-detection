This paper addresses the challenge of reconstructing high-fidelity 3D scenes from multi-view polarimetric images, a task critical for applications in computer vision, robotics, and augmented reality. Traditional inverse rendering methods often struggle with accurately recovering surface properties, such as reflectance and geometry, under complex lighting conditions. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel polarimetric multi-view inverse rendering framework that leverages polarization cues to enhance the estimation of surface normals, material properties, and lighting. Our approach integrates polarimetric imaging with a differentiable rendering pipeline, enabling the joint optimization of scene parameters through a physics-based model. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method significantly improves the accuracy of surface normal estimation and material decomposition compared to state-of-the-art techniques. Furthermore, the framework exhibits robustness in handling challenging scenarios, such as specular surfaces and low-texture regions. The contributions of this work include a new polarimetric-aware rendering model and a scalable optimization strategy, paving the way for more reliable 3D scene reconstruction in real-world environments. This research has broad implications for applications in autonomous navigation, virtual reality, and digital content creation.  

**Keywords**: polarimetric imaging, inverse rendering, 3D reconstruction, surface normals, material decomposition, differentiable rendering.
