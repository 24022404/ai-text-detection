Canonical Autocorrelation Analysis (CAA) is a novel statistical method designed to uncover latent relationships between two sets of time-series data by leveraging their autocorrelation structures. This paper introduces CAA as an extension of traditional canonical correlation analysis, specifically tailored for temporal data where dependencies over time are critical. The proposed approach employs a joint optimization framework to identify canonical components that maximize the correlation between the datasets while preserving their inherent temporal dynamics. We evaluate CAA using synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its superior performance in capturing time-dependent patterns compared to existing methods. Experimental results reveal that CAA achieves higher interpretability and predictive accuracy in applications such as financial forecasting and climate modeling. The method’s ability to integrate temporal dependencies offers significant potential for advancing research in fields requiring time-series analysis, including economics, environmental science, and signal processing. This work contributes a robust, scalable tool for analyzing complex temporal relationships, paving the way for future innovations in multivariate time-series analysis.  

**Keywords**: Canonical Autocorrelation Analysis, time-series analysis, temporal dependencies, multivariate data, optimization framework, predictive modeling.
