This paper presents a novel approach to understanding visual relations in images using explanation-based weakly-supervised learning. The motivation is to enhance machine learning models' ability to interpret and reason about complex visual scenes without relying on extensive annotated data.

Methods/Approach: The proposed method utilizes graph networks to model visual relations effectively. We introduce a unique framework that leverages weak supervision from explanations, allowing the model to learn rich relational representations. The graph network captures interactions between object pairs, facilitating a deeper understanding of scene dynamics. Key model components include node and edge feature extraction and a reasoner module driven by explanations to guide learning.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate significant improvements in visual relation detection tasks compared to traditional supervised models. The approach achieves enhanced generalization with fewer labeled examples, outperforming baseline methods across multiple benchmark datasets. The graph network's ability to leverage explanations results in more accurate and interpretable visual relation predictions.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes to the field of visual understanding by introducing an innovative method for learning visual relations with limited supervision. The combination of explanation-based learning with graph networks not only advances the capabilities of relation detection systems but also opens new pathways for integrating human-like reasoning in machine learning models. Potential applications span from image captioning and scene understanding to robotics and AI-driven surveillance systems.

Keywords: visual relations, graph networks, weakly-supervised learning, explanation-based learning, visual understanding, machine learning.
