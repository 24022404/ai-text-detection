Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have demonstrated remarkable success in generating realistic data. However, training GANs remains challenging due to issues like mode collapse and instability. This paper introduces a novel reparameterized sampling technique to improve GAN training. Our approach involves reparameterizing the noise vector sampled from the latent space, guiding it towards regions that enhance generator diversity and stability. Specifically, we employ a learned transformation that adapts the noise distribution based on the discriminator's feedback, effectively smoothing the generator's learning landscape. We evaluate our method on several benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10, and CelebA, demonstrating significant improvements in terms of Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception Score compared to traditional GAN training and other regularization techniques. The results indicate that reparameterized sampling leads to more stable training dynamics, reduced mode collapse, and ultimately, the generation of higher quality and more diverse samples. This work offers a new perspective on GAN training by directly manipulating the latent space exploration, paving the way for more robust and reliable generative models.

Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, Reparameterization, Sampling, Mode Collapse, Deep Learning.
