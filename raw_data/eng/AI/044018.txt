This paper explores the application of mutual information to enhance the explainability of deep learning models in multiscale systems. The objective is to develop a framework that provides insights into the complex interactions within these systems, enabling a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms. A novel approach is proposed, leveraging mutual information to identify and quantify the relationships between variables at different scales. The methodology involves the use of deep learning architectures, such as neural networks, to learn representations of the system that maximize mutual information. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach in capturing key patterns and relationships in multiscale systems, outperforming traditional methods in terms of accuracy and interpretability. The findings have significant implications for the development of explainable AI models, particularly in fields such as physics, biology, and engineering, where multiscale systems are prevalent. Key contributions include the introduction of a mutual information-based framework for explainable deep learning, and the demonstration of its potential to uncover new insights into complex systems. Relevant keywords: mutual information, explainable AI, deep learning, multiscale systems, neural networks.
