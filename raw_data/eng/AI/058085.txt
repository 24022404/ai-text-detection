This paper presents a novel approach to deep unsupervised learning of 3D point clouds, leveraging graph topology inference and filtering to extract meaningful representations. The objective is to develop an effective method for processing and analyzing large-scale 3D point cloud data without relying on labeled datasets. Our approach employs a graph-based neural network architecture that infers the underlying topology of the point cloud and filters out noise and redundant information. The method utilizes a combination of graph convolutional layers and attention mechanisms to learn robust and informative features. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our approach in various applications, including 3D object recognition, scene understanding, and point cloud denoising. Compared to existing methods, our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance and shows significant improvements in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The proposed technique has important implications for numerous fields, including computer vision, robotics, and geographic information systems, and contributes to the development of more sophisticated 3D point cloud processing pipelines. Key contributions include the introduction of a graph topology inference module and a filtering mechanism, enabling the learning of high-quality 3D point cloud representations without supervision. Relevant keywords: 3D point clouds, graph neural networks, unsupervised learning, topology inference, filtering, computer vision, robotics.
