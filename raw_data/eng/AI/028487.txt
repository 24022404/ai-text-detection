Visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) has become a cornerstone technology in robotics and augmented reality, enabling real-time environment mapping and self-localization using visual data. However, the use of camera-based SLAM systems raises significant privacy concerns, particularly when sensitive visual information is captured and processed. This paper presents a novel framework for privacy preserving visual SLAM that aims to secure user and bystander data without compromising the system’s localization and mapping accuracy. Our approach integrates on-device real-time semantic abstraction, visual data anonymization, and encrypted map representations to minimize exposure of personally identifiable information. We develop and evaluate several strategies, including face and license plate obfuscation, as well as selective feature extraction that limits mapping to non-sensitive regions. Experimental results conducted on publicly available datasets and real-world scenarios demonstrate that the proposed methods maintain high SLAM accuracy, with less than 5% deviation in trajectory estimation compared to standard SLAM pipelines, while significantly reducing the risk of privacy breaches. The framework is scalable, compatible with a range of visual SLAM algorithms, and suitable for deployment on mobile and edge devices. This work provides an effective solution for privacy-aware applications in smart environments, service robotics, and wearable augmented reality. Keywords: visual SLAM, privacy preservation, data anonymization, edge computing, secure mapping, computer vision, robotics.
