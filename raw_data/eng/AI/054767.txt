This paper introduces a novel approach to understanding and optimizing neural network architectures by explicitly learning their underlying connectivity patterns from a topological perspective. We propose a method that utilizes persistent homology, a technique from topological data analysis, to extract and analyze the essential structural features of neural networks during training. This allows us to quantify the evolution of network connectivity and identify critical connections that significantly impact performance. Our approach involves representing the network as a simplicial complex and tracking the birth and death of topological features (cycles) across different layers. By incorporating topological loss terms into the training objective, we guide the network to develop more robust and efficient connectivity structures. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves comparable or superior performance to standard training techniques while yielding networks with improved generalization and interpretability. Furthermore, the learned topological representations offer insights into the functional organization of neural networks, paving the way for automated architecture design and optimization. Keywords: Neural Networks, Topology, Persistent Homology, Connectivity, Machine Learning, Network Architecture.
