Image inpainting is a crucial task in computer vision that involves filling missing or damaged regions in images. The challenge lies in generating plausible and diverse structures that seamlessly blend with the existing visual content. This paper addresses this challenge by introducing a novel method for diverse structure generation using a Hierarchical Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE).

Methods/Approach: Our approach leverages a Hierarchical VQ-VAE model, which is specifically designed to learn a multi-scale representation of image structures. By employing a hierarchical structure, the model captures both global and local features, enabling it to generate coherent and contextually accurate inpainted regions. The VQ-VAE framework allows for quantization of latent spaces, facilitating efficient learning of diverse structural patterns. Furthermore, we introduce an innovative inference strategy that enhances the diversity of inpainted structures while maintaining quality and coherence.

Results/Findings: The proposed model demonstrates superior performance in generating diverse and realistic inpainting results when benchmarked against existing state-of-the-art methods. Quantitative evaluations highlight substantial improvements in both diversity and visual quality metrics. Qualitative assessments further confirm the model's ability to produce visually appealing inpainted images that blend seamlessly with the surrounding context.

Conclusion/Implications: The Hierarchical VQ-VAE model advances the field of image inpainting by addressing the limitations of prior methods in generating diverse structures. This approach not only enhances the aesthetic qualities of the inpainted images but also broadens the applicability of image inpainting in various domains, including digital restoration and content creation. Keywords: Image Inpainting, Hierarchical VQ-VAE, Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder, Computer Vision, Structural Diversity.
