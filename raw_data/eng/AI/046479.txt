Person image generation is a challenging task in computer vision, requiring the synthesis of realistic images of individuals in diverse poses while preserving identity and fine details. This paper introduces a novel framework, Progressive Pose Attention Transfer (PPAT), designed to address these challenges by leveraging pose-guided attention mechanisms. The proposed approach progressively refines the generation process by focusing on pose-specific features and spatial relationships, ensuring high-fidelity image synthesis. PPAT employs a multi-stage architecture that integrates pose estimation, attention-based feature alignment, and adversarial training to enhance the quality of generated images. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that PPAT outperforms existing methods in terms of visual quality, identity preservation, and pose accuracy. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance, with significant improvements in structural similarity and perceptual metrics. This research contributes to advancing person image generation by introducing a scalable and efficient solution with potential applications in virtual try-on, animation, and augmented reality.  

**Keywords**: Person image generation, pose transfer, attention mechanisms, adversarial training, computer vision.
