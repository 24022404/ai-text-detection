The increasing importance of graph-structured data in various domains has driven the development of Graph Contrastive Learning (GCL) techniques. This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the robustness of GCL methods to improve their generalization and reliability across diverse applications.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework that integrates adversarial training with graph contrastive learning to enhance robustness. Our method involves the development of a new loss function tailored to navigate adversarial perturbations, combined with a multi-view approach that captures diverse graph representations. We leverage Neural Network architectures known for their efficacy in graph analysis to refine contrastive learning processes.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing GCL models in terms of robustness and accuracy across several benchmark datasets. The proposed method achieves an improved resistance to adversarial attacks and demonstrates superior performance in semi-supervised node classification tasks when compared to traditional GCL methods.

Conclusion/Implications: Our research makes a substantial contribution to the field of Graph Contrastive Learning by introducing a robust framework capable of overcoming limitations posed by adversarial perturbations. Our approach not only enhances the stability and performance of GCL applications in real-world scenarios but also sets a new direction for future research in making machine learning models more resilient. Key applications include social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics, where robustness against noise and adversarial influences is critical.

Keywords: Graph Contrastive Learning, Robustness, Adversarial Training, Multi-view Learning, Neural Networks, Node Classification, Graph-Structured Data.
