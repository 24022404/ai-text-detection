This paper proposes a novel multi-scale video frame-synthesis network designed to generate high-quality intermediate frames in videos. The objective is to address the challenge of creating coherent and realistic frame sequences, which is crucial for various applications such as video interpolation, slow-motion video generation, and video editing. Our approach employs a deep neural network architecture that incorporates a transitive consistency loss function, ensuring temporal consistency across multiple scales. The network is trained on a large dataset of videos and learns to predict intermediate frames that are both spatially and temporally coherent. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing frame-synthesis techniques, achieving significant improvements in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM). The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a multi-scale framework and the transitive consistency loss function, which enable the generation of more realistic and coherent video frames. Our work has important implications for video processing and computer vision applications, and can be applied to areas such as video production, virtual reality, and autonomous systems. Key keywords: video frame synthesis, multi-scale networks, transitive consistency loss, deep learning, computer vision.
