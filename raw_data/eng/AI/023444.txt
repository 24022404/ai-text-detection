This paper addresses the challenge of moving object detection in event-based vision, a task crucial for applications such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and surveillance systems. Our objective is to develop an efficient and accurate method for detecting moving objects in scenes captured by event-based cameras, which output asynchronous streams of events representing changes in the visual scene. We propose a novel approach based on k-means clustering, which groups events into clusters that correspond to moving objects. Our method leverages the temporal and spatial characteristics of events to identify and separate moving objects from the background. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving high accuracy and robustness in detecting moving objects under various scenarios. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a clustering-based framework for event-based moving object detection and the demonstration of its superiority over existing methods. This work has significant implications for the development of event-based vision systems, enabling improved performance in applications such as object tracking, collision avoidance, and scene understanding. Key keywords: event-based vision, moving object detection, k-means clustering, robotics, autonomous vehicles, surveillance systems.
