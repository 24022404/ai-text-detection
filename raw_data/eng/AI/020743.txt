This paper addresses the challenge of policy discrepancy in Return-Based Deep Q-Networks (RB-DQN), a critical issue affecting the stability and convergence of reinforcement learning algorithms. We introduce a novel set of qualitative measurements designed to evaluate and characterize inconsistencies between target and behavior policies learned by RB-DQN. Our approach involves analyzing the distribution of Q-values, return estimations, and action selections to identify potential sources of divergence. Experiments conducted on benchmark Atari games reveal that significant policy discrepancies arise during training, particularly in environments with sparse rewards and high-dimensional state spaces. Furthermore, we demonstrate a correlation between these qualitative measurements and the overall performance of RB-DQN, highlighting the impact of policy consistency on learning outcomes. These measurements provide valuable insights for diagnosing and mitigating instability issues in RB-DQN and can inform the development of more robust and reliable reinforcement learning agents. Keywording: Reinforcement Learning, Deep Q-Network, Policy Discrepancy, Qualitative Measurement, Stability, Return-Based Methods.
