Object detection is a fundamental task in computer vision, traditionally approached through localization and classification pipelines using convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and complex post-processing techniques. This paper presents Pix2seq, a novel language modeling framework that formulates object detection as a sequence prediction problem. In Pix2seq, images are encoded into sequences, and object annotations—including locations and categories—are treated as tokens to be predicted by an autoregressive transformer-based model. This approach simplifies the object detection pipeline by unifying image understanding and prediction under a single sequence-to-sequence modeling architecture, removing the need for hand-crafted priors like anchor boxes or non-maximum suppression.

We evaluate Pix2seq on widely-used benchmarks such as COCO and Pascal VOC, demonstrating comparable or superior performance to traditional detection methods, while significantly reducing architectural complexity. Our experimental results highlight the model’s ability to generalize across diverse object categories and adapt to varying image resolutions. Furthermore, ablation studies show that Pix2seq achieves strong performance even with minimal post-processing.

The findings indicate that re-framing object detection as a language modeling task provides a flexible and extensible foundation for integrating vision and language in computer vision systems. This research contributes a new perspective to object detection, with implications for unified vision-language learning and downstream applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and digital content analysis.

Keywords: object detection, language modeling, Pix2seq, transformer, sequence prediction, computer vision, deep learning.
