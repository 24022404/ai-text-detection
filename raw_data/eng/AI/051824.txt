Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) have shown remarkable success in novel view synthesis. However, existing NeRF methods typically require controlled capture environments and accurate camera poses, limiting their applicability to casual, "in the wild" photo collections. This paper introduces a novel approach, "NeRF in the Wild," to address these limitations. Our method jointly optimizes the NeRF representation and refines camera poses directly from unconstrained image datasets, leveraging robust photometric error functions and a novel scene-adaptive regularizer to handle noisy and incomplete data. We introduce a learned prior based on a variational autoencoder (VAE) to encourage realistic and consistent geometry, especially in regions with sparse views. Experimental results on a variety of challenging real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly improves the quality and robustness of NeRF reconstructions compared to existing methods when applied to unconstrained photo collections. Key contributions include a robust framework for joint optimization of scene geometry and camera pose, a scene-adaptive regularizer, and a learned geometric prior for improved novel view synthesis. Keywords: Neural Radiance Fields, NeRF, Novel View Synthesis, 3D Reconstruction, Unconstrained Photo Collections, Camera Pose Estimation, Variational Autoencoder.
