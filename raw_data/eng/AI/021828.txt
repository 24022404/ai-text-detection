This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing teaching strategies by proposing a learner-aware framework that leverages inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) integrated with explicit modeling of learner preferences and constraints. Traditional IRL techniques typically focus on recovering reward functions from observed behaviors, but often neglect individual differences in learner goals, prior knowledge, and practical limitations such as time or cognitive resources. Our approach formulates teaching as a collaborative process, where the teacher adapts instructions and demonstrations by inferring not only the underlying reward structure but also the unique preferences and constraints of each learner. We introduce a novel algorithm that jointly estimates learner-specific reward functions and constraint sets from limited interaction data. Experimental results across synthetic and real-world teaching scenarios demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms standard IRL-based teaching paradigms, yielding faster and more effective learner adaptation. The findings highlight the importance of personalized teaching and provide a foundation for intelligent tutoring systems that dynamically tailor content based on inferred learner profiles. Keywords: inverse reinforcement learning, learner modeling, teaching strategies, personalized education, preference learning, constraint-aware AI.
