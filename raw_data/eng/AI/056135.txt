This paper investigates the problem of multi-armed bandits with feedback graphs and switching costs, a framework that extends traditional bandit problems by incorporating structured feedback and penalties for action changes. The objective is to develop efficient algorithms that minimize cumulative regret while accounting for the cost of switching between actions. We propose a novel algorithm that leverages the structure of the feedback graph to optimize decision-making under switching constraints. Our approach combines graph-based exploration with adaptive strategies to balance exploration-exploitation trade-offs and reduce unnecessary switches. Through theoretical analysis, we establish regret bounds that demonstrate the algorithm's efficiency, particularly in scenarios with sparse feedback graphs. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets validate the algorithm's performance, showing significant improvements over existing methods in terms of both regret and switching costs. The findings highlight the importance of incorporating feedback structures and switching penalties in bandit problems, offering practical insights for applications such as online recommendation systems, dynamic pricing, and resource allocation. This work contributes to the broader field of online learning by introducing a scalable and cost-aware solution for bandit problems with structured feedback.  

**Keywords**: Multi-armed bandits, feedback graphs, switching costs, online learning, regret minimization, adaptive algorithms.
