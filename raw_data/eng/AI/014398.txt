The primary objective of this study is to introduce and analyze HarperValleyBank, a domain-specific spoken dialog corpus designed to aid in the development and evaluation of conversational agents within the banking sector. The need for tailored dialog systems that cater to the intricacies of financial interactions has highlighted the importance of creating specialized datasets.

Methods/Approach: The HarperValleyBank corpus was constructed through a meticulous data collection process that involved authentic financial dialogs between customers and bank representatives. The dialogs were then annotated with a rich set of linguistic and domain-specific labels to facilitate various natural language processing tasks. Advanced methodologies were employed to ensure the diversity and representativeness of the data to mirror real-world banking conversations accurately.

Results/Findings: The curated corpus comprises a substantial volume of annotated dialogs, offering a valuable resource for training and testing conversational models. Initial evaluations of dialog systems using the HarperValleyBank corpus demonstrate improved performance in intent recognition and information retrieval tasks compared to models trained on generic datasets. The domain-specific nature of the corpus contributes to enhanced system accuracy and user satisfaction.

Conclusion/Implications: HarperValleyBank stands as a significant contribution to the field of dialog systems, providing a benchmark for financial-oriented conversational AI research. Its potential applications include the development of more responsive customer service chatbots and automated customer support solutions. This corpus paves the way for domain-specific advancements in spoken dialog systems, bridging the gap between general-purpose AI models and specialized financial communications. Keywords: spoken dialog systems, banking sector, domain-specific corpus, natural language processing, conversational agents.
