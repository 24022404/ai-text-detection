Video Object Segmentation (VOS) is a critical task in computer vision, with applications ranging from video editing to autonomous systems. However, achieving accurate and efficient segmentation in dynamic and complex video sequences remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces ALBA, a novel reinforcement learning (RL)-based framework designed to address the limitations of existing VOS methods. ALBA leverages a deep Q-network (DQN) architecture to learn optimal segmentation policies by interacting with video frames, enabling adaptive and context-aware object tracking. The proposed approach integrates spatial-temporal features and reward mechanisms to refine segmentation masks over time, ensuring robustness against occlusions and appearance changes.  

Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including DAVIS and YouTube-VOS, demonstrate that ALBA achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming traditional and deep learning-based methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The framework also exhibits superior generalization capabilities across diverse video scenarios. The results highlight the potential of RL in enhancing VOS by enabling dynamic decision-making and reducing dependency on large annotated datasets.  

In conclusion, ALBA represents a significant advancement in VOS by combining reinforcement learning with deep learning techniques, offering a scalable and adaptive solution for real-world applications. Its ability to handle complex video sequences with minimal supervision makes it a promising tool for future research and industry adoption.  

**Keywords**: Video Object Segmentation, Reinforcement Learning, Deep Q-Network, Spatial-Temporal Features, Adaptive Tracking, Computer Vision.
