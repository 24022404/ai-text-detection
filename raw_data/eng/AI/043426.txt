This paper introduces a novel unsupervised approach to solving inverse problems using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Addressing the challenge of requiring paired training data for traditional supervised methods, our approach leverages the generative capabilities of GANs to learn a prior distribution of solutions. Specifically, we train a generator network to map from a latent space to the solution space, and a discriminator network to distinguish between generated solutions and those consistent with the observed data. The inverse problem is then solved by finding the latent vector that produces a solution best fitting the observed data according to a defined consistency metric. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our method on several benchmark inverse problems, including image deblurring and compressive sensing, showing significant improvements in reconstruction accuracy compared to existing unsupervised techniques. Furthermore, we analyze the robustness of our approach to noisy measurements and variations in the forward model. This work offers a promising direction for solving inverse problems without relying on labeled data, opening up new possibilities for applications in various scientific and engineering domains. Keywords: Inverse problems, Generative Adversarial Networks, Unsupervised learning, Image reconstruction, GAN.
