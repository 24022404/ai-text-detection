Environmental sound classification (ESC) is a critical task in audio analysis, with applications ranging from smart environments to surveillance systems. This paper introduces ESResNet, a novel approach leveraging visual domain models for ESC. The primary objective is to bridge the gap between visual and auditory data processing by adapting convolutional neural networks (CNNs) traditionally used for image classification to the audio domain. We propose a method that transforms audio signals into visual representations, such as spectrograms, and employs a ResNet-based architecture optimized for sound classification. Our approach addresses the challenges of feature extraction and model generalization in ESC tasks. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that ESResNet achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and robustness. The model’s ability to generalize across diverse environmental sounds highlights its potential for real-world applications. This research contributes to the field by demonstrating the effectiveness of visual domain models for audio classification, offering a new perspective on cross-modal learning. Key innovations include the adaptation of ResNet architectures for spectrogram analysis and the introduction of a streamlined preprocessing pipeline for audio-to-visual transformation. The findings suggest that ESResNet can serve as a foundation for future advancements in ESC and related domains.  

**Keywords**: Environmental sound classification, ResNet, spectrogram, convolutional neural networks, cross-modal learning, audio analysis.
