Membership inference attacks (MIAs) pose a significant threat to the privacy of deep learning models by determining whether a specific data point was part of the training dataset. This paper investigates a novel class of attacks, termed Membership Model Inversion Attacks (MMIAs), which extend traditional MIAs by leveraging model inversion techniques to reconstruct sensitive training data. The objective of this research is to evaluate the vulnerability of deep networks to such attacks and propose mitigation strategies. We introduce a systematic approach combining gradient-based optimization and generative adversarial networks (GANs) to invert model outputs and infer membership status. Our experiments demonstrate that MMIAs achieve higher accuracy in identifying training data compared to conventional MIAs, particularly for models trained on high-dimensional datasets such as images and text. The findings reveal that overfitting and model complexity significantly increase susceptibility to MMIAs. To address these vulnerabilities, we propose a defense mechanism based on differential privacy and regularization techniques, which effectively reduces the success rate of MMIAs without compromising model performance. This study highlights the critical need for robust privacy-preserving measures in deep learning and provides actionable insights for securing models against advanced adversarial attacks.  

**Keywords**: Membership inference attacks, model inversion, deep learning, privacy, adversarial attacks, differential privacy.
