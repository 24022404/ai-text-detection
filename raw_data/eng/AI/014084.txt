**Abstract:**

The design and generation of novel molecules play a pivotal role in accelerating drug discovery and material science. This paper presents a deep generative model specifically tailored for fragment-based molecule generation, aiming to address challenges in producing chemically valid and diverse molecular structures. Our approach leverages an advanced neural architecture that synthesizes molecules by sequentially assembling predefined molecular fragments, achieving improved scalability over atom-based generation methods. The model employs a variational autoencoder framework to encode fragment-level representations and reconstruct complete molecules, ensuring both structural diversity and chemical plausibility. Experimental results on benchmark molecular datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing generative models in generating valid, unique, and novel molecules, with substantial improvements in scaffold diversity and synthesis-focused metrics. Additionally, we analyze the model’s interpretability and its capacity to guide fragment selection towards desired chemical properties. The proposed architecture provides a robust and extensible solution for fragment-based molecular design, offering significant potential to accelerate early-stage drug discovery and materials engineering. Keywords: deep generative model, fragment-based molecule generation, variational autoencoder, molecular design, drug discovery, deep learning.
