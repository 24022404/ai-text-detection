Video Story Question Answering (VSQA) is a challenging task that requires understanding complex narratives across visual and textual modalities. This paper introduces a novel Multimodal Dual Attention Memory (MDAM) framework designed to enhance the accuracy and interpretability of VSQA systems. The proposed approach integrates dual attention mechanisms—spatial-temporal attention for video frames and semantic attention for textual context—to effectively capture and align relevant information from both modalities. Additionally, a memory module is employed to store and retrieve critical narrative elements, enabling the model to handle long-term dependencies and dynamic storylines. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that MDAM outperforms existing methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance in terms of accuracy and robustness. The framework’s ability to provide interpretable attention maps further enhances its practical utility. This research contributes to advancing multimodal learning in AI, offering a scalable solution for applications in video understanding, education, and entertainment. Key innovations include the dual attention mechanism and memory-augmented architecture, which address the limitations of traditional unimodal approaches. Keywords: Video Story Question Answering, Multimodal Learning, Attention Mechanisms, Memory Networks, AI Models.
