This paper investigates the sample complexity of two prominent reinforcement learning (RL) approaches: actor-critic methods and direct policy search. The objective is to provide a comparative analysis of their efficiency in terms of the number of samples required to achieve optimal performance in various environments. We employ theoretical analysis and empirical evaluations to assess the convergence rates and scalability of both methods. Our experiments are conducted across a range of benchmark environments, including discrete and continuous control tasks, to ensure robustness and generalizability. Results indicate that actor-critic methods generally exhibit lower sample complexity in high-dimensional state spaces, while direct policy search demonstrates superior performance in simpler, low-dimensional settings. The findings highlight the trade-offs between these approaches and provide insights into their suitability for different RL applications. This study contributes to the broader understanding of RL algorithm selection by offering practical guidelines based on sample efficiency, which is critical for real-world implementations where data collection is costly or time-consuming. Keywords: reinforcement learning, actor-critic, direct policy search, sample complexity, optimization, control tasks.
