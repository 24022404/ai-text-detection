The study aims to address the challenge of unsupervised action recognition by introducing a novel framework that leverages augmented skeleton-based contrastive action learning. Traditional methods often rely on labeled datasets, limiting scalability and applicability in real-world scenarios.

Methods/Approach: We propose a Momentum Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network that enhances unsupervised learning through contrastive techniques. By integrating augmented skeleton data, our model captures rich temporal and spatial features of human actions. The use of a momentum update mechanism in LSTM networks facilitates robust and efficient learning of action dynamics without requiring labeled data.

Results/Findings: Our approach demonstrates superior performance in action recognition tasks compared to existing unsupervised models. Extensive experiments on benchmark skeleton action datasets highlight the effectiveness of our method in accurately classifying actions. The results showcase improvements in recognition accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency, underscoring the robustness of the momentum LSTM in handling diverse and complex movement patterns.

Conclusion/Implications: This research provides a significant contribution to the field of action recognition by presenting an innovative unsupervised learning technique that can be widely applicable to various human-computer interaction and surveillance systems. The use of augmented skeleton data with momentum LSTM opens new avenues for developing scalable, efficient, and adaptable action recognition systems in diverse environments. Key keywords include unsupervised learning, action recognition, augmented skeleton, momentum LSTM, and contrastive learning.
