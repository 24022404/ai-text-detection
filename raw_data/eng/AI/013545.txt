The identification of trees outside of closed-canopy forests (TOF) is critical for understanding global carbon stocks, biodiversity, and ecosystem services. However, existing methods often rely on high-resolution imagery, which is costly and computationally intensive, limiting scalability. This study proposes a novel global method to identify TOF using medium-resolution satellite imagery, balancing accuracy and efficiency. Our approach leverages a combination of spectral indices, machine learning algorithms, and spatial analysis to distinguish TOF from other land cover types. We trained and validated the model using a diverse dataset spanning multiple biomes and geographic regions. Results demonstrate that the method achieves high accuracy in detecting TOF, with performance comparable to high-resolution approaches while significantly reducing computational requirements. The proposed framework enables large-scale monitoring of TOF, providing valuable insights for climate change mitigation, land management, and conservation efforts. This research contributes a scalable and cost-effective solution for global TOF mapping, addressing a critical gap in remote sensing applications.  

**Keywords**: Trees outside forests (TOF), medium-resolution satellite imagery, machine learning, remote sensing, global monitoring, land cover classification.
