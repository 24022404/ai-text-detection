Understanding the structure of data in low-dimensional representations is crucial for various machine learning tasks. This paper addresses the problem of explaining groups of points in such representations, aiming to provide interpretable descriptions that capture the underlying relationships. We introduce a novel approach that combines density-based clustering with rule extraction techniques. First, clusters of points are identified using a modified DBSCAN algorithm optimized for low-dimensional spaces. Subsequently, interpretable rules are generated for each cluster using a decision tree induction method, where the rules describe the boundaries of the cluster in terms of the original features. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our method effectively identifies meaningful groups of points and generates concise, accurate explanations. The proposed approach outperforms existing methods in terms of both explanation accuracy and interpretability, providing valuable insights into the structure of low-dimensional data. This work contributes to the field of explainable AI by providing a framework for understanding complex data representations.

Keywords: Explainable AI, low-dimensional representations, clustering, rule extraction, interpretability.
