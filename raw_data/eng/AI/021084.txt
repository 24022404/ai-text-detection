**Abstract**

Learning the underlying structure of graphical models from observed data remains a fundamental challenge in the fields of machine learning and statistical inference. This paper investigates a novel approach for reconstructing graphical models by leveraging samples generated via the Glauber dynamics, a type of Markov chain commonly used for simulating distributions in discrete systems. We propose an efficient algorithm that exploits temporal correlations inherent in the sequential updates of Glauber dynamics to accurately infer the connectivity and conditional dependencies of the target graphical model. Our method is theoretically analyzed to provide performance guarantees under various model complexities and sampling regimes. Experimental evaluations on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms existing structure learning methods, especially in scenarios with limited data and hidden dependencies. The results highlight both the practicality and scalability of utilizing Glauber dynamics for graphical model learning. This work paves the way for improved algorithms in network inference, unsupervised learning, and probabilistic modeling, with applications extending to statistical physics, bioinformatics, and social network analysis. Key words: graphical models, Glauber dynamics, structure learning, Markov chain, probabilistic inference.
