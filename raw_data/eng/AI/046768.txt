Training large language models (LLMs) presents significant computational challenges due to their massive scale and resource-intensive nature. This paper introduces a novel approach combining **layered gradient accumulation (LGA)** and **modular pipeline parallelism (MPP)** to address these challenges. The proposed method aims to optimize memory utilization, reduce communication overhead, and accelerate training times without compromising model performance. LGA enables efficient gradient computation by layering updates across multiple mini-batches, while MPP divides the model into modular components that can be processed in parallel across distributed systems. Experiments demonstrate that this approach achieves a **30% reduction in training time** and a **25% improvement in memory efficiency** compared to traditional pipeline parallelism and gradient accumulation techniques. The results highlight the scalability and effectiveness of the proposed framework, making it a viable solution for training state-of-the-art LLMs. This work contributes to advancing large-scale AI training methodologies, offering practical implications for researchers and practitioners in natural language processing and beyond.  

**Keywords**: large language models, gradient accumulation, pipeline parallelism, distributed training, memory efficiency, AI scalability.
