Video inpainting, the task of filling in missing or corrupted regions in video frames, has gained remarkable progress due to advances in deep learning. However, current evaluation benchmarks often fail to reveal the nuanced strengths and weaknesses of state-of-the-art video inpainting algorithms, leading to incomplete assessments of their capabilities. This paper introduces DEVIL, a comprehensive diagnostic evaluation benchmark specifically designed for video inpainting. The benchmark comprises a diverse set of scenarios with varying object motions, occlusion patterns, texture complexities, and temporal inconsistencies, offering granular challenges reflective of real-world applications. We systematically analyze the performance of several leading video inpainting models using the DEVIL benchmark, leveraging quantitative metrics and fine-grained qualitative assessments. Our findings highlight specific failure modes and robustness gaps that are overlooked in existing evaluation protocols. The DEVIL benchmark not only enables a deeper understanding of video inpainting methods but also provides a standardized platform for fair comparison and transparent reporting. By releasing both the dataset and the benchmarking tools, this work fosters the development of more reliable and versatile video inpainting techniques. Key topics include video inpainting, benchmarking, diagnostic evaluation, deep learning, temporal consistency, and dataset development.
