This paper presents a novel self-supervised approach for estimating optical flow based on facial movements, addressing the growing need for accurate motion analysis in facial recognition, animation, and human-computer interaction applications. Traditional optical flow methods often rely on labeled datasets or handcrafted features, which can limit adaptability and scalability. Our method leverages self-supervised learning to autonomously extract motion information from facial video sequences, eliminating the dependence on manual annotations. The proposed framework utilizes a deep neural network to learn motion representations by predicting future frames and reconstructing motion patterns solely from unlabelled data. Experimental evaluations on benchmark facial video datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive accuracy in capturing subtle and complex facial movements, often surpassing state-of-the-art supervised techniques in terms of robustness and generalization. Additionally, our model exhibits strong performance in downstream tasks such as facial expression recognition and 3D facial animation. These findings indicate that self-supervised optical flow estimation, specifically tailored for facial dynamics, can significantly enhance motion analysis with reduced annotation effort. The proposed approach opens new avenues for efficient and scalable solutions in biometric analysis, affective computing, and virtual reality applications. Keywords: self-supervised learning, optical flow, facial movement analysis, deep neural networks, motion estimation, computer vision.
