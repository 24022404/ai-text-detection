This paper introduces R2-B2, a novel Recursive Reasoning-Based Bayesian Optimization algorithm designed to achieve no-regret learning in complex game environments. Addressing the challenge of efficiently exploring vast strategy spaces with limited feedback, R2-B2 leverages a hierarchical Bayesian framework to model opponent behavior and guide strategy selection. The algorithm recursively reasons about opponent strategies, allowing it to anticipate and adapt to evolving game dynamics. R2-B2 employs a modified Gaussian Process Upper Confidence Bound acquisition function to balance exploration and exploitation, focusing on regions of the strategy space that offer high potential reward while mitigating regret. Empirical evaluations across a range of benchmark games demonstrate that R2-B2 consistently outperforms existing Bayesian optimization and reinforcement learning methods in terms of convergence speed and cumulative regret. The results highlight the effectiveness of recursive reasoning in navigating complex strategic landscapes and achieving near-optimal performance with minimal interaction. R2-B2 offers a promising approach for no-regret learning in games and other sequential decision-making problems.

Keywords: Bayesian Optimization, No-Regret Learning, Game Theory, Recursive Reasoning, Gaussian Processes, Reinforcement Learning.
