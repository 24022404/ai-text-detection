Semi-supervised learning on graphs leverages labeled data to predict labels for unlabeled nodes, a task prevalent in network analysis and social science. This paper investigates the consistency of two common graph-based semi-supervised learning methods: Probit models, which estimate label probabilities, and one-hot encoding approaches, which assign discrete labels. We analyze their asymptotic behavior under various graph structures and data distributions, focusing on conditions that guarantee consistent label predictions as the number of nodes increases. Our analysis reveals that while both methods can achieve consistency under specific conditions, Probit models exhibit greater robustness to noise and model misspecification compared to one-hot methods. Furthermore, we identify scenarios where one-hot encoding can lead to inconsistent results due to its sensitivity to local graph structure. These findings provide theoretical insights into the behavior of graph-based semi-supervised learning algorithms and offer practical guidance for selecting appropriate methods based on the characteristics of the graph and available data. Key words: semi-supervised learning, graph algorithms, consistency, Probit model, one-hot encoding, network analysis.
