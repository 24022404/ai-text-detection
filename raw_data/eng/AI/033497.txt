Abstract:  
Text localisation in natural images presents significant challenges due to diverse backgrounds, distortions, and varying lighting conditions. This paper explores the generation and application of synthetic data to improve the performance of text localisation models. We propose a novel framework for creating high-quality synthetic datasets that mimic real-world conditions by integrating advanced image augmentation techniques, diverse font rendering, and realistic scene composition. Our approach enables the generation of large-scale annotated datasets, significantly reducing the reliance on costly and time-consuming manual labelling. We evaluate state-of-the-art deep learning models, including convolutional and transformer-based architectures, trained solely on our synthetic datasets and demonstrate robust performance on standard real-world benchmarks. Comparative analysis shows that models pre-trained on synthetic data and fine-tuned with limited real data achieve accuracy rates comparable to, or exceeding, models trained on extensive real datasets. The results highlight the capability of synthetic data to close the domain gap, increasing generalisation and efficiency for text localisation tasks. This research underlines the value of synthetic data generation for advancing scene text detection and supports its wider adoption in computer vision applications involving document analysis, augmented reality, and intelligent navigation systems. Key keywords: synthetic data, text localisation, natural images, deep learning, computer vision, scene text detection, data augmentation.
