This study addresses the challenge of continual semantic segmentation in evolving environments, focusing on unsupervised model adaptation. The traditional models often struggle to maintain performance when exposed to new, unlabeled data streams, highlighting the need for adaptive methods in dynamic contexts.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel unsupervised adaptation framework that leverages a combination of self-training, feature alignment, and adversarial learning to enable the semantic segmentation model to continuously learn from new data without requiring manual annotations. By employing a dynamic memory mechanism, the model efficiently retains crucial past knowledge while adapting to new, unseen categories.

Results/Findings: Experiments on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves superior performance compared to conventional methods, often requiring fixed-labeled data sets for retraining. Our method not only maintains high segmentation accuracy as the data distribution shifts but also significantly reduces the catastrophic forgetting problem.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed unsupervised adaptation framework makes significant strides in advancing continual learning for semantic segmentation, making models more robust in real-world applications where data is constantly evolving. This research contributes to the field by offering an effective strategy for reducing model dependency on labeled data while enhancing adaptability and performance continuity. Key implications include potential applications in autonomous driving, surveillance, and robotic vision systems, where ongoing environmental changes are prevalent.

Keywords: Continual Learning, Semantic Segmentation, Unsupervised Adaptation, Self-training, Adversarial Learning, Dynamic Memory, Model Adaptation.
