This paper presents a novel approach leveraging recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for both supervised and unsupervised learning of visual odometry and depth estimation from monocular video sequences. Traditional methods for visual odometry often rely on handcrafted features and struggle to accurately infer depth from single-view video, especially in dynamic or unstructured environments. To address these challenges, we introduce an end-to-end deep learning framework that processes continuous video frames using RNNs, enabling the model to capture temporal dependencies and sequential motion dynamics critical for odometry estimation. Our approach supports both supervised learning with labeled trajectory and depth data, as well as unsupervised learning by exploiting photometric consistency and motion constraints between frames. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets, outperforming existing monocular methods in trajectory accuracy and depth prediction robustness. Additionally, the system maintains competitive inference speed, making it suitable for real-time applications in robotics and autonomous navigation. The findings highlight the effectiveness of integrating recurrent architectures with deep visual encoders for monocular 3D scene understanding, and suggest promising directions for future research on self-supervised and adaptive learning in dynamic environments. Keywords: visual odometry, monocular depth estimation, recurrent neural networks, supervised learning, unsupervised learning, deep learning, video analysis.
