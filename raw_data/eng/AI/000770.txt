This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised action recognition by proposing a novel Augmented Skeleton Based Contrastive Action Learning framework utilizing a Momentum Long Short-Term Memory (M-LSTM) architecture. The primary objective is to enhance the discriminative power of skeleton-based representations for human action recognition in scenarios lacking labeled training data. Our approach leverages data augmentation techniques specifically tailored for skeleton sequences, generating diverse views that enrich the contrastive learning process. We introduce a momentum mechanism within the LSTM network to stabilize feature representations over temporal sequences, improving the model’s capacity to capture long-term action dependencies and variations. Extensive experiments on benchmark skeleton action datasets demonstrate that our method achieves superior performance compared to existing unsupervised methods, and approaches the accuracy of some supervised counterparts. The proposed system exhibits improved robustness to noise and irregularities in input skeletal data, attributing to its effective feature extraction and temporal modeling capabilities. This research contributes a scalable and annotation-efficient solution for action recognition in fields such as video surveillance, human-computer interaction, and physical therapy, highlighting the potential of momentum-based recurrent networks in unsupervised learning settings. Key keywords include action recognition, skeleton-based learning, unsupervised learning, contrastive learning, Momentum LSTM, and data augmentation.
