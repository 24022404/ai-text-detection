Physically-based rendering (PBR) provides highly realistic visualizations but is computationally intensive, posing significant challenges for real-time deployment on embedded devices with limited processing power and memory. This paper presents LumiPath, a novel framework designed to enable real-time physically-based rendering on embedded platforms such as mobile devices and single-board computers. LumiPath leverages a combination of lightweight neural network architectures and optimized ray tracing algorithms to approximate global illumination and complex material interactions with minimal computational overhead. Through adaptive sampling and data-driven denoising techniques, our approach achieves high visual fidelity while significantly reducing rendering time compared to conventional PBR pipelines. Experimental results demonstrate that LumiPath delivers interactive frame rates and visually convincing PBR effects on a range of embedded hardware, outperforming several existing solutions in both speed and image quality. This advancement opens new opportunities for embedded graphics applications in areas like mobile gaming, augmented reality, and portable visualization systems. The paper concludes by discussing potential extensions for further performance gains and broader applications in ubiquitous real-time 3D rendering scenarios.  
Keywords: physically-based rendering, real-time rendering, embedded devices, neural rendering, ray tracing, visual fidelity, mobile graphics.
