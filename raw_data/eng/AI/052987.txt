This paper addresses the challenge of associating observations across multiple camera views, a critical task in applications such as surveillance, smart cities, and autonomous systems. Traditional multi-camera association methods often struggle with ambiguities arising from appearance variations, occlusions, and overlapping fields of view. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel framework that leverages localization data—such as spatial coordinates or trajectory information—to enhance the accuracy and reliability of multi-camera association. Our approach integrates localization cues with visual features using a probabilistic model that dynamically weighs each source of information based on context and scene complexity. We evaluate our method on multiple public datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in association accuracy and robustness compared to existing appearance-based and hybrid techniques. The results highlight the effectiveness of combining localization data with visual analysis, especially in complex or crowded environments. This research advances the state of the art in multi-camera tracking by offering a scalable solution adaptable to various real-world settings. Key contributions include the design of an adaptive fusion algorithm, experimental validation on diverse scenarios, and an analysis of system performance under varying localization precision. Keywords: multi-camera association, localization, person re-identification, data fusion, surveillance, tracking systems.
