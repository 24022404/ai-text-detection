This paper introduces a novel approach to approximating self-attention mechanisms in transformer models, extending beyond the limitations of the Nyströmformer method. Our objective is to improve the efficiency and scalability of self-attention, a crucial component in many state-of-the-art natural language processing and computer vision architectures. We propose a spectral shifting technique that approximates self-attention by projecting the attention matrix onto a lower-dimensional subspace, leveraging the spectral properties of the attention matrix to preserve essential information. Our method achieves significant reductions in computational complexity and memory usage, making it particularly suitable for large-scale applications. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, showing comparable or even superior performance to existing self-attention approximation methods, including the Nyströmformer. The key contributions of this research include a theoretically grounded approximation method, improved computational efficiency, and enhanced scalability, paving the way for the application of transformer models to increasingly complex tasks and large datasets. Keywords: self-attention, transformer models, Nyströmformer, spectral shifting, approximation methods, efficient algorithms.
