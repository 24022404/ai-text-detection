This paper addresses the challenge of learning Linear Non-Gaussian Acyclic Models (LiNGAM) when the number of variables greatly exceeds the number of observations—a scenario commonly encountered in domains such as genomics and social network analysis. Traditional LiNGAM algorithms assume ample observation data relative to variables, leading to decreased accuracy or inapplicability in high-dimensional, small-sample settings. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel learning framework that leverages dimension reduction techniques alongside robust statistical estimation to reliably infer causal structures under the constraint of p >> n (variables much greater than observations). The proposed method integrates sparse estimation and regularization strategies to mitigate overfitting while preserving the essential causal signals among variables. Experimental results on both simulated and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing LiNGAM algorithms in terms of accuracy and stability, particularly in high-dimensional regimes. The findings highlight the effectiveness of the framework in reconstructing interpretable causal graphs from limited data. This work expands the applicability of LiNGAM to modern data-rich domains and provides a foundation for future research in high-dimensional causal inference. Key keywords: LiNGAM, causal discovery, high-dimensional data, sparse estimation, non-Gaussianity, dimension reduction.
