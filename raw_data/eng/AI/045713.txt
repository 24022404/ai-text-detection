Scene graphs have emerged as a powerful tool for representing the structural and semantic relationships within images, offering a promising approach for image captioning tasks. However, recent studies have questioned their effectiveness, citing limitations in scalability and computational overhead. This paper revisits the role of scene graphs in image captioning, arguing that their potential remains underexplored. We propose a novel framework that leverages scene graphs to enhance caption generation by explicitly modeling object interactions and contextual dependencies. Our approach integrates a graph-based encoder with a transformer-based decoder, enabling the model to capture fine-grained visual relationships while maintaining computational efficiency. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art captioning models, achieving significant improvements in both accuracy and diversity of generated captions. Furthermore, we provide a detailed analysis of how scene graphs contribute to better semantic understanding and context-aware caption generation. This work not only defends the utility of scene graphs in image captioning but also introduces a scalable and effective framework that bridges the gap between structural representation and natural language generation. The findings highlight the importance of structured visual representations in advancing image understanding and captioning systems.  

**Keywords**: scene graphs, image captioning, transformer models, object interactions, semantic relationships, natural language generation.
