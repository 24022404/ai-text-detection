This paper presents a novel approach to explainable disease classification using weakly-supervised segmentation techniques. The objective is to develop a deep learning model that can accurately classify diseases from medical images while providing insights into the decision-making process. Our approach utilizes a weakly-supervised segmentation framework, which leverages image-level labels to generate pixel-level annotations, enabling the model to focus on the most relevant regions of interest. The proposed model is evaluated on a benchmark dataset, demonstrating superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods. Key findings include improved classification accuracy and the ability to generate saliency maps that highlight the most informative image features. The implications of this research are significant, as it has the potential to enhance disease diagnosis and treatment by providing transparent and interpretable results. Our contributions include the development of a novel weakly-supervised segmentation approach and the demonstration of its effectiveness in explainable disease classification, with potential applications in computer-aided diagnosis and medical imaging analysis. Relevant keywords: explainable AI, weakly-supervised learning, disease classification, medical imaging, segmentation, deep learning.
