Deep models have demonstrated remarkable success across various domains, but their training often relies on computationally expensive and data-intensive methods. Variational Bayes (VB) offers a principled framework for approximating posterior distributions, enabling efficient inference in deep models. However, the performance of VB heavily depends on the initialization of its parameters, which can significantly impact convergence and model accuracy. This paper addresses the challenge of identifying good initializations for VB in deep models, aiming to improve both computational efficiency and inference quality. We propose a systematic approach to initialize VB parameters by leveraging pre-trained models, heuristic-based strategies, and data-driven techniques. Our experiments evaluate the proposed methods across multiple architectures and datasets, demonstrating improved convergence rates and enhanced model performance compared to random or naive initializations. The results highlight the importance of careful initialization in VB for deep models and provide practical guidelines for practitioners. This work contributes to the broader adoption of VB in deep learning by reducing computational overhead and improving the reliability of variational inference.  

**Keywords**: Variational Bayes, deep models, parameter initialization, variational inference, deep learning, Bayesian methods.
