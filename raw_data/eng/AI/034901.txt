Accurate prediction of human trajectories and head poses is crucial for various applications, including autonomous navigation, social robotics, and video surveillance. This paper introduces a novel approach, Tracklet-Vislet Joint Reasoning (TVJR), that jointly forecasts people's future trajectories and head poses by leveraging both tracklet-level and vislet-level cues. Tracklets capture motion patterns over short time intervals, while vislets represent visual features associated with head pose. TVJR utilizes a graph neural network to model interactions between tracklets and vislets, enabling the propagation of information and consistent predictions. We propose a joint loss function that encourages coherence between trajectory and head pose predictions. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that TVJR significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both trajectory and head pose forecasting accuracy. The results highlight the benefits of joint reasoning on motion and visual cues for improved human behavior prediction. Our code and data will be publicly available.
Keywords: trajectory prediction, head pose estimation, graph neural networks, human behavior forecasting, computer vision.
