This paper addresses the growing need for efficient video summarization by introducing a novel approach based on self-attention mechanisms. The objective is to enhance the quality and relevance of video summaries, making them more informative and engaging for users. We leverage the capabilities of Universal Transformers, which integrate self-attention and recurrence to capture both global context and sequential dependencies in video data. Our method processes video frames as sequences, using self-attention to dynamically weigh each frame's contribution to the summary without relying on pre-defined templates or extensive annotations. The results demonstrate superior performance over conventional methods, with our approach achieving increased precision and recall in summary generation across various datasets. These findings underscore the potential of Universal Transformers in addressing complex sequence modeling tasks inherent in video processing. By providing a scalable and efficient framework for video summarization, our work opens new avenues for applications in media management, content curation, and user-specific content delivery. Key innovations include the integration of self-attention for adaptive frame selection and the utilization of Universal Transformers for enhanced contextual understanding, setting a new benchmark in the realm of video summarization. Keywords: self-attention, video summarization, Universal Transformers, sequence modeling, content analysis.
