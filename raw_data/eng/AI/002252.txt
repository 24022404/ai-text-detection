This paper presents a novel approach to learning attention mechanisms in videos, leveraging human eye fixations as a supervisory signal. The objective is to develop a model that can automatically identify and attend to relevant regions in videos, mimicking human visual attention. Our method utilizes a deep neural network architecture that incorporates eye fixation data to guide the attention process. The model is trained on a large dataset of videos with corresponding eye fixation annotations, allowing it to learn the patterns and features that attract human attention. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency, with significant improvements in video object detection and tracking tasks. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of incorporating human visual attention cues into video analysis models, enabling more accurate and human-like understanding of video content. This work contributes to the development of more sophisticated video analysis systems, with potential applications in areas such as surveillance, healthcare, and human-computer interaction, and is relevant to topics including computer vision, eye tracking, and machine learning.
