Histopathology image analysis is critical for disease diagnosis and treatment planning, but the appearance variability of these images due to differences in staining protocols, scanners, and laboratories poses significant challenges for automated analysis systems. This paper proposes a novel approach leveraging domain-adversarial neural networks (DANNs) to address this variability and improve the generalizability of histopathology image classification models. The proposed method integrates adversarial training to minimize domain shifts between different datasets, enabling the model to learn domain-invariant features. We evaluate the approach on multiple publicly available histopathology datasets, comparing its performance against traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and other domain adaptation techniques. Experimental results demonstrate that the DANN-based model achieves superior classification accuracy and robustness across diverse datasets, outperforming baseline methods by a significant margin. The findings highlight the potential of domain-adversarial training to enhance the reliability of histopathology image analysis in real-world clinical settings, where appearance variability is a persistent challenge. This work contributes to advancing automated diagnostic tools by addressing a critical bottleneck in histopathology image processing.  

**Keywords**: Domain-adversarial neural networks, histopathology images, domain adaptation, image classification, appearance variability, deep learning.
