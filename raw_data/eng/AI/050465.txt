This paper presents a novel approach for solving multi-agent optimization problems in game-theoretic settings using a gradient-based adaptation of the Nikaido-Isoda function. The main objective is to provide an effective computational framework for finding Nash equilibria in non-cooperative games where traditional analytical methods may struggle due to scalability or non-convexity. Our method leverages the Nikaido-Isoda function as a potential function to quantify equilibrium deviation, integrating it into a gradient-based optimization algorithm that iteratively updates each player's strategy. We analyze the convergence properties of the proposed algorithm, demonstrating its theoretical robustness across a variety of game types, including both convex and selected classes of non-convex games. Experimental results on synthetic and benchmark multi-agent scenarios indicate that our method not only converges efficiently but also outperforms state-of-the-art techniques in terms of both computational speed and solution quality. The approach also exhibits strong scalability as the number of agents increases. These findings highlight the potential of gradient-based Nikaido-Isoda optimization as a practical and scalable tool for equilibrium computation in modern game-theoretic applications, such as network resource allocation, distributed control, and economic modeling. Key keywords: game theory, Nikaido-Isoda function, gradient-based optimization, Nash equilibrium, multi-agent systems, algorithmic game theory.
