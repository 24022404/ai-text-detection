This paper presents a comprehensive approach to data fusion of objects using laser scanning, structured light, and photogrammetry techniques, with a focus on cultural heritage applications. The objective is to develop a robust and accurate method for reconstructing and preserving cultural artifacts, monuments, and historical sites. Our approach combines the strengths of each technique to create detailed, high-resolution 3D models. Laser scanning provides precise geometric data, while structured light and photogrammetry offer detailed texture and color information. The results show that our data fusion method achieves high accuracy and completeness, outperforming individual techniques. The implications of this research are significant, enabling the creation of detailed digital archives of cultural heritage sites, facilitating conservation, restoration, and education efforts. Key contributions include the development of a novel data fusion framework, the evaluation of its performance on various cultural heritage objects, and the demonstration of its potential for large-scale applications. Relevant keywords: cultural heritage, 3D reconstruction, data fusion, laser scanning, structured light, photogrammetry, digital preservation.
