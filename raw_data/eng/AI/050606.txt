This paper introduces a novel approach to hierarchical reinforcement learning by learning goal embeddings via self-play. The objective is to enable agents to learn complex behaviors by representing sub-goals as embeddings, which can be composed to achieve long-term objectives. Our method employs a self-play framework, where the agent learns to represent goals in a compact and meaningful way through interactions with its environment. We utilize a combination of reinforcement learning and self-supervised learning to train the goal embedding model, allowing the agent to discover and represent a hierarchy of goals. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of learning efficiency and scalability, and can be applied to a variety of complex tasks. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of learning goal representations in hierarchical reinforcement learning, and demonstrate the potential of self-play as a powerful tool for discovering and representing complex behaviors. Our approach has significant implications for the development of autonomous systems that can learn and adapt in complex environments, and contributes to the advancement of hierarchical reinforcement learning, self-play, and goal-oriented learning. Key keywords: hierarchical reinforcement learning, self-play, goal embeddings, reinforcement learning, autonomous systems.
