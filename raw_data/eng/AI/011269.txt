This paper introduces MeNToS, a novel approach for multi-object tracking (MOT) that leverages a space-time memory network to enhance tracklet association. The objective is to improve the robustness and accuracy of MOT, particularly in challenging scenarios with frequent occlusions and complex object interactions. MeNToS employs a memory module to store and retrieve spatio-temporal information of tracklets, enabling the network to learn long-range dependencies and predict future trajectories. This architecture facilitates the re-identification of objects after occlusions and reduces fragmentation of tracks. We evaluate MeNToS on several benchmark datasets, including MOT17 and MOT20, demonstrating significant improvements in MOTA and IDF1 scores compared to state-of-the-art methods. The results highlight the effectiveness of the space-time memory network in capturing complex motion patterns and enhancing tracklet association. The proposed approach offers a promising direction for advancing MOT, with potential applications in autonomous driving, video surveillance, and robotics. Key words: Multi-Object Tracking, Tracklet Association, Memory Network, Space-Time Reasoning, Object Re-identification.
