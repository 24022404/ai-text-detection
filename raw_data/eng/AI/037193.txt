Abstract:

Overhead image segmentation plays a crucial role in applications such as urban planning, environmental monitoring, and disaster response, yet deep learning models often struggle to generalize across diverse landscapes and imaging conditions. This paper addresses the challenge of improving generalization in semantic segmentation of overhead imagery by introducing a novel pooling method based on the Getis-Ord Gi* statistic, a spatial analysis measure traditionally used for hotspot detection. The proposed Getis-Ord Gi* Pooling (GOP) layer is integrated into popular convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures to better capture spatial contextual information and enhance feature discrimination. Comprehensive experiments are conducted on several public overhead image datasets, comparing performance against standard max and average pooling approaches, as well as state-of-the-art adaptive pooling methods. Results demonstrate that models equipped with the GOP layer consistently achieve superior segmentation accuracy, particularly in heterogeneous and unseen geospatial regions. The findings highlight the effectiveness of spatial statistical pooling in reducing overfitting and improving model robustness for overhead image tasks. This work advances the field by offering a generalizable framework for deep segmentation models, facilitating wider adoption in real-world geospatial analysis. Key keywords: overhead image segmentation, Getis-Ord Gi*, pooling, deep learning, spatial statistics, convolutional neural networks, model generalization.
