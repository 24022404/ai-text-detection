Deep neural networks have achieved remarkable success in various applications; however, their computational cost and memory requirements pose significant challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices. This paper introduces a novel training methodology, "Go Wide, Then Narrow" (GWTN), designed to efficiently train deep yet thin networks. The GWTN approach initiates training with a wider network architecture, benefiting from enhanced exploration of the loss landscape. Subsequently, a progressive pruning strategy is employed to gradually reduce the network's width, eliminating redundant connections and promoting a more compact, efficient model. We evaluate GWTN on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, demonstrating significant reductions in computational complexity and parameter count compared to traditional training methods and other pruning techniques. Results show that GWTN achieves comparable or superior accuracy while using substantially fewer resources. The GWTN methodology offers a practical solution for training efficient deep networks suitable for deployment in edge computing and other resource-limited environments, contributing to the development of sustainable and scalable AI solutions.

**Keywords:** Deep learning, Neural networks, Network pruning, Model compression, Efficient training, Edge computing.
