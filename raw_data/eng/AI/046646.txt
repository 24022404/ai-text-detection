This paper presents a novel few-shot learning framework for the recognition of historical ciphered manuscripts, addressing the critical challenge of limited labeled data in manuscript decipherment. The objective of this research is to develop an automated system that can accurately identify and transcribe handwritten ciphers, which often exhibit unique scripts and scarce annotated examples. We propose a deep learning-based approach that leverages meta-learning algorithms to enable rapid adaptation to new cipher styles with minimal training samples. Our method incorporates a combination of convolutional neural networks for feature extraction and a meta-learner designed to generalize across diverse cipher manuscripts. Experimental evaluations conducted on a newly curated dataset of historical ciphers demonstrate that the proposed system outperforms traditional supervised and transfer learning methods in both accuracy and sample efficiency. Key findings include the effective transferability of learned representations and significant reductions in required annotation effort. Our findings highlight the potential of few-shot learning techniques to advance the field of historical document analysis, facilitating the decoding of rare and complex scripts. The proposed approach opens new opportunities for digital humanities applications, preservation of cultural heritage, and cryptographic research. Keywords: few-shot learning, historical manuscripts, cipher recognition, meta-learning, document analysis, deep learning.
