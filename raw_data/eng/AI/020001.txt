This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised ensemble classification in scenarios involving sequential and networked data, where labeled training data is unavailable. Traditional classification methods often rely on supervised learning, which limits their applicability in domains where annotations are costly or impractical. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel unsupervised ensemble framework that leverages the inherent structures of sequential and networked data to improve classification accuracy. Our approach integrates multiple unsupervised models, each tailored to capture distinct patterns in the data, such as temporal dependencies in sequences or relational dependencies in networks. The ensemble mechanism combines these models using an adaptive weighting scheme, ensuring robust performance across diverse datasets. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing unsupervised techniques, achieving significant improvements in classification accuracy and stability. Furthermore, the framework is computationally efficient and scalable, making it suitable for large-scale applications. The proposed approach contributes to advancing unsupervised learning in complex data environments and has potential applications in areas such as anomaly detection, social network analysis, and time-series forecasting.  

**Keywords**: unsupervised learning, ensemble classification, sequential data, networked data, adaptive weighting, anomaly detection.
