Semi-supervised transfer learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for leveraging limited labeled data and abundant unlabeled data to improve model performance across domains. However, existing methods often struggle with domain shifts and inconsistent predictions, limiting their effectiveness. This paper introduces **Adaptive Consistency Regularization (ACR)**, a novel framework designed to enhance the robustness and generalization of semi-supervised transfer learning models. ACR dynamically adjusts the consistency regularization strength based on the confidence of model predictions, ensuring reliable knowledge transfer while mitigating overfitting to noisy or uncertain data. We evaluate ACR on benchmark datasets across diverse domains, including image classification and text analysis, demonstrating significant improvements in accuracy and stability compared to state-of-the-art methods. Experimental results show that ACR achieves up to a 15% reduction in error rates and better adapts to domain shifts. The proposed framework not only advances semi-supervised learning but also offers practical implications for real-world applications with limited labeled data, such as medical imaging and natural language processing. Key contributions include the adaptive regularization mechanism, a theoretical analysis of its convergence properties, and empirical validation of its effectiveness. Keywords: semi-supervised learning, transfer learning, consistency regularization, domain adaptation, adaptive learning.
