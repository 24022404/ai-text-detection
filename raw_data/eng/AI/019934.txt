Building damage assessment from satellite imagery is critical for disaster response and recovery efforts. However, accurately identifying and classifying damage levels remains challenging due to the complexity of visual features and varying scales of destruction. This paper presents BDANet, a novel multiscale convolutional neural network (CNN) enhanced with cross-directional attention mechanisms for building damage assessment. The proposed model leverages a hierarchical architecture to capture both fine-grained and coarse-grained features, while the cross-directional attention module dynamically focuses on spatially relevant regions across different orientations, improving feature discrimination. We evaluate BDANet on a publicly available dataset of post-disaster satellite images, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art methods. Experimental results demonstrate that BDANet achieves superior accuracy in damage classification, with significant improvements in precision and recall metrics. The model’s ability to generalize across diverse disaster scenarios highlights its robustness and practical applicability. This research contributes to advancing automated damage assessment systems, offering a scalable solution for rapid disaster response and resource allocation.  

**Keywords**: Building damage assessment, satellite imagery, convolutional neural networks, cross-directional attention, multiscale feature extraction, disaster response.
