This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing computational efficiency and accuracy in machine learning models, focusing on the backpropagation process. The research explores the use of angle-parametrized orthogonal matrices to facilitate parallelized computation, a method which promises improvement in both speed and performance for large-scale neural network training.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel mathematical framework that leverages angle-parametrized orthogonal matrices to decompose the computational tasks in neural networks. This approach allows for the parallel execution of operations traditionally performed sequentially in the backpropagation algorithm. The proposed method ensures orthogonality constraints are maintained throughout the forward and backward passes, thereby reducing computational overhead. 

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our parallelized approach achieves significant reductions in training time compared to conventional methods, without sacrificing model accuracy. Tests conducted on benchmark datasets reveal a marked improvement in computational efficiency, with up to 40% faster training times and comparable model performance. The implementation scales effectively across multiple processors, ensuring adaptability to various hardware configurations.

Conclusion/Implications: The introduction of angle-parametrized orthogonal matrices for parallel computation in backpropagation represents a substantial advancement in neural network training methodologies. This research provides a pathway to more efficient machine learning models, with potential applications in real-time data processing and complex system simulations. Our findings pave the way for future exploration into optimized mathematical structures in AI, enhancing the scalability and accessibility of advanced computation techniques.

Keywords: Parallelized Computation, Backpropagation, Orthogonal Matrices, Neural Networks, Machine Learning, Angle-Parametrization, Optimization, Computational Efficiency.
