Scene understanding and retrieval are critical tasks in computer vision, with applications ranging from autonomous systems to multimedia search. This paper addresses the challenge of searching scenes by abstracting objects and their relationships, enabling efficient and interpretable scene retrieval. We propose a novel framework that leverages object-level abstraction to represent scenes as structured graphs, where nodes correspond to objects and edges encode spatial and semantic relationships. Our approach combines state-of-the-art object detection with graph-based reasoning to capture high-level scene semantics. To evaluate the framework, we conduct experiments on benchmark datasets, comparing our method against traditional scene retrieval techniques. Results demonstrate significant improvements in retrieval accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly in complex scenes with diverse object interactions. The proposed method also offers enhanced interpretability, as the abstracted graph representations provide intuitive insights into scene composition. This work contributes to advancing scene understanding by bridging the gap between low-level visual features and high-level semantic abstraction, with potential applications in robotics, augmented reality, and content-based image retrieval.  

**Keywords**: scene retrieval, object abstraction, graph-based reasoning, computer vision, semantic representation.
