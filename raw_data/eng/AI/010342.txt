This paper introduces Seasonal Contrast, a novel unsupervised pre-training method leveraging uncurated remote sensing data to enhance the performance of downstream computer vision tasks. The objective is to exploit the inherent seasonal variability in satellite imagery to learn robust and generalizable features without requiring labeled data. Our approach utilizes a contrastive learning framework, where pairs of images from different seasons are used to train a model to distinguish between similar and dissimilar samples. The results show that Seasonal Contrast outperforms existing self-supervised methods on various remote sensing benchmarks, demonstrating improved feature learning and transferability. The key findings highlight the effectiveness of leveraging seasonal information to learn meaningful representations from uncurated data. This research contributes to the development of more efficient and scalable remote sensing analysis pipelines, with potential applications in environmental monitoring, land cover classification, and climate change studies. The proposed method is particularly relevant for large-scale remote sensing applications, where labeled data is scarce or expensive to obtain. Key keywords: unsupervised pre-training, remote sensing, seasonal variability, contrastive learning, computer vision.
