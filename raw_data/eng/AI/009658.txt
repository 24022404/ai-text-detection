This paper presents a comprehensive tutorial on recursive models for analyzing and predicting path choice behavior, a critical area in transportation and decision-making systems. The objective is to provide a clear understanding of how recursive modeling techniques can be applied to capture the sequential and hierarchical nature of decision-making processes in path selection. We introduce a structured approach to recursive models, detailing their mathematical foundations and algorithmic implementations. The methods discussed include dynamic programming, Markov decision processes, and recursive utility frameworks, which are tailored to address the complexities of path choice behavior under varying constraints and preferences. Through illustrative examples and simulations, we demonstrate the effectiveness of these models in predicting realistic path choices and their adaptability to different transportation scenarios. The results highlight the superior performance of recursive models compared to traditional static approaches, particularly in capturing interdependencies and feedback loops in decision-making. This tutorial concludes with insights into the practical implications of recursive models for urban planning, traffic management, and personalized navigation systems. By bridging theoretical concepts with real-world applications, this work contributes to advancing the field of behavioral modeling and offers a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners in transportation and related domains.  

**Keywords**: recursive models, path choice behavior, dynamic programming, Markov decision processes, decision-making, transportation systems.
