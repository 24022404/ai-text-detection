Dimensionality reduction techniques are essential in information technology for visualizing and analyzing high-dimensional data; however, interpreting their results and identifying meaningful structures remain challenging. This paper introduces a novel framework that leverages contrastive learning to support analysis and enhance the interpretability of dimensionality reduction outcomes. Our approach utilizes contrastive learning strategies to automatically highlight salient patterns and clusters in low-dimensional embeddings produced by methods such as t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) and Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP). We present a system that integrates contrastive objectives with popular dimensionality reduction techniques, enabling users to assess the consistency and relevance of the resulting representations. Comparative experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our framework improves the identification of significant data relationships and enhances cluster separability without sacrificing computational efficiency. The findings also indicate increased robustness to noise and outliers compared to traditional workflows. This research contributes an interpretable and effective tool for information visualization, data exploration, and model validation in machine learning and data analysis pipelines. Keywords: dimensionality reduction, contrastive learning, visualization, cluster analysis, interpretability, t-SNE, UMAP.
