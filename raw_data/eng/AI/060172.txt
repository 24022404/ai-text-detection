Abstract:

Object detection plays a critical role in autonomous driving systems, enabling safe navigation and real-time decision-making. However, conventional object detection frameworks often encounter challenges such as suboptimal anchor generation and significant class imbalance, leading to reduced detection accuracy, especially for rare or small objects. This paper presents a novel methodology to enhance object detection for autonomous driving by introducing an optimized anchor generation mechanism alongside effective strategies for addressing class imbalance. The proposed approach leverages adaptive anchor box selection based on statistical analysis of object scale distributions within autonomous driving datasets. Additionally, we incorporate a hybrid loss function that combines focal loss with class-balanced sampling, which mitigates the dominance of frequent object classes and ensures improved representation of minority classes. Experimental evaluations on widely-used autonomous driving benchmarks demonstrate substantial improvements in mean Average Precision (mAP) and recall, particularly for challenging object categories. Our results show that the combined optimization not only outperforms conventional anchor-based detectors but also achieves more consistent detection across diverse driving scenarios. The findings highlight the importance of tailored anchor strategies and robust class balancing in advancing the reliability and effectiveness of perception systems for autonomous vehicles. Keywords: object detection, autonomous driving, anchor generation, class imbalance, deep learning, computer vision, perception systems.
