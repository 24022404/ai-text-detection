Deep Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have demonstrated significant potential in modeling complex, non-Euclidean data structures such as social networks, biological structures, and knowledge graphs. However, despite their promise, the successful design and optimization of deep GNN architectures remain a critical challenge due to issues like over-smoothing, vanishing gradients, and excessive computational cost. This paper systematically investigates the search for efficient and effective deep GNN architectures. We propose a novel framework that leverages automated neural architecture search (NAS) techniques tailored for graph domains, incorporating strategies to mitigate over-smoothing and maintain feature expressivity at increased depth. The experimental evaluation, conducted across several benchmark graph datasets, demonstrates that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art shallow and manually designed deep GNN models in terms of accuracy, scalability, and robustness. In addition, we provide comprehensive analyses highlighting the role of architectural components such as skip connections, normalization layers, and adaptive aggregation functions in enhancing the learning capability of deep GNNs. Our findings offer valuable insights into the principled design of deep GNN architectures and pave the way for their application in large-scale, real-world graph analysis tasks.  
**Keywords:** graph neural networks, deep learning, neural architecture search, over-smoothing, graph data, architecture optimization
