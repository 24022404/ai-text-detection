Multi-label classification in scenarios with limited or no labeled data presents significant challenges, particularly when dealing with complex relationships between labels. This paper introduces a novel framework for multi-label few-zero-shot learning by leveraging knowledge aggregated from multiple label graphs. The proposed approach addresses the limitations of existing methods by integrating structured label dependencies and semantic information from diverse label graphs, enabling effective generalization to unseen labels with minimal or no training examples. Our method employs a graph-based aggregation mechanism to combine knowledge from multiple sources, enhancing the model's ability to capture intricate label correlations. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed framework outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both few-shot and zero-shot settings, achieving significant improvements in prediction accuracy and robustness. The findings highlight the importance of leveraging diverse label graphs for knowledge transfer and suggest promising applications in domains such as text classification, image annotation, and recommendation systems. This work contributes to advancing multi-label learning by providing a scalable and efficient solution for scenarios with scarce labeled data.  

**Keywords**: multi-label classification, few-shot learning, zero-shot learning, label graphs, knowledge aggregation, graph-based learning.
