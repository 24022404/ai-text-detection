Objective: Foreground object extraction is a critical preprocessing step in applications ranging from photo editing and augmented reality to video surveillance. Manual segmentation is time-consuming, and many existing automatic methods either lack accuracy or impose high computational costs. This paper presents an automatic and efficient foreground object extraction scheme that balances precision and speed for still images.

Methods: Our approach employs a two-stage pipeline. First, a saliency map is generated by fusing color contrast and texture cues across multiple scales, identifying candidate object regions without manual seeds. Second, we refine these regions via an adaptive graph-based segmentation algorithm, in which node weights and edge costs are guided by saliency strength and local boundary confidence. A multi-resolution strategy coupled with morphological filtering suppresses noise and preserves fine details. The entire pipeline is implemented with parallelized routines to achieve real-time performance.

Results: On public benchmarks (e.g., PASCAL VOC and MSRA salient object datasets), our scheme yields an average F-measure of 0.91 and intersection-over-union (IoU) of 0.87, outperforming classic methods such as GrabCut and saliency-only extraction, while matching the accuracy of recent deep-learning approaches. Runtime is 0.12 seconds per 512×512 image on a standard GPU, representing a 3× speedup over competing algorithms.

Conclusion: The proposed method delivers accurate, fully automatic foreground extraction with low latency, making it suitable for interactive graphics and real-time vision systems. Its modular design allows straightforward extension to video sequences and dynamic scenes.

Keywords: foreground extraction, saliency detection, graph-based segmentation, image processing, real-time.
