The rapid advancement of deep learning has significantly improved action recognition in videos, yet the scarcity of diverse and annotated training data remains a critical bottleneck. This paper addresses the challenge by proposing a novel framework for the procedural generation of synthetic videos tailored for training deep action recognition networks. The objective is to create a scalable and cost-effective solution that generates realistic, diverse, and annotated video datasets, reducing reliance on manually collected data. Our approach leverages procedural generation techniques, combining 3D modeling, physics-based simulations, and customizable animation pipelines to produce videos with precise control over action types, environmental conditions, and camera perspectives. We evaluate the effectiveness of the generated datasets by training state-of-the-art action recognition models, including 3D convolutional neural networks (3D CNNs) and transformer-based architectures. Experimental results demonstrate that models trained on procedurally generated videos achieve competitive performance compared to those trained on real-world datasets, with significant improvements in generalization across diverse scenarios. The framework also enables the generation of rare or complex actions that are difficult to capture in real-world settings. This work contributes a scalable and innovative solution to the data scarcity problem in action recognition, with potential applications in robotics, surveillance, and human-computer interaction. Keywords: procedural generation, action recognition, synthetic datasets, deep learning, 3D modeling, video synthesis.
