This paper addresses the critical challenge of efficiently predicting toxicity in online content, a key factor in maintaining healthy online communities. We investigate the use of shallow neural networks and decision trees, leveraging a set of simple, computationally inexpensive features derived from text. Our approach aims to provide a rapid and accurate toxicity assessment without the computational overhead associated with more complex models. Results demonstrate that the proposed models, despite their simplicity, achieve competitive performance compared to more sophisticated deep learning architectures, while significantly reducing processing time. We evaluate our methods on a publicly available toxicity detection dataset, demonstrating a favorable trade-off between accuracy and efficiency. This research contributes a practical solution for real-time toxicity filtering, enabling scalable and responsive moderation systems. Keywords: Toxicity Prediction, Shallow Neural Networks, Decision Trees, Feature Engineering, Online Moderation, Natural Language Processing.
