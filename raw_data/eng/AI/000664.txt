Scene Graph Generation (SGG) is a critical task in computer vision, aiming to parse an image into a structured representation of objects and their relationships. Despite significant progress, existing SGG benchmarks often suffer from biases, limited scalability, and inconsistent evaluation metrics, hindering fair comparisons and advancements in the field. This paper introduces a novel Image Scene Graph Generation Benchmark designed to address these challenges. Our benchmark incorporates a diverse and balanced dataset, covering a wide range of object categories and relationship types, ensuring robust evaluation across various real-world scenarios. We propose a unified evaluation framework that integrates multiple metrics, including recall, precision, and relationship classification accuracy, to provide a comprehensive assessment of model performance. Additionally, we present a baseline model leveraging state-of-the-art graph neural networks (GNNs) and transformer-based architectures, demonstrating improved scalability and accuracy compared to existing approaches. Experimental results highlight the benchmark's effectiveness in identifying model strengths and weaknesses, fostering advancements in SGG research. This work contributes to the field by providing a standardized, bias-free evaluation platform, enabling researchers to develop more accurate and generalizable SGG models. Key innovations include the dataset's diversity, the unified evaluation framework, and the integration of advanced architectures. Keywords: Scene Graph Generation, Computer Vision, Graph Neural Networks, Transformer Models, Benchmarking, Object Relationships.
