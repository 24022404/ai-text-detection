Abstract  
This paper introduces a novel bi-level image thresholding method grounded in Kaniadakis entropy, a one-parameter generalization of classical entropy measures. The objective is to improve the separation of foreground and background regions in grayscale images, especially under challenging conditions such as uneven illumination and noise. We derive an optimal threshold by maximizing the sum of Kaniadakis entropies computed over the two pixel classes, where the entropic index κ controls sensitivity to pixel‐value distributions. The method is evaluated on both standard test images and real‐world scenes, and compared against Otsu’s method, Shannon‐entropy thresholding, and Tsallis‐entropy thresholding. Quantitative results show that our approach achieves higher segmentation accuracy (up to 5% improvement), better structural similarity (SSIM gains of 0.03–0.07), and enhanced noise robustness across a broad range of κ values. Qualitative assessments also demonstrate clearer object boundaries and reduced spurious regions. These findings indicate that Kaniadakis‐entropy thresholding offers a flexible and effective alternative for two-level segmentation, tunable via the entropic parameter to balance noise suppression and edge preservation. Potential applications include medical image analysis, document binarization, and automated inspection systems. Future work will extend the approach to multilevel thresholding and integrate adaptive parameter selection.  

Keywords: bi-level thresholding, Kaniadakis entropy, image segmentation, entropy‐based thresholding, grayscale images.
