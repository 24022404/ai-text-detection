This paper proposes a novel approach to contrastive learning of visual representations using Deep Bregman Divergence (DBD). The objective is to develop an effective method for learning compact and informative visual representations by leveraging the strengths of contrastive learning and Bregman divergence. Our approach employs a deep neural network to estimate the Bregman divergence between positive and negative pairs of images, allowing for more accurate and robust representation learning. The DBD method is compared to state-of-the-art contrastive learning techniques, demonstrating superior performance on benchmark datasets. Key findings include improved representation quality, increased robustness to noise and variations, and enhanced transferability to downstream tasks. The proposed DBD approach contributes to the field of computer vision and machine learning by providing a new perspective on contrastive learning, with potential applications in image classification, object detection, and image generation. Relevant keywords: contrastive learning, visual representations, Bregman divergence, deep learning, computer vision.
