**Abstract:**

This paper presents XGAN, a novel framework for unsupervised image-to-image translation capable of handling many-to-many mappings across multiple visual domains. Traditional image translation approaches are often limited to one-to-one domain mappings and typically require paired training data, which restricts their practical applicability. XGAN addresses these challenges by leveraging a dual adversarial network architecture and a shared latent space representation to learn cross-domain correspondences without the need for paired supervision. Our approach incorporates cycle-consistency constraints to preserve content structure and domain-invariant features, enabling flexible translation between diverse image categories. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that XGAN achieves superior visual quality and more diverse translation outcomes compared to existing methods, particularly in scenarios involving complex, multi-domain relationships. The model's scalability and robustness are further validated through ablation studies and quantitative evaluations. XGAN’s unsupervised, many-to-many translation capability opens new avenues for applications in image synthesis, data augmentation, and cross-domain visual understanding. Key contributions include the design of a unified network structure for many-to-many translation, the integration of shared latent representations, and enhanced diversity in output images, advancing the state-of-the-art in unsupervised image-to-image translation. Keywords: unsupervised learning, image-to-image translation, generative adversarial networks (GANs), many-to-many mappings, deep learning, computer vision.
