Deep Transfer Learning (DTL) has shown promise in medical image analysis, addressing challenges posed by limited labeled data and high computational costs. This paper investigates the underlying mechanisms that contribute to the success of DTL in this domain. We explore the impact of pre-trained models, fine-tuning strategies, and domain similarity on the performance of DTL frameworks. Specifically, we analyze how different layers of pre-trained convolutional neural networks capture generic and task-specific features relevant to medical imaging. Our approach involves fine-tuning several pre-trained models (e.g., ImageNet-trained ResNet, DenseNet) on a diverse set of medical imaging datasets, including X-rays, CT scans, and MRIs, and evaluating their performance in various diagnostic tasks. Results demonstrate that fine-tuning the later layers of pre-trained models yields the most significant performance gains, suggesting that these layers are more adaptable to domain-specific characteristics. Furthermore, we find that the effectiveness of DTL is highly dependent on the similarity between the pre-training dataset and the target medical imaging dataset. These findings offer insights into optimizing DTL pipelines for medical image analysis, potentially leading to improved diagnostic accuracy and reduced reliance on large labeled medical datasets. Keywords: Deep Transfer Learning, Medical Image Analysis, Convolutional Neural Networks, Fine-tuning, Domain Adaptation.
