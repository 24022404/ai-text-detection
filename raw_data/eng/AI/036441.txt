Domain generalization (DG) and domain adaptation (DA) aim to improve model performance on unseen target domains by leveraging knowledge from multiple source domains. However, feature distribution discrepancies across domains and information loss during adaptation often hinder the effectiveness of existing methods. This paper introduces a novel Feature Alignment and Restoration (FAR) framework designed to mitigate these challenges. FAR employs an adversarial alignment module to minimize feature distribution divergence across domains, promoting domain-invariant representations. Furthermore, a restoration module, utilizing a self-reconstruction loss, preserves critical domain-specific information, preventing over-alignment and information bottleneck. We evaluate FAR on several benchmark DG and DA datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in accuracy and robustness compared to state-of-the-art methods. The experimental results highlight FAR's ability to learn generalizable features and effectively adapt to new domains, offering a promising approach for real-world applications with limited labeled data. Keywords: Domain Generalization, Domain Adaptation, Feature Alignment, Representation Learning, Adversarial Learning, Self-Reconstruction.
