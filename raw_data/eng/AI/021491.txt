This paper addresses the challenge of generating detailed and customizable image captions by introducing a novel approach that leverages abstract scene graphs. The aim is to improve the granularity and control over the content and style of the descriptions produced by image captioning systems.

Methods/Approach: The proposed method incorporates abstract scene graphs as an intermediary representation between images and their corresponding captions. These scene graphs are designed to capture intricate details and relationships within an image, which are then used to guide a fine-grained caption generation process. The approach utilizes advanced techniques in natural language processing and computer vision, integrating them into a cohesive framework that allows for varying levels of control over the output captions.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that the use of abstract scene graphs significantly enhances the ability to generate customized and contextually accurate captions compared to traditional methods. Our system outperforms existing models in terms of precision and recall, as evidenced by extensive benchmarking on standard datasets. Additionally, user studies validate the increased satisfaction and preference for captions generated using our approach.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of abstract scene graphs into image captioning introduces a new paradigm of fine-grained control, offering substantial benefits in scenarios requiring personalized content or domain-specific language. This research contributes to the advancement of intelligent image description technologies, with potential applications in accessibility tools, personalized content generation, and enhanced human-computer interaction.

Keywords: image captioning, abstract scene graphs, natural language processing, computer vision, fine-grained control, personalized content generation, intelligent image description.
