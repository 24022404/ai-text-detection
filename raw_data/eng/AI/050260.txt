Person re-identification (Re-ID) across different domains remains a challenging problem due to variations in camera viewpoints, illumination changes, and background clutter. To address this, we propose an End-to-End Domain Adaptive Attention Network (EADA-Net) for cross-domain person Re-ID. EADA-Net leverages a novel attention mechanism integrated with adversarial learning to simultaneously learn domain-invariant feature representations and align feature distributions across source and target domains. Specifically, our network incorporates a spatial attention module to focus on discriminative body regions and a channel attention module to adaptively recalibrate feature responses. Furthermore, we employ an adversarial learning strategy to minimize the domain discrepancy at both the feature and attention levels. The EADA-Net is trained end-to-end, allowing for joint optimization of feature extraction, attention learning, and domain adaptation. Extensive experiments on several benchmark datasets demonstrate that EADA-Net significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in cross-domain person Re-ID. Our results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed attention-guided domain adaptation approach for learning robust and transferable feature representations. Keywords: Person Re-Identification, Domain Adaptation, Attention Mechanism, Adversarial Learning, Deep Learning.
