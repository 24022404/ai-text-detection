Subgraph recognition, a fundamental task in graph analysis, aims to identify specific structural patterns within a larger graph. This paper introduces a novel approach, the Graph Information Bottleneck (GIB), designed to enhance the accuracy and robustness of subgraph recognition. The GIB leverages information bottleneck principles to learn a compressed representation of the graph structure that is maximally informative about the presence or absence of the target subgraph, while filtering out irrelevant details. Specifically, we develop a neural network architecture that incorporates graph convolutional layers and a bottleneck layer to extract and compress relevant subgraph features. Our experiments on synthetic and real-world graph datasets demonstrate that the GIB significantly outperforms existing subgraph recognition methods, particularly in noisy or complex graph environments. Key findings reveal that the GIB's ability to discard extraneous information leads to improved generalization and resilience against structural variations. The proposed GIB offers a promising framework for various applications, including network security, bioinformatics, and social network analysis, where identifying specific graph patterns is crucial. Keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Information Bottleneck, Subgraph Recognition, Graph Mining, Representation Learning.
