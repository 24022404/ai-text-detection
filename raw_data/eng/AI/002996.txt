This paper addresses the challenge of efficient feature selection in factored Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), a crucial problem in reinforcement learning. The objective is to develop a sample-efficient approach that identifies the most relevant features in complex, factored MDPs, reducing the dimensionality of the state space and improving the learning process. Our method employs a novel combination of statistical techniques and reinforcement learning algorithms to select the most informative features. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of sample efficiency and learning speed. The key findings show that by selectively focusing on the most relevant features, our algorithm achieves better performance with fewer samples, making it particularly suitable for real-world applications where data collection is costly or time-consuming. This research contributes to the development of more efficient and scalable reinforcement learning methods, with potential applications in areas such as robotics, autonomous systems, and complex decision-making processes. Key keywords: factored MDPs, feature selection, sample efficiency, reinforcement learning, dimensionality reduction.
