Accurate segmentation of lung CT images is pivotal in diagnosing and managing various pulmonary diseases. This research addresses the challenge by introducing a knowledge-based Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) designed specifically to enhance segmentation accuracy and efficiency.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel FCN architecture that integrates domain knowledge of lung anatomy and CT imaging characteristics into the network training process. The methodology involves a multi-stage process where initial rough segmentation is refined through the application of anatomical constraints and context-aware enhancements. The model is trained on an extensive dataset of lung CT scans, utilizing advanced optimization techniques to learn intricate patterns within the images.

Results/Findings: The tailor-made FCN demonstrates superior performance in segmenting lung tissues when benchmarked against existing state-of-the-art segmentation models. Quantitative metrics such as Dice coefficient, Jaccard index, and boundary accuracy indicate significant improvements in segmentation precision and robustness. These results affirm the model's capacity to effectively delineate lung structures, even in images with pathologies or varying scan qualities.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of domain-specific knowledge into the FCN framework presents a noteworthy advancement in medical image analysis, offering a robust tool for automated lung CT segmentation. This approach not only improves the delineation of anatomical structures but also holds potential for application in other medical imaging domains. Practitioners can leverage this technology to facilitate early disease detection and tailored treatment planning, significantly impacting clinical workflows.

Keywords: Fully Convolutional Network, lung CT segmentation, medical image analysis, anatomical constraints, automated diagnosis, deep learning.
