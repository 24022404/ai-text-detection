This paper introduces a novel approach for addressing the problem of regression on hyperbolic manifolds, which are non-Euclidean spaces characterized by constant negative curvature. Such manifolds serve as a powerful tool for modeling complex data with hierarchical or tree-like structures, and our objective is to develop an effective regression framework specifically suited to these geometric settings.

Methods/Approach: We propose a hyperbolic regression model that leverages the unique properties of hyperbolic geometry to capture underlying patterns in data more effectively than traditional Euclidean models. Our approach involves formulating regression techniques adapted to the hyperbolic space, utilizing manifold-based optimization algorithms to efficiently navigate the curved geometry. 

Results/Findings: Through experimental evaluations on synthetic and real-world datasets reflected in hyperbolic space, our method demonstrates significant improvements in predictive accuracy and computational efficiency compared to existing alternatives. We benchmark our approach against traditional Euclidean regression models and recent manifold learning techniques, consistently showing superior performance in capturing hierarchical data relationships.

Conclusion/Implications: The results confirm that conducting regression within hyperbolic spaces offers valuable advantages in terms of both accuracy and representation power for hierarchical data. The research contributes a new toolset for machine learning practitioners dealing with data exhibiting hyperbolic-like characteristics, such as social networks or phylogenetic trees. These advancements open up potential applications across various domains requiring efficient regression in complex, non-linear spaces.

Key Keywords: hyperbolic manifold, regression, non-Euclidean space, hierarchical data, manifold-based optimization, machine learning, computational geometry.
