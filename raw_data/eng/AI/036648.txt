Action anticipation in first-person video is a challenging yet crucial task for enabling intelligent systems to proactively assist users in dynamic environments. This paper introduces a novel approach utilizing Rolling-Unrolling Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks specifically designed for early prediction of future actions from egocentric video streams. Our method models the temporal evolution of observed activities by “rolling” through past events and “unrolling” into prospective future states, effectively capturing both short-term cues and longer-term dependencies inherent in first-person perspective sequences. We evaluate our approach on benchmark egocentric video datasets and demonstrate significant improvements in action anticipation accuracy compared to standard LSTM architectures and state-of-the-art techniques. Experimental results show that Rolling-Unrolling LSTMs achieve higher precision in predicting upcoming user actions with longer anticipation horizons, while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for real-time deployment. The proposed framework advances the capability of wearable AI systems and situated robotics by enabling earlier and more reliable action predictions in real-world, egocentric scenarios. Key contributions include the innovative LSTM-based architecture tailored for first-person anticipation, comprehensive analysis against existing methods, and insights into the potential for context-aware, anticipatory human-computer interaction. Keywords: action anticipation, LSTM, first-person video, egocentric vision, sequence modeling, deep learning, wearable AI.
