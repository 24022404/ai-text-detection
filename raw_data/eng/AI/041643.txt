Unsupervised pre-training on remote sensing data offers a powerful pathway to leverage large volumes of unlabeled imagery. However, the inherent seasonal variations in such data pose a significant challenge. This paper introduces Seasonal Contrast, a novel unsupervised pre-training framework specifically designed to mitigate the adverse effects of seasonal contrast in uncurated remote sensing datasets. Our approach leverages a contrastive learning paradigm, employing carefully designed data augmentations that encourage the model to learn representations invariant to seasonal changes while remaining sensitive to semantically meaningful features. We evaluate Seasonal Contrast on downstream land cover classification tasks using diverse remote sensing datasets. Results demonstrate that models pre-trained with our method achieve significant performance gains compared to models pre-trained with standard contrastive learning techniques and those trained from scratch. The proposed framework enhances the robustness and generalizability of pre-trained models, facilitating improved performance in various remote sensing applications. Keywords: Unsupervised Learning, Remote Sensing, Contrastive Learning, Seasonal Variation, Pre-training, Land Cover Classification.
