This paper addresses the problem of learning linear non-Gaussian graphical models with multidirected edges, a crucial task in understanding complex relationships between variables. Our objective is to develop an efficient method for estimating the structure of such models from observational data. We propose a novel approach that leverages a combination of independence tests and graph optimization techniques to identify the underlying graph structure. Our method utilizes a sparse representation of the graph and incorporates a robust estimation procedure to handle non-Gaussian noise. The results show that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and scalability, especially in high-dimensional settings. The key findings include the development of a new algorithm for learning multidirected graphical models and the demonstration of its effectiveness in various simulated and real-world datasets. Our research contributes to the advancement of graphical modeling and has important implications for applications in genetics, finance, and social network analysis, where complex relationships between variables are common. The proposed method has the potential to uncover new insights into the underlying mechanisms of complex systems, and its novelty lies in its ability to handle non-Gaussian data and multidirected edges, making it a valuable tool for researchers and practitioners in the field of machine learning and statistics. Key keywords: graphical models, non-Gaussian data, multidirected edges, structure learning, independence tests.
