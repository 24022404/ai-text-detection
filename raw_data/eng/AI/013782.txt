Image segmentation is a critical task in computer vision, enabling the partitioning of images into meaningful regions for further analysis. This paper addresses the challenge of achieving accurate and efficient segmentation, particularly in scenarios with complex textures and varying illumination. We propose a novel multi-threshold technique based on histogram sampling, which enhances segmentation precision by dynamically identifying optimal threshold values. The method involves analyzing the image histogram to sample key intensity levels, followed by an iterative process to refine thresholds for distinct regions. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms traditional thresholding methods in terms of segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency. The proposed technique achieves a 15% improvement in boundary detection accuracy compared to Otsu’s method and reduces processing time by 20% on average. Furthermore, the method is robust to noise and adaptable to diverse image types, including medical and satellite imagery. This research contributes a scalable and effective solution for image segmentation, with potential applications in medical imaging, remote sensing, and autonomous systems. The key innovations lie in the adaptive histogram sampling mechanism and the integration of multi-threshold optimization, offering a significant advancement over conventional approaches.  

**Keywords**: Image segmentation, multi-thresholding, histogram sampling, computer vision, boundary detection, adaptive thresholding.
