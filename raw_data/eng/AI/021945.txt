Object tracking is a critical task in computer vision, yet many existing Siamese network-based trackers struggle with robustness to object scale variations. This paper addresses the limitation by introducing a scale equivariant architecture into the Siamese tracking framework. The proposed approach integrates scale equivariant convolutional layers, enabling the tracker to better recognize and localize objects regardless of size transformations within video sequences. Comprehensive experiments were conducted on benchmark datasets, showing that our method significantly improves tracking accuracy and robustness compared to traditional scale-invariant Siamese trackers. The results demonstrate enhanced performance in scenarios with challenging scale changes, while maintaining real-time speed. This work offers a novel contribution by establishing the benefits of scale equivariance for visual object tracking and lays the groundwork for further exploration of equivariant representations in deep tracking networks. Key advantages include improved adaptability to dynamic scene conditions and potential for deployment in applications such as surveillance, autonomous driving, and human-computer interaction. Keywords: Siamese tracking, scale equivariance, object tracking, convolutional neural networks, robustness.
