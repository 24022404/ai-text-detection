Instance segmentation on 3D point clouds is crucial for scene understanding in various applications, yet existing methods often struggle with complex scenes due to limitations in task definition and evaluation metrics. This paper re-evaluates the standard instance segmentation pipeline, proposing modifications to address prevalent issues like over-segmentation and inconsistent performance across different object categories. We introduce a novel loss function that incorporates both geometric and semantic information to improve segmentation accuracy, particularly for objects with intricate shapes. Furthermore, we propose a new metric, termed "Coverage-Weighted IoU," which better reflects segmentation quality by accounting for varying object sizes and densities. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of segmentation accuracy and robustness. Specifically, we achieve a 15% improvement in average precision compared to state-of-the-art techniques. Our analysis reveals that the proposed metric correlates more strongly with human perception of segmentation quality than traditional IoU-based metrics. This work offers insights into the limitations of current instance segmentation approaches and provides a more effective framework for future research and development. Keywords: 3D point clouds, instance segmentation, semantic segmentation, loss function, evaluation metric, scene understanding.
