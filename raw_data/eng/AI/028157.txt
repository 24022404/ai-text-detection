This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised image matching and object discovery, focusing on the problem of identifying and aligning corresponding objects across unlabeled image datasets. Traditional supervised methods rely heavily on annotated data, which is often costly and time-consuming to obtain. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel optimization-based framework that leverages unsupervised learning techniques to automatically discover and match objects across images without requiring labeled data. Our approach employs a combination of feature extraction, clustering, and iterative optimization to align objects based on their intrinsic visual patterns. We evaluate the proposed method on benchmark datasets, demonstrating its ability to achieve competitive performance compared to supervised approaches while significantly reducing the dependency on labeled data. The results highlight the framework's robustness and scalability, particularly in scenarios with diverse object appearances and backgrounds. This work contributes to advancing unsupervised learning in computer vision, offering a practical solution for applications such as image retrieval, object tracking, and 3D reconstruction. Key innovations include a self-supervised optimization mechanism and a flexible clustering strategy that adapts to varying object complexities. The proposed method opens new avenues for research in unsupervised object discovery and matching, with potential applications in autonomous systems and large-scale image analysis.  

**Keywords**: unsupervised learning, image matching, object discovery, optimization, computer vision, clustering, feature extraction.
