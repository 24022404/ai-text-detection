Molecular optimization is a critical task in drug discovery and materials science, aiming to transform molecular structures with desired properties while preserving key functionalities. This paper introduces a novel approach for molecular optimization through multimodal graph-to-graph translation, leveraging the complementary strengths of graph neural networks (GNNs) and generative models. We propose a framework that encodes molecular graphs into a latent space, captures multimodal representations of molecular properties, and decodes these representations into optimized molecular structures. Our method integrates property prediction and structure generation into a unified pipeline, enabling efficient exploration of the chemical space. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods in generating molecules with improved target properties, such as drug-likeness and binding affinity, while maintaining structural validity and diversity. The framework's ability to handle multimodal data and generate chemically feasible molecules highlights its potential for accelerating drug discovery and materials design. This work contributes a scalable and interpretable solution to molecular optimization, bridging the gap between property prediction and molecular generation.  

**Keywords**: molecular optimization, graph-to-graph translation, graph neural networks, generative models, drug discovery, multimodal learning.
