This paper addresses the challenge of multi-camera association, a critical task in surveillance and computer vision systems, where the goal is to accurately track and associate objects across multiple camera views. The primary objective is to improve the robustness and accuracy of cross-camera object matching by leveraging localization techniques. We propose a novel framework that integrates spatial and temporal localization data to enhance the association process. Our approach utilizes geometric transformations and feature-based matching algorithms to align camera views and establish correspondences between objects. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method significantly outperforms traditional appearance-based approaches, achieving higher precision and recall rates in multi-camera tracking scenarios. The framework is evaluated on benchmark datasets, showcasing its effectiveness in complex environments with occlusions and varying lighting conditions. The key contribution of this research lies in its ability to combine localization data with advanced matching techniques, providing a scalable and efficient solution for real-world applications. This work has significant implications for improving surveillance systems, autonomous navigation, and other domains reliant on accurate multi-camera object association.  

**Keywords**: multi-camera association, object tracking, localization, geometric transformation, feature matching, surveillance systems.
