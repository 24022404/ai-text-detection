This paper addresses the challenge of enhancing reinforcement learning (RL) in environments where understanding and predicting object dynamics are critical. Traditional RL models often struggle in dynamic settings, as they fail to effectively prioritize relevant features necessary for accurate decision-making and adaptation.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel Relevance-Guided Modeling (RGM) framework that integrates relevance learning mechanisms with reinforcement learning algorithms. Our approach involves the use of attention networks to identify and concentrate on significant dynamic features of objects within the environment. By combining these attention-driven insights with reinforcement learning, the approach dynamically adapts to relevant object states, consequently improving learning efficiency and decision-making processes.

Results/Findings: The proposed RGM framework was evaluated in several benchmark environments characterized by complex object interactions. Experimental results demonstrate a notable improvement in policy performance and learning speed compared to traditional reinforcement learning methods. Specifically, RGM achieved faster convergence rates and higher rewards in tasks where object dynamics played a significant role, outperforming baseline models by a substantial margin.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a significant advance in the field of reinforcement learning by introducing a method capable of discerning and prioritizing relevant elements within dynamic environments. The application of relevance-guided mechanisms enhances model robustness and adaptability, making it a valuable tool for real-world applications such as robotics and autonomous systems. The RGM framework opens avenues for further exploration into the integration of attention mechanisms in reinforcement learning, expanding the potential for intelligent decision systems to operate in dynamically evolving scenarios.

Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Object Dynamics, Relevance-Guided Modeling, Attention Networks, Decision-Making, Robotics, Autonomous Systems.
