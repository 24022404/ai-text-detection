Time series data is ubiquitous in various domains, such as healthcare, finance, and IoT, but extracting meaningful representations from it remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces a novel self-supervised learning framework for time series representation learning, leveraging inter-intra relational reasoning to capture both temporal dependencies and contextual relationships within the data. The proposed approach employs a dual-branch architecture that simultaneously models intra-series dynamics (within a single time series) and inter-series relationships (across multiple time series). By designing a pretext task that encourages the model to reason about these relationships, the framework learns robust and generalizable representations without requiring labeled data. Experiments on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method, achieving state-of-the-art performance in downstream tasks such as classification, clustering, and anomaly detection. The results highlight the model's ability to capture complex temporal patterns and its potential for real-world applications where labeled data is scarce. This work contributes to advancing self-supervised learning in time series analysis by introducing a relational reasoning mechanism that enhances representation quality and interpretability.  

**Keywords**: self-supervised learning, time series analysis, relational reasoning, representation learning, temporal dependencies, anomaly detection.
