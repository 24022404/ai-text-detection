Low-light image enhancement is crucial for various computer vision applications, yet many existing methods struggle with noise amplification, color distortion, and computational cost. This paper introduces a practical low-light image enhancer designed to address these limitations. Our approach, "Kindling the Darkness," combines a novel noise-aware illumination estimation module with a spatially adaptive enhancement network. The illumination estimation module leverages a multi-scale decomposition strategy to accurately approximate scene illumination while suppressing noise. Subsequently, the enhancement network, guided by the estimated illumination map, performs pixel-wise adjustments to enhance visibility and preserve image details. Experimental results on several benchmark datasets demonstrate that "Kindling the Darkness" achieves superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods in terms of both quantitative metrics (PSNR, SSIM) and visual quality, particularly in challenging scenarios with extreme low-light conditions. Furthermore, our method exhibits favorable computational efficiency, making it suitable for real-time applications. The proposed technique offers a robust and practical solution for enhancing low-light images, advancing applications in surveillance, autonomous driving, and medical imaging.

Keywords: Low-light image enhancement, illumination estimation, noise reduction, image processing, computer vision.
