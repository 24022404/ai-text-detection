Crowd counting is a crucial task in intelligent video surveillance and urban management. Existing methods often struggle with scale variations and occlusions in dense crowds. This paper introduces HA-CCN, a Hierarchical Attention-based Crowd Counting Network, designed to address these challenges. HA-CCN employs a hierarchical architecture with multiple attention modules to capture multi-scale features and focus on informative regions within the crowd. Specifically, we utilize a novel attention mechanism that integrates both spatial and channel attention to enhance feature representation at different levels of the hierarchy. The network is trained end-to-end using a hybrid loss function that combines density estimation and count regression. Experimental results on challenging benchmark datasets, including ShanghaiTech, UCF-QNRF, and NWPU-Crowd, demonstrate that HA-CCN achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of both counting accuracy and robustness. The hierarchical attention mechanism effectively improves the network's ability to handle scale variations and occlusions, leading to more accurate and reliable crowd counting. This work offers a significant advancement in crowd counting, with potential applications in real-world scenarios such as traffic monitoring and public safety.

Keywords: Crowd Counting, Attention Mechanism, Hierarchical Network, Density Estimation, Computer Vision.
