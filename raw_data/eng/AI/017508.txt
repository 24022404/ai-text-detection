This paper aims to investigate the impact of dataset bias on visual emotion recognition systems, with a focus on developing strategies to overcome this limitation. Our approach involves analyzing existing datasets to identify biases and proposing a novel framework that incorporates diverse and representative data sources. We employ a combination of machine learning and computer vision techniques to develop a more robust and accurate visual emotion recognition model. Our results show significant improvements in recognizing emotions across different demographics and contexts, outperforming existing state-of-the-art models. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of dataset diversity and the need for more inclusive and representative data collection methods. Our contributions have significant implications for the development of fair and unbiased AI systems, particularly in applications such as human-computer interaction, affective computing, and social robotics. By addressing dataset bias, our work paves the way for more accurate and reliable visual emotion recognition, enabling the creation of more empathetic and human-centered technologies. Key keywords: visual emotion recognition, dataset bias, machine learning, computer vision, affective computing, fairness and inclusivity.
