Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for leveraging unlabeled data to learn meaningful representations. However, the effectiveness of SSL heavily depends on the choice of data augmentation strategies, which are often manually designed and suboptimal. This paper introduces **SelfAugment**, a novel framework for automatically discovering optimal augmentation policies tailored for self-supervised learning tasks. The proposed approach leverages a reinforcement learning-based search mechanism to explore and evaluate augmentation combinations, optimizing for representation quality without requiring labeled data. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that SelfAugment outperforms existing handcrafted augmentation strategies, achieving significant improvements in downstream task performance across image classification and object detection. Furthermore, the framework is computationally efficient and generalizable across different SSL architectures. The results highlight the importance of adaptive augmentation policies in SSL and provide a scalable solution for enhancing representation learning. This work contributes to advancing the field of self-supervised learning by automating a critical yet understudied component, paving the way for more robust and efficient SSL systems.  

**Keywords**: self-supervised learning, data augmentation, automatic augmentation, reinforcement learning, representation learning, deep learning.
