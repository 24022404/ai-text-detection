Image inpainting, the task of filling in missing regions of an image, often suffers from generating repetitive or unrealistic structures. This paper introduces a novel approach to improve the diversity and structural coherence of image inpainting through a hierarchical Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE). Our method learns a discrete latent space representation of image structure at multiple levels of abstraction, enabling the generation of diverse and plausible structural hypotheses for the missing regions. Specifically, we employ a coarse-to-fine generation process, where higher levels of the hierarchy capture global structural information and lower levels refine the details. We further introduce a novel training scheme that encourages the exploration of the latent space to improve diversity. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of both quantitative metrics and qualitative visual assessment, generating more diverse and structurally consistent inpaintings. The hierarchical VQ-VAE provides a powerful framework for structured image generation, offering significant improvements in image inpainting and potentially other related tasks.
Keywords: Image Inpainting, VQ-VAE, Generative Models, Hierarchical Representation, Structure Generation, Diversity.
