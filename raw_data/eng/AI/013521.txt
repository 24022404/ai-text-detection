This research paper addresses the challenge of stereo matching from monocular images through an unsupervised learning approach, aimed at advancing the field of depth perception and 3D scene reconstruction. Traditional stereo matching methods rely heavily on stereo image pairs and supervised training data, limiting their applicability in various real-world scenarios where such data may be scarce. Our objective is to devise an unsupervised framework that effectively learns stereo correspondences from single monocular images without the need for annotated depth maps. To achieve this, we propose a novel algorithm that leverages a combination of deep learning techniques and photometric consistency constraints. By employing a carefully designed loss function, our approach aligns and compares pixel intensities across synthesized views, enabling accurate disparity estimation. Experimental results demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance when compared to state-of-the-art supervised techniques on benchmark datasets, highlighting its potential for real-time applications in environments with limited sensory data. The findings suggest that our unsupervised method can be generalized across different domains, offering a scalable solution for enhancing depth estimation and 3D reconstruction from monocular input. This contributes significantly to the fields of computer vision and autonomous systems, where robust and adaptable stereo matching is crucial. Key keywords include unsupervised learning, stereo matching, monocular images, disparity estimation, and 3D reconstruction.
