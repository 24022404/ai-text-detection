This paper introduces Deep Value Networks, a novel approach to evaluating and refining structured outputs in complex tasks. The objective is to develop a framework that learns to assess and iteratively improve the quality of generated outputs, such as parse trees, molecule structures, or image segmentations. Our method employs a combination of reinforcement learning and graph neural networks to train an evaluator model that predicts the value of a given output. This value is then used to guide an iterative refinement process, where the output is progressively improved through a series of local edits. Our results demonstrate that Deep Value Networks achieve state-of-the-art performance in several structured output tasks, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The key contribution of this work is the ability to learn a flexible and task-agnostic evaluation metric, enabling the model to adapt to diverse problem domains and output structures. This research has significant implications for applications in natural language processing, computer vision, and computational biology, where high-quality structured outputs are crucial. Key keywords: Deep Value Networks, structured outputs, reinforcement learning, graph neural networks, iterative refinement.
