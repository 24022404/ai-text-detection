Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for leveraging unlabeled data to learn meaningful representations, reducing the reliance on large annotated datasets. However, the performance of SSL methods often depends on the quality of pre-training and the ability to generalize across diverse tasks. This paper proposes a novel framework to enhance SSL by incorporating knowledge transfer techniques, enabling the transfer of learned representations from high-resource domains to low-resource ones. Our approach integrates a multi-stage knowledge distillation process, where a pre-trained teacher model guides the training of a student model, ensuring robust feature extraction and improved generalization. We evaluate our method on benchmark datasets across image classification and natural language processing tasks, demonstrating significant improvements in accuracy and convergence speed compared to traditional SSL methods. The results highlight the effectiveness of knowledge transfer in boosting SSL performance, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. This work contributes to advancing SSL by providing a scalable and efficient solution for knowledge transfer, with potential applications in domains such as healthcare, autonomous systems, and low-resource language processing.  

**Keywords**: Self-supervised learning, knowledge transfer, representation learning, knowledge distillation, low-resource domains, generalization.
