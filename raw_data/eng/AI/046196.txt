This paper presents a comprehensive pipeline for the fair comparison of graph neural networks (GNNs) in node classification tasks. The objective is to provide a standardized framework for evaluating the performance of various GNN architectures, enabling a more accurate assessment of their strengths and weaknesses. Our approach involves a multi-step pipeline, including data preprocessing, model implementation, and evaluation metrics. We implement a range of popular GNN models, including Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and Graph Attention Networks (GATs), and evaluate their performance on a variety of benchmark datasets. Our results show that the choice of GNN architecture and hyperparameters significantly impacts performance, and that our pipeline can effectively identify the most suitable model for a given task. The key findings of this study highlight the importance of careful model selection and hyperparameter tuning in node classification tasks. Our pipeline has significant implications for the development of more accurate and reliable GNN-based systems, and contributes to the advancement of graph neural network research. Key keywords: graph neural networks, node classification, fair comparison, pipeline, GNN architectures.
