In this paper, we address the challenges associated with off-policy reinforcement learning (RL) in environments where rewards are delayed. The objective is to enhance the learning efficiency and decision-making capabilities of RL agents operating under significant temporal lags in reward feedback. Our approach employs an advanced algorithm that integrates temporal difference learning with a novel reward shaping technique, allowing agents to better estimate the value of actions taken in previous time steps. We conducted a series of experiments comparing our proposed method with traditional off-policy algorithms, demonstrating significant improvements in both convergence speed and policy performance across various simulation environments. The results indicate that our algorithm effectively mitigates the issues of delayed reward processing, leading to more robust and agile RL agents. This research contributes valuable insights into the field of RL, emphasizing the importance of considering reward delay in the learning process and paving the way for more adaptive AI systems in real-world applications. Key contributions include improvements in learning efficiency and policy robustness, making our findings applicable to domains such as robotics, finance, and healthcare, where delayed feedback is prevalent. 

Keywords: Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning, Delayed Rewards, Temporal Difference Learning, Reward Shaping, Policy Performance.
