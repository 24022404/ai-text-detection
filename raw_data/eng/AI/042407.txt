Person re-identification (Re-ID) aims to match pedestrian images across different camera views, a crucial task for video surveillance and intelligent security. Current Re-ID methods often struggle with intra-class variations and inter-class similarity, leading to suboptimal performance. This paper introduces a novel Support Neighbor Loss (SNL) to address these challenges by explicitly enforcing tighter clustering of instances belonging to the same identity while simultaneously pushing away instances from different yet visually similar identities. SNL leverages support vectors within a mini-batch to define a more robust neighborhood for each identity, enhancing feature discrimination. We formulate a loss function that minimizes the distances between samples and their support neighbors, and maximizes the distances to support vectors of other identities. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including Market-1501, DukeMTMC-reID, and CUHK03, demonstrate that our proposed SNL significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods. The results show improvements in both rank-1 accuracy and mAP, indicating the effectiveness of SNL in learning discriminative features for person Re-ID. Our findings highlight the potential of support vector-guided loss functions in enhancing Re-ID performance and contribute a novel approach to feature learning for visual recognition tasks.
Keywords: Person Re-identification, Support Vector Machine, Deep Learning, Feature Learning, Metric Learning.
