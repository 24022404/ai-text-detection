This paper addresses the challenge of developing a generic representation framework for random variables in machine learning (ML) applications. Current ML models often struggle with handling diverse types of random variables, leading to inefficiencies and limitations in modeling complex data distributions. To overcome this, we propose a unified approach that leverages advanced mathematical constructs and probabilistic modeling techniques to create a flexible and scalable representation of random variables. Our method integrates key concepts from probability theory, such as moment-generating functions and characteristic functions, with modern ML architectures, enabling seamless adaptation to various data types, including continuous, discrete, and mixed distributions. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework significantly improves model performance across multiple benchmark datasets, outperforming traditional approaches in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. Furthermore, the framework exhibits robustness in handling high-dimensional and sparse data, making it suitable for real-world applications such as financial modeling, healthcare analytics, and natural language processing. This research contributes to the broader ML community by providing a foundational tool for enhancing the interpretability and generalizability of probabilistic models, paving the way for more effective and versatile ML systems.  

**Keywords**: random variables, machine learning, probabilistic modeling, data representation, moment-generating functions, high-dimensional data.
