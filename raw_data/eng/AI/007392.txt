Research on two-dimensional analogs of de Bruijn sequences, termed pseudo-random arrays, has expanded their theoretical and practical impact. In 1976, MacWilliams and Sloane provided a foundational description and deep analysis of these arrays, examining their combinatorial properties. Later, in 1988, Tuvi introduced a new form specifically designed for constructing perfect maps, furthering their utilitarian scope. Bruck Stein advanced the field by combining both de Bruijn and half de Bruijn sequences, which enhanced robustness and enabled efficient self-location. These pseudo-random arrays now play significant roles in modern applications, notably in robust and undetectable digital watermarking and in structured light systems.
