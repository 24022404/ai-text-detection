This paper introduces FIERY, a novel framework for future instance prediction in bird's-eye view (BEV) from surround monocular cameras. The primary objective of this work is to enable autonomous systems to anticipate the future states of dynamic agents, such as vehicles and pedestrians, in a BEV representation, which is critical for safe and efficient navigation. FIERY leverages a multi-camera setup to capture 360-degree views of the environment and employs a deep learning-based approach to predict future trajectories and instance segmentation maps. The proposed method integrates temporal information, spatial context, and semantic understanding to generate accurate and interpretable predictions.  

The framework combines a transformer-based architecture with a probabilistic model to account for uncertainty in future predictions. It processes raw monocular images, extracts features, and fuses them into a unified BEV representation. Experimental results on large-scale autonomous driving datasets demonstrate that FIERY outperforms existing methods in terms of prediction accuracy and robustness. The system achieves state-of-the-art performance in future instance segmentation and trajectory forecasting, with significant improvements in handling occlusions and complex traffic scenarios.  

The key contributions of this research include a scalable and end-to-end trainable model for future instance prediction, a novel probabilistic formulation to address prediction uncertainty, and a comprehensive evaluation on real-world datasets. FIERY's ability to predict future states in BEV has broad implications for autonomous driving, robotics, and surveillance systems, enabling safer and more reliable decision-making in dynamic environments.  

**Keywords**: Bird's-eye view, future prediction, instance segmentation, monocular cameras, autonomous driving, transformer models, probabilistic forecasting.
