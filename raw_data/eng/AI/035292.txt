This paper presents TernaryNet, a novel approach designed to improve the efficiency of deep model inference specifically targeted towards medical 3D segmentation tasks. The primary goal is to facilitate high-performance neural network processing on standard hardware without reliance on GPUs, making advanced medical imaging analysis more accessible.

Methods/Approach: TernaryNet employs a unique combination of sparse and binary convolutions within its architecture, significantly reducing the computational overhead traditionally associated with deep learning models. By using ternary weights, the approach optimizes memory usage and processing time while maintaining accuracy levels. This technique is implemented as a plug-and-play module, allowing integration with existing 3D segmentation frameworks.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrated that TernaryNet achieves comparable accuracy to conventional deep learning models but with vastly reduced computational demands. The system provides faster inference times on CPU-only configurations, making it suitable for resource-constrained environments typically found in medical practices. Comparative analysis reveals TernaryNet's performance exceeds other CPU-based methods while offering considerable speed advantages without sacrificing segmentation quality.

Conclusion/Implications: TernaryNet introduces a pivotal advancement in deep model inference for medical 3D segmentation by eliminating the necessity for GPU resources. The research contributes to ongoing efforts in democratizing access to AI-driven medical imaging technologies, enabling timely diagnostics and cost-effective solutions in healthcare settings. Future work will focus on further optimization and potential real-world deployment, paving the way for widespread adoption of AI-enhanced medical diagnostics.

Keywords: TernaryNet, medical 3D segmentation, deep model inference, sparse convolutions, binary convolutions, GPU-free, CPU optimization, neural network, medical imaging.
