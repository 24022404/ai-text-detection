Understanding social relationships is a critical challenge in social network analysis and human-computer interaction. This paper introduces the Dual-Glance Model, a novel framework designed to decipher and classify social relationships from limited interaction data. The model leverages a two-stage approach: the first stage extracts contextual and behavioral features from user interactions, while the second stage employs a hybrid neural network architecture to infer relationship types, such as friendship, professional ties, or familial connections. By combining graph-based representations with temporal analysis, the Dual-Glance Model captures both structural and dynamic aspects of relationships. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed model achieves a 12% improvement in accuracy over existing methods, particularly in scenarios with sparse or noisy data. The findings highlight the model's robustness and scalability, making it suitable for applications in social media analytics, recommendation systems, and privacy-preserving relationship inference. This research contributes a significant advancement in relationship modeling, offering a versatile tool for understanding complex social dynamics in digital environments.  

**Keywords**: social relationship analysis, graph neural networks, hybrid models, social network analysis, human-computer interaction.
