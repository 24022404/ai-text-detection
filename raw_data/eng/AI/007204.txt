Human parsing, the task of assigning semantic labels to each pixel in an image of a person, is critical for various applications, including virtual try-on and human-computer interaction. Current deep learning approaches often struggle with inaccuracies and inconsistencies, particularly in challenging scenarios such as occlusions and complex poses. To address these limitations, we propose a novel self-learning framework with a rectification strategy. This approach leverages a teacher-student model, where the teacher model generates pseudo-labels for unlabeled data, which are then used to train the student model. Critically, we introduce a rectification module that identifies and corrects noisy pseudo-labels generated by the teacher, enhancing the quality of training data for the student. Our rectification strategy incorporates both pixel-level and region-level consistency checks to refine the pseudo-labels. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our self-learning framework with rectification significantly outperforms existing methods, achieving state-of-the-art results in human parsing accuracy. The proposed approach exhibits improved robustness to noisy annotations and complex scenes, leading to more accurate and consistent parsing results. The findings suggest that self-learning with a well-designed rectification strategy is a promising direction for improving human parsing performance.
Keywords: Human Parsing, Self-Learning, Image Segmentation, Deep Learning, Rectification, Teacher-Student Model.
