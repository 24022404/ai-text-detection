This paper investigates the vulnerability of human perception to 3D adversarial logos designed to cloak individuals from object detectors. We explore the creation of geometrically optimized, physically realizable 3D logo designs that, when placed on or near a person, can significantly reduce the detection rate of state-of-the-art object detection models. Our methodology involves an iterative optimization process that considers both the visual appearance of the logo and its 3D geometry to maximize its adversarial effect across varying viewpoints and lighting conditions. We present experimental results using both simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating the effectiveness of our approach in fooling detectors such as YOLOv5 and Faster R-CNN. Key findings reveal that carefully crafted 3D adversarial logos can effectively cloak humans, highlighting a potential security risk in surveillance systems. This research contributes to understanding the limitations of current object detection systems and emphasizes the need for robust defenses against 3D adversarial attacks. Keywords: 3D Adversarial Attacks, Object Detection, Adversarial Logos, Cloaking, Computer Vision, Security.
