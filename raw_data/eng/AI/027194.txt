Implicit generative models (IGMs), such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), offer a powerful approach to generating complex data distributions. However, training IGMs often suffers from instability and mode collapse due to the implicit nature of the learned distribution and challenges in approximating the true data distribution. This paper investigates a novel approach to improve IGM training by explicitly learning the base distribution from which the generator samples. We propose a method that leverages an auxiliary network to map samples from the generated distribution back to the latent space, effectively learning an approximation of the inverse of the generator. This learned mapping is then used to regularize the generator, encouraging it to maintain a consistent and well-behaved base distribution. We evaluate our approach on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating improved training stability, reduced mode collapse, and superior sample quality compared to traditional IGM training methods. Our findings highlight the importance of explicitly modeling the base distribution in IGMs and offer a promising direction for future research in generative modeling. Keywords: Implicit Generative Models, GANs, Base Distribution, Inverse Mapping, Regularization, Generative Modeling.
