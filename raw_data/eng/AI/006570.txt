**Abstract**

Regulatory compliance and model validation remain critical challenges for implementing ensemble machine learning methods such as bagging in the retail banking sector. This paper introduces XtracTree, a simple yet effective method designed to enhance the transparency and regulatory validation of bagging algorithms, such as Random Forests and Bagged Decision Trees, widely used for credit scoring and risk assessment. XtracTree enables interpretable model auditing by extracting a representative set of decision trees that accurately reflect the underlying ensemble’s predictive logic, while maintaining high model performance. We detail the XtracTree methodology, which systematically selects and analyzes trees using statistical measures of importance and diversity, providing clear, regulator-friendly documentation of ensemble behavior. Experimental evaluations on publicly available retail banking datasets demonstrate that XtracTree achieves regulatory validation objectives with minimal accuracy loss compared to the original bagging models, and significantly improves interpretability and auditability. The results suggest that XtracTree bridges the long-standing gap between model performance and regulatory transparency, enabling financial institutions to deploy robust machine learning models without compromising compliance requirements. This method holds promising implications for broader adoption of AI-driven decision-making in regulated environments. **Keywords:** bagging, interpretability, regulatory compliance, Random Forest, retail banking, explainability, model validation, ensemble learning.
