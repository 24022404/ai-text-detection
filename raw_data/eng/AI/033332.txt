**Abstract:**

Deep visual domain adaptation has emerged as a critical area in computer vision, addressing the practical challenge of transferring knowledge from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled or sparsely labeled target domain with differing data distributions. This survey comprehensively reviews recent advances in deep learning-based domain adaptation techniques, with a primary focus on visual tasks such as image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation. We categorize state-of-the-art methods according to key approaches, including adversarial training, feature alignment, self-supervised learning, and generative models. The survey evaluates each approach in terms of methodology, effectiveness, computational efficiency, and adaptability to various visual domains. Comparative analyses highlight major trends, strengths, and limitations, as well as discussions on benchmark datasets, evaluation protocols, and performance metrics. Furthermore, the paper identifies open challenges—such as domain shift severity, scalability, and generalization—and outlines future directions, including multi-source adaptation, unsupervised and few-shot domain adaptation, and the integration of domain adaptation with other machine learning paradigms. The findings aim to provide researchers and practitioners with a comprehensive understanding of the landscape of deep visual domain adaptation, facilitating informed choices in method selection and inspiring innovative solutions for real-world visual recognition problems.

**Keywords:** domain adaptation, deep learning, computer vision, transfer learning, feature alignment, adversarial learning, visual recognition, unsupervised learning
