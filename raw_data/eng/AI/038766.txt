The paper addresses the challenge of automatic image colorization, particularly in scenarios with limited training data. Traditional deep learning models require large datasets, which are often unavailable, limiting their applicability. This research proposes an innovative approach to image colorization using few-shot learning techniques, enabling effective colorization with minimal data requirements.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a Memory-Augmented Network (MAN) architecture designed to tackle the few-shot image colorization problem. The system incorporates an external memory module that allows the network to leverage previously seen colorization patterns, enhancing its generalization capabilities. Additionally, the model employs a combination of attention mechanisms and feature extraction techniques to efficiently adapt to new images with limited data input.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that our Memory-Augmented Network outperforms existing few-shot and traditional colorization methods on standard benchmarking datasets. The model achieves superior colorization quality and consistency, particularly in scenarios where only a small amount of reference data is available. Comparative analysis shows an improvement in color accuracy and image fidelity, illustrating the effectiveness of the proposed approach.

Conclusion/Implications: The study presents a significant advancement in the field of image colorization by demonstrating that effective colorization can be achieved with limited training data. The Memory-Augmented Networks' ability to utilize previously learned representations offers a promising direction for future research in few-shot learning applications. This approach is particularly beneficial for domains where obtaining extensive labeled data is challenging, such as historical image restoration and niche artistic applications.

Keywords: Few-Shot Learning, Image Colorization, Memory-Augmented Networks, Deep Learning, Attention Mechanisms, Data Efficiency.
