Session-based recommendation (SBR) aims to predict user behavior based on short sequences of interactions, presenting a significant challenge due to the limited data per session. This paper addresses the exploration-exploitation trade-off in SBR by proposing a novel Batch-Constrained Distributional Reinforcement Learning (BC-DRL) framework. Unlike conventional RL methods that struggle with sparse rewards and off-policy learning instability in SBR, our approach learns a distributional representation of Q-values, capturing the uncertainty inherent in user behavior. Furthermore, batch constraints are introduced to mitigate the distributional shift when learning from offline session data, ensuring stable and efficient policy optimization. We empirically evaluate BC-DRL on several benchmark datasets for SBR, demonstrating significant improvements in recommendation accuracy and diversity compared to state-of-the-art SBR models and traditional RL algorithms. The results highlight the effectiveness of distributional RL in handling uncertainty and the importance of batch constraints for off-policy learning in the context of session-based recommendations. This work offers a promising direction for developing more robust and adaptive recommendation systems. Keywords: Session-based Recommendation, Reinforcement Learning, Distributional Reinforcement Learning, Batch-Constrained Learning, Recommendation Systems.
