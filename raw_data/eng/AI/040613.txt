This paper investigates the theoretical underpinnings of product embedding techniques within the realm of e-commerce machine learning. The primary objective is to understand how these embeddings can effectively capture product characteristics and relationships, thereby enhancing recommendation systems and customer insights.

Methods/Approach: We present a comprehensive analysis of various embedding methodologies, including neural embedding frameworks and graph-based approaches. Our study utilizes a combination of unsupervised learning techniques and deep learning models to derive embeddings. We systematically explore different dimensions and representations that contribute to an improved understanding of product interactions within e-commerce platforms.

Results/Findings: Our findings indicate that product embedding significantly enhances the accuracy of machine learning models used for recommendation systems. By mapping products into a high-dimensional space, we observe improved capability in predicting user preferences and enhancing product discovery. Comparative experiments demonstrate that our proposed models outperform traditional collaborative filtering techniques in terms of precision and recall metrics.

Conclusion/Implications: This research presents substantial advancements in the application of product embeddings for e-commerce, offering theoretical insights that could drive further innovation in the field. These embeddings not only optimize current recommendation systems but also open possibilities for new applications such as personalized marketing strategies and dynamic pricing models. Our work underscores the potential of embedding frameworks to revolutionize the way e-commerce systems understand and predict consumer behavior.

Keywords: product embedding, e-commerce, machine learning, recommendation systems, neural networks, graph-based approaches, unsupervised learning.
