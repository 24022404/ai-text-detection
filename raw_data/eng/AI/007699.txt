Reinforcement learning (RL) with rich, high-dimensional observations presents a significant challenge due to the difficulty in extracting relevant state information. This paper introduces a novel approach, Latent State Decoding (LSD), for provably efficient RL in such environments. LSD leverages a learned latent representation of the observation space to decode an approximate state, enabling the application of planning algorithms in the latent space. We provide theoretical guarantees on the sample complexity of LSD-based RL, demonstrating its efficiency under certain assumptions on the latent representation's quality. Empirical evaluations on benchmark tasks with image-based observations show that LSD achieves performance comparable to or exceeding state-of-the-art model-free and model-based RL algorithms, while also offering interpretability through the decoded latent states. Our results highlight the potential of latent state decoding for scaling RL to complex, real-world environments. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Latent State, Representation Learning, Provable Efficiency, High-Dimensional Observations.
