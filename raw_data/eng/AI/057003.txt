Environmental sound classification (ESC) is critical for numerous applications, including smart surveillance, urban monitoring, and assistive technologies. This paper introduces ESResNet, a novel approach that leverages advances in visual domain architectures, specifically the ResNet convolutional neural network, to address challenges in ESC. Unlike traditional methods that rely primarily on audio-specific feature extraction, ESResNet converts audio signals into spectrogram images, thus enabling the application of deep learning models originally designed for image recognition. The proposed framework processes these visual representations of sound to learn discriminative features, improving classification accuracy and robustness against noise. Extensive experiments were conducted on widely-used ESC benchmarks, demonstrating that ESResNet consistently outperforms conventional audio-based models as well as other image-based neural networks in both accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight the effectiveness of cross-domain model adaptation and suggest significant potential for future integration of visual domain technologies in audio analysis. ESResNet advances the state-of-the-art in environmental sound classification, providing a scalable and generalizable solution suitable for real-world deployments. Key contributions include superior model accuracy, robust feature learning, and an efficient transfer of visual domain techniques to ESC tasks.

Keywords: environmental sound classification, deep learning, ResNet, spectrogram, cross-domain adaptation, audio analysis, convolutional neural networks
