Understanding the complex dynamics of neural activity is essential for advancements in neuroscience and the development of brain-inspired computational models. This paper addresses the challenge of perceiving neuron point processes in the presence of "unknown unknowns"—latent factors or unexpected variability not captured by standard models. We propose a novel data-driven framework that leverages advanced machine learning techniques to analyze and interpret spike train data, enabling robust detection and representation of neuronal events even in the presence of unmodeled uncertainties. Our method utilizes a hybrid approach combining unsupervised learning for anomaly identification with deep generative models to uncover hidden patterns in neuronal firing. Experimental evaluations on synthetic and real-world neural datasets demonstrate significant improvements in accuracy and resilience compared to traditional point process analysis methods, particularly when faced with previously uncharacterized disturbances or data noise. These findings suggest that our approach can enhance the reliability of neural signal interpretation and facilitate new insights in computational neuroscience. The work underscores the importance of data-driven perception, anomaly detection, and model adaptability in handling the complex, noisy, and often incomplete nature of neural recordings. Keywords: neuron point processes, data-driven analysis, unknown unknowns, spike train, machine learning, anomaly detection, computational neuroscience.
