Learning Bayesian Networks (BNs) from observational data is a challenging task, particularly due to the discrete nature of structure learning. This paper introduces a novel approach that leverages continuous optimization to efficiently learn the Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) structure of BNs. Our method reformulates the structure learning problem into a continuous optimization problem by parameterizing the adjacency matrix and introducing a DAG constraint based on its matrix exponential. We employ gradient-based optimization techniques to iteratively update the network structure, guiding it towards a DAG that best fits the data. Results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive or superior performance compared to existing discrete and hybrid methods in terms of structure learning accuracy and computational efficiency. Furthermore, we present a comprehensive analysis of the learned DAGs, highlighting their interpretability and potential for downstream tasks such as causal inference. This work offers a scalable and efficient alternative for learning BNs, contributing to advancements in automated knowledge discovery and reasoning under uncertainty.

**Keywords:** Bayesian Networks, Structure Learning, Continuous Optimization, DAG Constraint, Causal Inference, Gradient-Based Optimization.
