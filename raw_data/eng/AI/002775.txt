The optimization of molecular structures is a critical challenge in fields such as drug discovery, materials science, and chemical engineering. This paper presents a novel approach to molecular optimization using deep reinforcement learning (DRL). The objective of this research is to develop a framework that efficiently explores the vast chemical space to identify molecules with desired properties, such as high binding affinity or stability. We propose a DRL-based model that integrates a graph neural network (GNN) to represent molecular structures and a policy gradient algorithm to guide the optimization process. The model is trained on a diverse dataset of molecular properties and evaluated against state-of-the-art methods, including genetic algorithms and Monte Carlo tree search. Results demonstrate that our approach achieves superior performance in generating optimized molecules, with significant improvements in both computational efficiency and property prediction accuracy. The proposed method also exhibits strong generalization capabilities across different molecular datasets. This research contributes to advancing molecular optimization by leveraging the power of DRL, offering a scalable and adaptable solution for real-world applications in drug design and material discovery. Key innovations include the integration of GNNs for molecular representation and the development of a reward function tailored to multi-objective optimization. The implications of this work extend to accelerating the discovery of novel compounds with tailored properties, paving the way for breakthroughs in computational chemistry.  

**Keywords**: molecular optimization, deep reinforcement learning, graph neural networks, drug discovery, chemical space exploration.
