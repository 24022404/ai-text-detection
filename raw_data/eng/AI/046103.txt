The increasing demands for effective image search systems necessitate advancements that incorporate both visual and linguistic cues. This research introduces TRACE, a novel framework designed to enhance image search capabilities by utilizing text feedback to refine visiolinguistic representations.

Methods/Approach: TRACE employs a multi-stage process to transform, aggregate, and compose visiolinguistic representations. Initially, the model extracts and transforms visual and textual features using advanced neural networks. These features are then aggregated through a cross-modal attention mechanism, allowing for seamless integration of visual cues and text feedback. The final composition step synthesizes a refined representation that aligns closely with the user's search intent.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that TRACE significantly outperforms traditional image search methodologies, particularly in scenarios where textual feedback is provided. The model's ability to dynamically adjust to specific user inputs results in higher precision and recall rates compared to existing benchmarks. Comparative analysis with leading models highlights TRACE's superior performance in accurately retrieving contextually relevant images.

Conclusion/Implications: TRACE represents a substantial contribution to the field of image retrieval, offering an innovative approach to incorporating user feedback into the search process. This research paves the way for more intuitive and responsive image search systems, with potential applications in e-commerce, digital asset management, and multimedia libraries. By enabling a more interactive searching experience, TRACE enhances the relevance of search results and user satisfaction.

Keywords: image search, text feedback, visiolinguistic representations, cross-modal attention, image retrieval system.
