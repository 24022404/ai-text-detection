This paper addresses the challenge of few-shot learning by introducing a novel meta-learning framework for neural architecture search. Our approach, MetaNAS, leverages a meta-learner to efficiently discover high-performing neural architectures tailored for few-shot tasks. Unlike existing methods that rely on hand-crafted architectures or computationally expensive search procedures, MetaNAS learns to predict optimal architectures based on meta-training across a distribution of few-shot learning tasks. We employ a recurrent neural network as the meta-learner to generate candidate architectures, which are then evaluated on a proxy task to estimate their performance. The meta-learner is trained to maximize the performance of the generated architectures. Experimental results on benchmark datasets, including miniImageNet and tieredImageNet, demonstrate that MetaNAS achieves significant improvements in accuracy compared to state-of-the-art few-shot learning algorithms. Furthermore, the learned architectures exhibit strong generalization capabilities to unseen datasets. The proposed method offers a more efficient and effective approach for adapting neural networks to new tasks with limited data. Keywords: Meta-learning, Neural Architecture Search, Few-Shot Learning, MetaNAS, Transfer Learning.
