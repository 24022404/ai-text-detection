Hyperspherical face recognition methods, such as SphereFace, enforce discriminative constraints by normalizing features to lie on a hypersphere. While effective, these methods often suffer from instability during training and suboptimal feature distribution. This paper revisits the SphereFace approach, analyzing its limitations and proposing a refined training strategy to enhance both stability and performance. Our method unifies various aspects of hyperspherical normalization, including margin design and temperature scaling, under a single framework. We introduce a novel adaptive margin adjustment technique that dynamically regulates the margin based on the training progress and feature distribution. Furthermore, we explore different temperature scaling methods to optimize the softmax probability distribution. Empirical results on several benchmark datasets, including LFW, CFP-FP, and AgeDB-30, demonstrate that our revived SphereFace outperforms existing hyperspherical face recognition methods and achieves state-of-the-art performance, while maintaining training stability. The proposed approach offers a robust and effective solution for large-scale face recognition.
Keywords: Face Recognition, Hyperspherical Embedding, SphereFace, Deep Learning, Computer Vision.
