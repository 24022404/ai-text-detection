Spectral methods offer powerful theoretical guarantees and computational efficiency in various machine learning tasks. However, they often lack the representational capacity of deep learning models. Conversely, deep learning excels in complex pattern recognition but can be difficult to analyze theoretically. This paper introduces Spectral Inference Networks (SINs), a novel framework that unifies deep and spectral learning. SINs leverage spectral initialization and parameterization within a deep neural network architecture to inherit the benefits of both paradigms. Specifically, we initialize network weights using solutions from spectral algorithms and constrain subsequent updates to maintain spectral properties. We demonstrate that SINs achieve competitive performance with state-of-the-art deep learning models on benchmark datasets while exhibiting improved robustness to adversarial attacks and interpretability compared to traditional deep networks. Furthermore, we present a theoretical analysis of SINs, providing insights into their convergence properties and generalization bounds. Our findings highlight the potential of SINs to bridge the gap between deep and spectral learning, leading to more robust, interpretable, and theoretically sound machine learning models. Keywords: Spectral Methods, Deep Learning, Neural Networks, Spectral Initialization, Robustness, Interpretability.
