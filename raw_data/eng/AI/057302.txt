Deep transfer learning has emerged as a powerful approach for advancing medical image analysis, enabling models to leverage knowledge from large, general-purpose datasets for domain-specific tasks with limited labeled data. This paper aims to systematically explore and understand the underlying mechanisms that drive the effectiveness of deep transfer learning in the context of medical imaging. We evaluate different transfer learning strategies—including feature extraction, fine-tuning, and layer-wise adaptation—across multiple medical imaging modalities such as MRI, CT, and X-ray. Our methodology involves comprehensive experiments on benchmark datasets, analyzing model performance, feature representation shifts, and domain adaptation processes. The findings reveal that both the selection of source models and the extent of adaptation significantly influence performance, with deeper layers requiring targeted fine-tuning for optimal results in complex medical tasks. Additionally, visualization of learned feature spaces uncovers how knowledge transfer reshapes model representations to better capture medically relevant patterns. The study highlights best practices for deploying transfer learning in medical image analysis, addresses common pitfalls, and suggests pathways for improving generalization to rare diseases or novel imaging scenarios. This work advances the understanding of deep transfer learning mechanisms, providing actionable guidance for researchers and practitioners working at the intersection of artificial intelligence, computer vision, and medical healthcare. Key keywords: deep transfer learning, medical images, domain adaptation, feature extraction, fine-tuning, medical image analysis, AI in healthcare, model interpretability.
