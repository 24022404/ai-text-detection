Ensuring fairness in machine learning models while maintaining their utility and transferability across tasks remains a significant challenge. This paper addresses the problem of learning representations that are both adversarially fair and transferable to downstream tasks. We propose a novel framework that integrates adversarial training with representation learning to minimize bias while preserving task-relevant information. Our approach employs a dual-objective optimization strategy, where one component enforces fairness by minimizing the ability of an adversary to predict sensitive attributes, and the other maximizes the utility of the learned representations for target tasks. Experiments conducted on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves superior fairness-utility trade-offs compared to existing approaches. Additionally, the learned representations exhibit strong transferability across diverse tasks, showcasing their generalizability. The results highlight the potential of our framework for deploying fair and adaptable machine learning systems in real-world applications. Key contributions include a scalable adversarial training mechanism and empirical evidence of improved fairness and transferability. Keywords: adversarial fairness, transfer learning, representation learning, bias mitigation, machine learning.
