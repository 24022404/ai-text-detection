**Abstract:**  
This paper introduces Wavelet Networks, a novel deep learning architecture designed to achieve scale equivariant learning directly from raw audio waveforms. Traditional neural networks for audio processing often struggle to effectively capture features that are invariant or equivariant to temporal scaling, which is critical for robust sound recognition and analysis across diverse inputs. Our approach leverages wavelet transforms as fundamental building blocks within the network, enabling the model to natively encode and process multiscale temporal patterns without relying on handcrafted feature extraction or preprocessing steps. We detail the design of the Wavelet Network, highlighting its integration of continuous wavelet transforms into convolutional layers to ensure scale-aware feature learning.

We evaluate Wavelet Networks on a variety of standard audio classification and event detection benchmarks. Results demonstrate superior performance compared to conventional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and other baseline models, particularly in scenarios involving time-stretched or compressed audio signals. Analysis shows that the proposed architecture offers improved generalization to data with varying temporal resolutions and demonstrates resilience to changes in speed and pitch without additional data augmentation.

Our study underscores the importance of scale equivariance in enabling robust audio learning and establishes Wavelet Networks as a compelling model for end-to-end audio analysis. Potential applications span speech recognition, music information retrieval, and real-time acoustic monitoring. Key contributions include the introduction of a scalable, trainable wavelet-based neural architecture and a thorough empirical validation of its advantages over traditional models.  
**Keywords:** Wavelet Networks, scale equivariance, raw waveform learning, audio classification, deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), multiscale representation.
