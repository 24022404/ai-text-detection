Abstract:

Recognizing complex player actions in ice hockey is a challenging computer vision problem due to rapid player movement, occlusions, and ambiguous interactions. This paper presents a novel temporal action recognition framework that combines pose estimation with optical flow analysis to enhance the detection and classification of hockey actions from video sequences. The proposed method leverages a multi-stream architecture, where one stream extracts skeletal pose keypoints to capture player posture and joint dynamics, while the other processes dense optical flow fields to model motion patterns and velocity information across frames. Both streams are integrated using a temporal fusion network designed to learn discriminative features for action recognition. Extensive experiments were conducted on a newly curated hockey action dataset, demonstrating that our approach significantly outperforms baseline appearance-only and single-modality models in terms of accuracy and robustness under varying lighting and occlusion conditions. The results highlight the complementary strength of pose and motion cues for distinguishing fine-grained actions such as shooting, passing, and checking. This research provides a foundation for advanced sports analytics, autonomous video understanding, and training support tools in ice hockey and other fast-paced team sports. Key contributions include a novel fusion strategy for temporal action recognition and empirical validation of the effectiveness of multi-modal video data. Keywords: action recognition, pose estimation, optical flow, temporal modeling, sports analytics, computer vision.
