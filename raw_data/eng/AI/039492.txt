This paper addresses the challenge of scaling multi-relational learning (MRL) to large datasets, a critical task in modern information systems where relationships between entities are complex and high-dimensional. Traditional MRL methods often struggle with computational efficiency and scalability, limiting their applicability to real-world problems. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel framework based on the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM), which decomposes the optimization problem into smaller, more manageable subproblems. Our approach leverages distributed computing to handle large-scale datasets efficiently while maintaining model accuracy. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves significant improvements in both computational speed and predictive performance compared to state-of-the-art MRL techniques. Furthermore, the framework is shown to be robust across diverse domains, including social network analysis, recommendation systems, and knowledge graph completion. The findings highlight the potential of ADMM-based optimization in enabling scalable multi-relational learning, offering a practical solution for applications requiring real-time processing of complex relational data. This work contributes to advancing the field by providing a scalable, efficient, and versatile approach to MRL, with implications for large-scale data-driven decision-making systems.  

**Keywords**: Multi-Relational Learning, ADMM, Scalability, Distributed Computing, Knowledge Graphs, Optimization.
