This paper presents a novel approach to structure learning of deep neural networks using Q-learning, a model-free reinforcement learning technique. The objective is to automatically determine the optimal architecture of a deep neural network for a given task, eliminating the need for manual design and trial-and-error approaches. Our method utilizes Q-learning to explore the vast space of possible network architectures, with the agent learning to select the most effective layers, connections, and hyperparameters. The results show that our Q-learning-based approach outperforms traditional methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets. Key findings include the ability to adapt to varying problem complexities and datasets, as well as the discovery of novel architectures that surpass human-designed counterparts. The implications of this research are significant, enabling the automated design of deep neural networks for a wide range of applications, including computer vision, natural language processing, and speech recognition. Our approach highlights the potential of reinforcement learning in neural architecture search, paving the way for further innovations in the field of deep learning. Keywords: Q-learning, deep neural networks, structure learning, reinforcement learning, neural architecture search.
