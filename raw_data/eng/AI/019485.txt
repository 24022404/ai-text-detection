Action recognition using 3D body skeletons offers a promising approach for human-computer interaction and video surveillance. However, performance often degrades when applied to new, unseen datasets due to domain shift. This paper introduces a novel Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architecture coupled with a supervised domain adaptation strategy to improve the generalization capability of action recognition systems. The proposed method leverages kernel feature maps generated from 3D skeleton data to enhance the discriminative power of the CNN. A supervised domain adaptation technique, employing maximum mean discrepancy (MMD), is integrated into the CNN training process to minimize the distribution divergence between the source and target domains. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in cross-dataset action recognition. Specifically, the incorporation of kernel feature maps and supervised domain adaptation leads to a substantial increase in accuracy and robustness. This research contributes a more effective and adaptable framework for 3D skeleton-based action recognition, reducing the need for extensive labeled data in new environments.
Keywords: Action Recognition, 3D Skeletons, CNN, Domain Adaptation, Kernel Feature Maps, Supervised Learning.
