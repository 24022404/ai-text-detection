Multiple Object Tracking (MOT) is critical for various applications, yet its vulnerability to adversarial attacks remains underexplored. This paper introduces a novel adversarial attack targeting MOT systems, demonstrating that fooling the detection component alone is insufficient for a successful attack. Our approach crafts adversarial perturbations that not only mislead the detector but also disrupt the tracking association process by manipulating object features and trajectories. We employ a gradient-based optimization method to generate adversarial examples that maximize the tracking errors while maintaining stealthiness. Experiments on benchmark MOT datasets, including MOT17 and MOT20, show a significant drop in tracking performance compared to baseline methods. Specifically, our attack reduces MOTA by over 40% in several scenarios, even when the detection accuracy remains relatively high. These findings highlight the importance of considering the entire MOT pipeline when evaluating adversarial robustness. Our research underscores the need for robust MOT systems that are resilient to sophisticated adversarial manipulations beyond just detection. Keywords: Adversarial Attack, Multiple Object Tracking, Object Detection, Robustness, Computer Vision.
