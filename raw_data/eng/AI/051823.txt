The rapid development of medical imaging technologies has led to the generation of vast amounts of image data, necessitating advanced analysis techniques to improve diagnosis, treatment planning, and patient outcomes. This paper provides a comprehensive review of the use of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for medical image analysis, highlighting their impact, challenges, and future prospects.

Methods/Approach: We systematically survey existing literature on CNN applications across various medical imaging modalities, including MRI, CT, Ultrasound, and X-rays. The review focuses on CNN architectures, such as AlexNet, VGG, and ResNet, and innovative training techniques like transfer learning and data augmentation. We explore domain-specific adaptations and the integration of CNNs with other AI models to enhance diagnostic accuracy.

Results/Findings: Our review reveals that CNNs consistently outperform traditional image analysis methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency, particularly in tasks including image classification, segmentation, and object detection. Notable advancements include enhanced image resolution handling, reduced bias in clinical decision-making, and real-time analysis capabilities. We identify predominant challenges such as the requirement for extensive labeled data and the interpretability of CNN models.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of CNNs into medical image analysis has revolutionized the field, offering transformative potential for personalized medicine and automated diagnostics. Future research should focus on overcoming data scarcity, improving model interpretability, and validating CNN performance across diverse populations and pathologies. This review underscores the substantial contributions of CNNs to medical imaging and encourages ongoing development and collaboration across disciplines to surmount current limitations.

Keywords: Convolutional Neural Networks, Medical Imaging, Image Segmentation, Deep Learning, Automated Diagnostics, Transfer Learning.
