This paper addresses the challenge of leveraging vast, uncurated remote sensing data for effective pre-training of models used in various remote sensing applications. Traditional supervised methods require extensive labeling, which is labor-intensive and costly. Our research explores the potential of unsupervised pre-training from this abundant but uncurated data.

Methods/Approach: We introduce Seasonal Contrast, an unsupervised pre-training framework that utilizes temporal variations and seasonal changes in the remote sensing data. By employing contrastive learning techniques, the framework captures informative visual features without reliance on labeled data. Our approach integrates temporal context and contrasts images across different seasons to enhance the model's ability to discern patterns and changes in diverse environments.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that models pre-trained using the Seasonal Contrast framework outperform those using conventional methods in downstream tasks, such as land cover classification and change detection. Benchmark tests reveal a significant improvement in performance, underscoring the efficacy of our method to generalize well across multiple geographic regions and temporal scenarios.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed Seasonal Contrast approach offers a novel pathway for utilizing uncurated remote sensing data, minimizing the dependency on labeled datasets. This method enhances the scalability and adaptability of remote sensing models, paving the way for more efficient Earth observation and environmental monitoring. Our research not only contributes to the field of remote sensing with novel unsupervised learning strategies but also highlights the untapped potential of seasonal data variations. Key Keywords: unsupervised pre-training, remote sensing, contrastive learning, seasonal data, Earth observation.
