This paper presents a selective overview of the fundamental theories and prominent algorithms in the field of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL). The objective is to synthesize key developments and current trends that address the unique challenges arising when multiple autonomous agents interact in dynamic environments. We begin by outlining the foundational concepts distinguishing MARL from single-agent reinforcement learning, including coordination, competition, scalability, and non-stationarity. Our approach systematically reviews leading MARL paradigms such as cooperative, competitive, and mixed-agent settings, examining core algorithms including value decomposition, policy gradient methods, actor-critic architectures, and emergence of communication protocols. The survey highlights notable theoretical advancements that underpin stability and convergence, as well as practical algorithmic strategies for scalable agent coordination. Comparative discussions illustrate the performance and limitations of representative MARL algorithms across benchmark environments. Concluding, we identify crucial open problems in multi-agent credit assignment, scalability, and generalization, and discuss potential applications in robotics, autonomous systems, distributed control, and complex simulations. This overview aims to provide both newcomers and established researchers with clear insights into the state-of-the-art in MARL, supporting informed algorithm selection and development for future multi-agent AI systems. Key keywords: multi-agent reinforcement learning, MARL, coordination, policy gradient, value decomposition, scalability, autonomous agents.
