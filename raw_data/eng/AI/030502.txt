This paper aims to address the growing necessity for interpretability and explainability within the machine learning (ML) domain, providing a comprehensive overview dubbed as a "mini-tour" through various methodologies that enhance the understanding of complex ML models.

Methods/Approach: We systematically explore several approaches across the machine learning landscape, focusing on techniques that contribute to both interpretability and explainability. Our analysis includes a diversity of models such as decision trees, ensembles, deep learning architectures, and model-agnostic methods like SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) and LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations). We emphasize evaluating these models based on their ability to provide intuitive insights into their decision-making processes.

Results/Findings: Our findings highlight the trade-offs between model complexity and interpretability, demonstrating that while advanced models such as deep neural networks, including transformers, often achieve superior predictive accuracy, they require sophisticated post-hoc interpretation tools to unravel their workings. Conversely, simpler models like decision trees naturally offer greater transparency at the expense of lower optimization power in complex scenarios. The paper showcases case studies where these methods are applied, elucidating their strengths and limitations in practical applications.

Conclusion/Implications: This research provides pivotal insights into the crucial balance between model accuracy and interpretability, advocating for the careful selection of methodologies based on application-specific needs. The study's contributions are significant in informing future ML application designs, particularly in sectors demanding transparent decision-making, such as healthcare and finance. By enhancing understanding, this work fosters greater trust and wider adoption of machine learning systems.

Keywords: interpretability, explainability, machine learning, SHAP, LIME, decision trees, deep learning, transparency, model-agnostic methods.
