This paper investigates the theoretical equivalence between Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) time series models and Variational Bayesian (VB) models. We aim to bridge the gap between these two seemingly disparate approaches for time series analysis, providing a unified perspective on their underlying mechanisms. Our approach involves demonstrating how specific RNN architectures, particularly those with gating mechanisms, can be interpreted as approximate inference schemes within a variational Bayesian framework. We establish a formal mapping between the RNN parameters and the variational parameters, revealing that training an RNN can be seen as optimizing a variational lower bound. The results show that under certain assumptions, the predictive distributions of RNNs closely approximate the posterior distributions obtained from VB models. This equivalence provides insights into the regularization properties of RNNs and suggests novel ways to incorporate prior knowledge into RNN training. This work contributes to a deeper understanding of RNNs and opens avenues for developing more robust and interpretable time series models by leveraging the strengths of both neural networks and Bayesian inference. Keywords: Recurrent Neural Networks, Variational Bayesian Methods, Time Series Analysis, Equivalence, Approximate Inference, Deep Learning.
