High-definition (HD) maps are critical for enabling safe and efficient autonomous driving, providing detailed representations of road layouts, traffic elements, and lane-level geometry. Despite their importance, scalable and automated generation of HD maps remains an open challenge due to the complexity and hierarchical structure of real-world road networks. This paper introduces HDMapGen, a novel hierarchical graph generative model designed to produce realistic and structurally accurate HD maps. Our approach leverages a two-tiered graph architecture, modeling both the macro-level topology of road networks and the micro-level details such as lane connectivity and traffic signal placement. Utilizing advanced graph neural networks and probabilistic generation techniques, HDMapGen can learn from existing map data and generate new, diverse map samples that preserve spatial, topological, and semantic consistency. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that HDMapGen outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on metrics of realism, diversity, and fidelity to true driving environments. The generated maps are validated using both simulation scenarios and machine learning-based map quality assessments, showing strong potential for applications in autonomous vehicle training, simulation environments, and rapid map prototyping. Our model offers a scalable solution for automatic HD map generation and opens new directions for map-based research and development in autonomous systems. Keywords: HD maps, generative models, hierarchical graphs, autonomous driving, graph neural networks, simulation.
