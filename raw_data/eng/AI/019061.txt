This paper addresses the challenge of estimating sparse graphical models from truncated samples, where a significant portion of the data is missing. The objective is to develop efficient statistical methods that can accurately recover the underlying graph structure from incomplete data. We propose a novel approach that combines advanced sampling techniques with state-of-the-art sparse recovery algorithms. Our method, termed Truncated Graphical Lasso, leverages the truncated samples to estimate the graph structure, while incorporating sparsity-inducing penalties to promote accurate recovery of the underlying relationships. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly in scenarios with high levels of truncation. The key findings of this research highlight the importance of accounting for truncation in graphical model estimation and demonstrate the potential of our method for applications in network analysis, gene expression, and social media analysis. Our contributions include the development of a novel statistical framework, efficient algorithms for sparse graph recovery, and insights into the robustness of graphical models to data truncation, with implications for machine learning, data mining, and statistical modeling. Key keywords: sparse graphical models, truncated samples, graph recovery, statistical modeling, machine learning.
