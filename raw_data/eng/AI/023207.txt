This paper introduces a novel approach to decision tree algorithms, specifically designed for classification and regression tasks involving functional data. The objective of our study is to address the challenges posed by functional data, which are complex due to their infinite-dimensional nature, and improve existing methods for their analysis. We propose the Measure Inducing Classification and Regression Trees (MICaRT) method, which leverages measure theory principles to enhance tree structure by effectively handling the function domain's continuity and variability. Our approach uses an innovative measure-based split criterion that optimizes decision boundaries in the functional space, providing significant improvements over traditional methods. Experimental results demonstrate that MICaRT surpasses conventional decision trees and other contemporary functional data analysis techniques in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency across various benchmark datasets. The implications of our study extend to domains that utilize functional data, such as bioinformatics, meteorology, and finance, enhancing predictive models' interpretability and adaptability. The significant contribution of this research lies in integrating measure theory within tree-based models, offering a robust framework for future exploration and application in handling functional data. Key keywords include functional data analysis (FDA), decision trees, measure theory, classification, regression, and data modeling.
