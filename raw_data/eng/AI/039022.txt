Integrating sensing functionality into cellular networks promises enhanced situational awareness for applications such as vehicle-to-everything (V2X), industrial automation, and smart cities. This paper investigates the joint design of communication and sensing over 5G New Radio (NR) sidelink interfaces, aiming to leverage existing sidelink resources for dual-use operation without compromising throughput. We propose a unified framework that incorporates embedded radar-style waveform patterns within sidelink physical channels, alongside a low-complexity resource allocation algorithm that jointly schedules sensing and data traffic while mitigating mutual interference. Our approach employs matched-filter based detection and angle-of-arrival estimation to extract range and velocity information from sidelink reference signals, and integrates these sensing measurements into the sidelink scheduling process via a priority-aware optimization model. Simulation results in highway and urban scenarios demonstrate that the proposed scheme achieves centimeter-level ranging accuracy (mean error <0.5 m) and over 90% detection probability at practical signal-to-noise ratios, while incurring less than 5% degradation in communication throughput compared to standard sidelink operation. These findings validate the feasibility of NR sidelink as a cost-effective platform for joint sensing and communication. The proposed methods offer a path toward holistic cellular architectures that can support emerging autonomous and safety-critical services.  
Keywords: 5G NR, sidelink, joint sensing and communication, V2X, resource allocation, waveform design.
