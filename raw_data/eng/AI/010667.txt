Objective:  
Retinal image quality assessment is crucial for the accurate diagnosis and automated analysis of ophthalmic diseases. This study investigates the effectiveness of deep learning-based image quality assessment networks when applied to retinal images represented in different color-spaces, aiming to understand how color-space transformations impact model performance.  

Methods:  
We evaluated several state-of-the-art convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures for retinal image quality assessment using common color-spaces, including RGB, HSV, YCbCr, and CIE Lab. A comprehensive dataset of labeled retinal fundus images was preprocessed into these color-spaces, and each model was trained and validated accordingly. Performance metrics such as accuracy, F1-score, precision, recall, and area under the ROC curve were used to systematically compare results among the color-space representations.  

Results:  
The experimental findings reveal that color-space selection significantly affects the performance of quality assessment networks. Networks trained on images in the CIE Lab and YCbCr color-spaces demonstrated improved sensitivity and overall accuracy in detecting poor-quality images compared to those trained on standard RGB representations. The HSV color-space also provided moderate performance gains, especially in detecting illumination issues and image artifacts.  

Conclusion:  
Our study highlights the importance of selecting an appropriate color-space in designing retinal image quality assessment networks. Utilizing alternate color-spaces such as CIE Lab and YCbCr can enhance the discriminatory power of AI-based assessment methods, enabling more reliable pre-screening for automated ophthalmic analysis. These insights can inform the development of robust, scalable AI systems for medical image quality assurance in teleophthalmology and clinical practice.  

Keywords: retinal image quality, deep learning, color-space, CNN, fundus images, medical imaging, AI assessment, image preprocessing.
