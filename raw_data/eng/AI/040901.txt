Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms often face challenges in exploration-exploitation trade-offs, particularly in environments with sparse or deceptive rewards. This paper introduces *Adaptive Symmetric Reward Noising* (ASRN), a novel technique designed to enhance exploration by strategically perturbing reward signals during training. The proposed method dynamically adjusts the magnitude and symmetry of reward noise based on the agent's learning progress, ensuring a balance between exploration and exploitation. ASRN leverages a lightweight adaptive mechanism that requires minimal computational overhead, making it suitable for a wide range of RL applications. Experiments conducted on benchmark environments, including sparse-reward tasks and complex control problems, demonstrate that ASRN significantly improves convergence rates and final performance compared to traditional exploration strategies such as epsilon-greedy and intrinsic motivation methods. The results highlight the robustness of ASRN in mitigating local optima and accelerating learning in challenging scenarios. This work contributes to the RL community by providing a simple yet effective approach to reward shaping, with potential applications in robotics, game AI, and autonomous systems. Key innovations include the adaptive noise mechanism and its symmetric design, which ensures unbiased exploration. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Reward Noising, Exploration Strategies, Adaptive Algorithms, Sparse Rewards.
