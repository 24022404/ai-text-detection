This paper introduces ADA, a novel game-theoretic approach to data augmentation in the field of object detection. Data augmentation is crucial for enhancing the performance of object detection models by diversifying and enriching the training dataset. The primary objective of ADA is to model data augmentation as a strategic game between an augmenter, which generates augmented data, and a detector, which aims to accurately identify objects in augmented images. By leveraging game theory, the proposed method strategically balances augmentation strategies to maximize detector performance, rather than relying on traditional heuristic-based methods. The approach involves defining a payoff structure to evaluate the impact of different augmentation strategies on detection accuracy. Our experiments demonstrate that ADA outperforms conventional data augmentation techniques, yielding significant improvements in detection results across diverse datasets and model architectures. These findings suggest that incorporating a game-theoretic perspective enhances the robustness and effectiveness of object detection systems by considering the complex interplay between data augmentation processes and detection objectives. The implications of this research extend to the development of more adaptive augmentation frameworks in AI applications, potentially affecting fields such as autonomous driving and surveillance systems. Key keywords for this study include data augmentation, game theory, object detection, strategic game, and performance optimization.
