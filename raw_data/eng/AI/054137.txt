This paper investigates the spectral properties underpinning the widely utilized word representation model, word2vec. It aims to enhance understanding of how word2vec’s architecture and training process facilitate effective natural language processing tasks, focusing on the spectral decomposition of the matrices utilized in the model.

Methods: We explore the mathematical foundations of word2vec by leveraging techniques in spectral graph theory and linear algebra to dissect the inner workings of both Continuous Bag of Words (CBOW) and Skip-gram architectures. Emphasis is placed on analyzing eigenvalue distributions and eigenspace structures to provide insights into the embeddings' ability to capture semantic relationships.

Results: Our analysis reveals that the spectral characteristics of word2vec embeddings explain their proficiency in preserving semantic similarities and relationships between words in the vector space. Specifically, the study uncovers how the dominant eigenspaces serve to enhance the positioning of synonymous words and highlight conjugate word pairs. Furthermore, comparative studies show that word2vec outperforms equivalent models in terms of capturing nuanced semantic meanings when evaluated using spectral analysis metrics.

Conclusion: The spectral understanding of word2vec contributes significant theoretical clarity to its empirical effectiveness and offers promising directions for further optimization of word embeddings. These insights can pave the way for developing more robust, efficient models that harness spectral properties for improved semantic representation. Potential applications of this research extend to enhanced information retrieval systems, advanced language models, and the development of more interpretable AI systems.

Keywords: word2vec, spectral graph theory, semantic embeddings, natural language processing, vector space, eigenvalues, eigenvectors.
