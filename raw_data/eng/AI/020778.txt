Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (SNE) is a widely used dimensionality reduction technique for visualizing high-dimensional data in low-dimensional spaces. This paper provides a comprehensive tutorial and survey on SNE, focusing on its two prominent variants: SNE with Gaussian distributions (G-SNE) and SNE with Student-t distributions (t-SNE). The objective of this work is to elucidate the theoretical foundations, algorithmic implementations, and practical applications of these methods, while highlighting their strengths and limitations. We begin by explaining the core principles of SNE, including the probabilistic modeling of pairwise similarities in high- and low-dimensional spaces. Next, we detail the optimization techniques employed in G-SNE and t-SNE, emphasizing the role of gradient descent and the impact of hyperparameters such as perplexity. Through comparative analysis, we demonstrate how t-SNE, with its heavy-tailed Student-t distribution, mitigates the crowding problem inherent in G-SNE, leading to improved visualization quality. Additionally, we discuss recent advancements, such as accelerated t-SNE variants and hybrid approaches, which enhance computational efficiency and scalability. The paper concludes with insights into the practical implications of SNE for diverse applications, including bioinformatics, image processing, and natural language processing. By synthesizing existing research and offering a clear, accessible guide, this work serves as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners seeking to leverage SNE for exploratory data analysis and visualization.  

**Keywords**: Stochastic Neighbor Embedding, t-SNE, dimensionality reduction, data visualization, Gaussian distribution, Student-t distribution, machine learning.
