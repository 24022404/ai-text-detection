This research addresses the challenge of accurately estimating 3D human poses from video sequences, a critical task in areas such as computer vision, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction. The study introduces a novel approach to improve precision and computational efficiency in pose estimation.

Methods/Approach: Our proposed method employs an attention-based neural network architecture integrated with dilated convolutions. The attention mechanism enhances the model's ability to focus on relevant spatial and temporal information within video frames, while dilated convolutions effectively capture multi-scale features by preserving fine-grained details through expansive receptive fields.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques in 3D pose estimation accuracy across multiple benchmark datasets. Our model shows notable improvements in handling occlusions and varied lighting conditions. Further analysis reveals a marked reduction in computational overhead, making it feasible for real-time applications.

Conclusion/Implications: The study contributes a scalable and robust solution for 3D human pose estimation, with potential applications in augmented reality, motion analysis, and interactive gaming. By harnessing attention mechanisms and dilated convolutions, the proposed method offers a substantial advancement in the fidelity and efficiency of pose estimation technology.

Keywords: 3D Human Pose Estimation, Attention-Based Neural Network, Dilated Convolutions, Video Analysis, Computer Vision.
