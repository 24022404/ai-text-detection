This paper explores the deployment of machine learning capabilities on microcontroller-based systems, a growing need in the realm of Internet of Things (IoT) applications. We introduce TensorFlow Lite Micro (TFLM), a variant of TensorFlow Lite, tailored for constrained environments such as TinyML systems, enabling the execution of machine learning models on devices with limited computational resources.

Methods/Approach: The research outlines the architecture of TensorFlow Lite Micro, emphasizing its lightweight design and portability across diverse microcontrollers. Key optimizations include selective kernel implementation, model quantization, and minimized memory footprint strategies to ensure feasibility on devices with restricted RAM and storage. To assess its practicality, TFLM is integrated and tested on a variety of TinyML hardware platforms.

Results/Findings: The implementation of TensorFlow Lite Micro demonstrates the successful execution of neural network models with comparable accuracy to traditional systems while maintaining a significantly reduced resource consumption. Benchmarking against other machine learning frameworks reveals that TFLM provides superior performance in terms of latency and memory usage, across several embedded devices, proving its efficiency and adaptability.

Conclusion/Implications: By enabling robust machine learning inference on microcontroller-based ecosystems, TensorFlow Lite Micro bridges the gap between high computational demand of machine learning models and the physical limitations of embedded systems. This work paves the way for expanded applications of machine learning across remote and resource-constrained environments, facilitating advancements in the domains such as ambient intelligence, wearable technology, and real-time data processing. Keywords: TensorFlow Lite Micro, TinyML, Embedded Systems, Machine Learning, IoT, Microcontrollers, Model Quantization.
