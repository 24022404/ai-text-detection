Implicit generative models have gained significant attention for their ability to model complex data distributions without explicitly defining a likelihood function. However, a critical challenge lies in effectively learning the base distribution, which serves as the foundation for generating high-quality samples. This paper addresses this challenge by proposing a novel framework for optimizing the base distribution in implicit generative models. Our approach leverages a combination of adaptive sampling techniques and gradient-based optimization to refine the base distribution, ensuring it aligns more closely with the target data distribution. We evaluate the proposed method on benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in sample quality and diversity compared to existing approaches. Additionally, our framework exhibits enhanced stability during training, reducing common issues such as mode collapse. The results highlight the importance of base distribution learning in implicit generative models and provide a robust foundation for future research in this area. This work contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in generative modeling, with potential applications in image synthesis, data augmentation, and anomaly detection.  

**Keywords**: implicit generative models, base distribution, adaptive sampling, gradient-based optimization, sample quality, mode collapse.
