This paper introduces Conditional Contrastive Learning, a novel approach designed to refine self-supervised representations by selectively removing undesirable information. The objective is to enhance the quality and specificity of learned features, making them more suitable for downstream tasks. Our method leverages a conditional contrastive loss function that adaptively focuses on the most relevant aspects of the data, filtering out irrelevant or nuisance factors. By employing this targeted learning strategy, we demonstrate significant improvements in representation quality, particularly in scenarios where unwanted variability is present. Experimental results show that our approach outperforms existing self-supervised learning techniques, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets. The key contributions of this work include the development of a flexible and efficient framework for conditional contrastive learning, as well as insights into the importance of selectively removing undesirable information in self-supervised representations. Our research has important implications for applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and other areas where self-supervised learning is crucial, with relevant keywords including self-supervised learning, contrastive learning, representation learning, and conditional modeling.
