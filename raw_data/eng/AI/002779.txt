This paper presents an information-theoretic framework for analyzing the credit assignment problem in reinforcement learning (RL), a fundamental challenge in which agents must appropriately attribute received rewards to their prior actions or decisions. We propose a novel approach that models the flow and preservation of information about rewards through agent-environment interactions using entropy-based measures. Our methodology leverages mutual information to quantify the influence of individual actions on future outcomes, providing a principled measure of credit assignment efficiency. Through theoretical analyses and empirical validation on benchmark RL environments, we demonstrate that our information-theoretic criteria reveal key limitations of traditional gradient-based and temporal difference learning algorithms, particularly in scenarios with sparse or delayed rewards. Experimental results highlight that integrating information-aware strategies improves both learning speed and final policy performance. The findings offer new insights into the design of RL algorithms with enhanced credit assignment mechanisms and suggest broader applications of information theory to sequential decision-making problems. Keywords: reinforcement learning, credit assignment, information theory, mutual information, reward propagation, policy optimization.
