This paper investigates the sample complexity of actor-critic (AC) and direct policy search (DPS) algorithms in reinforcement learning. While both approaches aim to optimize a policy, AC methods learn a value function to guide policy updates, whereas DPS methods directly optimize the policy parameters. This study provides a comparative analysis of the sample efficiency of AC and DPS algorithms, focusing on the number of samples required to achieve a near-optimal policy. We analyze the theoretical sample complexities of both algorithm classes under specific assumptions regarding the policy parameterization and the Markov Decision Process. Furthermore, we present empirical results from benchmark environments, validating our theoretical findings and demonstrating the trade-offs between the two approaches. Our analysis suggests that while AC methods can potentially achieve better sample complexity under certain conditions, DPS methods can be more robust in high-dimensional policy spaces. The findings offer practical guidelines for algorithm selection based on the characteristics of the reinforcement learning problem. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Actor-Critic, Direct Policy Search, Sample Complexity, Policy Optimization.
