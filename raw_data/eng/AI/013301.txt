**Abstract:**

The rapid advancement of large-scale language models has transformed natural language processing, yet the underlying mechanisms that govern the effectiveness of fine-tuning these models remain insufficiently understood. This paper investigates the role of intrinsic dimensionality in explaining why fine-tuning pre-trained language models yields strong performance, often with limited task-specific data. Employing a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical evaluation, we quantify the intrinsic dimensionality of parameter updates during fine-tuning across a range of tasks and model sizes. Our experiments leverage state-of-the-art transformer-based language models, measuring how variations in intrinsic dimensionality correlate with downstream task performance and generalization. Results demonstrate that successful fine-tuning consistently operates in a low-dimensional subspace, regardless of the complexity of the target task or the scale of the model. Furthermore, we show that explicitly constraining fine-tuning to subspaces of low intrinsic dimensionality preserves or even enhances performance, offering evidence for a new perspective on transfer learning efficiency. These findings imply that the parameter space of language models is highly redundant for most adaptation tasks, and intrinsic dimensionality serves as a key explanatory factor for fine-tuning success. Our work provides new insights into model adaptation, supports the development of more efficient fine-tuning methodologies, and has broad implications for scalable deployment of language models in real-world applications. **Keywords:** intrinsic dimensionality, language model, fine-tuning, transfer learning, transformer, NLP, model adaptation.
