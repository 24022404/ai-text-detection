This paper explores the theoretical foundations of neural networks through the lens of reproducing kernel Banach spaces (RKBS), offering a novel perspective on their mathematical properties and behavior. The objective is to bridge the gap between practical neural network applications and their underlying theoretical frameworks, providing deeper insights into their approximation capabilities and generalization performance. We propose a methodological approach that leverages RKBS to analyze the structure and dynamics of neural networks, focusing on their ability to model complex, high-dimensional data. Our findings demonstrate that RKBS provides a robust framework for understanding the expressiveness and stability of neural networks, particularly in scenarios involving non-Euclidean data spaces. The results highlight significant improvements in interpretability and theoretical guarantees compared to traditional reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) approaches. This research contributes to the broader understanding of neural networks by offering a mathematically rigorous framework that enhances their design and optimization. The implications extend to applications in machine learning, computer vision, and data science, where improved theoretical insights can lead to more efficient and reliable models. Keywords: neural networks, reproducing kernel Banach spaces, approximation theory, machine learning, non-Euclidean data.
