This study investigates the efficacy of machine learning techniques, specifically undersampling and bagging of decision trees, for predicting extubation readiness in extremely preterm infants based on cardiorespiratory behavior analysis. Accurate prediction of extubation success is crucial for minimizing respiratory support duration and improving neonatal outcomes. We propose a novel approach combining undersampling to address class imbalance in the dataset with an ensemble bagging method applied to decision trees to enhance predictive performance and robustness. Cardiorespiratory data, including heart rate, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation, were collected from a cohort of extremely preterm infants. The proposed method was compared to traditional decision tree models and other machine learning classifiers using metrics such as accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity. Results indicate that the undersampled bagged decision tree model significantly outperforms other approaches in predicting extubation readiness, achieving a higher balance between sensitivity and specificity. This research demonstrates the potential of machine learning to assist clinicians in making informed decisions regarding extubation, ultimately contributing to improved respiratory management and reduced morbidity in extremely preterm infants. Keywords: Extubation Readiness, Preterm Infants, Cardiorespiratory Monitoring, Decision Trees, Bagging, Undersampling, Machine Learning.
