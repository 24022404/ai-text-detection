This paper addresses the challenge of learning and planning in complex action spaces, where the dimensionality and intricacy of potential actions hinder efficient exploration and optimization. We propose a hierarchical reinforcement learning approach that combines action space abstraction with model-based planning. Specifically, we introduce a novel method for automatically learning a lower-dimensional, abstract action space from high-dimensional raw actions using a variational autoencoder conditioned on state information. This learned action space enables more efficient exploration and planning. We then integrate this abstract action space with a learned dynamics model to perform model-based planning, allowing the agent to reason about long-term consequences of its actions. Experimental results across a range of challenging continuous control tasks demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing model-free and model-based reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly in environments with sparse rewards and high-dimensional action spaces. The key contribution lies in the synergistic combination of learned action space abstraction and model-based planning, resulting in improved sample efficiency and performance. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Action Space Abstraction, Model-Based Planning, Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning, Continuous Control.
