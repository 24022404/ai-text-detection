This paper addresses the challenge of ranking structured objects—such as molecules, social network subgraphs, and semantic graphs—by leveraging the capabilities of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). Traditional ranking methods often overlook the rich relational information inherent in structured data, leading to suboptimal performance in tasks where the relationships between elements are crucial. In this study, we propose a novel framework that represents structured objects as graphs and employs advanced GNN architectures to learn effective object representations for ranking purposes. Our approach models both node-level and structural features, capturing local and global graph properties relevant to the ranking task. We evaluate the proposed method on multiple benchmark datasets across diverse domains, demonstrating significant improvements in ranking accuracy and robustness compared to conventional algorithms and deep learning baselines. The results highlight the effectiveness of graph-based learning for structured ranking problems and underline the adaptability of our model to various application settings. Our work advances the integration of graph neural networks in ranking pipelines, providing a generalizable solution for domains where data structure plays a pivotal role. Keywords: graph neural networks, structured objects, ranking, representation learning, graph-based models.
