This paper presents a novel approach to human pose estimation by jointly training a convolutional neural network (CNN) and a graphical model. The objective is to accurately localize body joints in images and videos, addressing the challenges of occlusion, variability, and complexity in human poses. Our method combines the strengths of CNNs in feature extraction and graphical models in structural representation, enabling the capture of both appearance and spatial relationships between body parts. The joint training framework allows for the optimization of both components simultaneously, leading to improved performance and robustness. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in benchmark datasets. The key contributions of this work include the development of a unified framework for pose estimation, the introduction of a novel loss function that balances appearance and structural cues, and the achievement of superior accuracy and efficiency. This research has significant implications for applications in computer vision, human-computer interaction, and robotics, with potential extensions to related tasks such as action recognition and tracking. Key keywords: human pose estimation, convolutional neural networks, graphical models, joint training, computer vision.
