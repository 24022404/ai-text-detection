Unsupervised action recognition remains a challenging task due to the lack of labeled data. This paper introduces a novel framework, Augmented Skeleton Based Contrastive Action Learning with Momentum LSTM (AS-CAL), designed to address this challenge by leveraging unlabeled skeleton sequence data. Our approach uses a contrastive learning strategy to learn robust action representations by maximizing the similarity between different augmented views of the same action while minimizing similarity between different actions. Specifically, we employ skeleton augmentation techniques including random joint removal, temporal warping, and noise injection to generate diverse views. A Momentum LSTM network is used to encode the augmented skeleton sequences into feature embeddings. The contrastive loss function then encourages the network to learn discriminative features. We evaluate AS-CAL on several benchmark datasets for action recognition. Experimental results demonstrate that AS-CAL outperforms state-of-the-art unsupervised methods, achieving significant improvements in clustering accuracy and normalized mutual information. The learned representations also show good generalization ability when transferred to downstream tasks. Our work provides a promising direction for unsupervised action recognition, reducing the reliance on costly labeled data.

**Keywords:** Unsupervised Action Recognition, Contrastive Learning, Skeleton Data, LSTM, Momentum, Data Augmentation.
