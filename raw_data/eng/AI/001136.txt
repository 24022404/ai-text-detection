This paper introduces Regression Prior Networks, a novel approach for enhancing uncertainty estimation within regression tasks using deep learning models. Traditional regression models often struggle to provide calibrated uncertainty measures, which are critical for robust decision-making in applications such as autonomous systems, finance, and healthcare. To address this challenge, our method integrates a prior probability distribution over regression outputs directly into the network architecture, guiding the model towards more accurate representation of prediction uncertainty. We propose a framework that adapts prior networks—originally developed for classification—by leveraging continuous target distributions and tailored loss functions suitable for regression scenarios. Experiments are conducted across multiple real-world datasets to compare our approach with standard regression techniques, ensembling methods, and Bayesian neural networks. The results demonstrate that Regression Prior Networks significantly improve both the sharpness and calibration of predictive uncertainties without substantial computational overhead. Our findings suggest that this approach effectively distinguishes between epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty, providing reliable confidence estimates across both in-distribution and out-of-distribution samples. The proposed method is scalable, easily implemented, and robust, making it broadly applicable for safety-critical and high-stakes regression problems. Key contributions include an original network architecture for prior-informed regression, comprehensive experimental validation, and new insights into uncertainty quantification in deep learning regression models. Keywords: regression, uncertainty estimation, prior networks, deep learning, out-of-distribution detection, confidence calibration, machine learning.
