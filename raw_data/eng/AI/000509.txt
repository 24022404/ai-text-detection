This paper introduces Extended Isolation Forest (EIF), a novel anomaly detection algorithm designed to overcome limitations inherent in the standard Isolation Forest (IF) algorithm. While IF demonstrates effectiveness in identifying anomalies, its performance degrades when anomalies are masked by the axis-parallel splits used in its tree construction. EIF addresses this challenge by incorporating oblique splits, allowing the algorithm to better isolate anomalies lying on non-axis-aligned subspaces. We achieve this by extending the splitting criteria of the isolation trees to consider random linear combinations of features, thereby improving the ability to detect anomalies in high-dimensional data. Empirical evaluation on several benchmark datasets demonstrates that EIF consistently outperforms IF and other state-of-the-art anomaly detection methods, particularly in scenarios with complex, correlated features. The results highlight EIF's enhanced sensitivity and precision in anomaly detection, offering a significant improvement over existing techniques. Keywords: Anomaly Detection, Isolation Forest, Oblique Splits, High-Dimensional Data, Outlier Detection.
