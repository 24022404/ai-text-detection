This paper presents an innovative approach to decomposing 3D scenes into individual objects using unsupervised volume segmentation. The objective is to enable the automatic separation of objects within complex 3D environments without prior knowledge or labeling. Our method leverages a novel combination of 3D convolutional neural networks and clustering algorithms to segment volumetric data into distinct objects. The approach is validated through extensive experiments on various 3D datasets, demonstrating superior performance in terms of object separation accuracy and robustness to noise and occlusions. Key findings include the ability to handle complex scenes with multiple objects, varying densities, and diverse shapes. The proposed technique has significant implications for applications such as 3D reconstruction, robotics, and computer-aided design, offering a unique solution for unsupervised 3D scene understanding. By introducing a new paradigm for object segmentation in 3D spaces, this research contributes to the advancement of 3D vision and scene analysis, with potential applications in fields like autonomous systems, virtual reality, and architectural modeling. Relevant keywords: 3D scene decomposition, unsupervised volume segmentation, 3D convolutional neural networks, clustering algorithms, object separation, 3D vision.
