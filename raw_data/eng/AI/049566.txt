This paper introduces a novel approach to sign language recognition (SLR) leveraging pose estimation, Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN), and Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT). Addressing the challenges of visual ambiguity and sequential dependencies in sign language, our method utilizes pose estimation to extract skeletal keypoints from video frames, representing sign articulations. These keypoints are then fed into a GCN to model the spatial relationships between joints within each frame. Subsequently, BERT is employed to capture the temporal context and dependencies across the sequence of GCN-processed frames. The proposed GCN-BERT framework outperforms existing methods on benchmark datasets, demonstrating improved accuracy and robustness in recognizing complex sign language gestures. The results highlight the effectiveness of combining spatial and temporal modeling for SLR, offering a promising direction for real-time communication and accessibility applications. Keywords: Sign Language Recognition, Pose Estimation, Graph Convolutional Networks, BERT, Deep Learning.
