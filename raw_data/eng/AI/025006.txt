This study aims to investigate the effectiveness of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in jointly detecting objects and estimating their poses in images. To achieve this, we employ a range of state-of-the-art CNN architectures and compare their performance on benchmark datasets. Our approach involves modifying existing object detection models to incorporate pose estimation capabilities, leveraging techniques such as keypoint detection and regression. The results show that our proposed models achieve competitive accuracy in both object detection and pose estimation tasks, outperforming traditional methods that address these tasks separately. Notably, our best-performing model achieves a significant improvement in pose estimation accuracy, demonstrating the benefits of joint learning. This research contributes to the development of more accurate and efficient computer vision systems, with potential applications in areas such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and surveillance. Key findings highlight the importance of architecture design and training strategies in achieving optimal performance. Overall, this study provides a comprehensive comparison of CNN-based approaches for joint object detection and pose estimation, offering insights into the strengths and limitations of current methods and identifying avenues for future research. Keywords: Convolutional Neural Networks, object detection, pose estimation, computer vision, deep learning.
