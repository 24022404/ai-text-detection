This paper introduces a novel approach to deep reinforcement learning (DRL) by incorporating a surrogate agent-environment interface. Traditional DRL methods often suffer from sample inefficiency and instability due to the complex interactions between the agent and the environment. To address these challenges, we propose a surrogate model that learns to mimic the environment's dynamics, providing the agent with a more stable and informative training signal. Our method utilizes a deep neural network to approximate the environment's state transition function, enabling the agent to learn policies more effectively through interactions with this learned surrogate. Experimental results on a suite of benchmark tasks demonstrate that our approach significantly improves sample efficiency and robustness compared to state-of-the-art DRL algorithms. Furthermore, we analyze the properties of the learned surrogate and show that it captures essential aspects of the environment's behavior, leading to improved policy generalization. This work contributes a practical and effective technique for enhancing DRL performance in complex environments. Keywords: Deep Reinforcement Learning, Surrogate Model, Agent-Environment Interaction, Sample Efficiency, Policy Generalization.
