This paper addresses the challenge of accurately documenting and preserving cultural heritage objects through advanced 3D digitization techniques. The primary objective is to develop a robust data fusion framework that integrates data acquired from laser scanning, structured light, and photogrammetry, each offering unique advantages in terms of precision, texture capture, and flexibility. The proposed approach involves the collection of multi-source data from representative cultural artifacts, followed by preprocessing, registration, and fusion using a hybrid algorithm that leverages the strengths of point cloud alignment and texture mapping. Experimental results demonstrate that the combined dataset yields higher geometric fidelity and richer surface detail compared to any single-method reconstruction. Quantitative assessments, including surface deviation analysis and visual completeness, confirm significant enhancements in data quality. The research further explores the practical implications for digital archiving, conservation planning, and virtual exhibition of cultural heritage. By providing an efficient technique for comprehensive 3D reconstruction, this study contributes a valuable tool for museums, archaeologists, and conservationists, facilitating more accurate documentation and broader public access to heritage objects. Key keywords include data fusion, laser scanning, structured light, photogrammetry, 3D reconstruction, and cultural heritage digitization.
