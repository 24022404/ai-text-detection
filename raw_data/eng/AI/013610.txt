This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for anchorless object detection, focusing on the transfer of knowledge from synthetic to real-world images. While synthetic data offers scalability and precise annotations, the domain gap between synthetic and real images often degrades model performance in real-world scenarios. To bridge this gap, we propose a novel UDA framework that leverages adversarial training and feature alignment techniques to adapt anchorless object detection models effectively. Our approach introduces a domain discriminator to align feature distributions between synthetic and real domains, coupled with a self-training mechanism to refine pseudo-labels for unlabeled real data. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing domain adaptation techniques in terms of detection accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the potential of our framework for real-world applications where labeled real data is scarce or unavailable. This work contributes to advancing domain adaptation in anchorless object detection, offering a scalable solution for deploying models trained on synthetic data in diverse real-world environments.  

**Keywords**: Unsupervised Domain Adaptation, Anchorless Object Detection, Synthetic-to-Real Transfer, Adversarial Training, Feature Alignment, Self-Training.
