This paper presents R4Dyn, a novel approach utilizing radar technology for self-supervised monocular depth estimation in dynamic scenes. Traditional methods often struggle with the inherent challenges posed by dynamic environments, such as object motion and varying lighting conditions. Our research seeks to address these issues by integrating radar data to enhance depth estimation accuracy and robustness.

Methods/Approach: R4Dyn leverages the complementary properties of radar signals alongside monocular vision to improve scene understanding. The model uses a self-supervised learning framework that does not rely on annotated depth data, instead learning from the spatial and temporal coherence present within radar and visual inputs. The technique involves the fusion of radar-derived measurements with visual data through a neural network trained to optimize depth prediction in dynamic settings.

Results/Findings: Our findings demonstrate that R4Dyn significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art monocular depth estimation models in dynamic scenes, particularly in situations involving rapid object motion or low lighting. Tests reveal notable improvements in prediction accuracy, confirming the efficacy of integrating radar data into the depth estimation process. Comparative analyses also show that R4Dyn maintains robustness across a range of environments, highlighting its adaptability and generalization capabilities.

Conclusion/Implications: R4Dyn represents a substantial advancement in the field of depth estimation for dynamic scenes. By incorporating radar, our approach not only enhances depth prediction accuracy but also broadens the potential applications of monocular vision systems, such as in autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality. This research lays a foundation for future innovations in leveraging multimodal sensor data for improved scene interpretation.

Keywords: R4Dyn, radar, self-supervised learning, monocular depth estimation, dynamic scenes, sensor fusion, autonomous driving, robotics.
