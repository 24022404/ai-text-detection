Domain adaptation remains a significant challenge in object detection, particularly when model performance degrades due to discrepancies between source and target domains. This paper introduces a novel approach, Vector-Decomposed Disentanglement (VDD), to enhance domain-invariant object detection. The proposed method decomposes visual features into domain-specific and domain-invariant components at the vector level, allowing the model to isolate and leverage image aspects critical for consistent detection across varying domains. We integrate the VDD framework into a standard object detection backbone and employ an adversarial training strategy to enforce effective disentanglement. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach achieves superior cross-domain detection accuracy compared to existing methods, with substantial improvements observed in challenging domain shift scenarios. Moreover, our analysis reveals that VDD promotes better generalization by reducing sensitivity to domain-specific noise and variations. This research advances the state-of-the-art in domain adaptation for object detection and offers promising potential for real-world applications where robust detection across diverse environments is essential. Keywords: domain adaptation, object detection, vector decomposition, feature disentanglement, invariance, adversarial learning.
