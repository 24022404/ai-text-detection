Reinforcement learning (RL) offers the potential to automate robot control, but deploying RL algorithms on real-world robots remains challenging. This paper presents a comprehensive benchmark of several state-of-the-art RL algorithms applied to a diverse set of robotic tasks, including manipulation, locomotion, and navigation, on physical robot platforms. We evaluate both model-free (e.g., SAC, TD3) and model-based RL methods, focusing on sample efficiency, robustness to sensor noise, and transferability across similar tasks. Our experimental results demonstrate significant performance variations across different algorithms and tasks, highlighting the critical need for careful algorithm selection and hyperparameter tuning. We identify key factors influencing the success of RL in real-world robotics, such as reward function design, exploration strategies, and the impact of sim-to-real transfer techniques. The benchmark provides valuable insights and practical guidelines for researchers and practitioners aiming to apply RL to real-world robotic systems, accelerating the development of autonomous and adaptive robots. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Robotics, Benchmarking, Real-World Experiments, Robot Control, Autonomous Systems.
