This paper presents a novel approach for flight controller synthesis using deep reinforcement learning (DRL) techniques. The research addresses the challenge of designing robust and adaptable controllers for autonomous aerial vehicles, a task traditionally reliant on manual tuning and model-based control strategies. Our methodology leverages a state-of-the-art DRL framework to automatically learn control policies directly from high-dimensional sensory data, without requiring explicit system modeling. We developed and trained deep neural network controllers within realistic flight simulators, employing policy gradient methods and reward shaping to accelerate convergence and ensure system stability. Experimental evaluation demonstrates that the DRL-synthesized controllers achieve superior tracking accuracy and resilience to disturbances compared to conventional PID and adaptive controllers across a range of flight scenarios. The results also indicate improved generalization to novel environments and flight conditions. This work highlights the potential of DRL for automating the synthesis of complex flight controllers, reducing reliance on manual engineering, and enabling rapid adaptation to new vehicle designs or mission requirements. Key contributions include the integration of deep learning architectures with reinforcement learning for control synthesis and thorough benchmarking against established methodologies. Keywords: deep reinforcement learning, flight control, autonomous aerial vehicles, controller synthesis, neural networks, policy optimization.
