This paper addresses the challenge of deriving interpretable, non-monotonic rules from statistical learning models, a critical task for enhancing decision-making processes in data-driven applications. Traditional rule induction methods often struggle with scalability and interpretability when applied to complex datasets. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel approach that integrates high-utility itemset mining (HUIM) with statistical learning models. Our method identifies high-utility patterns in data, which are then transformed into non-monotonic rules that capture exceptions and dependencies often overlooked by conventional rule-based systems. The proposed framework is evaluated on multiple benchmark datasets, demonstrating its ability to generate concise and meaningful rules while maintaining high predictive accuracy. Experimental results show that our approach outperforms existing rule induction techniques in terms of both utility and interpretability. The findings highlight the potential of combining HUIM with statistical learning for applications in areas such as healthcare, finance, and recommendation systems, where explainable and actionable insights are paramount. This research contributes a scalable and innovative methodology for bridging the gap between statistical models and rule-based reasoning, offering a new direction for interpretable machine learning.  

**Keywords**: non-monotonic rules, high-utility itemset mining, statistical learning models, interpretable machine learning, rule induction, decision-making.
