Objective: Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has achieved remarkable success across diverse control and decision-making tasks, yet sample inefficiency and high computational cost remain major obstacles for real-world deployment. This paper introduces a novel Surrogate Agent–Environment Interface (SAEI) to bridge this gap by providing an intermediate, learnable model of the environment that guides policy optimization more efficiently.

Methods: We design SAEI as a modular surrogate module trained alongside the primary policy network. SAEI employs a compact neural dynamics model to predict state transitions and rewards, allowing the agent to perform additional “mental” rollouts without interacting with the real environment. We integrate SAEI into standard DRL algorithms (e.g., Proximal Policy Optimization and Deep Q-Networks) and develop a joint training procedure that alternates policy updates with surrogate model refinement.

Results: Experiments on benchmark continuous-control and discrete-control tasks demonstrate that incorporating SAEI yields a 30–50% reduction in the number of environment samples required to reach target performance levels. Compared to baseline DRL approaches, our method achieves comparable or better final rewards while reducing wall-clock training time by up to 40%. Ablation studies confirm that the surrogate interface enhances stability and mitigates overfitting in high-dimensional state spaces.

Conclusion: The Surrogate Agent–Environment Interface offers a practical pathway to improve sample efficiency and computational scalability in DRL. By coupling policy learning with an adaptive surrogate environment, our framework accelerates convergence and broadens DRL applicability to domains with costly or limited real-world interactions. Potential applications include robotics, autonomous systems, and simulation-based optimization.  

Keywords: deep reinforcement learning, surrogate environment interface, sample efficiency, policy optimization, model-based RL.
