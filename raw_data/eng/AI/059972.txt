This paper explores the effectiveness of transfer learning techniques for material classification tasks using convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Addressing the challenge of limited labeled data in material recognition, we investigate the use of pre-trained CNN models to improve classification accuracy across multiple material categories, such as metal, wood, fabric, and plastic. Our approach involves fine-tuning widely used architectures, including ResNet and VGG, originally trained on large-scale image datasets, for application in a diverse material dataset. Experimental results demonstrate substantial improvements in accuracy and convergence speed compared to training CNNs from scratch, particularly when dealing with small or imbalanced datasets. The study also analyzes the impact of different layers for feature transfer and evaluates model performance against standard benchmarks. Our findings highlight the capability of transfer learning to reduce computational resources and annotation effort while delivering robust and generalizable material classification performance. The contributions of this work include a comprehensive assessment of transfer learning strategies for material recognition, guidelines for selecting pre-trained models based on target material properties, and directions for future research in industrial inspection, robotics, and visual search systems. Keywords: transfer learning, material classification, convolutional neural networks, deep learning, pre-trained models, image recognition.
