This paper introduces Interpretable Graph Capsule Networks (IGCN), a novel architecture for object recognition that combines the strengths of graph neural networks and capsule networks to enhance both performance and interpretability. IGCN leverages graph structures to model the relationships between object parts, enabling a more robust representation of object composition. Capsule networks are then employed to capture viewpoint-invariant features and encode part-whole hierarchies. The proposed model incorporates an attention mechanism within the graph network to identify and highlight salient object parts, thereby providing insights into the decision-making process. Experimental results on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and MNIST, demonstrate that IGCN achieves state-of-the-art accuracy while providing improved interpretability compared to existing methods. Specifically, visualization of the attention weights reveals which parts of the object contribute most significantly to the final classification. IGCN offers a promising approach for object recognition by integrating relational reasoning and hierarchical feature learning, with potential applications in areas requiring explainable AI. Keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Capsule Networks, Object Recognition, Interpretability, Attention Mechanism, Computer Vision.
