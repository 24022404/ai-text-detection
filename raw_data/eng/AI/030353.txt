This paper presents a novel approach to forecasting operating room data by combining the strengths of gradient boosting trees and neural networks. The objective is to improve the accuracy of predictions for operating room utilization, enabling better resource allocation and patient care. A hybrid model is proposed, leveraging the interpretability of gradient boosting trees and the learning capacity of neural networks. The approach utilizes a gradient boosting tree algorithm to identify key features and relationships in the data, which are then used to inform the architecture of a neural network. The results show significant improvements in forecasting accuracy compared to traditional methods, with a reduction in mean absolute error of up to 25%. The hybrid model demonstrates the potential to enhance operating room management, enabling data-driven decision-making and optimized resource utilization. Key contributions include the development of a novel hybrid architecture, the application of machine learning to operating room forecasting, and the demonstration of improved performance over existing methods. Relevant keywords: gradient boosting trees, neural networks, operating room forecasting, hybrid models, machine learning, predictive analytics.
