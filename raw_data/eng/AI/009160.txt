Change-point detection in time series data is a critical task in various domains, including finance, healthcare, and environmental monitoring. Traditional methods often rely on assumptions about data distributions or require extensive parameter tuning, limiting their applicability to diverse datasets. This paper proposes a novel approach to change-point detection based on direct density ratio estimation, which eliminates the need for explicit distribution assumptions and enhances generalization across different types of time series data. The proposed method leverages a non-parametric framework to estimate the ratio of probability densities between consecutive segments of the time series, enabling the identification of significant changes in underlying data patterns. Experiments conducted on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate the robustness and accuracy of the approach, outperforming conventional methods in terms of detection precision and computational efficiency. The results highlight the method's ability to adapt to varying data characteristics, making it a versatile tool for practical applications. This research contributes to the field by providing a flexible and scalable solution for change-point detection, with potential implications for anomaly detection, predictive maintenance, and real-time monitoring systems.  

**Keywords**: change-point detection, time series analysis, density ratio estimation, non-parametric methods, anomaly detection.
