This paper addresses the challenge of developing robust graph contrastive learning methods, which is crucial for improving the performance and generalizability of graph neural networks. Our objective is to design a novel framework that enhances the robustness of graph contrastive learning against adversarial attacks and noisy graph structures. We propose a new approach that combines graph augmentation techniques with a contrastive loss function, which encourages the model to learn robust and informative graph representations. Our method, termed Robust Graph Contrastive Learning (RGCL), utilizes a multi-task learning strategy to jointly optimize the graph representation learning and adversarial training processes. Experimental results demonstrate that RGCL outperforms state-of-the-art graph contrastive learning methods on several benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in node classification, graph classification, and clustering tasks. The key contributions of this work include the development of a robust graph contrastive learning framework, the introduction of a novel graph augmentation technique, and the demonstration of the effectiveness of RGCL in real-world applications. Our research has important implications for the development of robust and reliable graph neural networks, with potential applications in areas such as social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics. Key keywords: graph contrastive learning, robustness, graph neural networks, adversarial attacks, node classification, graph classification.
