This paper presents a novel few-shot learning approach for recognizing historical ciphered manuscripts, addressing the challenge of limited labeled data in this domain. Our objective is to develop a system that can accurately identify and decipher encrypted texts from historical manuscripts with minimal training examples. We propose a hybrid model that combines the strengths of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformer-based architectures to learn robust features from limited data. Our approach leverages self-supervised learning and meta-learning techniques to adapt to new, unseen ciphered manuscripts. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, achieving significant improvements in recognition accuracy compared to traditional machine learning approaches. The key findings of this study highlight the potential of few-shot learning for historical manuscript recognition, enabling the efficient deciphering of previously unknown or untranscribed texts. This research contributes to the development of AI-powered tools for historical document analysis, with potential applications in paleography, cryptography, and cultural heritage preservation. Key keywords: few-shot learning, historical ciphered manuscripts, convolutional neural networks, transformer architectures, self-supervised learning, meta-learning.
