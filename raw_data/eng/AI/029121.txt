Optical Braille Recognition (OBR) systems offer crucial accessibility for the visually impaired, yet challenges remain in accurately detecting and interpreting Braille dots from images. This paper introduces a novel OBR system leveraging the Circular Hough Transform (CHT) for robust dot detection. Our approach enhances traditional CHT by incorporating adaptive thresholding and spatial constraints to minimize false positives caused by noise and image artifacts. The system segments Braille cells, applies CHT to identify potential dot locations within each cell, and then validates these locations based on geometrical relationships inherent in Braille patterns. Experimental results, conducted on a diverse dataset of Braille images captured under varying lighting and quality conditions, demonstrate a significant improvement in dot detection accuracy compared to conventional methods. Specifically, our system achieves a recognition rate of 98.5%, a 10% increase over standard thresholding-based CHT implementations. The improved accuracy and robustness of this OBR system contribute to more reliable and accessible solutions for Braille users. Keywords: Optical Braille Recognition, Circular Hough Transform, Image Processing, Accessibility, Computer Vision.
