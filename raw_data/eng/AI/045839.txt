Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) has shown promise in solving complex tasks by decomposing them into manageable subgoals. However, learning effective goal representations remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces a novel approach to learning goal embeddings through self-play, enabling agents to autonomously discover and represent meaningful subgoals in hierarchical tasks. Our method leverages a two-phase training process: first, agents engage in self-play to explore the environment and generate diverse goal-directed behaviors; second, a goal embedding network is trained to encode these behaviors into a compact, actionable representation. We evaluate our approach on a suite of benchmark environments, demonstrating significant improvements in task completion rates and sample efficiency compared to existing HRL methods. The learned embeddings not only enhance the agent's ability to generalize across tasks but also provide interpretable representations of subgoals. Our results highlight the potential of self-play in advancing HRL, offering a scalable and adaptive framework for complex decision-making problems. Key contributions include a robust goal discovery mechanism, improved hierarchical policy learning, and a flexible architecture applicable to diverse domains.  

**Keywords**: Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning, Goal Embeddings, Self-Play, Subgoal Discovery, Decision-Making, AI.
