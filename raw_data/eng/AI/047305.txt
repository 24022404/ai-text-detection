High-Dynamic-Range (HDR) imaging has emerged as a powerful technique for capturing detailed visual information in challenging lighting conditions, making it particularly relevant for applications such as cloud segmentation. This paper addresses the problem of accurately segmenting cloud structures from HDR images, which is critical for weather prediction, environmental monitoring, and autonomous systems. We propose a novel approach that combines HDR image preprocessing with a deep learning-based segmentation model. The preprocessing step enhances image contrast and reduces noise, while the segmentation model leverages a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture optimized for HDR data. Our experiments demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior segmentation accuracy compared to traditional low-dynamic-range (LDR) approaches, with a 12% improvement in intersection-over-union (IoU) scores on benchmark datasets. Additionally, the model exhibits robustness to varying lighting conditions, a common challenge in outdoor imaging. The results highlight the potential of HDR imaging in improving cloud segmentation tasks, offering significant implications for meteorology, climate studies, and remote sensing. This work contributes to the growing body of research on HDR applications and provides a scalable solution for real-world environmental monitoring systems.  

**Keywords**: High-Dynamic-Range (HDR) imaging, cloud segmentation, deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNN), environmental monitoring, weather prediction.
