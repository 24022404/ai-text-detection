This study aims to investigate the application of persistent homology topological features in characterizing medical images, specifically in the context of lung and brain cancers. Our approach utilizes a novel framework that combines topological data analysis with traditional image processing techniques to extract unique features from medical images. The methodology involves computing persistent homology descriptors from imaging data, which are then used to train machine learning models for cancer classification and diagnosis. Our results show that the incorporation of topological features significantly improves the accuracy of cancer detection and classification, outperforming traditional methods in certain cases. The findings of this research highlight the potential of persistent homology in enhancing the analysis and interpretation of medical images, particularly in the field of oncology. The study contributes to the development of more accurate and reliable diagnostic tools, with potential applications in clinical practice and medical research. Key keywords: persistent homology, topological data analysis, medical image analysis, lung cancer, brain cancer, machine learning, cancer diagnosis.
