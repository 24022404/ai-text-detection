Image captioning, the task of generating descriptive sentences for images, remains a critical challenge in computer vision and natural language processing. While end-to-end deep learning architectures have become popular, intermediate structured representations such as scene graphs—graph-based descriptions capturing objects, attributes, and relationships within images—have often been underutilized or dismissed. This paper advocates for the renewed integration of scene graphs into the image captioning pipeline, presenting a systematic study on their effectiveness. We propose an enhanced scene graph extraction and encoding framework, combining advanced object detection, relationship modeling, and graph neural networks to bridge visual content with natural language descriptions. Experimental results demonstrate that leveraging scene graphs leads to notable improvements in caption relevance, coherence, and factual accuracy compared to purely end-to-end models, especially on complex scenes requiring relational reasoning. Our analysis further reveals that scene graphs facilitate better controllability and interpretability in caption generation. These findings underscore the importance of structured representations in achieving high-quality image-to-text systems. Potential applications include assistive technologies, visual content search, and AI-based storytelling. Keywords: scene graphs, image captioning, graph neural networks, structured representation, computer vision, natural language processing.
