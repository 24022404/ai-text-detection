Abstract:  
This paper presents CogView, a novel framework for text-to-image generation leveraging the power of transformer architectures. The objective of this research is to bridge the gap between natural language descriptions and high-quality image synthesis, addressing limitations in visual fidelity and semantic alignment found in prior models. CogView uses a large-scale transformer model trained on an extensive multilingual text-image paired dataset, enabling it to learn complex relationships between textual prompts and corresponding visual outputs. Unlike traditional generative models, CogView employs a discrete image tokenizer to convert images into sequences of visual tokens, allowing the transformer to treat image generation as a language modeling problem. Experimental results demonstrate that CogView achieves state-of-the-art performance in generating realistic and semantically accurate images from diverse and complex text inputs, outperforming existing methods in terms of both image quality and textual relevance. The approach also exhibits strong generalization capabilities across different domains and languages. This work highlights the potential of large transformers for multimodal generation tasks and sets a new benchmark for research in text-to-image synthesis. Key contributions include the introduction of a scalable transformer-based model for image generation, effective use of visual tokenization, and comprehensive evaluations on established text-to-image benchmarks. CogView paves the way for new applications in creative content generation, visual storytelling, and human-computer interaction. Keywords: text-to-image generation, transformer, image synthesis, multimodal AI, visual tokenization, natural language processing.
