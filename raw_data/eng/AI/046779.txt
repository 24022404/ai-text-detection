Objective: Accurate analysis and forecasting of correlated multivariate time series are critical in domains such as finance, environmental monitoring, and healthcare. Traditional models often struggle to capture both complex temporal dynamics and inter-series dependencies simultaneously. Methods: We propose a Deep Structural Model (DSM) that integrates a recurrent encoder for temporal pattern learning with a graph-based structural module to explicitly model correlations among multiple series. An attention mechanism dynamically weights dependencies, while a variational inference layer ensures robust latent representation learning. The entire architecture is trained end-to-end, allowing the model to adaptively discover and leverage underlying structural relationships. Results: Experiments on three benchmark datasets—including financial indices, sensor networks, and clinical recordings—demonstrate that DSM reduces forecasting error by 15–25% (measured in RMSE and MAE) compared to state-of-the-art baselines such as VAR, LSTM, and Graph WaveNet. Ablation studies confirm the individual contributions of the structural module and attention mechanism to overall performance gains. Conclusion: The proposed DSM effectively captures both temporal and structural characteristics of multivariate time series, yielding more accurate forecasts and offering interpretable insights into inter-series interactions. Its flexibility makes it suitable for real-time monitoring, anomaly detection, and imputation tasks. Future work will explore online adaptation and scalability to ultra-high-dimensional datasets.  
Keywords: Deep Structural Model, multivariate time series, correlation modeling, temporal forecasting, graph neural network, attention mechanism.
