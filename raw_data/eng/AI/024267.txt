This paper addresses the challenge of few-shot object detection, where the goal is to detect objects from novel classes with limited training examples. Our objective is to develop a generalized and incremental few-shot object detection framework that can adapt to new classes and improve its performance over time. We propose a novel approach that combines meta-learning and incremental learning techniques to achieve this goal. Our method utilizes a transformer-based architecture to learn robust and generalizable features from a few examples, and an incremental learning strategy to update the model as new classes are introduced. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, outperforming state-of-the-art few-shot object detection methods on several benchmark datasets. Our findings show that the proposed framework can detect objects from novel classes with high accuracy, using as few as one or five training examples per class. The contributions of this research include a new paradigm for few-shot object detection, which has significant implications for real-world applications such as autonomous driving, surveillance, and robotics. Key keywords: few-shot learning, object detection, meta-learning, incremental learning, transformer, computer vision, AI models.
