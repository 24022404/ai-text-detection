Fine-grained visual recognition (FGVR) is a challenging task in computer vision, requiring the identification of subtle differences within visually similar categories. While web images offer a vast and cost-effective resource for training FGVR models, they often contain noisy and irrelevant samples that degrade model performance. This paper addresses the dual challenge of eliminating noisy samples and effectively utilizing hard, informative ones to improve FGVR accuracy. We propose a novel framework that integrates a noise-robust sample selection mechanism with a hard sample mining strategy. The noise-robust mechanism leverages multi-modal information, including visual and textual cues, to filter out low-quality samples, while the hard sample mining approach focuses on identifying and emphasizing challenging examples that enhance model discriminability. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our framework significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of classification accuracy and robustness. The proposed approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, highlighting its potential for real-world applications in domains such as e-commerce, biodiversity monitoring, and medical imaging. This work contributes to advancing FGVR by providing a scalable and effective solution for leveraging web-sourced data, even in the presence of noise and variability.  

**Keywords**: Fine-grained visual recognition, web images, noisy sample elimination, hard sample mining, multi-modal learning, computer vision.
