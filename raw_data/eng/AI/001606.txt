This paper addresses the challenge of learning scale-free networks, a common phenomenon in real-world systems, by introducing a novel approach based on dynamic node-specific degree prior. The objective is to develop a method that can effectively capture the heterogeneous degree distributions and complex topological structures of scale-free networks. Our approach utilizes a probabilistic framework that incorporates node-specific degree priors, which are dynamically updated during the learning process. The method is based on a combination of Bayesian inference and stochastic optimization, allowing for efficient and scalable learning of large networks. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of network reconstruction accuracy and scalability. The key findings include the ability to learn networks with power-law degree distributions and the discovery of node-specific degree patterns that are consistent with real-world networks. The proposed method has important implications for understanding and analyzing complex networks, with potential applications in social network analysis, epidemiology, and recommendation systems. Key contributions of this research include the introduction of dynamic node-specific degree priors and the development of a scalable learning algorithm for scale-free networks, with relevant keywords including scale-free networks, node-specific degree prior, Bayesian inference, stochastic optimization, and network reconstruction.
