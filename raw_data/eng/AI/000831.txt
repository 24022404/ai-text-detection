Visual Question Answering (VQA) systems traditionally rely on extensive training datasets containing paired questions and answers to achieve high performance. However, this dependency on labeled data limits scalability and adaptability to new domains. This paper introduces a novel approach to VQA that eliminates the need for question-answer training pairs, addressing the challenge of data scarcity and reducing annotation costs. Our method leverages unsupervised learning techniques combined with pre-trained vision-language models to infer answers directly from visual inputs and contextual cues. By reformulating the VQA task as a self-supervised problem, we enable the system to generalize across diverse scenarios without explicit question-answer supervision. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive performance on benchmark datasets, outperforming traditional methods in low-resource settings. Furthermore, the proposed framework exhibits robustness to domain shifts and supports real-time applications. This work contributes to advancing VQA systems by reducing reliance on labeled data, opening new possibilities for scalable and adaptable visual understanding in real-world environments.  

**Keywords**: Visual Question Answering, unsupervised learning, vision-language models, self-supervised learning, scalability, domain adaptation.
