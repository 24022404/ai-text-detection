This paper explores the application of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) to address complex conservation decision-making challenges. The objective is to develop an adaptive framework that optimizes resource allocation and policy implementation for biodiversity preservation. Traditional conservation strategies often struggle with dynamic environmental conditions and limited data, prompting the need for innovative, data-driven approaches. We propose a DRL-based model that integrates environmental data, species population dynamics, and human intervention impacts to generate actionable conservation policies. The model employs a combination of deep neural networks and reinforcement learning algorithms to learn optimal strategies through iterative interactions with simulated ecosystems. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms conventional methods in terms of adaptability, scalability, and long-term ecological outcomes. Specifically, the model achieves a 20% improvement in species survival rates and a 15% reduction in resource wastage compared to baseline techniques. The findings highlight the potential of DRL to revolutionize conservation planning by enabling real-time decision-making and adaptive management. This research contributes to the growing field of AI-driven environmental solutions, offering a novel framework for addressing pressing global conservation challenges.  

**Keywords**: Deep Reinforcement Learning, Conservation Decision-Making, Biodiversity Preservation, Resource Allocation, Adaptive Management, Environmental AI.
