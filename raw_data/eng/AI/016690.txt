This paper addresses the problem of ranking items using both intrinsic features and pairwise comparisons, a critical challenge in recommendation systems, search engines, and decision-making applications. We propose a novel algorithm that integrates feature-based information with graph-theoretic techniques to improve ranking accuracy and robustness. The approach leverages a graph representation of pairwise comparisons, where nodes represent items and edges encode comparison outcomes, combined with feature vectors that capture item attributes. By formulating the ranking problem as an optimization task on this graph, our method ensures consistency between feature-based predictions and observed comparisons. We provide a rigorous theoretical analysis of the algorithm, establishing its convergence properties and robustness to noisy data. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of ranking accuracy and computational efficiency. The proposed framework not only advances the state-of-the-art in ranking algorithms but also offers practical insights for applications requiring scalable and interpretable ranking systems. Key contributions include the integration of feature-based learning with graph theory, a robust optimization framework, and empirical validation of its effectiveness. Keywords: ranking algorithms, graph theory, feature-based learning, optimization, recommendation systems.
