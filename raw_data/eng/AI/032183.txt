This paper investigates the fundamental question of which relations can be reliably embedded in Euclidean space, a crucial problem in machine learning, data analysis, and geometric modeling. Our objective is to identify the types of relations that can be accurately represented using Euclidean embeddings, which are essential in various applications, including natural language processing, computer vision, and recommender systems. We propose a novel framework that leverages geometric and topological techniques to analyze the embeddability of relations in Euclidean space. Our approach involves developing a set of necessary and sufficient conditions for a relation to be embeddable, which enables us to characterize the class of relations that can be reliably represented. Our results show that certain types of relations, such as metric and ultrametric relations, can be embedded with high fidelity, while others, like non-metric relations, pose significant challenges. We demonstrate the efficacy of our framework through extensive experiments on various datasets, including image and text data. Our findings have important implications for the development of more accurate and efficient embedding methods, with potential applications in areas like dimensionality reduction, clustering, and visualization. Key keywords: Euclidean embeddings, geometric modeling, machine learning, relation embeddability, ultrametric relations.
