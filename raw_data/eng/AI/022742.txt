Abstract

Leaf recognition has become a vital tool in plant species identification, supporting biodiversity studies and agricultural management. This paper presents a novel approach utilizing Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to automatically extract and learn discriminative features from leaf images, enhancing recognition accuracy. The proposed method involves preprocessing a diverse dataset of leaf images, followed by training a CNN architecture to perform feature extraction and classification. To evaluate effectiveness, the model’s performance is compared against traditional machine learning approaches employing handcrafted features such as shape, texture, and color descriptors. Experimental results demonstrate that the CNN-based model significantly outperforms classical methods in terms of both accuracy and robustness, particularly under varying image conditions and noise. The findings highlight the capacity of deep learning to generalize across leaf species and adapt to visual variability, reducing the need for manual feature engineering. Our approach offers a scalable, automated solution suitable for deployment in mobile applications and field-based plant identification systems. The primary contributions include the development of an end-to-end leaf recognition framework leveraging deep feature representations, and extensive benchmarking on multiple leaf datasets. This research underscores the value of CNNs in advancing plant image recognition tasks and paves the way for future integration with broader ecological and agricultural informatics systems. 

Keywords: leaf recognition, convolutional neural networks, deep learning, feature extraction, plant identification, image classification.
