Goal-oriented visual dialog systems require effective multi-turn communication to enable an AI agent (the answerer) to successfully assist a user (the questioner) based on visual content. This paper presents a novel information theoretic approach to optimizing the answerer's responses by modeling and reasoning about the questioner's knowledge and intentions—termed as "Answerer in Questioner's Mind" (AiQM). Leveraging principles from information theory, we formulate the answer selection process as one that maximizes expected information gain for the questioner, conditioned on both the visual context and dialog history. We develop an end-to-end neural architecture that combines visual feature extraction, dialog state tracking, and utility-based answer selection. Experiments are conducted on standard goal-oriented visual dialog benchmarks, demonstrating that our AiQM method leads to more informative and contextually relevant replies compared to conventional response generation models. Quantitative results show significant improvements in task completion rates and dialog efficiency, while qualitative analyses illustrate increased interpretability in answerer strategies. Our approach offers a principled framework for enhancing cooperative visual reasoning in AI systems, with potential applications in autonomous agents, human-computer interaction, and assistive technologies. Keywords: visual dialog, information theory, goal-oriented dialog, AI communication, answer generation, question modeling.
