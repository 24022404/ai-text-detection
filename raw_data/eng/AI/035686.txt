The increasing adoption of machine learning (ML) models in real-world applications necessitates robust and transparent validation mechanisms to ensure reliability and trustworthiness. This paper addresses the critical challenge of online validation of ML models, focusing on explainability and practicality. We propose a novel framework that integrates interpretability techniques with real-time performance monitoring to validate ML models during deployment. Our approach leverages explainable AI (XAI) methods, such as SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) and LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations), to provide insights into model predictions and identify potential biases or errors. Additionally, we introduce a dynamic validation mechanism that continuously evaluates model performance using streaming data, ensuring adaptability to evolving environments. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our framework in improving model transparency and reliability, outperforming traditional offline validation methods. The proposed solution is particularly valuable for high-stakes applications, such as healthcare and finance, where explainability and real-time validation are paramount. This research contributes to advancing the practical deployment of ML models by bridging the gap between theoretical validation and real-world operational requirements.  

**Keywords**: Explainable AI, online validation, machine learning, real-time monitoring, SHAP, LIME, model reliability.
