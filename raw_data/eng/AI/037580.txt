Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, demonstrating superior performance in various applications. However, their robustness to adversarial examples—inputs intentionally perturbed to mislead models—remains underexplored. This study investigates the resilience of Vision Transformers against adversarial attacks, comparing their performance with traditional CNNs under similar conditions. We evaluate multiple adversarial attack strategies, including gradient-based and optimization-based methods, on standard datasets such as CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. Our experiments reveal that while ViTs exhibit a degree of robustness due to their self-attention mechanisms, they are not inherently immune to adversarial perturbations. We propose a novel defense mechanism leveraging the unique architectural properties of ViTs, which enhances their adversarial robustness without compromising accuracy on clean data. The results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing defense strategies, offering a promising direction for improving the security of transformer-based vision models. This work contributes to the growing body of research on adversarial machine learning, providing insights into the strengths and vulnerabilities of Vision Transformers and paving the way for more robust AI systems in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Vision Transformers, adversarial robustness, self-attention, adversarial attacks, defense mechanisms, computer vision.
