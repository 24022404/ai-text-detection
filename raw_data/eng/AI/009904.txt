Objective: Effective coordination among multiple agents is crucial for tasks ranging from autonomous vehicle platooning to distributed resource allocation. Traditional coordination graph frameworks offer structured decomposition of joint action-value functions but struggle to scale to high-dimensional observations and complex interaction patterns. This paper introduces Deep Coordination Graphs (DCG), a novel end-to-end learning paradigm that combines representation learning with graph-based value decomposition to address these limitations.

Methods: DCG leverages a graph neural network (GNN) architecture to dynamically infer coordination structures from raw agent observations. Each agent encodes its local state into a latent embedding, while pairwise and higher-order interactions are captured by learned edge functions. The joint action-value function is factorized over this learned graph, enabling decentralized execution via locally computed Q-values. Training is performed using a centralized critic with a global Bellman error, regularized to encourage sparsity in learned coordination edges.

Results: We evaluate DCG on benchmark multi-agent tasks including traffic signal control, cooperative navigation, and StarCraft II micromanagement. Compared to state-of-the-art baselines (e.g., VDN, QMIX, QTRAN), DCG achieves up to 25% higher cumulative reward and demonstrates faster convergence. Ablation studies confirm the importance of graph sparsity and dynamic edge learning for both performance and interpretability.

Conclusion: Deep Coordination Graphs offer a scalable, interpretable, and high-performance framework for multi-agent coordination. By learning both agent representations and interaction structures, DCG adapts to diverse environments and paves the way for robust decentralized decision-making in complex real-world systems.

Keywords: deep coordination graphs, multi-agent reinforcement learning, graph neural network, value decomposition, decentralized control.
