This paper presents a novel approach to recognizing American Sign Language (ASL) fingerspelling in uncontrolled environments, leveraging computer vision and machine learning techniques. The objective is to develop a robust system that can accurately identify fingerspelled words in real-world settings, overcoming challenges such as varying lighting conditions, backgrounds, and signer styles. Our method employs a deep learning-based architecture, utilizing a convolutional neural network (CNN) to extract features from video sequences of fingerspelling, followed by a recurrent neural network (RNN) to model the temporal dynamics of signing. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving a significant improvement in recognition accuracy compared to existing methods. Our system's ability to recognize ASL fingerspelling in the wild has important implications for enhancing communication between signers and non-signers, and contributes to the development of more inclusive and accessible technologies. Key contributions include the collection of a large-scale dataset of ASL fingerspelling in diverse environments, and the introduction of a novel data augmentation strategy to improve model generalizability. Relevant keywords: American Sign Language, fingerspelling recognition, computer vision, machine learning, deep learning, sign language processing.
