The challenge of effective learning from small samples in the context of abundant, but often unlabelled, big data is addressed. This paper introduces a novel semi-supervised learning framework designed to leverage the information present in large unlabelled datasets to improve the performance of models trained on limited labelled data. Our approach combines a self-training strategy with a data augmentation technique specifically tailored for small sample scenarios. This mitigates overfitting and improves generalization. We evaluate our framework on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in classification accuracy compared to traditional supervised learning methods and other semi-supervised techniques. Results show an average increase of 15% in F1-score when learning from only 10% of the labelled data. The findings highlight the potential of leveraging readily available big data to enhance the performance of machine learning models even when labelled data is scarce. Keywords: Small sample learning, semi-supervised learning, big data, data augmentation, self-training, classification.
