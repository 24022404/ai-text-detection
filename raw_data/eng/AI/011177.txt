This paper explores the application of reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance the capabilities of multi-head automata (MHA) in language inference tasks. The objective is to address the challenge of efficiently recognizing and inferring complex patterns in formal languages, which is critical for applications in natural language processing (NLP) and computational linguistics. We propose a novel RL-based framework that trains MHA to dynamically adjust their transition rules and head movements, enabling them to learn optimal strategies for language recognition. The approach leverages a reward mechanism to guide the automata toward accurate inference, with a focus on scalability and adaptability to diverse language structures. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms traditional MHA approaches in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly for context-sensitive and ambiguous languages. The findings highlight the potential of combining RL with MHA to advance language inference systems, offering insights into their applicability in real-world NLP tasks such as syntax parsing and semantic analysis. This research contributes a new perspective on the integration of machine learning and automata theory, paving the way for future innovations in language modeling and inference.  

**Keywords**: multi-head automata, reinforcement learning, language inference, formal languages, natural language processing, computational linguistics.
