Semi-supervised learning for volumetric medical image segmentation is crucial due to the scarcity of labeled data. This paper introduces a novel momentum contrastive voxel-wise representation learning framework to address this challenge. Our approach leverages unlabeled data by learning robust voxel-level feature representations through a contrastive loss, encouraging similar voxels to cluster together while dissimilar voxels are separated. Specifically, we utilize a momentum update strategy to maintain a consistent and stable feature bank for contrastive learning. This mitigates the noise inherent in training with limited labeled data. We evaluate our method on two publicly available datasets for abdominal organ segmentation and cardiac structure segmentation. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing semi-supervised segmentation methods, achieving comparable performance to fully supervised methods while using only a fraction of the labeled data. The learned voxel-wise representations also prove to be more generalizable and robust to variations in image acquisition parameters. Our findings highlight the potential of contrastive representation learning for improving medical image segmentation accuracy and reducing the reliance on extensive manual annotations. Keywords: Semi-supervised learning, medical image segmentation, contrastive learning, voxel-wise representation, momentum contrast, deep learning.
