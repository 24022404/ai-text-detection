This paper explores the application of potential-based reward shaping in Sokoban, a classic puzzle game that requires strategic planning and problem-solving. The objective is to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of reinforcement learning agents in solving Sokoban levels. We propose a novel approach that utilizes potential-based reward shaping to guide the agent's exploration and decision-making process. Our method modifies the reward function to incorporate a potential function that captures the underlying structure of the game, enabling the agent to learn more informative and relevant policies. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly improves the agent's performance, achieving higher success rates and reduced solution lengths compared to traditional reinforcement learning methods. The key findings of this research highlight the potential of reward shaping in enhancing the capabilities of reinforcement learning agents in complex puzzle domains, with implications for applications in robotics, game playing, and other areas of artificial intelligence. Key keywords: reinforcement learning, reward shaping, potential-based methods, Sokoban, puzzle solving, game playing, AI.
