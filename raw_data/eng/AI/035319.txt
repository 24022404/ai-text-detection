This paper presents Local Stochastic Approximation (LSA), a unified theoretical and algorithmic framework that bridges Federated Learning (FL) and Distributed Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning (DMT-RL). The increasing demand for privacy-preserving and scalable learning across decentralized environments, such as edge devices and autonomous systems, has highlighted the need for robust algorithms capable of handling statistical heterogeneity across nodes. LSA leverages local updates with stochastic approximation techniques to enable efficient knowledge sharing while respecting data privacy and minimizing communication overhead. Through a generic formulation, LSA encapsulates both synchronous and asynchronous model aggregation strategies and supports personalized policy learning in reinforcement learning contexts, addressing non-independent and identically distributed (non-IID) data challenges across tasks. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LSA achieves superior convergence rates and improved task performance compared to conventional FL and DMT-RL baselines, especially in heterogeneous and resource-constrained environments. The results reveal that LSA offers significant flexibility for customizing local and global learning dynamics, providing a principled pathway for integrating advances from both federated and distributed RL domains. Our unified view not only simplifies the analysis of decentralization paradigms but also lays the groundwork for novel algorithmic developments in collaborative, privacy-conscious machine learning systems. Keywords: Local Stochastic Approximation, Federated Learning, Distributed Reinforcement Learning, Multi-Task Learning, Decentralized Algorithms, Non-IID Data, Privacy-Preserving Learning.
