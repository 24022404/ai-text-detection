This paper addresses the challenge of forecasting hands and objects in future video frames, a crucial task for human-computer interaction, robotics, and autonomous systems. Our objective is to develop a predictive model that can accurately forecast the future states of hands and objects, enabling proactive decision-making and interaction. We propose a novel approach that combines computer vision and machine learning techniques, utilizing a convolutional neural network (CNN) and a graph-based model to capture spatial and temporal relationships between hands and objects. Our results show significant improvements in forecasting accuracy, outperforming existing methods by 15% in terms of mean average precision. The key findings of our research demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in predicting future hand-object interactions, with potential applications in areas such as human-robot collaboration, virtual reality, and smart home automation. Our contributions include a new dataset for hand-object forecasting and a novel evaluation metric that assesses the accuracy of forecasting future interactions. Keywords: hand-object forecasting, computer vision, machine learning, human-computer interaction, robotics.
