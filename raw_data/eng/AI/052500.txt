This paper addresses the challenge of image segmentation in cross-country scenes captured in the infrared (IR) spectrum, a critical task for applications such as autonomous navigation, surveillance, and environmental monitoring. Traditional segmentation methods often struggle with the unique characteristics of IR imagery, including low contrast and thermal noise. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel deep learning-based approach that leverages a modified convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture optimized for IR spectral data. Our method incorporates multi-scale feature extraction and adaptive thresholding to enhance segmentation accuracy in complex outdoor environments. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves a significant improvement in segmentation precision compared to state-of-the-art methods, with a 12% increase in mean intersection-over-union (mIoU) on a custom dataset of IR cross-country scenes. Additionally, the model exhibits robust performance under varying environmental conditions, such as changes in lighting and terrain. The findings highlight the potential of our approach for real-world applications, particularly in scenarios where traditional RGB-based segmentation fails. This research contributes to advancing IR image analysis by providing a scalable and efficient solution for cross-country scene segmentation, paving the way for improved autonomous systems and remote sensing technologies.  

**Keywords**: Image segmentation, infrared spectrum, deep learning, convolutional neural networks, autonomous navigation, remote sensing.
