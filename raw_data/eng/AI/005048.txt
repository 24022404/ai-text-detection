This paper addresses the challenge of vision-language navigation in dynamic environments, where agents must interpret natural language instructions and navigate through unfamiliar spaces. Our objective is to develop a robust navigation system that can generalize across diverse environments and instructions. To achieve this, we propose a novel approach called Random Environmental Mixup, which combines reinforcement learning with a mixup strategy to augment environmental experiences. Our method involves training an agent to navigate in a simulated environment with randomly mixed-up scenes, objects, and lighting conditions, allowing it to learn more robust and generalizable policies. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly improves the navigation success rate and efficiency compared to existing methods. The key findings of this study highlight the importance of environmental diversity and randomness in vision-language navigation. Our research contributes to the development of more effective and adaptable navigation systems, with potential applications in areas such as robotics, virtual reality, and smart home automation. Key keywords: vision-language navigation, reinforcement learning, environmental mixup, generalization, robotics.
