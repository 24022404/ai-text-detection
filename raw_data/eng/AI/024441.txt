This paper addresses the challenge of reward misspecification in reinforcement learning (RL), where the provided reward signal imperfectly reflects the true desired behavior. We introduce a novel regularization framework that promotes reward robustness by penalizing policies highly sensitive to small reward perturbations. Our approach leverages a tractable approximation of policy sensitivity, enabling efficient integration into standard policy optimization algorithms. We demonstrate, through extensive experiments in both simulated and benchmark environments, that regularized policies exhibit significantly improved robustness to various types of reward noise and misspecification, including sparse, delayed, and deceptive rewards. Compared to existing robustness techniques, our method achieves comparable or superior performance while maintaining computational efficiency and sample complexity. Key contributions include the theoretical justification for our regularization term, a practical algorithm for implementation, and empirical evidence demonstrating the effectiveness of our approach in enhancing the reliability of RL agents under imperfect reward signals. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Reward Misspecification, Robustness, Regularization, Policy Optimization.
