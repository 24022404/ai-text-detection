Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) faces significant challenges in credit assignment, where agents must accurately attribute rewards to their individual actions in complex, collaborative environments. This paper introduces a novel approach, Meta-Policy Gradient (MPG), designed to address credit assignment by dynamically adjusting policy updates based on meta-learning principles. The proposed method leverages a meta-policy that learns to optimize the credit assignment process across agents, enabling more efficient and accurate reward distribution. We evaluate MPG in diverse multi-agent scenarios, including cooperative and competitive tasks, and compare its performance against state-of-the-art MARL algorithms. Experimental results demonstrate that MPG significantly improves learning efficiency, scalability, and overall task performance, particularly in environments with sparse or delayed rewards. The findings highlight the potential of meta-learning techniques to enhance credit assignment in MARL, offering a robust framework for future applications in autonomous systems, robotics, and multi-agent coordination. Key contributions include the integration of meta-learning with policy gradients, a scalable architecture for multi-agent systems, and empirical validation across benchmark environments. Keywords: Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning, Credit Assignment, Meta-Learning, Policy Gradient, Autonomous Systems.
