Causal effect estimation is a critical challenge in various domains, including healthcare, economics, and social sciences, where understanding the impact of interventions is essential. This paper introduces *Graph Intervention Networks (GIN)*, a novel framework designed to estimate causal effects by leveraging graph-structured data. GIN integrates graph neural networks (GNNs) with causal inference techniques to model complex dependencies and interactions among entities in a system. The proposed approach addresses key limitations of existing methods, such as handling high-dimensional data and capturing non-linear relationships. We evaluate GIN on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its superior performance in accurately estimating causal effects compared to state-of-the-art baselines. Experimental results highlight GIN's robustness in scenarios with sparse data and its ability to generalize across diverse domains. The framework's interpretability, enabled by its graph-based architecture, further enhances its practical utility. This research contributes to advancing causal inference methodologies by providing a scalable and effective solution for intervention effect estimation, with potential applications in personalized medicine, policy evaluation, and beyond.  

**Keywords**: Causal effect estimation, graph neural networks, intervention analysis, causal inference, graph-structured data.
