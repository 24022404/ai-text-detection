Quantum chemistry simulations offer insights into molecular properties but are computationally expensive. This paper explores the application of neural message passing networks (NMPNs) to predict molecular properties, aiming to reduce computational cost while maintaining accuracy. We developed an NMPN architecture that leverages node and edge features representing atoms and bonds within a molecule to learn representations through iterative message aggregation. This architecture is trained end-to-end on a dataset of molecular properties, including energy and forces, obtained from Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations. Our results demonstrate that NMPNs can accurately predict these properties, achieving comparable accuracy to DFT at a fraction of the computational cost. Further, we investigated the learned representations and found that the NMPN effectively captures crucial chemical information. This work presents a promising avenue for accelerating quantum chemistry simulations and enabling the efficient screening of molecules for materials discovery. Keywords: Quantum Chemistry, Neural Message Passing, Machine Learning, Molecular Property Prediction, Graph Neural Networks.
