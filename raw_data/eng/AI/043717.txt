Object detection models often struggle with domain shift, where performance degrades when applied to datasets different from the training data. This paper introduces a novel Vector-Decomposed Disentanglement (VDD) approach to improve domain invariance in object detection. VDD decomposes feature vectors into domain-specific and domain-invariant components using adversarial learning and a novel vector projection loss. This loss encourages orthogonality between the domain-specific and domain-invariant subspaces, promoting effective disentanglement. We evaluate VDD on several benchmark datasets involving synthetic-to-real and cross-camera domain shifts. Experimental results demonstrate that VDD significantly outperforms existing domain adaptation techniques, achieving state-of-the-art performance. Specifically, VDD improves average precision by up to 5% compared to competing methods. This approach offers a robust and effective solution for mitigating domain shift in object detection, leading to more reliable performance across diverse environments. Keywords: Object detection, domain adaptation, disentanglement, adversarial learning, domain invariance, deep learning.
