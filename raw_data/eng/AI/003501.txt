This paper addresses the challenge of automatic feature weight determination in multi-feature content-based image retrieval (CBIR) systems. Traditional CBIR methods often rely on manually assigned or heuristically fixed feature weights, which can lead to suboptimal retrieval accuracy due to varying feature relevance across query types and image databases. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel approach that integrates image indexing techniques with pseudo-relevance feedback to dynamically adjust feature weights during retrieval. Our framework employs an initial retrieval step using a composite feature representation, followed by pseudo-relevance feedback to analyze the characteristics of top-ranked images and automatically refine the weights assigned to individual visual features, such as color, texture, and shape. Extensive experiments on benchmark image datasets demonstrate that our method consistently improves retrieval performance, achieving higher precision and recall compared to conventional fixed-weight and manually tuned strategies. The proposed system also exhibits efficient indexing, scalability, and robustness against diverse image content. These results highlight the significance of adaptive feature weighting and the effectiveness of combining indexing with relevance feedback for next-generation CBIR systems. Keywords: content-based image retrieval, feature weighting, pseudo-relevance feedback, image indexing, adaptive retrieval, multimedia search.
