This paper presents GEE, a novel Gradient-based Explainable Variational Autoencoder framework designed for effective network anomaly detection. The objective of this research is to address the persistent challenge of balancing detection accuracy with model interpretability in network security applications. GEE leverages a variational autoencoder (VAE) architecture to capture complex, non-linear patterns in high-dimensional network traffic data, enabling the identification of subtle and previously unseen anomalies. To enhance explainability, we introduce a gradient-based attribution mechanism, allowing the model to highlight critical features contributing to each detected anomaly, thereby providing actionable insights for security analysts. Experimental evaluation was conducted on widely used benchmark datasets, where GEE demonstrated superior detection performance compared to existing unsupervised and semi-supervised anomaly detection approaches. Additionally, qualitative analyses confirm the effectiveness of the gradient-based explanations in revealing root causes of network threats. Our findings suggest that GEE not only achieves high accuracy and robustness in identifying network irregularities but also promotes model transparency, facilitating trust and informed decision-making in cybersecurity operations. Keywords: variational autoencoder, anomaly detection, explainable AI, network security, gradient-based attribution, interpretable machine learning.
