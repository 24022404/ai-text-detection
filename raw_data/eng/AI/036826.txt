Abstract:

The proliferation of facial manipulation techniques in videos, such as deepfakes, presents significant challenges to digital security and authenticity verification. This paper addresses the critical need for accurate and robust detection of manipulated facial content in videos by proposing an ensemble approach based on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). Our method leverages multiple CNN architectures, including both lightweight and deep models, to capture diverse spatial and temporal features indicative of manipulation artifacts. The ensemble framework integrates predictions from various CNNs, enhancing overall detection performance and resilience against different types of facial forgery techniques. Extensive experiments were conducted on publicly available video manipulation datasets, demonstrating that our ensemble significantly outperforms individual CNN models and several state-of-the-art baseline methods in terms of accuracy, precision, and robustness to novel attacks. The findings highlight the effectiveness of model diversity in capturing subtle inconsistencies introduced by video face manipulation. This research contributes a scalable and generalizable solution for multimedia forensics, with potential applications in law enforcement, social media content verification, and digital content authentication. Keywords: video face manipulation, deepfake detection, ensemble learning, convolutional neural networks, digital forensics, multimedia security.
