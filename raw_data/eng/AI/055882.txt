The objective of this research is to address the challenge of generating accurate depth maps from video sequences, a fundamental task in computer vision and 3D reconstruction. We propose DeepV2D, a novel framework that leverages a differentiable structure from motion (SfM) method for effective video-to-depth estimation. Our approach integrates deep learning techniques with traditional geometric SfM principles, enabling end-to-end learning and efficient handling of complex video dynamics. By utilizing a neural network architecture, DeepV2D learns to infer depth information directly from consecutive video frames, capturing fine-grained motion details and outperforming existing methods on benchmark datasets. The experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in accuracy and computational efficiency over state-of-the-art depth estimation models. Our findings indicate that incorporating differentiable SfM into a deep learning pipeline enhances the robustness and precision of depth prediction, making DeepV2D a valuable tool for applications in autonomous driving, augmented reality, and video-based 3D modeling. This research contributes to the field by introducing a highly effective framework for depth estimation, offering potential advancements in visual perception technologies. Key keywords include: deep learning, depth estimation, structure from motion, video analysis, 3D reconstruction.
