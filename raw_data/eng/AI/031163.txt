This paper presents LSALSA, a novel framework for accelerated source separation through learned sparse coding techniques. The objective of this research is to address the computational inefficiencies and limitations in accuracy prevalent in traditional source separation methods, particularly in scenarios that demand real-time or large-scale data processing. LSALSA leverages deep neural networks to approximate the iterative processes of conventional sparse coding, enabling fast and efficient decomposition of mixed signals into their constituent sources. Our approach incorporates a learned inference model that adapts to the statistical structure of input data, yielding more accurate and interpretable source representations. Extensive experiments on benchmark audio and image datasets demonstrate that LSALSA achieves superior separation accuracy while significantly reducing computational time when compared to classical algorithms such as ISTA and FISTA. The results indicate up to a fivefold speedup with no loss in quality, and enhanced robustness to noise and signal variations. The proposed method holds promise for applications in audio processing, biomedical signal analysis, and image denoising, where efficient and reliable source separation is critical. By bridging traditional optimization with modern deep learning advancements, LSALSA establishes a new paradigm for fast, scalable, and adaptive source separation. Key keywords: source separation, sparse coding, deep learning, accelerated algorithms, learned inference, signal processing.
