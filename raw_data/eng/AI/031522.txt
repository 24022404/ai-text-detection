The research aims to address the limitations of real-valued extreme learning machines (ELMs) by introducing a novel framework that leverages hypercomplex numbers, broadening the applicability and enhancing the performance of ELMs in various complex-valued signal processing tasks.

Methods/Approach: We develop and present a generalized framework for hypercomplex-valued ELMs, integrating quaternions and other hypercomplex systems to process multi-dimensional data more effectively than their real-valued counterparts. The methodology involves extending the traditional ELM formulation to accommodate hypercomplex operations, ensuring compatibility with vector spaces typified by hypercomplex algebra.

Results/Findings: Our framework demonstrates superior capabilities in learning and generalization across diverse datasets, particularly in domains requiring complex representation, such as electroencephalography (EEG) signal processing and image recognition tasks. Through extensive benchmarking, the hypercomplex-valued ELMs show marked improvements in accuracy, convergence speed, and computational efficiency compared to existing models.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed framework significantly enhances the scope and efficiency of extreme learning machines by incorporating hypercomplex numbers, providing a robust tool for high-dimensional data analysis. This advancement holds potential for a wide array of applications, including but not limited to, advanced neural processing, quantum computing models, and real-time image and signal sensing systems.

Keywords: hypercomplex numbers, extreme learning machines, quaternions, complex-valued signal processing, EEG, image recognition, high-dimensional data.
