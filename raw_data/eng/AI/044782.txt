Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) approaches often require substantial amounts of data to train effective representations, limiting their applicability in real-world settings. This research addresses the challenge of reducing data consumption in RL by investigating the pretraining of representations to facilitate more data-efficient learning.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel pretraining framework that leverages unsupervised learning techniques to develop robust representations before engaging in reinforcement learning tasks. This framework utilizes a variety of model architectures and algorithms to encode essential environmental features, thus enhancing the agent's ability to learn efficiently with minimal data. We specifically focus on deep learning architectures combined with a set of strategic pretraining objectives designed to align with subsequent RL phases.

Results/Findings: Through extensive experiments across multiple standard RL benchmarks, the pretrained models demonstrated significant improvements in learning efficiency, requiring up to 50% less data to achieve comparable or superior performance than models without pretraining. Additionally, our pretrained representations provided enhanced generalization capabilities across different tasks within the same environment, as evidenced by higher adaptability and reduced convergence times compared to traditional RL models.

Conclusion/Implications: This study introduces a powerful means to improve data efficiency in reinforcement learning through strategic representation pretraining. Our findings suggest that by optimizing the initial phase through unsupervised learning, RL practitioners can achieve more resource-efficient deployments, thereby broadening the practical applications of RL in data-constrained environments. This advancement lays the groundwork for future explorations into integrative pretraining strategies tailored to complex RL scenarios.

Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Pretraining, Data Efficiency, Representation Learning, Deep Learning, Unsupervised Learning, Generalization.
