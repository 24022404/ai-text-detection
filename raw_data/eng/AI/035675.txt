Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for learning representations of graph-structured data, with applications ranging from social network analysis to molecular property prediction. However, despite their widespread adoption, GCNs exhibit certain limitations that remain underexplored. This paper identifies a simple yet significant spectral failure mode in GCNs, where their performance degrades under specific spectral conditions of the input graph. We analyze this phenomenon by examining the relationship between the graph's spectral properties and the network's ability to propagate and aggregate information effectively. Through theoretical analysis and empirical experiments, we demonstrate that GCNs struggle to capture meaningful representations when the graph's Laplacian spectrum contains certain patterns, leading to suboptimal performance in downstream tasks. To address this issue, we propose a lightweight modification to the GCN architecture that mitigates the identified failure mode. Our experimental results on benchmark datasets show that the proposed approach improves robustness and generalization, particularly in scenarios where traditional GCNs underperform. This work contributes to a deeper understanding of GCN limitations and provides a practical solution for enhancing their reliability in real-world applications.  

**Keywords**: Graph Convolutional Networks, Spectral Analysis, Failure Modes, Graph Representation Learning, Robustness.
