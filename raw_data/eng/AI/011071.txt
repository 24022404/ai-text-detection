**Abstract**

Semi-supervised classification is critical in scenarios where labeled data is scarce but abundant unlabeled data is available. This paper introduces Masked Label Prediction, a unified message passing model designed to effectively leverage both labeled and unlabeled data for improved classification performance. The proposed approach employs a graph-based neural network framework in which node labels are systematically masked, and nodes are trained to predict the masked labels of their neighbors through iterative message passing. This mechanism enables the network to capture complex data structures and relationships within the graph, facilitating robust feature learning. Experimental evaluation on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrates that our model achieves superior accuracy and generalization compared to conventional semi-supervised and graph neural network methods. The results highlight the model's ability to propagate label information more efficiently across the graph, especially in settings with limited labeled samples. Our findings suggest that Masked Label Prediction provides a scalable and versatile solution for semi-supervised classification in domains such as social network analysis, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems. Key innovations include the unified handling of labeled and unlabeled nodes, improved label propagation, and enhanced classification robustness. **Keywords:** semi-supervised learning, message passing, graph neural networks, masked label prediction, node classification.
