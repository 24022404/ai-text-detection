This paper presents Neural Network iLQR, a novel reinforcement learning architecture designed to tackle complex trajectory optimization problems. The objective is to develop an efficient and adaptive framework that leverages the strengths of iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) and neural networks to improve the optimization of dynamic systems. Our approach combines the iLQR algorithm with a neural network to learn a feedback controller that can generalize across different initial conditions and system parameters. The neural network is trained using a model-free reinforcement learning method, allowing it to learn from trial and error without requiring an explicit system model. Results show that Neural Network iLQR outperforms traditional iLQR methods in terms of convergence speed and robustness, particularly in the presence of model uncertainty or nonlinear system dynamics. The proposed architecture has significant implications for real-world applications, such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and process control, where trajectory optimization is crucial for achieving efficient and safe operation. Key contributions include the integration of neural networks with iLQR, enabling improved adaptability and generalization, and the demonstration of the architecture's effectiveness in complex, high-dimensional control tasks. Relevant keywords: reinforcement learning, trajectory optimization, neural networks, iLQR, model-free control, adaptive control.
