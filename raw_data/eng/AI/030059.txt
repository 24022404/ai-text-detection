This paper addresses the challenge of training high-performing machine learning models with limited data, a common problem in many real-world applications. We introduce a novel modular learning approach that moves beyond traditional fine-tuning. Instead of adapting a pre-trained model holistically, our method decomposes the learning task into distinct modules, each responsible for a specific aspect of the desired functionality. These modules are then trained independently on smaller, targeted datasets and subsequently integrated to form the complete model. This modularity allows for efficient knowledge transfer and reduces the data requirements for each individual module. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach through experiments on image classification and natural language processing tasks, showing significant improvements in accuracy and generalization compared to standard fine-tuning techniques, especially when training data is scarce. Key contributions include the modular learning framework, a novel module integration strategy, and empirical validation of its superior performance on small datasets. Keywords: machine learning, small data, modular learning, transfer learning, fine-tuning, knowledge transfer, low-resource learning.
