Abstract  
This paper introduces a novel unsupervised learning framework in which hidden units compete to represent input data, yielding more distinct and informative feature encodings. Unlike traditional autoencoders or clustering methods that rely on fixed activation thresholds or global regularization, our approach employs a biologically inspired lateral-inhibition mechanism: during training, hidden units dynamically inhibit one another based on activation strength, encouraging sparse and decorrelated representations. We implement this competition layer within a shallow neural architecture and optimize it using standard stochastic gradient techniques augmented by a competition-based loss term. Empirical evaluations on image and sensor datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms baseline approaches—including k-means, independent component analysis, and conventional autoencoders—in clustering purity and reconstruction error. Moreover, features learned through competing hidden units yield up to a 12% improvement in downstream classification accuracy when paired with a simple support vector machine. These results highlight the capacity of competition-driven dynamics to discover robust latent structures without supervision. The proposed framework requires no labeled data, involves minimal parameter tuning, and can be seamlessly integrated into deeper networks. Our contributions pave the way for more efficient representation learning in domains where labeled samples are scarce.  
Keywords: Unsupervised learning, hidden-unit competition, sparse representations, lateral inhibition, neural networks, feature learning
