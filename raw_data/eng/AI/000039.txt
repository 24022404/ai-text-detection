Objective: Deepfake videos present growing risks to digital trust and security, necessitating robust detection methods that balance accuracy and efficiency. This paper introduces a novel hybrid architecture that integrates EfficientNet’s convolutional feature extractor with Vision Transformers (ViTs) to detect video deepfakes.  

Methods: We first employ EfficientNet to capture fine-grained spatial artifacts from individual frames. Extracted feature maps are then converted into a sequence of tokens via spatio-temporal patch embedding and fed into a ViT module, which models long-range dependencies across both space and time. The entire network is trained end-to-end on large-scale deepfake datasets, using a contrastive loss to enhance separability between real and manipulated footage.

Results: Evaluations on FaceForensics++, DFDC, and Celeb-DF benchmarks demonstrate that our method achieves detection accuracies of 97.8%, 94.2%, and 92.5%, respectively—outperforming state-of-the-art convolution-only and transformer-only approaches by 3–5% on average. Under video compression, resolution changes, and varied manipulation types, our hybrid model maintains robust performance, with only a 2–3% degradation. Computationally, it requires 30% fewer FLOPs than pure ViT pipelines and supports real-time inference at 45 frames per second on a single GPU.

Conclusion: By synergizing EfficientNet’s localized artifact detection with ViT’s global context modeling, our approach offers a practical, high-performance solution for video deepfake detection. This work advances secure AI applications in media forensics and paves the way for future research on domain adaptation and unsupervised detection.  

Keywords: Video Deepfake Detection; EfficientNet; Vision Transformer; Hybrid Architecture; Spatio-temporal Modeling; Media Forensics
