Automated grading of student assignments, particularly those with richly structured solutions like code or mathematical proofs, presents a significant challenge in education. This paper introduces "Generative Grading," a novel approach leveraging large language models (LLMs) to provide automated feedback with near human-level accuracy. Our method utilizes a fine-tuned LLM to generate expected solutions and identify discrepancies in student submissions, offering targeted feedback on errors in logic, syntax, and structure. We demonstrate that by framing the grading task as a generation problem, the model can effectively assess complex solutions and provide comprehensive feedback. Evaluated on datasets of student-generated code and mathematical proofs, Generative Grading achieves a grading accuracy comparable to human experts, significantly outperforming traditional automated grading systems. The implications of this research include reduced grading workload for educators, personalized learning experiences for students, and enhanced scalability of online education platforms. Key keywords: automated grading, large language models, LLM, code grading, mathematical proofs, education technology, AI in education, generative AI, feedback generation.
