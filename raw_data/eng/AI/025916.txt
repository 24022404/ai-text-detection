Dynamic assortment selection is a critical problem in modern retail and online marketplaces, where businesses must adaptively choose product assortments to maximize revenue while accounting for customer choice behaviors. This paper addresses the challenge of dynamic assortment selection under the nested logit models, which capture the hierarchical and correlated preferences common in real-world choice scenarios. We propose a novel algorithmic framework that leverages the structural properties of nested logit models to efficiently learn customer preferences and optimize assortments over time. The approach combines online learning with dynamic programming techniques to balance exploration and exploitation, thereby improving assortment quality as more data is collected. Through extensive simulation studies and numerical experiments, we demonstrate that our method outperforms traditional multinomial logit-based and static policies in terms of cumulative revenue and adaptability to changing customer behaviors. Theoretical analysis further establishes performance guarantees for convergence and regret bounds. Our findings provide new insights and practical tools for retailers and online platforms seeking to dynamically tailor their assortments to complex customer choice patterns. Key contributions include the integration of nested logit models into dynamic assortment optimization, development of efficient learning algorithms, and comprehensive evaluation against benchmark methods. Keywords: dynamic assortment, nested logit model, online learning, choice modeling, revenue optimization.
