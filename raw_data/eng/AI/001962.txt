Neural network optimization is a cornerstone of modern machine learning, yet it often encounters challenges related to nondeterminism and instability during training. This paper investigates the root causes of these phenomena and their impact on model performance and reproducibility. We propose a systematic approach to analyze the sources of nondeterminism, including random initialization, stochastic gradient descent (SGD) dynamics, and hardware-level variations. Through extensive experiments, we evaluate the effects of these factors on convergence behavior and model accuracy across diverse architectures and datasets. Our findings reveal that nondeterminism can lead to significant performance variability, even under identical training conditions. To address this, we introduce a novel stabilization technique that mitigates instability while preserving model expressiveness. The proposed method demonstrates improved convergence consistency and robustness across multiple benchmarks, outperforming existing approaches. This research provides valuable insights into the challenges of neural network optimization and offers practical solutions for enhancing training stability, with implications for applications requiring high reliability, such as autonomous systems and healthcare.  

**Keywords**: neural networks, optimization, nondeterminism, instability, stochastic gradient descent, reproducibility, machine learning.
