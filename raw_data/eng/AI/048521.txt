This paper presents a novel framework for signal processing by integrating variational models with Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). The primary objective is to address the challenge of effectively modeling and processing complex, high-dimensional data that exhibit irregular structures, such as social or sensor networks. We propose an approach that combines the flexibility of variational methods, known for their capability in regularization and denoising, with the representational power of GNNs to capture spatial and relational dependencies within graph-structured data. Our methodology involves formulating signal recovery and enhancement as a variational optimization problem, which is then solved using end-to-end trainable GNN architectures. Experimental evaluations on benchmark datasets for graph signal denoising, interpolation, and classification demonstrate that our approach outperforms conventional graph filtering techniques and state-of-the-art neural models across various metrics. The results highlight improvements in signal fidelity, robustness to noise, and generalization to different graph structures. This study advances the intersection of variational inference and deep learning on graphs, offering significant implications for applications in networked sensing, data compression, and graph-based data analytics. Key contributions include the novel variational-GNN hybrid model, extensive empirical analysis, and insights into the interpretability and scalability of the proposed approach. Keywords: variational models, graph signal processing, Graph Neural Networks, denoising, graph-structured data, deep learning, signal recovery.
