Understanding complex human activities within home environments is essential for advancing intelligent systems that interact naturally with daily life. This paper introduces the Home Action Genome, a novel framework designed to enable cooperative and compositional action understanding from visual and contextual data. Our approach models home activities as structured compositions of atomic actions and object interactions, facilitating a more granular and interpretable representation of complex behaviors. Leveraging a large-scale, annotated dataset of home-based actions, we employ a multi-modal deep learning architecture that integrates visual recognition, semantic embedding, and relational reasoning modules. Experiments demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing benchmarks in recognizing overlapping and sequential household actions, as well as in generalizing to novel activity combinations. The cooperative aspect is highlighted by the system’s ability to infer individual and joint actions among multiple agents, further reflecting the realistic social dynamics of home environments. Our findings suggest that the Home Action Genome can serve as a foundation for intelligent home assistants, robotics, and activity monitoring applications, promoting advancements in fine-grained activity recognition, context-aware reasoning, and human-robot cooperation. Keywords: action recognition, compositional modeling, cooperative understanding, home environments, deep learning, human activity analysis, intelligent systems.
