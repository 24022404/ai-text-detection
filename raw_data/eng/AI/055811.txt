This paper investigates the role of statistics and sampling methods in the realm of distributional reinforcement learning (RL), aiming to enhance the performance and accuracy of distributional RL algorithms. By analyzing the statistical properties of return distributions, we seek to improve the efficiency of policy learning in complex environments.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel approach that integrates advanced statistical techniques with distributional RL to optimize the sampling process. Specifically, we develop an algorithm that adjusts the sampling strategy based on the statistical distribution of returns, leveraging concepts such as quantile regression and statistical divergence measures. Our method is benchmarked against existing distributional RL approaches to demonstrate its effectiveness.

Results/Findings: The proposed method shows significant improvements in both convergence speed and policy performance across a variety of challenging RL environments. Our statistical sampling approach results in more accurate return distributions, which in turn supports the learning of superior policies. Experimental comparisons indicate that our approach outperforms traditional distributional RL methods in terms of stability and efficiency.

Conclusion/Implications: This research provides valuable insights into the interplay between statistics and reinforcement learning, highlighting the critical impact of effective sampling strategies in distributional RL. The findings contribute to the broader understanding of how statistical advancements can be harnessed to enhance machine learning algorithms. The proposed method has potential applications in fields requiring robust decision-making systems, such as robotics, autonomous systems, and complex simulations.

Keywords: distributional reinforcement learning, sampling strategies, statistical methods, quantile regression, return distributions, policy optimization.
