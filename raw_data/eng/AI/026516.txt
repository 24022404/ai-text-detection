Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have demonstrated remarkable success in generating high-quality synthetic data. However, their susceptibility to membership inference attacks (MIAs) poses significant privacy risks, as adversaries can infer whether specific data points were part of the training set. This paper introduces **privGAN**, a novel framework designed to protect GANs from MIAs while maintaining high data utility and computational efficiency. Unlike existing defense mechanisms that often incur substantial computational overhead or degrade the quality of generated samples, privGAN integrates a lightweight privacy-preserving module into the GAN architecture. This module employs a differential privacy-inspired noise injection strategy during training, effectively obfuscating the model's ability to memorize individual training samples. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that privGAN significantly reduces the success rate of MIAs by up to 70% compared to baseline GANs, while preserving the visual fidelity and diversity of generated outputs. Furthermore, the framework incurs minimal additional computational cost, making it suitable for real-world applications. The proposed approach represents a significant step forward in balancing privacy protection and data utility in generative models, with potential applications in healthcare, finance, and other privacy-sensitive domains.  

**Keywords**: GANs, membership inference attacks, privacy preservation, differential privacy, synthetic data generation, privGAN.
