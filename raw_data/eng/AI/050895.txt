Deep learning models have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in medical 3D segmentation, but their computational demands hinder deployment on resource-constrained devices. This paper introduces TernaryNet, a novel approach to accelerate inference speed without relying on GPUs. TernaryNet leverages sparse convolutions and binary convolutions to significantly reduce computational complexity and memory footprint. Specifically, we binarize the weights and activations within the convolutional layers, enabling efficient integer arithmetic operations. Furthermore, sparsity is introduced to further reduce the number of operations. The proposed method is evaluated on benchmark datasets for medical 3D segmentation, demonstrating a significant speedup compared to conventional full-precision models. Experimental results show that TernaryNet achieves comparable segmentation accuracy while reducing inference time by several folds and eliminating the need for GPUs. This allows for faster and more accessible medical image analysis, enabling real-time or near-real-time applications in point-of-care settings. Keywords: Deep learning, 3D Segmentation, Binary Convolution, Sparse Convolution, Medical Imaging, Model Compression, Efficient Inference.
