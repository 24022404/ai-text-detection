Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) are widely used for modeling sequential decision-making problems in artificial intelligence and machine learning. However, efficiently representing and solving large-scale MDPs remains a significant challenge due to their inherent complexity. This paper introduces a novel hierarchical representation learning framework for MDPs, designed to improve scalability and decision-making efficiency. The proposed approach leverages a multi-level abstraction mechanism to decompose complex state-action spaces into hierarchical structures, enabling more effective exploration and optimization. We employ deep reinforcement learning techniques to learn these hierarchical representations, ensuring adaptability across diverse environments. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms traditional flat MDP solvers in terms of computational efficiency and solution quality, particularly in high-dimensional and sparse-reward settings. The framework also exhibits strong generalization capabilities, making it suitable for real-world applications such as robotics, resource management, and autonomous systems. This work contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in MDP representation by providing a scalable and interpretable solution for complex decision-making tasks.  

**Keywords**: Markov Decision Processes, hierarchical representation learning, reinforcement learning, scalability, decision-making, artificial intelligence.
