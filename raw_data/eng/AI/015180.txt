This paper introduces Graph Intervention Networks (GINs), a novel approach for causal effect estimation in complex systems. The objective is to develop a robust and efficient method for estimating causal effects from observational data, addressing the challenges of confounding variables and selection bias. Our approach combines graph neural networks with intervention calculus to model causal relationships between variables. The GIN model learns to represent causal graphs and estimate interventional distributions, allowing for accurate estimation of causal effects. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of GINs in estimating causal effects on various benchmarks, outperforming existing methods. The key contributions of this research include the development of a scalable and flexible framework for causal effect estimation, and the introduction of a new loss function that encourages the model to learn causal relationships. This work has significant implications for applications in healthcare, social sciences, and economics, where causal effect estimation is crucial for decision-making. Key keywords: causal effect estimation, graph neural networks, intervention calculus, causal inference, graph intervention networks.
