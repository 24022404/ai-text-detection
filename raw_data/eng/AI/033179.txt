This paper investigates the impact of Q-function reuse on the total regret incurred by tabular, model-free reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms. Traditional model-free RL relies on learning action-value functions (Q-functions) from scratch, which can result in significant initial exploration costs and suboptimal decision-making. We propose and analyze a methodology for reusing previously learned Q-functions from related tasks or earlier episodes within the tabular RL setting. Our approach is evaluated through a series of experiments across multiple benchmark environments, measuring total regret as the principal performance metric. Results demonstrate that strategic Q-function reuse significantly reduces cumulative regret, particularly in scenarios with transferable structure or recurring patterns. Furthermore, we provide a theoretical analysis establishing conditions under which regret minimization is maximized through Q-function reuse. The findings reveal that, compared to baseline methods without reuse, our approach accelerates convergence and improves learning efficiency without introducing instability. This study highlights the potential of leveraging prior knowledge to enhance the practicality of tabular, model-free RL in resource-constrained or rapidly changing environments. Keywords: Q-function reuse, total regret, tabular reinforcement learning, model-free RL, knowledge transfer, learning efficiency.
