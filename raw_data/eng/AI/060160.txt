Fine-grained classification, which involves distinguishing between highly similar subcategories, remains a challenging task in computer vision due to the subtle differences between classes and the scarcity of labeled data. This paper presents a realistic evaluation of semi-supervised learning (SSL) techniques for fine-grained classification, aiming to address the limitations of fully supervised approaches in scenarios where labeled data is limited. We systematically compare state-of-the-art SSL methods, including consistency regularization and pseudo-labeling, across multiple fine-grained datasets to assess their effectiveness in leveraging unlabeled data. Our experiments reveal that SSL can significantly improve classification accuracy, particularly when combined with data augmentation strategies tailored to fine-grained tasks. However, we also identify key challenges, such as the sensitivity of SSL to class imbalance and the need for robust feature representations. The results demonstrate that SSL achieves competitive performance compared to fully supervised baselines, even with a fraction of labeled data, highlighting its potential for real-world applications. This study provides valuable insights into the practical applicability of SSL for fine-grained classification and offers recommendations for future research directions in this domain.  

**Keywords**: semi-supervised learning, fine-grained classification, consistency regularization, pseudo-labeling, data augmentation, computer vision.
