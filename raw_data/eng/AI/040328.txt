Deep learning (DL) has revolutionized remote sensing (RS) image analysis, yet its performance is often hindered by the scarcity of labeled training data. This paper presents a comprehensive review of state-of-the-art DL techniques designed to address the limited training data challenge in RS. We categorize and analyze various approaches, including transfer learning, data augmentation, meta-learning, self-supervised learning, and generative adversarial networks (GANs). Our analysis highlights the strengths and weaknesses of each technique in the context of different RS applications, such as land cover classification, object detection, and scene understanding. We further identify critical gaps in the existing literature, including the need for more robust methods that can generalize across diverse geographical regions and sensor modalities. The study also reveals a lack of standardized benchmarks for evaluating the performance of DL models trained on limited RS data. Finally, we discuss potential future research directions, emphasizing the importance of developing novel DL architectures and training strategies specifically tailored for low-data RS scenarios. This review provides valuable insights for researchers and practitioners aiming to leverage DL for RS applications with limited labeled data availability.

Keywords: Deep learning, Remote sensing, Limited data, Transfer learning, Data augmentation, Meta-learning, Self-supervised learning.
