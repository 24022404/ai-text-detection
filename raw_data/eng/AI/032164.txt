Reinforcement learning (RL) benchmarks are crucial for evaluating the performance of new algorithms. However, the computational expense of training agents on these benchmarks can be substantial. This paper explores the feasibility of using random weight guessing as a surprisingly effective baseline for assessing the inherent difficulty of RL environments. We introduce a novel methodology that evaluates benchmark performance by training multiple agents initialized with random weights and analyzing their achieved rewards. Our approach provides a computationally efficient alternative to traditional RL training, allowing for a rapid assessment of benchmark complexity. Results on a suite of standard RL benchmarks, including those from the OpenAI Gym and DeepMind Control Suite, demonstrate that random weight guessing can achieve surprisingly competitive performance, particularly in sparse reward environments. We find that the performance of random agents is correlated with environment complexity and the effectiveness of shaped reward functions. This work offers a new perspective on RL benchmark analysis and highlights the importance of considering simple baselines. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Benchmarks, Random Weights, Baseline, Environment Complexity.
