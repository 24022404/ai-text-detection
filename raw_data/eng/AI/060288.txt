This paper addresses the vulnerability of autoregressive models to environmental manipulations, with the objective of devising an optimal attack strategy. By leveraging techniques from reinforcement learning and game theory, we develop a novel approach that enables an adversary to manipulate the environment in a way that maximizes the error of the autoregressive model. Our method involves training an agent to identify the most critical input variables and perturbing them to create an adverse environment. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our approach, showing significant degradation in model performance. Our findings have important implications for the security and reliability of autoregressive models in real-world applications, highlighting the need for robustness and adaptability in the face of environmental uncertainties. The contributions of this research include the introduction of a new attack paradigm and the development of a framework for evaluating the vulnerability of autoregressive models to environmental manipulations, with potential applications in areas such as time series forecasting, anomaly detection, and sequential decision-making. Key keywords: autoregressive models, environmental manipulation, optimal attack, reinforcement learning, game theory, model vulnerability.
