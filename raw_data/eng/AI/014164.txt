Scene graph to image generation aims to create realistic images from abstract scene descriptions. Current methods often struggle with generating images that accurately reflect the relationships and attributes within the scene graph. This paper introduces a novel approach to improve image generation fidelity by learning canonical representations of scene graph elements. We propose a graph neural network architecture that encodes scene graphs into a latent space where nodes are represented in a canonical form, facilitating consistent and accurate image synthesis. Our method employs a contrastive learning strategy to enforce semantic consistency between scene graph nodes and their corresponding image regions. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of image quality and faithfulness to the input scene graph. Specifically, we observe significant improvements in object placement, attribute rendering, and relationship depiction. This work contributes a new paradigm for scene graph to image generation, offering enhanced control and realism in synthesized images. Keywords: Scene Graph, Image Generation, Graph Neural Networks, Canonical Representation, Contrastive Learning.
