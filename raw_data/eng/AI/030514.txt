This paper explores the application of transfer learning to estimate causal effects using neural networks, addressing the challenge of scarce data in treatment effect estimation. Our approach leverages pre-trained neural networks to initialize the weights of a target model, which is then fine-tuned on a smaller dataset to estimate causal effects. We employ a variant of the transformer architecture to model complex relationships between variables and account for confounding factors. Experimental results demonstrate that our transfer learning approach outperforms traditional methods in estimating causal effects, particularly in scenarios with limited data. The key findings highlight the potential of transfer learning to improve the accuracy and robustness of causal effect estimation. Our research contributes to the development of more reliable and efficient methods for causal inference, with significant implications for applications in healthcare, social sciences, and policy evaluation. Key keywords: transfer learning, neural networks, causal effects, treatment effect estimation, transformer architecture.
