This paper investigates the problem of causal discovery in linear acyclic models when the underlying data distributions are arbitrary and not restricted to specific forms such as Gaussian or exponential families. The objective is to accurately infer the causal structure among observed variables without relying on strong distributional assumptions, which often limit the applicability of traditional methods. We present a novel approach that leverages advances in statistical independence testing and structure learning to recover the underlying directed acyclic graph (DAG) in linear models, even when data exhibit heavy-tailed, multimodal, or otherwise complex distributions. The proposed method is evaluated through extensive simulations and real-world datasets, demonstrating its robustness and improved accuracy compared to existing algorithms that assume specific probabilistic forms or overlook distributional diversity. Experimental results show that our technique consistently identifies causal relationships with high precision and recall across a wide variety of distributions. This work expands the scope of causal discovery methods in information technology, making them suitable for more general and heterogeneous data scenarios. Key contributions include the development of a distribution-agnostic framework for linear acyclic model identification, as well as empirical validation of its effectiveness. The results have significant implications for automated causal inference in diverse fields such as machine learning, data analytics, and artificial intelligence. Keywords: causal discovery, linear acyclic models, arbitrary distributions, directed acyclic graph (DAG), independence testing, structure learning.
