Accurate forecasting of municipal water demand is critical for effective urban resource management and sustainable planning. This study addresses the challenge of univariate long-term forecasting of municipal water consumption, focusing solely on historical water usage data to predict future demand. We propose a comprehensive evaluation and comparison of classical time series models such as ARIMA and Exponential Smoothing, alongside state-of-the-art machine learning algorithms, including Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. Our methodology involves rigorous preprocessing of historical municipal water consumption datasets, model training, and performance assessment using metrics such as Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE). Experimental results demonstrate that advanced deep learning models, particularly LSTM, yield improved forecasting accuracy compared to traditional approaches, especially in capturing long-term consumption trends and seasonal variations. The study provides important insights into the capability of univariate models for reliable water demand forecasting, offering utility for municipalities in infrastructure planning, demand management, and policy formulation. The research highlights the advantages and limitations of relying solely on historical consumption data and underscores the potential for enhancing predictive accuracy through advanced time series modeling. Keywords: water demand forecasting, univariate time series, municipal water consumption, LSTM, ARIMA, deep learning, long-term prediction, urban planning.
