Phase retrieval is a critical problem in signal processing and imaging, with applications ranging from microscopy to wireless communications. This paper introduces a novel approach to phase retrieval using an Expectation Consistent Signal Recovery (ECSR) algorithm enhanced by a hypernetwork framework. The proposed method addresses the limitations of traditional phase retrieval techniques, which often struggle with convergence and accuracy in noisy or underdetermined scenarios. The ECSR algorithm leverages probabilistic inference to iteratively refine signal estimates, while the hypernetwork dynamically adapts the algorithm's parameters to optimize performance across diverse datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves superior reconstruction accuracy and robustness compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly in low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) environments. The integration of hypernetworks not only improves convergence rates but also reduces computational overhead, making the method scalable for real-world applications. This work contributes a significant advancement in phase retrieval by combining probabilistic inference with adaptive learning, offering a versatile and efficient solution for complex signal recovery tasks.  

**Keywords**: Phase retrieval, Expectation Consistent Signal Recovery, hypernetwork, probabilistic inference, signal processing, adaptive learning.
