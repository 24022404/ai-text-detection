Monocular 3D object detection is a critical challenge in computer vision, particularly for applications in autonomous driving and robotics, where accurate depth estimation from a single image is essential. This paper introduces FCOS3D, a fully convolutional one-stage framework designed to address the limitations of existing methods in monocular 3D object detection. Unlike traditional approaches that rely on complex multi-stage pipelines or anchor-based mechanisms, FCOS3D leverages a streamlined, anchor-free architecture to directly predict 3D bounding boxes from 2D image features. The proposed method integrates key innovations, including a novel depth-aware feature extraction module and a multi-task learning strategy that jointly optimizes object localization, orientation, and depth estimation. Extensive experiments on the KITTI and nuScenes datasets demonstrate that FCOS3D achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of both accuracy and inference speed. The results highlight the framework's robustness and scalability, making it a promising solution for real-time 3D perception tasks. This work contributes to advancing monocular 3D detection by offering a simpler yet highly effective approach, with potential applications in autonomous systems and augmented reality.  

**Keywords**: Monocular 3D object detection, fully convolutional networks, anchor-free detection, depth estimation, autonomous driving, computer vision.
