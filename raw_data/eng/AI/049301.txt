This paper presents a novel approach for self-supervised visual representation learning by leveraging hierarchical grouping mechanisms. The primary objective is to address the challenge of learning robust visual features from unlabeled image data, which is crucial for reducing reliance on large quantities of annotated samples. Our method introduces a hierarchical grouping framework that dynamically organizes image regions at multiple scales, enabling the model to capture both local and global visual structures without human supervision. The proposed system employs a combination of contrastive learning objectives and region-based clustering to guide the representation learning process. Experimental results on several benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing self-supervised learning baselines, particularly in tasks such as image classification and object detection. Notably, the learned representations exhibit improved transferability and generalization across diverse visual domains. This research contributes an effective and scalable solution to unsupervised feature learning and highlights the impact of hierarchical organization in self-supervised visual understanding. Potential applications include computer vision tasks where annotated data is scarce, such as medical imaging and autonomous navigation. 

Keywords: self-supervised learning, visual representation, hierarchical grouping, unsupervised learning, contrastive learning, computer vision.
