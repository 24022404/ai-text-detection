This study investigates the effectiveness of feature representation in deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for the classification of diffuse lung diseases, leveraging a two-stage feature transfer approach. The objective is to enhance the accuracy and robustness of disease classification by analyzing how intermediate and final layer features contribute to model performance. In the first stage, a pre-trained CNN model is fine-tuned on a large-scale medical imaging dataset to extract generalizable features. In the second stage, these features are transferred and further refined using a smaller, domain-specific dataset of diffuse lung disease images. The proposed method is evaluated on a publicly available dataset, with performance metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. Experimental results demonstrate that the two-stage feature transfer approach significantly improves classification performance compared to traditional single-stage transfer learning and baseline models. The analysis reveals that intermediate layers capture discriminative patterns crucial for distinguishing subtle disease characteristics, while deeper layers contribute to overall feature robustness. This research highlights the importance of feature representation analysis in medical image classification and provides a novel framework for improving diagnostic accuracy in diffuse lung disease. The findings have potential implications for developing more reliable AI-driven diagnostic tools in healthcare.  

**Keywords**: Deep convolutional neural networks, feature transfer, diffuse lung disease, medical image classification, two-stage learning, feature representation.
