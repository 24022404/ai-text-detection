This paper addresses the challenge of efficient knowledge transfer in Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) training by exploring the synergies between semantic and visual similarities. The objective is to develop a novel approach that leverages these similarities to improve the transferability of knowledge across different CNN architectures and datasets. Our method utilizes a dual-embedding framework, combining semantic embeddings from natural language processing with visual embeddings from image features, to create a unified representation space. This enables the efficient transfer of knowledge from a pre-trained CNN to a target network, reducing the need for extensive retraining. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms traditional transfer learning methods, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and reducing training time by up to 50%. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a semantic-visual similarity metric and a novel dual-embedding framework, which can be applied to various CNN-based applications, such as image classification, object detection, and segmentation. Our work has important implications for the development of more efficient and adaptable CNN training methods, with potential applications in areas like computer vision, robotics, and autonomous systems, and is relevant to keywords like deep learning, knowledge transfer, and neural network pruning.
