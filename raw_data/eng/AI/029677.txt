This paper presents a novel approach to extracting traffic primitives directly from naturalistically logged data, aiming to improve the decision-making capabilities of self-driving vehicles. Our objective is to develop a methodology that can accurately identify and characterize complex traffic scenarios from real-world data, enabling more effective and safe autonomous driving. We employ a combination of machine learning and computer vision techniques to analyze large datasets of naturalistic driving logs, extracting meaningful traffic primitives such as vehicle interactions, traffic rules, and road user behavior. Our results show that the proposed approach can effectively extract traffic primitives with high accuracy, outperforming existing methods in terms of precision and recall. The implications of this research are significant, as it can contribute to the development of more advanced and reliable self-driving systems, enhancing road safety and efficiency. Key contributions include the introduction of a new framework for traffic primitive extraction, the demonstration of its effectiveness in real-world scenarios, and the potential for integration with existing autonomous driving architectures. Relevant keywords: self-driving vehicles, traffic primitives, naturalistic logging, machine learning, computer vision, autonomous driving.
