**Abstract:**

Machine learning models frequently encounter challenges when required to learn and predict values spanning many orders of magnitude, a scenario common in applications such as scientific computing, financial analysis, sensor monitoring, and natural language processing. This paper addresses the limitations of conventional approaches, which often struggle with numerical instability, biased representations, and suboptimal generalization when dealing with such wide-ranging data. We propose a novel framework that integrates adaptive normalization techniques with scale-invariant architectures, enabling robust learning across disparate value ranges. Extensive experiments are conducted on synthetic benchmarks and real-world datasets exhibiting high dynamic range characteristics. Our methods demonstrate significant improvements in predictive accuracy, convergence speed, and stability compared to standard machine learning baselines, including deep neural networks and gradient boosting machines. The results also reveal enhanced model interpretability and improved handling of previously underrepresented data extremes. Our approach enables IT systems to better process heterogeneous data, facilitating applications in anomaly detection, risk assessment, and scientific modeling. By providing a reliable mechanism for learning values across many orders of magnitude, this work advances the scalability, effectiveness, and applicability of machine learning methods in complex, real-world scenarios.

**Keywords:** machine learning, value scaling, normalization, dynamic range, data representation, model stability, high-magnitude data
