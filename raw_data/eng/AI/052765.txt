Unsupervised action recognition remains a challenging task in computer vision due to the lack of labeled data and the complexity of modeling temporal dynamics. This paper introduces a novel framework, Augmented Skeleton-Based Contrastive Action Learning (ASCAL), combined with a Momentum Long Short-Term Memory (Momentum LSTM) network, to address these challenges. The proposed approach leverages skeleton data, which provides a compact and robust representation of human actions, and employs contrastive learning to learn discriminative features without requiring labeled datasets. The Momentum LSTM enhances temporal modeling by incorporating momentum-based updates, enabling more stable and efficient training. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that ASCAL outperforms existing unsupervised methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance in action recognition tasks. The framework’s ability to generalize across diverse datasets and its computational efficiency highlight its potential for real-world applications, such as surveillance, human-computer interaction, and healthcare. This work contributes a scalable and effective solution for unsupervised action recognition, paving the way for future research in self-supervised learning for temporal data.  

**Keywords**: unsupervised learning, action recognition, skeleton data, contrastive learning, Momentum LSTM, temporal modeling.
