Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have emerged as powerful tools for analyzing structured data, yet a significant challenge remains in effectively integrating spectral and spatial domain information to enhance model performance. This paper addresses this gap by proposing a novel hybrid framework that seamlessly combines spectral graph theory with spatial message-passing mechanisms. The proposed approach leverages spectral graph convolutions to capture global structural patterns while employing localized spatial aggregation to refine node representations. We introduce a dual-path architecture that dynamically balances spectral and spatial features, enabling the model to adapt to diverse graph topologies. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art GNNs in tasks such as node classification and graph classification, achieving improvements in accuracy and robustness. Furthermore, the framework exhibits superior scalability and computational efficiency, making it suitable for large-scale graph analysis. The results highlight the importance of bridging spectral and spatial domains in GNNs, offering new insights for advancing graph representation learning. This research contributes to the broader field of machine learning by providing a versatile and effective solution for graph-based applications, with potential implications in social network analysis, recommendation systems, and bioinformatics.  

**Keywords**: Graph Neural Networks, Spectral Graph Theory, Spatial Aggregation, Hybrid Models, Node Classification, Graph Representation Learning.
