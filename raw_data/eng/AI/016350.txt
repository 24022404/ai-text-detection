This paper introduces SMILE, a novel approach to efficient transfer learning leveraging self-distillation and mixup techniques. The objective is to improve the adaptability and performance of pre-trained models on downstream tasks with limited labeled data. SMILE employs a self-distillation framework that combines the benefits of mixup-based data augmentation with the knowledge distillation process, allowing the model to learn from its own predictions and generate more diverse and representative samples. Experimental results demonstrate that SMILE outperforms existing transfer learning methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets. The key findings highlight the effectiveness of self-distillation in preserving the knowledge of pre-trained models while adapting to new tasks. The proposed approach has significant implications for efficient transfer learning, enabling the development of more accurate and robust models with reduced labeling efforts. SMILE contributes to the advancement of transfer learning, self-distillation, and mixup techniques, with potential applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and other areas of artificial intelligence, including deep learning, knowledge distillation, and data augmentation.
