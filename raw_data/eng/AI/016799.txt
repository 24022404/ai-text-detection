**Abstract**

Phase contrast microscopy is a widely used technique in biomedical research for visualizing live, unstained cells. Accurate segmentation of cell populations in phase contrast images is essential for quantitative analysis in various biological studies, including cell tracking, morphology assessment, and high-content screening. This survey provides a comprehensive review of existing methods for cell population segmentation in phase contrast microscopy images, highlighting advances in both traditional image processing techniques and modern deep learning-based approaches. We systematically categorize segmentation algorithms based on their methodological foundations, such as thresholding, edge detection, region-based methods, as well as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and U-Net architectures. The survey examines each method's performance, robustness to imaging artifacts, and applicability to different cell types and densities. We also discuss key challenges such as halo artifacts, low contrast between cells and background, and variability in cell morphology. Comparative analysis of evaluation metrics, annotated datasets, and software tools is provided to guide practitioners in method selection. The review concludes by identifying current gaps in the field, such as the need for domain-adaptive and real-time segmentation solutions, and outlines emerging trends including the integration of attention mechanisms and self-supervised learning. Keywords: phase contrast microscopy, cell segmentation, image analysis, deep learning, biomedical imaging, U-Net, computer vision.
