Context-conditional dynamics models hold significant promise for robotics and autonomous systems, enabling them to adapt to varying environmental conditions and task requirements. However, the performance of these models heavily relies on the quality and relevance of the training data. This paper addresses the critical challenge of optimal data collection for context-conditional dynamics models. We introduce a novel active learning framework that strategically selects data points to maximize model accuracy and generalization. Our approach leverages an information-theoretic acquisition function that balances exploration of the context space with exploitation of regions where model uncertainty is high. We evaluate our framework on simulated robotic manipulation tasks with varying contextual factors, demonstrating significant improvements in prediction accuracy and sample efficiency compared to random data collection and other active learning baselines. The results highlight the importance of context-aware data acquisition for training robust and adaptable dynamics models, paving the way for more efficient and reliable robot learning.
Keywords: Context-conditional dynamics, active learning, data collection, robotics, model-based control, information theory.
