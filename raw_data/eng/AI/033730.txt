Dimensionality reduction techniques are crucial for processing high-dimensional data, yet interpreting their results remains challenging. This paper introduces a novel framework leveraging contrastive learning to enhance the analysis of dimensionality reduction outcomes. Our approach trains an encoder to map data points into a latent space where neighbors from the original high-dimensional space are closer than dissimilar points. This structured latent space facilitates the identification of meaningful data clusters and relationships. We evaluate our method on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating improved performance in downstream tasks such as clustering and classification compared to traditional dimensionality reduction techniques alone. The contrastive learning framework provides a valuable tool for understanding and validating dimensionality reduction results, offering insights into the underlying data structure and improving the reliability of subsequent analyses. Keywords: Dimensionality Reduction, Contrastive Learning, Data Analysis, Machine Learning, Embedding.
