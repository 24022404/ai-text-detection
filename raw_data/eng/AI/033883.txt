This paper presents a novel approach to night vision obstacle detection and avoidance using bio-inspired vision sensors. The objective is to develop a robust and efficient system that mimics the exceptional low-light sensitivity of nocturnal animals, enabling safe navigation in poorly lit environments. Our approach utilizes a custom-designed vision sensor that emulates the structure and function of the human retina, combined with advanced image processing algorithms inspired by the visual processing mechanisms of nocturnal creatures. The system is evaluated on a range of nighttime scenarios, demonstrating significant improvements in obstacle detection accuracy and response time compared to traditional computer vision methods. The results show that our bio-inspired system can effectively detect and avoid obstacles in low-light conditions, with potential applications in autonomous vehicles, robotics, and surveillance systems. Key contributions include the development of a unique vision sensor design and the implementation of a novel image processing framework that leverages the principles of biological vision. Relevant keywords: bio-inspired vision, night vision, obstacle detection, autonomous systems, computer vision, image processing.
