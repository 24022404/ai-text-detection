Lifelong learning aims to enable machine learning models to continuously acquire new knowledge while retaining previously learned tasks, a critical capability for real-world applications. However, this process often suffers from catastrophic forgetting and scalability challenges. This paper introduces a novel framework, *Regularize, Expand, and Compress (REC)*, which leverages multi-task learning and non-expansive AutoML techniques to address these limitations. The proposed approach regularizes model updates to preserve prior knowledge, dynamically expands the architecture to accommodate new tasks, and compresses the model to maintain efficiency. By integrating non-expansive optimization, REC ensures stable learning across tasks without compromising performance. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that REC achieves superior task retention and scalability compared to existing lifelong learning methods, with significant improvements in accuracy and computational efficiency. The framework’s ability to balance model complexity and performance makes it a promising solution for deploying lifelong learning systems in resource-constrained environments. This research contributes to advancing AutoML by introducing a scalable and adaptive lifelong learning paradigm with broad applications in robotics, natural language processing, and autonomous systems.  

**Keywords**: Lifelong learning, multi-task learning, AutoML, non-expansive optimization, catastrophic forgetting, model compression.
