This paper introduces *Fractal Gaussian Networks* (FGNs), a novel sparse random graph model grounded in Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos (GMC). The objective of this work is to address the limitations of existing graph models in capturing the intricate, self-similar structures observed in real-world networks, such as social, biological, and technological systems. FGNs leverage the mathematical framework of GMC to generate graphs with inherent fractal properties, enabling the modeling of networks with multi-scale dependencies and non-uniform edge distributions. The proposed approach involves constructing a weighted adjacency matrix using GMC-driven stochastic processes, followed by sparsification techniques to ensure scalability and computational efficiency. Experimental results demonstrate that FGNs outperform traditional models, such as Erdős-Rényi and Barabási-Albert networks, in replicating key topological features, including degree distribution, clustering coefficients, and community structures. Furthermore, FGNs exhibit robustness in preserving fractal characteristics across varying network sizes. The implications of this research are significant for applications in network science, including anomaly detection, community identification, and the study of complex systems. By bridging the gap between fractal geometry and random graph theory, FGNs offer a powerful tool for analyzing and simulating real-world networks with high fidelity.  

**Keywords**: Fractal Gaussian Networks, Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos, random graph models, fractal geometry, network science, sparse graphs, complex systems.
