This paper addresses the challenge of training robust image classifiers from positive unlabeled (PU) datasets, where only positive samples and a large pool of unlabeled data are available. We introduce a novel approach for generating relevant counter-examples from the unlabeled set, effectively augmenting the training data and mitigating confirmation bias inherent in PU learning. Our method leverages an adversarial training framework coupled with a similarity metric to identify and refine hard negative samples. Specifically, we generate counter-examples that are visually similar to positive examples but are confidently classified as negative by a dynamically updated discriminator. We evaluate our approach on several benchmark image classification datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in classification accuracy and robustness compared to state-of-the-art PU learning methods. Furthermore, we analyze the characteristics of the generated counter-examples, providing insights into their impact on model generalization. The results highlight the effectiveness of our approach in learning discriminative features from limited supervision, enabling the development of more accurate image classifiers in PU settings. Keywords: Positive Unlabeled Learning, Image Classification, Counter-Example Generation, Adversarial Training, Hard Negative Mining.
