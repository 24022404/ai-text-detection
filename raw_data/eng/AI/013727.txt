The rapid evolution of 5G and beyond networks necessitates efficient and scalable user association strategies to optimize network performance and resource utilization. This paper addresses the challenge of designing transferable and distributed user association policies that can adapt to diverse network environments and dynamic user demands. We propose a novel framework leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) to develop intelligent policies that dynamically associate users with base stations while considering factors such as load balancing, latency, and energy efficiency. Our approach employs a distributed architecture, enabling localized decision-making without centralized control, thus enhancing scalability and robustness. Through extensive simulations, we demonstrate that the proposed policies outperform traditional methods in terms of throughput, latency reduction, and energy savings. Furthermore, the transferability of these policies across different network configurations is validated, showcasing their adaptability to heterogeneous environments. The findings highlight the potential of RL-based distributed solutions for future networks, offering a pathway toward more efficient and resilient communication systems. Key contributions include the development of a scalable framework, empirical validation of transferability, and insights into the trade-offs between performance metrics. This research paves the way for intelligent, adaptive user association in next-generation networks.  

**Keywords**: 5G networks, user association, reinforcement learning, distributed systems, transferable policies, network optimization.
