This paper introduces MEVA, a large-scale multiview, multimodal video dataset designed to advance research in activity detection. The primary objective of MEVA is to address existing limitations in activity recognition datasets by providing synchronized video streams from multiple camera viewpoints, capturing diverse and complex real-world activities. The dataset incorporates multimodal data, including high-resolution RGB videos, audio recordings, and sensor metadata, offering extensive contextual information for robust model development. Activities are meticulously annotated, spanning a wide range of human interactions and events across various environments. To demonstrate MEVA’s utility, we benchmark several state-of-the-art activity detection algorithms, evaluating their performance in multiview and cross-modal settings. The results highlight significant improvements in activity recognition accuracy when leveraging MEVA’s comprehensive spatial and contextual data. Our findings underscore the advantages of integrating multiple views and modalities for fine-grained activity understanding. MEVA sets a new standard for dataset scale and diversity in the activity detection domain, fostering innovation in deep learning, computer vision, and multimodal signal processing. This contribution supports the development of more accurate and generalizable activity recognition systems, with potential applications in surveillance, smart environments, and human-computer interaction. 

Keywords: activity detection, multiview video, multimodal dataset, human activity recognition, computer vision, deep learning, surveillance.
