Graph Convolutional Neural Networks (GCNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for learning representations of graph-structured data, with applications ranging from social network analysis to molecular property prediction. However, deeper GCN architectures often suffer from performance degradation, limiting their ability to capture complex hierarchical patterns. This paper investigates the critical role of training strategies in unlocking the potential of deeper GCNs. We propose a novel training framework that addresses key challenges such as over-smoothing, gradient vanishing, and overfitting. Our approach integrates adaptive regularization techniques, layer-wise learning rate adjustments, and a dynamic dropout mechanism tailored for graph data. Through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, we demonstrate that our framework significantly improves the performance of deeper GCNs, achieving state-of-the-art results in node classification and graph classification tasks. Furthermore, we provide insights into the interplay between model depth, training dynamics, and graph structure, offering practical guidelines for designing and training deeper GCNs. Our findings highlight the importance of training strategies in advancing graph-based learning and open new avenues for scaling GCNs to more complex and large-scale graph datasets.  

**Keywords**: Graph Convolutional Neural Networks, Deep Learning, Training Strategies, Over-smoothing, Node Classification, Graph Classification.
