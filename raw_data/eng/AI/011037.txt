This study addresses the challenges associated with document layout analysis by exploring a Human-In-The-Loop (HITL) framework to enhance automatic processing methodologies. Traditional algorithms often struggle with the complexity and variability found in document layouts across different formats and stylistic elements. Our research aims to improve the accuracy and adaptability of layout analysis by incorporating human insights and expertise into the computational process.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel HITL framework that integrates human feedback with advanced machine learning and image processing techniques. The system employs an iterative process where human experts can provide input on the layout interpretation, guiding the machine learning models in refining their predictions. Key components include an interactive interface for real-time feedback, a robust algorithm for detecting diverse layout patterns, and a training mechanism for continual improvement of the model based on human interaction.

Results/Findings: The proposed system was evaluated against state-of-the-art automated layout analysis tools using a diverse dataset of digital documents. Results demonstrate a significant improvement in accuracy, with the HITL approach achieving higher precision and recall rates in identifying complex layout structures. The system also showed enhanced adaptability to new and unseen document styles compared to fully automated solutions.

Conclusion/Implications: The integration of human expertise within the document layout analysis process offers substantial benefits, primarily in achieving greater accuracy and flexibility. Our findings suggest that the HITL framework can be a viable solution for industries requiring precise document processing, such as digital archiving, legal documentation, and content management systems. Furthermore, this approach highlights the potential of human-machine collaboration in advancing the capabilities of document management technologies.

Keywords: Human-In-The-Loop, document layout analysis, machine learning, image processing, human-computer interaction, automation, digital documents.
