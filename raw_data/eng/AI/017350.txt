This paper presents an innovative approach to real-time dense 3D reconstruction by combining Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) with multi-spectral photometric stereo techniques. The primary objective is to enhance the accuracy and detail of 3D reconstructions in dynamic environments, a challenge faced in fields like augmented reality and robotics. Our method leverages the robust spatial mapping capabilities of SLAM with the surface detail capture of photometric stereo, which utilizes multiple light spectra to recover surface normal information. Through an integrated system design, we achieve real-time processing that can adapt to varying lighting and material properties, ensuring high fidelity in the reconstructed 3D models.

Our approach was validated through extensive experimental evaluations, demonstrating superior performance in both indoor and outdoor settings compared to traditional monocular SLAM systems and other stereophotometric methods. Results indicate significant improvements in the density and precision of the reconstructed surfaces, with enhanced resilience to changing lighting conditions. The framework handles complex textures and geometries more effectively, providing a comprehensive solution for detailed 3D mapping.

The implications of our findings suggest considerable advancements in applications that require detailed environmental understanding and interaction, such as virtual reality, autonomous navigation, and industrial inspection. The research contributes to the current landscape of 3D reconstruction technologies by offering a novel method that bridges the gap between real-time processing demands and high-accuracy requirements. Key keywords include SLAM, multi-spectral photometric stereo, 3D reconstruction, real-time processing, and surface normals.
