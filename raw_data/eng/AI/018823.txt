Offline reinforcement learning (Offline RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training intelligent agents directly from previously collected data, eliminating the need for costly and potentially unsafe online interactions. This paper presents a comprehensive hands-on exploration of offline RL, focusing on practical methodologies, challenges, and applications for deploying agents in real-world scenarios. We investigate foundational offline RL algorithms, including batch-constrained and conservative approaches, and provide a detailed experimental framework for evaluating their effectiveness across benchmark tasks. Our methodology encompasses data preprocessing, model selection, and policy evaluation using standard datasets, enabling a systematic comparison of algorithmic performance. Key findings reveal distinct trade-offs between exploration, exploitation, and dataset quality, as well as the critical role of robust policy evaluation metrics in stable learning. We further discuss implementation considerations, limitations of current offline RL algorithms, and opportunities for integrating advances from supervised learning and simulation environments. The hands-on perspective offered in this work serves as a practical guide for both researchers and practitioners, highlighting best practices, common pitfalls, and emerging trends. Our results underscore the promise of offline RL for scalable, efficient decision-making systems in areas such as robotics, healthcare, and recommendation engines. Keywords: offline reinforcement learning, batch RL, policy evaluation, machine learning, safe AI, hands-on tutorial, autonomous systems.
