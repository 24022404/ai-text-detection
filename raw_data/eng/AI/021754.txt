This paper addresses the challenge of medical image segmentation using semi-supervised few-shot learning, aiming to improve the accuracy and efficiency of segmenting medical images with limited labeled data. Our approach combines the strengths of few-shot learning and semi-supervised learning to develop a robust model that can learn from a few labeled examples and a large number of unlabeled images. We propose a novel framework that utilizes a transformer-based architecture and a self-supervised loss function to effectively leverage the unlabeled data and adapt to new tasks with few labeled samples. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several medical image segmentation benchmarks. Our approach has significant implications for clinical applications, enabling accurate and efficient segmentation of medical images with minimal labeled data. Key contributions include the development of a semi-supervised few-shot learning framework for medical image segmentation, improved performance on benchmark datasets, and the potential for application in real-world clinical settings, highlighting the importance of keywords such as few-shot learning, semi-supervised learning, medical image segmentation, transformer, and self-supervised learning.
