This paper presents a novel approach to forecasting people trajectories and head poses by jointly reasoning on tracklets and vislets. The objective is to improve the accuracy of human behavior prediction in complex scenes, enabling enhanced applications in surveillance, autonomous systems, and social robotics. Our method leverages a deep learning framework that integrates tracklet-based motion forecasting with vislet-based head pose estimation, allowing for a more comprehensive understanding of human movement and attention. The approach is evaluated on a large-scale dataset, demonstrating significant improvements in trajectory forecasting and head pose estimation accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods. Key findings include the importance of jointly modeling tracklets and vislets, as well as the effectiveness of our proposed attention mechanism in capturing complex human interactions. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to provide more accurate and nuanced predictions of human behavior, with potential applications in fields such as autonomous driving, human-robot interaction, and smart cities. Keywords: human trajectory forecasting, head pose estimation, tracklets, vislets, deep learning, computer vision.
