This paper introduces Gaze360, a novel approach to gaze estimation that operates in completely uncontrolled environments, eliminating the need for physical constraints on the user. The objective is to develop a system capable of accurately estimating gaze direction in everyday settings, leveraging advancements in computer vision and machine learning. Our method utilizes a deep learning-based framework, incorporating a convolutional neural network (CNN) to learn gaze patterns from a large dataset of images captured in various conditions. The results demonstrate significant improvements in gaze estimation accuracy, outperforming existing methods in both laboratory and real-world scenarios. Key findings include the ability to estimate gaze direction with high precision, even in the presence of varying lighting conditions, head poses, and distances. The implications of this research are substantial, enabling applications such as human-computer interaction, virtual reality, and automotive safety. Gaze360 contributes to the field by providing a robust, flexible, and widely applicable gaze estimation solution, paving the way for innovative applications in fields like AI, computer vision, and human-machine interaction. Relevant keywords: gaze estimation, computer vision, deep learning, CNN, human-computer interaction, virtual reality.
