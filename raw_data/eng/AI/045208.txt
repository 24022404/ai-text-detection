Objective: Scene Graph Generation (SGG) seeks to predict objects and their relationships in images, offering a structured representation that underpins applications such as visual question answering, image captioning, and robotic perception. Despite rapid advances, the field lacks a standardized evaluation framework, hindering fair comparison and progress.

Methods: We introduce the Image Scene Graph Generation (SGG) Benchmark, a unified suite comprising (1) a curated dataset split with balanced relation distributions, (2) a set of rigorous evaluation metrics—including Mean Recall@K, Zero-Shot Recall, and Graph Consistency Score—and (3) an open-source toolkit for model training, inference, and metric computation. We apply this benchmark to five representative SGG models spanning graph neural networks, transformer-based encoders, and joint detection-relation pipelines.

Results: Under the benchmark’s balanced splits, we observe that state-of-the-art methods often overfit to frequent relation categories, yielding high overall recall but low mean recall on rare relationships. Zero-shot evaluation further reveals that models struggle to generalize unseen object-relation pairs. Graph Consistency analysis highlights trade-offs between relation accuracy and overall scene coherence. Our toolkit enables reproducible, end-to-end comparisons, uncovering that a simple frequency-debiasing strategy boosts mean recall by up to 15% with minimal impact on overall performance.

Conclusion: The Image SGG Benchmark provides researchers with a transparent, extensible platform to measure and improve the robustness, fairness, and generalization of scene graph generation models. By standardizing datasets, metrics, and evaluation protocols, it lays the groundwork for accelerated innovation and more reliable integration of SGG into downstream vision–language systems.

Keywords: scene graph generation, benchmark, evaluation metrics, graph neural networks, zero-shot recall, visual understanding
