This paper presents an explainable deep vision system designed for animal classification and detection in trail-camera images, incorporating automatic post-deployment retraining to enhance accuracy and adaptability. The objective of this research is to address the challenges of wildlife monitoring, where traditional methods often struggle with varying environmental conditions, diverse species, and limited labeled datasets. Our approach leverages a deep learning framework based on convolutional neural networks (CNNs) combined with explainability techniques to provide transparent and interpretable predictions. The system is equipped with an automatic retraining mechanism that continuously updates the model using newly captured images, ensuring improved performance over time without manual intervention. Experimental results demonstrate the system's ability to achieve high classification accuracy and robust detection across multiple species, outperforming existing methods in dynamic real-world scenarios. The integration of explainability not only enhances trust in the model's predictions but also aids ecologists in understanding decision-making processes. This research contributes a novel, scalable solution for wildlife monitoring, with potential applications in biodiversity conservation, ecological research, and automated environmental surveillance. Key innovations include the combination of explainable AI with adaptive retraining, offering a significant advancement over static models in the field.  

**Keywords**: deep learning, explainable AI, animal classification, trail-camera images, automatic retraining, wildlife monitoring, convolutional neural networks.
