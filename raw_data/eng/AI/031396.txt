Predicting brain connectivity patterns is crucial for understanding neurological disorders. This paper introduces a novel topology-aware generative adversarial network (TAGAN) for jointly predicting multiple brain graphs from a single brain graph. TAGAN leverages a generator-discriminator framework, incorporating a topology-preserving loss function that enforces structural similarity between the predicted and ground truth graphs. The generator employs graph convolutional networks to learn node embeddings and predict edge probabilities for multiple target graphs simultaneously. The discriminator distinguishes between real and generated graph sets, guiding the generator to produce more realistic connectivity patterns. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that TAGAN outperforms existing methods in terms of prediction accuracy and topological consistency. Specifically, TAGAN achieves superior performance in capturing intricate relationships and dependencies between different brain networks. This approach facilitates a more comprehensive understanding of brain organization and provides a valuable tool for studying neurological diseases.
Keywords: Brain Graph Prediction, Generative Adversarial Networks, Graph Convolutional Networks, Connectome, Topology-Aware Learning.
