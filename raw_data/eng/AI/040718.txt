This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently adapting machine learning models to new tasks with limited data, a critical requirement in many real-world applications. We propose a novel Bayesian Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (BMAML) framework that integrates Bayesian inference with meta-learning to enhance generalization and uncertainty quantification. Unlike traditional meta-learning approaches, BMAML leverages probabilistic modeling to provide robust predictions and reliable uncertainty estimates, even in low-data regimes. Our method employs a gradient-based meta-learning algorithm combined with a variational inference technique to approximate the posterior distribution over model parameters. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that BMAML outperforms existing meta-learning methods in terms of task adaptation speed, prediction accuracy, and uncertainty calibration. The framework also exhibits improved robustness to noisy and sparse data, making it suitable for applications such as few-shot learning, robotics, and personalized recommendation systems. This work contributes a scalable and interpretable meta-learning approach that bridges the gap between Bayesian methods and meta-learning, offering significant potential for advancing adaptive AI systems.  

**Keywords**: Bayesian inference, meta-learning, few-shot learning, uncertainty quantification, variational inference, adaptive AI.
