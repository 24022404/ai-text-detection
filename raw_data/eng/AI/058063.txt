Multi-state autoregressive models are widely used for time series analysis in various domains, such as finance, healthcare, and environmental monitoring. However, determining the optimal number of states in such models remains a significant challenge, often requiring domain expertise or heuristic approaches. This paper addresses this problem by proposing a data-driven framework to automatically learn the number of states in multi-state autoregressive models. Our approach leverages unsupervised learning techniques combined with model selection criteria to identify the most suitable number of states directly from the data. We evaluate the proposed method on synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its ability to accurately infer the number of states while maintaining robust predictive performance. The results show significant improvements over traditional methods, particularly in scenarios with complex state transitions or noisy data. This research contributes a novel, scalable solution for state estimation in autoregressive models, enabling more accurate and interpretable time series analysis. The proposed framework has broad applications in fields requiring dynamic state modeling, such as anomaly detection, predictive maintenance, and system monitoring.  

**Keywords**: multi-state autoregressive models, time series analysis, unsupervised learning, model selection, state estimation, predictive modeling.
