This paper presents an experimental analysis of reinforcement learning (RL) techniques for optimizing spectrum sharing in radar systems. The objective is to address the growing challenge of spectrum congestion by enabling radar systems to dynamically allocate and share frequency bands with minimal interference. We propose a novel RL-based framework that leverages Q-learning and deep Q-networks (DQN) to adaptively manage spectrum resources in real-time. The approach is evaluated through extensive simulations, comparing its performance against traditional rule-based methods and other state-of-the-art RL algorithms. Results demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves superior spectrum utilization efficiency, reduces interference by up to 30%, and maintains robust performance under varying environmental conditions. The study highlights the potential of RL in enhancing spectrum sharing capabilities for next-generation radar systems, offering significant implications for applications in defense, autonomous vehicles, and wireless communication networks. Key contributions include the development of a scalable and adaptive RL model tailored for radar systems and empirical validation of its effectiveness in dynamic spectrum environments. Keywords: reinforcement learning, spectrum sharing, radar systems, Q-learning, deep Q-networks, dynamic spectrum access.
