This paper introduces DeepBlindness, a novel approach aimed at estimating blindness maps and classifying types of blindness from a single color image, specifically for outdoor scenes. The research addresses the challenge of rapid detection and categorization of various blindness effects, enhancing the accessibility and usability of image data for visually impaired individuals.

Methods/Approach: The proposed method utilizes deep learning techniques, leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to process input images and generate accurate blindness maps. The model is trained on a diverse dataset of outdoor images, incorporating multiple types of blindness effects. The process includes a two-stage pipeline that first predicts the area affected by blindness and then classifies the type of blindness effect, such as color blindness or glare.

Results/Findings: Extensive experiments demonstrate that DeepBlindness achieves superior performance in both accuracy and speed compared to existing methods. The model is capable of estimating blindness maps with high precision and categorizing blindness types effectively, ensuring rapid processing suitable for real-time applications. Comparisons with benchmark datasets show significant improvements in map estimation and classification accuracy.

Conclusion/Implications: DeepBlindness provides valuable insights into the detection and analysis of blindness effects in outdoor scenes, offering potential applications in automated accessibility solutions, augmented reality systems, and enhanced image processing tools for visually impaired users. The research contributes to the field by delivering a fast, reliable, and scalable solution, laying the groundwork for future advancements in visual accessibility technologies.

Keywords: DeepBlindness, blindness map estimation, machine learning, CNNs, outdoor scenes, visual accessibility, image classification.
