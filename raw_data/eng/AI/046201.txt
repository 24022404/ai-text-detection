This research addresses the challenge of effectively analyzing complex time series data to uncover low-dimensional state embeddings and identify metastable clusters, which are crucial for understanding dynamic systems across various contexts. By focusing on capturing the intrinsic structure embedded in temporal sequences, the study aims to facilitate improved interpretability and predictive modeling.

Methods/Approach: We propose an innovative approach that leverages a combination of dimensionality reduction techniques and clustering algorithms designed specifically for time series data. Our method integrates autoencoders with recurrent neural network architectures to learn compact state embeddings. Subsequently, a novel clustering algorithm is applied to these embeddings to detect metastable states, providing insights into system dynamics.

Results/Findings: The proposed framework was evaluated on multiple time series datasets from diverse application domains. Results demonstrate that our method consistently yields superior performance in discovering low-dimensional state representations compared to conventional techniques. Additionally, the identified metastable clusters exhibit high stability and coherence, showcasing their potential in capturing meaningful temporal patterns.

Conclusion/Implications: The findings highlight our approach's capability to enhance the analysis of time series data by extracting significant features and dynamic patterns that are often obscured in high-dimensional spaces. This research contributes to the field by offering a robust methodology for time series analysis that can be applied to various domains, including finance, healthcare, and environmental science, facilitating predictive analytics and decision-making processes. Keywords include state embeddings, metastable clusters, time series analysis, dimensionality reduction, and machine learning.
