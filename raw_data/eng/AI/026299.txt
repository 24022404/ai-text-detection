This paper introduces Fractal Gaussian Networks (FGNs), a novel sparse random graph model inspired by Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos (GMC). Our objective is to develop a network model exhibiting tunable sparsity and fractal properties, thereby capturing complex dependencies often observed in real-world networks. FGNs are constructed by leveraging the hierarchical structure inherent in GMC, where edge probabilities are modulated by a multiplicative cascade derived from a Gaussian field. This approach enables precise control over network density and the emergence of fractal-like connectivity patterns. We analyze the structural properties of FGNs, demonstrating their ability to generate graphs with varying degrees of sparsity and clustering. We further explore their robustness to node removal and compare their performance against existing random graph models, such as Erdős-Rényi and scale-free networks, across several network metrics. Results indicate that FGNs offer a flexible framework for modeling complex networks with tunable sparsity and fractal characteristics, potentially leading to improved understanding and simulation of real-world systems. Keywords: random graphs, Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos, sparse networks, fractal networks, network modeling.
