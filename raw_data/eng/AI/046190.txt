In this paper, we explore a formalized set of axioms defining rational behavior in the context of Reinforcement Learning (RL), aiming to bridge the gap between theoretical foundations and practical applications. The purpose of our research is to establish clear criteria and principles that govern rational decision-making processes in RL agents. We propose a novel framework of axioms inspired by classical decision theory and adapted to the dynamics of RL environments. Our approach involves constructing a mathematical model that encapsulates these axioms, facilitating the analysis and development of RL algorithms that align with rationality standards. Through rigorous experimentation, we apply our framework to a suite of benchmark tasks, demonstrating notable improvements in agent performance and decision consistency compared to existing methodologies. The findings reveal that adherence to these axioms not only enhances the logical coherence of decisions but also optimizes long-term reward acquisition. This research contributes to the field by providing a robust theoretical basis for evaluating and constructing RL systems, offering insights into the design of more effective algorithms. Potential applications of our axiomatized approach include autonomous systems, game theory, and strategic planning, where rational agent behavior is paramount. Keywords: reinforcement learning, rationality, decision-making, axioms, RL algorithms, theoretical model.
