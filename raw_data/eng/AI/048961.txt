This paper addresses the challenge of determining optimal feature weights in multi-feature content-based image retrieval systems. The objective is to develop an automatic method that leverages indexing and pseudo-relevance feedback to improve retrieval accuracy. Our approach utilizes a novel indexing technique to efficiently compute feature weights, which are then refined using pseudo-relevance feedback from initial retrieval results. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in retrieval performance compared to state-of-the-art methods, with an average precision increase of 15%. The proposed method offers a unique contribution by eliminating the need for manual feature weight tuning, making it a valuable tool for large-scale image retrieval applications. Key contributions include the introduction of a dynamic feature weighting scheme and the integration of pseudo-relevance feedback to adapt to changing retrieval contexts. This research has important implications for image retrieval, computer vision, and machine learning, with potential applications in areas such as image search, object recognition, and multimedia analysis. Relevant keywords: content-based image retrieval, feature weighting, indexing, pseudo-relevance feedback, machine learning, computer vision.
