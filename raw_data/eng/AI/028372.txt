This paper presents a novel logic-based framework for discriminative modeling driven by generative definitions. The proposed approach addresses the challenge of combining generative and discriminative paradigms, leveraging formal logic to define flexible and expressive generative structures, while enabling efficient and accurate discriminative learning. Our framework represents model structures using declarative logic rules, facilitating interpretability, modularity, and extensibility. Through automatic translation of these logical definitions, we derive discriminative models capable of robust classification, with the generative logic guiding feature selection and representation learning. Experimental evaluation on benchmark datasets demonstrates that our logic-based generatively defined discriminative models achieve competitive or superior performance compared to traditional purely discriminative and generative models. Furthermore, we show improvements in model transparency and adaptability to new data and requirements. This work bridges the gap between symbolic logic and statistical learning, offering a promising direction for interpretable and customizable machine learning systems. Key contributions include a unified logic-driven modeling framework, algorithms for translating logic structures into discriminative models, and empirical validation across diverse domains. Keywords: logic-based modeling, generative models, discriminative learning, interpretable AI, machine learning, feature selection.
