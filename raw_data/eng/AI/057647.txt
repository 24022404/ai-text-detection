Time series classification is a critical task in various domains, such as healthcare, finance, and IoT, where labeled data is often scarce. This paper presents an empirical study of graph-based approaches for semi-supervised time series classification, addressing the challenge of leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data to improve model performance. We explore the effectiveness of graph neural networks (GNNs) and graph-based regularization techniques in capturing temporal dependencies and structural relationships within time series data. Our methodology involves constructing graphs from time series data using similarity metrics and applying state-of-the-art GNN architectures, such as Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and Graph Attention Networks (GATs), to classify time series in a semi-supervised setting. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that graph-based approaches outperform traditional methods, achieving higher accuracy and robustness, particularly when labeled data is limited. The findings highlight the potential of graph-based methods to enhance time series classification by effectively utilizing unlabeled data and modeling complex temporal patterns. This study contributes to the growing body of research on semi-supervised learning and provides insights into the practical application of graph-based techniques for time series analysis.  

**Keywords**: time series classification, semi-supervised learning, graph neural networks, graph-based regularization, temporal dependencies, GCN, GAT.
