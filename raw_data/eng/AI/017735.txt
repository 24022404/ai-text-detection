This paper introduces a novel approach to learning approximate stochastic transition models for complex systems. Accurate transition models are crucial for planning and decision-making, but learning them directly from data can be computationally intractable. We propose a method that combines spectral learning techniques with function approximation to efficiently estimate these models. Our approach utilizes observed state transitions to learn a compact representation of the transition dynamics, enabling effective prediction of future states under uncertainty. Experiments on benchmark domains demonstrate that our method achieves significant improvements in both the accuracy and computational efficiency of transition model learning compared to existing techniques. Furthermore, we show that learned approximate models can be successfully integrated into model-based reinforcement learning algorithms, leading to improved policy performance. This work offers a scalable and practical solution for learning stochastic transition models in high-dimensional environments, facilitating the development of more robust and adaptive autonomous systems. Keywords: stochastic transition models, spectral learning, function approximation, reinforcement learning, system identification.
