This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised domain adaptive object detection, where the goal is to adapt an object detector trained on a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain without access to the source data. Our objective is to develop a novel approach that can effectively bridge the domain gap and improve detection performance on the target domain. We propose a method that leverages pseudo-labeling and self-supervised learning to adapt the detector, eliminating the need for source data. Our approach utilizes a teacher-student framework, where a teacher model generates pseudo-labels for the target domain, and a student model is trained using these labels. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, outperforming state-of-the-art unsupervised domain adaptation techniques. Our findings show that the proposed approach can achieve significant improvements in object detection accuracy on the target domain, with applications in areas such as autonomous driving and surveillance. The key contributions of this work include a novel domain adaptation framework, improved detection performance, and the ability to adapt to new domains without requiring source data, making it a valuable contribution to the field of computer vision and object detection. Keywords: unsupervised domain adaptation, object detection, pseudo-labeling, self-supervised learning, teacher-student framework.
