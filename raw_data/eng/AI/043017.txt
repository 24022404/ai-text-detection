Estimating the effects of interventions on continuous-valued treatments is a challenging problem in causal inference, particularly when dealing with complex and high-dimensional data. This paper introduces a novel approach leveraging Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to model the counterfactual distributions required for estimating interventional effects. Our method, Continuous-Treatment GAN (CT-GAN), employs a conditional GAN architecture to learn the relationship between covariates, treatment, and outcome variables. CT-GAN generates realistic counterfactual outcomes by conditioning on specific intervention values, allowing for the estimation of average treatment effects for continuous treatments. We evaluate CT-GAN on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its superior performance compared to existing methods in terms of bias reduction and accuracy of intervention effect estimation. The results highlight CT-GAN's ability to effectively handle non-linear relationships and high-dimensional data, providing a robust framework for causal inference in various domains. This work contributes a powerful tool for decision-making and policy evaluation in settings where interventions involve continuous variables.

**Keywords:** Causal Inference, Generative Adversarial Networks, Continuous Treatment Effects, Counterfactual Inference, GANs, Average Treatment Effect.
