**Abstract:**

Adversarial attacks in machine learning have primarily focused on data types such as images and text, leaving graph-structured data comparatively less explored despite its prevalence in domains like social networks, recommendation systems, and biological networks. This paper investigates adversarial attacks specifically targeting graph-structured data, aiming to evaluate the vulnerability of graph neural networks (GNNs) and related models. We propose a novel attack framework that exploits the inherent topological properties of graphs to craft imperceptible yet highly effective perturbations. The method systematically modifies node features and graph connectivity while maintaining the overall structural similarity, thereby evading conventional detection techniques. Extensive experiments on benchmark graph datasets demonstrate a significant decline in model performance under our adversarial scenarios, surpassing the effectiveness of existing attack strategies. The results highlight the urgent need for robust graph learning algorithms and offer new insights into the security implications of deploying GNNs in critical applications. Our findings pave the way for developing enhanced defense mechanisms and contribute to the broader understanding of adversarial robustness in graph-based machine learning systems.  
**Keywords:** adversarial attack, graph neural networks, graph-structured data, security, robustness, machine learning.
