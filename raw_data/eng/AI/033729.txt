The objective of this research is to address the challenges in video super-resolution (VSR) by incorporating compression awareness into the enhancement process. Traditional VSR methods often neglect the impact of video compression artifacts, which can degrade the quality of the upscaled videos. To tackle this issue, we introduce COMISR, an innovative Compression-Informed framework for Video Super-Resolution that integrates compression characteristics directly into the VSR model. Our approach leverages a novel neural network architecture that incorporates both spatial and temporal information while being explicitly aware of compression-induced artifacts. We conduct extensive experiments comparing COMISR with state-of-the-art VSR techniques on multiple benchmark datasets. Our findings reveal that COMISR significantly outperforms traditional models by achieving superior visual quality and sharper details, particularly in heavily compressed video content. The results demonstrate enhanced perceptual metrics, improved PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio), and SSIM (Structural Similarity Index) scores across various compression levels. In conclusion, COMISR offers a substantial advancement in video super-resolution by effectively mitigating the adverse effects of compression artifacts. The incorporation of compression-informed processes in VSR not only improves video quality but also broadens the scope for practical applications in streaming services and video restoration tasks. Key keywords include video super-resolution, compression artifacts, neural networks, spatial-temporal modeling, and visual quality enhancement.
