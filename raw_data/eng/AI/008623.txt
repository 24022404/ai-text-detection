Objective: Accurately identifying the functional role of individual parts within complex product assemblies is a critical step toward automated design analysis, cost estimation, and maintenance planning. Traditional rule-based or feature-engineering approaches often struggle to generalize across diverse products and connection patterns. 

Methods: We propose a novel graph-based framework that represents each assembly as a heterogeneous graph, where nodes correspond to individual components described by geometric shape embeddings and semantic metadata, and edges capture physical adjacency and connection type. A Graph Neural Network (GNN) backbone—with attention-weighted message passing—learns to aggregate local neighborhood information and global context to predict one of several predefined function categories (e.g., fastener, support, actuator). To enhance robustness, we integrate multi-scale shape descriptors and leverage a contrastive pretraining step on unlabeled assembly graphs.

Results: Experiments on two industrial datasets—comprising over 10,000 real-world CAD assemblies from mechanical and consumer-electronics domains—show that our model outperforms conventional machine-learning baselines by 12–18% in overall classification accuracy and reduces error on rare function classes by 25%. Ablation studies confirm the importance of attention mechanisms and multimodal feature fusion. 

Conclusion: This work demonstrates the effectiveness of GNNs for component-level function classification in diverse product assemblies. By automating a traditionally manual task, our approach can streamline downstream processes such as assembly instruction generation, digital twins, and predictive maintenance. 

Keywords: graph neural network, component classification, product assembly, CAD, 3D geometry.
