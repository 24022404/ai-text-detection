Object recognition remains a critical challenge in computer vision, requiring robust methods to handle variations in shape, texture, and occlusion. This paper introduces a novel approach for object recognition by combining contour sparse representation with Scale-Dependent Descriptor (SDD) features. The proposed method leverages the inherent structural information of object contours, encoding them into a sparse representation framework to enhance discriminative power. SDD features are integrated to capture multi-scale contextual information, enabling the model to adapt to varying object sizes and resolutions. The approach employs an optimization algorithm to efficiently solve the sparse representation problem, ensuring computational feasibility. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements in recognition accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly in scenarios with partial occlusion and cluttered backgrounds. The proposed method achieves an average recognition rate of 92.5%, outperforming existing techniques by 4.7%. This research contributes a scalable and efficient framework for object recognition, with potential applications in autonomous systems, surveillance, and robotics. Key innovations include the integration of contour-based sparse representation with SDD features and a robust optimization strategy. Keywords: object recognition, contour sparse representation, SDD features, computer vision, optimization, occlusion handling.
