Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have achieved remarkable success in generating high-quality images, but their reliance on convolutional neural networks (CNNs) introduces limitations in scalability and long-range dependency modeling. This paper introduces TransGAN, a novel GAN framework that leverages two pure transformer architectures for both the generator and discriminator, eliminating the need for CNNs entirely. The objective of this work is to explore the potential of transformers in generative tasks and address the scalability challenges of traditional GANs. We propose a hierarchical transformer-based generator and a patch-based discriminator, enabling efficient training and high-resolution image synthesis. Our approach incorporates advanced techniques such as progressive growing and self-attention mechanisms to enhance model performance. Experimental results demonstrate that TransGAN achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks, outperforming existing CNN-based GANs in terms of image quality and diversity. Notably, TransGAN scales effectively to higher resolutions, showcasing its potential for large-scale generative tasks. The findings highlight the versatility of transformers in generative modeling and open new avenues for research in transformer-based architectures. This work contributes to the growing body of research on transformer applications beyond natural language processing, offering a robust and scalable alternative for image generation.  

**Keywords**: GAN, transformers, generative models, image synthesis, self-attention, scalability.
