The accurate classification of peanut maturity is critical for optimizing harvest timing and improving crop yield. This study addresses the challenge of non-destructively assessing peanut maturity by leveraging hyperspectral imagery, a technology capable of capturing detailed spectral information across a wide range of wavelengths. We propose a novel machine learning-based framework that processes hyperspectral data to classify peanut pods into distinct maturity stages. The approach involves preprocessing the hyperspectral images to reduce noise, followed by feature extraction using advanced dimensionality reduction techniques. A deep learning model, specifically a convolutional neural network (CNN), is then employed to classify the maturity stages based on the extracted spectral features. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves high classification accuracy, outperforming traditional methods that rely on manual inspection or limited spectral bands. The system's robustness is validated through extensive testing on diverse peanut varieties under varying environmental conditions. This research contributes to precision agriculture by providing a scalable, non-invasive solution for peanut maturity assessment, with potential applications in optimizing harvest schedules and enhancing crop quality. Key innovations include the integration of hyperspectral imaging with deep learning, offering a significant improvement over conventional approaches. Keywords: hyperspectral imagery, peanut maturity, machine learning, convolutional neural networks, precision agriculture.
