This paper presents a novel approach to monocular video-based visual odometry and depth estimation using a recurrent neural network (RNN) architecture. The objective is to develop a robust and accurate method for estimating camera motion and scene depth from a single video stream, leveraging both supervised and unsupervised learning paradigms. Our approach employs a deep RNN model that integrates convolutional and recurrent layers to learn spatial and temporal features from monocular video sequences. The network is trained using a combination of supervised loss functions for odometry and depth estimation, as well as unsupervised loss functions to regularize the learning process. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets for visual odometry and depth estimation. The proposed approach enables accurate and efficient estimation of camera motion and scene depth, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous vehicles, and augmented reality. Key contributions include the development of a robust RNN architecture for monocular video-based visual odometry and depth estimation, and the exploration of unsupervised learning techniques to improve model generalization and accuracy. Relevant keywords: visual odometry, depth estimation, monocular video, recurrent neural network, unsupervised learning, computer vision.
