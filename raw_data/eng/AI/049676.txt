**Abstract:**

This paper introduces a novel framework for Neural Predictive Belief Representations, aimed at enhancing the capability of machine learning models to capture and utilize uncertainty in sequential decision-making tasks. The primary objective is to address the limitations of deterministic neural networks in environments where beliefs about unobserved states play a crucial role, such as in partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs). We propose an end-to-end trainable neural architecture that learns compact, actionable belief embeddings through predictive modeling. Our approach leverages recurrent neural networks combined with variational inference techniques to maintain and update beliefs over time, enabling more robust predictions and policy learning under uncertainty. Experimental results on benchmark domains demonstrate that our method achieves superior performance compared to traditional state estimation models and existing belief representation techniques, particularly in scenarios with sparse observations and high-dimensional state spaces. The findings suggest that neural predictive belief representations offer significant improvements in both accuracy and computational efficiency, providing a promising direction for research in reinforcement learning, robotics, and autonomous systems. Key contributions include a scalable belief representation learning algorithm, empirical analysis across multiple domains, and insights into integrating belief-aware reasoning into neural agents. **Keywords:** belief representation, neural networks, uncertainty modeling, predictive modeling, POMDP, sequential decision-making, reinforcement learning.
