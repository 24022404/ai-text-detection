This paper investigates the limitations of Deep Deterministic Policy Gradients (DDPG), a popular deep reinforcement learning algorithm, in deterministic environments with sparse rewards. The objective is to understand the causes of failures in such scenarios, where DDPG often struggles to learn effective policies. Our approach involves analyzing the algorithm's behavior in these environments, identifying key factors that contribute to its poor performance, and proposing modifications to improve its robustness. Our results show that DDPG's failures can be attributed to its inability to handle sparse rewards and its tendency to get stuck in local optima. We propose a novel extension to DDPG that incorporates techniques from exploration-exploitation trade-offs and reward shaping, leading to significant improvements in performance. Our findings have important implications for the development of more robust reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly in real-world applications where rewards are often sparse and environments are deterministic. Key contributions include a detailed analysis of DDPG's failures, a novel algorithmic extension, and insights into the importance of careful reward design and exploration strategies in deep reinforcement learning. Relevant keywords: DDPG, deep reinforcement learning, sparse rewards, deterministic environments, exploration-exploitation trade-offs, reward shaping.
