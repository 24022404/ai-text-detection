This paper explores the application of Bayesian inversion techniques to generative models for enhanced characterization and uncertainty quantification in geologic carbon dioxide (CO2) storage. The objective is to develop a robust framework for estimating subsurface properties relevant to CO2 sequestration, such as permeability and porosity, by integrating geological data with generative adversarial networks (GANs). Our method leverages a pre-trained GAN to represent the complex spatial correlations inherent in subsurface formations, effectively reducing the dimensionality of the inverse problem. We employ a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm within the Bayesian framework to sample from the posterior distribution of latent GAN parameters conditioned on observed data, enabling probabilistic predictions of subsurface structure. Results demonstrate improved accuracy in reconstructing subsurface properties compared to traditional inversion methods, along with comprehensive quantification of associated uncertainties. This approach offers a computationally efficient and statistically rigorous tool for optimizing CO2 storage site selection and monitoring, facilitating safer and more effective carbon sequestration strategies. Keywords: Bayesian Inversion, Generative Models, Carbon Sequestration, Geologic Storage, Uncertainty Quantification, GANs.
