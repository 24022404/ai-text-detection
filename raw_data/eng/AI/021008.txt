Estimating the 3D pose and shape of vehicles from monocular images is a challenging problem in computer vision, crucial for applications like autonomous driving and traffic monitoring. This paper introduces DirectShape, a novel approach that directly aligns a shape prior to image evidence through photometric error minimization. Unlike existing methods that rely on intermediate representations or feature matching, DirectShape leverages differentiable rendering to directly optimize for pose and shape parameters by minimizing the difference between the rendered image and the observed image. This direct photometric alignment allows for robust estimation even in the presence of occlusions and challenging lighting conditions. We demonstrate that DirectShape achieves state-of-the-art performance on standard benchmarks for vehicle pose and shape estimation, surpassing existing methods in accuracy and robustness. Furthermore, the direct optimization framework enables efficient inference and avoids the need for extensive training data. The results highlight the potential of direct photometric alignment for visual vehicle understanding and provide a promising direction for future research.

Keywords: Vehicle Pose Estimation, 3D Shape Estimation, Photometric Alignment, Differentiable Rendering, Computer Vision, Autonomous Driving.
