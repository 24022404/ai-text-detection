Inverse problems, which involve reconstructing unknown inputs from observed outputs, are prevalent across various domains such as medical imaging, computer vision, and signal processing. This paper explores the application of Invertible Neural Networks (INNs) to address these challenges, leveraging their unique ability to model bijective transformations efficiently. The objective of this research is to demonstrate how INNs can provide accurate and computationally efficient solutions to inverse problems while maintaining interpretability and robustness. We propose a novel framework that integrates INNs with regularization techniques to handle ill-posed inverse problems effectively. Our approach is evaluated on benchmark datasets, including image deblurring and tomography reconstruction tasks, and compared against traditional methods such as variational approaches and deep learning-based models. Experimental results show that the proposed method achieves superior performance in terms of reconstruction accuracy and computational efficiency, with significant improvements in handling noise and data scarcity. The findings highlight the potential of INNs as a powerful tool for solving inverse problems, offering insights into their scalability and applicability to real-world scenarios. This research contributes to advancing the understanding of invertible architectures and their role in addressing complex inverse problems, paving the way for future innovations in the field.  

**Keywords**: Inverse Problems, Invertible Neural Networks, Image Reconstruction, Regularization, Deep Learning, Computational Efficiency.
