Abstract:

The rapid development of graph neural networks (GNNs) has led to numerous models designed for node classification tasks, yet inconsistent evaluation protocols have made fair comparison challenging. This paper presents a comprehensive pipeline for the standardized assessment of GNN architectures in node classification scenarios. Our approach incorporates unified data preprocessing, reproducible train/validation/test splits, consistent hyperparameter tuning, and identical evaluation metrics, thereby minimizing experimental bias and facilitating transparent benchmarking. The pipeline is compatible with popular GNN variants such as Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN), Graph Attention Networks (GAT), and GraphSAGE. Experiments conducted across multiple publicly available datasets demonstrate the pipeline's effectiveness in revealing genuine model differences and ensuring repeatable results. Key findings indicate that certain models previously reported as superior may not consistently outperform others when evaluated under identical conditions. By providing an open-source, extensible framework, this research enables the community to conduct equitable and reliable GNN evaluations. The proposed pipeline supports advancement in graph representation learning and encourages best practices for reproducible machine learning research. Keywords: graph neural networks, node classification, benchmarking, reproducibility, fair comparison, machine learning pipeline.
