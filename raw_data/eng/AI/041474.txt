This paper presents SMILE (Self-Distilled MIxup for Efficient Transfer LEarning), a novel framework designed to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of transfer learning in deep neural networks. The primary objective is to address the challenge of optimizing model performance on target domains with limited labeled data, while reducing the computational and data requirements of traditional fine-tuning approaches. SMILE integrates two complementary strategies: self-distillation, where the model progressively refines its own knowledge during adaptation, and Mixup regularization, which augments training data through linear interpolation of example pairs to promote robustness and generalization.

We describe the architecture and training pipeline of SMILE, detailing how self-distillation and Mixup are jointly leveraged in a transfer learning scenario. Extensive experiments are conducted on benchmark datasets across several domains, including image classification and natural language processing. Results demonstrate that SMILE consistently outperforms conventional fine-tuning and other state-of-the-art transfer learning methods in terms of accuracy, sample efficiency, and training speed. Notably, SMILE achieves substantial improvements on small-data regimes and is compatible with a wide range of backbone models.

Our findings suggest that the synergy between self-distillation and Mixup regularization is crucial for efficient knowledge transfer. SMILE provides a scalable and generalizable solution for rapid adaptation to new tasks with minimal annotation effort. Potential applications include personalized AI, real-time adaptation of deployed models, and resource-constrained environments. 

Keywords: transfer learning, self-distillation, Mixup, data augmentation, deep learning, efficient adaptation, model generalization.
