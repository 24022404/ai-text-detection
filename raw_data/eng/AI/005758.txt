Adversarial examples pose a significant threat to the robustness of deep learning models, particularly in natural language processing. This paper explores the generation and impact of *semantic adversarial examples* – perturbations to text that, while preserving the original meaning, cause a model to misclassify the input. We introduce a novel method leveraging paraphrasing and back-translation techniques guided by semantic similarity metrics to craft these examples. Our approach ensures high semantic similarity between the original and adversarial texts, making the attacks more subtle and difficult to detect. We evaluate our method on several benchmark datasets for text classification and question answering, demonstrating a significant drop in model accuracy across various architectures, including transformer-based models. The results highlight the vulnerability of current NLP models to semantically similar adversarial attacks, even when they are robust to traditional word-level perturbations. This research underscores the need for developing defense mechanisms that focus on semantic understanding rather than solely relying on syntactic features. Keywords: adversarial attacks, semantic similarity, natural language processing, text classification, deep learning, robustness.
