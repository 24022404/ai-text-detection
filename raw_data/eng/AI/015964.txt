This study addresses the challenge of reducing the complexity of high-dimensional, nonlinear dynamic systems through an innovative model reduction technique. The primary goal is to enhance the computational efficiency of simulations for complex systems without compromising accuracy. 

Methods: We propose a novel High-order Differentiable Autoencoder (HDAE) specifically designed for nonlinear model reduction. The architecture combines the strength of deep autoencoders with high-order differentiation, which captures intricate nonlinear relationships within data more effectively than traditional methods. Techniques from deep learning are employed to train the network efficiently, ensuring that the reduced models maintain fidelity to the original systems across diverse output conditions. 

Results: Our experiments demonstrate that HDAE significantly outperforms traditional reduction techniques in preserving key performance indicators of the original systems, as evidenced by reduced reconstruction error and improved computational speed. Comparative analyses with existing methods such as Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) and traditional autoencoders show substantial improvements in both accuracy and efficiency.

Conclusion: The introduction of the HDAE marks a significant advancement in nonlinear model reduction, leveraging deep learning advancements to offer superior performance in complex system simulations. This method holds promising implications for fields requiring high-fidelity, real-time simulations, such as fluid dynamics, structural analysis, and control systems. By bridging the gap between deep learning and system reduction, HDAE presents a robust tool that enhances the capabilities of simulation-based predictive analytics. 

Keywords: High-order Differentiable Autoencoder, Nonlinear Model Reduction, Deep Learning, Computational Efficiency, System Simulation.
