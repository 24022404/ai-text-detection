This paper presents a novel approach to audio classification through the development of a shift-invariance sparse coding (SISC) framework. The objective of this work is to address the limitations of traditional sparse coding methods, which often struggle to consistently represent audio features that undergo temporal shifts due to variations in timing or onset within audio signals. Our method incorporates shift-invariance directly into the sparse coding process, enabling the automatic discovery of representative audio features regardless of their temporal positions. The proposed SISC model is evaluated on a range of benchmark audio classification datasets, where it demonstrates significant improvements in classification accuracy compared to conventional sparse coding and other feature learning techniques. Experimental results indicate that the shift-invariance property enhances the robustness of the learned representations, leading to more reliable performance across diverse audio domains, including environmental sounds, speech, and music. This research contributes to the field of audio signal processing by introducing a flexible and effective learning framework that can be integrated into various audio analysis applications. Keywords: audio classification, sparse coding, shift-invariance, feature learning, machine learning, signal processing.
