The embedding of relational data into Euclidean space is a fundamental problem in information technology, with applications ranging from knowledge graph representation to machine learning models. This paper investigates the conditions under which relational structures can be reliably embedded into Euclidean spaces while preserving their inherent properties. We propose a systematic framework to evaluate the embeddability of various types of relations, focusing on their geometric and topological characteristics. Using a combination of graph theory and optimization techniques, we develop a novel algorithm that determines the minimal dimensionality required for accurate embeddings. Our experiments demonstrate that certain classes of relations, such as hierarchical and symmetric structures, exhibit high embeddability, while others, like cyclic or highly irregular relations, pose significant challenges. The results reveal a clear trade-off between embedding dimensionality and preservation of relational properties. This work provides a theoretical foundation for designing efficient embedding methods and offers practical insights for applications in natural language processing, recommendation systems, and network analysis. The proposed approach advances the state-of-the-art by enabling more reliable and interpretable embeddings, with potential implications for improving the performance of downstream machine learning tasks.  

**Keywords**: relational embedding, Euclidean space, graph theory, dimensionality reduction, knowledge representation, machine learning.
