This paper addresses the challenge of articulated shape matching, a critical task in computer vision and 3D modeling, by proposing a novel approach that leverages Laplacian eigenfunctions and unsupervised point registration. The objective is to accurately align and match articulated shapes, such as human or animal models, despite variations in pose and deformation. Our method utilizes the spectral properties of Laplacian eigenfunctions to capture intrinsic geometric features of shapes, enabling robust correspondence estimation. An unsupervised point registration framework is then employed to align these features without requiring labeled data, significantly reducing the dependency on annotated datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly in scenarios involving complex deformations and partial occlusions. The proposed technique contributes to advancing applications in animation, medical imaging, and robotics, where precise shape matching is essential. Key innovations include the integration of spectral geometry with unsupervised learning, offering a scalable and generalizable solution for articulated shape analysis. Keywords: articulated shape matching, Laplacian eigenfunctions, unsupervised learning, point registration, spectral geometry, 3D modeling.
