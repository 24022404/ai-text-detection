Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) has emerged as a promising non-contact method for heart rate (HR) measurement, with applications in healthcare, fitness, and human-computer interaction. However, existing methods often rely on handcrafted features or predefined models, limiting their adaptability and robustness across diverse scenarios. This paper introduces **AutoHR**, a novel end-to-end framework for remote HR measurement that leverages neural architecture search (NAS) to automatically design optimal deep learning models tailored for rPPG tasks. The proposed approach eliminates the need for manual feature engineering by directly learning spatiotemporal representations from raw video data. AutoHR employs a lightweight and efficient search strategy to identify architectures that balance accuracy and computational efficiency. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that AutoHR outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of HR estimation accuracy, robustness to motion artifacts, and generalization across different skin tones and lighting conditions. The framework achieves a mean absolute error (MAE) of 2.1 BPM on the UBFC-rPPG dataset, setting a new benchmark for remote HR measurement. Furthermore, AutoHR’s modular design enables seamless integration with existing rPPG pipelines, making it a versatile solution for real-world applications. This work advances the field by demonstrating the potential of automated neural architecture search in enhancing the performance and adaptability of remote physiological sensing systems.  

**Keywords**: remote photoplethysmography, heart rate measurement, neural architecture search, end-to-end learning, deep learning, physiological sensing.
