This paper investigates the application of deep learning techniques for the classification of satellite imagery. The objective is to develop a robust and accurate method for automatically categorizing land cover types from satellite data, addressing the challenges of high dimensionality and spectral variability inherent in remote sensing. A convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, specifically a modified ResNet model, is employed and trained on a large, publicly available dataset of labeled satellite images. The proposed approach incorporates data augmentation and transfer learning to improve generalization and reduce overfitting. Experimental results demonstrate that the deep learning model achieves a significant improvement in classification accuracy compared to traditional machine learning algorithms, with an overall accuracy of 92% on the test set. Furthermore, the model exhibits strong performance in identifying various land cover types, including urban areas, forests, and water bodies. The findings highlight the potential of deep learning for automated and efficient satellite image analysis, offering valuable insights for environmental monitoring, urban planning, and resource management. Keywords: Satellite Image Classification, Deep Learning, Convolutional Neural Networks, Remote Sensing, Land Cover Classification.
