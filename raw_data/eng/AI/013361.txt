Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have demonstrated significant success in processing graph-structured data across applications such as social network analysis, molecular modeling, and recommendation systems. However, designing optimal GNN architectures typically requires expert knowledge and extensive manual experimentation, which impedes broader adoption. This paper proposes a novel framework that simplifies the architecture search process for GNNs by leveraging an automated, efficient search algorithm tailored to the unique characteristics of graph data. Our approach systematically explores the architectural space, optimizing key hyperparameters including layer aggregation, neighborhood selection, and feature transformation mechanisms. Experimental evaluation on multiple benchmark datasets reveals that the proposed method not only reduces search time and computational cost but also yields GNN models with competitive or superior performance compared to manually designed counterparts and existing automated techniques. The results suggest that automating GNN architecture search can democratize the use of advanced graph-based learning by making it more accessible to a wider range of practitioners. Our contributions facilitate scalable, efficient, and effective GNN deployment, opening new directions for automated machine learning on graphs. Key keywords: Graph Neural Network, architecture search, automated model design, hyperparameter optimization, neural architecture search (NAS), machine learning on graphs.
