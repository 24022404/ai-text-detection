**Abstract**

The accurate analysis of medical images is crucial for diagnosis and treatment planning, yet the scarcity of annotated data remains a significant challenge. This paper proposes an unsupervised learning framework to extract local discriminative representations from medical images, addressing the need for effective feature learning without reliance on manual labeling. Our approach leverages a novel contrastive learning paradigm, tailored to capture localized anatomical variations while maximizing discrimination between different image regions. Specifically, we employ a patch-based self-supervised model that learns to distinguish similar and dissimilar local regions within individual scans. Experimental results on multiple benchmark medical imaging datasets, including MRI and CT modalities, demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing unsupervised techniques in terms of representation quality and downstream tasks such as abnormality detection and tissue classification. The proposed framework achieves competitive performance even in settings with extremely limited or imbalanced labeled samples. These findings highlight the potential of unsupervised local representation learning to improve robustness and generalizability in medical image analysis. Our work provides a foundation for deploying accurate and scalable AI-driven diagnostics in clinical environments. Key keywords: unsupervised learning, local representation, medical images, discriminative features, self-supervised learning, contrastive learning, abnormality detection.
