Accurate prediction of crowd flow is critical for urban planning, traffic management, and public safety. However, existing methods often struggle to model the complex and heterogeneous relationships across multiple modes of transportation, such as walking, cycling, and public transit. This paper proposes a novel framework for potential crowd flow prediction by integrating heterogeneous relational data from diverse transportation modes. Our approach leverages a multi-modal graph neural network (GNN) to capture spatial, temporal, and modal dependencies simultaneously. The model incorporates dynamic attention mechanisms to prioritize influential relationships and adapt to varying crowd behaviors. Experiments on real-world datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in terms of prediction accuracy and robustness. Key findings reveal that modeling inter-modal interactions significantly enhances the ability to forecast crowd flow patterns, particularly in densely populated urban areas. The proposed framework offers practical implications for optimizing transportation systems and improving urban mobility. This research contributes to the field by introducing a scalable and interpretable solution for multi-modal crowd flow prediction, paving the way for smarter and more efficient city management.  

**Keywords**: crowd flow prediction, multi-modal transportation, graph neural networks, heterogeneous relations, urban mobility, dynamic attention mechanisms.
