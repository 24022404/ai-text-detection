This paper introduces a novel hierarchical representation learning framework for Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) aimed at improving sample efficiency and generalization in reinforcement learning. The approach learns a hierarchy of state representations, where higher levels capture more abstract and temporally extended features of the environment, enabling efficient planning and decision-making. We propose a deep neural network architecture trained end-to-end to extract these hierarchical features, using a combination of unsupervised and supervised learning techniques. Empirical evaluations on a suite of benchmark MDP environments demonstrate that our method achieves significant performance gains compared to state-of-the-art reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly in complex and sparse-reward settings. The learned hierarchical representations also exhibit strong transferability across related tasks. These findings suggest that hierarchical representation learning can provide a powerful mechanism for scaling reinforcement learning to more complex and real-world applications. Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Hierarchical Representation Learning, Markov Decision Processes, Deep Learning, Abstraction, Generalization.
