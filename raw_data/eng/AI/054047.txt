Overcomplete representation learning, where the number of features exceeds the input dimension, offers potential benefits for downstream tasks but poses challenges in optimization. This paper analyzes the optimization landscapes of overcomplete representation learning models, focusing on the structure and properties of critical points. We employ theoretical analysis and empirical experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets to characterize the loss function's geometry. Our approach involves studying the Hessian matrix at critical points to identify saddle points, local minima, and global minima. Results indicate that overcomplete models exhibit complex optimization landscapes with a high density of saddle points. However, we identify specific regularization techniques, such as sparsity constraints and orthogonalization penalties, that can effectively shape the landscape and facilitate convergence to desirable solutions. Furthermore, we demonstrate the impact of different initialization strategies on the final learned representation. The findings provide insights into the training dynamics of overcomplete models and offer guidance for designing more effective optimization algorithms. Key words: Overcomplete representation, optimization landscape, saddle point, regularization, sparsity, neural networks.
