This paper provides a comprehensive tutorial and review of the emerging framework that formulates reinforcement learning (RL) and control as problems of probabilistic inference. The objective is to present a unified perspective that connects classical RL algorithms and control theories with recent developments in probabilistic modeling, offering a new lens through which to understand and develop intelligent decision-making systems. The tutorial systematically introduces the mathematical foundation underlying probabilistic inference, and demonstrates how RL problems—such as reward maximization and policy optimization—can be recast in this framework using tools like graphical models, variational inference, and maximum entropy principles. Key algorithms, including soft Q-learning, policy gradients, and model-based planning, are analyzed with respect to their probabilistic interpretations and sample efficiency. The paper synthesizes empirical findings, highlighting the strengths of inference-based approaches in addressing challenges such as exploration, stability, and robustness in complex environments. Through comparative analysis, the review identifies limitations and open questions, such as scalability and sensitivity to model misspecification. The tutorial concludes by outlining future research directions and practical implications for both reinforcement learning and real-world control systems, emphasizing probabilistic inference as a powerful paradigm for advancing the state-of-the-art in autonomous decision-making. Keywords: reinforcement learning, probabilistic inference, control theory, maximum entropy, policy optimization, graphical models, decision-making.
