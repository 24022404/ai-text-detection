Time series forecasting is crucial across various domains such as finance, supply chain, and meteorology. Traditional deep learning approaches often face challenges in capturing complex temporal dependencies and patterns, particularly when the data exhibits shape and time distortions. This paper introduces a novel approach that incorporates a Shape and Time Distortion Loss (STDL) to enhance the training of deep learning models for time series forecasting.

Methods/Approach: We propose a deep time series forecasting model that integrates the Shape and Time Distortion Loss to better align predicted sequences with actual data by penalizing shape and time misalignments. The STDL framework is designed to adaptively focus on critical temporal and amplitude features, ensuring that the deep learning model can capture intricate time-dependent patterns and correlations. This technique is integrated with state-of-the-art architectures, including recurrent neural networks and transformers, to evaluate its effectiveness.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate that models trained with the STDL significantly outperform baseline models, achieving lower forecasting errors and improved alignment with true sequence characteristics. The approach is validated across multiple benchmark datasets, consistently showing superior performance in both short-term and long-term predictions. Comparative analysis indicates substantial improvements in capturing time-warped and shape-distorted patterns.

Conclusion/Implications: The proposed Shape and Time Distortion Loss introduces a robust mechanism for enhancing deep time series forecasting models, offering significant contributions to the field by addressing critical limitations in traditional models. The framework is adaptable to various deep learning architectures and is poised to improve predictive accuracy in real-world applications, including economic forecasting, climate modeling, and beyond.

Keywords: time series forecasting, Shape and Time Distortion Loss, deep learning, predictive models, sequence alignment, temporal dependencies.
