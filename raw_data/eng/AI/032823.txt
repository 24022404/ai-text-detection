This paper addresses the challenge of domain and task transferability in machine learning by proposing a novel approach that leverages clustering techniques to enhance cross-domain and cross-task generalization. Our objective is to develop a method that improves the adaptability of models, reducing the need for extensive retraining when applied to new data or tasks.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a learning framework that integrates clustering algorithms with transfer learning strategies. The proposed model learns to form meaningful clusters of data in a source domain and subsequently utilizes these clusters to facilitate knowledge transfer to target domains or tasks. Our approach employs an innovative clustering mechanism that captures domain-specific characteristics while maintaining the flexibility needed for effective adaptation. This framework is evaluated using a series of domain and task transfer scenarios, showcasing its versatility and effectiveness.

Results/Findings: Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of our method in significantly improving transfer performance across various domains and tasks compared to existing approaches. Our model consistently achieves superior results, with a notable increase in accuracy and efficiency. The clustering-augmented transfer learning strategy is shown to reduce the computational overhead associated with traditional retraining processes, thereby facilitating faster adaptation.

Conclusion/Implications: The research presents a significant advancement in the field of transfer learning by introducing a clustering-based mechanism that enhances domain and task adaptability. The findings underscore the potential of the proposed approach to transform how machine learning models are deployed and adapted in dynamic environments. Our method provides a foundation for future research in transfer learning, with potential applications in diverse areas such as natural language processing, computer vision, and autonomous systems.

Keywords: transfer learning, clustering, domain adaptation, task adaptation, machine learning, cross-domain, knowledge transfer.
