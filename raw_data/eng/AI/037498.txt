The Natural Wake-Sleep Algorithm (NWSA) addresses the challenge of unsupervised learning in neural networks by leveraging biologically inspired mechanisms to improve model training efficiency and stability. This paper introduces a novel approach that integrates principles from neuroscience, such as synaptic plasticity and neural oscillation, into a computational framework. The proposed algorithm alternates between "wake" and "sleep" phases, mimicking the natural brain states to optimize weight updates and reduce overfitting. During the wake phase, the network learns from input data, while the sleep phase refines internal representations through generative feedback. We evaluate NWSA on benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance in unsupervised feature extraction and clustering tasks compared to traditional methods like Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). Results indicate a 15% improvement in reconstruction accuracy and a 20% reduction in training time. The algorithm's ability to generalize across diverse datasets highlights its potential for applications in anomaly detection, image synthesis, and reinforcement learning. This work contributes to the growing field of biologically inspired machine learning, offering a scalable and interpretable alternative to conventional unsupervised learning techniques.  

**Keywords**: unsupervised learning, neural networks, synaptic plasticity, generative models, biologically inspired algorithms, Natural Wake-Sleep Algorithm.
