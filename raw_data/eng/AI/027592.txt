Semi-supervised metric learning has emerged as a critical approach for leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data to improve the performance of distance-based machine learning models. This paper introduces a novel deep learning framework, termed *Deep Resurrection*, which revitalizes traditional semi-supervised metric learning by integrating advanced neural architectures and self-supervised learning techniques. The proposed method addresses the limitations of existing approaches, such as sensitivity to noisy labels and scalability issues, by employing a dual-branch network that simultaneously optimizes feature representation and metric learning objectives. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that *Deep Resurrection* achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods by significant margins in tasks such as image retrieval and clustering. The framework also exhibits robustness to varying proportions of labeled data, making it highly practical for real-world applications. Key contributions include a novel loss function that balances supervised and unsupervised learning, as well as a scalable training strategy that reduces computational overhead. This research advances the field of semi-supervised learning and provides a foundation for future work in metric learning and representation learning.  

**Keywords**: Semi-supervised learning, metric learning, deep learning, self-supervised learning, feature representation, image retrieval, clustering.
