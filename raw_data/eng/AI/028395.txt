The increasing prevalence of dense crowds in urban environments necessitates an effective method for tracking individual pedestrian heads for security, safety, and crowd management applications. This paper addresses the challenge of accurately identifying and tracking pedestrian heads amidst densely populated settings.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel computer vision-based approach leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and advanced image processing techniques to detect and track individual heads within dense crowds. Our approach integrates a robust head detector with an innovative tracking algorithm designed to maintain tracking continuity despite occlusions and varying crowd densities.

Results/Findings: Through extensive testing on diverse datasets mimicking real-world crowd scenarios, our method demonstrated superior performance in precision and recall rates compared to existing solutions. The system can consistently track thousands of pedestrian heads with minimal drift and computational overhead, even in highly congested environments. Comparisons with traditional methods highlight a significant improvement in tracking accuracy and robustness.

Conclusion/Implications: This research presents a pioneering framework for pedestrian head tracking that enhances efficacy in crowded contexts. The methodology not only aids in improved crowd surveillance but also offers potential applications in pedestrian flow analysis, virtual reality environments, and emergency response planning. Our contributions provide a substantial advancement in the field of crowd analytics, paving the way for more sophisticated systems in public safety and urban management.

Keywords: pedestrian tracking, dense crowd, computer vision, CNN, head detection, crowd analytics.
