Abstract

The proliferation of advanced face manipulation techniques, such as Deepfake and FaceSwap, presents significant challenges for maintaining authenticity and trust in digital media. While prior forgery detection methods demonstrate effectiveness against specific attack types, their ability to generalize across diverse and unseen manipulation techniques remains limited. In this work, we introduce Face X-ray, a novel approach designed to enable more general and robust face forgery detection. Face X-ray operates by identifying abnormal blending artifacts that are commonly introduced during face manipulation processes, regardless of the underlying synthesis method. The proposed framework leverages a convolutional neural network (CNN) trained on a dataset annotated with ground-truth forgeries, explicitly focusing on boundary regions where genuine and fabricated contents merge. Extensive experiments on multiple public datasets, covering a broad spectrum of face forgery methods, demonstrate that Face X-ray achieves superior performance compared to existing detectors, particularly in cross-dataset and unseen attack scenarios. Our method not only improves detection accuracy but also provides interpretability by highlighting suspicious regions, aiding forensic analysis. The results suggest that Face X-ray represents a step towards more universal and explainable face forgery detection systems, with important implications for digital security, social media integrity, and multimedia forensics. Keywords: Face forgery detection, deep learning, Face X-ray, Deepfake, media forensics, generalization, explainability.
