This paper presents a novel approach to reinforcement learning, introducing Deep Ordinal Reinforcement Learning, a method designed to tackle complex decision-making problems by leveraging ordinal rewards. The objective is to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of reinforcement learning algorithms in situations where precise reward values are difficult to obtain or specify. Our approach utilizes a deep neural network architecture to learn from ordinal feedback, enabling the agent to understand the relative preferences between different outcomes. We employ a customized variant of the Q-learning algorithm, adapted to handle ordinal rewards, and demonstrate its efficacy through a series of experiments on various environments. The results show significant improvements in learning speed and convergence compared to traditional reinforcement learning methods. Our research contributes to the development of more robust and adaptable reinforcement learning systems, with potential applications in areas such as robotics, game playing, and autonomous decision-making. Key aspects of this work include ordinal reinforcement learning, deep Q-learning, and preference-based decision-making, offering a new perspective on the design of intelligent agents that can learn from nuanced and relative feedback.
