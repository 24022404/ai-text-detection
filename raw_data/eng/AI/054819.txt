Adversarial attacks pose a significant threat to the reliability of deep learning models, particularly in security-critical applications. This paper addresses the challenge of improving adversarial robustness by introducing a novel approach called Channel-wise Activation Suppressing (CAS). The proposed method focuses on selectively suppressing activations in specific channels of neural networks during training, thereby reducing the model's vulnerability to adversarial perturbations. CAS leverages a dynamic channel suppression mechanism that identifies and mitigates channels most susceptible to adversarial manipulation. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, demonstrate that CAS significantly enhances robustness against state-of-the-art adversarial attacks, such as PGD and FGSM, while maintaining competitive accuracy on clean data. Compared to existing adversarial training methods, CAS achieves a better trade-off between robustness and computational efficiency. The findings suggest that channel-wise suppression is a promising direction for developing more resilient deep learning models. This work contributes to advancing adversarial defense strategies and has potential applications in secure AI systems, such as autonomous driving and cybersecurity.  

**Keywords**: adversarial robustness, deep learning, channel-wise activation, neural networks, adversarial attacks, secure AI.
