This paper presents a novel approach to image harmonization through the introduction of a Spatial-Separated Curve Rendering Network (SSCRN). The objective is to efficiently and effectively harmonize images by separating spatial and curve rendering processes, addressing the long-standing challenge of achieving high-resolution image harmonization without sacrificing computational efficiency. Our method leverages a dual-branch architecture, where spatial information and curve details are processed separately, allowing for more precise control over the harmonization process. Experimental results demonstrate that SSCRN outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of visual quality and computational speed, achieving significant improvements in PSNR and SSIM metrics. The key contributions of this research lie in its ability to balance efficiency and quality, making it suitable for real-world applications such as image editing and computer vision. By highlighting the novelty of spatial-separated curve rendering, this work paves the way for future research in image harmonization, with potential applications in fields like graphics, vision, and artificial intelligence, particularly in areas such as deep learning, neural networks, and computer-generated imagery. Key keywords: image harmonization, curve rendering, spatial separation, deep learning, computer vision.
