The objective of this research is to explore the generation of small graphs using Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), addressing the challenge of producing diverse and realistic graph structures. Traditional methods for graph generation often struggle with scalability and preserving complex relationships inherent in graph data. We introduce GraphVAE, an innovative model leveraging the architecture of VAEs to effectively capture and reproduce small graph structures. The proposed approach utilizes an encoder-decoder framework where the encoder maps graphs into a latent space, and the decoder generates graphs from sampled latent variables, ensuring structural integrity and variability. Empirical results demonstrate that GraphVAE significantly outperforms existing models in terms of generation quality, diversity, and efficiency across multiple datasets. Our findings indicate that GraphVAE preserves important graph-theoretic properties while introducing novel structures, corroborating its capability to handle small-scale graph generation tasks. This advancement holds potential applications in fields such as computational chemistry, network science, and knowledge graph construction, where accurate and efficient graph generation is critical. By providing a robust and flexible framework for graph generation, this study contributes to the broader understanding and development of generative models in the context of graph data. Keywords for this research include graph generation, Variational Autoencoder, small graphs, latent space, and generative models.
