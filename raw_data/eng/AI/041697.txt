This paper introduces DoWhy, a comprehensive framework designed to enhance the process of expressing and validating causal assumptions in data analysis. The pressing challenge in causal inference is the reliable automation of assumptions validation, which is often hindered by complexity and ambiguity. DoWhy aims to streamline this process, allowing researchers and practitioners to gain more accurate insights from their data.

Methods/Approach: DoWhy leverages a combination of graph-based representations and statistical testing to facilitate the explicit expression of causal assumptions. The framework integrates methodologies from machine learning and statistical inference to provide a robust platform for analyzing causal relationships. It introduces a user-friendly interface that supports the seamless integration of various causal verification algorithms and models.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluation of DoWhy demonstrates its efficacy in identifying inconsistencies in causal assumptions and enhancing the precision of causal models. The framework consistently outperforms existing tools in both performance and ease of use. Comparative analysis reveals that DoWhy significantly reduces the time and computational resources required to validate causal assumptions, without compromising accuracy.

Conclusion/Implications: DoWhy represents a significant advancement in the domain of causal inference by offering a novel, efficient solution for validating causal assumptions. Its integration of diverse methodologies makes it a versatile tool that can be applied across various fields of data science and machine learning. The insights gained from using DoWhy have far-reaching implications, enabling researchers to derive more meaningful conclusions and make informed decisions based on their data. Keywords: causal inference, DoWhy, graph-based representations, statistical testing, machine learning, data analysis.
