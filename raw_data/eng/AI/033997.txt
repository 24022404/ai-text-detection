This paper presents a novel approach for accurately evaluating the depth of metal surface defects using Eddy Current Testing (ECT) combined with Deep Residual Convolutional Neural Networks (ResNet). The primary objective is to overcome limitations of traditional ECT methods, which struggle with complex defect characterization and are sensitive to noise and operator interpretation. Our method leverages a tailored ResNet architecture to automatically extract deep features from ECT signal data, enabling robust and precise defect depth estimation. An extensive dataset, comprising various metal samples with artificially induced defects of known depths, was collected and used to train and validate the proposed model. Experimental results demonstrate that our system significantly outperforms conventional signal processing techniques and standard convolutional neural networks in terms of depth prediction accuracy and generalization to unseen defect patterns. The findings reveal high correlation between predicted and actual defect depths, with notable resilience to measurement noise and variability in defect orientation. This work highlights the potential of integrating advanced deep learning frameworks, such as residual networks, into non-destructive testing workflows, paving the way for enhanced automation, reliability, and efficiency in industrial quality assurance. Key contributions include the development of a specialized deep residual model for ECT data interpretation, comprehensive comparative evaluation, and insights into practical deployment for metal defect assessment. Keywords: Eddy Current Testing, Deep Learning, Residual Networks, Metal Surface Defects, Defect Depth Evaluation, Non-Destructive Testing.
