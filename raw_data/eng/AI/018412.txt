This paper addresses the challenge of expert selection in ensemble learning, with a focus on systems employing Gaussian processes. The primary objective is to develop an effective framework for selecting a subset of Gaussian experts whose combined predictions enhance model accuracy and efficiency. To achieve this, we propose a novel approach based on probabilistic graphical models, which enables the systematic representation of dependencies and relationships among candidate experts. Our methodology formulates the selection problem as an inference task within a graphical framework, leveraging both structural properties and statistical performance metrics. Comprehensive experiments are carried out on benchmark datasets, demonstrating that our method outperforms traditional expert aggregation techniques in terms of prediction accuracy and computational scalability. Notably, the proposed framework excels in scenarios characterized by correlated expert outputs and data heterogeneity. The study’s findings underscore the potential of graphical models in optimizing ensemble construction by identifying the most informative and complementary Gaussian experts. This research contributes to advancing ensemble learning strategies, with significant implications for applications in uncertainty quantification, predictive modeling, and large-scale data analysis. Key keywords: Gaussian processes, expert selection, graphical models, ensemble learning, probabilistic inference, machine learning.
