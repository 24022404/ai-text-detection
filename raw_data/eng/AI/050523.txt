Point-wise analysis is a fundamental task in 3D data processing, with applications ranging from object recognition to scene understanding. Traditional methods, such as Farthest Point Sampling (FPS), have been widely adopted for their simplicity and effectiveness in selecting representative points. However, FPS often struggles with scalability and computational efficiency, particularly for large-scale datasets. This paper introduces a novel sampling framework that extends beyond FPS, addressing its limitations while maintaining high-quality point selection. Our approach leverages a hybrid sampling strategy that combines density-aware clustering with adaptive optimization techniques, enabling efficient and accurate point-wise analysis. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms FPS in terms of both computational speed and sampling quality, achieving up to a 30% reduction in processing time without compromising accuracy. Furthermore, the framework is shown to be robust across diverse applications, including 3D object classification and segmentation. The findings highlight the potential of this approach to enhance point cloud processing pipelines, offering a scalable solution for real-world scenarios. This work contributes to the advancement of 3D data analysis by providing a more efficient and effective alternative to traditional sampling methods.  

**Keywords**: point cloud, sampling methods, 3D data processing, Farthest Point Sampling, density-aware clustering, computational efficiency.
