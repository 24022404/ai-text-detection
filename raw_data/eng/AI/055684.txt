This paper presents an end-to-end computer vision approach for multi-class classification of blood cells, aiming to assist in the diagnosis of various blood-related disorders. The objective is to develop a robust and accurate system that can automatically classify blood cells into different categories. Our approach utilizes a deep learning-based methodology, leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to extract features from blood cell images. The proposed system is trained on a large dataset of annotated blood cell images, achieving high accuracy and outperforming traditional machine learning methods. The results show a significant improvement in classification accuracy, with an overall accuracy of 95% across multiple classes. The key contributions of this research include the development of a novel CNN architecture and a comprehensive evaluation framework for blood cell classification. This study demonstrates the potential of computer vision-based diagnosis in hematology, enabling rapid and accurate analysis of blood cells. The proposed system has significant implications for clinical practice, particularly in resource-constrained settings where access to expert hematologists is limited. Key keywords: blood cell classification, computer vision, deep learning, CNN, hematology, medical diagnosis.
