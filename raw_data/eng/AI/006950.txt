This paper presents a novel approach for gesture recognition in RGB videos utilizing human body keypoints and Dynamic Time Warping (DTW). The objective is to develop an accurate and efficient gesture recognition system applicable to real-time applications. The proposed method extracts 2D human body keypoints from RGB video frames using a pre-trained pose estimation model. These keypoints are then used to represent gestures as temporal sequences. DTW is employed to compare and classify these sequences against a pre-defined gesture library. We introduce a keypoint selection strategy that optimizes the feature set for specific gestures, improving recognition accuracy. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance compared to existing techniques, particularly in handling variations in speed and execution style. The system’s robustness to noise and occlusion is also evaluated. The findings suggest that the combination of keypoint-based representation and DTW provides a powerful framework for gesture recognition in video. This approach has implications for human-computer interaction, sign language recognition, and virtual reality applications.

Keywords: Gesture Recognition, Human Body Keypoints, Dynamic Time Warping, RGB Video, Pose Estimation, Action Recognition.
