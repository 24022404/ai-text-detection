This paper presents a novel approach to unconstrained scene generation using locally conditioned radiance fields. The objective is to generate photorealistic scenes without requiring explicit 3D models or environment maps. Our method employs a neural network-based architecture that learns to predict radiance fields from a given set of local conditions, enabling the generation of diverse and realistic scenes. We utilize a combination of generative models and differentiable rendering to achieve this goal. Our results demonstrate significant improvements in scene generation quality, with the ability to produce complex and detailed environments that rival those created by traditional rendering techniques. The key findings of this research include the development of a locally conditioned radiance field model, which allows for efficient and flexible scene generation, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in generating realistic scenes with varying lighting conditions and object placements. This work contributes to the field of computer vision and graphics, with potential applications in areas such as virtual reality, film production, and video games. Key keywords: scene generation, radiance fields, neural rendering, generative models, 3D reconstruction.
