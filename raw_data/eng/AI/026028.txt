Accurate depth estimation is pivotal in various computer vision applications, such as robotics and autonomous driving. This study introduces a novel approach named AdaBins for monocular depth estimation, which aims to improve accuracy by adaptively determining depth bin centers based on the input image.

Methods/Approach: The proposed AdaBins model utilizes a novel transformer-based architecture that dynamically assigns adaptive bins for depth prediction. The method leverages an attention mechanism to determine the optimal bin configuration for each image, enhancing depth estimation precision. This adaptive binning strategy is integrated with a convolutional neural network (CNN) to capture both local and global features effectively.

Results/Findings: Experiments conducted on benchmark depth estimation datasets demonstrate that AdaBins outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and robustness. The model achieves significant improvements in mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean squared error (RMSE) metrics, indicating substantial advances in depth prediction quality. Comparative evaluations highlight the advantages of adaptive binning over fixed binning methods.

Conclusion/Implications: The AdaBins approach offers a significant contribution to the field of monocular depth estimation by introducing a flexible and precise binning mechanism adapting to scene variability. The research not only showcases improved performance in depth estimation tasks but also opens avenues for further exploration of adaptive mechanisms in other computer vision domains. Potential applications include enhanced perception systems in autonomous vehicles, improved augmented reality experiences, and refined 3D scene reconstruction processes.

Keywords: Depth Estimation, Adaptive Bins, Transformer, Computer Vision, Monocular Depth, Bin Configuration, Neural Networks.
