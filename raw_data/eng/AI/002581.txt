This paper introduces DeeSIL, a novel Deep-Shallow Incremental Learning approach designed to efficiently adapt deep neural networks to evolving data streams. The objective is to address the challenge of catastrophic forgetting in incremental learning scenarios, where models must learn from a stream of data without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. DeeSIL achieves this by combining the strengths of deep and shallow learning techniques, leveraging the representational power of deep networks while incorporating the flexibility of shallow models to adapt to new data. Our approach utilizes a hybrid architecture that incrementally updates the model as new data arrives, ensuring a balance between knowledge retention and new information acquisition. Experimental results demonstrate that DeeSIL outperforms state-of-the-art incremental learning methods, exhibiting improved accuracy and reduced forgetting on several benchmark datasets. The proposed method has significant implications for real-world applications, such as continuous learning in autonomous systems, incremental data processing, and edge AI. Key contributions include the development of a novel deep-shallow incremental learning framework, improved performance on incremental learning tasks, and enhanced adaptability to changing data distributions. Relevant keywords: incremental learning, deep neural networks, shallow learning, catastrophic forgetting, edge AI, continuous learning.
