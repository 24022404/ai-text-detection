Scene flow estimation, a critical task in 3D vision, aims to predict the motion of points in dynamic scenes. While self-supervised methods have gained traction due to their ability to learn without labeled data, they often rely on neighborhood-based assumptions that can lead to inaccuracies in complex scenarios. This paper introduces *Adversarial Metric Learning for Self-Supervised Scene Flow Estimation*, a novel framework designed to address these limitations. Our approach leverages adversarial training to learn robust feature representations, minimizing reliance on unreliable neighborhood assumptions. Specifically, we propose a metric learning strategy that enforces discriminative feature embeddings, enabling the model to distinguish between accurate and misleading motion cues. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms existing self-supervised approaches, achieving state-of-the-art performance in terms of accuracy and robustness. The results highlight the effectiveness of adversarial metric learning in improving scene flow estimation, particularly in challenging scenarios with occlusions and fast-moving objects. This work contributes a new perspective to self-supervised learning in 3D vision, offering a scalable and reliable solution for real-world applications such as autonomous driving and robotics.  

**Keywords**: Scene flow estimation, self-supervised learning, adversarial training, metric learning, 3D vision, motion prediction.
