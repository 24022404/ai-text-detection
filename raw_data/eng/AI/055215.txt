This paper investigates the application of multi-task learning (MTL) to the analysis and prediction of time series data, with a particular focus on travel demand forecasting. The primary objective is to improve predictive accuracy and generalization by leveraging shared information across multiple related tasks, such as forecasting demand in different regions, transportation modes, or time intervals. The proposed approach utilizes a neural network-based MTL framework, wherein shared layers capture common temporal patterns while task-specific layers address unique characteristics of each series. We evaluate the performance of our model on real-world travel demand datasets, comparing it to traditional single-task learning methods and state-of-the-art time series models. The experimental results demonstrate that the MTL approach consistently outperforms baseline models in terms of both accuracy and robustness, particularly in scenarios with limited data for individual tasks. Additionally, our analysis reveals that knowledge transfer across related tasks enables more effective handling of anomalies and seasonal variations in travel demand. The findings highlight the potential of MTL to enhance demand forecasting, resource allocation, and decision-making in intelligent transportation systems. Key keywords: multi-task learning, time series forecasting, travel demand, neural networks, intelligent transportation systems.
