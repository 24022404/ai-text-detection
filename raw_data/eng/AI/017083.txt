This paper presents a novel approach to hierarchical reinforcement learning, focusing on data efficiency to improve the learning process in complex environments. The objective is to develop an agent that can learn to perform tasks with minimal interaction with the environment, reducing the need for extensive exploration and trial-and-error learning. Our method employs a hierarchical framework that combines reinforcement learning with a structured representation of the environment, allowing the agent to learn abstract skills and compose them to solve challenging tasks. The approach utilizes a combination of intrinsic motivation and extrinsic rewards to guide the learning process, enabling the agent to adapt to new situations and learn from limited data. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in achieving significant improvements in data efficiency, outperforming existing methods in several benchmark environments. The key contributions of this research include the development of a data-efficient hierarchical reinforcement learning algorithm, the introduction of a novel intrinsic motivation mechanism, and the demonstration of its potential for real-world applications, such as robotics and autonomous systems. Key keywords: hierarchical reinforcement learning, data efficiency, intrinsic motivation, autonomous systems, robotics.
