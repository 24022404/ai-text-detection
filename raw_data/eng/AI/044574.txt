This paper addresses the challenge of enabling effective coordination among multiple intelligent agents by introducing a novel graph-driven communication framework. The primary objective is to enhance collective decision-making and collaborative problem-solving in dynamic, distributed environments. Our approach models each agent as a node within a dynamically evolving communication graph, where edges represent real-time, adaptive information exchange pathways. Leveraging advances in graph neural networks (GNNs), agents learn optimal communication strategies and coordination policies through end-to-end reinforcement learning. Experimental results on benchmark multi-agent tasks demonstrate that our method significantly improves coordination efficiency, scalability, and robustness compared to existing message-passing and centralized approaches. The proposed system adapts to varying team sizes and topologies, facilitating generalization to unseen scenarios and tasks. Our findings illustrate the potential of graph-driven architectures for advancing multi-agent cooperation in complex domains such as autonomous vehicles, swarm robotics, and distributed sensor networks. Keywords: multi-agent coordination, graph neural networks, communication protocols, reinforcement learning, distributed systems, swarm intelligence.
