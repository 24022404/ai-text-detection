Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training machine learning models across decentralized and often resource-constrained client systems. However, effective client selection remains a critical challenge, primarily due to variance in client data and system capability. This paper introduces a novel approach called Clustered Sampling, designed to enhance the efficiency and representativity of client selection in FL scenarios. Clustered Sampling stratifies clients into clusters based on data similarity and system characteristics, ensuring a more balanced and representative participation in each training round. Our method employs advanced clustering algorithms to analyze client data distributions, resulting in a lower variance in model updates and improved overall model performance. Through extensive simulations, we show that our approach not only outperforms traditional random sampling but also significantly reduces communication overhead without compromising training accuracy. The results demonstrate notable improvements in convergence speed and model robustness, particularly in heterogeneous and non-IID environments. We conclude that Clustered Sampling offers a compelling advancement for federated learning, potentially facilitating its application in diverse and large-scale deployments. Key keywords: Federated Learning, Clustered Sampling, Client Selection, Low-Variance, Non-IID Data, Model Convergence.
