Object detection models often suffer from overfitting, especially when trained on limited datasets. This paper investigates a novel dropout strategy applied specifically to the Pool5 feature maps of convolutional neural networks to improve object detection performance. Unlike conventional dropout applied to fully connected layers, our approach introduces random dropouts within the spatially rich Pool5 feature maps, forcing the network to learn more robust and diverse feature representations. We hypothesize that this targeted dropout mitigates overfitting by encouraging the network to rely on a broader set of features for object localization and classification. We implement this "Pool5 Dropout" within the Faster R-CNN framework and evaluate its performance on benchmark datasets, including Pascal VOC and COCO. Experimental results demonstrate that our Pool5 Dropout strategy consistently improves mean Average Precision (mAP) compared to baseline models without dropout and those employing standard dropout techniques. Specifically, we observe a significant performance gain on datasets with limited training examples. This work contributes a simple yet effective regularization technique that enhances the generalization capability of object detection models, particularly beneficial in scenarios with scarce data. Keywords: Object Detection, Convolutional Neural Networks, Dropout, Regularization, Feature Maps, Overfitting, Deep Learning.
