This paper introduces a novel approach to 3D object detection that eliminates the need for manual labeling, a significant bottleneck in autonomous driving and robotics. Our method leverages an open-source simulator to generate synthetic data with perfect annotations, enabling the training of robust 3D detectors without human intervention. The proposed pipeline incorporates domain adaptation techniques to bridge the gap between synthetic and real-world data, improving the transferability of learned models. We evaluate our approach on benchmark datasets, demonstrating competitive performance compared to methods relying on manual labels. Specifically, our results show a significant reduction in annotation costs while maintaining high detection accuracy. The framework facilitates rapid development and deployment of 3D detection systems, particularly beneficial in scenarios where real-world data is scarce or expensive to annotate. This work contributes to the field by providing a cost-effective and scalable solution for 3D object detection, fostering further research in label-free learning and domain adaptation. Keywords: 3D object detection, simulation, domain adaptation, manual-label free, autonomous driving.
