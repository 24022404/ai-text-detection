This paper investigates the use of synthetic data for training robust text localisation models in natural images. The objective is to mitigate the challenges of limited and biased real-world data by leveraging synthetically generated images with accurately annotated text. We propose a novel pipeline for creating realistic synthetic scenes with diverse fonts, layouts, and background textures. This pipeline incorporates techniques for blending synthetic text into natural images, simulating various imaging conditions, and generating accurate ground truth annotations. We train a deep learning-based text localisation model using solely synthetic data and evaluate its performance on several benchmark datasets of real-world images. Our results demonstrate that models trained on synthetic data achieve competitive, and in some cases superior, performance compared to models trained on real data, especially in challenging scenarios with low resolution or complex backgrounds. We further explore the effectiveness of fine-tuning models trained on synthetic data with a small amount of real data to further boost performance. The findings indicate that synthetic data offers a viable and scalable alternative for training robust text localisation systems, reducing the reliance on expensive and time-consuming manual annotation. Keywords: Synthetic Data, Text Localisation, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, Natural Images.
