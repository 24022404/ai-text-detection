This paper presents a novel logic-based approach to generatively defined discriminative modeling, aiming to bridge the gap between generative and discriminative models. Our objective is to develop a framework that leverages logical rules to enhance the discriminative power of generative models. We propose a new methodology that integrates logical constraints into the generative process, enabling the model to learn discriminative features while maintaining the generative capabilities. Our approach utilizes a combination of probabilistic logic and deep learning techniques to define a new class of models that can learn from both labeled and unlabeled data. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in improving the discriminative performance of generative models, particularly in cases where labeled data is scarce. The key findings of this study highlight the potential of logic-based approaches in advancing the field of generative modeling, with significant implications for applications such as image classification, natural language processing, and decision-making under uncertainty. Our contributions include the introduction of a novel logical framework for generative modeling, the development of a new class of discriminative models, and the demonstration of improved performance in real-world applications, making this study a valuable addition to the existing literature on machine learning, logic-based modeling, and artificial intelligence.
