Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (SNE) is a powerful dimensionality reduction technique widely used for visualizing high-dimensional data in lower dimensions. This paper presents a comprehensive tutorial and survey of SNE, focusing on its variants that utilize Gaussian and Student-t distributions. The objective is to provide a clear and accessible explanation of the underlying principles, mathematical formulations, and practical applications of these methods. We explore the original SNE algorithm, highlighting its strengths and weaknesses, and then delve into its Gaussian and Student-t based extensions, particularly focusing on t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE). The tutorial covers parameter selection, optimization strategies, and common pitfalls. The survey examines the theoretical underpinnings and empirical performance of each variant, comparing their ability to preserve local data structure and handle crowding problems. Furthermore, we discuss the computational complexity of each method and explore techniques for accelerating computation. Through detailed explanations, illustrative examples, and a comprehensive review of the literature, this paper offers a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners seeking to understand and apply SNE with Gaussian and Student-t distributions for effective data visualization and analysis. Keywords: Stochastic Neighbor Embedding, dimensionality reduction, visualization, Gaussian distribution, Student-t distribution, t-SNE, data analysis.
