Accurate and timely building damage assessment following natural disasters is crucial for effective disaster response and humanitarian aid. This paper introduces BDANet, a novel multiscale convolutional neural network architecture enhanced with cross-directional attention mechanisms, designed for precise building damage assessment from satellite imagery. BDANet utilizes a multiscale feature extraction module to capture contextual information at varying resolutions, enabling robust identification of damage patterns. Furthermore, we introduce a cross-directional attention mechanism that adaptively weights feature maps in both spatial and channel dimensions, enhancing the network's ability to focus on damage-related features and suppress irrelevant background information. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that BDANet significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of precision, recall, and overall accuracy. The proposed approach offers a reliable and efficient solution for automated building damage assessment, contributing to improved disaster management and resource allocation. Keywords: building damage assessment, satellite imagery, convolutional neural network, multiscale feature extraction, attention mechanism, disaster response.
