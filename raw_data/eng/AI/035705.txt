This paper addresses the challenge of likelihood estimation in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), a crucial aspect of evaluating their performance and applicability. Our objective is to develop a novel approach for estimating the likelihood of generated samples, which is essential for tasks such as image and data generation. We propose a method that leverages the adversarial process inherent in GANs to estimate the likelihood of generated samples. Our approach utilizes a combination of density estimation techniques and adversarial training to learn a likelihood estimator that is capable of distinguishing between real and generated samples. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, showing significant improvements in likelihood estimation accuracy compared to existing approaches. Our research contributes to the advancement of GANs and has important implications for applications such as image generation, data augmentation, and anomaly detection. Key contributions include a novel likelihood estimation framework, improved performance, and potential applications in computer vision and machine learning, with relevant keywords including GANs, likelihood estimation, density estimation, and adversarial training.
