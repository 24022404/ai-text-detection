This paper introduces Probability Functional Descent (PFD), a novel framework that unifies core algorithms from Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Variational Inference (VI), and Reinforcement Learning (RL) under a single theoretical perspective. The objective is to bridge foundational techniques in machine learning by formulating their optimization processes as functionals over probability distributions. We rigorously analyze the fundamental connections between divergence minimization in GANs, evidence lower bound maximization in VI, and policy optimization in RL through the lens of probability functionals. Our approach systematically derives key algorithmic procedures—such as adversarial objectives, variational bounds, and policy gradients—as instances of the PFD paradigm. Empirical evaluations across standard benchmarks in generative modeling and reinforcement learning demonstrate that algorithms instantiated with the PFD framework achieve competitive performance, while offering clearer theoretical insights and improved convergence properties. By establishing a unified mathematical foundation, this work facilitates the transfer of methods and intuitions across traditionally separate domains, enabling new algorithmic designs and deeper understanding of existing models. Keywords: probability distributions, functional optimization, GANs, variational inference, reinforcement learning, unifying framework, machine learning theory.
