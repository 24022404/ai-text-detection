This paper addresses the problem of bandits with feedback graphs and switching costs, a scenario where a learner must make sequential decisions to maximize cumulative rewards while incurring costs for switching between actions. Our objective is to develop efficient algorithms that balance exploration and exploitation in this setting. We propose a novel approach that utilizes feedback graphs to model the partial feedback structure and incorporates switching costs into the decision-making process. Our method employs a probabilistic model to estimate the rewards and switching costs, and uses an adaptive strategy to select the next action. The results show that our algorithm achieves significant improvements in cumulative rewards compared to existing methods, particularly in scenarios with high switching costs. Our research contributes to the understanding of bandit problems with complex feedback structures and has implications for real-world applications, such as recommendation systems and resource allocation. Key contributions include the development of a feedback graph-based algorithm, the incorporation of switching costs, and the demonstration of improved performance in scenarios with high switching costs. Relevant keywords: bandits, feedback graphs, switching costs, online learning, sequential decision-making.
