The increasing use of computed tomography (CT) scans in clinical diagnosis necessitates efficient and accurate methods for lesion detection and segmentation. This study addresses the challenge of lesion segmentation with minimal annotation effort by proposing a weakly-supervised co-segmentation approach.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel framework utilizing weakly-supervised learning techniques for lesion segmentation in CT scans. Our method leverages co-segmentation to concurrently segment multiple lesions, enhancing the model's ability to recognize diverse lesion characteristics within similar or different CT images. The algorithm employs minimal labeled data, reinforced by leveraging complementary unlabeled data to improve segmentation accuracy. We employ a combination of deep learning architectures with feature-sharing techniques to solve the segmentation task effectively.

Results/Findings: The proposed approach was evaluated on a dataset of CT scans containing various types of lesions. Experimental results demonstrate that our framework significantly outperforms traditional fully-supervised models, achieving high accuracy in lesion boundary delineation with reduced dependency on extensive labeled datasets. Our method showed strong generalization across different types of lesions and patient demographics, highlighting its robustness and practicality.

Conclusion/Implications: This work contributes a significant advancement in the realm of medical imaging by introducing a weakly-supervised co-segmentation method that effectively balances accuracy and annotation effort. The framework's ability to perform with minimal labels represents a step forward in automating medical imaging processes, paving the way for practical applications in clinical settings. The approach can be extended to other medical imaging modalities, promoting broader applicability.

Keywords: Weakly-supervised learning, lesion segmentation, CT scans, co-segmentation, medical imaging, deep learning, automated diagnosis.
