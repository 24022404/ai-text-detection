This paper addresses the challenge of achieving robust k-anonymity in data publishing while preserving the original data distribution, a key requirement for accurate downstream analytics and machine learning applications. Traditional k-anonymity methods often distort data distributions, adversely affecting the utility of anonymized datasets. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel distribution-preserving k-anonymity framework that integrates advanced clustering techniques with distribution-aware optimization. Our approach constructs equivalence classes that satisfy k-anonymity constraints while minimizing alterations to the underlying data distribution. Experimental results on multiple real-world datasets demonstrate that our method consistently enhances statistical fidelity and model performance compared to standard k-anonymization and differential privacy approaches. The proposed technique also ensures scalability and practical efficiency for large datasets. These findings suggest that distribution-preserving k-anonymity offers a promising solution for privacy-preserving data sharing in domains such as healthcare, finance, and IoT, where the accuracy of anonymized data is critical. Keywords: k-anonymity, data privacy, distribution preservation, clustering, data utility, anonymization.
