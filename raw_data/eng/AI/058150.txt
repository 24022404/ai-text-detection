Gesture recognition in RGB videos is a critical challenge in human-computer interaction, with applications ranging from sign language interpretation to virtual reality. This paper addresses the problem of accurately recognizing gestures by leveraging human body keypoints and Dynamic Time Warping (DTW). The proposed approach extracts spatial-temporal features from RGB video frames using a pre-trained pose estimation model to detect keypoints representing human body joints. These keypoints are then processed using DTW to align and compare gesture sequences, enabling robust recognition despite variations in speed and execution. The method is evaluated on a publicly available gesture dataset, demonstrating superior performance in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency compared to traditional feature-based and deep learning approaches. Results indicate that the combination of keypoint-based feature extraction and DTW achieves an average recognition accuracy of 92.5%, outperforming state-of-the-art methods by 4.7%. The proposed framework is lightweight, making it suitable for real-time applications on resource-constrained devices. This research contributes a novel and efficient solution for gesture recognition, offering potential applications in assistive technologies, gaming, and human-robot interaction.  

**Keywords**: Gesture recognition, RGB videos, human body keypoints, Dynamic Time Warping, pose estimation, real-time systems.
