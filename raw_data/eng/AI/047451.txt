Sparse graphical models are widely used to represent complex dependencies in high-dimensional data, but their estimation from truncated samples remains a significant challenge. This paper addresses the problem of efficiently estimating sparse graphical models when data is subject to truncation, a common scenario in real-world applications such as sensor networks, genomics, and social network analysis. We propose a novel approach that combines penalized likelihood estimation with a tailored optimization framework to handle the constraints imposed by truncated samples. Our method leverages sparsity-inducing regularization to ensure scalability and robustness in high-dimensional settings. Through extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets, we demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing methods in terms of estimation accuracy and computational efficiency. The results highlight the ability of our method to recover the underlying graph structure even under severe truncation, making it a practical tool for applications where data incompleteness is a critical issue. This work contributes to the field by providing a computationally efficient and statistically sound solution for sparse graphical model estimation from truncated samples, with potential applications in diverse domains such as anomaly detection, network inference, and causal discovery.  

**Keywords**: sparse graphical models, truncated data, penalized likelihood, high-dimensional statistics, optimization, network inference.
