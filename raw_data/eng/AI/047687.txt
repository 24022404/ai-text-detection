Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have demonstrated remarkable performance across various graph-related tasks. However, effectively capturing multi-scale relationships within graphs remains a significant challenge. This paper introduces Graph Filtration Learning (GFL), a novel approach to learn hierarchical graph representations by progressively filtering graph signals through a series of learned graph filters. GFL employs a spectral-based filter design integrated with a differentiable pooling mechanism, enabling the network to adaptively learn graph structures at different scales. We evaluate GFL on a variety of graph classification and node classification benchmarks. Experimental results demonstrate that GFL consistently outperforms existing GNN models, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several datasets. The learned graph filters provide valuable insights into the underlying graph structure and highlight the importance of multi-scale analysis for effective graph representation learning. These findings suggest GFL’s potential for broader applications in network analysis, drug discovery, and social network analysis.
Keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Graph Filtration, Spectral Graph Theory, Graph Pooling, Multi-scale Learning.
