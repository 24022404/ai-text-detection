This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised domain adaptation in object detection, where a model trained on a labeled source domain is applied to an unlabeled target domain with different distributions. The objective is to develop an uncertainty-aware approach that can effectively adapt to the target domain while quantifying the uncertainty of its predictions. Our method employs a novel uncertainty estimation module that leverages Bayesian neural networks and Monte Carlo dropout to model the epistemic uncertainty of the detector. We also propose a domain-invariant feature learning framework that minimizes the domain shift between the source and target domains. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art unsupervised domain adaptation methods, achieving significant improvements in detection accuracy and uncertainty calibration. The key contributions of this work include the introduction of uncertainty-awareness in unsupervised domain adaptation and the development of a robust domain-invariant feature learning framework. Our approach has important implications for real-world applications, such as autonomous driving and surveillance, where object detection models are required to operate in diverse and uncertain environments. Key keywords: unsupervised domain adaptation, object detection, uncertainty estimation, Bayesian neural networks, domain-invariant feature learning.
