This paper presents a novel approach to representation learning of pedestrian trajectories using an actor-critic sequence-to-sequence autoencoder. The objective is to learn compact and informative representations of pedestrian movement patterns, enabling accurate prediction and analysis of future trajectories. Our method leverages a sequence-to-sequence autoencoder framework, where the encoder processes the input trajectory and the decoder generates a reconstructed trajectory, while an actor-critic module optimizes the representation by predicting future movements. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, outperforming existing methods in terms of reconstruction accuracy and future trajectory prediction. The proposed model has significant implications for various applications, including autonomous vehicles, surveillance, and smart cities. Key contributions include the integration of actor-critic reinforcement learning with sequence-to-sequence autoencoders, and the ability to learn robust and generalizable representations of pedestrian trajectories. Relevant keywords: pedestrian trajectory prediction, sequence-to-sequence autoencoder, actor-critic reinforcement learning, representation learning, autonomous vehicles, smart cities.
