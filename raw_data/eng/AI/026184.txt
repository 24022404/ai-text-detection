This paper introduces Side-Tuning, a novel approach for network adaptation that leverages additive side networks to enable efficient and flexible fine-tuning of pre-trained models. The objective of this work is to address the limitations of traditional fine-tuning methods, which often require extensive computational resources and risk catastrophic forgetting. Side-Tuning proposes a lightweight framework where a small side network is trained in parallel with a frozen pre-trained model, allowing the system to adapt to new tasks while preserving the original model's knowledge. The method is evaluated across multiple datasets and tasks, including image classification and natural language processing, demonstrating competitive performance compared to full fine-tuning and other adaptation techniques. Results show that Side-Tuning achieves significant computational savings while maintaining or exceeding the accuracy of existing methods. The approach is particularly advantageous in scenarios with limited computational resources or when adapting to multiple downstream tasks. This work contributes a practical and scalable baseline for network adaptation, offering insights into the trade-offs between model performance, resource efficiency, and adaptability. Keywords: network adaptation, fine-tuning, side networks, pre-trained models, computational efficiency.
