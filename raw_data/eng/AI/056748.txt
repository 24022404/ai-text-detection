This paper introduces Wavelet Networks, a novel deep learning framework designed to learn scale equivariant representations directly from raw waveforms. The objective is to develop a model that can effectively capture and process complex patterns in waveform data, such as audio or seismic signals, while being invariant to scale transformations. Our approach leverages the strengths of wavelet analysis and neural networks to create a scalable and efficient architecture. We employ a wavelet-based front-end to extract time-frequency features, which are then fed into a neural network to learn higher-level representations. Our results demonstrate that Wavelet Networks outperform traditional convolutional neural networks in various waveform classification tasks, showcasing improved robustness to scale variations and noise. The key contributions of this work include the introduction of a scale equivariant learning framework, the development of a wavelet-based neural network architecture, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in real-world applications. This research has significant implications for fields such as audio processing, signal processing, and geophysics, where scale equivariance is crucial. Key keywords: Wavelet Networks, scale equivariance, waveform classification, deep learning, time-frequency analysis.
