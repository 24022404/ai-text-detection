This paper addresses the challenge of action recognition in 3D body skeleton sequences by leveraging the power of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) combined with supervised domain adaptation techniques. Our primary objective is to enhance the accuracy and adaptability of action recognition systems when handling domain shifts between source and target datasets. The proposed approach utilizes kernel feature maps to effectively capture the inherent patterns within 3D skeletal data. By mapping skeletal sequences into a high-dimensional feature space, we can preserve unique action characteristics and improve cross-domain generalization. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our CNN-based method significantly outperforms existing techniques in terms of both recognition accuracy and domain adaptation capability. Furthermore, our system exhibits robustness in various real-world scenarios, illustrating its potential applicability in diverse fields such as human-computer interaction, sports analysis, and surveillance. The research contributes to the domain of action recognition by offering a novel method that seamlessly integrates 3D skeletal analysis with cutting-edge domain adaptation techniques, paving the way for more accurate and adaptable recognition systems. Key keywords include action recognition, convolutional neural networks, supervised domain adaptation, 3D body skeletons, and kernel feature maps.
