This paper presents a novel approach to inverse rendering, leveraging polarimetric multi-view techniques to estimate the reflectance and geometry of real-world scenes. Our objective is to develop a robust and accurate method for recovering detailed scene properties from a set of polarimetric images captured under varying viewing conditions. We propose a physics-based framework that combines polarimetric imaging with multi-view stereo, enabling the estimation of scene reflectance, normal maps, and depth information. Our approach utilizes a deep learning-based algorithm to infer the polarimetric properties of scenes, which are then used to refine the inverse rendering process. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our method in reconstructing high-quality scenes with accurate reflectance and geometry. The proposed technique has significant implications for various applications, including computer vision, robotics, and computer-generated imagery. Key contributions of this research include the integration of polarimetric imaging with multi-view inverse rendering, and the development of a deep learning-based framework for estimating scene properties. Relevant keywords: polarimetric imaging, multi-view stereo, inverse rendering, deep learning, computer vision, 3D reconstruction.
