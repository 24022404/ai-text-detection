This paper presents a novel approach to subjective annotation for a frame interpolation benchmark, leveraging artefact amplification to enhance the evaluation of video frame interpolation algorithms. The objective is to develop a reliable and efficient method for assessing the quality of interpolated frames, addressing the limitations of existing annotation techniques. Our approach employs a systematic artefact amplification process to exaggerate errors and artefacts in interpolated frames, facilitating more accurate and consistent subjective evaluations. The results show that our method yields more reliable and discriminating annotations, enabling a more comprehensive comparison of frame interpolation algorithms. The key findings highlight the importance of artefact amplification in revealing subtle differences in interpolation quality, leading to a more robust benchmark for evaluating frame interpolation techniques. This research contributes to the development of more accurate and efficient video processing algorithms, with potential applications in fields such as video editing, computer vision, and virtual reality. Key keywords: frame interpolation, artefact amplification, subjective annotation, video quality assessment, benchmarking.
