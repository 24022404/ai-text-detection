**Abstract:**  
This paper introduces a novel framework, Wasserstein Dictionary Learning (WDL), that leverages optimal transport theory for unsupervised non-linear dictionary learning. Traditional dictionary learning methods are predominantly linear, often struggling to effectively represent data with complex, non-linear structures. Our approach formulates dictionary learning as an optimal transport problem, utilizing Wasserstein distances to better capture the underlying geometry of data distributions. We develop an iterative algorithm that alternates between optimizing the dictionary atoms and sparse representations by computing optimal transport maps, which enables flexible modeling beyond linear combinations. Experimental evaluations on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that WDL significantly outperforms classical linear dictionary learning and kernel-based extensions in tasks such as image reconstruction and clustering, particularly in the presence of non-linear data manifolds. The proposed method is scalable and compatible with high-dimensional datasets due to algorithmic advances in computational optimal transport. Our findings indicate that integrating Wasserstein geometry into dictionary learning provides a powerful tool for unsupervised representation learning, with potential applications in signal processing, computer vision, and machine learning. **Keywords:** dictionary learning, optimal transport, Wasserstein distance, unsupervised learning, non-linear representation, machine learning.
