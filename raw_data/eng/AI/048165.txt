This paper introduces LEGAN, a novel framework designed to disentangle and manipulate directional lighting and facial expressions in facial images by leveraging human perceptual judgments. The objective of this research is to address the challenge of independently controlling lighting and expression attributes, which are often intertwined in facial image synthesis. To achieve this, LEGAN integrates a generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture with a perceptual loss function derived from human-annotated datasets, ensuring that the generated images align with human visual perception. The proposed method employs a two-stage training process: first, a disentanglement module separates lighting and expression features, and second, a refinement module fine-tunes the outputs based on perceptual feedback. Experimental results demonstrate that LEGAN outperforms existing methods in terms of both visual quality and attribute control, achieving superior disentanglement accuracy and photorealism. The framework’s ability to generate realistic facial images with precise control over lighting and expression has significant implications for applications in computer graphics, virtual reality, and facial expression analysis. This work contributes to the field by introducing a human-centric approach to disentangled image synthesis, offering a new direction for future research in facial attribute manipulation.  

**Keywords**: LEGAN, disentangled representation, facial expression synthesis, directional lighting, perceptual judgments, GAN, human-centric AI.
