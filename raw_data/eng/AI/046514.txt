This paper introduces Dam Burst, a novel region-merging-based image segmentation method designed to improve the accuracy and efficiency of partitioning digital images into meaningful regions. The primary objective is to address limitations of existing segmentation algorithms, particularly those resulting in over-segmentation or requiring extensive parameter tuning. Dam Burst initiates by partitioning an input image into small, homogeneous primitive regions and iteratively merges adjacent regions based on adaptive similarity measures encompassing color, texture, and spatial proximity. The proposed approach employs a dynamic thresholding mechanism, inspired by the concept of a "bursting dam," to effectively balance region merging without compromising structural details. Experimental evaluations were conducted on benchmark datasets, demonstrating that Dam Burst achieves higher segmentation quality and improved computational performance compared to conventional region-merging and clustering techniques. The method also shows robustness across varying image types and complexities. In conclusion, Dam Burst offers a scalable and reliable solution for image segmentation tasks in applications such as medical imaging, remote sensing, and computer vision. The key contributions of this work include an adaptive region similarity metric, a novel merging strategy, and comprehensive performance analysis. Keywords: image segmentation, region merging, clustering, adaptive thresholding, dam burst method, computer vision.
