This paper investigates the integration of causal reasoning into machine learning (ML) to address limitations in traditional statistical approaches. We introduce a framework that leverages causal inference techniques to improve model robustness, interpretability, and generalization. Our approach incorporates causal discovery algorithms to identify underlying causal structures within datasets, enabling the development of ML models that are less susceptible to spurious correlations. We demonstrate the efficacy of our framework through experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets, including a comparative analysis against standard ML models. Results show that causality-aware ML models exhibit enhanced predictive performance, particularly in scenarios with distributional shifts and confounding variables. Furthermore, the incorporation of causal knowledge facilitates improved model explainability and allows for more informed decision-making. This research contributes to the growing body of work on causal ML by providing a practical framework and empirical evidence of its benefits, paving the way for more reliable and trustworthy AI systems.

Keywords: Causality, Machine Learning, Causal Inference, Causal Discovery, Explainability, Robustness, Generalization.
