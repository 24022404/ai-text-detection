Abstract:

Evaluating text generation models requires a nuanced assessment of both the diversity and quality of their outputs, yet most existing metrics focus on these aspects independently, often overlooking their trade-offs. This paper introduces a novel framework for jointly measuring diversity and quality in text generation models, addressing critical limitations of current evaluation methodologies. Our approach leverages a combination of semantic similarity metrics and distributional statistics to capture the breadth of generated content alongside its linguistic coherence and relevance. We propose new composite scoring techniques that seamlessly integrate measures such as lexical variety, semantic distance, and fluency, enabling comprehensive evaluation across varied text generation tasks. Extensive experiments conducted on benchmark datasets using state-of-the-art neural language models—including transformers and large generative models—demonstrate that our metrics better correspond with human judgments compared to traditional isolated measures. The results reveal deeper insights into model performance, highlighting informative trade-offs and guiding the selection of generation strategies. This joint evaluation framework advances the field by offering a robust, interpretable tool for assessing and improving text generation systems. Keywords: text generation, diversity, quality assessment, evaluation metrics, natural language processing, neural language models.
