This paper presents a novel approach for efficient sampling of chemical space through the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) aimed at the inverse design of inorganic materials. The objective of this research is to address the challenges associated with the exploration of vast chemical spaces which can hinder material discovery. Our approach leverages the generative modeling capabilities of GANs to generate realistic chemical compounds, thereby streamlining the inverse design process. We implemented a GAN architecture tailored for the unique characteristics of inorganic materials, allowing for the generation of high-quality candidates with specific desired properties. Results demonstrate a significant improvement in the sampling efficiency compared to traditional techniques, with our model effectively generating a diverse set of chemical structures that meet predefined criteria. The findings indicate that our GAN-based method not only accelerates the discovery process but also enhances the likelihood of identifying novel materials with superior performance metrics. This research contributes important insights into the applicability of AI-driven methods in materials science and lays the groundwork for future advancements in computational material design. Potential applications of this work extend to various industries, including electronics, catalysis, and energy storage. 

Keywords: Generative adversarial networks, chemical space, inverse design, inorganic materials, material discovery.
