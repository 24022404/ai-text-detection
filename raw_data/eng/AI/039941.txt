Understanding social relationships from visual data is a challenging task due to the complexities of human interactions and contextual variations. This paper introduces the Dual-Glance Model (DGM), a novel approach that leverages both holistic scene understanding and detailed individual feature analysis for improved relationship recognition. DGM employs a two-branch architecture: a global branch capturing the overall scene context using a transformer network, and a local branch focusing on individual appearances and interactions via a graph convolutional network. These branches are fused using an attention mechanism, allowing the model to dynamically weight the importance of global and local cues. Experiments on benchmark datasets, including Visual Relationship Detection (VRD) and Visual Genome, demonstrate that DGM achieves state-of-the-art performance in relationship classification, outperforming existing methods by a significant margin. Further analysis reveals that DGM effectively captures subtle social cues and handles ambiguous scenarios. The proposed model offers a robust and interpretable framework for automated social relationship understanding with potential applications in social robotics, surveillance, and personalized services.

Keywords: Social Relationship Recognition, Visual Relationship Detection, Graph Convolutional Networks, Transformer Networks, Deep Learning, Scene Understanding.
