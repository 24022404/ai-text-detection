This paper introduces a novel approach to unsupervised text recognition, leveraging the inverse relationship between reading and spelling to develop a self-supervised learning framework. Our objective is to enable machines to learn reading by spelling, without relying on labeled datasets or extensive manual annotation. We propose a unique methodology that utilizes generative models to learn the mapping between spoken and written language, allowing the system to discover the underlying patterns and structures of text. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, achieving competitive performance with state-of-the-art supervised methods on benchmark datasets. Our findings suggest that the proposed framework can learn to recognize text in an unsupervised manner, with significant implications for applications in low-resource languages, historical document analysis, and autonomous learning systems. The key contributions of this research include the development of a self-supervised text recognition framework, the introduction of a novel spelling-to-reading paradigm, and the demonstration of its potential for unsupervised learning. Relevant keywords: unsupervised text recognition, self-supervised learning, generative models, reading by spelling, autonomous learning systems.
