This paper explores the challenge of training agents to cooperate effectively in multi-agent environments using policy search methods. We address the limitations of traditional reinforcement learning approaches in cooperative settings by introducing a novel policy search algorithm that explicitly encourages agents to learn policies that maximize joint rewards. Our approach combines a centralized critic with decentralized actor networks, enabling efficient credit assignment and exploration of cooperative strategies. We evaluate our method on a suite of benchmark cooperative games, including variations of the multi-agent particle environment and the Overcooked environment. Results demonstrate that our algorithm significantly outperforms existing policy gradient and evolutionary strategies in terms of both learning speed and achieved cooperation level. Furthermore, we analyze the emergent communication patterns and role specialization observed in the learned policies, providing insights into the dynamics of cooperation. The findings suggest that our policy search approach offers a promising avenue for training cooperative agents in complex, real-world scenarios. Keywords: multi-agent reinforcement learning, policy search, cooperation, game theory, decentralized control.
