Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have become integral to many data-driven applications due to their remarkable ability to synthesize realistic data. However, their vulnerability to membership inference attacks poses significant privacy risks, as adversaries may determine whether specific samples were part of the training dataset. This paper introduces privGAN, a novel framework designed to defend GANs against membership inference attacks while maintaining high data generation quality and incurring minimal computational overhead. Our approach integrates lightweight noise injection and adversarial regularization techniques to obscure sensitive membership information without significantly compromising the fidelity or diversity of generated samples. We systematically evaluate privGAN across multiple benchmark datasets and GAN architectures, demonstrating its effectiveness in reducing attack success rates by up to 60% compared to baseline models, with negligible impact on standard image quality metrics such as Inception Score (IS) and Frechet Inception Distance (FID). Additionally, privGAN exhibits low runtime overhead, making it a practical solution for privacy-preserving generative modeling in resource-constrained environments. The findings indicate that privGAN achieves a balance between robust privacy protection and efficient GAN performance, presenting a practical advancement in safeguarding deep learning models. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, privacy, membership inference attack, adversarial regularization, noise injection, deep learning security.
