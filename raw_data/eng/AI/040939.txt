This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised domain translation, where the goal is to transfer knowledge between domains without paired data. Traditional approaches often require extensive labeled datasets or fail to generalize across diverse domains. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel framework, *Learning to Transfer*, which leverages unsupervised meta-learning to enable efficient and adaptive domain translation. Our approach introduces a meta-optimization strategy that learns domain-invariant representations by iteratively adapting to multiple source-target domain pairs. The framework employs a dual-network architecture, combining a domain translator with a meta-learner, to dynamically adjust translation parameters based on domain-specific characteristics. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of translation accuracy and domain adaptability, outperforming existing unsupervised techniques by a significant margin. Furthermore, the proposed framework exhibits robust generalization capabilities, making it suitable for real-world applications such as cross-domain image synthesis and style transfer. This work contributes to advancing unsupervised domain translation by introducing a scalable and adaptive solution that reduces reliance on labeled data while maintaining high translation quality.  

**Keywords**: unsupervised learning, domain translation, meta-learning, cross-domain adaptation, image synthesis, style transfer.
