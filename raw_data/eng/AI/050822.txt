Salient object detection (SOD) is a critical task in computer vision, with applications ranging from image segmentation to object recognition. Traditional SOD methods often rely on predefined thresholds to determine saliency, which can limit their adaptability and performance across diverse datasets. This paper addresses the challenge of threshold-free salient object detection by proposing a novel optimization framework that directly maximizes the F-measure, a widely used metric for evaluating SOD performance. Our approach eliminates the need for manual threshold tuning by integrating F-measure optimization into the training process, enabling the model to learn saliency maps that are inherently aligned with the evaluation metric. We introduce a differentiable approximation of the F-measure, allowing gradient-based optimization within deep learning architectures. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing threshold-based and threshold-free approaches. The proposed framework not only improves detection accuracy but also enhances robustness across varying image complexities. This work contributes to the advancement of SOD by providing a more effective and practical solution for real-world applications, where adaptability and precision are paramount.  

**Keywords**: Salient object detection, F-measure optimization, threshold-free methods, deep learning, computer vision.
