Distributional Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has emerged as a promising approach for capturing the full distribution of returns in reinforcement learning tasks, offering deeper insights into decision-making under uncertainty. This paper investigates the role of statistics and sampling techniques in enhancing the performance and stability of DRL algorithms. We propose a novel framework that integrates advanced statistical methods, including quantile regression and kernel density estimation, to improve the accuracy of return distribution predictions. Additionally, we explore the impact of different sampling strategies, such as stratified and importance sampling, on the efficiency of learning and convergence. Through extensive experiments on benchmark environments, our approach demonstrates significant improvements in both prediction accuracy and computational efficiency compared to existing DRL methods. The results highlight the importance of leveraging statistical tools and optimized sampling techniques in DRL, offering new directions for future research. This work contributes to the growing body of knowledge in reinforcement learning by providing a robust and scalable framework for distributional value estimation, with potential applications in robotics, finance, and autonomous systems.  

**Keywords**: Distributional Reinforcement Learning, Quantile Regression, Kernel Density Estimation, Sampling Strategies, Decision-Making, Uncertainty.
