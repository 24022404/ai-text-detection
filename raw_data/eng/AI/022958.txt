This paper addresses the vulnerability of image retrieval systems to adversarial attacks, focusing on the development of a universal perturbation attack capable of deceiving retrieval models across diverse datasets. The objective is to investigate the robustness of state-of-the-art image retrieval systems and propose a method to generate perturbations that can universally degrade retrieval performance. We introduce a novel adversarial attack framework that leverages optimization techniques to craft perturbations applicable to any input image, regardless of its content. The approach is evaluated on multiple benchmark datasets, including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, using popular retrieval models such as deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed universal perturbation significantly reduces retrieval accuracy, achieving a notable drop in mean average precision (mAP) compared to baseline systems. Furthermore, the attack exhibits strong transferability across different retrieval models, highlighting a critical security concern for real-world applications. This study underscores the importance of enhancing the robustness of image retrieval systems against adversarial threats and provides insights into potential defense mechanisms. The findings contribute to the broader field of adversarial machine learning, emphasizing the need for secure and reliable retrieval systems in practical scenarios.  

**Keywords**: adversarial attack, image retrieval, universal perturbation, robustness, deep learning, CNN, transferability.
