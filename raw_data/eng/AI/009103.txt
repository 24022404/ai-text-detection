This paper addresses the challenge of applying reinforcement learning (RL) to continuous control problems when the agent's internal model of the environment differs from the true environment dynamics (model misspecification). We propose a robust RL algorithm that explicitly accounts for potential model inaccuracies during policy optimization. Our approach incorporates a novel uncertainty-aware penalty within the RL objective, discouraging policies that are overly sensitive to discrepancies between the learned model and the real world. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our algorithm on a suite of continuous control tasks, including robotic manipulation and locomotion, in environments with varying degrees of model misspecification. Experimental results show that our robust RL agent significantly outperforms standard RL methods, exhibiting improved stability, resilience to model errors, and generalization to unseen environmental conditions. The findings highlight the importance of considering model uncertainty in RL for real-world applications and offer a practical approach to enhance the reliability of learned control policies.

Keywords: Reinforcement Learning, Robust Control, Model Misspecification, Continuous Control, Uncertainty, Robotics.
