This paper introduces ALET (Automated Labeling of Equipment and Tools), a comprehensive dataset, a baseline detection model, and a real-world use case for tool detection “in the wild.” Objective: Rapid and accurate identification of tools and equipment in unconstrained environments remains a key challenge for applications in construction, manufacturing, robotics, and safety monitoring. Methods: We collected and annotated over 12,000 images featuring 25 common tools across diverse backgrounds, lighting conditions, and viewpoints. Building on this dataset, we implemented a baseline object detector using a state-of-the-art convolutional neural network with transfer learning, optimized for speed and robustness. Results: On a held-out test set, our baseline achieved a mean average precision (mAP) of 68.5% and real-time inference at 25 frames per second on a single GPU. Comparative analysis shows a 15% improvement over off-the-shelf models trained on general object datasets. Use Case: We demonstrate ALET’s applicability in an autonomous robotic arm setup for inventory management, achieving 92% accuracy in tool retrieval tasks under variable lighting. Conclusion: ALET fills a critical gap by providing a large-scale, diverse tool detection dataset and an open benchmark that fosters reproducibility and further research. Its contributions benefit a range of IT and engineering domains, from automated inspection to human–robot collaboration.  
Keywords: ALET, tool detection, dataset, object detection, baseline model, computer vision, industrial automation.
