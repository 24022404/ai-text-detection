This paper aims to improve the information propagation in Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) by introducing heterogeneous aggregations. The objective is to enhance the ability of GNNs to learn effective node representations by aggregating information from diverse neighbors. To achieve this, we propose a novel framework that combines multiple aggregation methods, including attention-based, graph convolutional, and graph attentional aggregations. Our approach allows for adaptive weighting of different aggregations, enabling the model to capture complex relationships between nodes. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing GNN models on several benchmark datasets, achieving significant improvements in node classification and graph classification tasks. The key findings highlight the importance of heterogeneous aggregations in enhancing information propagation and improving the overall performance of GNNs. This research contributes to the development of more effective GNN architectures, with potential applications in social network analysis, recommendation systems, and graph-based machine learning. Key keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Heterogeneous Aggregations, Information Propagation, Node Representation, Graph Classification.
