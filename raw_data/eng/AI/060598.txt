This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised monocular stereo matching, a critical task in 3D scene reconstruction and depth estimation. Traditional stereo matching methods rely on supervised learning with labeled datasets, which are often expensive and time-consuming to acquire. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel unsupervised framework that leverages monocular images to estimate depth without requiring ground-truth depth labels. Our approach integrates a self-supervised loss function based on photometric consistency and geometric constraints, enabling the model to learn depth estimation directly from unlabeled image sequences. Additionally, we introduce a multi-scale feature extraction module to enhance the accuracy of depth predictions across varying scene complexities. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art supervised approaches, while significantly reducing the dependency on labeled data. The proposed framework not only advances the field of unsupervised depth estimation but also offers practical applications in robotics, autonomous navigation, and augmented reality. Key contributions include a robust unsupervised learning pipeline, improved depth prediction accuracy, and a scalable solution for real-world scenarios. Keywords: unsupervised learning, monocular depth estimation, stereo matching, 3D reconstruction, photometric consistency.
