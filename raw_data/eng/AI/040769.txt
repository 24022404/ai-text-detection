The aim of this research is to address the challenge of effectively simulating defective samples to enhance automatic surface defect segmentation in industrial applications. Current methods often struggle with the variability and complexity of real-world defects, underscoring the need for innovative techniques.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel application of Neural Style Transfer (NST) for simulating defective samples. Our approach involves leveraging NST to blend textures and patterns from defective surfaces onto normal samples, thus generating a diverse set of defect-augmented data. This simulated dataset is then used to train and evaluate a deep learning-based segmentation algorithm designed for automatic defect detection.

Results/Findings: The application of NST significantly improves the data diversity, which, in turn, enhances the segmentation algorithm's performance. Compared to traditional methods, our approach demonstrates increased accuracy and robustness across various types of surface defects. The proposed system achieved notable improvements in precision and recall metrics when tested against benchmark datasets.

Conclusion/Implications: This research introduces an innovative use of neural style transfer to effectively simulate defective samples, presenting a valuable contribution to the field of automatic defect detection. The ability to generate realistic defect data can greatly enhance machine learning models' training processes, potentially reducing the need for exhaustive manual data collection. Our method's scalability and adaptability suggest promising applications in quality control and industrial automation, fostering improved inspection processes across multiple sectors.

Keywords: neural style transfer, surface defect segmentation, deep learning, data simulation, automatic defect detection, industrial automation, quality control.
