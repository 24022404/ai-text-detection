The rapid growth of machine learning applications has driven the need for efficient training of large-scale neural network architectures, such as the Legendre Memory Unit (LMU), which is designed for long-range sequence modeling and time series tasks. This paper addresses the computational challenges associated with training LMUs by proposing a parallelized training framework that leverages modern high-performance computing resources. Our approach restructures the LMU training process to exploit parallelism at both the data and computational graph levels, utilizing multi-core CPUs and GPUs to accelerate gradient computations and memory operations. Experimental results on standard sequence modeling benchmarks demonstrate significant reductions in training time without compromising model accuracy, achieving up to a 5x speedup compared to traditional sequential LMU training methods. Furthermore, we analyze the scalability of our framework across varying hardware configurations and dataset sizes, highlighting its adaptability to diverse computational environments. The proposed parallel training strategy not only enhances the practicality of deploying LMU-based models in real-world, large-scale applications but also opens avenues for more efficient research and development in memory-augmented neural networks. Keywords: Legendre Memory Unit, parallel computing, sequence modeling, neural network training, high-performance computing, GPU acceleration.
