Monocular depth estimation is a challenging problem in computer vision, crucial for applications like autonomous navigation and scene understanding. This paper introduces AdaBins, a novel approach to depth estimation that utilizes adaptive discretization of depth values into bins. Unlike existing methods that rely on uniform bin spacing, AdaBins dynamically adjusts bin boundaries based on the input image, allowing for more efficient and accurate depth prediction, particularly in scenes with varying depth distributions. The proposed method employs a transformer-based architecture to predict adaptive bin locations, followed by a bin classification and refinement module to estimate the final depth map. Experimental results on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate that AdaBins achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods in terms of accuracy and robustness. Key contributions include the adaptive binning strategy, the transformer-based bin prediction network, and a comprehensive evaluation demonstrating its effectiveness. This approach offers a significant improvement in monocular depth estimation and opens up new possibilities for depth-aware applications.
Keywords: Depth estimation, Monocular vision, Adaptive bins, Transformers, Deep learning, Scene understanding.
