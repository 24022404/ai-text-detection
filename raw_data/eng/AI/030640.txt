This paper introduces a novel geometric approach, termed A-expansion, designed to efficiently handle multiple "hedgehog" shapes in computational geometry. The objective is to develop a robust method for managing complex shapes with multiple protrusions, akin to hedgehogs, which pose significant challenges in traditional geometric processing pipelines. Our approach leverages a combination of mesh refinement and adaptive sampling to effectively capture the intricate details of these shapes. The A-expansion method is demonstrated to outperform existing techniques in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly in scenarios involving large numbers of hedgehog shapes. Key findings include significant reductions in processing time and improved robustness to noise and degenerate cases. The implications of this research are far-reaching, with potential applications in computer-aided design, robotics, and 3D reconstruction. By providing a fast and reliable means of handling complex shapes, A-expansion contributes to the advancement of geometric computing, enabling more sophisticated and realistic modeling in various fields. Relevant keywords: geometric processing, mesh refinement, adaptive sampling, hedgehog shapes, computational geometry.
