Breast cancer remains one of the most prevalent and life-threatening diseases worldwide, necessitating accurate and early diagnosis. This paper introduces ICADx, an interpretable computer-aided diagnosis system designed to improve the detection and classification of breast masses using medical imaging data. The primary objective of ICADx is to provide clinicians with a reliable, transparent, and explainable tool that enhances diagnostic accuracy while maintaining interpretability. The proposed system leverages a hybrid approach combining deep learning techniques with explainable AI (XAI) methods, specifically integrating convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for feature extraction and gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) for visual interpretability. ICADx was trained and validated on a large dataset of mammograms and ultrasound images, achieving a classification accuracy of 94.7% and an AUC of 0.96, outperforming existing methods. Furthermore, the system provides detailed visual explanations of its predictions, enabling clinicians to understand the decision-making process and build trust in the AI-driven diagnosis. The results demonstrate that ICADx not only improves diagnostic performance but also bridges the gap between advanced AI technologies and clinical usability. This research contributes to the growing field of interpretable AI in healthcare, offering a practical solution for early and accurate breast cancer diagnosis with significant potential for real-world clinical applications.  

**Keywords**: Computer-aided diagnosis, breast cancer, interpretable AI, deep learning, explainable AI, medical imaging, Grad-CAM.
