This paper introduces DAGMapper, a novel approach for creating accurate and topology-consistent lane maps from onboard sensor data. Current methods often struggle with topological errors and require extensive post-processing. DAGMapper addresses these challenges by formulating lane map creation as a directed acyclic graph (DAG) learning problem. Our method leverages a graph neural network (GNN) to predict lane connectivity and geometry directly from sensor data, effectively capturing complex lane relationships and ensuring structural validity. Specifically, we introduce a new loss function that encourages the discovery of meaningful lane topology. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that DAGMapper significantly improves lane map accuracy and topological correctness compared to state-of-the-art techniques. The learned lane maps exhibit fewer topological errors and require less manual correction. This approach has the potential to improve the reliability and efficiency of autonomous vehicle navigation systems. Key words: lane map, autonomous driving, directed acyclic graph, graph neural network, topological mapping.
