This paper introduces Probability Functional Descent (PFD), a novel framework that unifies Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), variational inference (VI), and reinforcement learning (RL) under a common optimization principle. PFD minimizes a functional representing the distance between probability distributions by iteratively updating parameters along the steepest descent direction in function space. This perspective reveals shared theoretical foundations and allows for the derivation of new algorithms by leveraging connections between these traditionally disparate fields. We demonstrate that existing GAN, VI, and RL algorithms can be interpreted as specific instances of PFD, differing primarily in their choice of functional and descent direction approximation. Furthermore, we present a novel algorithm derived from the PFD framework for improved variational approximation. Empirical results on benchmark datasets showcase the potential of PFD-inspired algorithms. This unifying framework offers new insights into the optimization landscape of probabilistic models and opens avenues for cross-pollination of techniques across GANs, VI, and RL.

Keywords: Probability Functional Descent, Generative Adversarial Networks, Variational Inference, Reinforcement Learning, Optimization, Unification, Probabilistic Models.
