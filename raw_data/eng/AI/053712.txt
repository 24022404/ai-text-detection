This paper presents a novel curriculum domain adaptation approach for the semantic segmentation of urban scenes, addressing the challenge of adapting models to new, unseen environments. Our objective is to develop a framework that enables accurate and efficient segmentation of urban scenes, despite differences in data distributions between the source and target domains. We propose a curriculum-based approach, where the model is trained on a series of increasingly difficult tasks, leveraging a combination of adversarial learning and self-supervised techniques to adapt to the target domain. Our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets, outperforming existing domain adaptation methods. Key findings include improved accuracy and reduced domain shift, demonstrating the effectiveness of our curriculum-based approach. The proposed method has significant implications for real-world applications, such as autonomous driving and urban planning, where accurate semantic segmentation is crucial. Our research contributes to the advancement of domain adaptation techniques, particularly in the context of semantic segmentation, and highlights the potential of curriculum learning for improving model adaptability and robustness in complex, dynamic environments, with relevant keywords including domain adaptation, semantic segmentation, urban scenes, curriculum learning, and deep learning.
