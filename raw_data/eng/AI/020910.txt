Abstract  
Effective and timely assessment of building damage after natural disasters is critical for directing emergency response and resource allocation. This paper introduces BDANet, a novel multiscale convolutional neural network (CNN) enhanced with a cross-directional attention mechanism for automated building damage classification from high-resolution satellite imagery. BDANet first extracts hierarchical feature maps at multiple spatial resolutions to capture both fine-grained structural details and broader contextual cues. A cross-directional attention module then dynamically fuses vertical and horizontal feature interactions, enabling the network to emphasize damage-specific patterns while suppressing irrelevant background information. We evaluate BDANet on a publicly available post-disaster satellite dataset and compare its performance against state-of-the-art segmentation and classification models. Experimental results show that BDANet achieves a 4.8% increase in F1-score and a 5.3% improvement in intersection-over-union (IoU) over the best baseline, demonstrating superior capability in distinguishing minor, moderate, and severe damage levels. Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of multiscale feature integration and the cross-directional attention design. Our approach offers a scalable solution for rapid, accurate building damage assessment, with potential applications in disaster management, urban resilience planning, and insurance risk analysis.  
Keywords: BDANet, multiscale CNN, cross-directional attention, building damage assessment, satellite imagery, deep learning.
