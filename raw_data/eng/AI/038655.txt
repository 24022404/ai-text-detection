Abstract:

This paper presents a novel approach for seismic data reconstruction by leveraging deep-seismic priors within convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The primary objective is to address the challenges associated with incomplete or noisy seismic data, which impede accurate subsurface imaging and subsequent geological interpretations. Our method integrates domain-specific prior knowledge into the architecture and training process of CNNs, effectively guiding the network to reconstruct missing or degraded seismic signals with enhanced fidelity. We develop a tailored CNN model that incorporates deep-seismic priors by utilizing representative seismic attributes and data-driven learning. Extensive experiments are conducted on synthetic and field seismic datasets to evaluate the performance of the proposed method. Results demonstrate significant improvements in signal-to-noise ratio and structural coherency of the reconstructed data compared to conventional interpolation and denoising techniques, as well as standard deep learning approaches lacking prior integration. The study highlights the effectiveness of using deep priors in overcoming limitations of purely data-driven models, thus offering a robust solution for seismic data enhancement. The proposed framework has strong implications for seismic imaging, exploration, and monitoring, potentially improving the quality and reliability of subsurface analysis. Key keywords include seismic data reconstruction, deep-seismic priors, convolutional neural networks, signal restoration, and geophysical data processing.
