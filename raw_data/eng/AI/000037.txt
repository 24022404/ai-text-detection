This paper addresses the challenge of accurately forecasting human trajectories and head poses in dynamic environments, a critical task for applications such as autonomous navigation, surveillance, and human-robot interaction. We propose a novel approach that jointly reasons on *tracklets* (short trajectory segments) and *vislets* (visual appearance features) to enhance prediction accuracy. Our method integrates spatiotemporal information from tracklets with contextual visual cues from vislets, leveraging a multi-modal deep learning framework. The model employs a transformer-based architecture to capture long-range dependencies and temporal dynamics, enabling robust predictions even in crowded and complex scenarios. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods, with a 12% reduction in trajectory prediction error and a 15% improvement in head pose estimation accuracy. The proposed approach not only enhances forecasting precision but also provides interpretable insights into the relationship between motion patterns and visual context. This work contributes to advancing human behavior understanding in real-world environments, with potential applications in robotics, augmented reality, and intelligent transportation systems.  

**Keywords**: trajectory forecasting, head pose estimation, tracklets, vislets, transformer models, multi-modal learning, human behavior analysis.
