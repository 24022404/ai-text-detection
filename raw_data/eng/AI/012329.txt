Software defect prediction is a critical task in software engineering, aiming to identify potential defects early in the development lifecycle to reduce costs and improve software quality. This paper proposes a novel multiple ensemble learning framework that leverages diverse datasets to enhance prediction accuracy and robustness. The objective is to address the limitations of single-model approaches and dataset-specific biases by integrating multiple base models trained on different datasets. Our approach combines state-of-the-art machine learning algorithms, including decision trees, support vector machines, and neural networks, into a weighted ensemble framework. The models are trained on publicly available defect prediction datasets, and their outputs are aggregated using an adaptive weighting mechanism to optimize performance. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework outperforms traditional single-model and homogeneous ensemble methods, achieving higher precision, recall, and F1-score across multiple datasets. The study highlights the importance of dataset diversity in improving generalization and provides insights into the effectiveness of ensemble learning for defect prediction. This research contributes to the field by introducing a scalable and adaptable framework that can be applied to various software projects, offering practical implications for developers and quality assurance teams.  

**Keywords**: software defect prediction, ensemble learning, machine learning, dataset diversity, adaptive weighting, software quality.
