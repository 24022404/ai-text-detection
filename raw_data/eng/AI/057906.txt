Monocular depth estimation is a critical task in computer vision, enabling 3D scene understanding from a single image. However, existing methods often struggle with generalization across diverse environments and suffer from inaccuracies in complex scenes. This paper proposes a novel approach to monocular depth estimation by integrating adversarial training into the learning framework. The objective is to enhance the robustness and accuracy of depth prediction models, particularly in challenging real-world scenarios. Our method leverages a generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture, where the generator predicts depth maps and the discriminator evaluates their realism against ground truth data. This adversarial process encourages the model to produce more precise and contextually aware depth estimations. We evaluate our approach on benchmark datasets, including KITTI and NYU Depth V2, and demonstrate significant improvements in depth prediction accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods. The results highlight the effectiveness of adversarial training in addressing common limitations such as texture copying and scale ambiguity. Furthermore, our model exhibits superior generalization capabilities across unseen environments, making it a promising solution for applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and augmented reality. This work contributes to advancing monocular depth estimation by introducing a robust adversarial training framework, offering new insights into improving depth perception from single images.  

**Keywords**: Monocular depth estimation, adversarial training, GAN, computer vision, 3D reconstruction, depth prediction.
