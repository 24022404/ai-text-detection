Abstract:

Understanding and identifying important objects from a first-person perspective is a critical challenge in computer vision, with significant implications for robotics, augmented reality, and human-computer interaction. This paper presents an unsupervised learning framework for automatically discovering and localizing salient objects within first-person (egocentric) videos, without the need for manually labeled data. Our approach leverages motion cues, spatiotemporal patterns, and attention modeling to infer object importance based on user interactions and visual context. We utilize a combination of deep feature extraction and clustering algorithms to generate candidate object regions, followed by an importance ranking mechanism derived from observer gaze alignment and hand-object interactions. Experimental evaluation on multiple publicly available egocentric video datasets demonstrates that our method effectively identifies task-relevant objects and outperforms baseline unsupervised approaches in both accuracy and object localization. The results indicate robust generalization across different activities and environments. Our findings suggest that unsupervised methods can significantly advance object understanding in first-person scenarios, eliminating the need for costly annotation while enabling adaptive, real-world applications such as personal assistant AI, context-aware navigation, and assistive technologies. Keywords: unsupervised learning, egocentric video, important objects, saliency detection, deep learning, object localization.
