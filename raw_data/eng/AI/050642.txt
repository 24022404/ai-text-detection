This paper introduces a novel diagnostic evaluation benchmark for video inpainting, dubbed DEVIL, designed to assess the performance and limitations of existing video inpainting models. The objective is to provide a comprehensive framework for evaluating the quality and coherence of inpainted videos, addressing the lack of standardized benchmarks in this field. Our approach involves creating a diverse dataset with various video sequences, each with carefully designed missing regions, and establishing a set of quantitative metrics to measure inpainting quality. The results show that DEVIL effectively reveals the strengths and weaknesses of state-of-the-art video inpainting models, highlighting areas for improvement. Key findings indicate that current models struggle with preserving temporal consistency and handling complex scenes. The DEVIL benchmark contributes to the advancement of video inpainting research by providing a standardized evaluation protocol, facilitating the development of more sophisticated models. This work has significant implications for applications such as video editing, restoration, and surveillance, where high-quality video inpainting is crucial. Relevant keywords: video inpainting, diagnostic evaluation, benchmark, computer vision, deep learning.
