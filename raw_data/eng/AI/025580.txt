This paper explores the conditions under which Vision Transformers (ViTs) can surpass the performance of Residual Networks (ResNets) without relying on pretraining or strong data augmentations. Our objective is to investigate the potential of ViTs in achieving state-of-the-art results on image classification tasks with minimal dependencies on external data or complex augmentation strategies. We employ a straightforward training approach, leveraging the inherent strengths of ViTs in capturing long-range dependencies and spatial relationships within images. Our results show that ViTs can outperform ResNets on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior generalization capabilities and robustness to overfitting. Notably, our ViT models achieve this performance without the need for pretraining on large-scale datasets or employing extensive data augmentation techniques, highlighting their potential for efficient and effective image classification. The findings of this study contribute to the ongoing discussion on the role of transformer architectures in computer vision, emphasizing the importance of careful model design and training strategies. Key contributions include the identification of critical factors influencing ViT performance and the demonstration of their competitiveness with traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) like ResNets, with implications for applications in image recognition, object detection, and related areas, leveraging keywords such as Vision Transformers, ResNets, image classification, and transformer architectures.
