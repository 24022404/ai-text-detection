This paper introduces a novel approach to parameter estimation for Influence Model-based Reinforcement Optimization (IM-RO) by integrating Bayesian inference with machine learning techniques. The objective is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of influence model parameter estimation, a critical step in optimizing interventions within complex social networks. We propose a hybrid framework that leverages Bayesian methods for quantifying uncertainty in parameter estimates and machine learning models for capturing intricate relationships within the observed data. Specifically, we employ variational inference to approximate the posterior distribution of influence probabilities, and integrate a neural network to learn complex interaction patterns. We evaluate our method on synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in parameter estimation accuracy and intervention policy optimization compared to traditional maximum likelihood estimation and other existing techniques. The results show that our approach leads to more effective intervention strategies. This work contributes a robust and scalable framework for influence maximization, facilitating better-informed decision-making in various domains, including public health, marketing, and social policy.

Keywords: Influence Model, Reinforcement Optimization, Bayesian Inference, Machine Learning, Parameter Estimation, Social Networks, Intervention Strategy.
