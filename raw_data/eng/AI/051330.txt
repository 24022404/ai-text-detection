This paper investigates the problem of achieving global optima in adversarial representation learning when leveraging kernel methods. The objective is to analyze and improve the theoretical understanding and practical effectiveness of kernelized adversarial frameworks for extracting robust and meaningful representations from data. We propose a novel approach that integrates kernel functions into adversarial training architectures, enabling the model to capture complex data structures and nonlinear relationships more effectively than traditional linear methods. Our method formulates the minimax optimization involved in adversarial learning within a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS), allowing for rigorous analysis of the optimization landscape. Through a combination of analytical derivations and empirical experiments on benchmark datasets, we demonstrate that the proposed kernelized adversarial method not only achieves superior representation quality but also exhibits improved convergence properties and enhanced capability to escape poor local minima compared to baseline adversarial and kernel-free techniques. Our findings provide new insights into the global optimality conditions for adversarial learning in kernelized spaces and open avenues for applying these principles to a wide range of machine learning tasks, such as classification, regression, and generative modeling. Key contributions include the theoretical characterization of global optima, the development of efficient algorithms for kernelized adversarial minimax problems, and extensive experimental validation. Keywords: kernel methods, adversarial representation learning, global optima, RKHS, optimization, machine learning.
