Adversarial attacks pose significant challenges to the robustness and reliability of machine learning models, particularly in image classification tasks. This research addresses the vulnerability of deep neural networks to such attacks by proposing a novel method for enhancing adversarial robustness through channel-wise activation suppressing.

Methods: The approach involves a systematic adjustment of neural network activations by selectively suppressing channel-wise outputs during both training and inference phases. This technique enables the model to prioritize robust features and diminish the impact of perturbations caused by adversarial inputs. The proposed method was integrated into standard convolutional neural network architectures to evaluate its effectiveness.

Results: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the channel-wise activation suppressing method significantly improves the adversarial robustness of various models. Performance against state-of-the-art adversarial attack methods such as Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) and Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) showed a notable increase in accuracy, minimizing error rates while maintaining or even enhancing standard task performance. Comparative analysis with existing adversarial defense mechanisms highlights the superior robustness achieved through the proposed approach.

Conclusion: The research provides a novel contribution to the field of adversarial defense in deep learning, introducing a scalable and efficient technique that enhances model robustness without sacrificing standard performance metrics. The implications of this work suggest potential applications in areas requiring high computational security, such as autonomous systems and cyber-physical infrastructure. Future work could explore integration with other defense strategies to further augment model resilience.

Keywords: adversarial robustness, channel-wise activation, deep neural networks, image classification, adversarial defense, perturbation resilience.
