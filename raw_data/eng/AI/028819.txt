**Abstract**

Neural Architecture Search (NAS) has emerged as a powerful approach for automating the design of deep learning models, yet its effectiveness is often limited by high computational costs and inefficiencies in evaluating a vast number of candidate architectures. In this paper, we present BRP-NAS, a novel prediction-based NAS framework that leverages Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to efficiently predict the performance of candidate neural architectures. Our approach models neural architectures as graphs, enabling the extraction of structural features and the learning of rich representations through GCNs. By integrating a bridge regression predictor, BRP-NAS rapidly estimates the accuracy of each candidate network without the need for exhaustive training, significantly reducing the overall search time. Extensive experiments on standard benchmark datasets demonstrate that BRP-NAS outperforms traditional NAS methods in terms of search efficiency and consistently discovers architectures with competitive or superior performance. Furthermore, ablation studies validate the effectiveness of GCN-based performance prediction within the NAS process. Our findings highlight the potential of graph-based predictive modeling in advancing the scalability and practicality of NAS for diverse machine learning tasks. Key keywords: Neural Architecture Search, Graph Convolutional Networks, performance prediction, automated model design, deep learning.
