This research addresses the challenge of drift in discriminative visual tracking caused by contaminated training datasets. The study aims to develop a unified approach to adaptively decontaminate the training set, thereby enhancing tracking accuracy and robustness.

Methods/Approach: We introduce an innovative algorithm that leverages adaptive mechanisms to identify and cleanse contaminated data within the training set utilized by visual tracking models. The proposed method is integrated into a discriminative tracking framework, employing machine learning techniques to dynamically assess and rectify data inconsistencies. This algorithm enhances the model's ability to discriminate between the target and non-target objects over time.

Results/Findings: The adaptive decontamination algorithm was evaluated on multiple benchmark datasets against state-of-the-art visual tracking methods. The results demonstrate a significant improvement in tracking performance, showcasing enhanced accuracy in identifying and maintaining the target's trajectory. Our approach consistently reduced the error rates and outperformed conventional models in maintaining tracking robustness under challenging conditions.

Conclusion/Implications: Our unified formulation for adaptive decontamination represents a substantial contribution to the field of visual tracking. By proactively managing training set contamination, this method enhances the reliability and precision of tracking systems. The research paves the way for improved applications in surveillance, autonomous systems, and augmented reality, highlighting the potential for adaptive data management in broader machine learning contexts.

Keywords: visual tracking, adaptive decontamination, discriminative models, training set, machine learning, target trajectory, algorithm.
