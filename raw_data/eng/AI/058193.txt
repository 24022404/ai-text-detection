The objective of this research is to address the challenges posed by uncertain feedback in online learning environments represented as graphs. Traditional models in online learning assume precise and reliable feedback, which is often not the case in real-world applications where feedback may vary in accuracy or relevance. This paper introduces a novel algorithmic framework for handling online learning with feedback graphs that are uncertain or subject to noise. Our approach leverages the power of probabilistic graph models, incorporating mechanisms to assess and adaptively respond to the uncertainty inherent in feedback. Through both theoretical analysis and empirical evaluations, we demonstrate that our model outperforms existing baseline methods under various levels of feedback uncertainty, achieving more robust learning outcomes and improved decision-making. The findings underscore significant advancements in online learning strategies, particularly in applications where feedback reliability cannot be guaranteed. This research contributes to the fields of machine learning and graph theory by providing a robust methodology for uncertain feedback environments, with implications for enhancing automated decision systems in complex, noisy settings. Key keywords include online learning, uncertainty, feedback graphs, probabilistic models, and adaptive algorithms.
