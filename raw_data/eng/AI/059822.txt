Hyperspectral image (HSI) classification accuracy is significantly affected by insufficient training samples and imbalanced class distributions. To address these challenges, we propose a novel Spectral-Spatial-Dependent Global Learning (SSDGL) framework. This framework leverages spectral and spatial information to generate pseudo-labels for unlabeled samples, thereby alleviating the problem of insufficient training data. Specifically, a spectral-dependent module adaptively selects relevant spectral bands, enhancing the discriminative power of spectral features. Simultaneously, a spatial-dependent module refines pseudo-labels by considering the spatial context, reducing the impact of noisy labels. Furthermore, a global learning strategy based on a contrastive loss function is employed to mitigate the effect of class imbalance by maximizing inter-class separation. Experiments on several benchmark HSI datasets demonstrate that the proposed SSDGL framework achieves superior classification performance compared to state-of-the-art methods, particularly when dealing with limited and imbalanced training data. The results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed framework in improving HSI classification accuracy and robustness. Keywords: Hyperspectral Image Classification, Imbalanced Learning, Semi-Supervised Learning, Spectral-Spatial Feature Fusion, Contrastive Learning.
