This paper addresses the challenge of recovering accurate spectral information from color image sensor measurements, a process known as numerical demultiplexing. Traditional color sensors, designed for standard RGB imaging, often lose critical spectral details due to their limited channel configuration, impeding high-fidelity applications in computer vision, medical imaging, and remote sensing. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel approach based on non-linear random forest modeling. Our method utilizes the capacity of random forest regression to learn complex, non-linear mappings between raw sensor responses and their corresponding high-dimensional spectral representations. We conduct comprehensive experiments on both simulated and real-world datasets, comparing the performance of our approach with conventional linear and alternative non-linear models such as neural networks and support vector regressors. The results demonstrate that our random forest-based demultiplexing method significantly improves spectral reconstruction accuracy, achieving lower mean squared error and higher correlation coefficients across test cases. Additionally, our approach offers practical advantages in terms of robustness to sensor noise and interpretability of feature importance. The findings highlight the potential of advanced machine learning models, particularly ensemble methods, for enhancing the capability of existing imaging hardware for spectral applications. Our work provides new insights and practical solutions for applications requiring precise color and spectral information, and paves the way for future development in cost-effective hyperspectral imaging. Key keywords: spectral reconstruction, image sensors, random forest, numerical demultiplexing, machine learning, non-linear modeling.
