Breast cancer screening is critical for early detection and improved patient outcomes, yet current evaluation metrics for automated diagnostic systems often overlook the importance of prediction confidence, potentially impacting clinical decision-making. This paper introduces an efficient confidence measure-based evaluation metric specifically designed for breast cancer screening applications utilizing Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs). The proposed metric incorporates uncertainty estimation to more accurately assess the reliability of model predictions, offering an advancement over traditional accuracy-focused metrics. We implemented and validated our approach using a publicly available mammography dataset, applying BNN architectures to generate both diagnostic predictions and associated confidence scores. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed evaluation metric provides a more nuanced understanding of model performance, particularly in distinguishing high-confidence true positives and mitigating the clinical risk associated with uncertain predictions. Comparative analysis with standard metrics, such as accuracy and area under the ROC curve, confirms the added value of integrating confidence measures. The findings indicate that the adoption of confidence-aware evaluation can enhance the trustworthiness and interpretability of AI-assisted breast cancer screening tools. This work supports the development of safer, more robust diagnostic systems, with broad implications for clinical integration and patient care. Key keywords: breast cancer screening, Bayesian neural networks, confidence measure, uncertainty estimation, evaluation metric, medical imaging, AI in healthcare.
