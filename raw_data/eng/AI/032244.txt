This paper presents a novel approach to automatically designing Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architectures for medical image segmentation tasks. The objective is to develop an efficient and effective method for segmenting medical images, such as tumors, organs, and tissues, without requiring extensive manual architecture design. Our approach utilizes a reinforcement learning-based framework to search for optimal CNN architectures, leveraging a combination of convolutional and pooling layers to achieve state-of-the-art performance. The results demonstrate that our automatically designed architectures outperform manually crafted ones on several benchmark datasets, including BraTS and ISBI. Key findings include improved segmentation accuracy, reduced computational requirements, and enhanced robustness to variations in image quality. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to automate the design process, reducing the need for expert knowledge and enabling widespread adoption of deep learning-based medical image segmentation. This work has significant implications for clinical applications, including disease diagnosis, treatment planning, and patient monitoring, and paves the way for further innovations in medical imaging analysis using AI and deep learning techniques, particularly CNNs, reinforcement learning, and medical image segmentation.
