**Abstract:**

Forecasting the behavior of high-dimensional dynamical systems is a fundamental challenge in fields ranging from climate science to finance and engineering. Traditional approaches often rely on optimal embeddings of system states, which can be computationally expensive and impractical for systems with complex dynamics and partial observations. This paper proposes a novel framework for forecasting high-dimensional time series by exploiting suboptimal embeddings, which require less stringent reconstruction conditions and reduce computational overhead. We introduce an embedding selection algorithm that balances information preservation with embedding dimensionality, enabling the effective representation of system dynamics even when full observability is unattainable. Using both synthetic and real-world datasets, we evaluate the proposed approach against state-of-the-art methods in terms of prediction accuracy and robustness to observational noise. Our results demonstrate that leveraging suboptimal embeddings not only maintains competitive forecasting performance but also enhances scalability and adaptability in high-dimensional settings. This research offers new insights into the efficient modeling of complex dynamical systems and provides practical guidelines for embedding selection in forecasting applications. Keywords: high-dimensional dynamics, time series forecasting, embedding theory, computational efficiency, complex systems, machine learning.
