This paper provides a comprehensive tutorial and survey on Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (SNE) with Gaussian and Student-t distributions, aiming to elucidate the theoretical foundations and practical applications of this dimensionality reduction technique. We describe the SNE approach, which utilizes probabilistic models to preserve the local structure of high-dimensional data in a lower-dimensional representation. Our methodology explores the role of Gaussian and Student-t distributions in SNE, highlighting their impact on the embedding quality and computational efficiency. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of SNE with these distributions in visualizing complex datasets, outperforming traditional methods in preserving cluster structures and revealing subtle patterns. This research contributes to the understanding of SNE's capabilities and limitations, offering insights into the selection of appropriate distributions for specific problem domains. The paper's findings have significant implications for data visualization, clustering, and anomaly detection, with potential applications in machine learning, computer vision, and data mining. Key keywords: Stochastic Neighbor Embedding, Gaussian distribution, Student-t distribution, dimensionality reduction, data visualization, machine learning.
