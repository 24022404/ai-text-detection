This paper presents a novel approach to unsupervised video depth estimation by leveraging ego-motion analysis and disparity consensus techniques. The primary objective is to accurately estimate the depth of scenes from monocular video sequences without relying on ground truth annotations or stereo image pairs. Our method incorporates an end-to-end neural network architecture that jointly optimizes for visual odometry (ego-motion) and disparity consistency across consecutive video frames. By enforcing geometric constraints between estimated camera motion and inter-frame disparities, our model achieves robust depth prediction and reduces the impact of occlusions and dynamic scene elements. Experimental results on established benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing unsupervised depth estimation approaches in both quantitative accuracy and qualitative scene understanding. The framework is scalable, applicable to various real-world scenarios such as autonomous navigation and augmented reality, and does not require domain-specific calibration or pre-training. Overall, our research contributes an efficient and generalizable solution to unsupervised depth estimation by integrating motion cues and multi-frame disparity consensus. Key keywords: unsupervised learning, video depth estimation, ego-motion, disparity consensus, monocular video, neural networks, visual odometry.
