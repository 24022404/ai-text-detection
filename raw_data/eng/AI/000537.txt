Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in generating realistic images. However, questions remain regarding whether GAN-generated images possess inherent, detectable artifacts or “fingerprints” that distinguish them from real images. This paper investigates the existence and characteristics of such artificial fingerprints. We employ a multi-faceted approach, combining frequency spectrum analysis, statistical anomaly detection, and machine learning classifiers trained to differentiate between real and GAN-generated images produced by various GAN architectures, including StyleGAN2, ProGAN, and DCGAN. Our findings reveal subtle but consistent statistical differences in the high-frequency components of GAN-generated images compared to real-world photographs, even after post-processing techniques aimed at removing artifacts. Furthermore, we demonstrate that a carefully trained convolutional neural network can achieve high accuracy in identifying GAN-generated content across different architectures and datasets. These results suggest that GANs do leave detectable fingerprints, posing implications for image forensics and the detection of synthetic media. Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, GAN, image forensics, artificial intelligence, deep learning, fingerprinting, synthetic media, CNN, image analysis.
