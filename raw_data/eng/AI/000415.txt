This paper addresses the challenge of efficient user association in 5G and beyond wireless networks, where network heterogeneity, dynamic topologies, and dense deployments necessitate adaptable and scalable solutions. We propose a novel framework for user association that leverages transferable and distributed policies, enabling mobile devices and network nodes to autonomously select optimal connections while adapting to varying network conditions. Our approach utilizes distributed optimization techniques and machine learning-based policy transfer mechanisms, allowing learned association strategies to be shared and reused across different network contexts and hardware infrastructures. Extensive simulations demonstrate that the proposed policies significantly improve network throughput, reduce latency, and ensure load balancing across multiple access points, outperforming conventional static and centralized association strategies. The results also highlight the framework’s robustness to user mobility and topology variations. This work offers important insights into the design of future-ready user association mechanisms, with implications for self-organizing networks, edge computing, and efficient resource management in ultra-dense wireless environments. Keywords: user association, 5G, distributed algorithms, machine learning, policy transfer, network optimization.
