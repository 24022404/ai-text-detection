Estimating scene lighting from a single image is a challenging problem in computer vision and graphics, crucial for photorealistic rendering and image manipulation. This paper introduces GMLight, a novel approach to lighting estimation based on approximating the environment map with a mixture of geometric distributions. Unlike existing methods that rely on complex spherical harmonic representations or deep learning models requiring extensive training data, GMLight leverages the inherent properties of geometric distributions to efficiently capture the spatial characteristics of light sources. Our method involves a two-stage process: first, we estimate surface normals and albedo using a differentiable rendering framework; second, we optimize geometric distribution parameters to best match the observed shading. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that GMLight achieves competitive accuracy with state-of-the-art techniques, while offering significant advantages in terms of computational efficiency and memory footprint. The geometric distribution approximation allows for compact representation of lighting, facilitating real-time rendering applications. We further show the utility of GMLight in relighting and inverse rendering tasks. Keywords: Lighting Estimation, Inverse Rendering, Geometric Distribution, Image-Based Lighting, Computer Graphics, Computer Vision.
