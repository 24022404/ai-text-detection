Approximate inference in probabilistic models is a fundamental challenge in machine learning, particularly for large-scale and complex systems. This paper introduces *Bucket Renormalization*, a novel approach designed to improve the efficiency and accuracy of approximate inference algorithms. The proposed method leverages a hierarchical renormalization framework to systematically reduce computational complexity while preserving critical probabilistic dependencies. By organizing variables into "buckets" and applying iterative renormalization steps, the approach achieves a balance between scalability and inference quality. We evaluate the method on benchmark datasets and real-world applications, demonstrating significant improvements in inference speed and accuracy compared to existing techniques such as variational inference and Monte Carlo sampling. The results highlight the potential of Bucket Renormalization for enabling scalable probabilistic reasoning in high-dimensional domains, including natural language processing, computer vision, and bioinformatics. This work contributes a robust and generalizable framework for approximate inference, offering new avenues for research and application in probabilistic modeling.  

**Keywords**: approximate inference, probabilistic models, renormalization, scalability, hierarchical methods, machine learning.
