This paper explores the concept of local geometry in hypergraphs, with a focus on the structural properties of hyperedges and their implications for social network analysis. Our objective is to develop a framework for understanding the intricate relationships between hyperedges and their surrounding graph topology. We propose a novel approach based on geometric and topological methods to characterize the local structure of hypergraphs, enabling the identification of key patterns and anomalies in social networks. Our results demonstrate the effectiveness of this framework in uncovering hidden community structures, predicting user behavior, and detecting influential nodes. The applications of this research have significant implications for social network modeling, information diffusion, and community detection, offering a new perspective on the complex interactions within hypergraph-structured data. Key contributions include the introduction of a local geometry metric for hyperedges, a hypergraph-based community detection algorithm, and a comprehensive evaluation of the approach on real-world social network datasets.
