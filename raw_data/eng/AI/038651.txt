Generative models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in synthesizing complex data across various domains, yet they often inherit or amplify biases present in their training datasets, which can compromise the fairness and reliability of downstream applications. This paper addresses the critical challenge of bias correction in learned generative models by proposing a novel likelihood-free importance weighting framework. The core methodology circumvents the need for explicit likelihood evaluation, often intractable in implicit generative models such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and certain Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). Instead, we leverage sample-based density ratio estimation to compute importance weights that adjust the generative model’s outputs towards a debiased target distribution. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach effectively mitigates representational and sampling biases, significantly improving alignment with desired data distributions without sacrificing sample quality or diversity. Comparative analysis further illustrates the advantages of our technique over conventional post-hoc correction and reweighting strategies, highlighting both its flexibility and robustness. The proposed method offers a scalable and model-agnostic solution for bias correction, with potential applications in data augmentation, fairness-aware machine learning, and responsible AI development. Keywords: generative models, bias correction, importance weighting, likelihood-free inference, density ratio estimation, fairness, AI ethics.
