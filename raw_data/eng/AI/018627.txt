Person re-identification is a critical task in computer vision with applications in surveillance, security, and human-computer interaction. This paper introduces a novel approach, Support Neighbor Loss, designed to enhance person re-identification by improving feature discrimination and robustness against intra-class variance.

Methods/Approach: The proposed Support Neighbor Loss utilizes a unique loss function that focuses on leveraging the relationships between support samples and their neighboring instances in the feature space. This method ensures that instances of the same identity are drawn closer together while increasing the separation from other identities, enhancing the model’s discriminative capability. For the evaluation, we implemented our method on a deep neural network architecture commonly used in person re-identification tasks, employing standard datasets to validate its effectiveness.

Results/Findings: Our experiments demonstrate that the Support Neighbor Loss significantly outperforms existing loss functions in terms of accuracy and robustness. Specifically, the approach yields improved Rank-1 accuracy and mean Average Precision (mAP) metrics, achieving competitive results compared to state-of-the-art methods. The results indicate that the proposed method efficiently reduces false match instances, thereby increasing the reliability of person re-identification systems.

Conclusion/Implications: The research presents a significant advancement in the field of person re-identification by addressing intra-class variance and enhancing discriminatory power through a novel loss function. The Support Neighbor Loss not only advances existing methodologies but also sets a new directive for future research in adaptive loss design. The implications of this study suggest substantial improvements in real-world surveillance and security applications, offering a more reliable system for identifying individuals in various challenging conditions.

Keywords: Person Re-Identification, Support Neighbor Loss, Deep Learning, Feature Discrimination, Surveillance Systems.
