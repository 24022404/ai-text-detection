The prediction of multiple brain graphs from a single brain graph is a critical challenge in neuroimaging and computational neuroscience, with significant implications for understanding brain connectivity and function. This paper introduces a novel Topology-Aware Generative Adversarial Network (TAGAN) designed to jointly predict multiple brain graphs from a single input graph while preserving the topological properties of brain networks. Unlike existing methods that often overlook the inherent structural relationships between brain regions, TAGAN incorporates a topology-aware loss function and a graph-based generator to ensure the predicted graphs maintain realistic connectivity patterns. The proposed approach leverages adversarial training to enhance the quality of predictions and employs a multi-task learning framework to simultaneously predict multiple graphs. Experimental results on publicly available neuroimaging datasets demonstrate that TAGAN outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of prediction accuracy and topological consistency. The model achieves significant improvements in graph similarity metrics and robustness across diverse brain networks. This research contributes to advancing brain graph prediction by introducing a topology-aware framework that can be applied to various neuroimaging tasks, such as disease diagnosis and brain network analysis. The proposed method opens new avenues for leveraging generative models in neuroscience and highlights the importance of preserving topological properties in brain graph prediction.  

**Keywords**: Brain graph prediction, Generative Adversarial Networks, Topology-aware learning, Neuroimaging, Multi-task learning, Graph-based models.
