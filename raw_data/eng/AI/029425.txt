Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across diverse applications, yet quantifying and managing uncertainties within these models remains a fundamental challenge. This paper investigates critical issues pertaining to uncertainties in ANNs, focusing on their sources, propagation, and impact on model reliability and decision-making. We analyze epistemic uncertainty, arising from limited training data, and aleatoric uncertainty, inherent in the data generating process itself. We explore the limitations of existing uncertainty estimation techniques, including Bayesian Neural Networks and ensemble methods, in capturing complex dependencies and distributional shifts. Furthermore, we examine the implications of miscalibrated uncertainty estimates on downstream tasks, particularly in safety-critical applications. Our findings highlight the need for improved uncertainty-aware training methodologies and robust evaluation metrics. This research contributes to a deeper understanding of uncertainty in ANNs, paving the way for more reliable and trustworthy AI systems.

Keywords: Artificial Neural Networks, Uncertainty Quantification, Epistemic Uncertainty, Aleatoric Uncertainty, Bayesian Neural Networks, Model Calibration, Reliability.
