This paper addresses the challenge of generalization in neural networks within the context of reinforcement learning (RL), where overgeneralized representations can hinder optimal policy development and adaptability to specific tasks. We investigate the impact of deliberately breaking generalization in neural network models to achieve improved performance in RL environments. Our proposed approach introduces controlled architectural modifications and targeted regularization techniques that restrict the neural network's ability to generalize across unrelated states or actions. Experimental evaluations were conducted on benchmark RL tasks, including both discrete and continuous action spaces, comparing our method with traditional deep RL architectures. Results demonstrate consistent improvements in task-specific performance, faster convergence, and enhanced learning stability. Notably, the intentional limitation of generalization enables the agent to more effectively capture critical, environment-specific features, leading to superior decision-making in complex domains. This work offers new insights into the trade-offs between generalization and specialization in deep reinforcement learning and introduces a practical framework for tailoring neural network behavior to maximize RL outcomes. Key contributions include a novel perspective on network design for RL, rigorous performance analysis, and broad implications for artificial intelligence systems that require precise policy optimization. Keywords: reinforcement learning, neural networks, generalization, policy specialization, deep learning.
