The exponential growth in medical imaging data has underscored the need for efficient analysis methodologies, particularly with limited labeled datasets. This paper addresses the challenge of effectively training deep learning models in the domain of medical image analysis when faced with sparse labeled data by introducing a novel surrogate supervision approach.

Methods/Approach: Our research leverages innovative surrogate supervision techniques, which utilize alternative sources of information or tasks to enhance model training on medical images. By integrating semi-supervised learning strategies and domain adaptation, we construct a deep learning framework capable of extracting meaningful patterns and insights from limited labeled data. The model's architecture and training regime are optimized to exploit available unlabeled data and auxiliary labels derived from unsupervised tasks.

Results/Findings: Extensive experiments conducted on standard medical imaging datasets demonstrate that our surrogate supervision approach significantly improves model performance, achieving higher accuracy and robustness compared to traditional techniques reliant on fully labeled datasets. Our model efficiently narrows the gap between fully supervised learning outcomes and those attainable with minimal labeled data, highlighting reductions in annotation costs without compromising diagnostic accuracy.

Conclusion/Implications: This study presents a breakthrough in medical image analysis by effectively mitigating the limitations posed by scarce labeled data through surrogate supervision. The proposed framework holds transformative potential for real-world applications, reducing dependency on manual annotations while maintaining high performance standards. It paves the way for more accessible and scalable deployment of deep learning models in medical diagnostics, ultimately contributing to enhanced healthcare services and resource optimization.

Keywords: Surrogate Supervision, Deep Learning, Medical Image Analysis, Semi-Supervised Learning, Domain Adaptation, Limited Labeled Data, Medical Diagnostics.
