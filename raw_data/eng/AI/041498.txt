Segmenting transparent objects remains a significant challenge in computer vision due to the unique optical properties and lack of distinctive color or texture cues. This paper presents a novel approach for accurate and efficient segmentation of transparent objects in real-world environments, addressing both indoor and outdoor scenes with diverse backgrounds and lighting conditions. Our method leverages a deep learning architecture that combines multi-modal image features, including edge information, background distortion patterns, and context-aware cues, to distinguish transparent objects from their surroundings. We construct and release a large-scale annotated dataset of transparent objects in the wild to facilitate training and evaluation. Experimental results demonstrate that our model outperforms recent state-of-the-art segmentation methods in terms of precision, recall, and robustness to challenging visual scenarios. The proposed technique generalizes well across various transparent materials, object shapes, and environmental setups. This research advances the field by enabling more reliable segmentation of transparent objects in complex, real-world situations, with potential applications in robotics, augmented reality, and visual object tracking. 

Keywords: transparent object segmentation, computer vision, deep learning, edge detection, real-world dataset, object recognition.
