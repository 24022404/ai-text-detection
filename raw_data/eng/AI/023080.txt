This paper addresses the challenge of hierarchical disentanglement, a crucial problem in representation learning where the goal is to separate complex data into its underlying, independent factors. Our objective is to establish a comprehensive framework for evaluating and improving disentanglement methods. We propose a set of benchmarks and metrics that assess the quality and hierarchy of disentangled representations, allowing for a more accurate comparison of different algorithms. Our approach involves the development of novel algorithms that leverage hierarchical variational autoencoders and graph neural networks to learn disentangled representations at multiple levels of abstraction. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed methods, outperforming existing state-of-the-art disentanglement techniques in terms of factorization quality and hierarchical consistency. The key findings of this research contribute to the advancement of representation learning, with potential applications in areas such as computer vision, natural language processing, and generative modeling. Our work provides a foundation for future research in hierarchical disentanglement, highlighting the importance of benchmarks, algorithms, and metrics in this field. Key keywords: hierarchical disentanglement, representation learning, variational autoencoders, graph neural networks, benchmarks, metrics.
