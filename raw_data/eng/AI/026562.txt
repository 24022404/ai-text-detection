Anomaly detection is a critical task in various domains, including cybersecurity, fraud detection, and industrial monitoring. However, the scarcity of labeled anomaly data often hinders the development of robust detection models. This paper addresses this challenge by proposing a novel framework for learning ensembles of anomaly detectors trained on synthetic data. The objective is to enhance the generalization and robustness of anomaly detection systems in real-world scenarios where labeled anomalies are limited. Our approach leverages generative models to create synthetic anomaly datasets that mimic real-world distributions, enabling the training of diverse anomaly detection models. These models are then combined into an ensemble using a weighted aggregation mechanism, which dynamically adjusts based on the confidence of individual detectors. Experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed ensemble framework outperforms traditional single-model approaches and achieves state-of-the-art performance in detecting anomalies across diverse domains. The results highlight the effectiveness of synthetic data in improving anomaly detection and the robustness of ensemble methods in handling complex, real-world scenarios. This research contributes a scalable and practical solution for anomaly detection, with potential applications in cybersecurity, predictive maintenance, and beyond.  

**Keywords**: anomaly detection, synthetic data, ensemble learning, generative models, cybersecurity, predictive maintenance.
