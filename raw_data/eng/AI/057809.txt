This paper introduces a novel ante-hoc explanation method that induces semantic grouping of latent concepts within neural networks, enhancing the interpretability of model decisions without sacrificing accuracy. Our approach addresses the challenge of understanding complex, high-dimensional latent spaces by organizing individual concepts into meaningful groups based on semantic similarity. We achieve this by incorporating a grouping loss during the training phase, encouraging the network to learn representations that naturally cluster related concepts. We evaluate our method on image classification and natural language processing tasks, demonstrating that it produces more coherent and human-understandable explanations compared to existing post-hoc techniques. Specifically, our approach allows for identifying higher-level semantic features that contribute to a model's prediction, such as "animal features" or "syntactic structure". Experimental results show that our method maintains competitive prediction performance while significantly improving explanation quality, as measured by human evaluation and quantitative metrics. The induced semantic groupings provide valuable insights into the inner workings of neural networks and offer a pathway towards building more transparent and trustworthy AI systems. Keywords: Explainable AI, Ante-hoc Explanation, Interpretability, Latent Space, Semantic Grouping, Neural Networks.
