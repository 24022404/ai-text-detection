This paper addresses the challenge of vehicle tracking and re-identification (Re-ID) across multi-camera networks, a critical task in intelligent transportation systems to enhance surveillance and traffic monitoring. The research focuses on developing a method that is not only robust but also extensible and swift, aimed at efficiently managing diverse and large-scale urban environments.

Methods/Approach: We introduce a novel approach utilizing teamed classifiers for vehicle tracking and Re-ID. The system combines the strength of multiple classifiers, each specializing in different attributes of the vehicle, thus improving the accuracy and speed of vehicle re-identification across different camera views. Advanced deep learning techniques are employed to recognize and differentiate vehicle features, leveraging algorithms optimized for parallel processing to ensure real-time performance.

Results/Findings: Our proposed method demonstrates significant improvements in both tracking accuracy and Re-ID precision when evaluated on benchmark datasets in simulated multi-camera setups. Compared to state-of-the-art methods, our approach shows a marked increase in processing speed, with a notable decrease in computational overhead. It can handle complex urban environments with high vehicle densities, maintaining robust performance under varying lighting and weather conditions.

Conclusion/Implications: This research presents a substantial advancement in multi-camera vehicle tracking and Re-ID by providing an approach that is adaptable to new environments and scalable for future applications. The unified classifier architecture not only streamlines the processing pipeline but also enhances the system's capacity to adapt to emerging vehicle models and network configurations. Potential applications span from urban traffic management to automated incident detection, benefiting smart city initiatives globally.

Keywords: Vehicle Tracking, Vehicle Re-ID, Multi-Camera Networks, Teamed Classifiers, Deep Learning, Intelligent Transportation Systems.
