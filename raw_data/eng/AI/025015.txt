This paper presents a novel approach to learning generative models of tissue organization using supervised Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). The objective is to develop a framework that can effectively capture the complex spatial relationships and patterns inherent in tissue structures. Our method employs a supervised GAN architecture, where the generator is conditioned on histological images to produce realistic tissue organization models. The discriminator is trained to distinguish between real and generated tissue samples, ensuring the generated models are highly realistic. Our results demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms existing methods in generating tissue organization models, with significant improvements in accuracy and visual fidelity. The key findings of this study highlight the potential of supervised GANs in modeling complex biological systems, with implications for applications in biomedical research, tissue engineering, and disease modeling. The novelty of this research lies in the integration of supervised learning with GANs, enabling the generation of highly realistic tissue organization models that can be used to advance our understanding of tissue development and disease progression. Key keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, tissue organization, supervised learning, biomedical research, tissue engineering.
