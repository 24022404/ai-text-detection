Objective: Ensuring reliable and safe behavior in reinforcement learning (RL) agents often requires satisfying explicit constraints on performance or risk, a challenge not addressed by standard unconstrained policy search. This paper introduces Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO), a novel algorithm that directly incorporates safety or resource constraints into policy updates while maintaining high sample efficiency and stable learning.

Methods: Building on the trust-region framework, CPO formulates RL as a constrained optimization problem where the expected return is maximized subject to cost or risk limits. We derive a practical update rule by approximating the policy improvement step with a quadratic surrogate for the objective and linear surrogates for the constraints. A dual-variable method enforces constraints during each policy iteration, and an efficient second-order optimizer ensures that updates remain within a safe trust region.

Results: We evaluate CPO on a suite of continuous-control benchmarks with safety or resource budgets—including obstacle avoidance tasks, torque-limited locomotion, and simulated robotic manipulation under force constraints. Compared to state-of-the-art unconstrained algorithms (e.g., PPO, TRPO) and heuristic penalty methods, CPO consistently satisfies specified constraints while achieving comparable or superior cumulative reward. Empirical analysis demonstrates that CPO enforces constraint satisfaction reliably even under high-dimensional observations and stochastic dynamics.

Conclusion: CPO offers a principled and extensible approach for training RL agents with hard constraints, providing theoretical guarantees on constraint satisfaction and strong empirical performance. This work paves the way for safer deployment of RL in real-world applications such as autonomous driving, robotic control, and resource-constrained decision making.

Keywords: reinforcement learning, constrained optimization, trust region, policy search, safety constraints, continuous control.
