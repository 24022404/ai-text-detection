**Abstract:**

In many real-world machine learning applications, models must make sequential predictions under strict computational or time budgets, necessitating a careful balance between accuracy and resource consumption. This paper introduces a novel Confidence-Budget Matching (CBM) framework for sequential budgeted learning, which dynamically allocates computational resources based on model confidence in a given prediction task. The proposed method leverages an adaptive mechanism to estimate prediction confidence at each decision step, aligning resource expenditures with the uncertainty of the input, thereby optimizing overall system performance under budget constraints. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art budgeted learning algorithms in terms of both accuracy and efficiency, especially in scenarios with tight resource limitations. The CBM strategy further shows robust generalization across various architectures and sequential prediction tasks. Our findings suggest that incorporating confidence-driven mechanisms into budgeted learning holds significant promise for scalable and resource-aware deployment in practical settings such as edge computing, real-time analytics, and autonomous systems.

**Keywords:** budgeted learning, sequential prediction, confidence estimation, resource allocation, adaptive inference, efficiency, edge computing
