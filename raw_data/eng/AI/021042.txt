This paper addresses the challenge of improving generalization in reinforcement learning (RL) by introducing an instance-based approach. The research explores how leveraging specific instances within an environment can enhance the adaptability and performance of RL algorithms across diverse tasks.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel instance-based generalization model that utilizes a combination of instance memory and similarity assessment techniques. The approach involves storing key instances encountered during the learning phase and employing a similarity function to guide decision-making in new, unseen situations. This method is integrated into existing RL frameworks, allowing for seamless adoption and evaluation.

Results/Findings: Experiments conducted on standard benchmark environments demonstrate the efficacy of our approach, with significant improvements in terms of generalization and policy performance compared to traditional RL methods. Our results highlight superior adaptability to novel scenarios, reducing the sample complexity and increasing the learning speed. The instance-based method consistently outperformed baseline models, affirming its capability to generalize from past experiences.

Conclusion/Implications: The research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by providing a scalable and effective solution to the generalization problem. The instance-based generalization framework not only enhances RL performance but also broadens the applicability of these algorithms to more complex real-world tasks. The insights gained open new avenues for developing RL systems capable of learning with less data and adapting to dynamic environments. Keywords associated with this research include reinforcement learning, instance memory, generalization, adaptability, and policy performance.
