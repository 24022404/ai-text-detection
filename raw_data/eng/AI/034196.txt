This paper introduces L^NAS, a novel approach to neural architecture search (NAS) that leverages continuous-action reinforcement learning to optimize neural architectures. The objective is to develop an efficient and effective method for automating the design of neural networks. L^NAS utilizes a reinforcement learning framework, where an agent learns to modify neural architectures through continuous actions, allowing for a more fine-grained and flexible search space. The approach is evaluated on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating improved performance and efficiency compared to existing NAS methods. Key findings include the ability of L^NAS to discover novel architectures that outperform state-of-the-art models, while reducing the computational cost associated with traditional NAS methods. The contributions of this research lie in its ability to bridge the gap between reinforcement learning and NAS, enabling the development of more efficient and scalable architecture search algorithms. This work has significant implications for the field of deep learning, enabling the automated design of neural networks for a wide range of applications, including computer vision, natural language processing, and robotics, with relevant keywords including neural architecture search, reinforcement learning, continuous-action, and deep learning.
