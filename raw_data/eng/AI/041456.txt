Accurate segmentation of medical images is crucial for diagnosis and treatment planning. Deep learning models have shown promise in automating this task, but their performance is often affected by inter-rater variability in manual annotations. This study investigates the impact of individual rater styles on deep learning uncertainty in medical image segmentation. We analyze segmentation results from a U-Net model trained on data annotated by multiple raters with varying levels of experience and annotation preferences. We quantify rater style through metrics capturing consistency, agreement, and bias. Our findings reveal a strong correlation between specific rater style characteristics and the model's predictive uncertainty, particularly in regions of anatomical ambiguity. Furthermore, we demonstrate that incorporating rater style information into the training process can improve model calibration and robustness. This research highlights the importance of accounting for rater variability in medical image analysis and proposes a method to mitigate its impact on deep learning model uncertainty. Keywords: Medical Image Segmentation, Deep Learning, Uncertainty, Rater Variability, U-Net, Annotation Style.
