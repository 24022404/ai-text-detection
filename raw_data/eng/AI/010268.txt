This paper presents an information-geometric approach to feature extraction and moment reconstruction in dynamical systems, addressing the challenge of efficiently capturing and representing system dynamics. The objective is to develop a robust framework that leverages geometric properties of information manifolds to extract meaningful features and reconstruct key statistical moments from complex, high-dimensional data. The proposed method integrates principles from differential geometry and information theory, enabling the construction of a feature extraction pipeline that is both computationally efficient and theoretically grounded. By modeling the system's state space as a Riemannian manifold, we derive a novel algorithm that identifies intrinsic geometric structures and extracts low-dimensional representations of the data. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the approach, showing significant improvements in reconstruction accuracy and computational efficiency compared to traditional methods. The findings highlight the potential of information-geometric techniques for analyzing dynamical systems, with applications in fields such as robotics, climate modeling, and biological systems. This work contributes a new perspective to feature extraction and moment reconstruction, offering a scalable and interpretable solution for complex dynamical systems analysis.  

**Keywords**: information geometry, feature extraction, moment reconstruction, dynamical systems, Riemannian manifolds, computational efficiency.
