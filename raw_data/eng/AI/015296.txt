Autonomous driving systems rely heavily on understanding and predicting traffic behavior. This paper introduces a novel approach for extracting traffic primitives directly from naturalistic driving data, bypassing traditional methods that depend on manually labeled datasets or predefined scenarios. Our method leverages a deep learning architecture incorporating convolutional and recurrent layers to automatically identify and classify common traffic maneuvers, such as lane changes, car following, and merges, using raw sensor data logged from real-world driving experiences. The system is trained end-to-end, learning directly from unlabeled data through a self-supervised learning framework based on temporal context prediction. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach on a large-scale dataset of naturalistic driving logs, achieving state-of-the-art performance in traffic primitive recognition. The extracted traffic primitives can then be used to improve the robustness and adaptability of self-driving algorithms by providing a more comprehensive and data-driven understanding of traffic dynamics. This work contributes a scalable and generalizable solution for learning traffic behavior, reducing reliance on expensive labeled data and improving the safety and efficiency of autonomous vehicles.

Keywords: Self-driving, traffic primitives, naturalistic driving data, deep learning, self-supervised learning, autonomous vehicles.
