Graph representation learning has emerged as a powerful technique for analyzing structured data across various domains. However, the reliance on labeled data for training often limits its applicability. This paper introduces a novel self-supervised approach to graph representation learning based on global context prediction. Our method trains a graph neural network to predict global graph properties, effectively capturing high-level structural information without requiring node or graph labels. Specifically, we design auxiliary tasks that leverage readily available graph statistics as prediction targets, such as average node degree and graph diameter. By optimizing these prediction tasks, the learned node embeddings encode information about the graph's global context. We evaluate our approach on a diverse set of graph datasets, demonstrating that our self-supervised method achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art supervised and unsupervised techniques on node classification and graph classification tasks. The results highlight the efficacy of global context prediction for learning informative and generalizable graph representations. Keywords: Graph Representation Learning, Self-Supervised Learning, Graph Neural Networks, Global Context, Unsupervised Learning.
