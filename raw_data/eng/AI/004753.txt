Seafloor image interpretation plays a crucial role in marine research, resource exploration, and environmental monitoring, yet existing methods often face challenges related to data scarcity, label acquisition, and spatial heterogeneity. This paper introduces GeoCLR, a novel Georeference Contrastive Learning framework designed to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of seafloor image interpretation by leveraging both visual content and spatial context. GeoCLR integrates georeferenced metadata with contrastive self-supervised learning, enabling the model to learn robust and meaningful representations from unlabeled underwater images. In our approach, geographically proximate image pairs are treated as positive samples, while distant pairs are considered negatives, encouraging the encoder to capture both local image features and spatial relationships. We evaluate GeoCLR on diverse seafloor datasets, demonstrating improved performance in downstream tasks such as habitat classification and anomaly detection compared to traditional supervised learning and baseline contrastive models without georeference cues. Our results indicate that GeoCLR reduces the requirement for large labeled datasets while preserving interpretability and generalizability across different marine environments. This work provides practical insights for scalable marine computer vision applications and underscores the potential of georeferenced contrastive learning in advancing autonomous seafloor mapping and analysis. Keywords: seafloor image interpretation, contrastive learning, georeferencing, self-supervised learning, marine computer vision, spatial context.
