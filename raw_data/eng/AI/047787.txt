Object detection and instance segmentation in long-tailed datasets present significant challenges due to the imbalanced distribution of object classes, where a few dominant classes overshadow the majority of rare ones. This paper addresses the critical issue of model calibration in such scenarios, where traditional methods often exhibit overconfidence in predictions for both frequent and infrequent classes. We propose a novel calibration framework tailored for long-tailed object detection and instance segmentation, leveraging a combination of class-balanced sampling strategies and adaptive loss functions to improve prediction reliability across all classes. Our approach integrates temperature scaling and label smoothing techniques, specifically optimized for long-tailed distributions, to mitigate overconfidence and enhance model generalization. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, including LVIS and COCO-LT, demonstrate that our method achieves superior calibration performance compared to existing approaches, while maintaining competitive detection and segmentation accuracy. The results highlight the importance of calibration in long-tailed learning and provide insights into improving model robustness for real-world applications where class imbalance is prevalent. This work contributes to advancing the field by offering a practical solution for achieving well-calibrated models in long-tailed object detection and instance segmentation tasks.  

**Keywords**: model calibration, long-tailed learning, object detection, instance segmentation, class imbalance, temperature scaling, label smoothing.
