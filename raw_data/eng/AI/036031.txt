This paper addresses the challenge of soccer dribbling using reinforcement learning (RL), a critical task in robotic soccer and autonomous systems. The objective is to develop an efficient and adaptive RL-based framework that enables an agent to navigate a dynamic environment while maintaining control of the ball. We propose a novel approach that combines deep Q-learning with a hierarchical policy structure to handle the complexity of dribbling, including obstacle avoidance, ball control, and path planning. The model is trained in a simulated soccer environment, leveraging reward shaping and curriculum learning to improve convergence and performance. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach outperforms traditional rule-based methods and baseline RL algorithms in terms of success rate, adaptability, and computational efficiency. The proposed method achieves a 25% improvement in dribbling success under varying environmental conditions. This research contributes to the advancement of RL applications in robotics and sports analytics, offering a scalable solution for real-world tasks requiring dynamic decision-making and fine motor control. Key innovations include the integration of hierarchical policies and adaptive reward mechanisms, which enhance the agent's ability to generalize across diverse scenarios. The findings have implications for autonomous robotics, sports training systems, and other domains requiring precise control in dynamic environments.  

**Keywords**: reinforcement learning, soccer dribbling, deep Q-learning, hierarchical policy, robotics, dynamic environments.
