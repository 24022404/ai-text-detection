This paper presents a novel approach to deciphering complex human or agent behaviors through Deep Adaptive Multi-Intention Inverse Reinforcement Learning (DAMIRL). Traditional inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) methods often assume that an agent’s behavior is driven by a single underlying intention, which limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where actions are frequently motivated by multiple, dynamic intentions. To address this, we propose an adaptive framework that leverages deep learning to simultaneously infer and disentangle multiple latent intentions from observed trajectories. Our method integrates a multi-head neural architecture with an intention-adaptive mechanism, allowing for automatic adjustment to varying numbers and types of intentions without explicit human supervision. Experimental evaluations on benchmark environments demonstrate that DAMIRL significantly improves intention identification accuracy and reward function estimation compared to conventional single-intention and fixed multi-intention IRL models. Additionally, our approach exhibits robust generalization to unseen tasks and adaptability to varying behavioral complexities. The results highlight the potential of DAMIRL in advancing intelligent systems for robotics, autonomous vehicles, and human-computer interaction, where understanding intricate intentions is essential. Key contributions include the adaptive intention modeling architecture and empirical validation on diverse, intention-rich scenarios. Keywords: inverse reinforcement learning, multi-intention, deep learning, adaptive modeling, reward learning, behavior understanding, artificial intelligence.
