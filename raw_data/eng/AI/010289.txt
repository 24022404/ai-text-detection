This paper presents a novel approach to articulated shape matching, leveraging Laplacian eigenfunctions and unsupervised point registration to establish correspondences between non-rigid shapes. Our objective is to develop a robust and efficient method for matching shapes with varying topology and geometry, which is a fundamental problem in computer vision and graphics. We propose a framework that combines the spectral properties of Laplacian eigenfunctions with an unsupervised point registration algorithm, allowing for accurate and dense correspondence estimation. Our method achieves state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets, demonstrating improved robustness to noise, outliers, and partial matches. The key contributions of our research include the introduction of a spectral shape descriptor and an unsupervised registration algorithm, which enable efficient and accurate shape matching. Our approach has significant implications for various applications, including 3D reconstruction, shape analysis, and computer-aided design. Key keywords: articulated shape matching, Laplacian eigenfunctions, unsupervised point registration, spectral shape descriptor, 3D reconstruction, computer vision, graphics.
