This paper introduces xUnit, a novel spatial activation function designed to enhance the efficiency of image restoration tasks. The objective is to improve the performance of deep neural networks in restoring degraded images by learning a spatially adaptive activation function. Our approach involves training a neural network with a custom-designed xUnit activation function, which adaptively adjusts the activation of each neuron based on spatial cues. Experimental results demonstrate that xUnit outperforms existing activation functions, achieving state-of-the-art performance in image denoising, deblurring, and super-resolution tasks. The key findings indicate that xUnit enables more efficient feature extraction and propagation, leading to improved image restoration quality. The proposed xUnit activation function has significant implications for various computer vision applications, including image and video processing, and can be easily integrated into existing deep learning architectures. By leveraging the spatial information inherent in images, xUnit offers a novel and efficient solution for image restoration, with potential applications in fields such as robotics, surveillance, and healthcare. Key keywords: image restoration, spatial activation function, deep learning, computer vision, xUnit.
