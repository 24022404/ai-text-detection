Deep Latent Defence (DLD) introduces a novel framework for improving the adversarial robustness of deep neural networks by operating directly in their latent representation. Objective: existing input‐level defenses often struggle to distinguish small, malicious perturbations from benign noise. DLD addresses this by embedding inputs into a learned low‐dimensional manifold and filtering out off‐manifold distortions. Methods: we train a variational autoencoder (VAE) to capture the clean data distribution and use its encoder to project inputs into latent space. A latent anomaly detector then flags and rejects samples lying outside a tight confidence region. Clean reconstructions are decoded back for classification by any off‐the‐shelf model. Results: on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and Tiny ImageNet benchmarks under FGSM, PGD, and CW attacks, DLD boosts robust accuracy by up to 18% compared to standard adversarial training, while maintaining over 92% accuracy on unperturbed data. Computation overhead remains below 15% per inference. Conclusion: by leveraging latent‐space constraints and anomaly detection, DLD offers a scalable, model‐agnostic defence that significantly enhances security without extensive retraining. This approach paves the way for more reliable deployment of AI systems in adversarial settings.  
Keywords: adversarial defence; latent space; variational autoencoder; anomaly detection; robustness; deep learning
