Graph representation learning has emerged as a critical area in machine learning, enabling the extraction of meaningful features from complex graph-structured data. This paper introduces a novel approach, *Kernel-Regularized Graph Representation Learning* (KGRL), which addresses the challenge of preserving both local and global structural properties in graph embeddings. The proposed method integrates kernel-based regularization techniques into graph neural networks (GNNs) to enhance the representation of graph topology while maintaining computational efficiency. By leveraging spectral graph theory and kernel functions, KGRL ensures robust feature extraction across diverse graph types, including sparse and densely connected networks. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that KGRL outperforms state-of-the-art methods in tasks such as node classification, link prediction, and graph clustering, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and scalability. The framework's ability to balance local node interactions with global graph structure highlights its potential for applications in social network analysis, bioinformatics, and recommendation systems. This work contributes a scalable and theoretically grounded solution to graph representation learning, offering a new perspective on integrating kernel methods with deep learning architectures.  

**Keywords**: graph representation learning, graph neural networks, kernel regularization, spectral graph theory, node classification, link prediction.
