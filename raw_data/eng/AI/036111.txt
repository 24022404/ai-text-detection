Zero-shot skeleton action recognition (ZS-SAR) is a challenging task in computer vision, requiring models to recognize actions not seen during training. This paper introduces a novel approach, Syntactically Guided Generative Embeddings (SGGE), to address the limitations of existing methods in capturing the syntactic structure of human actions. The proposed framework leverages a generative model to synthesize action embeddings by incorporating syntactic priors derived from the hierarchical relationships between body joints and their motion patterns. Specifically, SGGE employs a graph-based representation to model the skeletal structure and a transformer-based architecture to generate semantically rich embeddings. The model is trained using a combination of contrastive learning and syntactic regularization to ensure alignment between the generated embeddings and the underlying action semantics. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that SGGE outperforms state-of-the-art methods in zero-shot settings, achieving significant improvements in recognition accuracy. The findings highlight the importance of syntactic guidance in enhancing the generalization capabilities of action recognition models. This work contributes to advancing zero-shot learning in skeleton-based action recognition and has potential applications in human-computer interaction, surveillance, and robotics.  

**Keywords**: zero-shot learning, skeleton action recognition, generative embeddings, syntactic guidance, transformer models, graph-based representation.
