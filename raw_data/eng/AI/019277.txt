This paper addresses the challenge of protecting sensitive information in datasets while preserving their distributional properties, a crucial aspect for data analysis and modeling. The objective is to develop a novel approach to achieve k-anonymity, a widely used privacy metric, without compromising the underlying data distribution. Our method employs a combination of data transformation and anonymization techniques to ensure that the anonymized data retains the same statistical characteristics as the original data. The approach utilizes a proprietary algorithm that optimizes the trade-off between privacy and data utility. Experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms existing k-anonymity techniques in terms of distribution preservation, with significant improvements in data quality and analysis accuracy. The proposed technique has important implications for data privacy and security, enabling organizations to share and analyze sensitive data while maintaining confidentiality and integrity. Key contributions include the development of a distribution-preserving k-anonymity model, the introduction of a novel anonymization algorithm, and the demonstration of its effectiveness in real-world datasets, with relevant applications in data mining, machine learning, and statistical analysis, and related keywords include k-anonymity, data privacy, distribution preservation, and data anonymization.
