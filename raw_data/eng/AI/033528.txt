Understanding visual relations among objects in images is crucial for enabling comprehensive scene understanding in computer vision applications. However, the annotation cost for fully-supervised learning of visual relations remains a significant challenge, motivating the need for efficient weakly-supervised approaches. This paper proposes an explanation-based weakly-supervised learning framework utilizing graph networks to model and infer visual relations between objects. Our method leverages coarse, image-level supervision together with structured explanations derived from relational reasoning, effectively reducing reliance on dense annotations. We employ graph neural networks to represent objects as nodes and candidate relations as edges, enabling the integration of relational context and facilitating message passing for robust relation prediction. Experimental results on standard visual relation benchmarks demonstrate that our approach achieves competitive performance compared to fully- and weakly-supervised baselines, with notable improvements in generalization to unseen relations. Additionally, the explanation-based mechanism provides interpretable insights, enhancing model transparency and trustworthiness. Our study highlights the potential of combining graph networks and explainability in weakly-supervised settings, offering a scalable solution for visual relation understanding in complex real-world images.

Keywords: visual relation detection, weakly-supervised learning, graph neural networks, explanation-based learning, scene understanding, interpretable AI
