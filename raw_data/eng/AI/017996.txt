Animating static images presents a significant challenge in computer graphics and vision. This paper introduces a novel approach to animating pictures by leveraging Eulerian motion fields. Our method focuses on synthesizing plausible motion within a still image by first estimating a dense, pixel-level motion field using a combination of optical flow techniques and semantic segmentation. This field is then refined using a custom-designed energy function that enforces spatial coherence and smoothness, while respecting object boundaries. The refined motion field is subsequently used to advect pixel colors over time, generating an animation sequence. We present a novel blending strategy to minimize artifacts arising from disocclusion and motion discontinuities. Qualitative and quantitative evaluations demonstrate that our approach produces visually compelling animations with reduced artifacts compared to existing methods. Our work contributes a robust framework for animating static pictures, opening avenues for creative content generation and visual storytelling. Keywords: Image animation, Eulerian motion fields, optical flow, semantic segmentation, computer vision, animation synthesis.
