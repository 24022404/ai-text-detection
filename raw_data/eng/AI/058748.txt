**Abstract**

This paper presents TempoRL, a novel reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to address the challenge of adaptive action timing in sequential decision-making tasks. Traditional RL algorithms typically operate on fixed time steps, often resulting in suboptimal performance in environments where optimal action timing is crucial. TempoRL introduces a dedicated temporal policy that learns not only which action to take, but also when to act, enabling dynamic adjustment of action intervals based on the current state context. The proposed approach integrates temporal abstraction into standard RL models by augmenting the policy network with a timing module trained using policy gradients. Extensive experiments are conducted on benchmark continuous control and simulated robotics environments, demonstrating that TempoRL outperforms baseline RL algorithms in terms of sample efficiency, accumulated rewards, and adaptability to varying temporal dynamics. Through comprehensive analysis, we show that learning to modulate action timings leads to more efficient exploration and improved long-term decision-making, especially in tasks with complex or non-uniform event frequencies. TempoRL provides a flexible and extensible foundation for future research in temporally-aware RL agents, with potential applications in real-time system control, gaming AI, and autonomous robotics.  
**Keywords:** reinforcement learning, temporal abstraction, adaptive timing, action selection, policy gradients, sequential decision-making, robotics.
