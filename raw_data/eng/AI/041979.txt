This paper proposes an innovative approach to unsupervised action recognition, leveraging augmented skeleton-based contrastive learning and momentum LSTM. The objective is to develop a robust and efficient framework for recognizing human actions without relying on labeled data. Our method utilizes a contrastive learning framework to learn effective representations from skeleton sequences, which are then enhanced through momentum LSTM to capture long-term temporal dependencies. The results demonstrate significant improvements in action recognition accuracy, outperforming existing state-of-the-art unsupervised methods. Key findings include the effectiveness of augmented skeleton data in boosting model performance and the importance of momentum LSTM in preserving temporal information. This research contributes to the field of action recognition by introducing a novel, unsupervised approach that can be applied to various applications, including human-computer interaction, surveillance, and healthcare. The proposed framework highlights the potential of contrastive learning and momentum LSTM in advancing unsupervised action recognition, with relevant keywords including action recognition, unsupervised learning, contrastive learning, momentum LSTM, and skeleton-based analysis.
