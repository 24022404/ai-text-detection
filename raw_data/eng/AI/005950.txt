This paper presents a novel information-geometric approach to feature extraction and moment reconstruction in dynamical systems. The objective is to develop a framework that leverages geometric techniques to identify and extract relevant features from complex systems, enabling accurate reconstruction of moments and improved understanding of system behavior. Our approach employs a combination of differential geometry and information theory to analyze the underlying structure of dynamical systems, providing a unique perspective on feature extraction and moment reconstruction. The methodology involves the use of Riemannian metrics and geodesic equations to identify optimal feature sets, which are then used to reconstruct system moments. Results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in extracting relevant features and reconstructing moments in various dynamical systems, outperforming traditional methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The proposed framework has significant implications for fields such as signal processing, control theory, and machine learning, and contributes to the development of more accurate and robust models of complex systems. Key contributions include the introduction of a geometric framework for feature extraction and moment reconstruction, and the demonstration of its potential in analyzing and understanding complex dynamical systems. Relevant keywords: information geometry, dynamical systems, feature extraction, moment reconstruction, Riemannian metrics, geodesic equations.
