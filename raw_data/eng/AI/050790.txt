Objective: Estimating causal effects of continuous-valued interventions from observational data remains challenging due to confounding bias and limited support overlap. This paper introduces CI-GAN, a novel generative adversarial network framework designed to model potential outcomes under varying treatment intensities directly.  
Methods: CI-GAN comprises a generator that produces counterfactual outcomes conditioned on covariates and continuous treatment dosages, and a discriminator that enforces alignment between generated and observed data distributions. To ensure reliable estimation across the treatment spectrum, we incorporate an inverse-weighting regularization term derived from a learned propensity function, promoting stability in regions with sparse observations.  
Results: We evaluate CI-GAN on both synthetic benchmarks and a real-world medical dosage dataset, comparing its performance against leading dose-response models, causal forests, and deep propensity-based approaches. CI-GAN achieves up to 20% lower mean squared error in treatment effect estimation and demonstrates superior Precision in Estimation of Heterogeneous Effect (PEHE), particularly in low-density treatment regions.  
Conclusion: The proposed GAN-based architecture offers a flexible, scalable solution for continuous treatment effect estimation, delivering accurate counterfactual predictions and robustness to distributional shifts. CI-GAN’s ability to capture nonlinear dose-response relationships makes it well suited for applications in personalized medicine, economic policy evaluation, and resource allocation.  
Keywords: Generative Adversarial Networks, continuous-valued interventions, causal inference, dose-response estimation, counterfactual analysis.
