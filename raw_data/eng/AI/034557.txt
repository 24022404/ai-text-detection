Word embeddings, such as word2vec, are fundamental to modern natural language processing, yet their theoretical foundations remain partially understood. This paper investigates the spectral properties of word2vec embeddings, providing insights into their inherent structure. We demonstrate that word2vec, when interpreted through a spectral lens, implicitly performs dimensionality reduction and captures underlying semantic relationships within the corpus. Our analysis reveals a connection between the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the word-context matrix and the resulting word embeddings. Specifically, we show how the top eigenvectors of the word-context matrix correspond to dominant semantic themes, and how word2vec effectively approximates these principal components. Furthermore, we analyze the spectral decay of the embeddings and its impact on downstream task performance. Empirical evaluations on benchmark datasets confirm that our spectral interpretation aligns with observed empirical behavior, suggesting that word2vec's success is, in part, attributable to its ability to capture salient spectral features of language. This work provides a rigorous mathematical framework for understanding word2vec and offers potential avenues for improving embedding techniques. Keywords: word2vec, word embeddings, spectral analysis, singular value decomposition, natural language processing, dimensionality reduction.
