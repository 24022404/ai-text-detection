This paper addresses the challenge of designing robust decision-making frameworks under uncertainty, focusing on Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with ambiguous transition probabilities. Traditional robust MDP approaches often rely on overly conservative ambiguity sets, which can lead to suboptimal policies. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel method for constructing *tight Bayesian ambiguity sets* that balance robustness and performance. Our approach leverages Bayesian inference to incorporate prior knowledge and observed data, enabling the creation of ambiguity sets that are both statistically rigorous and computationally tractable. We introduce an efficient algorithm to solve the resulting robust MDP problem, demonstrating its effectiveness through extensive experiments on benchmark environments. Results show that our method outperforms existing approaches in terms of policy robustness and computational efficiency, particularly in scenarios with limited data. The proposed framework has significant implications for applications requiring reliable decision-making under uncertainty, such as robotics, autonomous systems, and healthcare. Key contributions include the integration of Bayesian principles into robust MDPs, the development of tight ambiguity sets, and a scalable solution algorithm. This work advances the state-of-the-art in robust decision-making by providing a principled and practical approach to handling uncertainty.  

**Keywords**: Robust MDPs, Bayesian ambiguity sets, decision-making under uncertainty, Markov Decision Processes, robust optimization.
