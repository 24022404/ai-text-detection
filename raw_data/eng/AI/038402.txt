This paper addresses the challenge of estimating underlying probability distributions from observed data contaminated by noise, a problem commonly encountered in signal processing, medical imaging, and econometrics. Traditional parametric approaches often impose restrictive assumptions on the data, limiting their applicability. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel nonparametric deconvolution framework that leverages kernel-based methods and adaptive bandwidth selection techniques. Our approach eliminates the need for predefined distributional assumptions, enabling robust and flexible estimation across diverse datasets. Through extensive simulations and real-world case studies, we demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing parametric and nonparametric techniques in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. Key findings reveal significant improvements in recovering the true underlying distributions, particularly in scenarios with complex noise structures. The results highlight the potential of this framework for applications requiring precise density estimation, such as anomaly detection and image reconstruction. This research contributes to the field by providing a versatile and scalable solution for deconvolution problems, offering new insights into nonparametric statistical modeling.  

**Keywords**: Nonparametric deconvolution, kernel methods, density estimation, adaptive bandwidth, noise reduction, statistical modeling.
