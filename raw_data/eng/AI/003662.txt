Multi-task learning (MTL) has shown promise in improving natural language processing (NLP) models through transfer learning. However, traditional MTL often requires substantial data and increases model complexity due to shared parameters across tasks. This paper introduces Conditionally Adaptive Multi-Task Learning (CAMTL), a novel approach that selectively activates task-specific parameters based on input conditions, thereby minimizing parameter overhead and data dependency. CAMTL employs a lightweight conditioning network to dynamically modulate task-specific layers, enabling adaptive transfer learning. Our experiments on a diverse set of NLP tasks demonstrate that CAMTL achieves comparable or superior performance to standard MTL methods while using significantly fewer parameters and requiring less training data. Specifically, CAMTL reduces the number of parameters by up to 40% and achieves a 15% improvement in low-resource settings compared to traditional MTL. The findings highlight the potential of CAMTL for efficient and effective transfer learning in NLP, particularly in scenarios with limited resources. Keywords: Multi-task learning, transfer learning, natural language processing, parameter efficiency, conditional computation, low-resource NLP.
