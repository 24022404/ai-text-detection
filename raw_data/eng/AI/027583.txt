This paper presents a novel approach to hierarchical agglomerative clustering, introducing an order preserving methodology that maintains the inherent structure of the data throughout the clustering process. The objective is to develop a clustering algorithm that not only groups similar data points but also preserves their natural order, enhancing the interpretability and usefulness of the clustering results. Our approach utilizes a customized distance metric and a hierarchical merging strategy, ensuring that the order of the data points is respected at each level of the clustering hierarchy. The results show that our order preserving hierarchical agglomerative clustering (OPHAC) algorithm outperforms traditional clustering methods in terms of cluster quality and order preservation, particularly in applications where data order is crucial, such as time-series analysis and genomic data analysis. The key contributions of this research include the introduction of a novel clustering algorithm, the development of a customized distance metric, and the demonstration of the algorithm's effectiveness in preserving data order. The OPHAC algorithm has significant implications for various fields, including data mining, bioinformatics, and machine learning, where order preservation is essential for meaningful insights and accurate analysis. Key keywords: hierarchical clustering, order preservation, agglomerative clustering, data mining, bioinformatics.
