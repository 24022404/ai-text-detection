This paper investigates the application of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for the synthesis of flight controllers, addressing the critical need for adaptive and robust control mechanisms in modern aeronautical systems.

Methods/Approach: We propose a novel framework employing advanced DRL algorithms to develop flight controllers capable of autonomously learning complex control strategies. The architecture utilizes a deep neural network to approximate the value function, allowing the controller to make decisions based on a continuous stream of sensor data. We implemented Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a state-of-the-art DRL approach, to enable efficient and stable learning in the high-dimensional, nonlinear dynamics of aircraft systems.

Results/Findings: Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the DRL-based flight controller significantly outperforms traditional PID-based controllers in terms of adaptability to dynamic environmental conditions and non-linear perturbations. In simulated flight environments, our controller achieved superior stability, precision, and responsiveness across various scenarios, including turbulence and system failure recovery. Comparative analysis highlights the controller's ability to optimize control policies without extensive hand-tuning.

Conclusion/Implications: This research contributes a tangible advancement in flight control technology by harnessing the capabilities of deep reinforcement learning. The findings suggest promising implications for the development of next-generation autonomously controlled air vehicles, enhancing their operational reliability and flexibility. Our approach sets the stage for future investigations into scaling DRL methodologies for real-time applications in complex aeronautical domains.

Keywords: Deep Reinforcement Learning, Flight Controller, Autonomous Systems, Proximal Policy Optimization, Neural Networks, Adaptive Control
