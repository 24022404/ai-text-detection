Obstacle detection and avoidance in low-light or nighttime environments is a critical challenge for autonomous systems, such as unmanned vehicles and robotics. This paper presents a novel approach to night vision obstacle detection and avoidance utilizing Bio-Inspired Vision Sensors (BIVS). Mimicking the high sensitivity and dynamic range of biological visual systems, the proposed method leverages event-driven neuromorphic cameras to capture visual information in real time, even under extremely low illumination conditions. The system combines advanced event-based processing algorithms with deep learning techniques to accurately distinguish obstacles from background noise and to determine optimal avoidance trajectories. Experimental evaluations were conducted using both controlled test scenarios and real-world outdoor environments, demonstrating substantial improvements in detection accuracy, response time, and robustness compared to conventional image-based night vision systems. Key results show a 25% enhancement in obstacle recognition rates and a significant reduction in false positives. The proposed framework offers significant implications for the deployment of autonomous vehicles and robotic platforms in night-time or adverse lighting conditions, paving the way for safer navigation and broader application of autonomous technologies. Keywords: night vision, obstacle detection, obstacle avoidance, bio-inspired vision, neuromorphic sensors, event-based camera, autonomous navigation.
